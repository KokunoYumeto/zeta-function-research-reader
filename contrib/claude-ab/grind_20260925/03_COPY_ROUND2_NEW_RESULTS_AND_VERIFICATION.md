### 2.5 Theorem E (velocity-spectrum characterization of the Eulerian weights)

**Definition.** Φ = Σ c_λ e^{−λs} (c₀ = 1) is *multiplicative over Π* if:
- Π ⊂ (0, ∞) is Q-linearly independent;
- Φ = ∏_{π∈Π} φ_π(e^{−πs}), absolutely convergent for Re s large, with each φ_π a power series satisfying φ_π(0) = 1.

No shape is imposed on the local factors.

**Theorem E.** For a > 0 the following are equivalent:
- (i) 𝒬_a is multiplicative over some Π;
- (ii) G_a = a^sζ(s,a) is multiplicative over some Π;
- (iii) a ∈ {1, 1/2}.

At a = 1, Π = {log p}; at a = 1/2, Π = {log p : p odd}.

**Proof.**

*(i) ⇔ (ii).*
- 𝒬_a = G_a(s+1)/G_a(s).
- G_a = ∏_{j≥0}𝒬_a(s+j)^{−1}: telescope and use G_a(σ+k) → 1; the product converges because 𝒬_a(σ+j) − 1 = O(q₁^{−j}).
- A Π-factorization of one side gives one of the other: ψ_π(e^{−π}x)/ψ_π(x) in one direction, ∏_jφ_π(e^{−πj}x)^{−1} in the other.
- Coefficients of absolutely convergent generalized Dirichlet series are unique (Bohr mean values).

*(iii) ⇒ (ii).* The usual Euler products for ζ and for Σ_{m odd} m^{−s}.

*(ii) ⇒ (iii).* G_a has coefficient 1 at each log q_n and 0 elsewhere, so every local coefficient is in {0, 1}. Hence Q_a = ∏_π E_π with unique representation, where E_π ⊂ Q_a.

1. **The smallest frequency is pure.** log q₁ = k₁π₁ for a single π₁; each pure component of a mixed frequency would be a smaller positive frequency.
2. **Some q_{n₀} avoids π₁.** Otherwise G_a = ψ_{π₁}(e^{−π₁s}), since any other factor would contribute a frequency with zero π₁-component. Then every q_n would be a power of θ = e^{π₁}. Consecutive distinct powers differ by ≥ θ^k(θ − 1) → ∞, while q_{n+1} − q_n = 1/a is constant.
3. **a is rational.** Disjoint supports give q₁q_{n₀} ∈ Q_a. Since q₁q_n = 1 + (n + 1 + n/a)/a, this forces n₀/a ∈ Z, so **a ∈ Q**; write a = u/v.
4. **a = 1/q.**
   - q₂ is not a pure π₁-power: that would force u = 1 and then (1+2v)^{k₁} = (1+v)^{k₂} with coprime bases > 1, which is impossible.
   - q₂ is not e·r with e ∈ E_{π₁} and r in the other factors, both ≠ 1: that would give q₂ ≥ q₁² > q₂.
   - So q₂ has support disjoint from π₁, hence q₁q₂ ∈ Q_a and **2/a ∈ Z**.
   - For q₃: the mixed case forces e = r = q₁, which is impossible. The pure case forces u = 1 and (1+3v)^{k₁} = (1+v)^{k₃}; since the gcd divides 2 and 3 − 2/(1+v) ∈ [2, 3), this forces v = 1, i.e. a = 1. Otherwise **3/a ∈ Z**.
   - Hence a = 1 or 1/a = 3/a − 2/a ∈ Z, so **a = 1/q**.
5. **q ≥ 3 is impossible.** Here Q_a = {m ≡ 1 mod q}.
   - By Dirichlet's theorem take a prime r ≢ 1 (mod q), coprime to q, of order o ≥ 2, and a prime r′ ≠ r with r′ ≡ r^{−1} (mod q).
   - r^o, r′^o and rr′ each lie in Q_a, and their only divisors in Q_a are 1 and themselves. Pure components divide their element, so all three are pure.
   - But log r^o + log r′^o = o·log rr′. This contradicts Q-independence of three distinct π's; and if two π's coincide, a rational relation between logs of distinct prime powers follows. ∎

**Relation to N1.** N1 assumes Beurling-type local factors. Theorem E allows arbitrary local factors and any Q-independent basis, so it is stronger. This proof does not use N1.

## 3. Target (2): the one-sided tail

### 3.1 Theorem D (velocity angle = Gram offset)

**Setup.** Suppose ρ₁, …, ρ_n are simple, on the line, and are all the zeros with 0 < γ ≤ γ_n; this holds for n ≤ 10¹³ (Platt–Trudgian). Put:
- S_n := n − 3/2 − θ(γ_n)/π, the average of S(γ_n ± 0);
- S₃/₂(t) := π^{−1} arg ζ(3/2 + it), the principal value, with |S₃/₂| < 0.43 because |arg| ≤ (π/2)P(3/2) < 1.34;
- φ_n := arctan(1/2γ_n).

**Statement.**
- (a) ζ′(ρ_n) = |ζ′(ρ_n)| e^{iπS_n}.
- (b) c₁(ρ_n) = ρ_n (|ζ(3/2+iγ_n)|/|ζ′(ρ_n)|) e^{iπ(S₃/₂ − S_n)}.
- (c) Re c₁ = |c₁| sin(π(S_n − S₃/₂) + φ_n) and Im c₁ = |c₁| cos(π(S_n − S₃/₂) + φ_n).

**Proof.**
- Z is real, Z(0) = ζ(½) < 0, and Z changes sign exactly at γ₁, …, γ_n, so sign Z′(γ_n) = (−1)^{n+1}.
- Hence ζ′(ρ_n) = −ie^{−iθ}Z′(γ_n) = |ζ′| e^{i(π(n+½) − θ(γ_n))}, which gives (a).
- (b) follows from (a); (c) from Re[(½ + iγ)e^{iψ}] = −|ρ| sin(ψ − φ). ∎

**D1 (Gram's law for velocities).**
- Gram's law for the n-th zero is equivalent to S_n ∈ (−½, ½).
- A zero in the lower part of its Gram interval (S_n > S₃/₂) moves right; one in the upper part moves left; at S_n = S₃/₂ − φ_n/π it moves vertically.

**D2 (close pairs).**
- S_{n+1} − S_n = 1 − (θ(γ_{n+1}) − θ(γ_n))/π, so the two velocities of a close pair are nearly antiparallel.
- The spike of E on (γ_n, γ_{n+1}) points in direction π(½ + S₃/₂ − S_n) − φ_n.
- It is negative real iff S_gap − S₃/₂ ≡ 0 (mod 2), positive iff ≡ 1, and vertical iff ≡ ±½.

**D4 (Gram-straddling pairs, exact).**
- Suppose γ_n < g_j < γ_{n+1}, where θ(g_j) = jπ, and η + |S₃/₂| + φ/π < ½ with η := (θ(γ_{n+1}) − θ(γ_n))/π.
- Then sign Re c₁(ρ_n) = −(−1)^{S(g_j)} and sign Re c₁(ρ_{n+1}) = +(−1)^{S(g_j)}, where S(g_j) = N(g_j) − j − 1 ∈ Z.
- Since Z(g_j) = (−1)^{j+S(g_j)}|ζ(½+ig_j)|, the spike is negative iff Gram's law holds at g_j.
- Proof: S_n = S(g_j) − ½ + η₁ with η₁ ∈ (0, η); substitute into (c).

### 3.3 Explanation and scope

- **Proved.** Theorem D and its corollaries fix the direction of every spike exactly.
- **Observed below 7.5·10⁴.**
  - At close pairs, S_gap − S₃/₂ ∈ (−0.38, 0.50), so every sizeable spike is negative.
  - The single exception sits at 0.502, with Re c₁/γ ≈ +0.006.
  - The positive side of Re E/T stays below 0.17 because a positive spike would need S_gap − S₃/₂ ≈ 1 (mod 2).
- **Partially derived.**
  - S jumps by +1 at each member of a close pair, so min S + 1 ≲ S_gap ≲ max S − 1. With the observed range of S, this forces |S_gap| ≲ 0.63.
  - The further concentration of S_gap (std 0.23, against 0.31 at random zeros) is not derived.
  - Heuristic, labelled as opinion: close pairs produced by the peak of 2cos θ at a Gram point have a Gram-correct excursion, hence S(g) even.
- **Open.**
  - The n = 1 Riemann–Siegel term's share of Z″ decays like 5/log√(t/2π), and the variance of S grows like (1/2π²) log log t.
  - So positive spikes are expected at larger heights. This is not proved.
  - Theorem C(i) is unsigned.

## 4. Target (3): exact hypotheses of Theorem C (a = 1; the same at a = 1/2)

| Statement | Hypothesis |
|---|---|
| A, B, F | none |
| (iii) mean square of the analytic smoothed error → (7/12π²)Σ b² e^{−Δ² log²n}/(n log²n) | none |
| (i′) sup_Δ lim sup \|Re W_Δ\|/T = sup_Δ lim sup \|Im W_Δ\|/T = ∞ | none (B + Lemma U + Kronecker) |
| (i) lim sup \|Re E\|/T = lim sup \|Im E\|/T = ∞ for sharp sums of Res F | all but finitely many zeros simple and on the line |
| (ii) lim sup X^{−3}∫\|E\|² ≥ 0.0373 | same |
| sharp coefficients equal −b(n)/(2π√n log n) | same, plus existence of Bohr means (would follow from a J₋₁-type mean-square bound) |

**Why these hypotheses are needed.** The explicit formula weights a zero by H(ρ) = h(γ − i(β − ½)); the sharp sums weight it by 1_{γ≤T}.
- **Off-line zeros.** An off-line pair ρ, 1 − ρ̄ shares an ordinate and contributes an edge term ≈ −iδh′(γ)(c(ρ) − c(ρ′)), of size about γ/|ζ″|. The sharp hypothesis controls only c(ρ) + c(ρ′).
- **Multiple zeros.** Their higher Laurent coefficients enter through h^{(j)} and are uncontrolled.
- **Finitely many exceptions are harmless**, since their edge terms decay like a Gaussian in T.
- **The a = 2 run shows concretely** that off-line zeros change both the mean and the spectrum of sharp sums.

## 5. Target (4): typed morphisms to L(ψ, s)

**Setting.** M_S = H¹(y² = x³ − x). J = [i]* with eigenvectors e_± (eigenvalues ±i). ψ((α)) = α for α ≡ 1 mod (2+2i), and d(n) = χ₋₄(n)n = ψ(nZ[i]).

**Proposition H.** As Frobenius modules at odd p:
- **(a) End(M_S)^J = span{id, J} ≅ Q ⊕ Q(χ₋₄).** Frobenius conjugates J to J^p = χ₋₄(p)J. This is TD12's H⁰ ⊕ H¹_c(U). The L-function is ζ(s)L(s,χ₋₄) = ζ_{Q(i)}(s); the complement is Ind(ψ/ψ̄).
- **(b) Λ²M_S ≅ Q(−1).** Frobenius is p, so this is TD3's H² with degree p. The L-function is ζ(s−1).
- **(c) (Sym²M_S)^J = span{e₊e₋} ≅ Q(−1)(χ₋₄).** Frobenius is d(p) = χ₋₄(p)p (ππ̄ at split p; −p at inert p). This is TD12's H¹(C^×) with its signed degree. The L-function is L(s−1, χ₋₄) = Σ_{n odd} d(n)n^{−s}, the Connes–Consani signed-degree series. The J-anti-invariant part is Ind(ψ²).
- **(d)** L(Sym²E, s) = L(ψ², s)·L(s−1, χ₋₄), and Σ_{n odd} ψ(nZ[i])N(nZ[i])^{−s} = L(2s−1, χ₋₄).

**Proof.** Frobenius on e_± is diag(π, π̄) at split p and antidiagonal with product −p at inert p. Use Sym²(Ind ψ) = Ind(ψ²) ⊕ χ₋₄N and End(Ind ψ) = 1 ⊕ χ₋₄ ⊕ Ind(ψ/ψ̄).

**Check** (`sym2_check.py`). Coefficients of L(Sym²E, s) from point counts a_p, against the Dirichlet convolution of Gaussian-integer ψ²-sums with d(n):
```

sample (n, coefficient): (3,-3) (5,-1) (9,18) (13,23) (15,3) (25,6)
```

**Reading.** The programme's weight-0 and weight-2 objects are exactly the J-invariant parts of the tensor constructions on the weight-one sector, and ζ(s) is the L-function of the identity line. This is a typing statement only: it does not place ζ's zeros in M_S.

## 6. Negative results and limits (exact scope)

1. **Sharp spectrum = Dirichlet spectrum fails off the Eulerian sheets** (a = 2, at every tested frequency). Only the analytically smoothed spectrum is universal.
2. **One-sided Ω** (separate lim sup Re E/T = +∞ and lim inf = −∞) is **not proved**. The data are one-sided, and §3.3 explains why at these heights.
3. **The asymptotic fate of the asymmetry** is open.
4. **κ(a):** only the accounting identity is proved; its value is numerical.
5. **Sharp Theorem C without RH** is not reachable by this method.
6. **Lemma U for rational non-Eulerian a** was not attempted; for transcendental a only a sketch exists.
7. **Proposition H** types the programme's data but gives no route from M_S to the zeros of ζ.
