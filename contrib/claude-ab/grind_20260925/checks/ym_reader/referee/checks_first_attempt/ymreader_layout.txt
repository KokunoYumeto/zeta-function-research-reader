     The Zenodo record 10.5281/zenodo.22883643, SU(2) lattice Yang–Mills: vacuum, energy, spectral
     bounds and continuum investigations, is the Yang–Mills part of the owner’s research work-
     bench (KokunoYumeto/yang-mills-interacting-workbench). It holds 43 files from five edi-
     tions, dated 8 to 21 September 2026. The record’s front file, README.md, is still the one written
     for the first edition of 8 September.
         This reader says:
     • which of the 43 files is current and which are superseded;
     • what each document claims, and on which regime;
     • what is proved, with proofs;
     • how far each statement was checked.
        The central result (Section 3) concerns the Kogut–Susskind Hamiltonian of 𝑆𝑈(2) lattice
     gauge theory on open cubic boxes. It is an explicit lower bound on the spectral gap above
     the ground state, on the whole gauge-invariant space, uniform in the box size and the lattice
     spacing, for bare coupling 𝑔2 ≥ 3.6836 in the workbench’s normalisation. The threshold rests
     on computed coefficient bounds of orders 3 to 5; with inputs checked independently here, the
     same argument gives a gap for 𝑔2 ≥ 4.0187.
        Section 4 states the heat-flow results of the latest edition:
     • every constant of the box-independent heat remainder is re-derived here;
     • the benchmark table for relaxation into the whole complementary physical space is recom-
       puted exactly from the stated inputs.
     Sections 5–6 map the earlier editions and the Jacobian-map, Navier–Stokes and 𝑆6 material,
     and Section 7 states the obstructions with their exact scope.
         All results hold at fixed lattice spacing and strong bare coupling. The workbench does not
     claim a continuum mass gap, and nothing here bears on that problem.

1 The problem, the setting, and what the workbench claims
1.1 The continuum problem
The Clay Mathematics Institute’s problem, described by Jaffe and Witten [3], asks for a proof that
for every compact simple gauge group a nontrivial quantum Yang–Mills theory exists on ℝ4 and
has a mass gap Δ > 0. The existence part asks for a continuum theory satisfying axioms at least
as strong as the Wightman or Osterwalder–Schrader axioms ([3], §4); estimates for a model at
fixed lattice spacing do not by themselves answer it.

1.2 The lattice setting of the workbench
The workbench works with a spatial lattice and continuous time, in the Hamiltonian formulation
of Kogut and Susskind [4], for the group 𝑆𝑈(2).
• Box. The vertices form the open box {−𝐿, … , 𝐿}3 , 𝐿 ≥ 2. The links 𝑒 are the positively oriented
  nearest-neighbour edges inside the box, and the plaquettes 𝑝 are the elementary faces inside it;
  there are 𝑀𝐿 = 12𝐿2 (2𝐿 + 1) of them.
• Space. Each link carries a matrix 𝑈𝑒 ∈ 𝑆𝑈(2), and the configuration space carries the product
  Haar probability measure. The physical Hilbert space is the subspace of 𝐿2 invariant under the
  gauge transformations at every vertex, boundary vertices included.
• Hamiltonian (the workbench’s normalisation):

opposite regime. The workbench records the conversion to the convention of Schütte, Zheng and
Hamer [8]: 𝑔𝐶 = 23/4 𝑔, and the Hamiltonians differ by the factor √2 and a constant. Numerical
thresholds below are in the workbench’s normalisation.

1.3 What is known
• Strong coupling. Mass gaps at strong coupling are classical for lattice gauge theories in the
  Euclidean setting (Osterwalder and Seiler [7]; cited, not re-read here).
• The exponential vacuum. Writing the ground state as 𝑒𝑣 and solving for 𝑣 order by order in the
  coupling is the coupled-cluster method of Hamiltonian lattice field theory; the workbench
  credits it, with the character and Casimir calculus it uses, to Schütte, Zheng and Hamer [8].

Whether an explicit bound of the kind proved in Section 3 (uniform in the box, on the whole
physical space, with a closed coupling threshold) is in the literature was not searched.

1.5 The approach in one paragraph
The ground state of 𝐻𝐿 is positive, so it can be written 𝜓 = 𝑒𝑣 /‖𝑒𝑣 ‖. Inserting this into the eigenvalue
equation gives a nonlinear equation for 𝑣, namely 𝑣 = 𝜉 𝑣1 + 𝐵(𝑣, 𝑣) with 𝑣1 = 𝑆/3, whose formal
solution is a power series ∑𝑛 𝜉 𝑛 𝑣𝑛 (Lemma 3.3). The workbench computes the first five coefficients
exactly, bounds them in weighted Fourier-algebra norms that are local, so that nothing grows
with the box, and solves for the remainder by a contraction argument. Returning to the eigenvalue
equation then gives a lower bound for every excitation energy (Proposition 3.4). The same control
of the vacuum gives box-independent bounds for heat correlations of plaquettes, and bounds for
how much the plaquette family mixes with the rest of the physical space (Section 4).

6.3 𝑆6

conclusion. Its attribution file, which it designates as the clarification, says that the manuscript
was circulated by Levent Alpöge and produced with Claude (older files say “with Fable”).
In this record the construction enters only through its regular-fibre period matrix. The spatial
and volume lines use its imaginary-period block to build a magnetic background, magnetic link

The 𝑆6 project’s own results, and the record in which they are published (10.5281/zen-

9 What remains open, and what was not checked
9.1 Open questions
1. The continuum. Every result of the record holds at a fixed lattice spacing and at strong bare
   coupling (Proposition 3.6). A continuum theory, and its mass gap, are outside what the record
   addresses; the spatial line’s conclusion names the interacting local continuum and a nonzero
   low-energy spectral weight as its unresolved target (§28).
2. Lower thresholds. The workbench records that re-centring its norm inequality cannot move the
   source domain past 𝛼5 . It names the next candidate, the same-support linearized action 𝐽5
   with a preconditioned residual, and records that the sixth-source catalogue is missing from
   the delivered sources (H7).
3. Independent confirmation of the inputs. The threshold 𝑔2 ≥ 3.6836 rests on computed coefficient
   bounds of orders 3 to 5. An implementation independent of the workbench’s programs would
   remove the condition; with the inputs checked here the threshold is 4.0187 (Corollary 3.5(b)).
