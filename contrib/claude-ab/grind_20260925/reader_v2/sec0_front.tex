\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This reader sets out what the split-zero programme around the Riemann zeta function has established, as found in an audit of the programme's files carried out between 22 and 26 September 2026.
The programme studies the zeros of $\zeta$ through the Hurwitz sheets $\zeta(s,1+t)$, through quotient modules of entire functions attached to the zeros (Meyer's zeta quotient and its relatives), and through arithmetic, cohomological and positivity constructions modelled on Weil's explicit formula and on Deligne's proof of the Weil conjectures.
The reader has three parts.
Sections~\ref{sec:sheets}--\ref{sec:other} state the proved results; the central ones are proved in full here, and every other one is given with the exact location of its proof.
Section~\ref{sec:negative} states the routes that are proved \emph{not} to decide the location of the zeros by themselves, each with the precise scope of what fails and, where the notes identify it, the additional input that would be needed.
Sections~\ref{sec:bridges}--\ref{sec:open} record the identified connections with other fields and with the author's other workbenches, and the questions that remain open.
Nothing in this reader proves or disproves the Riemann hypothesis, and no result is presented as doing so.
\end{abstract}

\section*{About this reader}\phantomsection\label{sec:about}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{About this reader}

\paragraph{What was read.}
The programme consists of several hundred numbered statements with proofs (labels such as \lbl{MCL5}, \lbl{OMS6}, \lbl{GSL4}), written in the author's workbenches together with ChatGPT and Codex lanes, and collected in this Zenodo record and in the public repository \href{https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/zeta-function-research-reader}{\texttt{KokunoYumeto/\allowbreak zeta-function-\allowbreak research-reader}}.
The audit read the blocks listed in Appendix~\ref{app:verification}: the second attempt's proofs, the continuation bulletins of 24--25 September, the programme's Deligne reader, and the final messages of the Codex sessions of 24--25 September.
It is not exhaustive: the main files that were not audited are named in Section~\ref{sec:open}, and each audit note lists its own unchecked inputs.

\paragraph{How the audit was done.}
Each block was read against its own proofs.
The audit notes \note{08\_}--\note{42\_} were each refereed by an independent Claude instance that had not written them (nineteen passes), and a two-part final pass by further instances checked every summary in the register and the digest against the notes; the content maps \note{01\_}, \note{04\_}, \note{05\_}, \note{07\_} and the copy's reports \note{02\_}, \note{03\_} were not refereed separately (the copy's Theorem~E had its own full referee pass, \note{06\_}).
Every numerical claim has a check script, and the scripts and their outputs are part of the audit notes included in this record (\note{00-claude-opus-5-5-audit-notes-and-checks.zip}).
Corrections found in these passes were applied; Appendix~\ref{app:corrections} lists them. Version~2 of this reader applies a later review of version~1 for understatement and missed generality as well as for overstatement: four corrections, and stronger forms of more than twenty statements (Appendix~\ref{app:corrections}, last subsection).

\paragraph{Who did what.}
The programme, its constructions and most of its theorems are the work of its author, KokunoYumeto, developed with ChatGPT and Codex lanes.
Two Claude instances contributed results of their own: the lane that prepared this reader (\emph{claude-ab}) and a second instance of the same model (\emph{the copy}), whose Theorems A--F on the Hurwitz sheets are reported in Section~\ref{sec:sheets}.
Version~2 adds results found by further Claude instances that wrote neither the reader nor the notes: the reviewer of version~1, whose report states and proves the stronger forms recorded in \note{48\_} (items F1--F22); a verifier of the minor items F6--F22, who corrected six of their wordings; and the referee of version~2, who found the angle condition of Proposition~\ref{prop:NB} and the extension of Proposition~\ref{prop:SWzeros} to composite moduli. claude-ab re-derived the corrections O1--O4, the major items F1--F5 and the two referee items, with checks of its own. Each result below says whose it is: \emph{programme} (proved in the programme's files, audited here), \emph{copy}, \emph{claude-ab}, or, in version~2, \emph{the review}; a form called \emph{version~2's} in a status line was found in the review and verified as just described.
Where two derivations of the same fact exist, the earlier one is credited first.

\paragraph{Status labels.}
Every statement carries one of these labels.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textsf{Proved here}: the proof is given in full in this reader.
\item \textsf{Proved in the programme}: the proof is in the programme's files, at the label given; the audit read it and found it correct, and states which steps were re-derived.
\item \textsf{Proved by the copy}: the proof is in the copy's reports (audit notes \note{02\_}, \note{03\_}); the audit checked it as stated.
\item \textsf{Checked numerically}: a statement supported by computation only; it is never presented as proved.
\item \textsf{Proposal} or \textsf{reading}: a typed matching between the author's terms and exact objects, or an interpretation; the objects and the facts about them are proved, the matching or the interpretation is not a theorem.
\end{itemize}
Novelty is reported only as the audit recorded it, in the words of the audit: for example \emph{not found in the literature searched} (a search was made), \emph{not searched}, \emph{possibly known}, \emph{believed new}, \emph{elementary} or \emph{classical}.
