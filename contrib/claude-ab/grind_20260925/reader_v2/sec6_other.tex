\section{Further results}\label{sec:other}

\subsection{The two poles, and the point where the nonzero numbers stop}

For an even Schwartz function $h$ put $\mathcal{M}_Sh(s)=\int_0^\infty h(v)v^{s-1}dv$ and $\Sigma h(u)=\sum_{n\ne0}h(nu)$, so that $\int_0^\infty\Sigma h(u)u^{s-1}du=2\zeta(s)\mathcal{M}_Sh(s)$ on $\re s>1$ (Müntz).

\begin{proposition}[the two poles differ in kind]\label{prop:poles}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item $2\zeta(s)\mathcal{M}_Sh(s)=E_h(s)+\hat h(0)/(s-1)-h(0)/s$ with $E_h$ entire: the pole at $0$ is the lattice's $n=0$ term and the pole at $1$ the dual lattice's. For the lattice $c\ZZ$ the residue at $0$ is $-h(0)$ for every $c$, while the residue at $1$ is $(\int h)/c$: the pole at $0$ counts one point and the pole at $1$ measures a density.
\item If $h(v)=\kappa v^\alpha+O(v^{\alpha+1})$ as $v\downarrow0$, then $\mathcal{M}_Sh$ has a pole at $-\alpha$ with residue $\kappa$, and $2\zeta\mathcal{M}_Sh$ has residue $2\zeta(-\alpha)\kappa$ there. For the smooth even model the poles of $\mathcal{M}_Sh$ at $0,-2,-4,\dots$ have residues $h^{(2k)}(0)/(2k)!$, and the trivial zeros cancel all of them except the one at $0$.
\item The moment conditions $h(0)=0$ and $\int h=0$ remove exactly the two poles; the pole at $1$ carries the average (its residue is the density of the integers, and through $-\zeta'/\zeta$ the main term $x$ of $\psi(x)$), and every zero contributes only an oscillation. For $F$ in the exact summation image, the jet of the source at $0$ is read from the trivial zeros: $f_F^{(2r)}(0)/(2r)!=F(-2r)/(2\zeta'(-2r))$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:summation-image}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\status{Proved here (claude-ab, \note{13\_} §§1--2, \note{12\_} §3; register S30 and F$_1$ items 10, 11, 15). The split identity is checked to $10^{-31}$ at four points, with both residues and the cancellation at $-2$. Classical in content (Riemann; Tate); the ledger of which ``$0$'' does what is set out in \note{13\_}.}

\begin{proof}
Split $\int_0^\infty$ at $u=1$ and use Poisson summation $\Sigma h(u)=u^{-1}\Sigma\hat h(u^{-1})+u^{-1}\hat h(0)-h(0)$ on $(0,1)$: the terms $u^{-1}\hat h(0)$ and $-h(0)$ integrate to $\hat h(0)/(s-1)-h(0)/s$, and the rest is entire. For $c\ZZ$ the $n=0$ term is still $h(0)$, while $\widehat{h(c\,\cdot)}(0)=\hat h(0)/c$. (b) $\int_0^1\kappa v^{\alpha+s-1}dv=\kappa/(s+\alpha)$. (c) $\zeta(s)=s\int_1^\infty\lfloor u\rfloor u^{-s-1}du$ has residue $1$ at $s=1$.
\end{proof}

In the programme the object at that point is $\tau$. A ``pole at $\tau$'' would be computed from a local model of the test functions at $\tau$ by (b), and the programme has not specified that model; the reading of the residue at $0$ as ``presence without an exchanged label'' ($0=-0$ is the only non-free orbit of $\pm1$) is a proposed typed map, not an identification with $\tau$, since the integer $0$ has even parity (\note{13\_} §4). The author's own placement is that $\tau$ is the pivot of the swing between $0$ and $1$; on the zeta side that is the reflection $s\mapsto1-\bar s$ about $\tfrac12$ (or $u\mapsto1/u$ about $u=1$), and there RH reads $\rho^\#=\rho$ for every zero. Centring at $0$ and centring at the pivot differ by exactly one shift, $s^\#=T_1(A(s))$, and only the $\tfrac12$-centred pairing is real (Proposition~\ref{prop:centres}).

\subsection{Clocks and Chebyshev's function}

\begin{proposition}[the clock stack is $\psi$; the completed prime clock is $\widehat{\ZZ}$]\label{prop:clocks}
The joint state set of the clocks $\ZZ/1,\dots,\ZZ/N$ is $\ZZ/L_N$ with $L_N=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\dots,N)$, and $\log L_N=\psi(N)$; so $-\zeta'/\zeta(s)=\sum_{N\ge2}(\log L_N-\log L_{N-1})N^{-s}$ on $\re s>1$. For every integer $a\ge2$ (for example a prime $p$), the closure of $a^{\ZZ}$ in $\prod_{\ell\nmid a}\ZZ_\ell^\times$ is a copy of $\widehat{\ZZ}$.
\end{proposition}
\status{Proved in the programme (\lbl{PMS M3--M6}), for a prime; checked exactly for $N\le20000$ and $d\le30$ (\note{15\_}; register S32). Classical in content; the clock-stack reading is the programme's. Refereed (fourth pass). The form for every integer $a\ge2$ is version~2's (\note{48\_} §3, F22; the proof is unchanged).}

\begin{proof}
By the Chinese remainder theorem the image of $\ZZ\to\prod_{k\le N}\ZZ/k$ is $\ZZ/L_N$. $v_p(L_N)=\max\{a:p^a\le N\}$, so $L_N/L_{N-1}=p$ if $N=p^a$ and $1$ otherwise, and $\log L_N=\sum_{p^a\le N}\log p=\psi(N)$. The number $a^d-1$ is prime to $a$, and since $0<a^k-1<a^d-1$ for $1\le k<d$, $a$ has exact order $d$ modulo $a^d-1$; so $n\mapsto a^n$ induces injections $\ZZ/d\hookrightarrow(\ZZ/(a^d-1))^\times$ for every $d$. The continuous extension $\widehat{\ZZ}\to\prod_{\ell\nmid a}\ZZ_\ell^\times$ is therefore injective, and its image is compact and contains $a^{\ZZ}$ as a dense subset, so it is an isomorphism of $\widehat{\ZZ}$ onto the closure.
\end{proof}

Classically (von Koch 1901), RH is equivalent to $\log L_N=N+O(\sqrt N\log^2N)$; this is not a consequence of the clocks.
In the programme's generic monoid $A_\eta=\{0\}\cup\{J^rT^m\}$ the fibre over winding $m=0$ is $\{\tau,J,\varepsilon,J^3\}$, which is present, while absence is the absorbing $0$ (\lbl{PMS M1}): ``has not moved'' is not ``absent''.

\subsection{Connes--Consani's signed winding group}

On the archimedean local data of Connes--Consani's absolute twistor line \cite{CCtwistor}, the unit group is $G=\langle T,J\mid[T,J]=1,\ J^4=1\rangle\cong\ZZ\times\ZZ/4$ with sign $\varepsilon=J^2$ and mirror $A(T^mJ^k)=T^{-m}J^{2m-k}$. The programme proves (and the audit checked; register S8, S9):
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{parity is derived from the mirror}: $g\mapsto A(g)g$ equals $\varepsilon^m$ on $g=\varepsilon^aT^mJ^b$, a homomorphism onto $\{1,\varepsilon\}$ with kernel $2L$ on the winding group $L=G/\langle J\rangle$; no group section of $G\to L$ is equivariant (\lbl{W2});
\item \emph{the sign-preserving, mirror-commuting endomorphisms} of $G$ are exactly $T\mapsto T^nJ^c$, $J\mapsto J^b$ with $n$ odd, $c\in\ZZ/4$, $b\in\{1,3\}$; for even $n$ the two sides differ by exactly $\varepsilon$, so doubling has no sign-preserving mirror-compatible lift although $(2)$ is a prime of $\operatorname{Spec}\operatorname{End}(L)\cong\operatorname{Spec}\ZZ$ (a lift that kills the sign, $T\mapsto T^2$, $J\mapsto1$, does commute with the mirror) (\lbl{W5}; exhaustive check for $|n|\le9$).
\end{itemize}
The copy identified the weight-one sign sector over the $\mathbb{F}_{1^2}$-points: the double cover of $\mathbb{P}^1$ branched at $\{0,\infty,\pm1\}$ is $y^2=x^3-x$, $J$ acts as complex multiplication by $i$, and the Hecke character on rational ideals is $\psi((n))=\chi_{-4}(n)n$, which equals Connes--Consani's signed winding degree; and $L(\operatorname{Sym}^2E,s)=L(\psi^2,s)L(s-1,\chi_{-4})$ (register S10--S11; classical ingredients). Its $L$-function has conductor $32$, so this sector is not $\zeta$'s (register negative result 11).
On $\ZZ[\mathbb{Q}/\ZZ]$ the programme's signed clocks give the integral Bost--Connes relations $\sigma_n\rho_n=n$, $\rho_n\sigma_n=E_n\cdot$, $\rho_n(a)\rho_n(b)=n\rho_n(ab)$, $\sigma_m\rho_n=d\rho_{n/d}\sigma_{m/d}$ ($d=\gcd(m,n)$), with $\ker\sigma_n=(e(1/n)-1)R$ and $\operatorname{im}\rho_n=E_nR$ mutual annihilators and $R/(\ker\sigma_n+E_nR)\cong R/nR$ (at a finite level $N$, of index $n^{N/n}$, claude-ab's addition); even-degree transfer is never a fibre map over a sign-faithful character (\lbl{CCT-1--CCT-8}; \note{28\_} §5.2; S54).

\subsection{Two primes, and lifting}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{At most two primes have $p^\rho$ algebraic}, for every nonreal $\rho$; hence the dilation eigenvalues $p^\rho$ are not all eigenvalues of finite-rank integral endomorphisms (\lbl{IH5.3}; the six exponentials theorem of Lang and Ramachandra; S14).
\item \emph{Two primes suffice to see every zero}: the values $F(\omega)$ at the zeros are determined by the grid $\sum_\omega m_\omega F(\omega)e^{-\omega(a\log2+b\log3)}$, $(a,b)\in\ZZ^2$ (\lbl{IH6}; S15). Relatedly, if $h/\ell$ is irrational, a distribution $V$ on $\R$ with $(E_h-1)^rV=0=(E_\ell-1)^sV$ is a polynomial of degree $<\min(r,s)$; so simultaneous generalised $p^\rho$- and $q^\rho$-eigenfunctionals are exactly the Mellin jets, while one prime alone has the aliases $\rho+2\pi ik/\log p$ (\lbl{MCL3.4}; S17; for continuous $V$ a case of L.~Schwartz's mean-periodic spectral synthesis \cite{SchwartzMP}).
\item \emph{Least-order lifting.} At a zero of multiplicity $m$, the $k$-th source Mellin jet lifts through the transposed summation map with least generalised-character order $m+k+1$, by an explicit formula; for a fixed annihilator $(X-b)^r$ with $b=1-\rho$, the obstruction class on the $j$-th source jet, read through the residue pairing, is $(-1)^jj!/2\cdot t^{r-1-j}\chi_\zeta(b+t)\bmod t^{\min(m,r)}$, the germ at $b$ of the functional-equation factor $\chi_\zeta$ ($\zeta(s)=\chi_\zeta(s)\zeta(1-s)$), and the connecting map has rank $\min(m,r)$ (\lbl{MCL5--MCL9}, \lbl{ORE4--ORE5}; \note{07\_}; verified numerically for $m=1$ and $m=2$ to $10^{-39}$; S18). The order depends on the multiplicity, not on the position of the zero.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Winding and ramification at the zeros}

\begin{lemma}[the winding at the zeros is hyperbolic]\label{lem:winding}
For every nontrivial zero $\rho=\sigma+i\gamma$ put $\lambda_\rho=e^{-2\pi i\rho}$. Then $|\lambda_\rho|=e^{2\pi\gamma}$, $\lambda_\rho$ is a negative real number if and only if $\sigma=\tfrac12$, and $|1/(\lambda_\rho-1)+1_{\gamma<0}|\le1/(e^{2\pi|\gamma|}-1)<2.7\cdot10^{-39}$.
\end{lemma}
\status{Proved here (claude-ab, \note{33\_} Lemma 33.1; S64). Refereed (eleventh pass).}

\begin{proof}
$|e^{-2\pi i(\sigma+i\gamma)}|=e^{2\pi\gamma}$ and $\arg\lambda_\rho=-2\pi\sigma$. For $\gamma>0$, $|1/(\lambda_\rho-1)|\le1/(e^{2\pi\gamma}-1)$; for $\gamma<0$, $1/(\lambda_\rho-1)+1=\lambda_\rho/(\lambda_\rho-1)$ has modulus at most $e^{2\pi\gamma}/(1-e^{2\pi\gamma})$. No zero has $|\gamma|<\gamma_1=14.1347\ldots$, and $e^{2\pi\gamma_1}\approx3.7\cdot10^{38}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[escape under ramification]\label{lem:escape}
Let $Z$ be any set of points with $0<\re\rho<1$ and let $a>1$ be real. Then $\{k\ge0:a^k\rho\in Z\}\subset\{k:a^k\re\rho<1\}$ is finite, no nonempty $S\subset Z$ has $aS\subset S$, and only points with $\re\rho<1/a$ can return. If $a\ge2$ and $Z=1-\bar Z$, no reflected pair returns together; for $1<a<2$ a pair with $1-1/a<\re\rho<1/a$ can return. Two points collide, $a\rho=a'\rho'$ with $\rho\ne\rho'$ and $a,a'>1$, only if $\rho/\rho'=a'/a>0$ (rational when $a$ and $a'$ are integers), which is impossible for distinct points of the critical line.
\end{lemma}
\status{Proved here (claude-ab, \note{33\_} Lemma 33.4, for integers $a\ge2$; S65); the form for real $a>1$ is version~2's (\note{48\_} §3, F22, where an independent verification corrected the review's wording of the reflected-pair and collision clauses). The programme's iterated- and reflected-return theorems (\lbl{BRC}, \lbl{GJN}, \lbl{GJNR}) therefore hold for every divisor in the open strip with their other hypotheses; they are not evidence about the zeros (Section~\ref{sec:negative}).}

\begin{proof}
$\re(a^k\rho)=a^k\re\rho$, which must stay below $1$. If $aS\subset S$ then $a^kS\subset S$ for all $k$, impossible. $\re\rho<1/a$ and $1-\re\rho<1/a$ cannot both hold for $a\ge2$; for $a=\tfrac32$ and $\rho=0.55+i$, the set $\{\rho,\rho^\#,a\rho,a\rho^\#,(a\rho)^\#,(a\rho^\#)^\#\}$ lies in the strip, is $\#$-closed, and contains the returns of both $\rho$ and $\rho^\#$. $(\tfrac12+i\gamma)/(\tfrac12+i\gamma')$ is real only if $\gamma=\gamma'$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Every failure of RH is detectable}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:detect}
Let $T$ be a recursively axiomatised theory that proves every true $\Sigma_1$ sentence and proves ``$M$ halts $\leftrightarrow\neg$RH'' for an explicit Turing machine $M$ (the $744$-state machine of Matiyasevich and O'Rear reported by Aaronson \cite{Aaronson}, Theorem~9, or any machine searching for a counterexample to a $\Pi_1$ form of RH \cite{DMR}; ZFC is an example). If RH is false, $T\vdash\neg$RH; so if RH is independent of $T$, it is true. If $T$ is moreover $\Sigma_1$-sound, then RH $\iff T\nvdash\neg$RH $\iff\operatorname{Con}(T+\text{RH})$.
\end{proposition}
\status{Proved here (claude-ab from the author's argument, \note{23\_} Proposition 23.1; register S42). Standard logic; no novelty claimed. Refereed (eighteenth pass).}

\begin{proof}
If RH is false, $M$ halts; ``$M$ halts'' is a true $\Sigma_1$ sentence, so $T$ proves it, and with $T\vdash(M\text{ halts}\to\neg\text{RH})$, $T\vdash\neg$RH. If RH is true and $T\vdash\neg$RH, then $T\vdash$``$M$ halts'', a false $\Sigma_1$ sentence, against $\Sigma_1$-soundness.
\end{proof}

So the author's argument is valid in this exact form: ``no detectable failure'' (that is, $T+$RH consistent) implies RH, and RH cannot fail undetectably, since every failure has a finite certificate (the halting computation, or an interval-arithmetic argument-principle computation around an off-line zero). It gives no shortcut: for $\Sigma_1$-sound $T$, ``no detectable failure'' is equivalent to RH, and proving $\operatorname{Con}(T+\text{RH})$ inside $T$ is impossible by Gödel's second incompleteness theorem if $T$ is consistent.

\subsection{Cohomology over the three-point base}

On Connes--Consani's three-point base $Y=\{c_+,c_-,\eta\}$ the programme builds a residue pairing, its diagonal return, a doubled source $X^{\mathrm{dbl}}=\operatorname{Spec}\ZZ\cup\{\mathfrak m_+,\mathfrak m_-\}$ and the pullback to it. The audited statements (register S36, S61, S70) are these.
\begin{itemize}
\item The right-adjoint dualizing complex on $Y$ is $j_!k[1]$, so the degree-two residue pairing $B_\zeta$ cannot be a degree-zero sheaf map on $Y$; it is the trace of a sheaf map on $Y\times Y$, and the diagonal's right adjoint $R\Delta^!=Rq_*$ keeps the trace with coefficient $+1$, while ordinary pullback loses a degree. Paired characters multiply to $p$, with weights $2\re\rho$ and $2-2\re\rho$ (\lbl{FTD}, \lbl{PRS}, \lbl{DER}, \lbl{FSC}, \lbl{FEM}; \note{19\_}).
\item For $f:X^{\mathrm{dbl}}\to Y$ and every sheaf $\Omega$ on $Y$: $f_*f^{-1}\Omega=\Omega$ and $R^qf_*f^{-1}\Omega=0$ for $q>0$, so $R\Gamma(X^{\mathrm{dbl}},f^{-1}\Omega)\cong R\Gamma(Y,\Omega)$, with supports as well (\lbl{DCP5}; for every $\Omega$ and with supports, claude-ab, \note{39\_} Lemma 39.3). In the trivial finite-unit sector for $\mathbb{Q}$, the programme's cohomology is exactly $H^*(\mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{F}_1},\Omega)$ of Connes--Consani \cite{CCP1}, whose $H^1$ is the spectral realisation of the zeros.
\item The programme's residue extension $0\to\B\to E\to\C_\chi\to0$, pushed out along evaluation at $s_0$, splits for $s_0\ne0$; at $s_0=0$ it is $n\mapsto n(I-\log n\cdot N)$, with class $-\log$ in $\operatorname{Ext}^1(\C_\chi,\C_\chi)=\operatorname{Hom}((\NN_{\ge1},\times),\C)$, and its tensor Euler functions are those of the split sum: the pole at $s=0$ carries the Jordan block that Euler products miss (\lbl{NPE4}; \note{31\_} Lemma 31.2).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Results from the other workbenches used here}

\begin{lemma}[Keller maps give incompressible polynomial flows]\label{lem:keller}
Let $F:\R^n\to\R^n$ be polynomial with $\det DF\equiv c\ne0$, $V$ a $C^1$ vector field and $U=DF^{-1}(V\circ F)$. Then $U$ is $C^1$ with $\operatorname{div}U=(\operatorname{div}V)\circ F$, it is polynomial if $V$ is, and $F$ maps integral curves of $U$ to integral curves of $V$; if $F$ is a polynomial automorphism, $U$ is complete if and only if $V$ is (for example, $V$ affine).
For the map
\[
F=\big((1+xy)^3w+y^2(1+xy)(4+3xy),\ y+3x(1+xy)^2w+3xy^2(4+3xy),\ 2x-3x^2y-x^3w\big),
\]
which has $\det DF=-2$ and three preimages of $(-\tfrac14,0,0)$, namely $(0,0,-\tfrac14)$ and $(\pm1,\mp\tfrac32,\tfrac{13}2)$, the curve $\gamma(\tau)=(z^{-1},-3z/2,13z^2/2)$, $z=\sqrt{1-8\tau}$, is an integral curve of $U$ for $V=(2,0,0)$, escapes every compact set as $\tau\uparrow1/8$, and has $F(\gamma(\tau))=(-\tfrac14+2\tau,0,0)$.
\end{lemma}
\status{Proved here (claude-ab, \note{28\_} Lemma 28.1, for affine $V$; S52); the $C^1$ form and the equivalence of completeness are version~2's (\note{48\_} §3, F22; checked exactly at six rational points with a cubic $V$); the determinant and the three-point fibre re-verified symbolically for this reader. The map is recorded in the author's Yang--Mills workbench as the counterexample to the Jacobian conjecture announced by Levent Alpöge, whose announcement, as the workbench's attribution file records, credits Akhil for the question and Fable for the work; the curve is the one in that workbench. Novelty not searched.}

\begin{proof}
$DF^{-1}=\operatorname{adj}(DF)/c$ is polynomial. By the Piola identity $\sum_j\partial_j\operatorname{adj}(DF)_{ji}=0$ and the chain rule, $c\operatorname{div}U=\sum_{i,j,k}\operatorname{adj}(DF)_{ji}(\partial_kV_i)(F)\partial_jF_k=c(\operatorname{div}V)(F)$, since $\sum_j\partial_jF_k\operatorname{adj}(DF)_{ji}=c\delta_{ki}$. $\frac d{d\tau}F(\gamma)=DF(\gamma)\gamma'=V(F(\gamma))$. For an automorphism with inverse $G$, $\Phi^U_\tau=G\circ\Phi^V_\tau\circ F$, so $U$ is complete exactly when $V$ is; affine flows are complete. On the displayed curve $xy=-\tfrac32$, and substitution gives the three components of $F(\gamma)$.
\end{proof}

Two further exact statements from the other workbenches enter the bridges of Section~\ref{sec:bridges}: the exact aliasing of the Navier--Stokes workbench's torus endomorphism on finite character groups (register S40) and the Gram sandwich $(1-\delta)^2\Phi^*\Phi\preceq\Phi_J^*\Phi_J\preceq(1+\delta)^2\Phi^*\Phi$ whenever $\|(\Phi_J-\Phi)x\|\le\delta\|\Phi x\|$ for all $x$ with $\delta\le1$ (the triangle inequality; the hypothesis $\delta\le1$ is needed for the lower bound; register S41), of which the zeta programme's Schur-complement replacements and the Yang--Mills heat semigroup are instances.
