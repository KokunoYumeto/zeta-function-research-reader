\section{How the audit was verified}\label{app:wverification}

\paragraph{First-pass audits.} One Claude subagent per workbench (four in all) read the workbench read-only at the commits listed in the front matter, restated each result with its hypotheses, followed the proofs step by step and wrote independent checks, importing no workbench code; the Collatz and Yang--Mills first passes also re-ran the workbenches' own checkers, while the Erdős--Straus and 817 first passes wrote independent checks instead. Their reports are reproduced unchanged as \note{43a\_}--\note{46a\_}, and an inventory of the Erdős--Straus, Yang--Mills, 817 and zeta repositories (made before the audits; the Collatz repository was cloned later, for its audit) as \note{47a\_}.

\paragraph{Re-runs.} The first-pass Python scripts were re-run on 26 September by the author of this reader, except one 168-second script; the outputs are identical apart from timing lines. The first-pass C searches for $g_4$ were not re-run by the author; a separate search of the author's and, later, one of the referee's replace them for $n\le6$, and the referee re-ran one of them. The Yang--Mills first pass had re-run the workbench's fifth-order producer (662 rows, 51 seconds) and its polynomial and dual-certificate auditor (20 seconds): both outputs are byte-identical to the published ones.

\paragraph{Independent checks.} The script \note{checks/workbenches/workbench\_reader\_claims\_checks.py} (47 items, all pass, about 7 seconds) tests the statements proved in this reader from their definitions alone: the shell count against brute force and the two sieves (Section~\ref{sec:es}); the $\nu_3$ lemma, the merging families (600 pairs), the one-step cycles, the convergence of all odd $n\le330{,}749$, the least $m=1636$ and $k\ge679$, and the model inequality (Section~\ref{sec:collatz}); the digit set, the lift, $\kappa_m$, the certificates $B^*$ and $A^\sharp$ and the composition identity (Section~\ref{sec:ep817}); $\alpha_5$, the threshold and $d_5$ by root isolation, the Casimir lemma on the cube, the face count, Proposition~\ref{prop:s65}, the Kasner identities and the map $F$ (Section~\ref{sec:ym}). A third exhaustive search for $g_4$ (\note{g4\_bitset.c}) found $g_4(6)=79$ in 30 seconds. The generalisations of this reader are checked in \note{checks/workbenches/generality\_checks.py} (items G1--G30, all pass, about 21 seconds): Theorem~\ref{thm:shell} for all odd primes below $260$, for $a$ up to $3p$, and its range $x<p/2$ on all solutions at primes below $400$; Proposition~\ref{prop:r7} for every prime $p\equiv1\pmod4$ below $3\cdot10^4$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:r3} with its corrected form at $p\equiv5\pmod{12}$; Propositions~\ref{prop:survivors} and~\ref{prop:mod840}; a Pocklington proof of the $22$-digit prime of item~3; Proposition~\ref{prop:cyl} and the bounds of Theorem~\ref{thm:threshold}(c) against the exact $\Delta$; Proposition~\ref{prop:ref3} and its converse; the jet fibres of Theorem~\ref{thm:encoding}; the Eliahou-type bounds of Corollary~\ref{cor:barina} (which return Eliahou's $17{,}087{,}915$ at $2^{40}$), the bounds from the verification to $2^{33}$ (\note{descent\_2e33.c}, 38 seconds) and the arithmetic of the published bound after it; Proposition~\ref{prop:lift} and the digit sets modulo $19$; $\Lambda_3=3$ and the injectivity of ternary sums for $3$-admissible sets; the $M_n$ lower bound; Lemma~\ref{lem:casimir} by exhaustive spin search and by linear programming; Corollary~\ref{cor:ym-orderN}; the radii of Proposition~\ref{prop:neumann}; and Proposition~\ref{prop:s65} on $\ZZ^4$.

\paragraph{Referee passes.} The notes \note{43\_}--\note{47\_} were refereed by a Claude instance that had written none of them (the twentieth pass of this audit). It re-derived every proof the notes present as complete and found no error in them; it reported 14 major findings (a hypothesis missing from the $S^6$ covers of Erdős--Straus, a false summary line, status labels stronger than the evidence, an incomplete credit, a false ``only place'' statement, an infimum stated as attained, and two intersections left out) and 56 minor ones. All were checked against the sources and applied before this reader was written.

This reader then had a referee pass of its own, by a further Claude instance that had written neither the notes nor the reader. It checked every written-out proof and found no error, recomputed the numbers with its own programs (among them a fourth exhaustive search for $g_4(n)$, $n\le6$, and a lex Gröbner basis showing that the fibre of $F$ over $(-\frac14,0,0)$ is exactly the three stated points), and spot-checked at least four statements per section against the repositories. It reported 8 major findings: an external claim about $S^6$ stated without attribution; one status label (NS-04(a)); three statements about how the audit was done; a sentence promising this paragraph; two scope conditions dropped in the intersections table (edges 4 and 11); one overstatement in §\ref{sec:intersections}'s patterns; and an incomplete Erdős--Straus credit line. It also reported 52 minor ones (definitions, labels, locators, precision and typography, including the observation that Proposition~\ref{prop:ref3} does not use one of the workbench's hypotheses). All were checked against the sources and applied.

At the owner's request the reader was then read for understatement and missed generality as well as for overstatement: first by its author, and then by a further Claude instance that had written none of it (the twenty-second pass). That pass reported 7 major findings: the gap theorem holds with audited inputs at truncation order $2$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:ym-orderN}); Corollary~\ref{cor:barina} needs only Barina's 2021 range; the least denominator of an Erdős--Straus solution is below $p/2$; the Casimir constant is the girth; the two-shell survivors and the reduction modulo $840$ are theorems (Propositions~\ref{prop:survivors} and~\ref{prop:mod840}); and the $k=4$ construction lifts any admissible top level (Proposition~\ref{prop:lift}). It also reported 11 minor ones, among them the converse in Proposition~\ref{prop:ref3}, the rates in Theorem~\ref{thm:threshold}(c), $\Lambda_3=3$, the physical-space radius of Proposition~\ref{prop:neumann} and a primality certificate, and two overstatements: the Neumann radii were compared across the full and the physical space, and YM-05 was said to depend on order $5$. Each stronger statement was re-derived by the author of this reader and checked (G6--G30 above) before it was applied; the Barina 2021 range was checked against the 2025 paper and the project page. The results marked \textsf{Generalised here} come from these two readings.

A twenty-third pass, by a further Claude instance, then read the revised reader in both directions. It verified the new proofs and numbers with its own programs, and reported 5 major findings, each checked against the sources before being applied. One stronger statement taken over from the twenty-second pass was false: Proposition~\ref{prop:r3} does not hold as stated at $p\equiv5\pmod{12}$ (at $p=5$, $E_a=\{2\}$ and $M_a=\{1,4\}$), and the check claimed for it had tested only occupancy; the corrected counts are now stated and checked. Four were credit or scope: the published cycle bound of Hercher and Barina is about $1.9$ times Corollary~\ref{cor:barina} and is now stated; Corollary~\ref{cor:ym-orderN}(a) is the workbench's own YM-06 theorem, and YM-06 also has order-2 theorems; the rates in Theorem~\ref{thm:threshold}(c) and the uniformity in $b$ are the workbench's own (4.3)--(4.4); and the $M_n$ form of the linear lower bound is the workbench's (Theorem~\ref{thm:variance}). It also reported 9 minor findings (among them Theorem~\ref{thm:shell}(c) for every odd prime, part (a) of Theorem~\ref{thm:threshold} for every $H\ge0$, a proof of $\Lambda_3=3$ without citation, the per-member descent criterion, and two independent re-runs of the verification to $2^{33}$), all applied.
