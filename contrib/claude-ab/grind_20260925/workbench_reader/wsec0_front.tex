\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This reader sets out what four public workbenches have established, as found in an audit carried out on 25--26 September 2026, and where they meet each other and the split-zero programme around the Riemann zeta function.
The workbenches concern the Erdős--Straus equation $4/p=1/x+1/y+1/z$; the Collatz map; Erdős Problem~817 on subset sums without arithmetic progressions; and the Hamiltonian lattice Yang--Mills theory, together with the material the Yang--Mills workbench holds on the Navier--Stokes equations, on a manuscript that constructs a compact complex threefold which it states is diffeomorphic to $S^6$ (not checked here), and on a counterexample to the Jacobian conjecture.
Sections~\ref{sec:es}--\ref{sec:ym} state each workbench's results; the central ones are proved in full here, several of them in greater generality than the workbench states, and the others are listed with their status or by a pointer to the first-pass reports.
Each section ends with the negative results that were checked, each within the exact scope of what fails.
Section~\ref{sec:intersections} states every intersection found between the workbenches, and with the zeta programme, as a typed map with its status, and records three patterns that recur.
Nothing in this reader proves or disproves the Erdős--Straus conjecture or the Collatz conjecture, or establishes or refutes a mass gap for the continuum Yang--Mills theory, and none of the workbenches claims to.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{How the audit was done.}
For each workbench a first-pass audit was made by a Claude subagent: it restated each result with its hypotheses, followed the written proof step by step and wrote independent checks; the Collatz and Yang--Mills first passes also re-ran the workbenches' own checkers.
The author of this reader then re-ran the first-pass Python scripts except one 168-second script (identical outputs apart from timing lines; the first-pass C searches were not re-run, and a separate search of the author's replaces them for small $n$), wrote a further independent check script from the definitions alone, re-derived the proofs given here, looked for statements that hold more generally than stated, and wrote five audit notes (\note{43\_}--\note{47\_}).
Claude instances that had written none of this refereed the notes, then this reader, then this reader twice more for understatement and missed generality as well as for overstatement. None found an error in a written-out proof; the last pass found one false statement of generality, three results of a workbench that had been credited to the audit, and a published cycle bound stronger than the one derived here; all are corrected. Every finding (Appendix~\ref{app:wverification}) was checked against the sources before it was applied.
The notes, the first-pass reports, the scripts and their outputs are on the branch \href{https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/zeta-function-research-reader/tree/claude/claude-ab-grind-20260925/contrib/claude-ab/grind_20260925}{\nolinkurl{claude/claude-ab-grind-20260925}} of the zeta repository.

\paragraph{Who did what.}
The results are the workbenches' and the contributors' they credit; each section opens with the workbench's own credit line.
The audit's own contributions are the re-derivations, the checks, and the generalisations marked \textsf{Generalised here}. Among these are the range $x<p/2$ of the Erdős--Straus shell criterion, extended from the workbench's primes $p\equiv1\pmod{12}$ to every odd prime; the order-2 gap theorem with the threshold lowered to $g^2\ge4.0187$ using audited inputs only (the workbench itself has the order-1 case and order-2 thresholds $4.1156$ and, with a further computed input, $3.8260$); the girth form of the Casimir inequality; and the lifting of the $k=4$ construction. They also include the proofs of the two-shell survivor classes and of the reduction modulo $840$; the Eliahou-type cycle bounds from a verification to $2^{33}$ made here and from Barina's 2021 range, which are weaker than the published bound of Hercher and Barina, also stated (Section~\ref{sec:collatz}); a Pocklington certificate for an Erdős--Straus prime; the exact small values of $g_4$ (Section~\ref{sec:ep817}); and the statement of the intersections (Section~\ref{sec:intersections}). No novelty is claimed for any of them.

\paragraph{Status labels.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textsf{Proved here}: the proof is given in full in this reader and was checked.
\item \textsf{Generalised here}: a workbench result stated and proved here under weaker hypotheses than the workbench states.
\item \textsf{Audited}: the proof is in the workbench; it was read in full, every step was checked, and independent checks were run; it is not reproduced here.
\item \textsf{Audited (certificate)}: the finite certificate on which the proof rests was recomputed independently, and the deduction from it was read.
\item \textsf{Audited in part}: the parts named were checked; the rest is located.
\item \textsf{Conditional}: audited or proved here, given an input that was not re-derived; the input is named.
\item \textsf{Computed}: established by an exhaustive computer search (a computer proof); the programs are named and were run independently of each other.
\item \textsf{Located}: the statement and its proof file were found and described; the proof was not checked.
\item \textsf{Checked numerically}: supported by computation only; never presented as proved.
\end{itemize}
Novelty is reported only as recorded: no literature search for novelty was made except where a search is described.
