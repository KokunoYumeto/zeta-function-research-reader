\section{Where the workbenches meet}\label{sec:intersections}

This section states each intersection found between the workbenches, and with the split-zero zeta programme, as a typed map: its domain and codomain, what crosses and what does not, and its status. The types are: \textsf{P}, a proved map or lemma used on both sides; \textsf{I}, the same identity or object on both sides; \textsf{D}, one side imports the other's theorem as an unproved dependency; \textsf{R}, use as a regression test only; \textsf{A}, an analogy or proposal; \textsf{M}, methodology only. Audit note: \note{47\_}.

\subsection{The substantive intersections}

\subsubsection{zeta \texorpdfstring{$\to$}{to} Collatz: the Hurwitz jets store moments (edge 1)}\label{ssec:zeta-cz}
Fix the $K$ odd-return words with residues $r_w$ and a nontrivial zero $\rho$ of $\zeta$ (§\ref{ssec:hurwitz-encoding}). The map from probability vectors $\lambda$ to the order-$J$ jet of the zero cluster of $\sum_w\lambda_w\zeta(s,1+tr_w)$ near $\rho$ factors as
\[
\lambda\ \longmapsto\ (M_1,\dots,M_J),\ M_i=\textstyle\sum_w\lambda_wr_w^i\ \longmapsto\ (c_1,\dots,c_J),
\]
a moment map depending only on the residues, followed by a triangular polynomial bijection whose coefficients are built from the derivatives at $\rho$ of $\zeta$ and of the jet coefficients $b_j$ (the diagonal coefficients being $\zeta'(\rho)$ and $b_J(\rho)$). The first factor is injective for $J=K-1$ and not for $J=K-2$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:encoding}). From zeta cross the reconstruction theorem and its ingredients (the jets $\partial_t^j\zeta(s,1+t)|_{t=0}=(-1)^j(s)_j\zeta(s+j)$, also the zeta reader's jet theorem, and $\zeta\ne0$ on $\re s\ge1$); from Collatz only the residues. Nothing about the location of $\rho$ and nothing about orbits crosses. The zeta reader studies the same flow $t\mapsto\zeta(s,1+t)$ at $t=0$ through its jets (the first jet vanishes in $-1<\re s<0$ exactly on the shifted zeros, and the flow does not translate the zero set); here the motion of one zero, taken at several shifts at once and averaged with weights, serves as storage. The Collatz workbench is the only one besides zeta found to use this flow.

\subsubsection{NS \texorpdfstring{$\to$}{to} ES: an operator, not a fluid theorem (edge 2)}
The auxiliary torus endomorphism $J$ of the NS manuscript (determinant $14$, eigenvalues $4\pm\sqrt2$) enters 21 Erdős--Straus statements as an operator only. Their content is the identity $\{(u,v)\in G^2:u^3v=1=uv^5\}=\{(u,u^{-3}):u^{14}=1\}$ in a finite abelian group $G$ (in the application the unit group modulo $R_a$), which gives exact aliasing on finite character groups, and the sharp constant $\inf_{k\ne0}|v\cdot k|\,\|k\|=1/\sqrt{4+2\sqrt2}$ for the unnormalised eigen-directions $v_r=(1,1-\sqrt2)$, $v_t=(\sqrt2-1,1)$: the inequality is strict for every $k\ne0$, and the infimum is approached along Pell vectors. No NS existence or blow-up theorem is used.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{$S^6$}{S6} \texorpdfstring{$\to$}{to} ES and \texorpdfstring{$S^6$}{S6} \texorpdfstring{$\to$}{to} YM: two uses of one period system (edges 3, 4)}
Erdős--Straus uses the integral monodromies of the $(3,4,\infty)$ period system to build affine-torsion covers of its divisor atlas (§\ref{ssec:es-s6}); Yang--Mills uses the imaginary period block $B$ of the period matrix as a magnetic background, and on the non-wrapping patch used by the workbench only $\det B$ enters. Neither uses the global $S^6$ statement; both use displayed matrices.

\subsubsection{The name ``Fable'': one name, three objects (edges 5--7)}
Fable is credited in Alpöge's announcement, as the workbench records it, for the work that produced the map $F$. In the repositories the name refers to three different objects: the three-dimensional Keller map $F$ of the Jacobian-conjecture counterexample; the $S^6$ construction (in older files); and the four-dimensional Keller map $P$ of the Erdős--Straus crosswalk. $F$ enters Yang--Mills through incompressible flows and ends in the free two-gluon threshold (§\ref{sec:ym}); it enters zeta through constructions written within zeta (located); $P$ has $\det DP=-2$, and its fibres, monodromy and conductor are located.

\subsubsection{zeta \texorpdfstring{$\to$}{to} YM: linear algebra, not arithmetic (edges 8--10)}
The transfers state that no map between arithmetic zeta objects and gauge fields is asserted. What crosses is the Gram sandwich $(1-\delta)^2\Phi^*\Phi\preceq\Phi_J^*\Phi_J\preceq(1+\delta)^2\Phi^*\Phi$ when $\|(\Phi_J-\Phi)x\|\le\delta\|\Phi x\|$ for all $x$ and $\delta\le1$ (the zeta reader, §6; the zeta instance takes $\delta$ from a dilation factor, the Yang--Mills instance from $e^{-Ta_0}$ for a heat semigroup); a left-inverse observability pattern; and standard Schur-complement identities. None carries an arithmetic constant into a Yang--Mills bound, and none enters Theorem~\ref{thm:ym01}.

\subsubsection{NS \texorpdfstring{$\to$}{to} YM (edge 11)}
A divergence-free velocity $u$ becomes the reducible connection $A_i=\lambda u_iT$, with $-2\sum_{i<j}\tr F_{ij}^2=\lambda^2|\operatorname{curl}u|^2$. Given the Yang--Mills inputs that the audit did not read (a free norm formula, an energy estimate and a convergence theorem), the resulting zero-quotient bound holds for every admissible curvature weight, of which the NS profile is one; the diagonal sequence exists only with coupling tending to $0$, and at fixed coupling nothing follows (\note{21\_} §2).

\subsubsection{Erdős 817 \texorpdfstring{$\to$}{to} zeta (edge 16)}
Ten interface notes restate 817's finite constructions in the zeta programme's supported-quotient vocabulary. The one audited (the finite-period receiving map) is a correct, elementary restatement: free vector spaces on coefficient words, the evaluation quotient, the cut map $e_{(x,y)}\mapsto e_{x+Q(u)y}$ with $\dim\ker=F(u)F(v)-F(uv)$, and $\kappa(u,v)\kappa(uv,w)=\kappa(v,w)\kappa(u,vw)$ for $\kappa(u,v)=F(u)F(v)/F(uv)$, both sides being $F(u)F(v)F(w)/F(uvw)$. Nothing flows back into the 817 proofs, and nothing is claimed about $\zeta$.

\subsection{Shared identities}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Erdős--Straus and Collatz} (edge 17). $3(4n+1)+1=4(3n+1)$, so $n$ and $4n+1$ have the same odd successor. It is the workbench result R05 of Erdős--Straus (which credits the underlying diagrams to u/CivQ17) and the meeting-point step of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:merge}. No recorded dependency either way.
\item \emph{Collatz, Erdős--Straus and zeta} (edge 18). The homogenisation $(x,1)\mapsto(x,h)$, $(x,h)\mapsto(ax+bh,h)$ makes affine branch maps commute with support inclusions. The zeta foundation cites it from the Clankers' Zenodo record 19900461; Erdős--Straus proves the diagram (X4); the Collatz note cites a local April 2026 note of the same title and uses its finite branch matrices.
\item \emph{Collatz and Yang--Mills} (edge 19). The constant terms $\frac12+\frac q{32}$ and $\frac18+\frac q{16}+\frac{q^2}{32}$ of two parity words differ by $(q-3)(q+4)/32$ and agree at $q=3$; Yang--Mills uses the identity as a regression fixture only.
\item \emph{Erdős--Straus and zeta} (edges 20, 21). In $G(R)=R\sqcup\{\tau\}$, $\{\tau\}$ is a prime ideal and $\{\tau,0_R\}$ is prime iff $R$ is a domain; the finite Weyl operators $T_aM_\chi$ form an orthogonal basis of $\operatorname{End}\C[A]$.
\item \emph{Erdős--Straus and $S^6$} (edge 22). Both name the Niemeier lattice with root system $A_5^4D_4$; $|\operatorname{disc}(A_5^4D_4)|=6^4\cdot4=5184$, so the glue index is $72$. The Erdős--Straus glue code (order $72$, isotropic, cost spectrum $\{0:1,4:46,6:25\}$) was checked exhaustively; the $S^6$ side is only on Zenodo, so the two codes were not compared.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Three patterns that recur}

Each pattern is a list of instances, each with the status it has above; nothing is claimed beyond the instances.

\emph{Exact restatements carry no new constraint.} Theorem~\ref{thm:shell} and its re-encoding as sections of covers (§\ref{ssec:es-s6}); the Collatz conjecture as the vanishing of an abelian group, or of $H_0$ (§\ref{sec:collatz}); the Hurwitz encoding (Theorem~\ref{thm:encoding}); velocity data as gravitational constraint data (§\ref{sec:ym}); 817's constructions in the supported-quotient vocabulary. Each is an equivalence or an injective map. An exact restatement moves a difficulty and does not reduce it unless the new side carries a constraint that the old side lacks; none of the instances exhibits such a constraint (for the Hurwitz encoding and the 817 interface there is no open problem being moved, only information being stored). The Collatz, 817 and Navier--Stokes sources say this of their own restatements; for the Erdős--Straus re-encoding the assessment is this audit's.

\emph{Method obstructions.} A method or certificate family is shown unable to establish a property, usually by an explicit instance where the property holds and the method fails: the Erdős--Straus rows of §\ref{sec:es} (every certificate prime is solvable); the global Neumann radius of Proposition~\ref{prop:neumann}, which shrinks with the box on the full and on the physical space and for every contour, whereas Theorem~\ref{thm:ym01} and Corollary~\ref{cor:ym-orderN} obtain, on the physical space and for real $\xi$, a box-uniform gap by local norms, with audited inputs for $g^2\ge4.02$ and conditionally down to $g^2\ge3.68$; 817's modular certificates, which are sufficient but not necessary. The Collatz threshold, Theorem~\ref{thm:threshold}(c), is of a different kind: a limit on what the geometric model describes. The zeta reader's negative results have the same form as the first kind.

\emph{Regimes other than the problem's.} Theorem~\ref{thm:ym01} and Corollary~\ref{cor:ym-orderN} hold at fixed spacing and strong coupling; the low-mode theorem and the Navier--Stokes diagonal at coupling tending to $0$ in growing boxes, where they reproduce free-field values; the regularity theorem for a modified Navier--Stokes equation; the Tao-clock laws, which are statements about first-entry laws in logarithmic density, along one scale sequence. Each workbench states its regime correctly.
