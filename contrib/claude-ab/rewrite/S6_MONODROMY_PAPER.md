# The integral monodromy of the (3,4,∞) period system of the S⁶ construction: an audited record with new results

*Claude (Anthropic, model `claude-opus-5-5`), 27 September 2026. Published at the author's request ahead of the author's review; the author may revise or withdraw it. This record replaces note 52_ (§§1–3) and the corresponding part of the S⁶ reader, which were withdrawn on 27 September 2026.*

## Abstract

The candidate complex structure on S⁶ circulated by Levent Alpöge is built from a family of complex 2-tori over the projective line whose integral period system is a pair of matrices T₁, T₂ ∈ GL₄(ℤ) with T₁³ = T₂⁴ = I, preserving an alternating form Q₀ of type (1,6) and fixing a primitive isotropic vector γ. We determine the monodromy group G = ⟨T₁, T₂⟩ exactly: it is the kernel of an explicit character Φ of order 12 on the stabilizer G_ℤ = SL₂(ℤ) ⋉ Heis(ℤ) of γ, a normal congruence subgroup of exact level 12 with cyclic quotient, arithmetic in its Zariski closure. The Levi half of Φ is precisely the marked peripheral character of the author's higher-torus update, and its Heisenberg half is the multiplier of the odd Jacobi theta function, so the update's character is the invariant that cuts the monodromy group out of its arithmetic hull. We also classify, at every level, the orbits of the dual action that the Erdős–Straus workbench uses to build its (3,4,∞) covers.

## 0. Introduction

### 0.1 Why this record

The S⁶ construction [A] was examined in a validate/repair/disprove record produced with AI systems [R], which the author extended with a higher-torus and cusp update (files 14_ and 15_ of [R]). The integral period system is the part of the construction that can be determined completely by algebra, and it is used elsewhere: the author's higher-torus update attaches a character to its peripheral monodromy, and the Erdős–Straus workbench builds covers of the projective line from its dual action. This record determines the monodromy group exactly and explains how these two uses fit it.

### 0.2 Main results

- **Theorem 3.1.** G = ker Φ for an explicit surjective character Φ: G_ℤ → ℤ/12. So G is normal of index 12 in G_ℤ, with cyclic quotient generated by the central transvection; it is a congruence subgroup of exact level 12; its Levi image is all of SL₂(ℤ), while its intersection with the Levi subgroup is the commutator subgroup; and it is Zariski dense and arithmetic in the algebraic stabilizer of γ.
- **Propositions 4.1–4.2.** The Heisenberg part of G is the subgroup cut out by the odd quadratic refinement of the mod-2 form, equivalently the kernel of the Heisenberg multiplier of the odd Jacobi theta function; G is one of twelve twists of it by the abelianization of SL₂(ℤ), and the extension does not split.
- **Theorem 5.1.** The marked peripheral character χ of the author's update equals −ab, its values (−1, 4, −3) are those of ab on the Levi parts of T₀, T₁, T₂, and on G it is determined by the Heisenberg coordinates: χ(M) ≡ s − 6(p² + pr + r²) (mod 12).
- **Theorem 6.1.** At every level D the orbits of G on the affine hyperplane H_D are classified by gcd(b, c, gcd(D, 6)), and they coincide with the orbits of G_ℤ.

### 0.3 What the results mean

The monodromy group is as large as it can be subject to one constraint, and the constraint is the author's peripheral character: G is exactly the locus where the Levi character and the Heisenberg theta multiplier cancel. So the update's character is not an auxiliary label but the invariant that separates the monodromy from its arithmetic hull. Because G is arithmetic rather than thin, the tools of congruence subgroups apply, and they give the complete orbit classification that the Erdős–Straus covers need. The Heisenberg part links the period system to spin structures and theta characteristics, and the ambient group Sp(Q₀, ℤ) is the paramodular group of level 6 of Gritsenko and Hulek, which suggests the identification of Φ with one of their characters (Question 1).

# Part A. Verified results

## 1. Setting

The period system is taken from the manuscript [A], as reconstructed in file 03_ of the record [R], §2. Let V = ℤ⁴ with basis (γ, u, w, δ). The local monodromies at the two elliptic points are, by formula (2.2) of [A],

- T₁: γ ↦ γ, u ↦ −u − w, w ↦ −6γ + u, δ ↦ 2γ + u + w + δ,
- T₂: γ ↦ γ, u ↦ 6γ + w, w ↦ −u, δ ↦ −3γ + u + δ,

and the monodromy at the cusp is T₀ = (T₁T₂)⁻¹. By Lemma 2.2 of [A], T₁³ = T₂⁴ = I, and T₀ = I + N with N² = 0, Nγ = Nu = 0, Nw = −u, Nδ = γ, so ker N = im N = ⟨γ, u⟩.

By Lemma 2.8 of [A], the group of ⟨T₁, T₂⟩-invariant alternating forms is infinite cyclic, generated by Q₀ with Q₀(γ, δ) = 1 and Q₀(u, w) = 6, all other pairings of basis vectors being zero. Its elementary divisors are (1, 1, 6, 6), so Q₀ has type (1,6). We write G = ⟨T₁, T₂⟩ and

  G_ℤ = { g ∈ Sp(Q₀, ℤ) : gγ = γ }.

Every T_i fixes γ and preserves Q₀, so G ⊆ G_ℤ.

## 2. The stabilizer of γ

For M ∈ SL₂(ℤ), let L_M act by M on the plane ⟨u, w⟩, in the basis (u, w), and fix γ and δ. For p, r, s ∈ ℤ, let h(p, r, s) be the map

  γ ↦ γ,  u ↦ u − 6rγ,  w ↦ w + 6pγ,  δ ↦ δ + pu + rw + sγ.

**Lemma 2.1.** Every element of G_ℤ can be written uniquely as L_M h(p, r, s). The elements h(p, r, s) form a group Heis(ℤ) with the law

  h(p₁, r₁, s₁) h(p₂, r₂, s₂) = h(p₁ + p₂, r₁ + r₂, s₁ + s₂ + 6(p₁r₂ − p₂r₁)),

and L_M h(p, r, s) L_M⁻¹ = h(M(p, r), s). Hence G_ℤ = SL₂(ℤ) ⋉ Heis(ℤ).

*Proof.* Let g ∈ G_ℤ. Since g fixes γ, it preserves γ^⊥ = ⟨γ, u, w⟩. The form induced on γ^⊥/⟨γ⟩ is 6·(u* ∧ w*), so the induced map M has determinant 1. On V/γ^⊥, g acts trivially, since Q₀(γ, gx) = Q₀(g⁻¹γ, x) = Q₀(γ, x).

Then L_M⁻¹g acts trivially on ⟨γ⟩, on γ^⊥/⟨γ⟩ and on V/γ^⊥. Such a unipotent map has the form u ↦ u + a_u γ, w ↦ w + a_w γ, δ ↦ δ + pu + rw + sγ. It preserves Q₀ exactly when a_u = −6r and a_w = 6p. The law and the conjugation formula are matrix products. ∎

Two consequences are used repeatedly. Inside Heis(ℤ), conjugation acts by h(p₀, r₀, s₀) h(p, r, s) h(p₀, r₀, s₀)⁻¹ = h(p, r, s + 12(p₀r − pr₀)). The centre of G_ℤ is {h(0, 0, s)} = ⟨t_γ⟩, where t_γ = h(0, 0, 1) is the transvection δ ↦ δ + γ, that is, x ↦ x + Q₀(γ, x)γ.

The subgroup H(ℤ) = {h(p, r, 6κ)} carries the standard Heisenberg law κ₁ + κ₂ + (p₁r₂ − p₂r₁). It has index 6 in Heis(ℤ). So G_ℤ contains the Jacobi group SL₂(ℤ) ⋉ H(ℤ) with index 6, and is generated by it together with t_γ.

## 3. The monodromy group is the kernel of a character of order 12

Let ab: SL₂(ℤ) → ℤ/12 be the abelianization, normalized by ab(T) = 1 for T = [[1,1],[0,1]]. Then ab(S) = 9 for S = [[0,−1],[1,0]], ab(R) = 4 for R = [[−1,1],[−1,0]], and ab(−I) = 6. These values are forced:
- SL₂(ℤ)^ab ≅ ℤ/12 is generated by the class of T; this is standard, and [R, 15_] proves it by a Smith normal form in Theorem 6.1;
- S and R have orders 4 and 3;
- ab(S) + ab(R) = ab(SR) = ab(T), because SR = [[1,0],[−1,1]] is conjugate to T.

On Heis(ℤ) put

  φ(h(p, r, s)) = s − 6q(p, r) mod 12,  with q(p, r) = p² + pr + r² mod 2,

and on G_ℤ put Φ(L_M h) = ab(M) + φ(h) mod 12.

**Theorem 3.1.**
1. Φ: G_ℤ → ℤ/12 is a surjective homomorphism, and G = ker Φ.
2. The local monodromies decompose as T₁ = L_R h(−1, 0, 2), T₂ = L_S h(0, −1, −3) and T₀ = L_{T⁻¹} t_γ. In particular the Levi image of G is all of SL₂(ℤ).
3. G is normal in G_ℤ, and G_ℤ/G ≅ ℤ/12 is cyclic, generated by the class of the central transvection t_γ. Moreover G_ℤ = G·⟨t_γ⟩ and G ∩ ⟨t_γ⟩ = ⟨t_γ¹²⟩.
4. G ∩ Heis(ℤ) = H′ := {h(p, r, s) : s ≡ 6(p² + pr + r²) mod 12}. This subgroup has index 12, with coset representatives h(0, 0, k), 0 ≤ k ≤ 11. It is generated by the two elements c = [T₁, T₂²] = h(1, 0, −6) and c′ = T₂cT₂⁻¹ = h(0, 1, 6).
5. G ∩ SL₂(ℤ) = SL₂(ℤ)′, the commutator subgroup, which is free of rank 2 and of index 12.
6. The abelianization of G_ℤ is (ℤ/12)², via g ↦ (ab(M), φ(h)), and [G_ℤ, G_ℤ] = SL₂(ℤ)′ ⋉ H′.
7. G contains the principal congruence subgroup of level 12, and no principal congruence subgroup of level 4 or 6. For every D ≥ 1, the image of G in GL₄(ℤ/D) has index gcd(D, 12) in the image of G_ℤ.
8. G is Zariski dense in P = SL₂ ⋉ Heis, the algebraic stabilizer of γ, and it is an arithmetic subgroup of P, since it has finite index in P(ℤ) = G_ℤ.

*Proof.*

*(1), Φ is a homomorphism.* φ is a homomorphism on Heis(ℤ), because q is a quadratic refinement of the mod-2 symplectic form ω(x, y) = p_x r_y − p_y r_x:

  q(x + y) ≡ q(x) + q(y) + ω(x, y) (mod 2),

and therefore 6q(x + y) ≡ 6q(x) + 6q(y) + 6ω(x, y) (mod 12). Moreover φ is invariant under the Levi action. q equals 1 exactly on the three nonzero vectors of 𝔽₂², so q(Mx) ≡ q(x). Writing L_{M₁}h₁ · L_{M₂}h₂ = L_{M₁M₂} · (L_{M₂}⁻¹h₁L_{M₂}) · h₂ gives Φ(g₁g₂) = Φ(g₁) + Φ(g₂).

*(1), G ⊆ ker Φ and the lower bound.* By (2), Φ(T₁) = 4 + (2 − 6) = 0 and Φ(T₂) = 9 + (−3 − 6) = 0, so G ⊆ ker Φ. Since Φ(t_γ) = 1, ker Φ has index 12. Hence [G_ℤ : G] ≥ 12 and G ∩ Heis(ℤ) ⊆ ker φ = H′.

*(1), G = ker Φ.* The words c^p c′^r [c, c′]^m equal h(p, r, −6p + 6r + 6pr + 12m). Since −6p + 6r + 6pr ≡ 6q(p, r) (mod 12), these words exhaust H′, so H′ = ⟨c, c′⟩ ⊆ G. Since R and S generate SL₂(ℤ) (S(SR)S⁻¹ = T), G maps onto SL₂(ℤ). Therefore

  [G_ℤ : G] = [Heis(ℤ) : G ∩ Heis(ℤ)] ≤ [Heis(ℤ) : H′] = 12,

and G = ker Φ.

*(2)* is a direct computation.

*(3)* follows from (1) and from the description of the centre in §2.

*(4)* H′ is ker φ. The identity h(0, 0, k) h(p, r, s) = h(p, r, s + k) gives the coset representatives.

*(5)* L_M ∈ G exactly when ab(M) = 0. The commutator subgroup SL₂(ℤ)′ has index 12 and is torsion-free:
- every element of finite order in SL₂(ℤ) is conjugate to a power of S or of −R;
- these powers have ab-values 9, 6, 3 and 10, 2, 8, 4, 6, all nonzero.

A torsion-free subgroup of the amalgam ℤ/4 *_{ℤ/2} ℤ/6 acts freely on its Bass–Serre tree and is free [Se, Ch. I]. Its rank follows from the Euler characteristic: 12 · (−1/12) = −1 = 1 − rank.

*(6)* The map is a homomorphism by the computation in (1), applied to each coordinate. It is onto, since L_T ↦ (1, 0) and t_γ ↦ (0, 1). Its kernel SL₂(ℤ)′ ⋉ H′ is generated by commutators:
- the commutators of Levi elements give SL₂(ℤ)′;
- the commutators [L_M, h(x, s)] = h((M − I)x, −6ω(Mx, x)) have projections (M − I)x that span ℤ², for example (T − I)(0, 1) = (1, 0) and (Tᵀ − I)(1, 0) = (0, 1);
- the commutators of these give h(0, 0, 12).

By Proposition 4.1 the subgroup they generate is H′.

*(7)* If g ≡ I (mod 12), then ab(M) = 0, because ab factors through SL₂(ℤ/12) (checked by a breadth-first search over all 1152 elements, §9), and φ(h) = 0. On the other hand h(0, 0, 4) and h(0, 0, 6) lie in the level-4 and level-6 principal congruence subgroups and have Φ = 4 and 6.

For the index, write K_D for the principal congruence subgroup of level D. Then [G_ℤ mod D : G mod D] = [G_ℤ : G K_D] = 12/|Φ(K_D)|.
- Since t_γ^D ∈ K_D, Φ(K_D) contains gcd(D,12)ℤ/12.
- Conversely, Φ mod d factors through reduction mod d for d = gcd(D, 12): SL₂(ℤ/12) = SL₂(ℤ/4) × SL₂(ℤ/3) and SL₂(ℤ/3)^ab = ℤ/3, and 6q(p, r) mod d depends only on p, r mod 2.

*(8)* h(2, 0, 0), h(0, 2, 0) and h(0, 0, 12) lie in H′ ⊆ G. Their cyclic groups are Zariski dense in the three one-parameter subgroups that generate Heis, so the Zariski closure of G contains Heis. Its Levi image is closed and contains SL₂(ℤ), which is dense in SL₂. ∎

In particular G is not a thin group in the sense of Sarnak [Sa]: it has finite index in the integral points of its Zariski closure. This places the S⁶ period system among the arithmetic cases of rank-4 symplectic monodromy. Brav and Thomas [BT] exhibit hypergeometric monodromy groups in Sp(4, ℤ) of the other, thin kind.

## 4. The Heisenberg part, and the twist

**Proposition 4.1.**
1. The quadratic refinements of ω mod 2 on 𝔽₂² are the four functions αp² + pr + βr² (α, β ∈ 𝔽₂). Three have Arf invariant 0, and q = p² + pr + r² has Arf invariant 1. SL₂(𝔽₂) permutes the three even refinements and fixes q.
2. Let N ⊆ Heis(ℤ) be a subgroup that is normalized by the Levi group and maps onto ℤ² under (p, r). Then N = N_k := {h(p, r, s) : s ≡ 6q(p, r) mod k} for a unique divisor k of 12. Conversely, every N_k is such a subgroup, of index k.
3. H′ = N₁₂ is the smallest of them, and H′ = [G_ℤ, Heis(ℤ)] = [G_ℤ, G_ℤ] ∩ Heis(ℤ).
4. On H(ℤ) = {h(p, r, 6κ)}, H′ is the kernel of the character v_H(h(p, r, 6κ)) = (−1)^{p + r + pr + κ}.

*Proof.* (1) is a finite check.

(2) Lifts of (1, 0) and (0, 1) in N have commutator h(0, 0, 12). So N meets the centre in kℤ for some k dividing 12, and N_x = σ(x) + kℤ for each x ∈ ℤ². The function λ(x) = σ(x) − 6q(x) mod k is additive, by the law and (1), and it is Levi-invariant. It therefore vanishes on every (M − I)ℤ², and these span ℤ². So λ = 0.

(3) The subgroup [G_ℤ, Heis(ℤ)] is Levi-normal, maps onto ℤ², contains h(0, 0, 12) and lies in H′. So it is N₁₂ = H′.

(4) With s = 6κ, the condition s ≡ 6q(p, r) (mod 12) says κ ≡ q(p, r) ≡ p + pr + r (mod 2). ∎

Two readings of Proposition 4.1 connect the Heisenberg part to established theory.

Quadratic refinements of the mod-2 intersection form of a surface are its spin structures, with the Arf invariant as their parity [Jo]. On a Riemann surface they are its theta characteristics [At]. On a torus, the odd spin structure is the one fixed by the mapping class group SL₂(ℤ), and (2) shows that SL₂-invariance forces it.

The character v_H of (4) is the Heisenberg multiplier of the odd Jacobi theta function ϑ. Gritsenko and Hulek [GH, (1.4)–(1.5)] show that ϑ ∈ J_{1/2,1/2}(v_η³ × v_H). So the Heisenberg part of the monodromy group is the kernel of the odd theta multiplier.

**Proposition 4.2.** For k ∈ ℤ/12 put G_k = {L_M h : φ(h) + k·ab(M) ≡ 0}. The G_k are twelve distinct normal subgroups of index 12. Each has Levi image SL₂(ℤ) and Heisenberg part H′, and G = G₁. The extension 1 → H′ → G → SL₂(ℤ) → 1 does not split, whereas G₀ = SL₂(ℤ) ⋉ H′ does.

*Proof.* Distinctness: L_T h(0, 0, s) ∈ G_k exactly when s ≡ −k.

Non-splitting: a splitting σ would provide an involution σ(−I) ∈ G over −I, commuting with an element σ(R) ∈ G over R.
- Since (L_{−I}h(x, s))² = h(0, 2s), the involutions of G over −I are the L_{−I}h(x, 0) with Φ = 6 − 6q(x) ≡ 0, that is, with x ≢ 0 (mod 2).
- Conjugation by L_R h(y, t) sends L_{−I}h(x, 0) to L_{−I}h(R(x − 2y), 0). Commuting would therefore require Rx ≡ x (mod 2).
- But R has no nonzero fixed vector on 𝔽₂². ∎

So the pair "index 12 in the centre, odd refinement mod 2" determines the Heisenberg part H′. The group G itself is the twist of H′ by the abelianization of the Levi part.

## 5. The marked peripheral character of the S⁶ update

The higher-torus update of the S⁶ record [R, 15_, §3.2 and Theorem 6.1; see also 14_, §2] works with the peripheral matrices

  C₁ = [[−1,−1],[1,0]],  C₂′ = [[0,1],[−1,0]],  C₀ = [[1,1],[0,1]].

It defines the marked peripheral character χ = ν∘ab: SL₂(ℤ) ↠ ℤ/12 by χ(C₀) = −1, and shows that (χ(C₀), χ(C₁), χ(C₂′)) = (−1, 4, −3).

**Theorem 5.1.**
- C₁ = π(T₁)ᵀ, C₂′ = π(T₂)ᵀ and C₀ = T, where π denotes the Levi part, and χ = −ab.
- The triple (−1, 4, −3) equals (ab π(T₀), ab π(T₁), ab π(T₂)).
- For every g = L_M h(p, r, s) in the monodromy group G,

  χ(M) ≡ s − 6(p² + pr + r²)  (mod 12).

*Proof.* The matrix identities are direct. χ and −ab are homomorphisms that agree on T, which generates the abelianization, so they are equal. The triple follows from Theorem 3.1(2). The last formula is Φ(g) = 0 rewritten. ∎

So the character that the update attaches to the peripheral monodromy is exactly the Levi half of the character whose kernel is the monodromy group. On G it is determined by the Heisenberg coordinates, and the monodromy group is the locus where the two halves cancel.

The same update also exhibits a Lie-framed torus whose spin quadratic form is q(x, y) = x + xy + y over 𝔽₂, with Arf invariant one [R, 15_, (147); 14_, §1]. Since x + xy + y ≡ x² + xy + y² (mod 2), this is the odd refinement of Proposition 4.1(1). Whether that torus's first homology is the lattice ⟨ū, w̄⟩ of Proposition 4.1 is Question 2.

## 6. The orbits of the (3,4,∞) covers at every level

The Erdős–Straus workbench (its package ES-09) uses the dual matrices A_j = (T_j⁻¹)ᵀ, acting on Λ = Hom(V, ℤ) with dual basis (γ̂, û, ŵ, δ̂), to build covers from the orbits of the affine hyperplane

  H_D = {λ ∈ Λ/DΛ : γ̂-coordinate of λ = 1}.

Write λ = γ̂ + bû + cŵ + dδ̂ and e = gcd(D, 6).

**Theorem 6.1.** For every D ≥ 1, the orbits of G on H_D coincide with the orbits of G_ℤ. The orbit of λ is

  {λ′ ∈ H_D : gcd(b′, c′, e) = gcd(b, c, e)},

of size D³ · #{v ∈ (ℤ/e)² : gcd(v, e) = gcd(b, c, e)}/e².

*Proof.*

*The dual action.* The dual action of h(p, r, s) is (b, c, d) ↦ (b + 6r, c − 6p, d − s − pb − rc), and L_M acts on (b, c) by M^{−T} and fixes d.

*The orbits of G_ℤ.* d can be moved freely, and (b, c) matters only modulo 6(ℤ/D)². So the G_ℤ-orbits correspond to the SL₂(ℤ/e)-orbits on (ℤ/e)², which are classified by content, since SL₂(ℤ) maps onto SL₂(ℤ/e).

*G has the same orbits.* G is normal and G_ℤ = G⟨t_γ⟩ with t_γ central, so it suffices to show t_γλ ∈ Gλ.
- At a point with (b, c) = (g, 0), the Levi element L_{(Tᵀ)⁻¹} fixes λ and has Φ = 1. Hence t_γL_{(Tᵀ)⁻¹}⁻¹ ∈ G moves λ exactly as t_γ does.
- Every point is G_ℤ-equivalent to one with c = 0.
- Normality and centrality transport the conclusion to every point. ∎

**Corollary 6.2.**
- For D = 2ᵏ there are exactly two orbits, of sizes D³/4 and 3D³/4, for every k ≥ 1.
- For odd D there is one orbit if 3 ∤ D, and there are two, of sizes D³/9 and 8D³/9, if 3 | D.
- For 6 | D there are four orbits. At D = 6 their sizes are 6, 18, 48 and 144; at D = 12 they are 48, 144, 384 and 1152.
- In general the orbits at D = 2ᵏm (m odd) are the products of those at 2ᵏ and at m.

This settles the question, raised when these covers were examined at even levels for k ≤ 5, whether the two-orbit structure at D = 2ᵏ persists for all k and whether mixed levels decompose as products.

# Part B. Proved negative statements

## 7. What the period system does not have

Three statements of Part A and of the manuscript are negative in form, and each is proved.
- **The extension does not split** (Proposition 4.2). No subgroup of G maps isomorphically onto SL₂(ℤ): an involution over −I would have to commute with an element over R, and R has no non-zero fixed vector on 𝔽₂².
- **The level is exactly 12** (Theorem 3.1(7)). G contains no principal congruence subgroup of level 4 or 6, since h(0, 0, 4) and h(0, 0, 6) lie in them and have Φ = 4 and 6.
- **No polarized limit mixed Hodge structure at the cusp** (the manuscript's Remark 3.23). With N as in §1, the form b_N(x, y) = Q₀(x, Ny) on V/ker N = ⟨w, δ⟩ has b_N(w, w) = Q₀(w, −u) = 6, b_N(δ, δ) = Q₀(δ, γ) = −1 and b_N(w, δ) = b_N(δ, w) = 0. So b_N = diag(6, −1) is indefinite, and (V, Q₀, N) does not support a polarized limit mixed Hodge structure.

These results say which standard tools apply as they stand. Methods that need a split extension or a polarized limit do not; methods for congruence subgroups of level 12 and for arithmetic groups do.

# Part C. Connections to other areas

## 8. Further structure and connections

The period system connects to several established theories. For each connection this section states what is established and what is open, and gives my assessment.

**The paramodular group of level 6.** Gritsenko and Hulek [GH, §1] call Sp(W_t, ℤ), for an alternating form W_t of type (1, t), the integral paramodular group Γ_t. So Sp(Q₀, ℤ) ≅ Γ₆. Its maximal parabolic subgroups at one-dimensional cusps are the Jacobi groups extended by central elements. The parabolic that [GH] display fixes a vector of divisor t, corresponding to our u, while G_ℤ is the parabolic at the other cusp type, of divisor 1. It has the same shape: it contains SL₂(ℤ) ⋉ H(ℤ) with index 6 (§2). [GH, Lemma 1.1] also gives characters χ₃ and χ₄ of orders 3 and 4 of Γ₆, whose restrictions to the Heisenberg group involve v_H; Proposition 4.1(4) identifies v_H with the Heisenberg half of Φ. Whether Φ is the restriction of χ₃χ₄⁻¹ to G_ℤ is not verified here and is Question 1. My assessment: I think this identification is likely, because the two characters agree on the Heisenberg part and have the same order, but the relative normalization at the two cusp types has to be computed before it can be stated.

**Spin structures and theta characteristics.** Quadratic refinements of the mod-2 intersection form of a surface are its spin structures, with the Arf invariant as their parity [Jo]; on a Riemann surface they are its theta characteristics [At]. On a torus, the odd spin structure is the one fixed by the mapping class group SL₂(ℤ), and Proposition 4.1(2) shows that SL₂-invariance forces it in the monodromy group. The update exhibits a Lie-framed torus whose spin form q(x, y) = x + xy + y has Arf invariant one [R, 15_, (147); 14_, §1]; since x + xy + y ≡ x² + xy + y² (mod 2), this is the odd refinement of Proposition 4.1(1). Whether that torus's first homology is the lattice ⟨ū, w̄⟩ of Proposition 4.1 is not verified here (Question 2). My assessment: if it is, the update realizes geometrically the spin structure that cuts out the Heisenberg part of the monodromy, which would make the update's construction and the monodromy computation two descriptions of the same object.

**The cusp.** The cusp monodromy has N² = 0 and rank N = 2, with kernel and image the Lagrangian ⟨γ, u⟩ [A, Lemma 2.2]. On Λ²V the induced nilpotent N₂ satisfies N₂² ≠ 0 and N₂³ = 0. This is automatic for rank 2, since N₂²(a∧b) = 2Na∧Nb, and it is the Jordan type of Kulikov's type III for H² of semistable degenerations of surfaces with trivial canonical class [Ku, PP]. The manuscript's central fibre at the cusp [A, §4] has the combinatorics of Mumford's toric degenerations of abelian surfaces: dP₆ with opposite sides of its hexagon identified, two triple points, and dual complex the A₂-triangulation of the torus. Since b_N is indefinite (§7), the typed comparison with the Kulikov–Persson–Pinkham picture holds at the level of Jordan type and of combinatorics, and a Hodge-theoretic comparison needs a replacement for the polarization. My assessment: I think the combinatorial match is a strong indication that the degeneration is of Mumford type, and finding the right substitute for the polarization is the natural next step.

**The modular part.** Up to the factor 6 in the induced form, the Levi group acts on γ^⊥/⟨γ⟩ as SL₂(ℤ) acts on the first homology of an elliptic curve, and the Heisenberg part records the extension data. Theorem 3.1 and Proposition 4.1 describe both exactly. Whether γ^⊥/⟨γ⟩ is the homology of an elliptic family inside or under the tori is Question 3.

**Arithmetic, not thin.** G has finite index in the integral points of its Zariski closure, so it is not thin in the sense of Sarnak [Sa]. This places the S⁶ period system among the arithmetic cases of rank-4 symplectic monodromy; Brav and Thomas [BT] exhibit hypergeometric monodromy groups in Sp(4, ℤ) of the thin kind. Arithmeticity makes strong approximation and the orbit classification of §6 available, and the Erdős–Straus workbench uses exactly this action to build its covers.

## 10. Questions

1. Is Φ the restriction to G_ℤ of the character χ₃χ₄⁻¹ of Gritsenko and Hulek? It agrees with it on the Heisenberg part (Proposition 4.1(4)). What remains is the relative normalization of χ₃χ₄⁻¹ at the two positive transvections L_T and t_γ, which lie at cusps of different divisor. If the values agree, G = Stab(γ) ∩ ker χ₃χ₄⁻¹.
2. Is the first homology of the Arf-one torus of [R, 15_, (147)] the lattice ⟨ū, w̄⟩ of Proposition 4.1? If so, the odd spin structure that cuts out H′ is realized geometrically in the construction.
3. Is the SL₂(ℤ)-graded piece γ^⊥/⟨γ⟩ the homology of an elliptic family inside or under the tori?
4. The index 12 of G in G_ℤ equals the order of SL₂(ℤ)^ab and of the peripheral character. Theorem 5.1 explains the coincidence at the level of groups. Does it have a meaning in the geometry of the completed threefold, for instance through the mod-24 boundary of [R, 15_, Theorem 6.1]?
