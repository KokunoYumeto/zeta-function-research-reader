\section{The setting}\label{sec:setting}

\paragraph{The programme's idea.}
The programme's coefficient algebra keeps a ring's internal zero apart from a separate symbol for absence, so that a zero element in a given support fibre is not confused with an absent fibre.
On the zeta side it follows the zeros of $\zeta$ along the one-parameter family of Hurwitz sheets $t\mapsto\zeta(s,1+t)$ and asks which constructions (quotient modules, receivers, positivity forms, cohomology over a reconstructed arithmetic) can see where the zeros lie.
In the author's picture the support $\tau$ is the pivot of the swing between $0$ and $1$; on the zeta side the natural pivot is the reflection $s\mapsto 1-\bar s$ about $\re s=\tfrac12$ (a proposed typed matching, see Section~\ref{sec:bridges}).

\paragraph{Sheets.}
For $a>0$ and $\re s>1$ let $\zeta(s,a)=\sum_{n\ge0}(n+a)^{-s}$, continued meromorphically to $\C$ with a single simple pole at $s=1$.
The programme's flow is $t\mapsto\zeta(s,1+t)$.
The sheets $a=1$ and $a=\tfrac12$ are the Eulerian ones: $\zeta(s,1)=\zeta(s)$ and $\zeta(s,\tfrac12)=(2^s-1)\zeta(s)$.
The even lattice sums are $Z_a(s)=\zeta(s,a)+\zeta(s,1-a)$ for $0<a<1$.

\paragraph{Zeros.}
Put $\xi(s)=\tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$ and $F_*(s)=\xi(s)/4$ (the programme's $F_0$).
Let $\Zs$ be the set of distinct nontrivial zeros $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ of $\zeta$, with multiplicities $m_\rho$; sums over $\Zs$ run over distinct zeros with the weights $m_\rho$ written out.
Write $\rho^{\#}=1-\bar\rho$.
Since $\xi(1-s)=\xi(s)$ and $\xi(\bar s)=\cl{\xi(s)}$, the map $\rho\mapsto\rho^{\#}$ permutes $\Zs$ preserving multiplicities, and the Riemann hypothesis (RH) says exactly that $\rho^{\#}=\rho$ for every $\rho$.
For $T>0$ not an ordinate, $N(T)$ counts the zeros with $0<\gamma\le T$ (with multiplicity), $N_0(T)$ those on the line, and $P(T)$ those with $\beta<\tfrac12$; Hardy's function is $Z(t)=e^{i\theta(t)}\zeta(\tfrac12+it)$, real for real $t$, and $V(T)$ is its number of sign changes on $(0,T)$.

\paragraph{The strip space and Meyer's quotient.}
Let $\B$ be the space of entire functions $F$ with
\[
b_{A,M}(F)=\sup_{|\sigma|\le A,\ t\in\R}(1+|t|)^M|F(\sigma+it)|<\infty\qquad\text{for all }A,M\ge0.
\]
With these seminorms $\B$ is a Fréchet space.
For a divisor $D$ let $I_D\subset\B$ be the closed ideal of functions vanishing to order at least $D(\lambda)$ at each point $\lambda$, and write $I_\zeta=I_{\Zs}$ (full multiplicity at every nontrivial zero).
The programme's Meyer quotient is $Q=\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{E}S\cong\B/I_\zeta$ (\lbl{OMS3.14}; Meyer, \cite{Meyer}); here $\mathcal{E}$ is Connes' summation map and the isomorphism is the Mellin transform of the exact summation image (Proposition~\ref{prop:summation-image}).
Multiplication by $s$ acts on $Q$; its spectrum is $\Zs$ and each zero carries one Jordan block of length $m_\rho$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:spectral-L}).
The jet at $\rho$ is $j_\rho[F]=F(\rho+T)\bmod T^{m_\rho}\in A_\rho=\C[T]/(T^{m_\rho})$, and the primary projector $\Pi_\rho=s_\rho j_\rho$ uses the programme's sections $s_\rho$ (\lbl{OMS6}).

\paragraph{Two lines and three maps.}
The two-line function is
\[
G(s)=F_*(s)F_*(s+1)=\xi(s)\xi(s+1)/16,
\]
whose zero set is $\Zs\sqcup(\Zs-1)$, the two parts lying in $0<\re s<1$ and $-1<\re s<0$.
Three maps organise it: the reflection $s\mapsto s^{\#}=1-\bar s$ through $\tfrac12$, the mirror $A(s)=-\bar s$ through the pole at $0$, and the translation $T$ between the lines.
On $\Zs$ they satisfy $A(\rho)=\rho^{\#}-1$, that is, $T^{-1}A=\#$.

\paragraph{Weil's pairing.}
For finitely supported data $u$ on $\Zs$ put $\Omega(u)=\sum_{\rho\in\Zs}m_\rho\,u(\rho)\cl{u(\rho^{\#})}$, the zero side of Weil's quadratic form \cite{Weil,Bombieri}.
Weil's criterion says that RH holds if and only if Weil's functional is nonnegative on every $g*g^{\#}$; on finitely supported data, $\Omega\ge0$ for all $u$ exactly when every zero is on the line (Proposition~\ref{prop:chiral}(b)).
