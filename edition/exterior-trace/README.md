# Zeta research: cyclic sums, exterior traces and arithmetic control

This collection asks how finite packets of zeros of the Riemann zeta function can be represented by the original theta-function source, and what the resulting inner products say about their spectral positions. A packet records selected zeros together with their full multiplicities. Repeated zeros are retained as Taylor jets, not replaced by one eigenvalue.

## What the new calculation does

Start with the full local algebra of a zero packet and form the subspace generated by polynomials in the sum of its spectral coordinates. This **cyclic sum packet** retains the complete jet depth at each distinct sum, including collisions between different tuples. Its inner product is obtained by a least-norm lift from the original theta source, with all polynomial moments and the original mass retained.

For this metric, the Hermitian departure of the sum operator from the critical-line centre has rank at most two. Its nonzero eigenvalues are a pair, +epsilon and -epsilon. Taking exterior powers therefore keeps the same epsilon allowance: it does not multiply that allowance by the number of selected directions. The exterior power of the positive spectral part records their total real excess through a determinant trace. This turns a possible off-line quartet into an explicit lower bound on the arithmetic control needed to accommodate it.

For an exact quartet at 1/2 +/- delta +/- i gamma, with delta and gamma positive and full common zero multiplicity m, the sum-packet calculation gives

`L = 2 delta [1 + k(m - 1)] (k + 1) floor((k + 1)^2 / 4) <= epsilon`.

Thus simple quartet zeros demand cubic growth in k; repeated quartet zeros demand quartic growth. A larger packet preserves the quartet lower bound, but its own complete degree determines the admissible source cutoff. The proof does not infer an upper bound on epsilon. In particular, a subcubic arithmetic estimate has not been obtained, and these calculations neither prove nor disprove the Riemann hypothesis.
