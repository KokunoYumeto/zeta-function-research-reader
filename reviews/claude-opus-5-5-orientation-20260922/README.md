### 3.3 Conjugation reverses the current

**Proposition 1.** Let κ act on polynomials in y by conjugating their coefficients. The letter κ is used because C already denotes the self-adjoint part of M (RC32, ST3) and the cyclic matrix of OPG4. Here y = (S−k/2)/i is the coordinate in which M = multiplication by y.

1. κ is antiunitary for every source metric G_N on E, and κM = Mκ.
2. κ maps the generalized eigenspace of ω onto that of ω̄.
3. Let K be the kernel of the observation, and suppose κ(K) = K. Then for every x ∈ E with P_B x ≠ 0, Φ(Λ κx) = −Φ(Λx) and j(κx) = −j(x). Here Φ(Λx) = i[⟨y,My⟩ − ⟨My,y⟩], with y = P_B x (RC32), and j(x) = Φ(Λx)/‖y‖² (SC2).

**Proof.**

*Step 1: reality and evenness of the measure.* BT3 (`02_FULL_GAMMA_PROOFS.tex:9725–9737`) shows that w_h is positive and even: "w_h(−t)=w_h(t)". Its k-fold convolution m_{h,k} is therefore a positive, even measure on ℝ.

*Step 2: κ is antiunitary on the lift spaces.* For real y, (κp)(y) is the complex conjugate of p(y). Hence ∫|κp|² dm = ∫|p|² dm. So κ is antiunitary on each space ℂ[y]_{≤N}.

*Step 3: κ descends to E.* In the y-coordinate, the roots of χ are ω_{ab} = (2b−k)γ − i(2a−k)δ. Their conjugates are ω̄_{ab} = ω_{k−a,b}, with the same multiplicity ℓ_k. So χ has real coefficients in y, and κ preserves the ideal (χ) and the degree. Therefore κ maps the set of degree-≤N lifts of e bijectively onto the set of lifts of κe. The least-norm quotient norm then satisfies ‖κe‖_{G_N} = ‖e‖_{G_N}.

*Step 4: κ commutes with M.* κ(yp) = y·κp, so κM = Mκ. This also gives κ(M−ω)^ℓ = (M−ω̄)^ℓ κ, which proves item 2.

*Step 5: κ commutes with the projections.* If κ(K) = K, then κ(K^⊥) = K^⊥, because κ is antiunitary. Hence P_K κ = κ P_K and P_B κ = κ P_B.

*Step 6: the current changes sign.* With y = P_B x, antiunitarity gives ⟨Ca, Cb⟩ = conj⟨a,b⟩. Therefore

Φ(Λκx) = i[conj⟨y,My⟩ − conj⟨My,y⟩] = −conj(Φ(Λx)) = −Φ(Λx).

The last step uses that Φ(Λx) = 2 Im⟨My,y⟩ is real. Since ‖κy‖ = ‖y‖, the claim for j follows. ∎

**Consequence.** Suppose K is κ-invariant, which holds when the observation intertwines κ with an antilinear bijection of its target, for example on a real-period locus. Then:

- The terminal class at ω = kγ − ikδ and its conjugate class at ω̄ = kγ + ikδ carry exactly opposite currents.
- The sign of the current at one corner is therefore always accompanied by the opposite sign at the conjugate corner of the same configuration.

**[Inference]** No sign of this current can, by itself, contradict δ>0. A contradiction would need an inequality that holds for ζ and is violated by an off-line quartet, that is, a positivity statement. Section 4 supplies one.

Whether κ(K_u) = K_ū holds for the actual OPG4 construction is task B1 requested from a separate session (§10).

**Corollary (balanced inertia).** Let Φ be a Hermitian form on a finite-dimensional space V. Let κ be an antiunitary bijection of V with Φ(κx) = −Φ(x). Then Φ has equally many positive and negative eigenvalues.

**Proof.** Let V₊ be a subspace of maximal dimension on which Φ is positive definite. Then κ(V₊) has the same complex dimension, since κ is an antilinear bijection. Φ is negative definite on κ(V₊). Hence n₋ ≥ n₊. Applying the same argument to −Φ gives n₊ ≥ n₋. ∎

**Proposition 2 (control completion).**

- Let γ₁, γ₂ be the ordinates of the first two zeros of ξ. Both zeros are simple and lie on the critical line.
- Choose δ₀ ∈ (0,1/2) and γ₀ ≥ max(2, 1000δ₀), with γ₀ ∉ {γ₁, γ₂}, such that ρ₀ = 1/2+δ₀+iγ₀ is not a zero of ξ. Any γ₀ < 3·10¹² qualifies, by Platt and Trudgian, *Bull. LMS* 53 (2021) 792–797.
- Define:
  - Q₀(s) = ((s−½)² − (δ₀+iγ₀)²)((s−½)² − (δ₀−iγ₀)²);
  - Q₁(s) = ((s−½)² + γ₁²)((s−½)² + γ₂²);
  - g̃ = 2ξ·Q₀/Q₁.

Then:

- (i) g̃ is entire.
- (ii) g̃(1−s) = g̃(s), and g̃ is real on ℝ.
- (iii) g̃ has simple zeros at ρ₀, ρ̄₀, 1−ρ₀ and 1−ρ̄₀.
- (iv) g̃/2ξ → 1 as |s| → ∞.
- (v) There is no Dirichlet series D, convergent at some point, with g̃(s) = s(s−1)π^{−s/2}Γ(s/2)D(s).

**Proof of (i)–(iv).**

- *(i)* The zeros of Q₁ are 1/2 ± iγ₁ and 1/2 ± iγ₂, each simple, and they are simple zeros of ξ. So 2ξ/Q₁ is entire.
- *(ii)* Q₀ and Q₁ are polynomials in (s−½)², so they are invariant under s ↦ 1−s. The two factors of Q₀ are complex-conjugate polynomials, so Q₀ has real coefficients.
- *(iii)* The zeros of Q₀ are s − ½ = ±(δ₀ ± iγ₀). These four points are distinct and not on Re s = ½. At each of them ξ ≠ 0 and Q₁ ≠ 0.
- *(iv)* Q₀ and Q₁ are both monic of degree 4 in s − ½.

**Proof of (v).**

*Step 1: reduce to a rational function.* Since 2ξ(s) = s(s−1)π^{−s/2}Γ(s/2)ζ(s), such a D would satisfy D = ζR, with R = Q₀/Q₁. A Dirichlet series that converges at one point converges absolutely on a right half-plane. Fix σ₁ > 1 in that half-plane. Then 1/ζ = Σμ(n)n^{−s} converges absolutely on Re s = σ₁. So R(σ₁+it) = Σ_n b_n n^{−σ₁} e^{−it log n}, with Σ_n |b_n| n^{−σ₁} < ∞.

*Step 2: almost-periods.* Put φ(t) = R(σ₁+it) − 1. Given ε > 0, choose N so that the tail beyond N contributes less than ε/3. Let T be a positive integer. Apply the pigeonhole principle to the points ({jT log n/2π})_{n≤N} ∈ [0,1)^N for j = 0, …, Q^N. Two of them lie in the same box of side 1/Q, so some τ = dT with 1 ≤ d ≤ Q^N satisfies ‖τ log n/2π‖ < 1/Q for all n ≤ N. With Q large, this gives |φ(t+τ) − φ(t)| < ε for all t, with τ ≥ T.

*Step 3: conclusion.* R(σ₁+it) → 1 as |t| → ∞, so φ(t+τ) → 0 as τ → ∞. Hence |φ(t)| ≤ 2ε for every t. Since ε is arbitrary, φ ≡ 0. Then R ≡ 1 by analytic continuation. This contradicts R(ρ₀) = 0. ∎

Hamburger's theorem (Math. Z. 10 (1921) 240–254) is a stronger classical statement of the same kind.

**Principle.**

- Any argument that uses only properties which g̃ also has holds for g̃ and its actual off-line quartet. Such an argument cannot contradict δ > 0.
- By (v), the Dirichlet-series structure, and hence the Euler product and the primes, is a property that 2ξ has and g̃ lacks.
- Whether the CK/RC/TR/SC/ST/MP/DF/EE chain uses only properties shared by g̃ is task A3 requested from a separate session (§10).
- The evidence listed above for (a)–(d) supports "only shared properties". The transfer of (b) is the step to be written out, because 2ξ/Q₁ changes the constants near t = ±γ₁ and t = ±γ₂.
- This conclusion is **[open]** until the audit is returned.

## 4. New angle A: Weil's quadratic form localizes to the quartet on the programme's own source space

This section uses the conventions of the repository's own explicit-formula section, `satellites/22_rh_counterexample_routes.tex:348–398`:

- f̂(z) = ∫ f(u)e^{−iuz}du;
- W(f,g) = Σ_ρ m_ρ conj(f̂(γ̄_ρ)) ĝ(γ_ρ), with γ_ρ = (ρ−½)/i;
- the complete explicit formula `eq:rh-full-weil-test`: pole terms, plus the Gamma integral with weight (Re ψ(¼+it/2) − log π)/2π, minus Σ_n Λ(n)n^{−1/2}{h(log n) + h(−log n)}.

**Proposition 3.** Assume OPG1. For P ∈ ℂ[z], define F_P(z) = P(z)·v_h(½+iz) and let f_P be the function with f̂_P = F_P.

- (i) F_P is entire, and F_P(γ_{ρ′}) = 0 for every zero ρ′ ∉ R of ξ.
- (ii) W(f_P, f_{P′}) = m Σ_{α∈R} conj(F_P(γ̄_α)) F_{P′}(γ_α).
- (iii) In the s-coordinate s = ½+iz, the point γ_α corresponds to α and γ̄_α corresponds to 1−ᾱ. Hence W(f_P, f_{P′}) = m Σ_{α∈R} conj(P(1−ᾱ)v_h(1−ᾱ))·P′(α)v_h(α). This is a Hermitian form B_h on the zero packet: it factors through the values on R, and it pairs α with 1−ᾱ, so ρ ↔ 1−ρ̄ and ρ̄ ↔ 1−ρ.
- (iv) Let m = 1 and δ > 0. On {f_P : deg P ≤ 3}, the form P ↦ W(f_P, f_P) has signature (2,2). For example, it equals −2 when the values of P·v_h at (ρ, 1−ρ̄, ρ̄, 1−ρ) are (1, −1, 0, 0). If instead the packet lies on the line (δ = 0, so 1−ᾱ = α), then W(f_P, f_P) = m Σ_α |P(α)v_h(α)|² ≥ 0.
- (v) W(f_P, f_{P′}) equals the explicit-formula side, which includes the prime sum.

**Proof.**

- *(i)* v_h = 2ξ/h is entire because every root of h is a zero of 2ξ of at least the same order. At a zero ρ′ ∉ R, h(ρ′) ≠ 0 and ξ(ρ′) = 0.
- *(ii)* In W, the term for ρ′ ∉ R contains the factor F_{P′}(γ_{ρ′}) = 0. The terms for α ∈ R carry multiplicity m.
- *(iii)* The computation is γ̄_α = i(ᾱ−½), and ½ + i·i(ᾱ−½) = 1−ᾱ.
- *(iv)*
  - The four points of R are distinct, and v_h ≠ 0 on R. So by Lagrange interpolation, P ↦ (P·v_h)|_R is a bijection from ℂ[z]_{≤3} onto ℂ⁴.
  - Write a, b, c, d for the values at ρ, 1−ρ̄, ρ̄, 1−ρ. Then W = m(b̄a + āb + d̄c + c̄d) = 2m[Re(āb) + Re(c̄d)].
  - Each summand has matrix m·[[0,1],[1,0]], whose eigenvalues are ±m. Hence the signature is (2,2).
- *(v)* This is Weil's explicit formula (Weil 1952).
  - The repository states it for C_c² test functions. The class used here, with F_P entire and of exponential decay in the strip |Im z| ≤ 1 (from Stirling's formula), is covered by the standard contour argument for Σ_ρ F(ρ) against ξ′/ξ. The needed bound log-derivative ≪ log²T on suitable horizontals is in Davenport, *Multiplicative Number Theory*, Ch. 17.
  - Writing that proof out in the programme's standard is listed in §9.
  - It is checked numerically to 33 digits below. ∎

## 5. New angle B: what Deligne's tensor-power step needs, compared with the packet

Deligne, *La conjecture de Weil. I* (Publ. Math. IHÉS 43, 1974), §3 "La majoration fondamentale", Theorem 3.2 with Lemmas 3.3 and 3.6 (Milne's English translation). The hypotheses are:

- the local characteristic polynomials have rational coefficients;
- the image of π₁ is open in the symplectic group;
- a nondegenerate alternating form with values in ℚ_ℓ(β).

The proof applies Rankin's method to ⊗^{2k}F:

- The logarithm of L(⊗^{2k}F) has nonnegative coefficients (Lemma 3.3).
- The pole location comes from the coinvariants of the symplectic monodromy.
- Together these give |α| ≤ q_x^{β/2 + 1/(2k)}. Letting k → ∞ removes the 1/(2k).

How this compares with the programme:

- **Present in the programme:** the tensor power E ↪ E_h^{⊗k} (OPG2), and an average over a group, P_k, the cyclic group of order n+1 (OPG4).
- **[Inference] Not present in the SC/TR/CK chain:**
  - a positivity input playing the role of Lemma 3.3;
  - a large group whose coinvariants fix the pole. The programme's group is cyclic of order n+1, not an open subgroup of a symplectic group.
- **[Recommendation]** Section 4 supplies a positivity candidate. The open question is which tensor construction on E carries a positivity statement of Rankin type for ζ.
