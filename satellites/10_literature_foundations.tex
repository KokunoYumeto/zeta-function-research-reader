\section{Literature foundations}
\label{sec:literature-foundations}

\subsection{Status and source editions}

This section is the first bounded content-level literature tranche.  It does not treat a
search hit, a title, or a bibliography entry as mathematical evidence.  Fourteen human-authored
sources now have content-level records: Bogomolny's complete review, Goldston's complete
lecture notes, bounded analytic/random-matrix portions of the Schumayer--Hutchinson and
Connes surveys, the complete Keating--Snaith, Montgomery, Grover--Mezzadri--Simm,
Bourgade--Hughes--Nikeghbali--Yor, and Bourgade--Nikeghbali--Rouault primary papers,
Guinand's complete journal article, Weil's complete 1952 article, the bounded
Fourier-analysis chapters of Rudin used in the exact transform proofs, Bourgade's complete
conditional-Haar paper, and the complete fifth chapter of Bourgade's thesis.  No OCR was
used.  The sources are cited conventionally throughout this chapter and collected in the
bibliography~\cite{bogomolny2007,goldston2004,schumayerHutchinson2011,connes2026,
keatingSnaith2000,montgomery1973,guinand1948,weil1952,rudin1990,
groverMezzadriSimm2026,bourgadeHughesNikeghbaliYor2007,
bourgadeNikeghbaliRouault2008,bourgadeConditionalHaar2009,bourgadeThesis2010}.
The fourth identity was checked against the native first page and PDF metadata as well as
the public record.  The identifier \texttt{2501.06560}, previously attached to it by a
generated bibliographic graph, belongs to a different work cited inside the survey.

Weil was read first through the native text of Denise Vella--Chemla's public 2020 French
transcription.  Every one of the fourteen pages of the original collected-works reprint was
then obtained as a separate public scan leaf and checked visually.  The original scan controls
conjugation bars, signs, kernels, and denominators; the transcription remains a distinct
edition.  Guinand was read visually from all thirteen pages of the public journal-volume scan.

The sources do not all have the same evidentiary role.  Goldston gives a detailed analytic
derivation and carefully separates theorems from conjectures.  Bogomolny supplies an
especially useful map of the quantum-chaos and random-matrix interfaces, while repeatedly
identifying the semiclassical calculations as heuristic.  The other two sources are routing
surveys whose theorem-level assertions must be checked against their primary references.
Keating--Snaith is a primary source: its finite circular-ensemble integrals, cumulant
calculations, and random-matrix limits are theorem content, while its transfers to zeta
moments and prime-factor models are explicitly conjectural or heuristic.  Montgomery,
Guinand, Weil, and Grover--Mezzadri--Simm are primary sources; their different hypotheses,
parameter spaces, limits, and theorem/conjecture boundaries are retained term by term below.
The three Bourgade-led papers and the thesis chapter supply the direct human coordinate and
probability precedents used in the derivative-state crosswalk below.

\subsection{Explicit-formula presentations}

The following presentations are deliberately retained as separate objects.  Their common subject
does not make their test spaces, transforms, convergence rules, or boundary terms identical.

\subsubsection{A sharp Chebyshev presentation}

Let
\[
  \psi(x)=\sum_{p^m\leq x}\log p,
  \qquad
  \psi_0(x)=\frac{\psi(x+0)+\psi(x-0)}2.
\]
Goldston records, for $x>1$~\cite[eqs.~(3.1), (3.3), and~(3.4)]{goldston2004},
\begin{equation}
 \psi_0(x)=x-\sum_\rho\frac{x^\rho}{\rho}
 -\log(2\pi)-\frac12\log(1-x^{-2}),
 \label{eq:sharp-explicit}
\end{equation}
where conjugate zeros are grouped because the zero sum is not absolutely convergent.  A
truncated presentation retains additional variables and jump data:
\begin{equation}
 \psi(x)=x-\sum_{|\gamma|\leq T}\frac{x^\rho}{\rho}
 +O\!\left(\frac{x}{T}(\log xT)^2\right)
 +O\!\left((\log x)\min\!\left(1,\frac{x}{T\lVert x\rVert}\right)\right),
 \label{eq:truncated-explicit}
\end{equation}
where $\lVert x\rVert$ is the distance to the nearest integer.  The final term records the
jumps at prime powers; it is not disposable presentation noise.

\subsubsection{An additive even-test/Fourier presentation}

Bogomolny takes a function $h$ analytic in
$|\operatorname{Im}r|\leq \tfrac12+\delta$, even, and satisfying
$|h(r)|\leq A(1+|r|)^{-2-\delta}$, with transform
\cite[explicit-formula discussion]{bogomolny2007}
\begin{equation}
  g(u)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\R}h(r)e^{-iru}\,dr.
  \label{eq:bogomolny-fourier}
\end{equation}
The displayed explicit formula has an archimedean $\Gamma'/\Gamma$ integral, a prime-power
sum weighted by $g(n\log p)$, two pole terms, and $-g(0)\log\pi$.  The source writes the
zero-side summand as $h(s_n)$ while defining $h$ in an ordinate variable.  Until the
$s_n\leftrightarrow r_n$ shift is checked against the primary formula, this is a recorded
presentation rather than an admitted theorem statement.  In particular, the later formal
substitution $h=\delta_E$, which the source introduces only after explicitly setting aside
convergence, is not licensed by the stated test space.

\subsubsection{Guinand's primary half-line cosine/Hankel presentation}

Guinand assumes RH throughout the 1948 paper and writes every nontrivial zero as
$\tfrac12+i\gamma$, with multiplicity retained.  His cosine-transform convention is
\cite[pp.~107--108, eq.~(1.1)]{guinand1948}
\begin{equation}
 g(y)=\left(\frac2\pi\right)^{1/2}
       \int_0^\infty f(x)\cos(xy)\,dx.
 \label{eq:guinand-cosine}
\end{equation}
For Theorem~3, $f$ is an integral, tends to zero at infinity, and
$xf'(x)\in L^p(0,\infty)$ for some $1<p\leq2$.  Put
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal P_f(T)&=\sum_{0<m\log p<T}^{\prime}
   \frac{\log p}{p^{m/2}}f(m\log p),
 &\mathcal P_1(T)&=\sum_{0<m\log p<T}^{\prime}\frac{\log p}{p^{m/2}},\\
 \mathcal A_f(T)&=\int_0^T f(x)e^{x/2}\,dx,
 &\mathcal H_f&=\frac12\int_0^\infty
 f(x)\left(\frac1x-\frac{e^{-x/2}}{\sinh x}\right)dx,\\
 \mathcal Z_g(T)&=\sum_{0<\gamma<T}g(\gamma),
 &\mathcal D_g(T)&=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^Tg(x)\log\frac{x}{2\pi}\,dx.
\end{align*}
The dash halves a prime-power term exactly on the cutoff.  Guinand's literal theorem is
\cite[pp.~112--115, Thms.~3 and~3$'$]{guinand1948}
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{T\to\infty}\!\left\{
 \mathcal P_f(T)-\mathcal A_f(T)
 -f(T)\bigl(\mathcal P_1(T)-2e^{T/2}\bigr)\right\}
 -\mathcal H_f
 =-\sqrt{2\pi}\lim_{T\to\infty}
 \bigl\{\mathcal Z_g(T)-\mathcal D_g(T)\bigr\}.
 \label{eq:guinand-theorem-3}
\end{equation}
The boundary term is part of the theorem.  Theorem~$3'$ permits its omission if either
\[
 \sum_{n\leq N}\Lambda(n)-N=O\!\left(N^{1/2}\log^\alpha N\right)
 \quad\hbox{with }\alpha=1/p,
\]
or $T^{1/2}f(T)\to0$.  It is not omitted merely because a later presentation looks cleaner.

The paper also supplies a distinct order-$3/2$ Hankel-transform pair.  With
\[
 \mathcal F(x)=\frac1x\left\{N(x)-\left(\frac{x}{2\pi}\log\frac{x}{2\pi}
 -\frac{x}{2\pi}\right)\right\},
\]
the explicitly displayed prime-power function $\mathcal G(x)$ in Guinand's equation~(2.9)
is the order-$3/2$ Hankel transform of $\mathcal F$.  The inversion lemmas retain their
$L^p$ hypotheses and recover the averaged value
$\{\mathcal F(y+0)+\mathcal F(y-0)\}/2$.  The resulting Theorem~2 is an exact formula for
$\{N(T+0)+N(T-0)\}/2$; neither averaging operation is erased here
\cite[pp.~108--112, Thms.~1--2]{guinand1948}.

Guinand records the multiplicative change of variables rather than leaving it implicit
\cite[p.~115, immediately after Thm.~3$'$]{guinand1948}:
\begin{equation}
 t=e^x,\qquad U=e^T,\qquad dx=\frac{dt}{t},\qquad
 e^{x/2}dx=t^{-1/2}dt,
 \label{eq:guinand-log-change}
\end{equation}
so the prime/main-term expression becomes
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{U\to\infty}\left\{
 \sum_{n<U}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{1/2}}f(\log n)
 -\int_1^U f(\log t)t^{-1/2}\,dt\right\}.
 \label{eq:guinand-multiplicative}
\end{equation}
Finally, the compact cosine cutoff in Theorem~4 produces a term $-X+O(1)$ precisely when
$y$ is a positive zero ordinate and $O(1)$ otherwise.  The inverse resonance is
\[
 \sum_{0<\gamma<X}\cos(y\gamma)=
 \begin{cases}
 -\dfrac{X}{2\pi}\dfrac{\log p}{p^{m/2}}+O(\log X),&y=m\log p,\\[4pt]
 O(\log X),&y\neq m\log p.
 \end{cases}
\]
These are RH-conditional summation theorems, not an operator construction
\cite[pp.~115--119, Thms.~4--5]{guinand1948}.

\subsubsection{A multiplicative Mellin/local-place presentation}

Connes presents a function $f:\R_{>0}\to\C$ with piecewise $C^1$ regularity, averaged
one-sided values at its finitely many jumps, and, for some $\delta>0$,
\cite[explicit-formula presentation]{connes2026}
\[
 f(x)=O(x^\delta)\quad(x\to0+),
 \qquad
 f(x)=O(x^{-1-\delta})\quad(x\to\infty).
\]
Set
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde f(s)=\int_0^\infty f(x)x^{s-1}\,dx,
 \qquad
 f^\sharp(x)=x^{-1}f(x^{-1}).
 \label{eq:mellin-sharp}
\end{equation}
The survey attributes to Guinand and Weil the presentation
\begin{equation}
 \sum_\rho \widetilde f(\rho)
 =\widetilde f(0)+\widetilde f(1)-\sum_v\mathcal W_v(f),
 \label{eq:local-explicit}
\end{equation}
with finite-place distributions
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal W_p(f)=(\log p)\sum_{m\geq1}
 \bigl(f(p^m)+f^\sharp(p^m)\bigr)
 \label{eq:finite-place}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal W_\R(f)=(\log(4\pi)+\gamma_E)f(1)
 +\int_1^\infty
 \left(f(x)+f^\sharp(x)-\frac{2f(1)}x\right)\frac{dx}{x-x^{-1}}.
 \label{eq:arch-place}
\end{equation}
The zero sum is again generally conditional.  This later survey specialization is useful, but
the exact 1952 source is the next, separately retained object.

\subsubsection{Weil's primary Hecke/id\`ele-class presentation}

Let $k$ be a number field of degree $d$, $I_k$ its id\`ele group, $P_k$ the principal
id\`eles, and $C_k=I_k/P_k$.  Weil fixes a character $\chi$ of $C_k$, its conductor
$\mathfrak f$, and archimedean data $\eta_\lambda\in\{1,2\}$,
$f_\lambda$, and $\varphi_\lambda$, choosing the associated representative with
$\sum_\lambda\eta_\lambda\varphi_\lambda=0$
\cite[Collected Papers II, pp.~48--52, eqs.~(1)--(7)]{weil1952}.  For
\[
 A=(2\pi)^{-d}|\Delta|N\mathfrak f,
\]
the completed Hecke function $\Lambda(s)=G(s)L(s)$ satisfies the source's conjugated
functional equation
\[
 \Lambda(1-\overline{s})=\kappa\,\overline{\Lambda(s)},
 \qquad |\kappa|=1.
\]

Weil's additive logarithmic test is $F:\R\to\C$ with
\begin{equation}
 \Phi(s)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}F(x)e^{(s-1/2)x}\,dx.
 \label{eq:weil-Phi}
\end{equation}
Condition (A) requires $F$ to be continuous and continuously differentiable away from
finitely many first-kind jumps of $F$ and $F'$, with the value at each jump equal to the
average of its one-sided limits.  Condition (B) requires, for some $b>0$,
\[
 F(x),F'(x)=O\!\left(e^{-(1/2+b)|x|}\right).
\]
The singular archimedean functional uses Weil's literal finite-part convention.  If
$\beta(x)=|x|a(x)$, then
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{PF}\!\int_\R a(x)\,dx
 =\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\left\{
 \int_\R(1-e^{-\lambda|x|})a(x)\,dx-2\beta(0)\log\lambda\right\}.
 \label{eq:weil-PF}
\end{equation}
It differs from Schwartz's $\operatorname{Pf}$ by a Dirac multiple and is not replaced by an
ordinary integral.  Define
\begin{equation}
 K_{1,f}(x)=\frac{e^{(1/2-f)|x|}}{|e^x-e^{-x}|},
 \qquad
 K_{2,f}(x)=\frac{e^{-f|x|/2}}{|e^{x/2}-e^{-x/2}|}.
 \label{eq:weil-kernels}
\end{equation}
If $\omega=\beta+i\gamma$ runs over the zeros of $\Lambda$ with multiplicity, Weil's
formula~(11) is~\cite[Collected Papers II, pp.~57--58, eq.~(11)]{weil1952}
\begin{align}
 \lim_{T\to\infty}\sum_{|\gamma|<T}\Phi(\omega)
 ={}&\delta_\chi\int_\R F(x)(e^{x/2}+e^{-x/2})\,dx+F(0)\log A \notag\\
 &-\sum_{\mathfrak p}\sum_{n\geq1}
 \frac{\log N\mathfrak p}{(N\mathfrak p)^{n/2}}
 \left\{\chi(\mathfrak p)^nF(\log N\mathfrak p^n)
 +\chi(\mathfrak p)^{-n}F(\log N\mathfrak p^{-n})\right\}\notag\\
 &-\sum_\lambda\operatorname{PF}\!\int_\R
 F(x)e^{-i\varphi_\lambda x}K_{\eta_\lambda,f_\lambda}(x)\,dx.
 \label{eq:weil-11}
\end{align}
The cutoff is symmetric in $\gamma$; the finite-place orientations $\pm n\log N\mathfrak p$
both remain present.

Weil then proves a positivity equivalence
\cite[Collected Papers II, pp.~58--59]{weil1952}.  For
\begin{equation}
 F(x)=\int_\R F_0(x+t)\overline{F_0(t)}\,dt,
 \qquad
 \Phi(s)=\Phi_0(s)\overline{\Phi_0(1-\overline{s})},
 \label{eq:weil-positive-test}
\end{equation}
$L(s)$ satisfies RH if and only if the common value of \eqref{eq:weil-11} is nonnegative for
every $F_0$ satisfying (A) and (B).  The converse is constructive.  From an off-line zero
$\omega_0=\beta_0+i\gamma_0$, set
\[
 z=i(s-\tfrac12-i\gamma_0),\qquad
 \eta=i(\omega-\tfrac12-i\gamma_0).
\]
Choose a real polynomial $Q$ vanishing at the bounded competing $\eta$'s but not at the
selected pair, put $P(z)=zQ(z)Q(-z)$, and use
$\Psi_0(z)=P(z)e^{-Az^2}$.  For sufficiently large $A$, the explicitly displayed negative
selected-zero contribution dominates the Gaussian tail.  Thus the converse is not merely an
abstract separation assertion.

The global form fixes the Haar normalizations rather than suppressing them:
\[
 d^\times x=\frac{dx}{2|x|}\quad(k_v=\R),\qquad
 d^\times x=\frac{dr\,d\theta}{\pi r}\quad(k_v=\C),
\]
and a nonarchimedean unit group has measure $\log N\mathfrak p$.  For the positive-type
id\`ele-class function $P$, formula~(12) is
\cite[Collected Papers II, pp.~59--61, eqs.~(12)--(13)]{weil1952}
\begin{align}
 D(P)={}&\rho P(1)+\int_{C_k}P(\xi)
 (\|\xi\|^{1/2}+\|\xi\|^{-1/2})\,d\xi\notag\\
 &-\sum_v\int_{k_v^*}
 \frac{P(x)-P(1)\theta_v(\|x\|)}
 {\|x-1\|\,\|x\|^{-1/2}}\,d^\times x,
 \label{eq:weil-12}
\end{align}
where
\begin{equation}
 \rho=\log|\Delta|+\log(2\pi)^{-d}
 -\sum_\lambda\operatorname{PF}\!\int_\R
 \theta_\lambda(e^{-x})K_{\eta_\lambda,0}(x)\,dx.
 \label{eq:weil-13}
\end{equation}
$D$ is independent of the permitted $\theta_v$ choices and is of positive type if and only
if all Hecke $L$-functions over $k$ satisfy RH.  In the one-dimensional function-field case,
$\rho=(2g-2)\log q$, and positivity holds because the corresponding theorem is known.

\subsubsection{Montgomery's Poisson-weighted presentation}

Montgomery's 1973 paper assumes RH and writes every nontrivial zero as
$\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma$ with $\gamma\in\R$.  For $1<\sigma<2$, $x\geq1$, and
$\tau=|t|+2$, its retained explicit formula is
\cite[pp.~185--187, eqs.~(18)--(22)]{montgomery1973}
\begin{align}
 &(2\sigma-1)\sum_\gamma
 \frac{x^{i\gamma}}{(\sigma-\tfrac12)^2+(t-\gamma)^2} \notag\\
 &=-x^{-1/2}\left(
   \sum_{n\leq x}\Lambda(n)(x/n)^{1-\sigma+it}
   +\sum_{n>x}\Lambda(n)(x/n)^{\sigma+it}\right) \notag\\
 &\quad-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(1-\sigma+it)x^{1/2-\sigma+it}
 +\frac{x^{1/2}(2\sigma-1)}{(\sigma-1+it)(\sigma-it)} \notag\\
 &\quad-x^{-1/2}\sum_{m\geq1}
 \frac{(2\sigma-1)x^{-2m}}
 { (\sigma-1-it-2m)(\sigma+it+2m)}.
 \label{eq:montgomery-explicit-full}
\end{align}
The two prime ranges, logarithmic-derivative term, pole term, and trivial-zero series are
distinct terms.  The derivation first excludes $x=1$ and prime powers in Landau's identity;
continuity after subtraction restores every $x\geq1$.  The functional equation gives
\[
 \frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(1-\sigma+it)
 =-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma-it)-\log\tau+O_\sigma(1),
\]
so \eqref{eq:montgomery-explicit-full} also yields the same two prime sums together with
\[
 x^{1/2-\sigma+it}\bigl(\log\tau+O_\sigma(1)\bigr)
 +O_\sigma(x^{1/2}\tau^{-2})+O_\sigma(x^{-2}\tau^{-1}).
\]

For the later $\sigma=\tfrac32$ coordinate presentation, define
\begin{equation}
 a_n(x)=\min\!\left((n/x)^{1/2},(x/n)^{3/2}\right).
 \label{eq:montgomery-an}
\end{equation}
Multiplying the specialization of \eqref{eq:montgomery-explicit-full} by
$x^{1/2-it}$ gives Goldston's algebraically equivalent form
\cite[eqs.~(3.11)--(3.14)]{goldston2004}
\begin{align}
 2x^{1/2-it}\sum_\gamma\frac{x^{i\gamma}}{1+(t-\gamma)^2}
 ={}&-\sum_{n\geq1}\frac{\Lambda(n)a_n(x)}{n^{it}}
 +\frac{2x^{1-it}}{(\tfrac12+it)(\tfrac32-it)} \notag\\
 &+x^{-1/2}\bigl(\log(|t|+2)+O(1)\bigr)
 +O\!\left(\frac{x^{-2}}{|t|+2}\right).
 \label{eq:montgomery-explicit}
\end{align}
Goldston separately observes that one can write every zero as
$\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma$ with complex $\gamma$ to obtain an unconditional identity.  That
complex-coordinate convention cannot be substituted into later real-ordinate estimates
without rechecking them.

\subsection{Exact logarithmic-coordinate morphisms}

\begin{proposition}[Additive--multiplicative coordinate isomorphism]
The map
\[
 E:(\R,+)\longrightarrow(\R_{>0},\cdot),\qquad E(u)=e^u,
\]
is a real-analytic group isomorphism with inverse $L(x)=\log x$.  It transports Haar
coordinates by $dx/x=du$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$E(u+v)=E(u)E(v)$.  Strict monotonicity and the range of the real exponential give a
singleton fibre over each $x>0$ and the two-sided inverse $L$.  Differentiating
$x=e^u$ gives $dx=e^u du=xdu$.
\end{proof}

Fix $c\in\R$ and put $g_c(u)=e^{cu}f(e^u)$.  Whenever the displayed integrals are
absolutely convergent, the same coordinate map gives
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde f(c+it)
 =\int_\R g_c(u)e^{itu}\,du.
 \label{eq:mellin-fourier-interface}
\end{equation}
Thus, for the convention
$\mathcal F_-[g](r)=\int_\R g(u)e^{-iru}\,du$,
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde f(c+it)=\mathcal F_-[g_c](-t).
 \label{eq:mellin-fourier-sign}
\end{equation}
The sign and the $1/(2\pi)$ in \eqref{eq:bogomolny-fourier} are therefore convention data,
not factors to be silently removed.  The inverse coordinate formula is
$f(x)=x^{-c}g_c(\log x)$; Fourier inversion has its own regularity hypotheses.

There is also an exact convolution morphism.  For
\begin{equation}
 (f*_\times h)(x)=\int_0^\infty f(y)h(x/y)\frac{dy}{y},
 \qquad U(f)(u)=f(e^u),
\end{equation}
absolute convergence and $y=e^v$ give
\begin{equation}
 U(f*_\times h)(u)=\int_\R U(f)(v)U(h)(u-v)\,dv
 =(U(f)*_+U(h))(u).
 \label{eq:convolution-interface}
\end{equation}
For Weil's normalization~\cite[Collected Papers II, pp.~52--58]{weil1952}
there is a particularly exact specialization.  Put
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal B(F)(v)=v^{-1/2}F(\log v),
 \qquad
 \mathcal B^{-1}(\phi)(x)=e^{x/2}\phi(e^x).
 \label{eq:weil-log-bijection}
\end{equation}
These are two-sided inverses and
\[
 \int_0^\infty\mathcal B(F)(v)v^{s-1}\,dv=\Phi(s).
\]
Condition (B) transports exactly to
$\mathcal B(F)(v)=O(v^b)$ at $0+$ and $O(v^{-1-b})$ at infinity; jump locations
$a_i$ map bijectively to $e^{a_i}$.  No fibre is merged.

Now restrict to half-line functions for which the following even extension satisfies Weil's
conditions:
\begin{equation}
 E(f)(x)=\frac{f(|x|)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}.
 \label{eq:guinand-weil-even-map}
\end{equation}
The inverse on the even subspace is $f(t)=\sqrt{2\pi}E(f)(t)$ for $t\geq0$, and
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_{E(f)}(\tfrac12+iy)
 =\int_\R E(f)(x)e^{iyx}\,dx
 =\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\int_0^\infty f(x)\cos(xy)\,dx
 =g(y).
 \label{eq:guinand-weil-transform}
\end{equation}
This proves the transform factor rather than normalizing it away.  Weil's exponential decay
also gives $T^{1/2}f(T)\to0$, so Guinand's boundary term
\cite[pp.~112--115, Thm.~3$'$]{guinand1948} vanishes on this explicit
intersection subspace by Theorem~$3'$.

For $k=\Q$ and the trivial character,
\[
 d=1,\quad\Delta=1,\quad N\mathfrak f=1,\quad A=(2\pi)^{-1},
 \quad(\eta_1,f_1,\varphi_1)=(1,0,0).
\]
Consequently, the finite-place and pole coordinates specialize term by term as
\begin{align}
 &\sum_p\sum_{m\geq1}\frac{\log p}{p^{m/2}}
 \{E(f)(m\log p)+E(f)(-m\log p)\}
 =\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\sum_p\sum_{m\geq1}
 \frac{\log p}{p^{m/2}}f(m\log p),
 \label{eq:guinand-weil-prime-map}\\
 &\int_\R E(f)(x)(e^{x/2}+e^{-x/2})\,dx
 =\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\int_0^\infty
 f(x)(e^{x/2}+e^{-x/2})\,dx.
 \label{eq:guinand-weil-pole-map}
\end{align}
Under RH, evenness pairs $\gamma$ and $-\gamma$.  There is no central zeta zero: the
alternating eta series at $1/2$ has strictly positive paired terms, while
$1-\sqrt2<0$, so $\zeta(1/2)<0$.  Hence
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{|\gamma|<T}\Phi_{E(f)}(\tfrac12+i\gamma)
 =2\sum_{0<\gamma<T}g(\gamma).
 \label{eq:guinand-weil-zero-map}
\end{equation}
For a different self-dual function with a central zero, its multiplicity times $g(0)$ would
remain as an exceptional term.

Subtracting the two primary formulae only after
\eqref{eq:guinand-weil-prime-map}--\eqref{eq:guinand-weil-zero-map} gives an exact residual
archimedean identity.  With
\[
 K(x)=\frac{e^{|x|/2}}{|e^x-e^{-x}|},\qquad
 L_g=\lim_{T\to\infty}\int_0^Tg(t)\log\frac{t}{2\pi}\,dt,
\]
one obtains
\begin{align}
 &2\int_0^\infty f(x)e^{-x/2}\,dx
 -\operatorname{PF}\!\int_\R f(|x|)K(x)\,dx-f(0)\log(2\pi)\notag\\
 &\qquad=2\mathcal H_f+\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\,L_g.
 \label{eq:guinand-weil-arch-map}
\end{align}
This is proved on the stated common subspace as an algebraic corollary of the two primary
theorems.  A direct distribution-level proof of
\eqref{eq:guinand-weil-arch-map}, independent of subtracting them, remains a formalized
analytic obligation.

There is also an exact candidate for Montgomery's Poisson kernel, using the retained
Montgomery kernel~\cite[pp.~185--187, eqs.~(18)--(22)]{montgomery1973}.  For
\[
 a=\sigma-\tfrac12,\qquad u=\log x,\qquad
 F_{a,t,u}(v)=e^{-a|v-u|}e^{-it(v-u)},
\]
where $1<\sigma<2$ and $x\geq1$, condition (B) holds and direct integration gives
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_{a,t,u}(s)=
 \frac{2a\,x^{s-1/2}}{a^2-(s-1/2-it)^2}.
 \label{eq:montgomery-weil-test}
\end{equation}
Therefore
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_{a,t,u}(\tfrac12+i\gamma)=
 \frac{(2\sigma-1)x^{i\gamma}}
 { (\sigma-\tfrac12)^2+(t-\gamma)^2},
 \label{eq:montgomery-weil-zero-kernel}
\end{equation}
exactly Montgomery's zero kernel.  At a prime power $n$,
\begin{align*}
 n^{-1/2}F(\log n)&=
 \begin{cases}
 x^{1/2-\sigma+it}n^{\sigma-1-it},&n\leq x,\\
 x^{\sigma-1/2+it}n^{-\sigma-it},&n>x,
 \end{cases}\\
 n^{-1/2}F(-\log n)&=x^{1/2-\sigma+it}n^{-\sigma+it},
\end{align*}
so both of Montgomery's prime ranges and the $\zeta'/\zeta(\sigma-it)$ series are recovered
without approximation.  Moreover,
\[
 \Phi_{a,t,u}(1)=
 \frac{x^{1/2}(2\sigma-1)}{(\sigma-1+it)(\sigma-it)},
\]
which is exactly his rational pole term.  What is still open is one explicit commutator:
$\Phi(0)$, $F(0)\log A$, Weil's real-place PF term, and the negative-log prime series must be
shown from the zeta functional equation and the gamma partial fractions to equal
Montgomery's $-x^{1/2-\sigma+it}\zeta'/\zeta(1-\sigma+it)$ plus his complete trivial-zero
series.  No term is discarded while that identity remains open.

\subsection{The pair-correlation hierarchy}

Assume RH for this subsection, as Montgomery does throughout the 1973 paper
\cite[pp.~181--192]{montgomery1973}, and count
zeros with multiplicity.  Put
\begin{equation}
 w(u)=\frac4{4+u^2},
 \qquad
 c_T=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log T,
 \qquad T\geq2,
 \label{eq:montgomery-weight-normalization}
\end{equation}
and retain both of the following presentations:
\begin{equation}
 F(x,T)=\sum_{0<\gamma,\gamma'\leq T}
 x^{i(\gamma-\gamma')}w(\gamma-\gamma'),
 \qquad x>0,
 \label{eq:F-xT}
\end{equation}
and Montgomery's normalized form
\begin{equation}
 F_M(\alpha,T)=c_T^{-1}
 \sum_{0<\gamma,\gamma'\leq T}
 T^{i\alpha(\gamma-\gamma')}w(\gamma-\gamma').
 \label{eq:F-alphaT}
\end{equation}
For each retained $T>1$, $x=T^\alpha$ is a bijection
$\R\to\R_{>0}$ with inverse $\alpha=(\log x)/(\log T)$, and
\[
 F_M(\alpha,T)=c_T^{-1}F(T^\alpha,T).
\]
This coordinate bijection does not erase either presentation.

\begin{theorem}[Montgomery's theorem as printed in 1973]
\cite[pp.~181 and~187--191, eqs.~(2) and~(18)--(34)]{montgomery1973}
The function $F_M(\alpha,T)$ is real and even.  For every $\varepsilon>0$ there is
$T_0(\varepsilon)$ such that
\[
 F_M(\alpha,T)\geq-\varepsilon
 \qquad(\alpha\in\R,\ T>T_0(\varepsilon)).
\]
For fixed $\varepsilon>0$, uniformly for $0\leq\alpha\leq1-\varepsilon$,
\begin{equation}
 F_M(\alpha,T)=\alpha+o(1)+(1+o(1))T^{-2\alpha}\log T
 \qquad(T\to\infty).
 \label{eq:montgomery-range}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The complete primary paper has now been content-read~\cite{montgomery1973}.  It says that
more effort could extend the uniform estimate through
$\alpha=1$, but its printed proof establishes \eqref{eq:montgomery-range} on
$[0,1-\varepsilon]$.

Two later refinements must remain later refinements.  Goldston's exact identity
\cite[eqs.~(4.1)--(4.11)]{goldston2004}
\begin{equation}
 F(x,T)=\frac2\pi\int_\R
 \left|\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}
 \frac{x^{i\gamma}}{1+(t-\gamma)^2}\right|^2dt
 \label{eq:goldston-positive-square}
\end{equation}
proves $F(x,T)\geq0$ and $F(x,T)=F(x^{-1},T)$; a sieve refinement recorded by
Goldston extends the asymptotic range through $\alpha=1$.  Neither result is silently
retrojected into the weaker literal theorem statement above.

\subsubsection{Fourier conventions, pair measure, and support loss}

Montgomery uses~\cite[pp.~181--182, eqs.~(3)--(6)]{montgomery1973}
\[
 \widehat r_M(\alpha)=\int_\R r(u)e^{-2\pi i\alpha u}\,du,
 \qquad
 r(u)=\int_\R\widehat r_M(\alpha)e^{2\pi i\alpha u}\,d\alpha,
\]
whereas Goldston uses~\cite[Sec.~5]{goldston2004}
\[
 \widehat r_G(\alpha)=\int_\R r(u)e^{2\pi i\alpha u}\,du,
 \qquad
 r(u)=\int_\R\widehat r_G(\alpha)e^{-2\pi i\alpha u}\,d\alpha.
\]
The convention morphism is the involution
\begin{equation}
 \widehat r_G(\alpha)=\widehat r_M(-\alpha).
 \label{eq:fourier-sign-reflection}
\end{equation}
Because $F_M$ is even, changing $\alpha$ to $-\alpha$ proves that the two resulting
pairings agree.  Under Montgomery's convention the exact bridge is
\begin{align}
 &\sum_{0<\gamma,\gamma'\leq T}
 r\!\left((\gamma-\gamma')\frac{\log T}{2\pi}\right)w(\gamma-\gamma') \notag\\
 &\hspace{30mm}=c_T
 \int_\R\widehat r_M(\alpha)F_M(\alpha,T)\,d\alpha.
 \label{eq:test-pair-bridge}
\end{align}
The primary theorem mainly permits
$\operatorname{supp}\widehat r_M\subseteq[-1+\delta,1-\delta]$; Goldston's endpoint
refinement permits support through $[-1,1]$.

This boundary can be stated as an exact measure map.  Define
\begin{equation}
 \mu_T=c_T^{-1}\sum_{0<\gamma,\gamma'\leq T}
 w(\gamma-\gamma')\,
 \delta_{(\gamma-\gamma')\log T/(2\pi)}.
 \label{eq:weighted-pair-measure}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 F_M(\alpha,T)=\int_\R e^{2\pi i\alpha u}\,d\mu_T(u),
 \qquad
 \int_\R r(u)\,d\mu_T(u)
 =\int_\R F_M(\alpha,T)\widehat r_M(\alpha)\,d\alpha.
 \label{eq:pair-measure-transform}
\end{equation}
The full Fourier--Stieltjes transform is injective on finite measures, but restriction to
$[-1,1]$ is not injective on finite signed measures.  Indeed, choose a nonzero even real
$\phi\in C_c^\infty((-3,-2)\cup(2,3))$, let $g$ be its inverse Fourier transform, and put
$d\nu=g(u)\,du$.  Then $g$ is real Schwartz, $\nu\neq0$, and
$\widehat\nu=\phi$ vanishes on $[-1,1]$.  Thus $\mu$ and $\mu+\nu$ have identical
restricted transforms in the signed-measure category.  No stronger fibre claim inside the
positive-measure cone is being made.  In particular, the support-limited theorem does not
determine arbitrary interval indicators or the full pair measure.

\subsubsection{The two finite-height spacing coordinates}

Montgomery's test coordinate~\cite{montgomery1973} and the local mean-density coordinate
used in the random-matrix comparison~\cite{keatingSnaith2000} are respectively
\begin{equation}
 u_M=\frac{\log T}{2\pi}(\gamma-\gamma'),
 \qquad
 u_D=\frac{\log(T/(2\pi))}{2\pi}(\gamma-\gamma').
 \label{eq:two-unfoldings}
\end{equation}
For $T\neq2\pi$ their exact transition and inverse are
\begin{equation}
 u_D=\frac{\log(T/(2\pi))}{\log T}\,u_M,
 \qquad
 u_M=\frac{\log T}{\log(T/(2\pi))}\,u_D.
 \label{eq:unfolding-transition}
\end{equation}
The fibres are singletons for fixed $T\geq2$, $T\neq2\pi$.  At $T=2\pi$, $u_D$
collapses every difference to zero while $u_M$ remains bijective.  For
$2\leq T<2\pi$ the transition reverses orientation; for $T>2\pi$ it preserves
orientation.  Its factor tends to one as $T\to\infty$, but it is not replaced by one at
finite height.

\subsubsection{Conjectures, orientations, and the diagonal}

Montgomery's literal signed-interval conjecture is: for fixed real $a<b$
\cite[pp.~183--184, eqs.~(11)--(15)]{montgomery1973},
\begin{align}
 &\sum_{\substack{0<\gamma,\gamma'\leq T\\
  2\pi a/\log T\leq\gamma-\gamma'\leq2\pi b/\log T}}1 \notag\\
 &\quad\sim\left[
 \int_a^b\left(1-\left(\frac{\sin\pi u}{\pi u}\right)^2\right)du
 +\delta(a,b)\right]c_T,
 \label{eq:montgomery-signed-PCC}
\end{align}
where $\delta(a,b)=1$ when $0\in[a,b]$ and is zero otherwise.  The extra term is
exactly the diagonal $\gamma=\gamma'$.  The paper separately proposes
$F_M(\alpha,T)=1+o(1)$ for $\alpha\geq1$, uniformly in bounded intervals, and calls
the two assertions essentially equivalent; that phrase is not a printed biconditional proof.

Goldston later separates the following two conjectures
\cite[Sec.~6, eqs.~(6.6)--(6.9)]{goldston2004}:
\begin{description}
  \item[Strong pair correlation (SPC).]
  For each fixed bounded $M$,
  $F_M(\alpha,T)=1+o(1)$ for $1\leq\alpha\leq M$.
  A strong Hardy--Littlewood prime-pair input motivates a bounded subrange but does not turn
  the full statement into the preceding theorem.
  \item[Interval pair correlation (PCC).]
  For fixed $\beta>0$, the scaled count of pairs with
  $0<\gamma'-\gamma\leq2\pi\beta/\log T$ is conjectured to tend to
  \begin{equation}
    \int_0^\beta\left(1-\left(\frac{\sin\pi u}{\pi u}\right)^2\right)du.
    \label{eq:PCC}
  \end{equation}
\end{description}
Goldston proves, under the real-ordinate hypothesis, that SPC implies PCC together with the
corresponding average-multiplicity statement.  This implication does not identify SPC and
PCC.  For $0<a<b$, swapping $(\gamma,\gamma')$ is an exact bijection between the two
opposite signed orientations.  If the interval contains zero, the diagonal term must instead
be retained or explicitly subtracted.  Montgomery's primary paper itself therefore confirms
that Schumayer--Hutchinson's pair-correlation discussion collapses a conjecture into the
proved theorem~\cite[pair-correlation discussion]{schumayerHutchinson2011}.
The conditional simple-zero consequence remains the support-limited bound
$(2/3-o(1))N(T)$; the primary gap calculation explicitly permits the constant $0.68$,
whereas Goldston's later exposition reports $0.669\ldots$.

\subsection{GUE, CUE, and the maps that are still missing}

In the bulk, after scaling a GUE spectrum to unit mean density and sending the matrix size to
infinity, the correlation functions have the determinantal presentation
\cite[random-matrix correlation discussion]{bogomolny2007}
\begin{equation}
 R_n(E_1,\dots,E_n)=\det\bigl[K(E_i,E_j)\bigr]_{i,j=1}^n,
 \qquad
 K(E_i,E_j)=\frac{\sin\pi(E_i-E_j)}{\pi(E_i-E_j)}.
 \label{eq:GUE-kernel}
\end{equation}
In particular, $R_2(u)=1-(\sin(\pi u)/(\pi u))^2$.  This explains the comparison with
\eqref{eq:PCC} and \eqref{eq:montgomery-signed-PCC}.  Montgomery reports that Dyson
identified this same pair function for large random complex Hermitian or unitary matrices,
and then proposes the higher-correlation determinant in \eqref{eq:GUE-kernel}
\cite[pp.~184--185, eqs.~(15)--(17)]{montgomery1973}.  This records
the historical bridge from the primary zeta paper; the cited Mehta random-matrix source has
not yet been independently content-read.  The bridge supplies no map from an individual
zeta zero to an individual matrix eigenvalue and constructs no fixed operator.

For a retained height $T>2\pi$, the exact affine scaling map is
\begin{equation}
 U_T(\gamma)=\frac{\log(T/(2\pi))}{2\pi}(\gamma-T),
 \qquad
 U_T^{-1}(u)=T+\frac{2\pi}{\log(T/(2\pi))}u.
 \label{eq:unfolding-map}
\end{equation}
For fixed $T$ this map has singleton fibres.  If $T$ is forgotten, or if only differences are
retained, absolute height and a common translation are lost.  At $T=2\pi$ the scale vanishes.
The asymptotic convergence of the resulting point configurations to GUE statistics is a
separate conjectural statement.  Conversely, the height dependence of
\eqref{eq:unfolding-map} does not prove that a fixed spectral operator cannot exist.

\paragraph{Finite CUE theorem.}
CUE enters through a different finite object.  For $U\in U(N)$ with normalized Haar measure
and $\theta\in\R$, put
\begin{equation}
 Z_N(U;\theta)=\det(I_N-Ue^{-i\theta}).
 \label{eq:CUE-characteristic}
\end{equation}
Keating--Snaith prove, for $\Re s>-1$
\cite[pp.~57--64, eqs.~(1), (6), and~(20)--(26)]{keatingSnaith2000},
\begin{equation}
 M_N(s):=\mathbb E_{U(N)}|Z_N(U;\theta)|^s
 =\prod_{j=1}^N
 \frac{\Gamma(j)\Gamma(j+s)}{\Gamma(j+s/2)^2}.
 \label{eq:CUE-moment-s}
\end{equation}
Their proof inserts the CUE joint eigenphase density and evaluates the resulting $N$-fold
integral by Selberg's integral.  The product has meromorphic continuation in $s$; outside
$\Re s>-1$ that continuation is not automatically the original Haar expectation.  The exact
parameter morphism $s=2\lambda$ has inverse $\lambda=s/2$ and transports the moment domain
to $\Re\lambda>-1/2$.  Thus \eqref{eq:CUE-moment-s} becomes
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E_{U(N)}|Z_N(U;\theta)|^{2\lambda}
 =\prod_{j=1}^N
 \frac{\Gamma(j)\Gamma(j+2\lambda)}{\Gamma(j+\lambda)^2}.
 \label{eq:CUE-moment}
\end{equation}

They also retain the branch data needed for the phase-ratio moment and obtain
\cite[eqs.~(7) and~(27)--(33)]{keatingSnaith2000}
\begin{equation}
 L_N(s):=\mathbb E_{U(N)}
 \left(\frac{Z_N(U;\theta)}{Z_N(U;\theta)^*}\right)^{s/2}
 =\prod_{j=1}^N
 \frac{\Gamma(j)^2}{\Gamma(j+s/2)\Gamma(j-s/2)}.
 \label{eq:CUE-phase-moment}
\end{equation}
The branch is defined by continuous variation along $\theta-i\varepsilon$ away from
eigenphases.  Erasing this prescription changes the object.

\paragraph{Large-$N$ theorem and log-characteristic-polynomial limit.}
For fixed $\lambda$ in the initial moment domain
\cite[eqs.~(15)--(16) and~(80)--(88)]{keatingSnaith2000},
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{N\to\infty}N^{-\lambda^2}
 \mathbb E_{U(N)}|Z_N(U;\theta)|^{2\lambda}
 =\frac{G(1+\lambda)^2}{G(1+2\lambda)}.
 \label{eq:CUE-Barnes}
\end{equation}
For integers $k\geq1$, this coefficient is
\begin{equation}
 f_{\mathrm{CUE}}(k)=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{j!}{(j+k)!},
 \label{eq:CUE-integer-coefficient}
\end{equation}
giving $1$, $1/12$, $42/9!$, and $24024/16!$ at $k=1,2,3,4$.
The same primary paper proves that
\cite[eqs.~(9)--(14) and~(34)--(79)]{keatingSnaith2000}
\begin{equation}
 \left(
 \frac{\Re\log Z_N}{\sqrt{\tfrac12\log N}},
 \frac{\Im\log Z_N}{\sqrt{\tfrac12\log N}}
 \right)
 \Longrightarrow (X,Y),
 \label{eq:CUE-joint-CLT}
\end{equation}
where $X$ and $Y$ are independent standard normal variables.  The proof differentiates the
exact generating products, computes polygamma cumulants, and shows that every normalized
higher pure or mixed cumulant vanishes.  At finite $N$, independence is not asserted.

For $\beta=1,2,4$, corresponding respectively to COE, CUE, and CSE, the same Selberg
calculation gives the retained family
\cite[eqs.~(109)--(111)]{keatingSnaith2000}
\begin{equation}
 M_N^{(\beta)}(s)=
 \prod_{j=0}^{N-1}
 \frac{\Gamma(1+j\beta/2)\Gamma(1+s+j\beta/2)}
      {\Gamma(1+s/2+j\beta/2)^2}.
 \label{eq:circular-beta-moment}
\end{equation}
At $\beta=2$, the index change $k=j+1$ recovers \eqref{eq:CUE-moment-s}.  This
compatibility does not assign an $L$-function family to the other two finite ensembles.

\paragraph{Exact spacing-parameter map and conjectural zeta transfer.}
Keating--Snaith equate the CUE mean eigenphase spacing $2\pi/N$ with the high-height
zeta-zero spacing $2\pi/\log(T/(2\pi))$.  This gives the exact continuous parameter map
\cite[eqs.~(8) and~(92)]{keatingSnaith2000}
\begin{equation}
 D:(2\pi,\infty)\longrightarrow(0,\infty),
 \qquad D(T)=\log\frac{T}{2\pi},
 \qquad D^{-1}(n)=2\pi e^n.
 \label{eq:KS-density-map}
\end{equation}
It has singleton fibres and satisfies
$2\pi/D(T)=2\pi/\log(T/(2\pi))$.  Actual matrix size is integer; applying a floor,
ceiling, or nearest-integer map is an additional lossy operation with interval fibres.  Even
before that projection, \eqref{eq:KS-density-map} maps scale parameters only: it maps neither
individual zeros to eigenphases nor zeta functions to characteristic polynomials.

For real $\lambda$ where the factors are defined, set
\begin{equation}
 a(\lambda)=\prod_p(1-p^{-1})^{\lambda^2}
 \sum_{m=0}^{\infty}
 \left(\frac{(\lambda)_m}{m!}\right)^2p^{-m}.
 \label{eq:KS-arithmetic-factor}
\end{equation}
The Keating--Snaith conjecture is
\cite[eqs.~(4)--(5), (19), and~(93)--(96)]{keatingSnaith2000}
\begin{equation}
 \frac1T\int_0^T
 \left|\zeta\!\left(\frac12+it\right)\right|^{2\lambda}dt
 \sim
 a(\lambda)\frac{G(1+\lambda)^2}{G(1+2\lambda)}
 (\log T)^{\lambda^2}.
 \label{eq:KS-moment-conjecture}
\end{equation}
The Barnes-$G$ factor is supplied by the proved CUE limit; the arithmetic factor is motivated
by a truncated Euler product and an independence model for prime contributions.  Those steps,
the transfer through \eqref{eq:KS-density-map}, and \eqref{eq:KS-moment-conjecture} are not
proved by the finite Haar integral.  The paper explicitly distinguishes local random-matrix
statistics from long-range prime corrections.  This primary reading also resolves the survey
defects: the ensemble is CUE, $1/12$ is $f_{\mathrm{CUE}}(2)$, the cited classical author is
A.~E.~Ingham, and the retained scale is $\log(T/(2\pi))$, not merely ``of order $\log T$.''

Finally, Bogomolny's finite-height two-point formula is a valuable heuristic object but not a
theorem.  It uses smoothing, a diagonal approximation, the Hardy--Littlewood prime-pair
conjecture, $\log((n+r)/n)\approx r/n$, and a sum-to-integral passage.  Its diagonal and
off-diagonal terms retain arithmetic Euler factors and approach the GUE expression after the
height-dependent scaling.  The source itself describes the error as supposed and concludes
that these random-matrix results are not rigorously proved.  The equation label
\texttt{exact\_R} therefore cannot be used as an evidence status
\cite[finite-height two-point calculation]{bogomolny2007}.

\subsection{Higher derivative CUE moments: exact exponents and the missing Lie arrow}

Grover, Mezzadri, and Simm work with Haar probability measure on $\mathrm U(N)$ and the
characteristic polynomial
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda_N(z)=\det(1-zU^\dagger).
\end{equation}
For finite lists of nonnegative integers
$\mu=(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_K)$ and $\nu=(\nu_1,\ldots,\nu_L)$, their joint derivative moment is
\begin{equation}
 M_{\mu,\nu}(z,N)
 =\mathbb E\!\left[
   \prod_{i=1}^{K}\Lambda_N^{(\mu_i)}(z)
   \prod_{j=1}^{L}\overline{\Lambda_N^{(\nu_j)}(z)}
 \right].
 \label{eq:GMS-moment-definition}
\end{equation}
Here $|\mu|=\sum_i\mu_i$, $\mu!=\prod_i\mu_i!$, and similarly for $\nu$
\cite[eq.~(1.1)]{groverMezzadriSimm2026}.
The companion Easy Riders exposition identifies this as the channel owner's recent paper
written with a PhD supervisor and collaborator, describes the work as primarily random
matrix theory, and gives the author's geometric account of the global, mesoscopic, and
microscopic probe-point regimes.  In that account, the microscopic scale moves the probe
toward the unit circle at the eigenvalue-spacing scale and is the regime compared with the
zeta function near its critical-line zeros
\cite[0:25--0:35, 3:03--3:08, and 7:44--10:52]{easyRiders2026CUE}.
A later video records a separate open random-matrix problem concerning a one-mode/two-mode
transition and the search for a human-readable analytic mechanism rather than an opaque
machine certificate \cite[3:19--6:15 and 7:20--12:08]{easyRiders2026SevenDays}.
These videos are author-side motivation and geometric exposition.  The theorem statements,
hypotheses, and proofs below are taken from the paper, while the later first-peeled
$N=3$ coordinates are independently derived in
\cref{thm:n3-base-quotient,thm:n3-physical-trace-cover}; neither video states those
coordinates.
For $\boldsymbol z=(z_1,\ldots,z_K)$ and $\boldsymbol w=(w_1,\ldots,w_L)$, retain the
source's differential and coalescence notation
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal D_{\boldsymbol z}^{\mu}
 =\prod_{i=1}^{K}\frac{\partial^{\mu_i}}{\partial z_i^{\mu_i}},
 \qquad
 \left.F(\boldsymbol z)\right|_{\boldsymbol z=z}
 =\lim_{z_1,\ldots,z_K\to z}F(\boldsymbol z),
 \label{eq:GMS-differential-coalescence}
\end{equation}
where coalescence occurs after differentiation.  Let $\mathcal M_{\mu,\cdot}$ be the set of
$K\times L$ nonnegative-integer matrices with row sums $\mu$, and let
$\mathcal M_{\cdot,\nu}$ be the set of such matrices with column sums $\nu$.  Define
\begin{equation}
 p_{n,m}(z,\overline z)
 =\sum_{r=0}^{\min(n,m)}\binom nr\binom mr z^{m-r}\overline z^{n-r}.
 \label{eq:GMS-pnm}
\end{equation}
Then for each fixed $\alpha\in(0,1)$, uniformly for
$|z|<1-N^{-\alpha}$ as $N\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}
 M_{\mu,\nu}(z,N)
 =\frac{\mu!\nu!}{(1-|z|^2)^{KL+|\mu|+|\nu|}}
 \sum_{\substack{Q\in\mathcal M_{\mu,\cdot}\\R\in\mathcal M_{\cdot,\nu}}}
 \prod_{i=1}^{K}\prod_{j=1}^{L}
 p_{Q_{ij},R_{ij}}(z,\overline z)
 +O\!\left(e^{-N^\delta}\right)
 \label{eq:GMS-mesoscopic}
\end{equation}
for some $\delta>0$~\cite[Thm.~1.1]{groverMezzadriSimm2026}.  At $z=0$ the polynomial
in \eqref{eq:GMS-pnm} is nonzero precisely when its two indices agree.  Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \frac{1}{\mu!\nu!}\lim_{N\to\infty}M_{\mu,\nu}(0,N)
 =\#\bigl(\mathcal M_{\mu,\cdot}\cap\mathcal M_{\cdot,\nu}\bigr),
 \label{eq:GMS-magic-square-limit}
\end{equation}
the exact contingency-table count recorded by the source.

The equal-length microscopic theorem has substantially more content than its exponent.  Fix
$K=L=s$, put
\begin{equation}
 z_N=1-\frac{c}{N},\qquad \tau=c+\overline c,
\end{equation}
and, for partitions $\lambda,\rho$ of lengths at most $s$, put
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_i(\lambda)=\lambda_i+s-i,\qquad
 \beta_j(\rho)=\rho_j+s-j,\qquad
 \lambda!_{(s)}=\prod_{i=1}^{s}\alpha_i(\lambda)!,
\end{equation}
with the analogous definition of $\rho!_{(s)}$, and
\begin{equation}
 I_r(\tau)=\int_0^1x^r e^{-\tau x}\,dx.
\end{equation}
When $K_{\lambda\mu}$ or $K_{\rho\nu}$ is formed, the source first rearranges the relevant
list in weakly decreasing order and discards zero entries; this Kostka-content convention is
not a change to the list lengths $K$ and $L$ in \eqref{eq:GMS-moment-definition}.
Then, uniformly for $c$ in compact subsets of $\mathbb C$,
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{N\to\infty}
 \frac{M_{\mu,\nu}(z_N,N)}{N^{|\mu|+|\nu|+s^2}}
 =\mu!\nu!
 \sum_{\substack{\lambda\vdash|\mu|,\ \ell(\lambda)\le s\\
                   \rho\vdash|\nu|,\ \ell(\rho)\le s}}
 \frac{K_{\lambda\mu}K_{\rho\nu}}{\lambda!_{(s)}\rho!_{(s)}}
 \det\!\left(I_{\alpha_i(\lambda)+\beta_j(\rho)}(\tau)\right)_{i,j=1}^{s}.
 \label{eq:GMS-microscopic-equal}
\end{equation}
At $c=0$, this is exactly
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{N\to\infty}
 \frac{M_{\mu,\nu}(1,N)}{N^{|\mu|+|\nu|+s^2}}
 =\mu!\nu!
 \sum_{\substack{\lambda\vdash|\mu|,\ \ell(\lambda)\le s\\
                   \rho\vdash|\nu|,\ \ell(\rho)\le s}}
 \frac{K_{\lambda\mu}K_{\rho\nu}}{\lambda!_{(s)}\rho!_{(s)}}
 \det\!\left(
 \frac{1}{\lambda_i+\rho_j+2s-i-j+1}
 \right)_{i,j=1}^{s}.
 \label{eq:GMS-microscopic-circle}
\end{equation}
Both right-hand sides are strictly positive~\cite[Thm.~1.2]{groverMezzadriSimm2026}.
The proof passes through an exact finite-$N$ Kostka/determinant formula, a confluent
determinant identity, the microscopic derivative asymptotic, and an Andr\'eief--Schur
positivity argument~\cite[Sec.~2]{groverMezzadriSimm2026}.

The unequal-length coordinates show where the quadratic term comes from.  They also expose a
sign defect in version~1 of the source.  If $K\ge L$, put $d=K-L$, set
$z_i=1-a_i/N$ and $w_j=1-b_j/N$, and let $\Omega$ be the $K\times K$ matrix whose $i$th row is
\begin{equation}
 \left(1,a_i,\ldots,a_i^{K-L-1},
 I(a_i+b_1),\ldots,I(a_i+b_L)\right),\qquad
 I(x)=\frac{1-e^{-x}}{x}.
\end{equation}
With the source's convention
$\Delta(\boldsymbol a)=\prod_{i<j}(a_j-a_i)$ and with the excess columns displayed before the
kernel columns, the corrected formula is
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{N\to\infty}
 \frac{M_{\mu,\nu}(1-c/N,N)}{N^{|\mu|+|\nu|+KL}}
 =(-1)^{|\mu|+|\nu|+L(K-L)}
 \left.
 \mathcal D_{\boldsymbol a}^{\mu}\mathcal D_{\boldsymbol b}^{\nu}
 \frac{\det\Omega}{\Delta(\boldsymbol a)\Delta(\boldsymbol b)}
 \right|_{\boldsymbol a=c,\ \boldsymbol b=\overline c}.
 \label{eq:GMS-microscopic-unequal}
\end{equation}
Version~1 prints the same display without $(-1)^{L(K-L)}$
\cite[Sec.~2, theorem labelled \texttt{micro}]{groverMezzadriSimm2026}.  The missing parity is
not a matter of normalization: the exact $K=2,L=1,N=1$ Haar integral has the opposite sign
from the printed determinant quotient.  The proof-bearing audit below derives the finite-$N$
factor by padding the equal-length determinant with zero coordinates, proves
\eqref{eq:GMS-microscopic-unequal} coordinate by coordinate, and records the counterexample.
All equal-length formulas have $K-L=0$ and are therefore unchanged.  The power of $N$ is still
obtained term by term: the derivative change of variables contributes $|\mu|+|\nu|$, and the
two Vandermonde factors, the $L$ kernel columns, and the $K-L$ excess columns contribute $KL$.

The number-theoretic statement is separate.  Assuming the Lindel\"of hypothesis, the authors
prove, as $\sigma\downarrow\tfrac12$,
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1T\int_1^T
 \prod_{i=1}^{K}\zeta^{(\mu_i)}(\sigma+it)
 \prod_{j=1}^{L}\overline{\zeta^{(\nu_j)}(\sigma+it)}\,dt
 \sim
 \frac{(-1)^{|\mu|+|\nu|}a_{\mu,\nu}h_{\mu,\nu}}
 {(2\sigma-1)^{KL+|\mu|+|\nu|}},
 \label{eq:GMS-zeta-off-line}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
 a_{\mu,\nu}
 &=\prod_p\left\{(1-p^{-1})^{KL}
 \sum_{m\ge0}
 \frac{\Gamma(K+m)}{m!\Gamma(K)}
 \frac{\Gamma(L+m)}{m!\Gamma(L)}p^{-m}\right\},\\
 h_{\mu,\nu}
 &=\mu!\nu!
 \sum_{\substack{Q\in\mathcal M_{\mu,\cdot}\\R\in\mathcal M_{\cdot,\nu}}}
 \prod_{i=1}^{K}\prod_{j=1}^{L}
 \binom{Q_{ij}+R_{ij}}{Q_{ij}}.
\end{align}
This is unconditional when $K,L\le2$.  In particular, for every positive integer $k$ the
source proves unconditionally that
\begin{align}
 \lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1T\int_1^T
 \left|\zeta^{(k)}(\sigma+it)\right|^4\,dt
 &\sim \frac{6}{\pi^2}\frac{h_k}{(2\sigma-1)^{4(k+1)}},
 \qquad \sigma\downarrow\tfrac12,\label{eq:GMS-zeta-fourth}\\
 h_k&=(k!)^4(k+1)
 \left\{\binom{4k+3}{2k+2}-2\binom{2k+1}{k}^2\right\}.
 \label{eq:GMS-hk}
\end{align}

$\sigma=\tfrac12+c/\log T$ and transporting the coefficient from

\subsubsection{Exact CMV/Verblunsky coordinates behind the derivative peel}

The local peeling notes preserve two different source layers that must not be conflated.
Grover--Mezzadri--Simm supply the derivative-moment theorems above.  Killip--Nenciu supply
the finite Haar spectral-measure coordinates: the Szeg\H{o} recurrences, the independent
Verblunsky laws, and the CMV realization of the circular ensemble
\cite[eqs.~(2.1)--(2.2), Prop.~3.3, Thm.~1, and App.~B]{killipNenciu2004}.
The formulas denoted $T_n$ and $\eta_n^{(r)}$ in the local notes were attributed there to
video screenshots.  The following calculation derives the actual Haar ladder from the
Killip--Nenciu coordinates and therefore makes the mathematical dependency independently
checkable.  It does not assert that Killip--Nenciu printed the final $T_n$ notation.

\begin{theorem}[Exact Haar--Verblunsky peel and telescoping derivative]
\label{thm:verblunsky-peel-derived}
Let $N\geq1$.  For a spectral measure of a Haar-distributed $U\in\mathrm U(N)$, let
$\Phi_0=1$ and let $\Phi_n$ be the monic orthogonal polynomials in the Killip--Nenciu
convention
\begin{align}
 \Phi_{n+1}(z)&=z\Phi_n(z)-\overline{\alpha_n}\Phi_n^*(z),
 \label{eq:KN-szego-forward}\\
 \Phi_{n+1}^*(z)&=\Phi_n^*(z)-\alpha_n z\Phi_n(z).
 \label{eq:KN-szego-reversed}
\end{align}
Almost surely,
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_n\sim\Theta_{2N-2n-1}\quad(0\leq n\leq N-2),
 \qquad
 \alpha_{N-1}\sim\operatorname{Unif}\{z:|z|=1\},
 \label{eq:KN-alpha-laws}
\end{equation}
and these variables are independent.  Define, for $0\leq n\leq N-1$,
\begin{equation}
 A_n=\Phi_n(1),\qquad
 u_n=\frac{\Phi_n'(1)}{\Phi_n(1)},\qquad
 \beta_n=\overline{\alpha_n}\frac{\overline{A_n}}{A_n}.
 \label{eq:peel-triangular-coordinates}
\end{equation}
Then the following statements hold.

\begin{enumerate}
 \item The triangular coordinate map
 \begin{equation}
  \mathcal K_N:(\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_{N-1})\longmapsto
  (\beta_0,\ldots,\beta_{N-1})
  \label{eq:KN-triangular-map}
 \end{equation}
 is a bijection of $\mathbb D^{N-1}\times\{z:|z|=1\}$.  Its inverse is explicit:
 \begin{equation}
  A_0=1,\qquad A_n=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(1-\beta_j),\qquad
  \alpha_n=\overline{\beta_n}\frac{\overline{A_n}}{A_n}.
  \label{eq:KN-triangular-inverse}
 \end{equation}
 It transports the product law in \eqref{eq:KN-alpha-laws} to independent variables
 \begin{equation}
  d\mu_{N-n-1}(\beta_n)
  =\frac{N-n-1}{\pi}(1-|\beta_n|^2)^{N-n-2}d^2\beta_n
  \quad(0\leq n\leq N-2),
  \label{eq:KN-mu-exact}
 \end{equation}
 with $\beta_{N-1}$ uniform on the unit circle.  Thus the local measure
 $\mu_m$ is literally $\Theta_{2m+1}$, not an asymptotic or normalized surrogate.

 \item For $0\leq n\leq N-2$, the logarithmic-derivative state obeys exactly
 \begin{equation}
  u_{n+1}=T_n(\beta_n;u_n)
  :=\frac{1+u_n-\beta_n(n-\overline{u_n})}{1-\beta_n}.
  \label{eq:KN-derived-T}
 \end{equation}
 If $w_n=2u_n-n$, then
 \begin{equation}
  \Re w_{n+1}
  =\frac{1-|\beta_n|^2}{|1-\beta_n|^2}\bigl(1+\Re w_n\bigr).
  \label{eq:KN-half-plane-propagation}
 \end{equation}
 Consequently $\Re w_0=0$ and $\Re w_n>0$ for $1\leq n\leq N-1$.

 \item On the actual size-$N$ ladder, put
 \begin{equation}
  \eta_n(u)=\eta_n^{(N-n-1)}(u)
  :=\frac{N-1-\overline u}{N-n+u}.
  \label{eq:KN-derived-eta}
 \end{equation}
 Every denominator in \eqref{eq:KN-derived-eta} is nonzero and
 $|\eta_n(u_n)|<1$.  The exact one-step identity is
 \begin{equation}
  (1-\beta_n)\bigl(N-n-1+u_{n+1}\bigr)
  =(N-n+u_n)\bigl(1-\beta_n\eta_n(u_n)\bigr).
  \label{eq:KN-one-step-telescope}
 \end{equation}

 \item The complete product telescopes to the characteristic-polynomial derivative:
 \begin{equation}
  \frac{\Phi_N'(1)}{N}
  =\prod_{n=0}^{N-1}\bigl(1-\beta_n\eta_n(u_n)\bigr).
  \label{eq:KN-complete-peel}
 \end{equation}
 Therefore, pathwise,
 \begin{equation}
  \frac{|\Phi_N'(1)|^2}{N^2}
  =\prod_{n=0}^{N-1}|1-\beta_n\eta_n(u_n)|^2,
  \label{eq:KN-complete-peel-modulus}
 \end{equation}
 where the last factor is the boundary-circle peel and the preceding factors are the
 independent disk peels.
\end{enumerate}
Finally, the Grover--Mezzadri--Simm and local characteristic polynomial conventions are
related without loss of information by
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda_N(z)=\det(1-zU^\dagger)
 =(-1)^N\overline{\det U}\,\det(zI-U)
 =(-1)^N\overline{\det U}\,\Phi_N(z).
 \label{eq:GMS-Phi-exact-phase}
\end{equation}
The factor has modulus one and is independent of $z$, so the derivative magnitudes in the
two conventions agree exactly.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Killip--Nenciu define $\Theta_\nu$ by the disk density
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\nu-1}{2\pi}(1-|z|^2)^{(\nu-3)/2}d^2z.
\end{equation}
Putting $\nu=2N-2n-1=2(N-n-1)+1$ gives \eqref{eq:KN-mu-exact}
coefficient by coefficient and exponent by exponent.  It remains to prove that the phase
change in \eqref{eq:peel-triangular-coordinates} preserves the product law.

First, $A_0=1$.  Evaluating \eqref{eq:KN-szego-forward} at $z=1$ gives
\begin{equation}
 A_{n+1}=A_n-\overline{\alpha_n}\,\overline{A_n}
 =A_n(1-\beta_n).
 \label{eq:KN-A-transition}
\end{equation}
If $n\leq N-2$, then $|\beta_n|=|\alpha_n|<1$, so induction proves $A_n\ne0$
for every $n\leq N-1$ and proves the product formula in
\eqref{eq:KN-triangular-inverse}.  Solving
$\beta_n=\overline{\alpha_n}\overline{A_n}/A_n$ for $\alpha_n$ gives the
displayed inverse, including the terminal circle coordinate.  This proves bijectivity.
Conditionally on $\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_{n-1}$, the multiplier
$\overline{A_n}/A_n$ is fixed and unimodular.  Complex conjugation followed by multiplication
by that phase preserves every radial $\Theta_{2m+1}$ law, and it preserves the terminal
uniform circle law.  Iterated conditioning therefore proves both the stated marginal laws
and independence of all the $\beta_n$.

Write $D_n=\Phi_n'(1)$.  The coefficient definition of reversal gives
\begin{equation}
 (\Phi_n^*)'(1)=n\overline{A_n}-\overline{D_n}
 =\overline{A_n}(n-\overline{u_n}).
 \label{eq:KN-star-derivative}
\end{equation}
Differentiating \eqref{eq:KN-szego-forward} at $1$ now yields
\begin{align}
 D_{n+1}
 &=A_n+D_n-\overline{\alpha_n}
   \bigl(n\overline{A_n}-\overline{D_n}\bigr)\notag\\
 &=A_n\bigl(1+u_n-\beta_n(n-\overline{u_n})\bigr).
 \label{eq:KN-D-transition}
\end{align}
Division by \eqref{eq:KN-A-transition} proves \eqref{eq:KN-derived-T}.
With $w_n=2u_n-n$, direct substitution into that transition gives
\begin{equation}
 w_{n+1}=\frac{w_n+1+\beta_n(\overline{w_n}+1)}{1-\beta_n}.
\end{equation}
Multiplication by $1-\overline{\beta_n}$ and taking real parts gives
\eqref{eq:KN-half-plane-propagation}; no inequality has been discarded.

For $u=u_n$ one has
\begin{align}
 |N-n+u|^2-|N-1-\overline u|^2
 &=(2N-n-1)(1-n+2\Re u)\notag\\
 &=(2N-n-1)(1+\Re w_n)>0.
 \label{eq:KN-eta-difference}
\end{align}
This proves simultaneously that the denominator of \eqref{eq:KN-derived-eta} is nonzero
and that its modulus is strictly less than one.  Substituting
\eqref{eq:KN-derived-T} and collecting the two terms containing $\beta_n$ gives
\begin{align}
 &(1-\beta_n)(N-n-1+u_{n+1})\notag\\
 &\qquad=N-n+u_n-\beta_n(N-1-\overline{u_n})
 =(N-n+u_n)(1-\beta_n\eta_n(u_n)),
\end{align}
which is \eqref{eq:KN-one-step-telescope}.  Multiplying it for
$0\leq n\leq N-2$, using $u_0=0$, and cancelling only the displayed nonzero
intermediate factors gives
\begin{equation}
 \prod_{n=0}^{N-2}(1-\beta_n\eta_n(u_n))
 =\frac{A_{N-1}(1+u_{N-1})}{N}.
 \label{eq:KN-partial-telescope}
\end{equation}
The terminal instance of \eqref{eq:KN-D-transition}, for which no division by
$1-\beta_{N-1}$ is needed, is
\begin{equation}
 D_N=A_{N-1}(1+u_{N-1})
 \bigl(1-\beta_{N-1}\eta_{N-1}(u_{N-1})\bigr).
\end{equation}
Together with \eqref{eq:KN-partial-telescope}, this proves
\eqref{eq:KN-complete-peel} and its squared-modulus form.

For a spectral measure supported at the $N$ eigenvalues, the monic $\Phi_N$ is their
product polynomial, hence $\Phi_N(z)=\det(zI-U)$ almost surely
\cite[finite-support discussion and App.~B]{killipNenciu2004}.  Finally,
$1-zU^\dagger=-U^\dagger(zI-U)$ proves \eqref{eq:GMS-Phi-exact-phase} by taking
determinants.
\end{proof}

There is one literal source inconsistency outside the argument just used.  In the
Killip--Nenciu proposition transferring the Jacobi measure to real Verblunsky coordinates,
the final parameter is printed as $\alpha_{2n-1}\equiv1$, whereas the same section's setup,
the theorem it proves, and the determinant calculation use $\alpha_{2n-1}=-1$
\cite[Prop.~5.3 and the surrounding discussion]{killipNenciu2004}.  The line is retained as
a source defect; no part of \cref{thm:verblunsky-peel-derived} uses it.

\subsubsection{Human prior art and the exact deformed-coordinate derivative crosswalk}

\begin{sloppypar}
The finite derivative peel must be situated against two directly relevant human-authored
probability constructions before any novelty language is used.  Bourgade--Hughes--Nikeghbali--Yor
derive a recursive Haar construction and prove, for Haar $V_N\in\mathrm U(N)$,
\begin{equation}
 \det(I_N-e^{\mathrm i\theta}V_N)\ \stackrel{\mathrm{law}}{=}
 \prod_{k=1}^{N}\left(1+e^{\mathrm i\theta_k}\sqrt{B_{1,k-1}}\right),
 \label{eq:bhn-york-independent-product}
\end{equation}
where the phases are independent uniform variables and the $B_{1,k-1}$ are independent beta
variables (with $B_{1,0}=1$), and they derive the corresponding Mellin--Fourier transform
\cite[Prop.~2.1, Prop.~2.2, eqs.~(1.1), (2.6)]{bourgadeHughesNikeghbaliYor2007}.
Their introduction explicitly places this random characteristic-polynomial model beside the
Keating--Snaith model for $\zeta(1/2+it)$; this is a sourced zeta edge, not an endpoint theorem.
They also prove the joint independent sum/product representation
\begin{equation}
 (\Im\log Z_N,|Z_N|)\ \stackrel{\mathrm{law}}{=}
 \left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}W_j,\prod_{j=1}^{N}B_{j,j-1}\,2\cos W_j\right),
 \label{eq:bhn-york-joint-product}
\end{equation}
with the explicit cosine-power densities for $W_j$
\cite[Thm.~3.1, eqs.~(3.7)--(3.8)]{bourgadeHughesNikeghbaliYor2007}.  These are
determinant-value and logarithmic probability laws; neither theorem differentiates the
Szeg\H{o} recurrence at $z=1$.

Bourgade--Nikeghbali--Rouault supply a second, closer coordinate precedent.  For finite OPUC
data they define
\begin{equation}
 \gamma_k(z)=z-\frac{\Phi_{k+1}(z)}{\Phi_k(z)},\qquad
 \gamma_k=\gamma_k(1),
 \label{eq:bnr-gamma-definition}
\end{equation}
and prove the recursive identities
\begin{equation}
 \gamma_k=\overline{\alpha_k}
 \prod_{j<k}\frac{1-\overline{\gamma_j}}{1-\gamma_j},
 \qquad |\gamma_k|=|\alpha_k|,
 \label{eq:bnr-gamma-alpha-map}
\end{equation}
with a bijection between the finite $\alpha$ and $\gamma$ coordinates.  They then prove
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_N(1)=\det(I-U)=\prod_{k=0}^{N-1}(1-\gamma_k)
 \label{eq:bnr-determinant-product}
\end{equation}
and, under circular-Jacobi sampling, independence of the $\gamma_k$ together with explicit
disk/circle densities and the Mellin--Fourier product formula
\cite[Defs.~(3.1)--(3.3), Prop.~3.2, Thm.~3.1, Prop.~4.1]{bourgadeNikeghbaliRouault2008}.

The apparent coordinate gap can in fact be closed exactly.  Write
$A_k=\Phi_k(1)$ as in \cref{thm:verblunsky-peel-derived}, and retain the inverse Schur
iterate $b_k(z)=\Phi_k(z)/\Phi_k^*(z)$ from the source.

\begin{theorem}[Literal $\gamma$--$\beta$ identity and lossless derivative-state lift]
\label{thm:gamma-beta-jet-lift}
Let $N\geq1$, and put
\[
 \mathcal G_N=\mathbb D^{N-1}\times\mathbb T,
 \qquad \mathbb T=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|=1\}.
\]
For finite Szeg\H{o} data
$(\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_{N-1})\in\mathcal G_N$, let
$\gamma_k$ be the Bourgade--Nikeghbali--Rouault deformed coefficient and let
$\beta_k$ be the phase-rotated coefficient in
\eqref{eq:peel-triangular-coordinates}.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item For every $0\leq k\leq N-1$,
 \begin{equation}
  \gamma_k=\beta_k
  =\overline{\alpha_k}\frac{\overline{A_k}}{A_k}.
  \label{eq:gamma-beta-literal}
 \end{equation}
 Thus the sourced triangular $\alpha\mapsto\gamma$ map and the independently
 reconstructed $\alpha\mapsto\beta$ map are the same coordinate map, not merely two
 equidistributed maps.  The inverse is
 \begin{equation}
  A_0=1,
  \qquad A_k=\prod_{j<k}(1-\gamma_j),
  \qquad
  \alpha_k=\overline{\gamma_k}\frac{\overline{A_k}}{A_k}.
  \label{eq:gamma-alpha-explicit-inverse}
 \end{equation}
 Its image is all of $\mathcal G_N$, every fibre is a singleton, and there is no
 exceptional locus on $\mathcal G_N$.

 \item Define the retained state graph
 \begin{align}
  \mathcal S_N=\{&(\gamma_0,\ldots,\gamma_{N-1};u_0,\ldots,u_{N-1}):
  \gamma\in\mathcal G_N,\ u_0=0,\notag\\
  &u_{k+1}=T_k(\gamma_k;u_k)\quad(0\leq k\leq N-2)\}.
  \label{eq:gamma-state-graph}
 \end{align}
 The map
 \begin{equation}
  \mathcal J_N:\mathcal G_N\longrightarrow\mathcal S_N,
  \qquad
  \gamma\longmapsto(\gamma;u(\gamma))
  \label{eq:gamma-jet-lift-map}
 \end{equation}
 is a bijection.  Its inverse is the projection
 $(\gamma;u)\mapsto\gamma$; hence its fibres are singletons and it loses no
 information.  Every denominator in the recursion and in
 \begin{equation}
  \eta_k(u_k)=\frac{N-1-\overline{u_k}}{N-k+u_k}
  \label{eq:gamma-lift-eta}
 \end{equation}
 is nonzero on this graph.

 \item Let $m_{\mathbb T}$ be normalized Haar measure on $\mathbb T$ and put
 \begin{equation}
  \Pi_N=
  \left(\bigotimes_{k=0}^{N-2}\mu_{N-k-1}\right)\otimes m_{\mathbb T}.
  \label{eq:gamma-cue-product-law}
 \end{equation}
 Under Haar measure on $\mathrm U(N)$, the $\gamma$ coordinate has law $\Pi_N$.
 Consequently the full lifted state has the exact push-forward law
 \begin{equation}
  (\mathcal J_N)_*\Pi_N
  =\delta_0(du_0)
   \prod_{k=0}^{N-2}
   \left[
    \mu_{N-k-1}(d\gamma_k)
    \delta_{T_k(\gamma_k;u_k)}(du_{k+1})
   \right]
   m_{\mathbb T}(d\gamma_{N-1}).
  \label{eq:gamma-state-markov-kernel}
 \end{equation}
 In particular, the one-step conditional kernel is explicitly
 \begin{equation}
  K_k(u,d\gamma\,du')
  =\mu_{N-k-1}(d\gamma)\,
   \delta_{T_k(\gamma;u)}(du')
  \qquad(0\leq k\leq N-2).
  \label{eq:gamma-one-step-kernel}
 \end{equation}

 \item The determinant value and its derivative are two explicitly different
 functions on the same retained coordinate space:
 \begin{align}
  V_N(\gamma)&=\prod_{k=0}^{N-1}(1-\gamma_k)=\Phi_N(1),
  \label{eq:gamma-value-map}\\
  F_N(\gamma)&=\prod_{k=0}^{N-1}
   \left(1-\gamma_k\eta_k(u_k(\gamma))\right)
   =\frac{\Phi_N'(1)}{N}.
  \label{eq:gamma-derivative-map}
 \end{align}
 Therefore the exact derivative law is $(F_N)_*\Pi_N$.  Although the coordinates
 $\gamma_k$ are independent, the displayed derivative factors are not asserted to be
 independent: $u_k$ is the deterministic function of the complete prefix
 $(\gamma_0,\ldots,\gamma_{k-1})$ specified above.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the source convention,
$\Phi_k^*(z)=z^k\overline{\Phi_k(\overline z^{-1})}$.  Hence
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_k^*(1)=\overline{\Phi_k(1)}=\overline{A_k},
 \qquad
 b_k(1)=\frac{A_k}{\overline{A_k}}.
\end{equation}
For $k\leq N-1$, the product formula
$A_k=\prod_{j<k}(1-\beta_j)$ contains only disk coordinates, so $A_k\ne0$.
Substitution into the sourced definition
$\gamma_k(1)=\overline{\alpha_k}/b_k(1)$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \gamma_k
 =\frac{\overline{\alpha_k}}{A_k/\overline{A_k}}
 =\overline{\alpha_k}\frac{\overline{A_k}}{A_k}
 =\beta_k,
\end{equation}
which proves \eqref{eq:gamma-beta-literal}.  The inverse, image, fibres, and absence
of an exceptional locus now follow from the already proved triangular inverse
\eqref{eq:KN-triangular-inverse}; no coordinate has been discarded.

Given $\gamma$, the equations defining \eqref{eq:gamma-state-graph} determine $u_0$,
then $u_1$, and so on through $u_{N-1}$.  Induction proves uniqueness.  Projection
onto the retained $\gamma$ coordinates is therefore a two-sided inverse to
$\mathcal J_N$.  The denominator and strict-modulus statements are precisely the
ordered identities \eqref{eq:KN-half-plane-propagation} and
\eqref{eq:KN-eta-difference}, now with $\gamma_k=\beta_k$.

Specialize the Bourgade--Nikeghbali--Rouault circular-Jacobi theorem to its Dyson
parameter $\beta_{\rm D}=2$ and singularity parameter $\delta=0$.  Their interior
density becomes, with $m=N-k-1$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{m}{\pi}(1-|z|^2)^{m-1}d^2z=d\mu_m(z),
\end{equation}
and their terminal density is $m_{\mathbb T}$.  Their theorem supplies independence,
so the law is exactly \eqref{eq:gamma-cue-product-law}.  Since
$u_{k+1}=T_k(\gamma_k;u_k)$ is a deterministic Borel map and $\gamma_k$ is independent
of its prefix, disintegration one coordinate at a time gives
\eqref{eq:gamma-state-markov-kernel}; equivalently, testing both sides against any
bounded Borel function and applying Fubini gives identical iterated integrals.
Finally \eqref{eq:gamma-value-map} is the sourced determinant factorization, while
\eqref{eq:gamma-derivative-map} is
\eqref{eq:KN-complete-peel} after \eqref{eq:gamma-beta-literal}.  This proves every
asserted map and push-forward formula.
\end{proof}

Bourgade's conditional-Haar paper gives a second derivative product, but on a
different measure.  Its relation to the preceding coordinates is also exact rather
than categorical.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional-Haar residual map, coordinate reversal, and determinant tilt]
\label{prop:conditional-haar-gamma-crosswalk}
Let $n\geq1$ and $p\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}$.  Endow a unitary matrix of size $n+p$
with Bourgade's Haar measure conditioned to have $p$ eigenvalues equal to $1$.
Write its remaining eigenvalues as $e^{\mathrm i\theta_1},\ldots,e^{\mathrm i\theta_n}$
and set
\begin{equation}
 Z_U(z)=\det(zI-U)=(z-1)^p Q_n(z),
 \qquad
 Q_n(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-e^{\mathrm i\theta_j}).
 \label{eq:conditional-residual-polynomial}
\end{equation}
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item The residual spectrum has the circular-Jacobi law
 $\mathrm{CJ}^{(n)}_{p,2}$.  Its deformed coefficients
 $(\gamma_0,\ldots,\gamma_{n-1})$ are independent and
 \begin{equation}
  \frac{Z_U^{(p)}(1)}{p!}=Q_n(1)
  =\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(1-\gamma_k).
  \label{eq:conditional-derivative-gamma-product}
 \end{equation}

 \item For $0\leq k\leq n-2$, with $m=n-k-1$, $\gamma_k$ has density
 \begin{equation}
  c_{m,p}(1-|z|^2)^{m-1}|1-z|^{2p}\,d^2z,
  \qquad
  c_{m,p}=\frac{\Gamma(m+p+1)^2}
  {\pi\Gamma(m)\Gamma(m+2p+1)}.
  \label{eq:conditional-interior-gamma-density}
 \end{equation}
 The terminal coordinate has density
 \begin{equation}
  \frac{\Gamma(p+1)^2}{\Gamma(2p+1)}|1-z|^{2p}\,dm_{\mathbb T}(z).
  \label{eq:conditional-terminal-gamma-density}
 \end{equation}

 \item Put $\mathcal X_n=\mathbb T\times\mathbb D^{n-1}$, ordered as
 $(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$, and define the coordinate reversal
 \begin{equation}
  \mathcal R_n:\mathcal G_n\longrightarrow\mathcal X_n,
  \qquad
  X_\ell=(\mathcal R_n\gamma)_\ell=\gamma_{n-\ell}
  \qquad(1\leq\ell\leq n).
  \label{eq:bourgade-X-gamma-reversal}
 \end{equation}
 This is a bijection with inverse $\gamma_k=X_{n-k}$.  Its push-forward of
 $\Pi_{n,p}$ is exactly the product law of the independent variables in Bourgade's
 derivative-product theorem.  This is a law-preserving coordinate identification;
 it does not silently identify two source constructions pointwise on an unstated
 common sample space.
 Thus $X_1$ is the sampled circle coordinate, while for $\ell\geq2$ the
 unsampled radial variable satisfies
 $|X_\ell|^2\sim\operatorname{Beta}(1,\ell-1)$.

 \item Let $\Pi_{n,p}$ be the product law in
 \eqref{eq:conditional-interior-gamma-density}--\eqref{eq:conditional-terminal-gamma-density}
 and let $\Pi_{n,0}$ be the CUE product law.  The exact change of measure is
 \begin{equation}
  \frac{d\Pi_{n,p}}{d\Pi_{n,0}}(\gamma)
  =\frac{|V_n(\gamma)|^{2p}}{\mathcal Z_{n,p}},
  \qquad
  \mathcal Z_{n,p}
  =\prod_{m=0}^{n-1}
   \frac{\Gamma(m+1)\Gamma(m+2p+1)}{\Gamma(m+p+1)^2}.
  \label{eq:conditional-radon-nikodym}
 \end{equation}
 Hence the conditional first-nonzero derivative and the unconditioned derivative
 $F_n$ from \eqref{eq:gamma-derivative-map} are explicitly placed on the same
 coordinate space, with their observable maps and measures retained.  No equality
 of those two random variables is asserted.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The residual eigenvalue density is
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{const}\cdot
 \prod_{j<\ell}|e^{\mathrm i\theta_j}-e^{\mathrm i\theta_\ell}|^2
 \prod_{j=1}^{n}|1-e^{\mathrm i\theta_j}|^{2p},
 d\theta_1\cdots d\theta_n,
\end{equation}
which is exactly $\mathrm{CJ}^{(n)}_{p,2}$ in the source coordinates
\cite[eq.~(1.2), Def.~2.5, Cor.~4.1]{bourgadeConditionalHaar2009}.  Differentiating
\eqref{eq:conditional-residual-polynomial} exactly $p$ times at $1$ leaves only the
term in which all $p$ derivatives hit $(z-1)^p$, proving
$Z_U^{(p)}(1)/p!=Q_n(1)$.  The sourced deformed-coefficient factorization proves the
last equality in \eqref{eq:conditional-derivative-gamma-product}.

In the circular-Jacobi density theorem, set $\delta=p$, $\beta_{\rm D}=2$, and
$m=n-k-1$.  The source constant becomes $c_{m,p}$ and gives
\eqref{eq:conditional-interior-gamma-density}; its terminal density gives
\eqref{eq:conditional-terminal-gamma-density}
\cite[Thm.~3.1 and eqs.~(3.1)--(3.3)]{bourgadeNikeghbaliRouault2008}.
For $m\geq1$, under the CUE base law write
$z=e^{\mathrm i\Theta}\sqrt B$ with $\Theta$ uniform and
$B\sim\operatorname{Beta}(1,m)$.  Expanding both integer powers and integrating
the phase gives
\begin{align}
 \mathbb E|1-z|^{2p}
 &=\sum_{j=0}^{p}\binom pj^2\mathbb E B^j\notag\\
 &=\sum_{j=0}^{p}\binom pj^2
   \frac{j!\,\Gamma(m+1)}{\Gamma(m+j+1)}
 =\frac{\Gamma(m+1)\Gamma(m+2p+1)}{\Gamma(m+p+1)^2}.
 \label{eq:conditional-one-factor-normalization}
\end{align}
The last finite sum is the Chu--Vandermonde evaluation of
${}_2F_1(-p,-p;m+1;1)$.  For $m=0$, phase integration on the circle gives the same
formula through
$\sum_{j=0}^{p}\binom pj^2=\binom{2p}{p}$.  Taking reciprocals of these
normalizing factors gives exactly the constants in
\eqref{eq:conditional-interior-gamma-density} and
\eqref{eq:conditional-terminal-gamma-density}.

For the source variable $X_\ell$, the unsampled radial square has
$\operatorname{Beta}(1,\ell-1)$ law.  The coordinate $\gamma_k$ has the same base
radial law when $m=n-k-1=\ell-1$, which is precisely
$k=n-\ell$; the $|1-z|^{2p}$ sampling is identical.  Independence on both
product spaces therefore proves
$(\mathcal R_n)_*\Pi_{n,p}=\operatorname{Law}(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$ coordinate by
coordinate.  The displayed inverse proves bijectivity, including the terminal
$m=0$ coordinate, without asserting an unproved pointwise coupling of the
reflection and OPUC realizations.
Finally, multiplication of the independent one-factor density ratios yields
\begin{equation}
 \prod_{k=0}^{n-1}|1-\gamma_k|^{2p}
 =\left|\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(1-\gamma_k)\right|^{2p}
 =|V_n(\gamma)|^{2p},
\end{equation}
and multiplication of \eqref{eq:conditional-one-factor-normalization} for
$m=0,\ldots,n-1$ gives $\mathcal Z_{n,p}$.  This proves
\eqref{eq:conditional-radon-nikodym} and the complete crosswalk.
\end{proof}

The paper's zeta edge is the Keating--Snaith value model and Selberg-type logarithmic
limit, not a statement about zeros.  Bourgade's thesis repeats the conditional derivative
theorem as Theorem~5.1 and then retains several further results that do not occur in the
paper: the trace central limit theorem under finitely pinned eigenvalues, the martingale
realization of the circular-$\beta_{\rm D}$ trace limit, and the joint convergence
\begin{equation}
 \left(\frac{\log\det(I-u_n)}{\sqrt{\log n}},\operatorname{Tr}u_n\right)
 \ \Longrightarrow\
 \sqrt{\frac{2}{\beta_{\rm D}}}(X_1,X_2),
 \label{eq:bourgade-thesis-logdet-trace}
\end{equation}
where $X_1,X_2$ are independent standard complex normal variables
\cite[Chap.~5, Thms.~5.1, 5.11, 5.14 and Cor.~5.13]{bourgadeThesis2010}.
The proof of the joint limit uses the same independent deformed coefficients but splits a
finite prefix controlling the trace from a tail controlling the normalized logarithm, then
uses the $L^2$ tail estimate and Slutsky's lemma.  This is human prior art for a
state-dependent use of the independent coordinates; it does not make the derivative factors
in \eqref{eq:gamma-derivative-map} independent.  The exact $\gamma=\beta$ identity and state
uniqueness are also replayed in bounded Lean file
\texttt{GammaBetaCrosswalk.lean}; the probability disintegration above remains a standard
human proof rather than an axiom in the formal kernel.
\end{sloppypar}

\subsubsection{The Winn--Forrester Cauchy trace as the terminal deformed-coordinate state}

Winn's finite-$N$ argument and Forrester's circular-$\beta_{\rm D}$ extension use the
stereographic variables $x_j=\cot(\theta_j/2)$ to turn a completed-characteristic-
polynomial derivative into the trace of a symmetric Cauchy ensemble
\cite[\S3.2]{winn2012}\cite[Prop.~2.1]{forrester2022}.  The following result proves the
missing coordinate map from that trace all the way back to the retained
$\gamma$-state.  In particular, the Cauchy trace is not merely analogous to the terminal
state in \cref{thm:gamma-beta-jet-lift}: it is an explicit rational observable of that
state on its exact open domain.

\begin{theorem}[Stereographic, trace, and terminal-state factorization]
\label{thm:winn-forrester-terminal-state}
Let $N\geq1$.  On the ordered eigenangle chart
\[
 \Theta_N^\circ=(0,2\pi)^N
\]
define
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{St}_N:\Theta_N^\circ\longrightarrow\mathbb R^N,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{St}_N(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_N)
 =\left(\cot\frac{\theta_1}{2},\ldots,\cot\frac{\theta_N}{2}\right).
 \label{eq:winn-stereographic-map}
\end{equation}
Put $x_j=\cot(\theta_j/2)$ and
\[
 T=\Sigma_N(\mathbf x):=\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_j.
\]
Then the following assertions hold.

\begin{enumerate}
 \item The map \eqref{eq:winn-stereographic-map} is a bijection, with inverse
 \begin{equation}
  \operatorname{St}_N^{-1}(x_1,\ldots,x_N)
  =\left(2\operatorname{arccot}x_1,\ldots,
  2\operatorname{arccot}x_N\right),
  \qquad \operatorname{arccot}x\in(0,\pi).
  \label{eq:winn-stereographic-inverse}
 \end{equation}
 It is equivariant for simultaneous permutation of coordinates and therefore descends
 to a bijection of the corresponding unordered configuration spaces.  Coordinatewise,
 \begin{align}
  e^{\mathrm i\theta_j}&=\frac{x_j+\mathrm i}{x_j-\mathrm i},
  &|1-e^{\mathrm i\theta_j}|&=\frac{2}{\sqrt{1+x_j^2}},
  \label{eq:winn-stereographic-point-identities}\\
  |e^{\mathrm i\theta_k}-e^{\mathrm i\theta_j}|
  &=\frac{2|x_k-x_j|}
  {\sqrt{1+x_k^2}\sqrt{1+x_j^2}},
  &|d\theta_j|&=\frac{2\,dx_j}{1+x_j^2}.
  \label{eq:winn-stereographic-jacobian-identities}
 \end{align}
 The excluded locus is exactly an eigenangle $0$ modulo $2\pi$, equivalently an
 eigenvalue $1$; it lies at the infinite boundary of this finite $x$-chart.

 The trace map $\Sigma_N:\mathbb R^N\to\mathbb R$ is surjective, has the explicit
 right inverse $j_N(t)=(t,0,\ldots,0)$, and has fibres
 \begin{equation}
  \Sigma_N^{-1}(t)=j_N(t)+\ker\Sigma_N
  =\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^N:x_1+\cdots+x_N=t\}.
  \label{eq:winn-trace-fibres}
 \end{equation}
 Thus it loses no coordinate when $N=1$ and identifies precisely the affine translates
 of the $(N-1)$-dimensional hyperplane $\ker\Sigma_N$ when $N>1$.

 \item Let
 \[
  \Phi_N(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{N}(z-e^{\mathrm i\theta_j}),
  \qquad
  u_N=\frac{\Phi_N'(1)}{\Phi_N(1)}.
 \]
 On $\Theta_N^\circ$ one has the exact terminal identity
 \begin{equation}
  u_N=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{1-e^{\mathrm i\theta_j}}
  =\frac N2+\frac{\mathrm i}{2}T,
  \qquad
  2u_N-N=\mathrm iT.
  \label{eq:winn-terminal-log-derivative}
 \end{equation}
 In the deformed coordinates of \cref{thm:gamma-beta-jet-lift}, put
 \begin{equation}
  \mathcal G_N^\circ
  =\{\gamma\in\mathcal G_N:V_N(\gamma)\ne0\}
  =\{\gamma\in\mathcal G_N:\gamma_{N-1}\ne1\}.
  \label{eq:winn-gamma-open-locus}
 \end{equation}
 The Cauchy trace is the explicit observable
 \begin{equation}
  \mathcal T_N:\mathcal G_N^\circ\longrightarrow\mathbb R,
  \qquad
  \mathcal T_N(\gamma)
  =-\mathrm i\left(2N\frac{F_N(\gamma)}{V_N(\gamma)}-N\right).
  \label{eq:winn-gamma-trace-map}
 \end{equation}
 Its complete fibre over $t\in\mathbb R$ is
 \begin{equation}
  \mathcal T_N^{-1}(t)
  =\left\{\gamma\in\mathcal G_N^\circ:
  2N F_N(\gamma)=(N+\mathrm it)V_N(\gamma)\right\}.
  \label{eq:winn-gamma-trace-fibre}
 \end{equation}
 Hence two retained states are identified by this observable exactly when their terminal
 ratios $F_N/V_N$ agree; no stronger independence or injectivity statement is being made.

 \item Define Winn's real completed characteristic polynomial by
 \begin{equation}
  \mathcal V_U(\vartheta)=
  \exp\!\left(\frac{\mathrm iN(\vartheta+\pi)}2
  -\frac{\mathrm i}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\theta_j\right)
  \prod_{j=1}^{N}\left(1-e^{\mathrm i(\theta_j-\vartheta)}\right).
  \label{eq:winn-completed-characteristic-polynomial}
 \end{equation}
 Then $|\mathcal V_U(0)|=|V_N|$ and
 \begin{equation}
  \frac{\mathcal V_U'(0)}{\mathcal V_U(0)}
  =\mathrm i\left(u_N-\frac N2\right)=-\frac T2.
  \label{eq:winn-completed-log-derivative}
 \end{equation}
 For $s\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}$ and real
 $-\tfrac12<h<s+\tfrac12$, let
 \begin{equation}
  \mathcal F_N(s,h)=
  \int_{\mathrm U(N)}|\mathcal V_U(0)|^{2(s-h)}
  |\mathcal V_U'(0)|^{2h}\,d\mu_N^{\rm Haar}(U).
  \label{eq:winn-joint-moment-definition}
 \end{equation}
 With $\Pi_{N,s}$ and $\mathcal Z_{N,s}$ as in
 \eqref{eq:conditional-radon-nikodym}, the exact factorization is
 \begin{equation}
  \mathcal F_N(s,h)
  =2^{-2h}\mathcal Z_{N,s}
  \mathbb E_{\Pi_{N,s}}\!\left[|\mathcal T_N|^{2h}\right],
  \qquad
  \mathcal Z_{N,s}=\mathcal F_N(s,0).
  \label{eq:winn-moment-gamma-tilt}
 \end{equation}
 The locus removed in \eqref{eq:winn-gamma-open-locus} has zero
 $\Pi_{N,s}$-measure, so no choice of a value for $\mathcal T_N$ there changes the
 expectation.

 \item The push-forward of the determinant-tilted CUE eigenangle law under
 $\operatorname{St}_N$ is the symmetric Cauchy unitary ensemble with the literal
 density
 \begin{equation}
  d\nu_{N,s}(\mathbf x)
  =\frac{2^{N^2+2sN}}
  {(2\pi)^N N!\,\mathcal Z_{N,s}}
  \prod_{1\leq j<k\leq N}(x_k-x_j)^2
  \prod_{j=1}^{N}(1+x_j^2)^{-(N+s)}\,d\mathbf x.
  \label{eq:winn-cauchy-density-exact}
 \end{equation}
 Consequently
 \begin{equation}
  \mathbb E_{\Pi_{N,s}}[|\mathcal T_N|^{2h}]
  =\int_{\mathbb R^N}\left|\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_j\right|^{2h}
  d\nu_{N,s}(\mathbf x).
  \label{eq:winn-cauchy-trace-pushforward}
 \end{equation}
 This is an equality of push-forward laws and observables.  It does not identify a
 particular Haar matrix, a particular deformed-coordinate sample, and a particular
 Cauchy matrix pointwise without specifying a coupling.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $x=\cot(\theta/2)$ with $0<\theta<2\pi$, elementary half-angle algebra gives
\[
 e^{\mathrm i\theta}=\frac{x+\mathrm i}{x-\mathrm i},
 \qquad
 \frac{dx}{d\theta}=-\frac12(1+x^2).
\]
The first equation and its inverse prove the coordinatewise bijection and
\eqref{eq:winn-stereographic-inverse}; applying it to two coordinates and taking
absolute values proves \eqref{eq:winn-stereographic-point-identities}--
\eqref{eq:winn-stereographic-jacobian-identities}.  Coordinatewise application also
proves permutation equivariance.  The statement about the excluded locus follows from
the endpoint behaviour of $\cot(\theta/2)$.  The assertions about $\Sigma_N$ follow by
direct substitution of $j_N$, and subtracting $j_N(t)$ from the equation
$\Sigma_N(\mathbf x)=t$ proves the full fibre formula
\eqref{eq:winn-trace-fibres}.

Logarithmic differentiation of $\Phi_N$ gives the first sum in
\eqref{eq:winn-terminal-log-derivative}.  For each coordinate,
\[
 \frac1{1-e^{\mathrm i\theta}}
 =\frac12+\frac{\mathrm i}{2}\cot\frac\theta2.
\]
Summing proves the rest of that equation.  By
\eqref{eq:gamma-value-map}--\eqref{eq:gamma-derivative-map},
\[
 u_N=\frac{\Phi_N'(1)}{\Phi_N(1)}
 =N\frac{F_N(\gamma)}{V_N(\gamma)}.
\]
All nonterminal factors in $V_N=\prod_k(1-\gamma_k)$ are nonzero because
$\gamma_k\in\mathbb D$ for $k<N-1$; this proves both equalities in
\eqref{eq:winn-gamma-open-locus}.  Substitution in
\eqref{eq:winn-terminal-log-derivative} proves
\eqref{eq:winn-gamma-trace-map}, including that its value is real on the stated
domain.  Multiplying the equation $\mathcal T_N(\gamma)=t$ by
$\mathrm iV_N(\gamma)$, and using $V_N\ne0$, gives exactly
\eqref{eq:winn-gamma-trace-fibre} in both directions.

Let
$Z_U(\vartheta)=\prod_j(1-e^{\mathrm i(\theta_j-\vartheta)})$.  At zero,
$Z_U(0)=\Phi_N(1)=V_N$.  Direct differentiation gives
\[
 \frac{Z_U'(0)}{Z_U(0)}
 =\mathrm i\sum_{j=1}^{N}
 \frac{e^{\mathrm i\theta_j}}{1-e^{\mathrm i\theta_j}}
 =\mathrm i(u_N-N).
\]
The derivative of the exponential prefactor in
\eqref{eq:winn-completed-characteristic-polynomial} contributes
$\mathrm iN/2$.  Therefore
\[
 \frac{\mathcal V_U'(0)}{\mathcal V_U(0)}
 =\mathrm i(u_N-N/2)=-T/2,
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:winn-completed-log-derivative}.  Its absolute-value form
turns the integrand of \eqref{eq:winn-joint-moment-definition} into
\[
 2^{-2h}|V_N|^{2s}|T|^{2h}.
\]
Now apply the exact Radon--Nikodym identity
\eqref{eq:conditional-radon-nikodym}; its normalizing factor at $p=s$ is
$\mathcal Z_{N,s}$.  This proves \eqref{eq:winn-moment-gamma-tilt}.  The terminal
circle coordinate has a density relative to Haar measure, so its singleton value $1$
has probability zero.

Finally Weyl's eigenangle density is
\[
 \frac1{(2\pi)^N N!}
 \prod_{j<k}|e^{\mathrm i\theta_k}-e^{\mathrm i\theta_j}|^2
 \,d\boldsymbol\theta.
\]
Multiplying the pair factors and Jacobians in
\eqref{eq:winn-stereographic-jacobian-identities} gives
\[
 2^{N(N-1)}2^N
 \prod_{j<k}(x_k-x_j)^2\prod_j(1+x_j^2)^{-N}
 =2^{N^2}\prod_{j<k}(x_k-x_j)^2\prod_j(1+x_j^2)^{-N}.
\]
Moreover
$|V_N|^{2s}=2^{2sN}\prod_j(1+x_j^2)^{-s}$ by
\eqref{eq:winn-stereographic-point-identities}.  Division by the exact tilt
normalizer $\mathcal Z_{N,s}$ proves \eqref{eq:winn-cauchy-density-exact}; applying
the trace map $\Sigma_N$ proves \eqref{eq:winn-cauchy-trace-pushforward}.
\end{proof}

For completeness, Winn's finite-$N$ theorem can now be stated without hiding the
coordinate change.  For a partition $\lambda$, write $h_\lambda$ for its hook-length
product and $[a]_\lambda$ for the generalized Pochhammer symbol, and set
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal C_N(p,s)=(-2)^p
 \sum_{\lambda\vdash_s p}
 \frac{[s]_\lambda[-N]_\lambda}{[2s]_\lambda h_\lambda^2}.
 \label{eq:winn-partition-coefficient}
\end{equation}
If $h=(2m-1)/2$, $m\in\mathbb N$, and $s\in\mathbb N$ with
$s>h-1/2$, then Winn proves
\begin{multline}
 \mathcal F_N(s,h)=
 \frac{2(-1)^{h+1/2}}{2^{2h}\pi}\mathcal F_N(s,0)
 \Bigg\{
 \sum_{p=1}^{2h}\sum_{\ell=1}^{p}
 \binom{2h}{p-\ell}\frac{(-1)^\ell}{\ell}
 (-N)^{2h-p}p!\,\mathcal C_N(p,s)\\
 {}+\sum_{p=2h+1}^{sN}
 \frac{(2h)!(p-2h-1)!}{N^{p-2h}}\mathcal C_N(p,s)
 \Bigg\}.
 \label{eq:winn-finite-N-half-integer-moment}
\end{multline}
This is Theorem~2.2 of the source.  Its proof is not a formal manipulation of the final
answer: Winn derives the regularized Fourier kernel, proves the exact Cauchy integral,
evaluates its characteristic function by Laguerre Wronskians and Jack hypergeometric
functions, establishes the required small-regularizer limits, and only then collects the
partition coefficients in Sections~3--6 \cite[Thm.~2.2 and \S\S3--6]{winn2012}.

Forrester retains the same Cauchy trace but replaces the Vandermonde square by the
Dyson power $\beta_{\rm D}>0$.  For $s\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}$ and
$-1/2<\operatorname{Re}h<s+1/2$, his large-$N$ theorem is
\begin{multline}
 \lim_{N\to\infty}N^{-2s^2/\beta_{\rm D}-2h}
 \mathcal F_{N,\beta_{\rm D}}(s,h)
 =\frac1{2^{2h}}
 \prod_{j=1}^{s}
 \frac{\Gamma(2j/\beta_{\rm D})}
 {\Gamma(2(s+j)/\beta_{\rm D})}\\
 {}\times\frac1{\cos(\pi h)}
 \sum_\kappa
 \frac{(-2h)_{|\kappa|}}
 {|\kappa|!\,[4s/\beta_{\rm D}]_\kappa^{(\beta_{\rm D}/2)}}
 \left(\frac4{\beta_{\rm D}}\right)^{|\kappa|}
 C_\kappa^{(\beta_{\rm D}/2)}((1)^s),
 \label{eq:forrester-large-N-continuous-h}
\end{multline}
with limiting values taken at removable half-integer singularities.  He also gives the
limiting trace density as an explicit inverse Fourier--Laplace integral of a Jack
${}_0F_1^{(\beta_{\rm D}/2)}$ function
\cite[Thms.~1.1--1.2]{forrester2022}.  Equation
\eqref{eq:winn-gamma-trace-map} supplies the exact $\beta_{\rm D}=2$ deformed-coordinate
lift of that trace.  No $\beta_{\rm D}\ne2$ lift to the present $\gamma$-state has been
constructed here, and none of these moment identities is a statement about individual
zeta zeros.

\subsubsection{The Conrey--Rubinstein--Snaith first jet and its exact
place in the Cauchy trace}

Conrey, Rubinstein, and Snaith use a different characteristic-polynomial
coordinate from the one above.  The conversion is an everywhere invertible phase and
an explicit triangular first-jet map; writing it out fixes every constant in their two
derivative moments \cite[\S\S1--4]{conreyRubinsteinSnaith2006}.

\begin{theorem}[Exact CRS polynomial and first-jet crosswalk]
\label{thm:crs-first-jet-crosswalk}
Let $A\in\mathrm U(N)$ have ordered eigenvalues
$z_j=e^{\mathrm i\theta_j}$, and put
\[
 \delta_A=\det A=\prod_{j=1}^{N}z_j,\qquad
 \Phi_N(s)=\prod_{j=1}^{N}(s-z_j),\qquad
 V_N=\Phi_N(1),\quad F_N=\frac1N\Phi_N'(1).
\]
The CRS characteristic polynomial is
\[
 \Lambda_A(s)=\det(I-sA^*)=\prod_{j=1}^{N}(1-sz_j^{-1}).
\]
Choose the eigenangle lifts and the branch of $s^{-N/2}$ used in their
completed function, and define
\[
 \chi_A=(-1)^N\delta_A^{-1},\qquad
 \rho_A=e^{-\pi\mathrm iN/2}
 e^{\frac{\mathrm i}{2}\sum_j\theta_j}\chi_A .
\]
Both $\chi_A$ and $\rho_A$ have modulus one.  Then:

\begin{enumerate}
 \item For every $s\in\mathbb C$,
 \begin{equation}
  \Lambda_A(s)=\chi_A\Phi_N(s).
  \label{eq:crs-polynomial-phase-map}
 \end{equation}
 For fixed $A$ this is a linear bijection of polynomial spaces, with inverse
 $\Phi_N=\chi_A^{-1}\Lambda_A$, singleton fibres, empty exceptional locus, and no
 information loss.

 \item If
 \[
  \mathcal Z_A(s)=e^{-\pi\mathrm iN/2}
  e^{\frac{\mathrm i}{2}\sum_j\theta_j}s^{-N/2}\Lambda_A(s),
 \]
 then the value--derivative jets at $s=1$ satisfy
 \begin{equation}
  \begin{pmatrix}\mathcal Z_A(1)\\ \mathcal Z_A'(1)\end{pmatrix}
  =
  \rho_A
  \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\ -N/2&1\end{pmatrix}
  \begin{pmatrix}V_N\\ \Phi_N'(1)\end{pmatrix}.
  \label{eq:crs-completed-jet-map}
 \end{equation}
 The inverse is
 \begin{equation}
  \begin{pmatrix}V_N\\ \Phi_N'(1)\end{pmatrix}
  =
  \rho_A^{-1}
  \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\ N/2&1\end{pmatrix}
  \begin{pmatrix}\mathcal Z_A(1)\\ \mathcal Z_A'(1)\end{pmatrix}.
  \label{eq:crs-completed-jet-inverse}
 \end{equation}
 Thus the jet map has singleton fibres, empty exceptional locus, and zero information
 loss.  Changing one eigenangle lift by $2\pi$ changes the displayed square-root phase
 by a sign, exactly as it changes the chosen completed function; all absolute-value
 identities below are lift-independent.

 \item Pointwise on the whole eigenangle space, including $V_N=0$,
 \begin{align}
  \Lambda_A'(1)&=\chi_A\,N F_N,
  \label{eq:crs-lambda-derivative-coordinate}\\
  \mathcal Z_A'(1)&=\rho_A\,N\left(F_N-\frac12V_N\right).
  \label{eq:crs-z-derivative-coordinate}
 \end{align}
 On the open set $\mathcal G_N^\circ$ of
 \eqref{eq:winn-gamma-open-locus}, this becomes
 \begin{equation}
  \mathcal Z_A'(1)=\frac{\mathrm i\rho_A}{2}V_N\mathcal T_N.
  \label{eq:crs-z-derivative-trace}
 \end{equation}
 The centered polynomial coordinate
 $D_N:=F_N-\tfrac12V_N$ remains defined at $V_N=0$; only the quotient
 $\mathcal T_N=-\mathrm i(2NF_N/V_N-N)$ fails there.  Equivalently,
 \[
  \frac{\mathrm i}{2}V_N\mathcal T_N
  =N\left(F_N-\frac12V_N\right)
 \]
 has the unique polynomial continuation displayed on the right.

 \item Let $\Pi_{N,0}$ be the CUE $\gamma$-coordinate law and, for an integer
 $k\geq1$, define
 \[
  M^\Lambda_{N,k}=\int_{\mathrm U(N)}|\Lambda_A'(1)|^{2k}\,dA,\qquad
  M^{\mathcal Z}_{N,k}
  =\int_{\mathrm U(N)}|\mathcal Z_A'(1)|^{2k}\,dA.
 \]
 Then the exact finite-$N$ identities are
 \begin{align}
  M^\Lambda_{N,k}
  &=N^{2k}\mathbb E_{\Pi_{N,0}}|F_N|^{2k},
  \label{eq:crs-lambda-moment-crosswalk}\\
  M^{\mathcal Z}_{N,k}
  &=N^{2k}\mathbb E_{\Pi_{N,0}}
    \left|F_N-\frac12V_N\right|^{2k}
   =\mathcal F_N(k,k) \notag\\
  &=2^{-2k}\mathcal Z_{N,k}
    \mathbb E_{\Pi_{N,k}}|\mathcal T_N|^{2k}.
  \label{eq:crs-z-moment-crosswalk}
 \end{align}
 Consequently the CRS $b_k'$ asymptotic is exactly the $s=h=k$ member of
 Winn's joint-moment family, after the explicit jet and tilt maps above.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each $j$,
\[
 s-z_j=-z_j(1-sz_j^{-1}).
\]
Multiplication gives
$\Phi_N(s)=(-1)^N\delta_A\Lambda_A(s)$, proving
\eqref{eq:crs-polynomial-phase-map} and its inverse.  Multiplication by the
nonzero scalar $\chi_A$ proves the image, fibre, and exceptional-locus assertions.

Substitution into the definition of $\mathcal Z_A$ gives
$\mathcal Z_A(s)=\rho_A s^{-N/2}\Phi_N(s)$.  Evaluation and differentiation
at $s=1$ give
\[
 \mathcal Z_A(1)=\rho_A V_N,\qquad
 \mathcal Z_A'(1)=\rho_A\left(\Phi_N'(1)-\frac N2V_N\right),
\]
which is \eqref{eq:crs-completed-jet-map}.  The two triangular matrices in
\eqref{eq:crs-completed-jet-map}--\eqref{eq:crs-completed-jet-inverse}
multiply to the identity; because $\rho_A\ne0$, the inverse and fibre assertions
follow.  Differentiating \eqref{eq:crs-polynomial-phase-map} and using
$\Phi_N'(1)=NF_N$ proves
\eqref{eq:crs-lambda-derivative-coordinate}--\eqref{eq:crs-z-derivative-coordinate}.
Equation \eqref{eq:winn-gamma-trace-map} gives
\[
 \frac{\mathrm i}{2}V_N\mathcal T_N
 =\frac12(2NF_N-NV_N)
 =N\left(F_N-\frac12V_N\right)
\]
when $V_N\ne0$, proving \eqref{eq:crs-z-derivative-trace} and its stated
polynomial continuation.

Taking absolute values in
\eqref{eq:crs-lambda-derivative-coordinate}--\eqref{eq:crs-z-derivative-coordinate},
using $|\chi_A|=|\rho_A|=1$, and pushing Haar measure through the proved
CUE-to-$\gamma$ coordinate map gives the first equalities in
\eqref{eq:crs-lambda-moment-crosswalk}--\eqref{eq:crs-z-moment-crosswalk}.
The pointwise identity
$|\mathcal Z_A'(1)|=|V_N||\mathcal T_N|/2$ also equals the absolute value of
Winn's completed derivative at zero by
\eqref{eq:winn-completed-log-derivative}.  Hence
$M^{\mathcal Z}_{N,k}=\mathcal F_N(k,k)$, and
\eqref{eq:winn-moment-gamma-tilt} with $s=h=k$ proves the final equality.
\end{proof}

 \label{eq:crs-bkprime-bessel}
\end{align}
They also prove the independent combinatorial evaluation

 \label{eq:crs-bkprime-combinatorial}
\end{equation}
These are Theorems~1--3 of the source

\begin{proof}[Proof mechanism for
\eqref{eq:crs-lambda-asymptotic}--\eqref{eq:crs-bkprime-combinatorial}]
The source begins with the shifted CUE product formula and converts its
$\binom{2k}{k}$ shuffle sum into a $k$-fold residue integral.  For shifts
$\alpha_j=O(N^{-1})$, replacing
$z(w)=1/(1-e^{-w})$ by $1/w$ produces the leading contour integral with
error $O_k(N^{k^2-1})$.  Differentiating all $2k$ shifts gives the
$O_k(N^{k^2+2k-1})$ remainders above.

After moving the squared Vandermonde outside as
$\Delta^2(d/dL)$, the completed derivative gives the leading operator
\begin{equation}
 \frac{(-1)^{k(k+1)/2}}{k!}
 \Delta^2\!\left(\frac d{dL}\right)
 \left(\frac d{dt}\right)^{2k}
 \left[
 e^{-Nt/2}
 \prod_{r=1}^{k}
 \frac1{2\pi\mathrm i}\oint
 \frac{e^{L_rw+t/w}}{w^{2k}}\,dw
 \right]_{\substack{L_r=N\\t=0}}.
 \label{eq:crs-z-leading-operator}
\end{equation}
For the uncompleted derivative, the exact reciprocal-power identity
\[
 \left(\sum_r w_r^{-1}-N\right)^k
 \left(\sum_r w_r^{-1}\right)^k
 =
 \sum_{h=0}^{k}\binom{k}{h}N^{k-h}
 \left(\sum_r w_r^{-1}-N\right)^{k+h}
\]
gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{(-1)^{k(k+1)/2}}{k!}
 \sum_{h=0}^{k}\binom{k}{h}N^{k-h}
 \Delta^2\!\left(\frac d{dL}\right)
 \left(\frac d{dt}\right)^{k+h}
 \left[
 e^{-Nt}
 \prod_{r=1}^{k}
 \frac1{2\pi\mathrm i}\oint
 \frac{e^{L_rw+t/w}}{w^{2k}}\,dw
 \right]_{\substack{L_r=N\\t=0}}.
 \label{eq:crs-lambda-leading-operator}
\end{equation}
The one-variable integral is checked coefficientwise:
\[
 \frac1{2\pi\mathrm i}\oint
 \frac{e^{Lw+t/w}}{w^{2k}}\,dw
 =
 \frac{L^{2k-1}I_{2k-1}(2\sqrt{Lt})}{(Lt)^{k-1/2}}.
\]
For
\[
 f_t(L)=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}
 \frac{t^rL^{2k-1+r}}{r!(2k-1+r)!},
\]
direct expansion of both Vandermonde determinants gives
\[
 \left.
 \Delta^2\!\left(\frac d{dL}\right)
 \prod_{r=1}^{k}f_t(L_r)\right|_{L_r=N}
 =
 k!\det_{1\leq i,j\leq k}
 \left(f_t^{(i+j-2)}(N)\right).
\]
Substitution of the Bessel series followed by $x=Nt$ extracts
$N^{k^2+2k}$ and yields
\eqref{eq:crs-bk-bessel}--\eqref{eq:crs-bkprime-bessel}, including the
stated error terms.

For the second form of $b_k'$, the source instead uses the elementary
Laplace-convolution identity
\[
 \frac1{2\pi\mathrm i}\oint
 \frac{e^{Lw}}{w\prod_{j=1}^{2k}(w-\alpha_j)}\,dw
 =
 \int_{\substack{x_j\geq0\\\sum_jx_j\leq L}}
 e^{\sum_j\alpha_jx_j}\,d\mathbf x
\]
and the simplex monomial evaluation
\[
 \int_{\substack{x_j\geq0\\\sum_jx_j\leq L}}
 x_1\cdots x_n\,d\mathbf x
 =\frac{L^{2k+n}}{(2k+n)!}.
\]
Differentiating the $2k$ shifts, expanding by multiplicities
$m_0,\ldots,m_k$, and applying the same squared-Vandermonde determinant
produces a row-polynomial determinant.  Its top-degree term is Vandermonde
and its vanishing hyperplanes are $m_j-m_i=j-i$; therefore it equals
$\prod_{i<j}(m_j-m_i+i-j)$.  Factoring
$1/(2k-i+m_i)!$ from row $i$ gives
\eqref{eq:crs-bkprime-combinatorial}.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Dehaye's joint-moment programme and the exact angular--radial jet}

Dehaye studies the same value--derivative data in an angular coordinate and then
passes through Schur functions, Frobenius coordinates, and finite determinant sums
\cite{dehaye2008}.  None of the phases or derivative conventions may be silently
identified: the angular derivative contains a translation by $N$, whereas the
completed angular derivative is the pull-back of the CRS radial derivative.  The
following theorem records the complete coordinate map before any asymptotic or
meromorphic continuation is used.

\begin{theorem}[Dehaye angular jet, completed pull-back, and moment transport]
\label{thm:dehaye-angular-jet-crosswalk}
Let $U\in\mathrm U(N)$ have lifted eigenangles $\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_N$ and
put $z_j=e^{\mathrm i\theta_j}$.  Retain
\[
 \Phi_N(s)=\prod_{j=1}^{N}(s-z_j),\qquad
 V_N=\Phi_N(1),\qquad F_N=\frac1N\Phi_N'(1),
\]
and let $\rho_U$ be the phase in \eqref{eq:crs-completed-jet-map}.  Define
Dehaye's angular functions by
\begin{align}
 Z_U^{\mathrm D}(\vartheta)
 &=\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left(1-e^{\mathrm i(\theta_j-\vartheta)}\right),
 \label{eq:dehaye-angular-polynomial}\\
 \mathcal V_U^{\mathrm D}(\vartheta)
 &=e^{\mathrm iN(\vartheta+\pi)/2}
   e^{-\mathrm i\sum_j\theta_j/2}Z_U^{\mathrm D}(\vartheta).
 \label{eq:dehaye-completed-angular-polynomial}
\end{align}
Then:

\begin{enumerate}
 \item On the lifted angular cover $s=e^{\mathrm i\vartheta}$,
 \begin{equation}
  Z_U^{\mathrm D}(\vartheta)
  =e^{-\mathrm iN\vartheta}\Phi_N(e^{\mathrm i\vartheta}).
  \label{eq:dehaye-angular-radial-map}
 \end{equation}
 Consequently the value--angular-derivative jet is
 \begin{equation}
  \begin{pmatrix}Z_U^{\mathrm D}(0)\\(Z_U^{\mathrm D})'(0)\end{pmatrix}
  =
  \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-\mathrm iN&\mathrm i\end{pmatrix}
  \begin{pmatrix}V_N\\\Phi_N'(1)\end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  \begin{pmatrix}V_N\\\Phi_N'(1)\end{pmatrix}
  =
  \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\N&-\mathrm i\end{pmatrix}
  \begin{pmatrix}Z_U^{\mathrm D}(0)\\(Z_U^{\mathrm D})'(0)\end{pmatrix}.
  \label{eq:dehaye-angular-jet-bijection}
 \end{equation}
 This is an everywhere invertible linear map of $\mathbb C^2$, with singleton
 fibres, empty exceptional locus, and no information loss.

 \item The phase is not discarded.  From
 $\chi_U=(-1)^N(\det U)^{-1}$ one obtains exactly
 \[
  \rho_U=e^{\mathrm iN\pi/2}e^{-\mathrm i\sum_j\theta_j/2}.
 \]
 With the branch of $s^{-N/2}$ transported along the same lifted angle,
 \begin{equation}
  \mathcal V_U^{\mathrm D}(\vartheta)
  =\mathcal Z_U(e^{\mathrm i\vartheta}).
  \label{eq:dehaye-crs-completed-pullback}
 \end{equation}
 Hence the two completed first jets satisfy
 \begin{equation}
  \begin{pmatrix}\mathcal V_U^{\mathrm D}(0)\\
  (\mathcal V_U^{\mathrm D})'(0)\end{pmatrix}
  =\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&\mathrm i\end{pmatrix}
  \begin{pmatrix}\mathcal Z_U(1)\\\mathcal Z_U'(1)\end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  \begin{pmatrix}\mathcal Z_U(1)\\\mathcal Z_U'(1)\end{pmatrix}
  =\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-\mathrm i\end{pmatrix}
  \begin{pmatrix}\mathcal V_U^{\mathrm D}(0)\\
  (\mathcal V_U^{\mathrm D})'(0)\end{pmatrix}.
  \label{eq:dehaye-completed-jet-bijection}
 \end{equation}
 These are again bijections with singleton fibres and no exceptional locus.  The
 global map $\vartheta\mapsto e^{\mathrm i\vartheta}$ has fibres
 $\vartheta+2\pi\mathbb Z$ on the unlifted circle; the completed square-root phase
 changes by $(-1)^N$ around one turn.  Equation
 \eqref{eq:dehaye-crs-completed-pullback} therefore lives on the retained lifted
 cover, not on a quotient which forgets that sign.

 \item On $V_N\ne0$, put $u_N=\Phi_N'(1)/\Phi_N(1)$ and retain the
 Cauchy trace $\mathcal T_N$ from \eqref{eq:winn-gamma-trace-map}.  Then
 \begin{align}
  \frac{(Z_U^{\mathrm D})'(0)}{Z_U^{\mathrm D}(0)}
  &=\mathrm i(u_N-N)=-\frac{\mathcal T_N+\mathrm iN}{2},
  \label{eq:dehaye-uncompleted-log-trace}\\
  \frac{(\mathcal V_U^{\mathrm D})'(0)}{\mathcal V_U^{\mathrm D}(0)}
  &=\mathrm i\left(u_N-\frac N2\right)=-\frac{\mathcal T_N}{2}.
  \label{eq:dehaye-completed-log-trace}
 \end{align}
 The quotient exceptional locus is exactly $V_N=0$; the underlying angular and
 completed jets in the first two items remain polynomial there.

 \item Preserve Dehaye's three finite-$N$ moment symbols:
 \begin{align}
  |\mathcal M|_N^{\mathrm D}(2k,r)
  &=\int_{\mathrm U(N)}|Z_U^{\mathrm D}(0)|^{2k}
    \left|\frac{(Z_U^{\mathrm D})'(0)}{Z_U^{\mathrm D}(0)}\right|^r dU,
  \label{eq:dehaye-absolute-M-definition}\\
  (\mathcal M)_N^{\mathrm D}(2k,r)
  &=\int_{\mathrm U(N)}|Z_U^{\mathrm D}(0)|^{2k}
    \left(\frac{(Z_U^{\mathrm D})'(0)}{Z_U^{\mathrm D}(0)}\right)^r dU,
  \label{eq:dehaye-complex-M-definition}\\
  |\mathcal V|_N^{\mathrm D}(2k,r)
  &=\int_{\mathrm U(N)}|\mathcal V_U^{\mathrm D}(0)|^{2k}
    \left|\frac{(\mathcal V_U^{\mathrm D})'(0)}
    {\mathcal V_U^{\mathrm D}(0)}\right|^r dU.
  \label{eq:dehaye-absolute-V-definition}
 \end{align}
 For $k\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}$ and real $r\geq0$ with $2k-r>-1$, the
 exact determinant-tilt transports are
 \begin{align}
  |\mathcal M|_N^{\mathrm D}(2k,r)
  &=2^{-r}\mathcal Z_{N,k}
    \mathbb E_{\Pi_{N,k}}(\mathcal T_N^2+N^2)^{r/2},
  \label{eq:dehaye-absolute-M-trace}\\
  |\mathcal V|_N^{\mathrm D}(2k,r)
  &=2^{-r}\mathcal Z_{N,k}
    \mathbb E_{\Pi_{N,k}}|\mathcal T_N|^r.
  \label{eq:dehaye-absolute-V-trace}
 \end{align}
 For $r\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}$ in the same integrability range,
 \begin{equation}
  (\mathcal M)_N^{\mathrm D}(2k,r)
  =\left(-\frac12\right)^r\mathcal Z_{N,k}
   \mathbb E_{\Pi_{N,k}}(\mathcal T_N+\mathrm iN)^r.
  \label{eq:dehaye-complex-M-trace}
 \end{equation}
 In particular, for $r=2h$,
 $|\mathcal V|_N^{\mathrm D}(2k,2h)=\mathcal F_N(k,h)$ exactly.
 The expectation map is a scalar observable, not an injective map on probability
 laws: its fibre consists of all laws having the displayed moment.  On finite signed
 measures its kernel is the explicit hyperplane on which the integral of the displayed
 observable is zero.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For every $j$,
\[
 e^{-\mathrm i\vartheta}(e^{\mathrm i\vartheta}-z_j)
 =1-z_je^{-\mathrm i\vartheta},
\]
so multiplication proves \eqref{eq:dehaye-angular-radial-map}.  Evaluation and the
chain rule give
\[
 Z_U^{\mathrm D}(0)=V_N,\qquad
 (Z_U^{\mathrm D})'(0)=\mathrm i\bigl(\Phi_N'(1)-NV_N\bigr),
\]
which is the first matrix in \eqref{eq:dehaye-angular-jet-bijection}.  Direct matrix
multiplication proves the displayed inverse.

Using $\det U=e^{\mathrm i\sum_j\theta_j}$ in the definition of $\rho_U$ gives
the retained phase formula.  Substitution of
\eqref{eq:dehaye-angular-radial-map} into
\eqref{eq:dehaye-completed-angular-polynomial} then gives
\[
 \mathcal V_U^{\mathrm D}(\vartheta)
 =\rho_Ue^{-\mathrm iN\vartheta/2}\Phi_N(e^{\mathrm i\vartheta})
 =\mathcal Z_U(e^{\mathrm i\vartheta}),
\]
including the branch on the lifted cover.  The chain rule at zero gives the diagonal
jet map and its inverse.

Division of the angular derivative identity by $V_N$ gives
$\mathrm i(u_N-N)$.  Equation \eqref{eq:winn-terminal-log-derivative} says
$u_N=N/2+\mathrm i\mathcal T_N/2$, proving
\eqref{eq:dehaye-uncompleted-log-trace}.  The completed identity follows either by
differentiating its phase or from the diagonal jet map and
\eqref{eq:crs-z-derivative-trace}.

Finally $|Z_U^{\mathrm D}(0)|=|V_N|$ and the exact Radon--Nikodym formula
\eqref{eq:conditional-radon-nikodym} changes Haar integration weighted by
$|V_N|^{2k}$ into $\mathcal Z_{N,k}\Pi_{N,k}$.  Substitution of the two logarithmic
derivatives proves \eqref{eq:dehaye-absolute-M-trace}--
\eqref{eq:dehaye-complex-M-trace}.  The source's local integrability calculation at
the divisor $V_N=0$ is precisely $2k-r>-1$.
\end{proof}

The finite algebra above immediately reproduces Dehaye's four triangular relations.
For $h\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}$, the two finite-$N$ relations are
\begin{align}
 |\mathcal M|_N^{\mathrm D}(2k,2h)
 &=\sum_{j=0}^{h}(\mathrm iN)^{h-j}\binom hj
   (\mathcal M)_N^{\mathrm D}(2k,h+j),
 \label{eq:dehaye-finite-relation-one}\\
 |\mathcal V|_N^{\mathrm D}(2k,2h)
 &=\sum_{j=0}^{h}\binom hj\left(-\frac{N^2}{4}\right)^{h-j}
   |\mathcal M|_N^{\mathrm D}(2k,2j).
 \label{eq:dehaye-finite-relation-three}
\end{align}
After division by $N^{k^2+2h}$ and passage to the corresponding existing limits,
the remaining two relations are literally
\begin{align}
 |\mathcal M|^{\mathrm D}(2k,2h)
 &=\sum_{j=0}^{h}\mathrm i^{,h-j}\binom hj
   (\mathcal M)^{\mathrm D}(2k,h+j),
 \label{eq:dehaye-limit-relation-two}\\
 |\mathcal V|^{\mathrm D}(2k,2h)
 &=\sum_{j=0}^{h}\binom hj\left(-\frac14\right)^{h-j}
   |\mathcal M|^{\mathrm D}(2k,2j).
 \label{eq:dehaye-limit-relation-four}
\end{align}
All four are finite binomial transforms at integer $h$.  Dehaye also records the
separate integer identity
\begin{equation}
 |\mathcal V|^{\mathrm D}(2k,2h)
 =\sum_{j=0}^{2h}\binom{2h}{j}
   \left(\frac{\mathrm i}{2}\right)^j
   (\mathcal M)^{\mathrm D}(2k,2h-j),
 \label{eq:dehaye-not-analytic-continuation}
\end{equation}
but explicitly warns that its right side is not the analytic continuation in $h$ of
the left side: its derivation uses $|x|^{2h}=x^{2h}$ for real $x$ and integer $h$
\cite[\S2]{dehaye2008}.

We now record the source theorem without merging proved random-matrix statements with
its number-theoretic proposals.  Put
\begin{equation}
 g(k)=\frac{G(k+1)^2}{G(2k+1)},\qquad
 Y_r(u)=\prod_{\substack{1\leq a\leq r-1\\a\ \mathrm{odd}}}
 (u^2-a^2)^{\alpha_a(r)},\qquad
 \alpha_a(r)=\left\lfloor\frac{-a+\sqrt{a^2+4r}}2\right\rfloor.
 \label{eq:dehaye-g-Y-alpha}
\end{equation}
For those real parameters for which the integrals exist, define the scaled limits
by retaining Dehaye's normalization:
\[
 \mathcal A(2k,r)=\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{-k^2-r}\mathcal A_N(2k,r),
 \qquad
 \mathcal A\in\{|\mathcal M|^{\mathrm D},(\mathcal M)^{\mathrm D},
 |\mathcal V|^{\mathrm D}\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Dehaye's sourced rationality and determinant formulas]
\label{thm:dehaye-rational-source}
For $r\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}$, Dehaye proves, as a meromorphic function of $k$,
\begin{equation}
 (\mathcal M)^{\mathrm D}(2k,r)
 =\left(-\frac{\mathrm i}{2}\right)^r g(k)
   \frac{X_r(2k)}{Y_r(2k)},
 \label{eq:dehaye-complex-rational-form}
\end{equation}
where $X_r$ and $Y_r$ are even monic polynomials in $\mathbb Z[u]$ of equal
degree and $Y_r$ is the literal product in \eqref{eq:dehaye-g-Y-alpha}.

For $h\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}$, he also proves the existence of integer polynomials
$\widehat X_{2h}$ and $\widetilde X_{2h}$ and constants
$\widehat C(h),\widetilde C(h)$ such that
\begin{align}
 |\mathcal M|^{\mathrm D}(2k,2h)
 &=\widehat C(h)g(k)\frac{\widehat X_{2h}(2k)}{Y_{2h}(2k)},
 \label{eq:dehaye-absolute-M-rational-form}\\
 |\mathcal V|^{\mathrm D}(2k,2h)
 &=\widetilde C(h)g(k)\frac{\widetilde X_{2h}(2k)}{Y_{2h}(2k)},
 \label{eq:dehaye-absolute-V-rational-form}
\end{align}
with
$\deg\widehat X_{2h}=\deg X_{2h}>\deg\widetilde X_{2h}$.
The further assertions
\[
 \widehat C(h)=2^{-2h},\qquad
 \widetilde C(h)=\frac{(2h)!}{h!2^{3h}},\qquad
 \deg X_{2h}-\deg\widetilde X_{2h}=2h,
\]
with both displayed numerators monic, are labelled conjectural in the source and are
not part of the proved theorem.
\end{theorem}

Here is the complete algebraic-combinatorial route to that statement.  If
$\lambda=\Frob{\sort{s}}{\sort{t}}$ has rank $d$, Dehaye uses the reduced
El--Samra--King determinant
\begin{equation}
 s_\lambda(1^R)=
 \det_{1\leq i,j\leq d}
 \left[\frac{(R+s_i)!}{(R-t_j-1)!s_i!t_j!(1+s_i+t_j)}\right]
 \label{eq:dehaye-frobenius-dimension}
\end{equation}
and its Cauchy-product expansion.  For vectors with repeated coordinates the
alternating extension is zero; thus no division by an informal ordering choice is
made.  The rectangle insertion is the exact identity
\begin{multline}
 s_{\langle N^k\rangle\cup\Frob{\vect{s}}{\vect{t}}}(1^{2k})
 =s_{\langle N^k\rangle}(1^{2k})s_{\Frob{\vect{s}}{\vect{t}}}(1^{2k})\\
 {}\times\prod_{i=1}^{d}
 \frac{(N-s_i)^{(k)}(k-t_i)^{(k)}}
 {(s_i+k+1)^{(k)}(N+t_i+1)^{(k)}}.
 \label{eq:dehaye-rectangle-insertion}
\end{multline}

The dual Cauchy identity, Abel-regularized logarithmic derivative, Murnaghan--
Nakayama rule, and $\mathrm U(N)$ character orthogonality first give
\begin{equation}
 (\mathcal M)_N^{\mathrm D}(2k,r)
 =(-\mathrm i)^r
 \sum_{\boldsymbol\mu\in\mathbb N_+^r}
 \sum_{\lambda\subseteq\langle N^k\rangle}
 \chi_{\boldsymbol\mu}^{\lambda}
 s_{\langle N^k\rangle\cup\lambda}(1^{2k}).
 \label{eq:dehaye-finite-schur-series}
\end{equation}
The sum is finite after character orthogonality: only partitions between the
$k\times N$ and $2k\times N$ rectangles survive.  This finiteness also justifies
removing the Abel parameter by dominated convergence precisely in the range
$2k-r>-1$.

For completeness, the filled-structure count used to evaluate
\eqref{eq:dehaye-finite-schur-series} is
\begin{multline}
 \#T(r,d,\vect p,\vect q,v)
 =\sum_{\substack{\vect s,\vect t\in\mathbb N^d\\
 \sum_i t_i=v,\ d+\sum_i(s_i+t_i)=r}}
 \frac{r!\prod_i\binom{p_i}{s_i}\binom{q_i}{t_i}}
 {\prod_i s_i!t_i!\prod_{i=1}^{d}
  \left(d+1-i+\sum_{j=i}^{d}(s_j+t_j)\right)}.
 \label{eq:dehaye-filled-structure-count}
\end{multline}
Antisymmetrizing the unsorted Frobenius coordinates uses the exact determinant
identity
\begin{multline}
 \sum_{\sigma,\tau\in\mathfrak S_d}
 \frac{\sgn(\sigma)\sgn(\tau)}
 {\prod_{i=1}^{d}\left(d+1-i+
  \sum_{j=i}^{d}(s_{\sigma(j)}+t_{\tau(j)})\right)}\\
 =\prod_{i<j}(s_i-s_j)(t_i-t_j)
  \prod_{i,j=1}^{d}\frac1{1+s_i+t_j}.
 \label{eq:dehaye-alternating-cauchy-identity}
\end{multline}
The two sides have the same alternating zeros, the same poles, and the same degree;
the proportionality constant is fixed to one by sending one $s$ coordinate to
infinity.

Define the finite generating series
$\mathcal E_{N,k}(z)=\sum_{r>0}(\mathcal M)_N^{\mathrm D}(2k,r)
(\mathrm iz)^r/r!$.  Dehaye's exact finite-$N$ determinant formula states that,
coefficientwise for $r<2k+1$,
\begin{multline}
 \mathcal E_{N,k}(z)=s_{\langle N^k\rangle}(1^{2k})
 \sum_{d\geq1}\sum_{\substack{\sort{s},\sort{t}\in\mathbb N^d}}
 \det_{i,j}\left[\frac{z^{1+s_i+t_j}}
 {s_i!t_j!(1+s_i+t_j)}\right]\\
 {}\times\sum_{\substack{\vect p\in[0,N-1]^d\\
 \vect q\in[0,k-1]^d}}
 \det_{i,j}\left[
 \frac{k\binom{p_i}{s_i}\binom{q_j}{t_j}
 \binom{k+p_i}{p_i}\binom{k-1}{q_j}(N-p_i)^{(k)}(-1)^{q_j}}
 {(N+q_j+1)^{(k)}(1+p_i+q_j)}
 \right].
 \label{eq:dehaye-finite-determinant-series}
\end{multline}
Every coefficient is a finite sum because $s_i\leq p_i$ and $t_j\leq q_j$.

The single kernel remaining in the large-$N$ limit is
\begin{align}
 H^{N,k,s,t}
 &=s!t!\sum_{\substack{0\leq p<N\\0\leq q<k}}
 \frac{k(N-p)^{(k)}(-1)^q}{(N+q+1)^{(k)}(1+p+q)}
 \binom{k+p}{p}\binom{k-1}{q}\binom ps\binom qt,
 \label{eq:dehaye-finite-H-kernel}\\
 H^{k,s,t}
 &=\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{-1-s-t}H^{N,k,s,t}
 =\frac1{1+s+t}
 \frac{\Gamma(2k-t)\Gamma(k+s+1)}
 {\Gamma(k-t)\Gamma(2k+s+1)}
 \label{eq:dehaye-limit-H-kernel}\\
 &=\frac1{1+s+t}\prod_{a=-t}^{s}\frac{k+a}{2k+a}.
 \label{eq:dehaye-H-rational-product}
\end{align}
Initially $k$ is an integer with $k>t$.  The proof expands the $q$ dependence and
uses
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\sum_{q=0}^{k-1}(-1)^q\binom{k-1}{q}q^jX^j
 =(-1)^{k+1}(k-1)!X^{k-1};
\]
all lower powers cancel.  The remaining $p$ sum is a Riemann sum for a beta integral.
The resulting terminating sum is evaluated by the stated Saalsch\"utzian
${}_3F_2(1)$ identity, giving \eqref{eq:dehaye-limit-H-kernel}; gamma recurrence
gives the product form.

Substitution into \eqref{eq:dehaye-finite-determinant-series}, together with
$s_{\langle N^k\rangle}(1^{2k})\sim g(k)N^{k^2}$, gives the coefficientwise
asymptotic series
\begin{multline}
 \sum_{r>0}(\mathcal M)^{\mathrm D}(2k,r)\frac{(\mathrm iz)^r}{r!}
 =g(k)\sum_{d\geq1}
 \sum_{\sort{s},\sort{t}\in\mathbb N^d}
 \det_{i,j}\left[\frac1{s_i!t_j!(1+s_i+t_j)}\right]^2\\
 {}\times\prod_{i,j=1}^{d}
 \frac{\Gamma(2k-t_j)\Gamma(k+s_i+1)}
 {\Gamma(k-t_j)\Gamma(2k+s_i+1)}
 z^{d+\sum_i(s_i+t_i)}.
 \label{eq:dehaye-asymptotic-determinant-series}
\end{multline}
At integer $k$ this initially covers coefficients $r<2k+1$.  For each fixed $r$,
the source invokes a Pochhammer-contour continuation on the integral side and
Carlson's theorem on the difference to continue meromorphically in $k$.  The
growth verification is attributed also to Dehaye's thesis; it remains a human-source
analytic proof boundary, not an axiom in the finite coordinate certificate.

Equation \eqref{eq:dehaye-H-rational-product} makes every coefficient a rational
function of $k$, satisfies $H^{k,s,t}=H^{-k,t,s}$, and exposes possible poles only
at the odd coordinates $2k=\pm a$.  Minimizing
$r=d+\sum_i(s_i+t_i)$ subject to distinct Frobenius coordinates gives exactly the
exponent $\alpha_a(r)$ in \eqref{eq:dehaye-g-Y-alpha}.  The source fixes the monic
normalization through
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{\substack{d\geq1,\ \sort{s},\sort{t}\in\mathbb N^d\\
 d+\sum_i(s_i+t_i)=r}}
 \det_{i,j}\left[\frac1{s_i!t_j!(1+s_i+t_j)}\right]^2
 2^{-r}=\frac1{r!2^r}.
 \label{eq:dehaye-monic-normalization-sum}
\end{equation}
The final equality is left to the reader in the source.  It is part of the published
proof of the general theorem; independently, the attached coefficient certificate
below verifies every resulting monicity and transformation through the complete
provided range, not for arbitrary $r$.

There is a second, lossless presentation of the same coefficient data.  Define
Macdonald's ninth variation by
\[
 \widetilde h_m^{(R)}=\frac{(R-1)!}{(R+m-1)!m!},\qquad
 \widetilde s_\lambda^{(R)}=
 \det\left[\widetilde h_{\lambda_i-i+j}^{(R-j+1)}\right].
\]
Dehaye proves the Giambelli form
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde s_\lambda^{(R)}
 =\det_{1\leq i,j\leq d}
 \left[\frac{\Gamma(R-t_j)/\Gamma(R+s_i+1)}
 {s_i!t_j!(1+s_i+t_j)}\right]
 \label{eq:dehaye-ninth-variation-giambelli}
\end{equation}
by an induction on hook partitions followed by the standard Giambelli exercise.
Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{r>0}(\mathcal M)^{\mathrm D}(2k,r)\frac{(\mathrm iz)^r}{r!}
 =g(k)\sum_\lambda \widetilde s_\lambda^{(2k)}s_\lambda(1^k)z^{|\lambda|}
 \label{eq:dehaye-ninth-variation-series}
\end{equation}
coefficientwise for fixed $r$ and sufficiently large integer $k$, followed by the
same meromorphic-continuation boundary.  An attempted Gessel--Cauchy reduction ends
with a matrix which is not Toeplitz, so the proposed Szeg\H{o} step does not apply;
the source records this as a failed simplification, not a theorem.

\subsubsection{Shifted-Hurwitz source boundary and exact finite
\texorpdfstring{$U(2)$}{U(2)} coordinates}

The standard analytic input is narrower than the local consequences drawn from it.  For
$\Re a>0$, the Hurwitz function $\zeta(s,a)$ is meromorphic in $s$, with its only singularity
a simple pole at $s=1$ of residue $1$, and is analytic in $a$ for fixed $s\ne1$.  Moreover
\begin{equation}
 \zeta(0,a)=\frac12-a,
 \qquad
 \zeta(s,a)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
 \frac{(s)_n}{n!}\zeta(s+n)(1-a)^n
 \quad (|a-1|<1),
 \label{eq:hurwitz-apostol-coordinates}
\end{equation}
where the second identity is an identity of meromorphic functions of $s$ away from its
displayed singular coordinates.  These are Apostol's equations 25.11.1, 25.11.10,
25.11.13, and 25.11.17 in DLMF Chapter~25
\cite[\S25.11]{apostolDLMF25}.  Substituting $a=1+ty$ in
\eqref{eq:hurwitz-apostol-coordinates} gives the local shifted expansion with the literal
factor $(-t)^n$; if $0\le y\le L$ and $|t|L<1$, its convergence is uniform in $y$ on compact
$s$-sets avoiding the resulting poles.  Integration against a compact probability measure
then gives the moment coefficients without changing variables or deleting any endpoint.

Fekih-Ahmed gives additional Hurwitz representations, but one proof in that source does not
establish its stated full parameter range $0<a\le1$: the final domination writes
$e^{-at}=e^{-t/2}e^{-(a-1/2)t}$ and evaluates the latter gamma integral, which requires
$a>1/2$ \cite[proof of Thm.~1, eqs.~(20)--(23)]{fekihAhmed2012}.  No statement below uses
that estimate.  The pole, value, parameter derivative, and Taylor coordinates are taken from
the Apostol source just cited; the compact-measure and zero-deformation consequences remain
independent local arguments.

\begin{theorem}[Completed compact shifted-Hurwitz principal part]
\label{thm:compact-shifted-hurwitz-principal-part}
Let $\mu$ be a probability measure supported in $[0,L]$, let
\[
 m_n=\int_0^L y^n\,d\mu(y),
 \qquad
 Z_{\mu,t}(s)=\int_0^L\zeta(s,1+ty)\,d\mu(y),
\]
and assume $1+ty>0$ on the support.  Define
\[
 \Lambda_{\mu,t}(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)Z_{\mu,t}(s),
 \qquad
 u=s(s-1),\quad v=2s-1,\quad q=\frac1u,\quad\ell=\frac vu.
\]
Then
\begin{align}
 \operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}\Lambda_{\mu,t}(s)&=1,\\
 \operatorname*{Res}_{s=0}\Lambda_{\mu,t}(s)&=-1-2tm_1,
 \label{eq:compact-hurwitz-endpoint-residues}\\
 \operatorname{PP}_{\{0,1\}}\Lambda_{\mu,t}
 &=(1+tm_1)q-tm_1\ell.
 \label{eq:compact-hurwitz-principal-part}
\end{align}
The sum of the two finite endpoint residues is $-2tm_1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Hurwitz pole at $s=1$ has residue $1$, independent of its positive second argument.
Because $\mu$ has total mass one,
$\operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}Z_{\mu,t}=1$.  The completing factor at $s=1$ is
$\pi^{-1/2}\Gamma(1/2)=1$, giving the first residue.

At $s=0$, \eqref{eq:hurwitz-apostol-coordinates} gives
\[
 Z_{\mu,t}(0)
 =\int_0^L\left(\frac12-(1+ty)\right)d\mu(y)
 =-\frac12-tm_1.
\]
Since $\Gamma(s/2)=2/s+O(1)$ and $\pi^{-s/2}=1+O(s)$, the residue of the
completion is twice this value, proving \eqref{eq:compact-hurwitz-endpoint-residues}.

Write the endpoint principal part as $a/s+b/(s-1)$.  The two residues just computed give
$a=-1-2tm_1$ and $b=1$.  The retained quotient and lift coordinates satisfy
\[
 q=\frac1{s-1}-\frac1s,
 \qquad
 \ell=\frac1{s-1}+\frac1s.
\]
Thus $Aq+B\ell$ has endpoint residues $(-A+B,A+B)$.  The coordinate map
\[
 (A,B)\longmapsto(-A+B,A+B)
\]
has inverse $(a,b)\mapsto((b-a)/2,(a+b)/2)$, zero kernel, full image, and singleton fibres.
Substituting $(a,b)=(-1-2tm_1,1)$ gives $A=1+tm_1$ and $B=-tm_1$, which is
\eqref{eq:compact-hurwitz-principal-part}.  Adding the two residues proves the final
statement.
\end{proof}

For the zero-deformation calculation one needs the full moment lattice, not only $m_1$.
When $|t|L<1$, the uniform substitution into
\eqref{eq:hurwitz-apostol-coordinates} gives
\begin{equation}
 Z_{\mu,t}(s)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
 (-t)^n\frac{(s)_n}{n!}m_n\zeta(s+n),
 \label{eq:compact-hurwitz-moment-expansion}
\end{equation}
first in the common defining half-plane and then meromorphically in $s$.

\begin{theorem}[All-order motion of a simple zeta zero]
\label{thm:compact-shifted-hurwitz-zero-motion}
Let $\rho$ be a simple nontrivial zero of $\zeta$, and put
\[
 F_n(s)=(-1)^n\frac{(s)_n}{n!}m_n\zeta(s+n),
 \qquad
 a_{n,j}=\frac1{j!}F_n^{(j)}(\rho).
\]
For sufficiently small $t$, there is a unique holomorphic branch
\[
 \rho_\mu(t)=\rho+\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}c_Nt^N
\]
with $Z_{\mu,t}(\rho_\mu(t))=0$.  Its coefficients are determined without discarded orders
by
\begin{equation}
 c_N=-\frac1{\zeta'(\rho)}
 \sum_{\substack{n,j\ge0\\(n,j)\ne(0,1)}}
 a_{n,j}[t^N]\left[
 t^n\left(\sum_{m=1}^{N-1}c_mt^m\right)^j
 \right].
 \label{eq:compact-hurwitz-zero-recursion}
\end{equation}
Only finitely many summands contribute to a fixed $N$.  In particular,
\begin{align}
 c_1&=m_1\frac{\rho\zeta(\rho+1)}{\zeta'(\rho)},\\
 c_2&=-\frac{
 \frac12\zeta''(\rho)c_1^2+F_1'(\rho)c_1+F_2(\rho)}
 {\zeta'(\rho)},
 \label{eq:compact-hurwitz-first-two-zero-coordinates}
\end{align}
where
\[
 F_1(s)=-s\,m_1\zeta(s+1),
 \qquad
 F_2(s)=\frac{s(s+1)}2m_2\zeta(s+2).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
No shifted term $\rho+n$ equals $1$.  Hence
\eqref{eq:compact-hurwitz-moment-expansion} is holomorphic in a neighborhood of
$(s,t)=(\rho,0)$.  At $t=0$ it is $\zeta(s)$, and its $s$-derivative at $\rho$ is the
nonzero number $\zeta'(\rho)$.  The analytic implicit-function theorem gives the unique
holomorphic branch.

Set $w(t)=\rho_\mu(t)-\rho$.  Taylor expansion in the retained $s$ coordinate gives
\[
 0=Z_{\mu,t}(\rho+w(t))
 =\sum_{n,j\ge0}a_{n,j}t^nw(t)^j.
\]
Here $a_{0,0}=\zeta(\rho)=0$ and $a_{0,1}=\zeta'(\rho)$.  In the coefficient of $t^N$,
the single term $(n,j)=(0,1)$ contributes $\zeta'(\rho)c_N$.  Every other term depends
only on $c_1,\ldots,c_{N-1}$: if $n\ge1$, the leading $t^n$ lowers the required coefficient
of $w^j$, while if $n=0$ and $j\ge2$, a monomial containing $c_Nt^N$ also contains at
least one further positive power of $t$.  Thus $w$ may be replaced by its truncation through
order $N-1$ in the remaining sum.  Solving the coefficient equation for $c_N$ proves
\eqref{eq:compact-hurwitz-zero-recursion}.  The bounds $n\le N$ and $j\le N$ make the sum
finite.  Collecting orders one and two gives
\eqref{eq:compact-hurwitz-first-two-zero-coordinates}.
\end{proof}

The theorem follows each simple zero only in its local implicit branch.  It does not assert
that the moment sequence is recovered injectively from the branches, that different branches
remain disjoint globally, or that any line or region is preserved under the deformation.

The weak-measure reconstruction used by the local shifted-Hurwitz programme also has a
terminal classical source.  Bernstein's original two-page proof introduces
\[
 B_mf(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}f(j/m)\binom mjx^j(1-x)^{m-j}
\]
and proves $B_mf\to f$ uniformly for every $f\in C([0,1])$
\cite[eqs.~(1)--(5)]{bernstein1912}.  Retaining all measure coordinates gives the following
exact consequence.

\begin{proposition}[Bernstein pushforward reconstruction]
\label{prop:bernstein-measure-reconstruction}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on $[0,1]$ and set
\[
 \lambda_{m,j}=\binom mj\int_0^1x^j(1-x)^{m-j}\,d\nu(x),
 \qquad
 \nu_m=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\lambda_{m,j}\delta_{j/m}.
\]
Then every $\lambda_{m,j}$ is nonnegative,
$\sum_{j=0}^{m}\lambda_{m,j}=1$, and $\nu_m\Rightarrow\nu$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Nonnegativity is pointwise, and the binomial identity gives
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{m}\lambda_{m,j}
 =\int_0^1\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom mjx^j(1-x)^{m-j}\,d\nu(x)
 =\int_0^1 1\,d\nu=1.
\]
For every $f\in C([0,1])$,
\[
 \int f\,d\nu_m
 =\int_0^1 B_mf(x)\,d\nu(x).
\]
Hence
\[
 \left|\int f\,d\nu_m-\int f\,d\nu\right|
 \le \lVert B_mf-f\rVert_\infty\longrightarrow0
\]
by Bernstein's uniform approximation theorem.  This is exactly weak convergence on the
compact interval.
\end{proof}

We next close the proof omitted by the local exact-$N=2$ statement.  Put
$\mathbb T=\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)$, let $U$ be Haar distributed in $U(2)$, and set
\[
 \Phi_2(z)=\det(zI-U),
 \qquad
 X_2=|\Phi_2'(1)|^2.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact Haar \texorpdfstring{$U(2)$}{U(2)} derivative law]
\label{thm:u2-exact-derivative-law}
There are independent coordinates
\[
 R\sim\operatorname{Beta}\!\left(\frac12,\frac32\right),
 \qquad
 \phi\sim\operatorname{Unif}(\mathbb T),
\]
for which
\begin{equation}
 X_2=4\bigl(1+R-2\sqrt R\cos\phi\bigr)
     =4\left|1-\sqrt R\,e^{i\phi}\right|^2.
 \label{eq:u2-exact-law}
\end{equation}
The density of $R$ is
\begin{equation}
 f_R(r)=\frac2\pi r^{-1/2}(1-r)^{1/2},
 \qquad 0<r<1.
 \label{eq:u2-beta-density}
\end{equation}
The folding map used to obtain $(R,\phi)$ has two-point fibres in the interior and
single boundary fibres after the periodic endpoint is identified.  The support of $X_2$ is
$[0,16]$, and $\mathbb E X_2=5$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write the eigenvalues as $z_j=e^{i\theta_j}$.  The normalized Haar eigenangle density is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{1}{2!(2\pi)^2}
 |e^{i\theta_1}-e^{i\theta_2}|^2\,d\theta_1\,d\theta_2
 \label{eq:u2-haar-density}
\end{equation}
by the Weyl integration formula \cite[eq.~(1.3)]{forrester2022}.  The torus automorphism
\[
 (\theta_1,\theta_2)\longmapsto
 (\alpha,\beta)=(\theta_1-\theta_2,\theta_2)
\]
has integer matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-1\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, determinant
$1$, inverse $(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto(\alpha+\beta,\beta)$, zero kernel, full image, and
singleton fibres.  Therefore it preserves torus Lebesgue measure.  Since
$|e^{i\alpha}-1|^2=4\sin^2(\alpha/2)$, the joint density becomes
\[
 \frac{\sin^2(\alpha/2)}{2\pi^2}\,d\alpha\,d\beta.
\]

Choose the half-open representative $\alpha\in[0,2\pi)$ and put
\[
 \delta=\frac{\alpha}{2}\in[0,\pi),
 \qquad
 \phi_0=\beta+\delta\pmod{2\pi}.
\]
For fixed $\delta$, the second coordinate is a circle translation, so its Jacobian is one and
its inverse is $\beta=\phi_0-\delta$.  Thus $\delta$ and $\phi_0$ are independent, $\phi_0$
is uniform, and
\begin{equation}
 f_\delta(\delta)=\frac2\pi\sin^2\delta.
 \label{eq:u2-delta-density}
\end{equation}
The two eigenvalues retain the exact centre-and-difference coordinates
\[
 z_1=e^{i(\phi_0+\delta)},
 \qquad
 z_2=e^{i(\phi_0-\delta)}.
\]
Consequently
\begin{align}
 \Phi_2'(1)&=2-z_1-z_2=2-2e^{i\phi_0}\cos\delta,\\
 X_2&=4\bigl(1+\cos^2\delta-2\cos\delta\cos\phi_0\bigr).
 \label{eq:u2-centre-difference-law}
\end{align}

Now retain the sign lost by squaring the cosine in a translated phase coordinate:
\begin{equation}
 r=\cos^2\delta,
 \qquad
 \phi=
 \begin{cases}
  \phi_0,&0\le\delta\le\pi/2,\\
  \phi_0+\pi\pmod{2\pi},&\pi/2<\delta<\pi.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:u2-folding-map}
\end{equation}
For $0<r<1$, the two inverse branches are
\begin{equation}
 (\delta,\phi_0)=
 \left(\arccos\sqrt r,\phi\right),
 \qquad
 \left(\pi-\arccos\sqrt r,\phi-\pi\right),
 \label{eq:u2-folding-fibres}
\end{equation}
and on either branch
\[
 \left|\frac{dr}{d\delta}\right|
 =2\sqrt r\sqrt{1-r}.
\]
Both branches have the density $(2/\pi)(1-r)$ inherited from
\eqref{eq:u2-delta-density}.  Summing their Jacobian contributions gives
\[
 2\frac{(2/\pi)(1-r)}{2\sqrt r\sqrt{1-r}}
 =\frac2\pi r^{-1/2}(1-r)^{1/2}.
\]
This integrates to one because
$B(1/2,3/2)=\Gamma(1/2)\Gamma(3/2)/\Gamma(2)=\pi/2$.  The phase change on each branch is a
circle translation.  More explicitly, the joint pushforward density is
\[
 \frac2\pi r^{-1/2}(1-r)^{1/2}\,dr\,\frac{d\phi}{2\pi},
\]
which proves both the beta law and independence.  At $r=0$ the two formulas in
\eqref{eq:u2-folding-fibres} meet at $\delta=\pi/2$; at $r=1$, the representatives
$\delta=0$ and $\delta=\pi$ are the same periodic $\alpha$-point.  These null boundary
coordinates therefore have single fibres on the torus, while every interior fibre has the
two points displayed in \eqref{eq:u2-folding-fibres}.

By construction,
$\cos\delta\cos\phi_0=\sqrt r\cos\phi$.  Substitution into
\eqref{eq:u2-centre-difference-law} proves \eqref{eq:u2-exact-law}.  The product measure has
full topological support $[0,1]\times\mathbb T$, and at $r=1$ the expression
$8(1-\cos\phi)$ ranges over $[0,16]$, proving the support statement.  Finally,
\[
 \mathbb E R=\frac{1/2}{1/2+3/2}=\frac14,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\cos\phi=0,
\]
so \eqref{eq:u2-exact-law} gives $\mathbb E X_2=4(1+1/4)=5$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact \texorpdfstring{$U(2)$}{U(2)} shifted-Hurwitz transform]
\label{cor:u2-shifted-hurwitz-transform}
For $t\ge0$, define
\[
 Z_{2,t}(s)=\mathbb E\,\zeta(s,1+tX_2).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
 Z_{2,t}(s)=\frac2\pi\int_0^1r^{-1/2}(1-r)^{1/2}
 \left[
  \frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
  \zeta\!\left(s,1+4t(1+r-2\sqrt r\cos\phi)\right)\,d\phi
 \right]dr.
 \label{eq:u2-hurwitz-integral}
\end{equation}
For $\Re s>1$, this equals
\begin{equation}
 \frac2\pi\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_0^1
 r^{-1/2}(1-r)^{1/2}A_n(r)^{-s}
 {}_2F_1\!\left(
 \frac s2,\frac{s+1}{2};1;\frac{B(r)^2}{A_n(r)^2}
 \right)dr,
 \label{eq:u2-hurwitz-hypergeometric}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 A_n(r)=n+1+4t(1+r),
 \qquad
 B(r)=8t\sqrt r.
 \label{eq:u2-An-B}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:u2-hurwitz-integral} is the pushforward formula from
Theorem~\ref{thm:u2-exact-derivative-law}.  If $\sigma=\Re s>1$, then
\[
 \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
 \mathbb E\left|(n+1+tX_2)^{-s}\right|
 \le\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+1)^{-\sigma}<\infty.
\]
Fubini's theorem therefore permits the Hurwitz series to pass through both probability
integrals.  Its $n$th denominator is
\[
 n+1+tX_2=A_n(r)-B(r)\cos\phi,
\]
and
\begin{equation}
 A_n(r)-B(r)=n+1+4t(1-\sqrt r)^2>0,
 \label{eq:u2-positive-denominator}
\end{equation}
so $0\le B(r)/A_n(r)<1$.

For arbitrary complex $s$ and $0\le q<1$, the binomial series is uniformly absolutely
convergent in $\phi$:
\[
 (1-q\cos\phi)^{-s}
 =\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(s)_k}{k!}q^k\cos^k\phi.
\]
The odd powers average to zero, while
\[
 \frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\cos^{2m}\phi\,d\phi
 =\frac{(2m)!}{4^m(m!)^2}.
\]
Using the duplication identity
$(s)_{2m}=4^m(s/2)_m((s+1)/2)_m$ gives
\begin{align}
 \frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}(A-B\cos\phi)^{-s}\,d\phi
 &=A^{-s}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}
 \frac{(s/2)_m((s+1)/2)_m}{(m!)^2}
 \left(\frac{B^2}{A^2}\right)^m\\
 &=A^{-s}{}_2F_1\!\left(
 \frac s2,\frac{s+1}{2};1;\frac{B^2}{A^2}
 \right).
 \label{eq:u2-angular-hypergeometric}
\end{align}
This also fixes the beta and Gauss-hypergeometric conventions used by
\eqref{eq:u2-hurwitz-hypergeometric}; compare Forrester's equations (J1), (J1x), and (J2)
\cite{forrester2022}.  Applying \eqref{eq:u2-angular-hypergeometric} term by term proves
\eqref{eq:u2-hurwitz-hypergeometric}.
\end{proof}

\eqref{eq:u2-exact-law} or \eqref{eq:u2-hurwitz-hypergeometric}.  The latter two are the

\subsubsection{Finite Weyl operator coordinates with an explicit inverse}

Schwinger constructs the cyclic operators
$N^{-1/2}U^mV^n$ and proves their completeness and Hilbert--Schmidt orthonormality
\cite[pp.~575--576]{schwinger1960}.  Tolar restates that cyclic result and classifies finite
quantum kinematics by the elementary-divisor tensor decomposition of a finite abelian
configuration group \cite[Secs.~2--3]{tolar2014}.  The following direct coordinate theorem
retains an arbitrary finite abelian group and supplies the inverse map, fibres, and twisted
product explicitly.

Let $A$ be a finite abelian group of order $r$, let
$\widehat A=\operatorname{Hom}(A,\mathbb C^\times)$, and let
$H_A=\mathbb C[A]$ with basis $(e_x)_{x\in A}$.  Define
\begin{equation}
 T_ae_x=e_{a+x},
 \qquad
 M_\chi e_x=\chi(x)e_x,
 \qquad
 W_{a,\chi}=T_aM_\chi.
 \label{eq:finite-weyl-coordinates}
\end{equation}
Let $\mathbb C_\sigma[A\times\widehat A]$ denote the vector space with basis
$[a,\chi]$ and the retained twisted product
\begin{equation}
 [a,\chi]\star[b,\psi]=\chi(b)[a+b,\chi\psi].
 \label{eq:finite-weyl-twisted-product}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Finite Weyl coordinate isomorphism]
\label{prop:finite-weyl-coordinate-isomorphism}
The linear map
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal W:\mathbb C_\sigma[A\times\widehat A]\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(H_A),
 \qquad
 \mathcal W([a,\chi])=W_{a,\chi},
 \label{eq:finite-weyl-linear-map}
\end{equation}
is an algebra isomorphism.  Its inverse is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal W^{-1}(X)=\frac1r
 \sum_{a\in A}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat A}
 \operatorname{Tr}(W_{a,\chi}^*X)[a,\chi].
 \label{eq:finite-weyl-inverse}
\end{equation}
In particular, $\ker\mathcal W=0$, its image is the entire endomorphism algebra, and every
fibre is a singleton.  The restriction to the coordinate hyperplane with zero
$[0,1]$-coefficient is a linear isomorphism onto
$\mathfrak{sl}(H_A)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For every $x\in A$,
\[
 M_\chi T_b e_x
 =\chi(b+x)e_{b+x}
 =\chi(b)T_bM_\chi e_x.
\]
Hence
\begin{equation}
 W_{a,\chi}W_{b,\psi}
 =\chi(b)W_{a+b,\chi\psi},
 \label{eq:finite-weyl-multiplication}
\end{equation}
which proves multiplicativity of \eqref{eq:finite-weyl-linear-map}.  It also proves
associativity of the displayed twisted product without deleting its cocycle: both bracketings
of three basis coordinates acquire the scalar
$\chi(b)\chi(c)\psi(c)$.

The Hilbert--Schmidt product can be computed on the retained basis:
\begin{align}
 \operatorname{Tr}(W_{a,\chi}^*W_{b,\psi})
 &=\sum_{x\in A}
   \langle W_{a,\chi}e_x,W_{b,\psi}e_x\rangle\\
 &=\delta_{a,b}\sum_{x\in A}\overline{\chi(x)}\psi(x)
 =r\,\delta_{a,b}\delta_{\chi,\psi}.
 \label{eq:finite-weyl-orthogonality}
\end{align}
The last equality follows directly from character orthogonality: if
$\eta=\psi\chi^{-1}$ is nontrivial and $\eta(y)\ne1$, then translation by $y$ gives
\[
 \sum_{x\in A}\eta(x)
 =\sum_{x\in A}\eta(x+y)
 =\eta(y)\sum_{x\in A}\eta(x),
\]
so the sum is zero; for $\eta=1$ it is $r$.  Thus the $r^2$ operators
$W_{a,\chi}$ are nonzero and mutually orthogonal.  Since
$|\widehat A|=|A|=r$ and
$\dim_{\mathbb C}\operatorname{End}(H_A)=r^2$, they form a basis.

For an arbitrary $X\in\operatorname{End}(H_A)$, orthogonal projection onto that basis gives
\[
 X=\frac1r\sum_{a,\chi}
 \operatorname{Tr}(W_{a,\chi}^*X)W_{a,\chi},
\]
which proves both inverse equations for \eqref{eq:finite-weyl-inverse}.  Those equations give
the asserted kernel, image, and fibres.

Finally, $W_{0,1}=I$, while
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(W_{a,\chi})
 =r\,\delta_{a,0}\delta_{\chi,1}.
\]
Therefore the $[0,1]$ coefficient of $\mathcal W^{-1}(X)$ is
$r^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}(X)$.  It vanishes exactly when $X$ is traceless, proving the final
restricted isomorphism.
\end{proof}

The theorem is not a claim that the ordinary group algebra of
$A\times\widehat A$ has been identified with the endomorphism algebra: the cocycle
$\chi(b)$ in \eqref{eq:finite-weyl-twisted-product} is part of the domain.  Nor is the
arbitrary-$A$ inverse \eqref{eq:finite-weyl-inverse} attributed to Schwinger or Tolar; it is
proved above.  Tolar's displayed Weyl composition line literally prints the first output
coordinate as $\rho+\rho$ rather than an expression involving both inputs.  That source line
is not silently repaired and is not used in this proof.  No map from
\eqref{eq:finite-weyl-linear-map} to a CUE measure, shifted-Hurwitz transform, or
Kostka-labelled summand has been constructed.

Then
\begin{equation}
 u=\frac{1-4x+4q_0}{D_0},\qquad

 \label{eq:n3-forward-base-coordinates}

Define

 h(u,v)&=1-3u+8v,\\

 \Omega_3=

 \label{eq:n3-base-domain}

 x=\frac{1-9u+32v}{4h},\qquad

 \label{eq:n3-base-inverse}

If $\Delta_3>0$, the fibre consists of the conjugate pair
$x\pm\mathrm i\sqrt{\Delta_3}/(4h)$.  If $\Delta_3=0$, the fibre is the
single real point $x$.  Thus $\pi_3$ loses exactly the sign of $y$ away from the
branch locus.  Moreover $u<1$ everywhere on $\Omega_3$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since $|\beta_0|<1$, neither $3-2\beta_0$ nor $1-\overline{\beta_0}$ vanishes.
Taking absolute values in \eqref{eq:n3-peeled-lambda-c-d} and cancelling only the
equal nonzero factors $|1-\beta_0|=|1-\overline{\beta_0}|$ gives
\[
 |\lambda|^2=\frac{|1-2\beta_0|^2}{|3-2\beta_0|^2},
 \qquad
 |c|^2=\frac{|\beta_0|^2}{|3-2\beta_0|^2},
 \qquad
 |d|^2=\frac{|2-\beta_0|^2}{|3-2\beta_0|^2}.

Expanding the four squared moduli gives the first three fractions in
\eqref{eq:n3-forward-base-coordinates}.  If
$A_0=1-4x+4q_0$, then
\[
 D_0-A_0+2q_0=8-8x+2q_0=2(4-4x+q_0),

which proves the formula for $w$ without deleting any coordinate.

Substitution of $u=A_0/D_0$ and $v=q_0/D_0$ gives the exact identities
\begin{equation}
 h=\frac6{D_0},\qquad
 g=\frac{6(1-q_0)}{D_0},\qquad
 \Delta_3=16h^2y^2.
 \label{eq:n3-forward-domain-identities}
\end{equation}
Here $D_0=|3-2\beta_0|^2>0$ and $q_0<1$.  Consequently $u,v\ge0$,
$g>0$, and $\Delta_3\ge0$, so $\pi_3$ maps into $\Omega_3$.

Conversely, let $(u,v)\in\Omega_3$.  Since
\[
 h=g+6v>0,
\]
all expressions in \eqref{eq:n3-base-inverse} are defined.  Set
\[
 x=\frac{1-9u+32v}{4h},\qquad
 q_0=\frac{6v}{h},\qquad
 y^2=\frac{\Delta_3}{16h^2}.
\]
The following polynomial identity proves that the proposed radial and Cartesian
coordinates agree:
\begin{equation}
 96vh-(1-9u+32v)^2-\Delta_3=0,
 \label{eq:n3-inverse-radius-identity}
\end{equation}
because division by $16h^2$ yields $q_0-x^2-y^2=0$.  Direct substitution also
gives
\begin{equation}
 9-12x+4q_0=\frac6h,\qquad
 1-4x+4q_0=\frac{6u}{h},\qquad
 1-q_0=\frac gh.
 \label{eq:n3-inverse-reconstruction}
\end{equation}
The last identity and $g,h>0$ show that $q_0<1$.  Hence both choices
$\beta_0=x\pm\mathrm i y$ lie in $\mathbb D$, and the first two identities in
\eqref{eq:n3-inverse-reconstruction} reconstruct the prescribed $u$ and $v$
through \eqref{eq:n3-forward-base-coordinates}.  This proves surjectivity.
The same inverse formulas show that no other points occur in a fibre and give the
stated branch behaviour.

Finally,
\[
 1-u=\frac{D_0-A_0}{D_0}=\frac{8(1-x)}{D_0}>0,
\]
because $x<1$ for every $x^2+y^2<1$.  Thus $u<1$ on the entire image.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact trace equations and lifted physical cover]
\label{thm:n3-physical-trace-cover}
For the coordinates above,
\begin{align}
 Z+\overline Z
 &=R(u,v):=\frac{9u^2-16uv-10u-16v+1}{16},
 \label{eq:n3-Z-trace}\\
 Z\overline Z
 &=P(u,v):=uv\frac{1-u+2v}{2}.
 \label{eq:n3-Z-norm}
\end{align}
Consequently $Z$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 Z^2-RZ+P=0.
 \label{eq:n3-Z-quadratic}
\end{equation}
Put
\begin{equation}
 X=9u-16v+1,\qquad
 \Delta=36u-X^2,\qquad
 S=P-\frac{R^2}{4},\qquad
 J=Z-\frac R2.
 \label{eq:n3-trace-cover-coordinates}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 \Delta=\Delta_3,\qquad
 S=\frac{(u-1)^2\Delta}{1024},\qquad
 \overline J=-J,\qquad J^2=-S.
 \label{eq:n3-trace-cover-relations}
\end{equation}
Let
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde\Omega_3=
 \left\{(u,v,J)\in\mathbb R^2\times\mathrm i\mathbb R:
 (u,v)\in\Omega_3,\ J^2+S(u,v)=0\right\}.
 \label{eq:n3-physical-cover}
\end{equation}
The lifted map
\[
 \widetilde\pi_3:\mathbb D\longrightarrow\widetilde\Omega_3,\qquad
 \beta_0\longmapsto(u,v,J)
\]
is a bijection.  Its inverse is
\begin{equation}
 \beta_0=
 \frac{1-9u+32v}{4h}
 +
 \frac{8J}{(u-1)h},
 \label{eq:n3-lifted-inverse-complex}
\end{equation}
where the second summand is purely imaginary; equivalently,
\begin{equation}
 x=\frac{1-9u+32v}{4h},
 \qquad
 y=-\frac{8\mathrm iJ}{(u-1)h}.
 \label{eq:n3-lifted-inverse-real}
\end{equation}
Complex conjugation on $\mathbb D$ is transported exactly to
$(u,v,J)\mapsto(u,v,-J)$.  The lifted map has singleton fibres and zero
information loss.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Using \eqref{eq:n3-peeled-lambda-c-d}, cancel the same nonzero
$1-\beta_0$ and $1-\overline{\beta_0}$ factors as in the preceding proof and
rationalize the remaining denominator.  This gives
\begin{equation}
 Z=\frac{N}{D_0^2},\qquad
 N=\overline{\beta_0}(2-\overline{\beta_0})
 (1-2\beta_0)(3-2\beta_0).
 \label{eq:n3-Z-rationalized}
\end{equation}
With $\beta_0=x+\mathrm i y$ and $q_0=x^2+y^2$, direct expansion gives
\begin{align}
 \operatorname{Re}N
 &=-4q_0^2+16q_0x-13q_0-6x^2+6x,
 \label{eq:n3-N-real}\\
 \operatorname{Im}N&=6y(x-1).
 \label{eq:n3-N-imaginary}
\end{align}
The trace numerator is fixed by the exact polynomial identity
\begin{equation}
 9A_0^2-16A_0q_0-10A_0D_0-16q_0D_0+D_0^2
 =32\operatorname{Re}N.
 \label{eq:n3-trace-polynomial-identity}
\end{equation}
Substituting $u=A_0/D_0$ and $v=q_0/D_0$ into the definition of $R$ therefore
yields
\[
 R=\frac{32\operatorname{Re}N}{16D_0^2}
 =2\operatorname{Re}Z=Z+\overline Z,
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:n3-Z-trace}.  The norm equation does not require an
expansion:
\[
 Z\overline Z=|c|^2|d|^2|\lambda|^2=uvw
 =uv\frac{1-u+2v}{2}.
\]
Equation \eqref{eq:n3-Z-quadratic} follows from
$Z^2-(Z+\overline Z)Z+Z\overline Z=0$.

The base coordinate change has the explicit affine inverse
\begin{equation}
 X=9u-16v+1,\qquad
 v=\frac{9u+1-X}{16}.
 \label{eq:n3-u-v-u-X-isomorphism}
\end{equation}
If $Y=9u-16v$, then
\[
 \Delta_3=18u+32v-1-Y^2
 =36u-(Y+1)^2=\Delta.
\]
Substitution of the inverse in \eqref{eq:n3-u-v-u-X-isomorphism} gives, without
discarding $v$,
\begin{equation}
 R=\frac{X(u+1)-20u}{16},
 \qquad
 P=\frac{u(u+9-X)(9u+1-X)}{256}.
 \label{eq:n3-R-P-in-u-X}
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
 4P-R^2=\frac{(u-1)^2(36u-X^2)}{256},
 \label{eq:n3-S-factorization-step}
\end{equation}
which is precisely the displayed factorization of $S=P-R^2/4$.

By \eqref{eq:n3-Z-trace}, $J=(Z-\overline Z)/2$ and therefore
$\overline J=-J$.  Equations \eqref{eq:n3-Z-rationalized} and
\eqref{eq:n3-N-imaginary} give
\[
 J=\mathrm i\,\frac{6y(x-1)}{D_0^2}.
\]
Using $h=6/D_0$ and
\[
 u-1=\frac{A_0-D_0}{D_0}=\frac{8(x-1)}{D_0},
\]
this becomes the coordinate formula
\begin{equation}
 J=\frac{\mathrm i\,y(u-1)h}{8}.
 \label{eq:n3-J-y-coordinate}
\end{equation}
Together with $y^2=\Delta/(16h^2)$ from
\eqref{eq:n3-base-inverse}, it yields
\[
 J^2=-\frac{y^2(u-1)^2h^2}{64}
 =-\frac{(u-1)^2\Delta}{1024}=-S.
\]
Thus $\widetilde\pi_3$ lands in $\widetilde\Omega_3$.

For the inverse direction, take $(u,v,J)\in\widetilde\Omega_3$.  The coordinate
$J$ is purely imaginary, and \cref{thm:n3-base-quotient} gives $h>0$ and $u<1$,
so \eqref{eq:n3-lifted-inverse-real} is defined and has real $y$.  Modulo
$J^2=-S$,
\[
 y^2
 =\frac{-64J^2}{(u-1)^2h^2}
 =\frac{64S}{(u-1)^2h^2}
 =\frac{\Delta}{16h^2}.
\]
The base inverse proof now shows that $\beta_0=x+\mathrm i y$ lies in
$\mathbb D$ and reconstructs $u,v$.  Finally,
\[
 \frac{\mathrm i\,y(u-1)h}{8}
 =\frac{\mathrm i(-8\mathrm iJ)}8=J,
\]
so it also reconstructs the sheet coordinate.  This proves both inverse
compositions, hence bijectivity and singleton fibres.  Replacing $y$ by $-y$
in \eqref{eq:n3-J-y-coordinate} fixes $u,v$ and replaces $J$ by $-J$, proving
the conjugation statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact carrier, equivariant sheet map, and fixed-locus obstruction]
\label{thm:n3-deck-interface}
For $(u,v,J)\in\widetilde\Omega_3$, retain
\[
 h=1-3u+8v
\]
and define the recovered disc coordinate
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak b(u,v,J)=
 \frac{1-9u+32v}{4h}+\frac{8J}{(u-1)h}.
 \label{eq:n3-recovered-beta-interface}
\end{equation}
The following statements hold.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The carrier map
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal M_{\mathrm{car}}:\widetilde\Omega_3\longrightarrow\mathcal C_{a,b},
 \qquad
 \mathcal M_{\mathrm{car}}(u,v,J)=
 \bigl(\mathfrak b(\mathfrak b-1),2\mathfrak b-1\bigr)
 \label{eq:n3-deck-carrier-map}
\end{equation}
is a real-rational semialgebraic isomorphism onto
\[
 \mathcal C_{\mathbb D}:=F_{a,b}(\mathbb D).
\]
It has singleton fibres and zero information loss.  Explicitly, for
$(a,b)\in\mathcal C_{\mathbb D}$ set
\begin{align}
 \beta&=\frac{b+1}{2},
 &D_\beta&=(3-2\beta)(3-2\overline\beta),\nonumber\\
 u_\beta&=\frac{(1-2\beta)(1-2\overline\beta)}{D_\beta},
 &v_\beta&=\frac{\beta\overline\beta}{D_\beta},
 &h_\beta&=\frac6{D_\beta},\nonumber\\
 J_\beta&=
 \frac{(\beta-\overline\beta)(u_\beta-1)h_\beta}{16}.
 \label{eq:n3-deck-carrier-inverse}
\end{align}
Then $(a,b)\mapsto(u_\beta,v_\beta,J_\beta)$ is the complete inverse of
\eqref{eq:n3-deck-carrier-map}.

The carrier does not intertwine the two involutions everywhere.  Its exact
intertwining locus is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal M_{\mathrm{car}}\!\left(\kappa(u,v,J)\right)
 =\tau\!\left(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{car}}(u,v,J)\right)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{Re}\mathfrak b(u,v,J)=\frac12.
 \label{eq:n3-carrier-equivariance-locus}
\end{equation}

\item Define the retained imaginary coordinate and the sheet parameter by
\begin{equation}
 y_*=-\frac{8\mathrm iJ}{(u-1)h},
 \qquad
 s_{\mathrm{sh}}=\frac12+\mathrm i y_*
 =\frac12+\frac{8J}{(u-1)h}.
 \label{eq:n3-equivariant-sheet-parameter}
\end{equation}
The sheet map
\begin{align}
 \mathcal M_{\mathrm{sh}}:
 \widetilde\Omega_3&\longrightarrow\mathcal C_{a,b},
 &\mathcal M_{\mathrm{sh}}(u,v,J)
 &=F_{a,b}(s_{\mathrm{sh}})\nonumber\\
 &=\left(
 \frac{64J^2}{(u-1)^2h^2}-\frac14,
 \frac{16J}{(u-1)h}
 \right)\nonumber\\
 &=\left(-\frac14-y_*^2,2\mathrm i y_*\right)
 \label{eq:n3-equivariant-sheet-map}
\end{align}
is real-rational and exactly equivariant:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal M_{\mathrm{sh}}\circ\kappa
 =\tau\circ\mathcal M_{\mathrm{sh}}.
 \label{eq:n3-sheet-map-equivariance}
\end{equation}
Its image and every fibre are
\begin{align}
 \operatorname{im}\mathcal M_{\mathrm{sh}}
 &=\left\{\left(-\frac14-t^2,2\mathrm i t\right):-1<t<1\right\},
 \label{eq:n3-sheet-map-image}\\
 \mathcal M_{\mathrm{sh}}^{-1}
 \left(-\frac14-t^2,2\mathrm i t\right)
 &=\left\{\widetilde\pi_3(x+\mathrm i t):
 -\sqrt{1-t^2}<x<\sqrt{1-t^2}\right\}.
 \label{eq:n3-sheet-map-fibres}
\end{align}
Thus this map retains the sign and magnitude of the physical sheet coordinate
$y_*$ and loses exactly the real coordinate $x$ along the displayed horizontal
open interval.  In particular, the fibre at $t=0$ is the whole real diameter.

\item Let
\[
 p_3:\widetilde\Omega_3\to\Omega_3,
 \quad p_3(u,v,J)=(u,v),
 \qquad
 p_{a}:\mathcal C_{a,b}\to\mathbb A^1_a,
 \quad p_a(a,b)=a.
\]
The induced base map is the explicit rational function
\begin{equation}
 m_{\mathrm{sh}}:\Omega_3\longrightarrow\mathbb A^1_a,
 \qquad
 m_{\mathrm{sh}}(u,v)
 =-\frac14-\frac{\Delta_3(u,v)}{16h(u,v)^2},
 \label{eq:n3-sheet-base-map}
\end{equation}
and the square commutes exactly:
\[
\begin{CD}
 \widetilde\Omega_3 @>{\mathcal M_{\mathrm{sh}}}>> \mathcal C_{a,b}\\
 @V{p_3}VV @VV{p_a}V\\
 \Omega_3 @>{m_{\mathrm{sh}}}>> \mathbb A^1_a.
\end{CD}
\]
Its image is $(-5/4,-1/4]$.  If $0\le r<1$, its fibre over
$a=-1/4-r$ is
\begin{equation}
 m_{\mathrm{sh}}^{-1}\!\left(-\frac14-r\right)
 =\left\{\pi_3(x+\mathrm i\sqrt r):
 -\sqrt{1-r}<x<\sqrt{1-r}\right\},
 \label{eq:n3-sheet-base-fibres}
\end{equation}
where $\pi_3(x+\mathrm i\sqrt r)=\pi_3(x-\mathrm i\sqrt r)$ records the
already proved conjugation quotient rather than deleting that identification.

\item There is no injective set map
\[
 \mathcal M:\widetilde\Omega_3\longrightarrow\mathcal C_{a,b}
 \]
that satisfies $\mathcal M\circ\kappa=\tau\circ\mathcal M$.  Hence, in
particular, there is no involution-equivariant semialgebraic, continuous,
real-analytic, or algebraic isomorphism between these two specified involutive
spaces.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:n3-base-quotient}, $h>0$ and $u<1$ throughout $\Omega_3$.
Every denominator in \eqref{eq:n3-recovered-beta-interface}--
\eqref{eq:n3-sheet-base-map} is therefore nonzero.  The inverse formula
\eqref{eq:n3-lifted-inverse-complex} says exactly that
$\mathfrak b(u,v,J)=\beta_0$.  Since $\widetilde\pi_3$ is a bijection and
$F_{a,b}$ is the coordinate-renamed isomorphism proved in
\cref{prop:local-deck-coordinate-isomorphism}, their composition
$\mathcal M_{\mathrm{car}}=F_{a,b}\circ\widetilde\pi_3^{-1}$ is a
bijection onto $F_{a,b}(\mathbb D)$.  The first two formulas in
\eqref{eq:n3-deck-carrier-inverse} are the forward coordinates
\eqref{eq:n3-forward-base-coordinates}, while the last is
\eqref{eq:n3-J-y-coordinate} written with
$\beta-\overline\beta=2\mathrm i\operatorname{Im}\beta$.  They therefore
give both inverse compositions explicitly.

The involution $\kappa$ replaces $\mathfrak b$ by $\overline{\mathfrak b}$,
whereas $\tau$ replaces the parameter of $F_{a,b}$ by $1-\mathfrak b$.
Because $F_{a,b}$ is injective, the two outputs agree precisely when
\[
 \overline{\mathfrak b}=1-\mathfrak b,
\]
which is equivalent to $2\operatorname{Re}\mathfrak b=1$.  This proves
\eqref{eq:n3-carrier-equivariance-locus} without asserting global
equivariance.

Equation \eqref{eq:n3-lifted-inverse-real} gives $y_*=y$.  Therefore
$-1<y_*<1$, and direct multiplication gives
\[
 s_{\mathrm{sh}}(s_{\mathrm{sh}}-1)
 =-\frac14-y_*^2,
 \qquad
 2s_{\mathrm{sh}}-1=2\mathrm i y_*.
\]
This proves that \eqref{eq:n3-equivariant-sheet-map} lands in the curve and
gives its image.  Under $\kappa$, $y_*$ changes to $-y_*$, so
$s_{\mathrm{sh}}$ changes to $1-s_{\mathrm{sh}}$; applying $F_{a,b}$ gives
\eqref{eq:n3-sheet-map-equivariance}.  Since $F_{a,b}$ is injective, two
source points have the same image exactly when they have the same $y_*$.  The
bijection $\widetilde\pi_3:\mathbb D\to\widetilde\Omega_3$ then gives the
complete fibres \eqref{eq:n3-sheet-map-fibres}.

From \eqref{eq:n3-base-inverse},
\[
 y_*^2=\frac{\Delta_3(u,v)}{16h(u,v)^2}.
\]
Consequently the first coordinate of \eqref{eq:n3-equivariant-sheet-map} is
exactly \eqref{eq:n3-sheet-base-map}, which proves the commuting square.  The
disc inequality $x^2+y_*^2<1$ gives $0\le y_*^2<1$, the stated image interval,
and the complete base fibres \eqref{eq:n3-sheet-base-fibres}.

It remains to prove the obstruction rather than infer it from the different
presentations.  The fixed locus of the physical involution is
\[
 \operatorname{Fix}(\kappa)
 =\{\widetilde\pi_3(x):-1<x<1\},
\]
because $\kappa$ corresponds bijectively to conjugation on $\mathbb D$.  The
fixed locus of the target involution is
\[
 \operatorname{Fix}(\tau)=\{(-1/4,0)\},
\]
because $b=-b$ forces $b=0$ and the curve equation then forces $a=-1/4$.
For any equivariant set map $\mathcal M$, every point fixed by $\kappa$ must
map to a point fixed by $\tau$.  Thus $\mathcal M$ sends the entire open real
diameter to the single point $(-1/4,0)$ and cannot be injective.  This proves
the last assertion in every listed subcategory.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Exact scope of the interface]
The carrier map is a lossless composition through the fully recovered
$\beta_0$ coordinate; it is not asserted to encode CUE dynamics, Haar measure,
or the derivative-moment recursion.  The equivariant sheet map proves a genuine
compatibility of involutions, but its exact fibres prove the accompanying loss
of $x$.  The fixed-locus argument proves that no alternative equivariant map can
also be injective.  In particular, the target coordinate $b$ has been constructed
explicitly and is not identified with the source modulus coordinate $v=|c|^2$.
These maps and the obstruction establish the precise relation between the two
presentations; they imply no critical-line or zeta-zero statement.
\end{remark}
