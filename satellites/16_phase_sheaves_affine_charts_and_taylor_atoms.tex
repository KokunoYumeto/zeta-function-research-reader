\section{Phase sheaves, affine charts, and Taylor atoms}
\label{sec:phase-sheaf-audit}

The preceding section constructed the divisor, jet, tail, and packet objects.
We now develop the complementary phase layer and its exact maps to those
objects.  Two classical inputs are used at their literal scope.
Lee records that a nonvanishing holomorphic function on a simply connected
domain has a holomorphic logarithm
\cite[p.~145, Problem~5-11]{lee2024}; below we give the normalized local-disc
construction directly from the logarithm power series.  Connes and Consani
construct their circle cycles and prove the exact positive-integer family of
lengths attached to a critical-line ordinate
\cite[\S6]{connesConsani2021ZetaCycles}; that result is stated separately in
\cref{prop:phase-cycle-coordinate}.  No map from a local derivative phase to
their Hilbert-space construction is presumed.

\subsection{The general fixed locus and Boolean affine points}

We first retain two algebraic refinements which are independent of the analytic
phase construction.  For a commutative ring \(R\), write
\[
 G(R)=R\sqcup\{\tau\},
\]
with the same operations as in \cref{sec:gcue-audited-core}: \(\tau\) is the
additive identity and the multiplicative absorber, while the operations between
supported elements are the ring operations.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed locus over a general coefficient ring]
\label{prop:phase-general-fixed-locus}
Let \(u\in R^\times\).  Multiplication by \(u\) on \(G(R)\) has fixed set
\[
 \operatorname{Fix}_{G(R)}(u\cdot-)
 =
 \{\tau\}\sqcup \operatorname{Ann}_R(u-1),
\]
where
\[
 \operatorname{Ann}_R(u-1)=\{r\in R:(u-1)r=0\}.
\]
If \(R\) is an integral domain and \(u\ne1\), this reduces to
\[
 \operatorname{Fix}_{G(R)}(u\cdot-)=\{\tau,0_R\}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The absorber is fixed because \(u\tau=\tau\).  A supported element
\(r\in R\subset G(R)\) is fixed precisely when
\[
 ur=r
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 (u-1)r=0.
\]
This proves the first formula, including every possible zero-divisor
contribution.  If \(R\) is a domain and \(u-1\ne0\), its annihilator is
\(\{0_R\}\), which gives the second formula.
\end{proof}

Retain the Boolean coordinate isomorphism
\(\beta:A\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb B\) from
\cref{prop:ds-fixed-pair}.  The multiplicity cube has a precise functor-of-points
description; this is an additional map, not a replacement for the Artin-chain
comparison of \cref{thm:ds-chain-cube}.

\begin{proposition}[Boolean affine points and the two polynomial maps]
\label{prop:phase-boolean-affine-points}
For every \(m\geq1\), evaluation at the variables gives a bijection
\[
 \operatorname{ev}_m:
 \mathbb B^m
 \overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb B\text{-alg}}
 \bigl(\mathbb B[T_1,\ldots,T_m],\mathbb B\bigr),
\]
\[
 (b_1,\ldots,b_m)\longmapsto
 \bigl(P\longmapsto P(b_1,\ldots,b_m)\bigr).
\]
Under this bijection, the Boolean diagonal
\[
 \delta_m:\mathbb B\longrightarrow\mathbb B^m,
 \qquad b\longmapsto(b,\ldots,b),
\]
is induced contravariantly by
\[
 \delta_m^*:
 \mathbb B[T_1,\ldots,T_m]\longrightarrow\mathbb B[T],
 \qquad T_j\longmapsto T,
\]
and the nonempty-support map
\[
 \sigma_m:\mathbb B^m\longrightarrow\mathbb B,
 \qquad
 (b_1,\ldots,b_m)\longmapsto b_1+\cdots+b_m,
\]
is induced contravariantly by
\[
 \sigma_m^*:
 \mathbb B[T]\longrightarrow\mathbb B[T_1,\ldots,T_m],
 \qquad
 T\longmapsto T_1+\cdots+T_m.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \sigma_m\delta_m=\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb B}.
\]
Transport through \(\beta\) recovers exactly the maps
\(\Delta_m:A\to A^m\) and \(\Sigma_m:A^m\to A\) of
\cref{prop:ds-boolean-retract}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A \(\mathbb B\)-algebra homomorphism out of the polynomial semiring is
determined by the \(m\) values assigned to \(T_1,\ldots,T_m\).  Conversely, any
\((b_1,\ldots,b_m)\in\mathbb B^m\) defines evaluation on a finite Boolean
polynomial:
\[
  \sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb N^m}c_\alpha T^\alpha
  \longmapsto
  \sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb N^m}c_\alpha
  \prod_{j=1}^m b_j^{\alpha_j},
\]
where only finitely many \(c_\alpha\) are nonzero.
Evaluation preserves \(0,1\), addition, multiplication, and the coefficient
copy of \(\mathbb B\).  These two constructions are inverse, proving the
bijection.

If \(b\in\mathbb B\) is a point of the affine line, composition of its evaluation
map with \(\delta_m^*\) sends every \(T_j\) to \(b\); this is \(\delta_m(b)\).
If \((b_1,\ldots,b_m)\) is an \(m\)-dimensional point, composition of its
evaluation map with \(\sigma_m^*\) sends \(T\) to
\(b_1+\cdots+b_m\); this is \(\sigma_m\).  Finally, Boolean addition is
idempotent, so
\[
 \sigma_m\delta_m(b)=\underbrace{b+\cdots+b}_{m\ \mathrm{terms}}=b.
\]
The last statement follows by applying \(\beta\) in every coordinate.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A phase-decorated sheaf over the simple-zero locus}

Let \(\Omega\subset\C\) be open and let \(f\) be a holomorphic function on
\(\Omega\) which is not identically zero on any connected component.  Its
simple-zero set
\[
Z_f^{\mathrm{simp}}
:=
\{x\in\Omega:f(x)=0,\ f'(x)\ne0\}
\]
is closed and discrete in \(\Omega\).  The derivative below is explicitly
the derivative in the displayed global coordinate on \(\Omega\subset\C\).
Write
\[
 D_f^{\mathrm{simp}}:=\sum_{x\in Z_f^{\mathrm{simp}}}[x].
\]

Define the pointed set
\[
 M:=\{\star\}\sqcup S^1
\]
with basepoint \(\star\), and define
\[
 \pi:M\longrightarrow A,
 \qquad
 \pi(\star)=\tau,\qquad \pi(u)=e\quad(u\in S^1).
\]
There is a pointed section
\[
 \iota:A\longrightarrow M,
 \qquad
 \iota(\tau)=\star,\qquad \iota(e)=1,
\]
so \(\pi\iota=\operatorname{id}_A\).

\begin{theorem}[Phase-decorated simple-zero sheaf]
\label{thm:phase-simple-sheaf}
For an open \(U\subset\Omega\), set
\[
 \mathcal M_f^{\mathrm{ph}}(U):=
 \prod_{x\in U\cap Z_f^{\mathrm{simp}}}M.
\]
Then \(\mathcal M_f^{\mathrm{ph}}\) is a sheaf of pointed sets.  Coordinatewise
application of
\(\pi\) and \(\iota\) gives a split morphism
\[
 \mathcal M_f^{\mathrm{ph}}
 \underset{\iota_f}{\overset{\Pi_f}{\rightleftarrows}}
 \mathcal A_{D_f^{\mathrm{simp}}},
 \qquad
 \Pi_f\iota_f=\operatorname{id}.
\]
At a simple zero \(x\), the two fibres of the stalk map are
\[
 \pi^{-1}(\tau)=\{\star\},
 \qquad
 \pi^{-1}(e)=S^1.
\]
The family
\[
 \varphi_f(U)
 :=
 \left(\frac{f'(x)}{|f'(x)|}\right)_
 {x\in U\cap Z_f^{\mathrm{simp}}}
\]
is a section compatible with restriction, and
\(\Pi_f(\varphi_f)\) is the everywhere-\(e\) section of the reduced Boolean
shadow on the simple locus.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The sheaf proof is coordinatewise.  For a cover \(U=\bigcup_iU_i\), compatible
families on the \(U_i\) assign a unique value in \(M\) to every
\(x\in U\cap Z_f^{\mathrm{simp}}\), and those values form the unique glued
family.  Coordinatewise application of a pointed map commutes with coordinate
projections, hence defines a sheaf morphism.  The equation
\(\Pi_f\iota_f=\operatorname{id}\) follows coordinatewise from
\(\pi\iota=\operatorname{id}_A\).  The displayed fibres follow directly from
the definition of \(\pi\): discreteness gives a neighbourhood \(W\) of a simple
zero \(x\) with \(W\cap Z_f^{\mathrm{simp}}=\{x\}\), so the two stalks identify
with \(M\) and \(A\), respectively, and the induced stalk map is literally
\(\pi\).

At a simple zero \(x\), \(f'(x)\ne0\), so
\(f'(x)/|f'(x)|\in S^1\).  The resulting families restrict by forgetting
coordinates and therefore define a compatible section.  Every one of its
coordinates is sent by \(\pi\) to \(e\), which proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

Thus \(\Pi_f\) forgets the entire circle-valued derivative phase while retaining
whether the stalk is occupied.  The section \(\iota_f\) selects the phase \(1\);
it is not an inverse on \(\mathcal M_f^{\mathrm{ph}}\).  In particular, the fibre
calculation
states the exact information loss rather than identifying the two sheaves.

\begin{corollary}[The negative-even derivative phase]
\label{cor:phase-trivial-section}
On the negative-even zero sector of \(\zeta\),
\[
 \varphi_\zeta(-2n)=(-1)^n
 \qquad(n\geq1).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{thm:phase-simple-sheaf} on
\(\Omega=\C\setminus\{1\}\), where \(\zeta\) is holomorphic.
By \cref{prop:ds-trivial-derivative},
\[
 \zeta'(-2n)
 =
 (-1)^n
 \frac{(2n)!}{2(2\pi)^{2n}}\zeta(2n+1).
\]
The factor after \((-1)^n\) is positive, so division by the absolute value
leaves exactly \((-1)^n\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Normalized real affine factors and their complete fibres}

Write
\[
 S^1_+:=\{z\in S^1:\Re z>0\}.
\]
For \(a\in\R\), define the normalized real affine factor
\[
 \mathsf u(a):=\frac{1+\mathrm ia}{\sqrt{1+a^2}}.
\]
This notation is reserved for the phase-factor map; unit directions will be
denoted by \(\nu\).

\begin{proposition}[One-factor coordinate and its differential]
\label{prop:phase-one-factor}
The map
\[
 \mathsf u:\R\longrightarrow S^1_+
\]
is a real-analytic bijection with inverse
\[
 \mathsf u^{-1}(z)=\frac{\Im z}{\Re z}.
\]
It satisfies
\[
 \mathsf u(a)=\exp\bigl(\mathrm i\arctan a\bigr),
 \qquad
 \mathsf u'(a)=\frac{\mathrm i}{1+a^2}\mathsf u(a).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Its modulus is one and its real part is
\((1+a^2)^{-1/2}>0\), so the image lies in \(S^1_+\).  If
\(z=x+\mathrm iy\in S^1_+\), put \(a=y/x\).  Since \(x>0\) and
\(x^2+y^2=1\),
\[
 \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+a^2}}
 =
 \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+y^2/x^2}}
 =x,
\]
and consequently \(\mathsf u(a)=x+\mathrm iy=z\).  This proves surjectivity
and the inverse formula, hence also injectivity.

Because \(\arctan a\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)\),
\[
 \cos(\arctan a)=\frac1{\sqrt{1+a^2}},
 \qquad
 \sin(\arctan a)=\frac a{\sqrt{1+a^2}},
\]
which proves the exponential formula.  Differentiating it gives
\[
 \mathsf u'(a)
 =
 \frac{\mathrm i}{1+a^2}
 \exp(\mathrm i\arctan a)
 =
 \frac{\mathrm i}{1+a^2}\mathsf u(a).
\]
\end{proof}

For \(N\geq1\), define
\[
 P_N:\R^N\longrightarrow S^1,
 \qquad
 P_N(a_1,\ldots,a_N):=\prod_{k=1}^N\mathsf u(a_k).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Finite phase products, fibres, and a three-factor section]
\label{thm:phase-finite-products}
For every \((a_1,\ldots,a_N)\in\R^N\),
\[
 P_N(a_1,\ldots,a_N)
 =
 \exp\left(\mathrm i\sum_{k=1}^N\arctan a_k\right).
\]
If \(z=e^{\mathrm i\theta}\) with \(\theta\in(-\pi,\pi]\), then
\[
 P_N^{-1}(z)
 =
 \bigcup_{\substack{\ell\in\mathbb Z\\
 \theta+2\pi\ell\in(-N\pi/2,N\pi/2)}}
 \left\{
 a\in\R^N:
 \sum_{k=1}^N\arctan a_k=\theta+2\pi\ell
 \right\}.
\]
In particular,
\[
 \operatorname{im}P_1=S^1_+,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{im}P_2=S^1\setminus\{-1\},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{im}P_N=S^1\quad(N\geq3).
\]
An explicit set-theoretic section of \(P_3\) is
\[
 s_3(e^{\mathrm i\theta})
 =
 \left(
 \tan\frac\theta3,\,
 \tan\frac\theta3,\,
 \tan\frac\theta3
 \right),
 \qquad \theta\in(-\pi,\pi].
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Multiplying the exponential identity in \cref{prop:phase-one-factor} proves
the first formula.  Equality with \(e^{\mathrm i\theta}\) holds exactly when
\[
 \sum_{k=1}^N\arctan a_k=\theta+2\pi\ell
\]
for some integer \(\ell\).  Every arctangent lies in
\((-\pi/2,\pi/2)\), so only the integers satisfying the displayed interval
condition can occur.  Conversely, every point on any displayed level set maps
to \(z\).  This proves the complete fibre formula.

For \(N=1\), the angle range is \((-\pi/2,\pi/2)\), which is \(S^1_+\).
For \(N=2\), the sum ranges over \((-\pi,\pi)\), whose exponential image omits
only \(-1\).  If \(N=3\), then for every principal argument
\(\theta\in(-\pi,\pi]\), one has \(\theta/3\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)\), and therefore
\[
 P_3\left(\tan\frac\theta3,\tan\frac\theta3,\tan\frac\theta3\right)
 =
 e^{\mathrm i\theta}.
\]
For \(N>3\), append zero coordinates, since \(\mathsf u(0)=1\).
The displayed principal-argument section is not claimed to be continuous at
\(-1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Orthogonal-walk expansion with signed steps]
\label{prop:phase-orthogonal-walk}
Let \(e_q=e_q(a_1,\ldots,a_N)\) be the elementary symmetric polynomials and
put
\[
 W_r:=\sum_{q=0}^r\mathrm i^q e_q
 \qquad(0\leq r\leq N).
\]
Then
\[
 \prod_{k=1}^N(1+\mathrm ia_k)
 =
 \sum_{q=0}^N\mathrm i^q e_q
 =
 A_N+\mathrm iB_N,
\]
where
\[
 A_N=\sum_{0\leq2j\leq N}(-1)^j e_{2j},
 \qquad
 B_N=\sum_{0\leq2j+1\leq N}(-1)^j e_{2j+1}.
\]
For \(1\leq q\leq N\), the \(q\)-th increment is
\[
 W_q-W_{q-1}=\mathrm i^q e_q;
\]
it is horizontal for even \(q\), vertical for odd \(q\), has signed scalar
\(e_q\), and has Euclidean length \(|e_q|\).  Moreover,
\[
 A_N^2+B_N^2=\prod_{k=1}^N(1+a_k^2),
\]
and the normalized endpoint is
\[
 \frac{A_N+\mathrm iB_N}{\sqrt{A_N^2+B_N^2}}
 =
 P_N(a_1,\ldots,a_N).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In expanding the product, selecting \(\mathrm ia_k\) from exactly the indices
in a subset \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}\) contributes
\(\mathrm i^{|I|}\prod_{k\in I}a_k\).  Grouping by \(|I|=q\) gives
\[
 \prod_{k=1}^N(1+\mathrm ia_k)
 =
 \sum_{q=0}^N\mathrm i^q
 \sum_{|I|=q}\prod_{k\in I}a_k
 =
 \sum_{q=0}^N\mathrm i^qe_q.
\]
Separating even and odd \(q\) gives \(A_N+\mathrm iB_N\).  The increment
formula is immediate, including the sign and absolute Euclidean length.
Taking squared absolute values in the product identity gives
\[
 A_N^2+B_N^2
 =
 \prod_{k=1}^N|1+\mathrm ia_k|^2
 =
 \prod_{k=1}^N(1+a_k^2).
\]
Division by the positive square root factorizes coordinatewise and gives
\(\prod_k\mathsf u(a_k)=P_N(a)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Equal-step limit]
\label{cor:phase-equal-step-limit}
For every \(\theta\in\R\),
\[
 \lim_{N\to\infty}\mathsf u(\theta/N)^N=e^{\mathrm i\theta}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:phase-finite-products},
\[
 \mathsf u(\theta/N)^N
 =
 \exp\bigl(\mathrm iN\arctan(\theta/N)\bigr).
\]
Since \(\arctan x/x\to1\) as \(x\to0\),
\(N\arctan(\theta/N)\to\theta\), and continuity of the exponential proves the
claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Branch-correct affine cyclotomic charts}

The next theorem uses the continuous argument on the entire upper-half-plane
path, so its formula remains valid even when the real part changes sign.

\begin{theorem}[Affine phase chart in a fixed upper-half-plane direction]
\label{thm:phase-affine-chart}
Fix \(0<\phi<\pi\), put \(\omega=e^{\mathrm i\phi}\), and define
\[
 L_\phi(t):=1+t\omega
 \qquad(t>0).
\]
There is a unique continuous argument
\[
 \Theta_\phi:(0,\infty)\longrightarrow(0,\phi),
 \qquad
 \frac{L_\phi(t)}{|L_\phi(t)|}=e^{\mathrm i\Theta_\phi(t)}.
\]
It is real analytic and satisfies
\[
 \Theta_\phi'(t)
 =
 \frac{\sin\phi}{1+2t\cos\phi+t^2}>0.
\]
It is a bijection, with exact inverse and radius
\[
 t_\phi(\theta)
 =
 \frac{\sin\theta}{\sin(\phi-\theta)},
 \qquad
 |L_\phi(t_\phi(\theta))|
 =
 \frac{\sin\phi}{\sin(\phi-\theta)}
 \quad(0<\theta<\phi).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
 1+t_\phi(\theta)e^{\mathrm i\phi}
 =
 \frac{\sin\phi}{\sin(\phi-\theta)}e^{\mathrm i\theta}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The imaginary part of \(L_\phi(t)\) is \(t\sin\phi>0\).  Thus the path lies
entirely in the upper half-plane and has a unique continuous argument in
\((0,\pi)\).  Since \(L_\phi(t)\) is a positive linear combination of the rays
with arguments \(0\) and \(\phi\), and the open sector between those rays is
convex for \(0<\phi<\pi\), this argument lies in \((0,\phi)\).

For a nonzero differentiable complex path \(z(t)\), differentiation of a local
logarithm gives
\[
 \frac{d}{dt}\arg z(t)=\Im\frac{z'(t)}{z(t)}.
\]
With \(z=L_\phi\),
\[
 \Theta_\phi'(t)
 =
 \Im\frac{e^{\mathrm i\phi}}{1+te^{\mathrm i\phi}}
 =
 \Im\frac{e^{\mathrm i\phi}+t}
 {|1+te^{\mathrm i\phi}|^2}
 =
 \frac{\sin\phi}{1+2t\cos\phi+t^2}>0.
\]
Hence \(\Theta_\phi\) is real analytic and strictly increasing.  Directly,
\[
 \lim_{t\to0^+}\Theta_\phi(t)=0,
 \qquad
 \lim_{t\to\infty}\Theta_\phi(t)=\phi,
\]
so it is a bijection onto \((0,\phi)\).

For \(0<\theta<\phi\), write
\[
 1+te^{\mathrm i\phi}=re^{\mathrm i\theta}
\]
with \(r,t>0\).  Equating imaginary and real parts gives
\[
 t\sin\phi=r\sin\theta,
 \qquad
 1+t\cos\phi=r\cos\theta.
\]
Eliminating \(r\) yields
\[
 t\sin(\phi-\theta)=\sin\theta,
\]
and therefore \(t=\sin\theta/\sin(\phi-\theta)\).  Substitution in the first
equation gives \(r=\sin\phi/\sin(\phi-\theta)\).  These positive values satisfy
the original complex equation, proving both inverse formulas.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Cyclotomic-direction charts]
\label{cor:phase-cyclotomic-direction}
Let \(p\) be an odd prime, let
\(u\in\{1,\ldots,(p-1)/2\}\), and put
\[
 \omega_{p,u}=e^{2\pi\mathrm iu/p},
\]
Then \(t\mapsto(1+t\omega_{p,u})/|1+t\omega_{p,u}|\) is a bijection from
\((0,\infty)\) to the open unit-circle arc with arguments
\((0,2\pi u/p)\), with the inverse in
\cref{thm:phase-affine-chart}.  In particular, for \(p=5,u=1\),
\[
 t(\theta)=\frac{\sin\theta}{\sin(2\pi/5-\theta)}
 \qquad(0<\theta<2\pi/5).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{thm:phase-affine-chart} with \(\phi=2\pi u/p\), which lies in
\((0,\pi)\) by the bound on \(u\).
\end{proof}

The term \emph{cyclotomic} here describes the fixed direction
\(\omega_{p,u}\).  For an arbitrary real \(t\), the affine factor
\(1+t\omega_{p,u}\) need not belong to the number field
\(\mathbb Q(\omega_{p,u})\): subtracting \(1\) and dividing by the nonzero
\(\omega_{p,u}\) shows that membership would imply
\(t\in\mathbb Q(\omega_{p,u})\), whereas every transcendental real \(t\) lies
outside this algebraic number field.  No arithmetic condition on a zeta
derivative is implied by placing its phase in one of these real-analytic charts.
\subsection{Uniform Taylor atomization of a real-analytic phase}

\begin{theorem}[Exact Taylor atomization]
\label{thm:phase-taylor-atomization}
Let
\[
 \Theta(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_nt^n
\]
be real analytic for \(|t|<R\), with \(\beta_n\in\R\) and \(\Theta(0)=0\).
There is \(r\in(0,R)\) such that
\[
 \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\beta_n|r^n<\frac{\pi}{4}.
\]
For \(|t|\leq r\), define
\[
 \alpha_n(t):=\tan(\beta_nt^n).
\]
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(\sum_{n\geq1}|\alpha_n(t)|\) converges uniformly on \([-r,r]\);
\item for every \(N\geq1\),
\[
 \prod_{n=1}^N\mathsf u(\alpha_n(t))
 =
 \exp\left(\mathrm i\sum_{n=1}^N\beta_nt^n\right);
\]
\item the infinite product, defined as the uniform limit of its partial products,
satisfies
\[
 \prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathsf u(\alpha_n(t))
 =
 e^{\mathrm i\Theta(t)}
\]
uniformly on \([-r,r]\);
\item the unnormalized \(N\)-th product is the exact orthogonal-walk endpoint
\[
 \prod_{n=1}^N(1+\mathrm i\alpha_n(t))
 =
 \sum_{q=0}^N\mathrm i^q
 e_q(\alpha_1(t),\ldots,\alpha_N(t));
\]
\item for \(|t|<r\),
\[
 \frac{d}{dt}e^{\mathrm i\Theta(t)}
 =
 \mathrm i\Theta'(t)e^{\mathrm i\Theta(t)},
\qquad
 \frac{\alpha_n'(t)}{1+\alpha_n(t)^2}
 =
 n\beta_nt^{n-1}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Absolute convergence of the power series at a sufficiently small positive
radius permits a choice of \(r\) with the displayed strict bound.  For
\(|t|\leq r\), every term satisfies
\[
 |\beta_nt^n|
 \leq|\beta_n|r^n
 <\frac{\pi}{4}.
\]
On \([-\pi/4,\pi/4]\), the mean-value theorem and
\(\sec^2x\leq2\) give
\[
 |\tan x|\leq2|x|.
\]
Hence
\[
 |\alpha_n(t)|
 \leq2|\beta_n|r^n,
\]
and the Weierstrass test proves uniform absolute convergence in (1).

Because \(\beta_nt^n\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)\),
\[
 \arctan\alpha_n(t)
 =
 \arctan(\tan(\beta_nt^n))
 =
 \beta_nt^n.
\]
Applying \cref{thm:phase-finite-products} gives (2).  The Taylor partial sums
\(\Theta_N(t)=\sum_{n\leq N}\beta_nt^n\) converge uniformly to \(\Theta\) on
\([-r,r]\).  Since \(z\mapsto e^{\mathrm iz}\) is uniformly continuous on
the compact real interval containing their ranges, (2) converges uniformly to
\(e^{\mathrm i\Theta(t)}\), proving (3).  Part (4) is
\cref{prop:phase-orthogonal-walk} applied to the finite slope vector.

The first identity in (5) is differentiation of the analytic composite.  For
the second,
\[
 \alpha_n'(t)
 =
 \sec^2(\beta_nt^n)n\beta_nt^{n-1}
 =
 (1+\alpha_n(t)^2)n\beta_nt^{n-1}.
\]
\end{proof}

The atomization is unique after the Taylor coefficients and the small tangent
branch have been fixed.  It is not a uniqueness statement among all possible
finite or infinite factorizations of the same unit phase; the complete finite
fibres in \cref{thm:phase-finite-products} show why such a stronger assertion
would fail.

\subsection{Constructive logarithms and directional phase germs}

For \(w\in\C^\times\), write
\[
 \operatorname{ph}(w):=\frac{w}{|w|}\in S^1.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Normalized logarithm and all Taylor coordinates]
\label{thm:phase-local-logarithm}
Let \(g\) be holomorphic near \(0\) and suppose \(g(0)\ne0\).  After shrinking
to a disc \(|z|<\varepsilon\), there is a unique holomorphic function
\[
 h(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_nz^n,
 \qquad h(0)=0,
\]
such that
\[
 g(z)=g(0)e^{h(z)}.
\]
If
\[
 \frac{g(z)}{g(0)}
 =
 1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_nz^n,
\]
then every logarithmic coefficient is explicitly
\[
 c_n
 =
 \sum_{k=1}^n\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k}
 \sum_{\substack{n_1+\cdots+n_k=n\\n_j\geq1}}
 a_{n_1}\cdots a_{n_k}.
\]
For a unit direction \(\nu\in S^1\), put
\[
 \Theta_{g,\nu}(t)
 :=
 \Im h(t\nu)
 =
 \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{g,\nu,n}t^n,
\qquad
 \beta_{g,\nu,n}:=\Im(c_n\nu^n).
\]
For real \(|t|<\varepsilon\),
\[
 |g(t\nu)|
 =
 |g(0)|
 \exp\bigl(\Re h(t\nu)\bigr),
\]
\[
 \operatorname{ph}(g(t\nu))
 =
 \operatorname{ph}(g(0))e^{\mathrm i\Theta_{g,\nu}(t)}.
\]
After decreasing \(\varepsilon\), the final factor has the uniform atomization
of \cref{thm:phase-taylor-atomization}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Continuity and \(g(0)\ne0\) allow a disc on which
\[
 \left|\frac{g(z)}{g(0)}-1\right|<\frac12.
\]
Put \(q(z)=g(z)/g(0)-1\).  The series
\[
 h(z):=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k}q(z)^k
\]
converges normally on every smaller closed disc, hence is holomorphic.  It is
the usual logarithm series, so \(e^{h(z)}=1+q(z)=g(z)/g(0)\), and \(h(0)=0\).
If \(h_1,h_2\) are two such logarithms normalized by zero at the origin, then
\(e^{h_1-h_2}=1\).  The continuous function \(h_1-h_2\) takes values in the
discrete set \(2\pi\mathrm i\mathbb Z\); on the connected disc it is constant,
and its value at zero is zero.  Thus \(h_1=h_2\).

Expanding the \(k\)-th power,
\[
 q(z)^k
 =
 \sum_{n\geq k}
 \left(
 \sum_{\substack{n_1+\cdots+n_k=n\\n_j\geq1}}
 a_{n_1}\cdots a_{n_k}
 \right)z^n.
\]
For fixed \(n\), only \(1\leq k\leq n\) contributes.  Taking the coefficient of
\(z^n\) in the logarithm series proves the displayed formula for \(c_n\).

Substitute \(z=t\nu\):
\[
 g(t\nu)
 =
 g(0)\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq1}c_n\nu^nt^n\right).
\]
Taking the absolute value keeps the real part of the exponent; dividing by the
absolute value keeps the imaginary part.  This proves the modulus and phase
formulas.  The real power series for \(\Theta_{g,\nu}\) has zero constant term,
so \cref{thm:phase-taylor-atomization} applies on a smaller interval.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[First two directional atoms]
\label{cor:phase-first-two-atoms}
In the notation of \cref{thm:phase-local-logarithm},
\[
 c_1=a_1,
 \qquad
 c_2=a_2-\frac12a_1^2,
\]
and therefore
\[
 \beta_{g,\nu,1}=\Im(\nu a_1),
\qquad
 \beta_{g,\nu,2}
 =
 \Im\left(\nu^2\left(a_2-\frac12a_1^2\right)\right).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The coefficient formula in \cref{thm:phase-local-logarithm} gives
\[
 c_1=a_1,
\qquad
 c_2=a_2-\frac12a_1a_1.
\]
Taking \(\Im(c_n\nu^n)\) gives the two directional formulas.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Local completed-function phase packets}

Let \(\xi\) be the completed function used in
\cref{thm:ds-packet-descent,prop:ds-phase-transport}.  Its entirety and exact
zero set are sourced in \cref{sec:divisor-sheaf-audit}; the calculations below
use only local holomorphy, exact order of vanishing, and the two functional
symmetries.

Fix a zero \(\rho\) of multiplicity \(m=m_\rho\), and set
\[
 \mu_\rho:=\frac{\xi^{(m)}(\rho)}{m!}\in\C^\times,
\qquad
 \psi_\rho(z):=
 \frac{\xi(\rho+z)}{\mu_\rho z^m}
\quad(z\ne0),
\qquad
 \psi_\rho(0):=1.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Local phase packet with explicit derivative coordinates]
\label{thm:phase-local-packet}
There is a disc on which a unique holomorphic
\[
 h_\rho(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{\rho,n}z^n,
\qquad h_\rho(0)=0,
\]
satisfies
\[
 \xi(\rho+z)=\mu_\rho z^m e^{h_\rho(z)}.
\]
Writing
\[
 \psi_\rho(z)=1+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{\rho,j}z^j,
\]
one has the exact derivative coordinates
\[
 a_{\rho,j}
 =
 \frac{m!\,\xi^{(m+j)}(\rho)}
 {(m+j)!\,\xi^{(m)}(\rho)}
\qquad(j\geq1),
\]
and
\[
 c_{\rho,n}
 =
 \sum_{k=1}^n\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k}
 \sum_{\substack{n_1+\cdots+n_k=n\\n_j\geq1}}
 a_{\rho,n_1}\cdots a_{\rho,n_k}.
\]
For a unit direction \(\nu\in S^1\), define
\[
 \Theta_{\rho,\nu}(t)
 =
 \Im h_\rho(t\nu)
 =
 \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{\rho,\nu,n}t^n,
\qquad
 \beta_{\rho,\nu,n}:=\Im(c_{\rho,n}\nu^n).
\]
For all sufficiently small \(t>0\),
\[
 |\xi(\rho+t\nu)|
 =
 t^m|\mu_\rho|
 \exp\bigl(\Re h_\rho(t\nu)\bigr),
\]
\[
 \operatorname{ph}(\xi(\rho+t\nu))
 =
 \nu^m\operatorname{ph}(\mu_\rho)
 e^{\mathrm i\Theta_{\rho,\nu}(t)}.
\]
After decreasing the radius, put
\[
 \alpha_{\rho,\nu,n}(t)
 :=
 \tan(\beta_{\rho,\nu,n}t^n).
\]
Then
\[
 e^{\mathrm i\Theta_{\rho,\nu}(t)}
 =
 \prod_{n=1}^{\infty}
 \mathsf u(\alpha_{\rho,\nu,n}(t))
\]
uniformly on the resulting closed interval, and every finite truncation has the
orthogonal-walk endpoint of
\cref{prop:phase-orthogonal-walk}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Exact order \(m\) means that the Taylor series begins
\[
 \xi(\rho+z)
 =
 \sum_{j=0}^{\infty}
 \frac{\xi^{(m+j)}(\rho)}{(m+j)!}z^{m+j},
\qquad
 \xi^{(m)}(\rho)\ne0.
\]
 Dividing by \(\mu_\rho z^m\) and using
 \(\mu_\rho=\xi^{(m)}(\rho)/m!\) gives a holomorphic extension
\[
 \psi_\rho(z)
 =
 1+
 \sum_{j=1}^{\infty}
 \frac{m!\,\xi^{(m+j)}(\rho)}
 {(m+j)!\,\xi^{(m)}(\rho)}z^j.
\]
This proves the formula for \(a_{\rho,j}\).  Apply
\cref{thm:phase-local-logarithm} to \(g=\psi_\rho\), whose value at zero is one.
It produces the unique normalized \(h_\rho\), the composition formula for
\(c_{\rho,n}\), and
\[
 \psi_\rho(z)=e^{h_\rho(z)}.
\]
 Multiplication by \(\mu_\rho z^m\) gives the factorization of \(\xi\).

At \(z=t\nu\), with \(t>0\) and \(|\nu|=1\),
\[
 \xi(\rho+t\nu)
 =
 \mu_\rho t^m\nu^m e^{h_\rho(t\nu)}.
\]
Its absolute value and phase are exactly the two displayed formulas.  The final
product and finite-walk assertions are
\cref{thm:phase-taylor-atomization,prop:phase-orthogonal-walk} applied to the
real-analytic phase \(\Theta_{\rho,\nu}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[First two local packet atoms]
\label{cor:phase-packet-first-two}
For every unit direction \(\nu\),
\[
 \beta_{\rho,\nu,1}
 =
 \Im\left(
 \nu\frac{\xi^{(m+1)}(\rho)}
 {(m+1)\xi^{(m)}(\rho)}
 \right),
\]
and
\[
 \beta_{\rho,\nu,2}
 =
 \Im\left[
 \nu^2\left(
 \frac{\xi^{(m+2)}(\rho)}
 {(m+2)(m+1)\xi^{(m)}(\rho)}
 -
 \frac12
 \left(
 \frac{\xi^{(m+1)}(\rho)}
 {(m+1)\xi^{(m)}(\rho)}
 \right)^2
 \right)
 \right].
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The derivative-coordinate formula gives
\[
 a_{\rho,1}
 =
 \frac{\xi^{(m+1)}(\rho)}
 {(m+1)\xi^{(m)}(\rho)},
\qquad
 a_{\rho,2}
 =
 \frac{\xi^{(m+2)}(\rho)}
 {(m+2)(m+1)\xi^{(m)}(\rho)}.
\]
Substitute these into \cref{cor:phase-first-two-atoms}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact packet transport of every local atom]
\label{prop:phase-packet-atom-transport}
After choosing a common sufficiently small radius,
\[
 \mu_{1-\rho}=(-1)^m\mu_\rho,
\qquad
 \mu_{\overline\rho}=\overline{\mu_\rho},
\]
\[
 \psi_{1-\rho}(z)=\psi_\rho(-z),
\qquad
 \psi_{\overline\rho}(z)=\overline{\psi_\rho(\overline z)},
\]
\[
 h_{1-\rho}(z)=h_\rho(-z),
\qquad
 h_{\overline\rho}(z)=\overline{h_\rho(\overline z)}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 c_{1-\rho,n}=(-1)^nc_{\rho,n},
\qquad
 c_{\overline\rho,n}=\overline{c_{\rho,n}},
\]
and for every unit direction \(\nu\),
\[
 \beta_{1-\rho,-\nu,n}=\beta_{\rho,\nu,n},
\qquad
 \beta_{\overline\rho,\overline\nu,n}=-\beta_{\rho,\nu,n},
\]
\[
 \alpha_{1-\rho,-\nu,n}(t)=\alpha_{\rho,\nu,n}(t),
\qquad
 \alpha_{\overline\rho,\overline\nu,n}(t)
 =
 -\alpha_{\rho,\nu,n}(t).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The leading-coefficient formulas are
\cref{prop:ds-phase-transport}.  From \(\xi(1-s)=\xi(s)\),
\[
 \xi(1-\rho+z)
 =
 \xi(\rho-z)
 =
 \mu_\rho(-z)^m\psi_\rho(-z)
 =
 \mu_{1-\rho}z^m\psi_\rho(-z),
\]
so division by the nonzero leading factor gives
\(\psi_{1-\rho}(z)=\psi_\rho(-z)\).  Likewise,
\[
 \xi(\overline\rho+z)
 =
 \overline{\xi(\rho+\overline z)}
 =
 \overline{\mu_\rho\overline z^{\,m}
 \,\psi_\rho(\overline z)}
 =
 \mu_{\overline\rho}z^m
 \overline{\psi_\rho(\overline z)},
\]
which gives the conjugation formula for \(\psi\).

Both sides of each proposed \(h\)-identity exponentiate to the same
\(\psi\)-germ and vanish at zero.  Uniqueness in
\cref{thm:phase-local-logarithm} proves the two \(h\)-identities.  Comparing
Taylor coefficients gives
\[
 c_{1-\rho,n}=(-1)^nc_{\rho,n},
\qquad
 c_{\overline\rho,n}=\overline{c_{\rho,n}}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \Im\bigl(c_{1-\rho,n}(-\nu)^n\bigr)
 =
 \Im(c_{\rho,n}\nu^n),
\]
while
\[
 \Im\bigl(c_{\overline\rho,n}\overline\nu^{\,n}\bigr)
 =
 \Im\overline{c_{\rho,n}\nu^n}
 =
 -\Im(c_{\rho,n}\nu^n).
\]
These are the two \(\beta\)-formulas.  The \(\alpha\)-formulas follow because
\(\tan\) is odd.
\end{proof}

For a packet \(O\), a representative \(\rho\in O\), and \(m=m_O\), the leading
phase \(\operatorname{ph}(\mu_\rho)\) descends to the quotient
\(S^1/\Gamma_{m_O}\) constructed after
\cref{prop:ds-phase-transport}.  The local Taylor factor is instead a germ of
a curve
\[
 t\longmapsto e^{\mathrm i\Theta_{\rho,\nu}(t)}
\]
based at \(1\).  Let
\[
 \mathfrak G_1
 :=
 \{\gamma:(\mathbb R,0)\to(S^1,1):
 \gamma\text{ is a real-analytic germ}\},
\]
and, with \(b_{\rho,\nu}:=\nu^m\operatorname{ph}(\mu_\rho)\), let
\[
 \mathfrak G_{\rho,\nu}
 :=
 \{\eta:(\mathbb R,0)\to(S^1,b_{\rho,\nu}):
 \eta\text{ is a real-analytic germ}\}.
\]
Multiplication gives the exact bijection
\[
 T_{\rho,\nu}:\mathfrak G_1\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}
 \mathfrak G_{\rho,\nu},
 \qquad
 T_{\rho,\nu}(\gamma)(t)=b_{\rho,\nu}\gamma(t),
\]
with inverse
\(T_{\rho,\nu}^{-1}(\eta)(t)=b_{\rho,\nu}^{-1}\eta(t)\).
The Taylor phase germ is the image of
\(\gamma_{\rho,\nu}(t)=e^{\mathrm i\Theta_{\rho,\nu}(t)}\).
Evaluation at zero on \(\mathfrak G_{\rho,\nu}\) has the constant value
\(b_{\rho,\nu}\).  Thus it forgets the
 complete sequence \((\beta_{\rho,\nu,n})_{n\geq1}\), while the full phase germ
 retains exactly that sequence for the chosen direction \(\nu\).  It need not
 determine the real parts of the complex coefficients \(c_{\rho,n}\).  This is
coordinate-exact: if two normalized real phase germs have the same exponential,
their difference is a continuous \(2\pi\mathbb Z\)-valued germ vanishing at zero,
so it is zero and their Taylor coefficients agree.  For fixed \((\nu,n)\), the
fibre of \(c\mapsto\Im(c\nu^n)\) through \(c_0\) is
\(c_0+\nu^{-n}\mathbb R\).  This is the precise interface between the leading
phase decoration and the Taylor-atomic refinement.

\subsection{The independent circle-length coordinate from the literature}

The following is a typed literature interface, not a consequence of the phase
atomization.

\begin{proposition}[Critical ordinate to the circle-length family]
\label{prop:phase-cycle-coordinate}
Let
\[
 \mathcal C_{\mathrm{cyc}}
 :=
 \left\{
 (s,n):
 s>0,\ \zeta\left(\frac12+\mathrm is\right)=0,\
 n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}
 \right\}.
\]
The coordinate map
\[
 \ell:\mathcal C_{\mathrm{cyc}}\longrightarrow\R_{>0},
 \qquad
 \ell(s,n)=\frac{2\pi n}{s},
\]
has fibre
\[
 \ell^{-1}(L)
 =
 \left\{
 \left(\frac{2\pi n}{L},n\right):
 n\geq1,\
 \zeta\left(\frac12+\frac{2\pi\mathrm in}{L}\right)=0
 \right\}.
\]
For every \(s>0\) with \(\zeta(\tfrac12+\mathrm is)=0\), the restriction
\(n\mapsto\ell(s,n)\) has image the positive-integer family
\[
 \left\{\frac{2\pi n}{s}:n\geq1\right\},
\]
whose least member is \(2\pi/s\).  Connes and Consani prove that every circle
with one of these lengths is a zeta-cycle and that its orthogonal complement
carries the scaling character \(u\mapsto u^{\mathrm is}\)
\cite[\S6]{connesConsani2021ZetaCycles}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The fibre formula follows by solving
\[
 L=\frac{2\pi n}{s}
\]
for \(s\), while retaining the defining zero condition of
\(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{cyc}}\).
For each such fixed \(s\), strict increase in \(n\) gives the displayed image and
its least member.  The final sentence is the cited theorem: with
\(L=2\pi n/s\) and \(\mu=e^L\),
\[
 \mu^{\mathrm is}=e^{\mathrm isL}=e^{2\pi\mathrm in}=1,
\]
so the character descends to the circle
\(\R_+^*/\mu^{\mathbb Z}\); the source proves its orthogonality to the relevant
Riemann-sum image.
\end{proof}

Different pairs \((s,n)\) can in principle lie in one fibre of \(\ell\), as the
displayed fibre formula makes explicit.  Hence \(2\pi/s\) is only the least
member of the family attached to that fixed \(s\); it is not a global
shortest-cycle assertion.  No arrow from
\((\beta_{\rho,\nu,n})_{n\geq1}\) to the Connes--Consani Hilbert space has been
constructed here.

\subsection{Certificate and formalization boundary}

The finite certificate accompanying this chapter checks the Boolean polynomial
point maps, the split retraction, complete finite-product fibres on a bounded
grid, the elementary-symmetric endpoint identity, the affine-chart inverse,
the first logarithmic coefficients, and the packet sign/conjugation laws.  Its
floating-point checks use explicit error bounds and do not replace the standard
proofs above.  The uniform-convergence and holomorphic-logarithm arguments are
proved in the text and remain separate Lean targets.

The retained object classes are now explicit.  The Boolean affine cube is a
functor-of-points object; the phase decoration is a sheaf of pointed sets; the
coefficient support map forgets an \(S^1\)-fibre at a simple zero; finite affine
factors map to phases through \(P_N\) with the complete displayed fibres; the
Taylor atomization maps one fixed real power series to a uniformly convergent
phase product; and the circle-length coordinate is a separately sourced map
\((s,n)\mapsto2\pi n/s\).  These positive morphisms are the asserted
connections.  No categorical nonrelation, arithmetic nature of an individual
derivative, zero-location conclusion, or spectral realization by the local
phase atoms is asserted.
