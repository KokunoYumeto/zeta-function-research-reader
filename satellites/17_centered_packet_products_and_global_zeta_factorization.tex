\section{Centered packet products and the global zeta factorization}
\label{sec:centered-packet-products}

This section reconstructs the analytic packet-product branch at the level of
functions, divisors, coordinates, products, and local germs.  The published
inputs are kept separate from the deductions made here.  DLMF supplies the
normalization and reflection symmetry of Riemann's completed function
\cite[\S25.4, Eqs.~25.4.3--25.4.4]{apostolDLMF25}, the genus-one product over
the nontrivial zeros
\cite[\S25.2(iv), Eq.~25.2.12]{apostolDLMF25}, and the reciprocal-gamma
product
\cite[\S5.8, Eq.~5.8.2]{askeyRoyDLMF5}.  McMullen states and proves the
canonical-product and Hadamard factorization theorems used to pass from an
order-one entire function to its zero product
\cite[Theorems~3.14--3.15, pp.~79--82]{mcMullen2023AdvancedComplex}.
Lagarias and Montague record that the completed function is entire and that
its zeros are exactly the nontrivial zeta zeros
\cite[\S1, Eq.~(1.1) and the following paragraph]{lagariasMontague2011}.

The local source under audit used a multiset of arbitrarily chosen
representatives of sign pairs and then said that conjugation permuted that
multiset.  That sentence need not hold for an arbitrary choice.  We avoid the
choice entirely: sign orbits are the primary objects, conjugation acts on the
orbit set, and the quotient is exhibited explicitly as the packet quotient of
\cref{thm:ds-packet-descent}.  The second repair is analytic rather than
set-theoretic: every termwise logarithmic differentiation below is justified by
a compact-uniform \(O(n^{-2})\) or \(O(|\alpha|^{-2})\) estimate.

\subsection{Centered coordinates and their two involutions}

Retain the literal convention
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-xi-definition}
 \xi(s)
 =
 \frac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma\!\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\zeta(s),
\end{equation}
and introduce the centered coordinate
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-centered-definition}
 z=s-\frac12,
 \qquad
 \Xi(z)=\xi\!\left(\frac12+z\right).
\end{equation}
No rescaling of \(z\), \(\xi\), or \(\Xi\) is made.

\begin{proposition}[Evenness, real structure, and the nonzero center]
\label{prop:cpp-centered-symmetry}
The function \(\Xi\) is entire and satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-Xi-symmetries}
 \Xi(-z)=\Xi(z),
 \qquad
 \overline{\Xi(\overline z)}=\Xi(z).
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-Xi-center-positive}
 \Xi(0)=\xi\!\left(\frac12\right)>0.
\end{equation}
Consequently \(0\) is not a zero of \(\Xi\), every odd Taylor coefficient of
\(\Xi\) at \(0\) vanishes, and every even Taylor coefficient is real.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Entirety is the cited completed-function input.  The functional equation gives
\[
 \Xi(-z)
 =
 \xi\!\left(\frac12-z\right)
 =
 \xi\!\left(1-\left(\frac12+z\right)\right)
 =
 \Xi(z).
\]
The Dirichlet coefficients of \(\zeta\) are real in its half-plane of absolute
convergence, and the gamma and exponential factors in
\eqref{eq:cpp-xi-definition} respect complex conjugation there.  Analytic
continuation therefore gives the second identity in
\eqref{eq:cpp-Xi-symmetries} on all of \(\C\).

For \(0<x<1\), DLMF's alternating-series identity
\cite[\S25.2, Eq.~25.2.3]{apostolDLMF25} reads
\[
 \eta(x)
 :=
 \sum_{n\geq1}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n^x}
 =
 \bigl(1-2^{1-x}\bigr)\zeta(x).
\]
Pairing consecutive summands gives
\[
 \eta(x)
 =
 \sum_{k\geq1}\bigl((2k-1)^{-x}-(2k)^{-x}\bigr)>0.
\]
Since \(1-2^{1-x}<0\), it follows that \(\zeta(x)<0\).  At \(x=1/2\),
\[
 \xi\!\left(\frac12\right)
 =
 -\frac18\pi^{-1/4}\Gamma\!\left(\frac14\right)
 \zeta\!\left(\frac12\right)>0.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:cpp-Xi-center-positive}.  The Taylor assertions now follow
from evenness and the real structure.
\end{proof}

Let \(Z_\Xi\) denote the set of distinct zeros of \(\Xi\).  For
\(\alpha\in Z_\Xi\), let
\(m_\alpha=\operatorname{ord}_\alpha(\Xi)\).  Define
\[
 \iota_-(\alpha)=-\alpha,
 \qquad
 \iota_c(\alpha)=\overline\alpha.
\]
By \cref{prop:cpp-centered-symmetry}, these commuting involutions preserve
\(Z_\Xi\) and its multiplicity function.  Since \(0\notin Z_\Xi\),
\(\iota_-\) has no fixed point.  Put
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-sign-orbit-space}
 S_\Xi:=Z_\Xi/\langle\iota_-\rangle,
 \qquad
 p_-:Z_\Xi\longrightarrow S_\Xi.
\end{equation}
For \(u=p_-(\alpha)\), set \(m_u=m_\alpha\) and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-sign-factor}
 Q_u(z):=1-\frac{z^2}{\alpha^2}.
\end{equation}
Both definitions are independent of the representative because replacing
\(\alpha\) by \(-\alpha\) changes neither \(m_\alpha\) nor \(\alpha^2\).
Conjugation induces
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-conjugation-on-sign-orbits}
 \kappa:S_\Xi\longrightarrow S_\Xi,
 \qquad
 \kappa\bigl(p_-(\alpha)\bigr)=p_-(\overline\alpha).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Centered-zero packet quotient]
\label{thm:cpp-sign-packet-quotient}
Let \(D_\xi\), \(W=\{1,c,w,cw\}\), and
\(Y_\xi=|D_\xi|/W\) be as in \cref{thm:ds-packet-descent}.  Translation by
\(1/2\),
\[
 t:Z_\Xi\longrightarrow |D_\xi|,
 \qquad
 t(\alpha)=\frac12+\alpha,
\]
is a multiplicity-preserving bijection satisfying
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-translation-equivariance}
 t\iota_-=wt,
 \qquad
 t\iota_c=ct.
\end{equation}
It induces a bijection
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-sign-packet-bijection}
 \Psi:S_\Xi/\langle\kappa\rangle
 \overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}Y_\xi,
 \qquad
 \Psi\!\left(\langle\kappa\rangle p_-(\alpha)\right)
 =
 W\!\left(\frac12+\alpha\right).
\end{equation}
The composite
\[
 Z_\Xi\longrightarrow S_\Xi
 \longrightarrow S_\Xi/\langle\kappa\rangle
 \overset{\Psi}{\longrightarrow}Y_\xi
\]
has fibre over \(\Psi(\langle\kappa\rangle p_-(\alpha))\) equal to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-zero-to-packet-fibre}
 \{\alpha,-\alpha,\overline\alpha,-\overline\alpha\},
\end{equation}
with repetitions removed as a set.  It has two elements precisely when
\(\overline\alpha=-\alpha\), and otherwise has four elements.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:cpp-centered-definition} gives a bijection between zeros,
including equality of vanishing orders.  Direct coordinate calculation gives
\[
 t(-\alpha)=\frac12-\alpha
 =1-\left(\frac12+\alpha\right)=w(t(\alpha))
\]
and
\[
 t(\overline\alpha)
 =\frac12+\overline\alpha
 =\overline{\frac12+\alpha}=c(t(\alpha)),
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:cpp-translation-equivariance}.  Thus \(t\) maps every
orbit of \(\langle\iota_-,\iota_c\rangle\) onto one \(W\)-orbit and induces
\eqref{eq:cpp-sign-packet-bijection}.  Conversely, if
\(t(\beta)=g(t(\alpha))\) for \(g\in W\), then
\(\beta\) is respectively
\(\alpha,\overline\alpha,-\alpha\), or \(-\overline\alpha\).  This proves
injectivity and the fibre formula.  Since \(0\notin Z_\Xi\), the points
\(\alpha\) and \(-\alpha\) are distinct.  A fixed sign orbit under
conjugation would have \(\overline\alpha=\alpha\) or
\(\overline\alpha=-\alpha\).  The first case would make
\(1/2+\alpha\) a real zero in \(0<s<1\), which was ruled out in the proof of
\cref{prop:cpp-centered-symmetry}.  Hence the only two-element fibre has
\(\overline\alpha=-\alpha\), exactly the purely imaginary case.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The centered sign-orbit product}

The order-one property and the genus-one zero product are classical analytic
inputs.  DLMF records the genus-one product explicitly for \(\zeta\), citing
Titchmarsh \S2.12
\cite[\S25.2(iv), Eq.~25.2.12]{apostolDLMF25}.  Hadamard factorization in the
form used below is proved in
\cite[Theorems~3.14--3.15, pp.~79--82]{mcMullen2023AdvancedComplex}.
We retain the exponential factor until evenness removes it; it is not silently
normalized away.

\begin{theorem}[Choice-free centered product]
\label{thm:cpp-centered-product}
The series
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-reciprocal-square-sum}
 \sum_{u\in S_\Xi}\frac{m_u}{|\alpha_u|^2}
\end{equation}
converges, where \(\alpha_u\) is either representative of \(u\).  The product
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-centered-product}
 \Xi(z)
 =
 \Xi(0)\prod_{u\in S_\Xi}Q_u(z)^{m_u}
 =
 \Xi(0)\prod_{u\in S_\Xi}
 \left(1-\frac{z^2}{\alpha_u^2}\right)^{m_u}
\end{equation}
converges absolutely and locally uniformly on \(\C\).  It is independent of
the ordering of \(S_\Xi\) and of every representative \(\alpha_u\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Hadamard factorization of the order-one entire function \(\Xi\), using
\(\Xi(0)\ne0\), has the form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-hadamard-unpaired}
 \Xi(z)
 =
 e^{a+bz}
 \prod_{\alpha\in Z_\Xi}
 E_1\!\left(\frac z\alpha\right)^{m_\alpha},
 \qquad
 E_1(w)=(1-w)e^w.
\end{equation}
The exponent of convergence of the zeros is at most one; in particular
\[
 \sum_{\alpha\in Z_\Xi}\frac{m_\alpha}{|\alpha|^2}<\infty.
\]
Taking one term from each two-element sign orbit gives
\eqref{eq:cpp-reciprocal-square-sum}.

For either representative \(\alpha_u\) of \(u\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-E1-pair}
 E_1\!\left(\frac z{\alpha_u}\right)
 E_1\!\left(-\frac z{\alpha_u}\right)
 =
 (1-z/\alpha_u)e^{z/\alpha_u}
 (1+z/\alpha_u)e^{-z/\alpha_u}
 =
 Q_u(z).
\end{equation}
The canonical product in \eqref{eq:cpp-hadamard-unpaired} is locally normally
convergent, so the two factors belonging to each sign orbit may be grouped.
This gives
\[
 \Xi(z)=e^{a+bz}\prod_{u\in S_\Xi}Q_u(z)^{m_u}.
\]
The product on the right is even.  Dividing the identity at \(z\) by the
identity at \(-z\), initially away from the discrete zero set and then by
analytic continuation, gives \(e^{2bz}=1\) for every \(z\).  Differentiation at
\(z=0\) gives \(b=0\).  Evaluation at \(z=0\) then gives
\(e^a=\Xi(0)\), proving \eqref{eq:cpp-centered-product}.

It remains to check the stronger convergence assertion for the grouped
product.  Fix \(R>0\).  For \(|z|\leq R\),
\[
 \sum_{u\in S_\Xi}
 m_u\left|\frac{z^2}{\alpha_u^2}\right|
 \leq
 R^2\sum_{u\in S_\Xi}\frac{m_u}{|\alpha_u|^2}<\infty.
\]
The standard infinite-product criterion therefore gives absolute and uniform
convergence on \(|z|\leq R\).  Since \(R\) is arbitrary, convergence is local
uniform on \(\C\).  Absolute convergence makes the product invariant under
reordering, and \(\alpha_u^2=(-\alpha_u)^2\) removes representative choice.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Packet polynomials and their coordinate inverse}

For \(O\in Y_\xi\), define the normalized packet polynomial
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-packet-polynomial}
 P_O(s)
 :=
 \prod_{\eta\in O}
 \frac{s-\eta}{\frac12-\eta}.
\end{equation}
The denominator is nonzero because
\(\xi(1/2)=\Xi(0)>0\).  This normalization is displayed because it is
mathematical data: it fixes the otherwise undetermined nonzero scalar of the
polynomial.

\begin{proposition}[All packet coordinates, without a representative convention]
\label{prop:cpp-packet-polynomial-coordinates}
Every \(P_O\) lies in \(\mathbb R[s]\), satisfies \(P_O(1/2)=1\), and has
simple root set exactly \(O\).

If \(O\) has two points, then for either nonzero real coordinate
\(\gamma\) with
\[
 O=\left\{\frac12+\mathrm i\gamma,\frac12-\mathrm i\gamma\right\},
\]
one has
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-packet-quadratic}
 P_O\!\left(\frac12+z\right)
 =
 1+\frac{z^2}{\gamma^2}.
\end{equation}
If \(O\) has four points, then for any coordinates
\(a,\gamma\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}\) with
\[
 O=
 \left\{
 \frac12+a+\mathrm i\gamma,\,
 \frac12+a-\mathrm i\gamma,\,
 \frac12-a+\mathrm i\gamma,\,
 \frac12-a-\mathrm i\gamma
 \right\},
\]
and \(\alpha=a+\mathrm i\gamma\), one has
\begin{align}
\label{eq:cpp-packet-quartic-factored}
 P_O\!\left(\frac12+z\right)
 &=
 \frac{\bigl((z-a)^2+\gamma^2\bigr)
       \bigl((z+a)^2+\gamma^2\bigr)}
      {(a^2+\gamma^2)^2}\\
\label{eq:cpp-packet-quartic-sign-factors}
 &=
 \left(1-\frac{z^2}{\alpha^2}\right)
 \left(1-\frac{z^2}{\overline\alpha^2}\right)\\
\label{eq:cpp-packet-quartic-expanded}
 &=
 1-2\Re(\alpha^{-2})z^2+|\alpha|^{-4}z^4.
\end{align}
All displayed expressions are invariant under the sign changes of \(a\) and
\(\gamma\) generated by the packet action.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The orbit \(O\) is closed under conjugation, so conjugate roots occur with the
same multiplicity in the defining product.  Its coefficients are therefore
real.  Substitution of \(s=1/2\) makes every factor equal to one, and the
numerator shows that the root set is \(O\).

For the two-point packet,
\[
 \frac{(z-\mathrm i\gamma)(z+\mathrm i\gamma)}
 {(-\mathrm i\gamma)(\mathrm i\gamma)}
 =
 \frac{z^2+\gamma^2}{\gamma^2},
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:cpp-packet-quadratic}.  For the four-point packet,
pairing conjugate roots first gives
\[
 \prod_{\eta\in O}\left(\frac12+z-\eta\right)
 =
 \bigl((z-a)^2+\gamma^2\bigr)
 \bigl((z+a)^2+\gamma^2\bigr).
\]
At \(z=0\), the same expression equals \((a^2+\gamma^2)^2\).  This proves
\eqref{eq:cpp-packet-quartic-factored}.  Pairing instead by the sign
involution gives \eqref{eq:cpp-packet-quartic-sign-factors}.  Multiplication of
those two factors gives \eqref{eq:cpp-packet-quartic-expanded}.  The final
invariance follows either from the root set or by direct substitution.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Coordinate inverse and exact fibres]
\label{thm:cpp-packet-polynomial-inverse}
The map
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-packet-polynomial-map}
 \mathcal P:Y_\xi\longrightarrow\mathbb R[s],
 \qquad O\longmapsto P_O,
\end{equation}
is injective.  More explicitly:
\begin{enumerate}
\item If
\[
 P_O\!\left(\frac12+z\right)=1+c_2z^2
\]
is quadratic, then \(c_2>0\), \(\gamma^2=c_2^{-1}\), and its root set
recovers \(O=\{1/2\pm\mathrm i\gamma\}\).
\item If
\[
 P_O\!\left(\frac12+z\right)=1+c_2z^2+c_4z^4
\]
is quartic, then \(c_4>0\), and the squared coordinates are recovered by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-quartic-coordinate-inverse}
 R^2=c_4^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 \Delta=\frac{c_2}{2c_4},
 \qquad
 a^2=\frac{R^2-\Delta}{2},
 \qquad
 \gamma^2=\frac{R^2+\Delta}{2}.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
The composite
\[
 |D_\xi|\xrightarrow{q}Y_\xi
 \xrightarrow{\mathcal P}\mathbb R[s]
\]
has fibre over \(P_O\) exactly \(O\).  The augmented coordinate
\((P_O,m_O)\) recovers the effective packet divisor
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-packet-divisor-recovery}
 D_O=m_O\sum_{\eta\in O}[\eta].
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a quadratic packet, \eqref{eq:cpp-packet-quadratic} gives
\(c_2=\gamma^{-2}\), proving the first inverse.  For a quartic packet, put
\(R^2=a^2+\gamma^2=|\alpha|^2\).  From
\eqref{eq:cpp-packet-quartic-expanded},
\[
 c_4=R^{-4}
\]
and
\[
 c_2
 =
 -2\frac{a^2-\gamma^2}{R^4}
 =
 2c_4(\gamma^2-a^2).
\]
Thus \(\Delta=\gamma^2-a^2\), and solving the two linear equations
\[
 a^2+\gamma^2=R^2,
 \qquad
 \gamma^2-a^2=\Delta
\]
gives \eqref{eq:cpp-quartic-coordinate-inverse}.  The signs of \(a\) and
\(\gamma\) are not discarded accidentally: all four sign choices are exactly
the points already identified inside the packet \(O\).  Hence the coefficients
recover the packet, proving injectivity.

The root set of \(P_O\) is \(O\), so two points of \(|D_\xi|\) have the same
polynomial precisely when they lie in the same packet.  This proves the fibre
formula.  The polynomial has each packet point once; adjoining the common
multiplicity and multiplying the corresponding point divisors proves
\eqref{eq:cpp-packet-divisor-recovery}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The product on the packet quotient}

\begin{theorem}[Packet-grouped product]
\label{thm:cpp-packet-product}
For every \(O\in Y_\xi\), let \(m_O\) be the common vanishing order on that
packet.  Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-packet-product}
 \xi(s)
 =
 \xi\!\left(\frac12\right)
 \prod_{O\in Y_\xi}P_O(s)^{m_O},
\end{equation}
and the product converges absolutely and locally uniformly on \(\C\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put \(z=s-1/2\).  By \cref{thm:cpp-sign-packet-quotient}, a
\(\kappa\)-orbit in \(S_\Xi\) corresponds to exactly one packet.
If \(u=p_-(\alpha)\) is fixed by \(\kappa\), then
\(\overline\alpha=-\alpha\), and
\eqref{eq:cpp-packet-quadratic} gives
\[
 Q_u(z)=P_{\Psi(\langle\kappa\rangle u)}(s).
\]
If \(u\ne\kappa(u)\), then
\eqref{eq:cpp-packet-quartic-sign-factors} gives
\[
 Q_u(z)Q_{\kappa(u)}(z)
 =
 P_{\Psi(\langle\kappa\rangle u)}(s).
\]
Multiplicities are preserved by both involutions.  Grouping the
absolutely convergent product of \cref{thm:cpp-centered-product} over these
one- or two-element \(\kappa\)-orbits therefore gives
\eqref{eq:cpp-packet-product}.  Grouping finitely many factors at a time
preserves absolute and local uniform convergence.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Degree records orbit size, and nothing further is inferred]
\label{rem:cpp-packet-degree-scope}
At this exact coordinate level, a two-point packet contributes one quadratic
factor and a four-point packet contributes one quartic factor.  This is a
description of the quotient and its product.  No statement about the location
of all packets, the simplicity of their zeros, or a spectral realization is
deduced from the factor degrees.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Centered Taylor atoms as symmetric coordinates}

To retain multiplicity without choosing repeated representatives, define the
countable index set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-symmetric-index-set}
 I_\Xi
 :=
 \{(u,\ell):u\in S_\Xi,\ 1\leq\ell\leq m_u\},
 \qquad
 a_{(u,\ell)}:=\alpha_u^{-2}.
\end{equation}
The number \(a_{(u,\ell)}\) is independent of the representative of \(u\), and
\cref{eq:cpp-reciprocal-square-sum} says
\[
 \sum_{i\in I_\Xi}|a_i|<\infty.
\]
For \(n\geq1\), set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-elementary-symmetric}
 \sigma_n
 :=
 \sum_{\substack{J\subset I_\Xi\\|J|=n}}
 \prod_{i\in J}a_i,
 \qquad
 \sigma_0:=1.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Absolute symmetric expansion]
\label{lem:cpp-symmetric-expansion}
Every sum in \eqref{eq:cpp-elementary-symmetric} is absolutely convergent, and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-infinite-symmetric-expansion}
 \prod_{i\in I_\Xi}(1-a_i z^2)
 =
 \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^n\sigma_n z^{2n}
\end{equation}
with absolute and locally uniform convergence on \(\C\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For fixed \(n\),
\[
 \sum_{\substack{J\subset I_\Xi\\|J|=n}}
 \prod_{i\in J}|a_i|
 \leq
 \frac1{n!}\left(\sum_{i\in I_\Xi}|a_i|\right)^n<\infty.
\]
For a finite subset \(F\subset I_\Xi\), ordinary polynomial multiplication
gives
\[
 \prod_{i\in F}(1-a_i z^2)
 =
 \sum_{n=0}^{|F|}(-1)^n\sigma_{n,F}z^{2n}.
\]
As \(F\) increases through finite subsets, absolute convergence gives
\(\sigma_{n,F}\to\sigma_n\).  On \(|z|\leq R\),
\[
 \sum_{n\geq0}|\sigma_n|R^{2n}
 \leq
 \prod_{i\in I_\Xi}(1+|a_i|R^2)<\infty.
\]
The Weierstrass \(M\)-test now permits passage to the limit uniformly on the
disc and proves \eqref{eq:cpp-infinite-symmetric-expansion}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact centered Taylor coordinates]
\label{thm:cpp-centered-taylor}
The Taylor expansion of the completed function at its symmetry center is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-Xi-taylor}
 \Xi(z)
 =
 \Xi(0)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^n\sigma_nz^{2n}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, for every \(n\geq0\),
\begin{align}
\label{eq:cpp-Xi-derivative-coordinates}
 \frac{\Xi^{(2n)}(0)}{(2n)!\Xi(0)}
 &=
 (-1)^n\sigma_n,
 &
 \Xi^{(2n+1)}(0)&=0,\\
\label{eq:cpp-xi-derivative-coordinates}
 \frac{\xi^{(2n)}(1/2)}{(2n)!\xi(1/2)}
 &=
 (-1)^n\sigma_n,
 &
 \xi^{(2n+1)}(1/2)&=0.
\end{align}
In particular,
\begin{align}
\label{eq:cpp-second-central-derivative}
 \frac{\xi''(1/2)}{2\xi(1/2)}
 &=
 -\sum_{i\in I_\Xi}a_i,\\
\label{eq:cpp-fourth-central-derivative}
 \frac{\xi^{(4)}(1/2)}{24\xi(1/2)}
 &=
 \sum_{\substack{J\subset I_\Xi\\|J|=2}}
 \prod_{i\in J}a_i.
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write the product in \cref{thm:cpp-centered-product} with one factor for each
index \(i=(u,\ell)\in I_\Xi\).  Apply
\cref{lem:cpp-symmetric-expansion}, multiply by \(\Xi(0)\), and compare the
result with the Taylor series of \(\Xi\).  Translation by
\(s=1/2+z\) gives the formulas for \(\xi\).  The last two identities are the
cases \(n=1\) and \(n=2\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Power-sum form on the zero-free centered disc]
\label{prop:cpp-power-sum-log}
Let
\[
 r_0:=\inf_{\alpha\in Z_\Xi}|\alpha|>0,
\]
and for \(k\geq1\) define the absolutely convergent power sum
\[
 p_k:=\sum_{i\in I_\Xi}a_i^k.
\]
Indeed, only finitely many \(i\) have \(|a_i|>1\), while
\(|a_i|^k\leq|a_i|\) for every remaining index.  Thus
\(\sum_i|a_i|^k<\infty\) follows directly from
\(\sum_i|a_i|<\infty\), with no rearrangement convention.
For \(|z|<r_0\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-power-sum-log}
 \log\frac{\Xi(z)}{\Xi(0)}
 =
 -\sum_{k\geq1}\frac{p_k}{k}z^{2k},
\end{equation}
where the logarithm is the branch taking value \(0\) at \(z=0\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The zero set of a nonzero entire function is discrete, and \(0\notin Z_\Xi\),
so \(r_0>0\).  Fix \(r<r_0\).  Then
\(|a_i z^2|\leq r^2/|\alpha_i|^2<1\) for \(|z|\leq r\), and
\[
 \sum_i\sum_{k\geq1}\frac{|a_i z^2|^k}{k}
 \leq
 \sum_i\frac{|a_i|r^2}{1-|a_i|r^2}.
\]
Only finitely many denominators can be close to zero; the remaining tail is
bounded by a fixed multiple of \(\sum_i|a_i|\).  Thus the double series is
absolutely and uniformly convergent on \(|z|\leq r\).  Applying
\(\log(1-w)=-\sum_{k\geq1}w^k/k\) term by term to
\cref{eq:cpp-centered-product} proves \eqref{eq:cpp-power-sum-log}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The pole, trivial sector, and packet sector}

\begin{proposition}[Reciprocal-gamma product in the \(s\)-coordinate]
\label{prop:cpp-reciprocal-gamma}
For every \(s\in\C\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-gamma-product}
 \frac1{\Gamma(s/2)}
 =
 \frac{s}{2}e^{\gamma s/2}
 \prod_{n\geq1}
 \left(1+\frac{s}{2n}\right)e^{-s/(2n)},
\end{equation}
with local uniform convergence.  Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-zeta-from-xi}
 \zeta(s)
 =
 \frac{\pi^{s/2}e^{\gamma s/2}}{s-1}
 \left[
 \prod_{n\geq1}
 \left(1+\frac{s}{2n}\right)e^{-s/(2n)}
 \right]\xi(s)
\end{equation}
as an identity of meromorphic functions.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
DLMF's reciprocal-gamma product
\cite[\S5.8, Eq.~5.8.2]{askeyRoyDLMF5} is
\[
 \frac1{\Gamma(w)}
 =
 we^{\gamma w}\prod_{n\geq1}
 \left(1+\frac wn\right)e^{-w/n}.
\]
Substitution of \(w=s/2\) proves \eqref{eq:cpp-gamma-product}.  Solving
\eqref{eq:cpp-xi-definition} for \(\zeta\) gives
\[
 \zeta(s)
 =
 \frac{2\pi^{s/2}}{s(s-1)}
 \frac{\xi(s)}{\Gamma(s/2)}.
\]
Substitution of \eqref{eq:cpp-gamma-product} cancels the displayed factor
\(2/s\) and proves \eqref{eq:cpp-zeta-from-xi}.  Both sides are meromorphic,
so the identity extends across removable intermediate singularities.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Global pole-trivial-packet factorization]
\label{thm:cpp-global-zeta-factorization}
The exact factorization
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-global-zeta-factorization}
 \zeta(s)
 =
 \frac{\xi(1/2)e^{(\gamma+\log\pi)s/2}}{s-1}
 \prod_{n\geq1}
 \left(1+\frac{s}{2n}\right)e^{-s/(2n)}
 \prod_{O\in Y_\xi}P_O(s)^{m_O}
\end{equation}
holds on \(\C\) as an identity of meromorphic functions.  The displayed
coordinates retain separately:
\[
 \frac1{s-1},
 \qquad
 e^{(\gamma+\log\pi)s/2}
 \prod_{n\geq1}
 \left(1+\frac{s}{2n}\right)e^{-s/(2n)},
 \qquad
 \prod_{O\in Y_\xi}P_O(s)^{m_O}.
\]
They have, respectively, the pole at \(1\), the simple zeros at the negative
even integers, and the nontrivial zero divisor.  No factor is absorbed into
another.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Insert \eqref{eq:cpp-packet-product} into
\eqref{eq:cpp-zeta-from-xi} and use
\[
 \pi^{s/2}e^{\gamma s/2}
 =
 e^{(\gamma+\log\pi)s/2}.
\]
The first factor has divisor \(-[1]\).  In the second sector, every exponential
is nonzero, while \(1+s/(2n)\) has one simple zero at \(-2n\); the
reciprocal-gamma identity proves that there are no additional zeros.  By
\cref{thm:cpp-packet-polynomial-inverse}, the final sector has effective
divisor
\(\sum_{O\in Y_\xi}m_O\sum_{\eta\in O}[\eta]=D_\xi\).
This proves both the function identity and the stated divisor accounting.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Normally convergent logarithmic derivatives}

\begin{lemma}[Compact-uniform derivative bounds]
\label{lem:cpp-log-derivative-bounds}
Let \(K\subset\C\) be compact and put
\[
 R_s=\max_{s\in K}|s|,
 \qquad
 R_z=\max_{s\in K}\left|s-\frac12\right|.
\]
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every integer \(n>R_s\) and \(s\in K\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-gamma-derivative-bound}
 \left|
 \frac1{s+2n}-\frac1{2n}
 \right|
 =
 \left|\frac{-s}{2n(s+2n)}\right|
 \leq
 \frac{R_s}{2n^2}.
\end{equation}
\item For \(u=p_-(\alpha)\in S_\Xi\) with
\(|\alpha|>2R_z\), \(z=s-1/2\), and \(s\in K\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-packet-derivative-bound}
 \left|
 \frac{d}{dz}\log Q_u(z)
 \right|
 =
 \left|\frac{-2z}{\alpha^2-z^2}\right|
 \leq
 \frac{8R_z}{3|\alpha|^2}.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the two derivative series obtained from the trivial factors and
the sign-orbit factors converge normally on compact subsets avoiding their
respective divisors.  The same is true after grouping sign orbits into
packets.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(n>R_s\), then
\[
 |s+2n|\geq2n-|s|\geq2n-R_s>n,
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:cpp-gamma-derivative-bound}.  If
\(|\alpha|>2R_z\), then
\[
 |\alpha^2-z^2|
 \geq|\alpha|^2-|z|^2
 >
 \frac34|\alpha|^2,
\]
which gives \eqref{eq:cpp-packet-derivative-bound}.  The first majorant is a
constant multiple of \(\sum n^{-2}\); the second is a constant multiple of
\eqref{eq:cpp-reciprocal-square-sum}.  Only finitely many excluded small
indices remain on a fixed compact set.  The Weierstrass \(M\)-test proves
normal convergence.  A packet contains one or two sign orbits, so finite
grouping preserves it.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[One packet's rational logarithmic derivative]
\label{prop:cpp-one-packet-log-derivative}
For \(s\notin O\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-one-packet-log-derivative}
 \frac{P_O'(s)}{P_O(s)}
 =
 \sum_{\eta\in O}\frac1{s-\eta}.
\end{equation}
The two sides are the same rational function in \(\mathbb R(s)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply the finite product rule to
\eqref{eq:cpp-packet-polynomial}.  The constant denominators have derivative
zero, so
\[
 \frac{d}{ds}\log P_O(s)
 =
 \sum_{\eta\in O}\frac{d}{ds}\log(s-\eta)
 =
 \sum_{\eta\in O}\frac1{s-\eta}.
\]
The left side belongs to \(\mathbb R(s)\) because \(P_O\in\mathbb R[s]\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Packet-grouped partial fractions]
\label{thm:cpp-zeta-log-derivative}
On
\[
 \C\setminus
 \left(
 \{1\}\cup\{-2,-4,-6,\ldots\}\cup|D_\xi|
 \right),
\]
the normally convergent identity
\begin{align}
\label{eq:cpp-zeta-log-derivative}
 \frac{\zeta'(s)}{\zeta(s)}
 &=
 \frac{\gamma+\log\pi}{2}
 -\frac1{s-1}
 +\sum_{n\geq1}
 \left(\frac1{s+2n}-\frac1{2n}\right)
 +\sum_{O\in Y_\xi}
 m_O\frac{P_O'(s)}{P_O(s)}\\
\label{eq:cpp-zeta-log-derivative-expanded}
 &=
 \frac{\gamma+\log\pi}{2}
 -\frac1{s-1}
 +\sum_{n\geq1}
 \left(\frac1{s+2n}-\frac1{2n}\right)
 +\sum_{O\in Y_\xi}
 m_O\sum_{\eta\in O}\frac1{s-\eta}
\end{align}
holds.  On \(\C\setminus|D_\xi|\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-xi-log-derivative}
 \frac{\xi'(s)}{\xi(s)}
 =
 \sum_{O\in Y_\xi}
 m_O\frac{P_O'(s)}{P_O(s)}
 =
 \sum_{O\in Y_\xi}
 m_O\sum_{\eta\in O}\frac1{s-\eta}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix a point outside the displayed divisor and choose a closed disc \(K\)
around it which still avoids the divisor.  All factors in
\eqref{eq:cpp-global-zeta-factorization} are nonzero on \(K\).  Choose local
holomorphic logarithms there.  Finite partial products satisfy the ordinary
finite logarithmic derivative formula.  By
\cref{lem:cpp-log-derivative-bounds}, both infinite derivative tails converge
uniformly on \(K\).  The products themselves converge locally uniformly by
\cref{prop:cpp-reciprocal-gamma,thm:cpp-packet-product}.  Passing to the limit
in the finite identities is therefore valid and gives
\eqref{eq:cpp-zeta-log-derivative}.  Substitution of
\cref{eq:cpp-one-packet-log-derivative} gives
\eqref{eq:cpp-zeta-log-derivative-expanded}.

Applying the same argument directly to
\eqref{eq:cpp-packet-product} removes the pole and gamma sectors and proves
\eqref{eq:cpp-xi-log-derivative}.  Since the point was arbitrary, the
identities hold on the stated domains.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Residues retain multiplicity]
At \(s=1\), the term \(-1/(s-1)\) has residue \(-1\), as required for a
simple pole of \(\zeta\).  At \(s=-2n\), the \(n\)-th trivial term has residue
\(1\).  At \(\eta\in O\), the packet term has residue \(m_O\).  Thus
\cref{eq:cpp-zeta-log-derivative-expanded} retains the signed divisor
coordinate at every pole and zero.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Exact global-to-local morphisms}

For \(O\in Y_\xi\), remove its factor from the global packet product and set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-complementary-factor}
 H_O(s)
 :=
 \xi\!\left(\frac12\right)
 \prod_{\substack{O'\in Y_\xi\\O'\ne O}}
 P_{O'}(s)^{m_{O'}}.
\end{equation}
Absolute local uniform convergence makes \(H_O\) entire, and its zero divisor
is the union of all packets other than \(O\).  Hence
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-packet-complement-decomposition}
 \xi(s)=H_O(s)P_O(s)^{m_O},
 \qquad
 H_O(\rho)\ne0\quad(\rho\in O).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Leading jet and normalized germ from the packet system]
\label{thm:cpp-global-to-local-jet}
Let \(\rho\in O\), let \(m=m_O\), and retain the leading coefficient
\[
 \mu_\rho=\frac{\xi^{(m)}(\rho)}{m!}
\]
from \cref{prop:ds-phase-transport}.  Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-leading-jet-from-packets}
 \mu_\rho
 =
 H_O(\rho)\bigl(P_O'(\rho)\bigr)^m.
\end{equation}
On a sufficiently small disc about \(\rho\), the normalized local germ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-normalized-germ-from-packets}
 \frac{\xi(s)}{\mu_\rho(s-\rho)^m}
 =
 \frac{H_O(s)}{H_O(\rho)}
 \left(
 \frac{P_O(s)}
 {P_O'(\rho)(s-\rho)}
 \right)^m.
\end{equation}
Both factors on the right extend holomorphically across \(s=\rho\) and take
the value \(1\) there.  Thus the complete global packet system
\[
 \left(
 \xi(1/2),\{(P_O,m_O):O\in Y_\xi\}
 \right)
\]
determines every leading jet, its phase, and every normalized Taylor atom of
\cref{thm:phase-taylor-atomization,thm:phase-local-logarithm}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The roots of \(P_O\) are simple because the elements of \(O\) are distinct.
Taylor expansion at \(\rho\) gives
\[
 P_O(s)=P_O'(\rho)(s-\rho)+O((s-\rho)^2),
\]
with \(P_O'(\rho)\ne0\).  Equation
\eqref{eq:cpp-packet-complement-decomposition} and
\(H_O(s)=H_O(\rho)+O(s-\rho)\) therefore give
\[
 \xi(s)
 =
 H_O(\rho)\bigl(P_O'(\rho)\bigr)^m(s-\rho)^m
 +O((s-\rho)^{m+1}).
\]
Comparison with the definition of \(\mu_\rho\) proves
\eqref{eq:cpp-leading-jet-from-packets}.  Dividing the exact decomposition by
that coefficient and by \((s-\rho)^m\) gives
\eqref{eq:cpp-normalized-germ-from-packets}.  The removable factor
\[
 \frac{P_O(s)}{P_O'(\rho)(s-\rho)}
\]
has value \(1\) at \(\rho\), as does \(H_O(s)/H_O(\rho)\).  Their product
therefore supplies the normalized germ, whose Taylor coefficients and
normalized logarithm are the atoms constructed in Chapter~16.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact information loss of the one-packet projection]
\label{prop:cpp-one-packet-information-loss}
The packet coordinate \((P_O,m_O)\) recovers \(D_O\) by
\eqref{eq:cpp-packet-divisor-recovery}, but it does not by itself determine
\(\mu_\rho\).  The missing coordinate is exactly the nonzero complementary
value \(H_O(\rho)\) in \eqref{eq:cpp-leading-jet-from-packets}.

More concretely, fix \(O\), \(m\geq1\), and \(\rho\in O\).  Choose
\(\lambda\in\mathbb R_{>0}\) such that
\[
 Q_\lambda(s):=1+\lambda(s-1/2)^2
\]
has no zero in \(O\) and \(Q_\lambda(\rho)\ne1\).  The two finite functions
\[
 F_1(s)=P_O(s)^m,
 \qquad
 F_2(s)=Q_\lambda(s)P_O(s)^m
\]
have the same packet polynomial and multiplicity at \(O\), but their leading
coefficients at \(\rho\) are
\[
 \bigl(P_O'(\rho)\bigr)^m
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad
 Q_\lambda(\rho)\bigl(P_O'(\rho)\bigr)^m.
\]
Both functions remain even in \(s-1/2\) and respect complex conjugation;
the additional roots of \(Q_\lambda\) are the two-point packet
\(\{1/2\pm\mathrm i/\sqrt\lambda\}\), chosen disjoint from \(O\).
Thus the fibre of the forgetful map from a full factor system to one packet
contains distinct leading-jet values whenever the complementary coordinate is
allowed to vary.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion is exactly
\cref{eq:cpp-leading-jet-from-packets}.  Only finitely many values of
\(\lambda>0\) make \(Q_\lambda\) vanish at a point of \(O\).  Moreover
\(\rho\ne1/2\), so \(Q_\lambda(\rho)=1\) would force \(\lambda=0\); every
admissible positive choice therefore avoids it.  Choose any positive
\(\lambda\) outside the finite excluded set.
The displayed leading coefficients follow by the same first-order expansion
used in \cref{thm:cpp-global-to-local-jet}.  This supplies two explicit points
in one fibre and identifies the omitted coordinate rather than inferring
nonidentity from different presentations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Reconstruction map on admissible packet data]
\label{thm:cpp-reconstruction-map}
Let \(\mathfrak P_1\) be the set of data
\[
 \mathcal D=
 \left(C,\{(P_O,m_O)\}_{O\in Y}\right)
\]
such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(C\in\mathbb R^\times\);
\item each \(P_O\in\mathbb R[s]\) has one of the explicit forms in
      \cref{prop:cpp-packet-polynomial-coordinates} and
      \(P_O(1/2)=1\);
\item \(m_O\) is a positive integer; and
\item after resolving every \(P_O\) into its one or two sign-orbit factors,
      the resulting reciprocal-square sum converges and the reconstructed
      entire function has order at most one.
\end{enumerate}
For data whose union of root packets is discrete, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cpp-reconstruction-morphism}
 \mathcal R(\mathcal D)(s)
 :=
 C\prod_{O\in Y}P_O(s)^{m_O}.
\end{equation}
Then \(\mathcal R(\mathcal D)\) is an even function of \(s-1/2\), respects
complex conjugation, takes the value \(C\) at \(1/2\), and has effective zero
divisor
\[
 \sum_{O\in Y}m_O\sum_{\eta\in O}[\eta].
\]
Conversely, within the class of order-at-most-one entire functions satisfying
those two symmetries and nonvanishing at \(1/2\), the center value and packet
divisor recover the function uniquely.  Hence extraction of packet data and
\(\mathcal R\) are mutually inverse on that class.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The convergence condition and the proof of
\cref{thm:cpp-centered-product} give an entire product.  Each packet factor is
even in \(s-1/2\), has real coefficients, and is one at the center, proving the
three stated properties.  Its roots and multiplicity give the displayed
divisor.

For uniqueness, let \(F_1,F_2\) be two functions in the stated class with the
same packet divisor and center value.  Apply Hadamard factorization separately
to \(F_1(1/2+z)\) and \(F_2(1/2+z)\).  Their canonical zero products are the
same, including multiplicities, so division gives
\[
 \frac{F_1(1/2+z)}{F_2(1/2+z)}=e^{a+bz}.
\]
Both functions are even in \(z\), so the quotient is even and \(b=0\), by the
same derivative-at-zero argument used in
\cref{thm:cpp-centered-product}.  Equality of the center values gives
\(e^a=1\).  Thus \(F_1=F_2\).  Extraction followed by reconstruction and
reconstruction followed by extraction are therefore both identities.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Certificate and theorem boundaries}

The finite algebra in this chapter admits a deterministic certificate:
the two- and four-point packet formulas, the coefficient inverse
\eqref{eq:cpp-quartic-coordinate-inverse}, the one-packet logarithmic
derivative, the first symmetric coefficients, and the leading-jet formula can
all be checked as polynomial or rational identities over exact symbolic
coordinates.  The companion script
\texttt{verify\_centered\_packet\_products.py} passes seventeen named exact
checks under Python/SymPy 3.13.9/1.13.1 and 3.13.1/1.13.3; receipt
\texttt{CERT-GNTE-PACKET-20260828-0001} binds both runs.  Such a certificate
checks those identities only.  It does not
certify Hadamard factorization, the DLMF products, convergence of the actual
zero product, analytic continuation, or any assertion about an individual
zero.  Those analytic statements are proved above from their cited inputs and
the explicit convergence estimates.

The resulting interface is exact.  The sign-orbit quotient maps bijectively to
the packet quotient; the packet polynomial has an explicit coordinate inverse;
the augmented packet polynomial recovers the packet divisor; the complete
packet system reconstructs the completed function in the stated order-one
class; and \eqref{eq:cpp-leading-jet-from-packets} maps the global product to
the local phase and Taylor-atom layer.  No endpoint, simplicity, or spectral
claim is added.
