\section{Vacuum fluids, thermal time, and arithmetic spectral tests}
\label{sec:rh-counterexample-routes}

This investigation follows two concrete constructions: the Rindler
fluid--gravity map and the Bost--Connes cooling map. A second, independent
calculation evaluates the complete Weil form without imposing RH. The aim is
to determine whether these constructions expose an off-critical zeta zero.
No such zero or negative Weil certificate has been established here.
The explicit calculations below are retained regardless of that outcome;
no novelty claim is made for elementary consequences of the cited constructions.

\subsection{A Ricci-flat fluid metric and its temperature coordinates}

Bredberg--Keeler--Lysov--Strominger construct the following metric
\cite[\S5.1, (14)--(20)]{bkls2011}. Coordinates are
$(\tau,r,x^1,\ldots,x^p)$, the timelike cutoff is $r=r_c>0$, and
the future Rindler horizon is at $r=0$. Spatial indices use $\delta_{ij}$.
Retain their original velocity $v_i(\tau,x)$ and pressure $P(\tau,x)$:
\begin{align}
 ds^2={}&-r\,d\tau^2+2\,d\tau\,dr+\delta_{ij}dx^i dx^j \notag\\
 &-2(1-r/r_c)v_i\,dx^i d\tau-2r_c^{-1}v_i\,dx^i dr \notag\\
 &+(1-r/r_c)\bigl[(v^2+2P)d\tau^2+r_c^{-1}v_i v_jdx^i dx^j\bigr]
   +r_c^{-1}(v^2+2P)d\tau\,dr \notag\\
 &-r_c^{-1}(r^2-r_c^2)\Delta v_i\,dx^i d\tau+\cdots .
 \label{eq:rh-bkls-metric}
\end{align}
The grading is $v=O(\epsilon)$, $P=O(\epsilon^2)$,
$\partial_i=O(\epsilon)$, $\partial_\tau=O(\epsilon^2)$.
The displayed metric solves the vacuum equations through the displayed
hydrodynamic orders when
\begin{equation}
 \partial_i v^i=0,\qquad
 \partial_\tau v_i+v^j\partial_jv_i+\partial_iP-r_c\Delta v_i=0.
 \label{eq:rh-bkls-ns}
\end{equation}
This is a source-level perturbative reconstruction, not a claimed convergent
metric for an arbitrary singular fluid. The boundary conditions are a flat
induced metric at $r_c$, regularity at the future horizon, and the incoming-data
condition specified in the source. Changing these data changes the reconstruction.

Here is the exact coordinate calculation relating its viscosity to temperature.
It uses the flat zeroth-order metric and introduces no identification of bulk
quantum algebras. For $r>0$ put
\[
 \eta=\tau-\log r,\qquad \xi=2\sqrt r,
 \qquad r=\xi^2/4,\quad \tau=\eta+\log(\xi^2/4).
\]
Substitution of $d\tau=d\eta+dr/r$ gives
\begin{equation}
 -r\,d\tau^2+2\,d\tau\,dr
 =-r\,d\eta^2+\frac{dr^2}{r}
 =-\frac{\xi^2}{4}d\eta^2+d\xi^2.
 \label{eq:rh-rindler-coordinates}
\end{equation}
The further map to Minkowski coordinates is
$X^0=\xi\sinh(\eta/2)$, $X^1=\xi\cosh(\eta/2)$.
Its image is the right wedge $X^1>|X^0|$; the inverse is
$\xi=\sqrt{(X^1)^2-(X^0)^2}$ and
$\eta=2\operatorname{arctanh}(X^0/X^1)$.
The singularity of these inverse coordinates on the horizon is not by itself
a curvature singularity: the metric in $(X^0,X^1,x)$ is flat.

On a fixed-$\xi$ orbit proper time is $dt=(\xi/2)d\eta$.
Twice differentiating its Minkowski coordinates with respect to $t$ gives
proper acceleration $a=1/\xi$. The Rindler-vacuum temperature relation used by
Eling--Fouxon--Oz is $T_{\rm loc}=a/(2\pi)$, in units
$\hbar=c=k_B=1$ \cite[(32)--(33) and following discussion]{efo2009}.
At the cutoff this yields
\begin{equation}
 T_{\rm loc}=\frac{1}{4\pi\sqrt{r_c}},\qquad
 t=\sqrt{r_c}\,\tau,\quad
 V_i(t,x)=\frac{v_i(t/\sqrt{r_c},x)}{\sqrt{r_c}},\quad
 \Pi(t,x)=\frac{P(t/\sqrt{r_c},x)}{r_c}.
 \label{eq:rh-local-temperature-map}
\end{equation}
On this orbit the exact relation is
$t=\sqrt{r_c}(\eta+\log r_c)$.
The inverse field map is
$v_i(\tau,x)=\sqrt{r_c}V_i(\sqrt{r_c}\tau,x)$ and
$P(\tau,x)=r_c\Pi(\sqrt{r_c}\tau,x)$.
Every term in \eqref{eq:rh-bkls-ns} then transforms explicitly:
\[
 \partial_\tau v_i=r_c\partial_tV_i,\quad
 v^j\partial_jv_i=r_cV^j\partial_jV_i,\quad
 \partial_iP=r_c\partial_i\Pi,\quad
 r_c\Delta v_i=r_c^{3/2}\Delta V_i.
\]
Consequently the proper-time equation is
\begin{equation}
 \partial_tV_i+V^j\partial_jV_i+\partial_i\Pi
   -\nu_{\rm loc}\Delta V_i=0,\qquad
 \nu_{\rm loc}=\sqrt{r_c}=\frac1{4\pi T_{\rm loc}}.
 \label{eq:rh-viscosity-temperature}
\end{equation}
Both the source viscosity $r_c$ and the proper-time viscosity $\sqrt{r_c}$
are therefore retained, together with the map between them.

Eling--Fouxon--Oz also derive Navier--Stokes by projecting the Einstein
equations onto a horizon. The normal--normal equation supplies
incompressibility, and the normal--tangential equation supplies momentum
balance. Their Rindler specialization has $R_{AB}=0$ and explicitly identifies
the thermal interpretation with the vacuum viewed by accelerated observers
\cite[(21)--(25), (32)--(33)]{efo2009}.
The reconstruction-to-singularity question is part of the published programme:
Bredberg--Strominger discuss its convergence requirement in
\cite[\S5]{bs2011}. To apply it at blowup, the particular fluid, boundary data,
and reconstructed geometric invariant must be followed up to that time.
The displayed formal expansion alone does not supply such control.

\subsection{Thermal time in the arithmetic energy basis}

In the rational BC representation on $\mathcal H=\ell^2(\N^\times)$, write
\begin{equation}
 H\varepsilon_n=(\log n)\varepsilon_n,\quad
 \mu_q\varepsilon_n=\varepsilon_{qn},\quad
 e(r)\varepsilon_n=e^{2\pi i nr}\varepsilon_n,
 \qquad q\in\N^\times,\ r\in\Q/\Z.
 \label{eq:rh-bc-representation}
\end{equation}
Here $H$ is the self-adjoint diagonal operator with domain
$\{(a_n):\sum_n(\log n)^2|a_n|^2<\infty\}$.
Let $\gamma_t(a)=e^{itH}ae^{-itH}$. Direct basis evaluation gives
$\gamma_t(\mu_q)=q^{it}\mu_q$ and $\gamma_t(e(r))=e(r)$.
For $\beta>1$ the trace and the corresponding Gibbs state are
\begin{equation}
 Z(\beta)=\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-\beta}=\zeta(\beta),\qquad
 D_\beta=Z(\beta)^{-1}e^{-\beta H},\qquad
 \varphi_\beta(a)=\operatorname{Tr}(D_\beta a).
 \label{eq:rh-gibbs-state}
\end{equation}
These are the low-temperature formulas of Bost--Connes
\cite[\S\S5--7]{bostConnes1995}.

\begin{proposition}[Exact modular-time calculation]
With the Connes--Rovelli modular sign convention,
$\alpha_u(a)=D_\beta^{-iu}aD_\beta^{iu}$ on $B(\mathcal H)$, one has
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_u=\gamma_{\beta u},\qquad
 \alpha_u(E_{mn})=(m/n)^{i\beta u}E_{mn},
 \quad E_{mn}=|\varepsilon_m\rangle\langle\varepsilon_n|.
 \label{eq:rh-modular-matrix-units}
\end{equation}
The standard opposite modular convention gives $\gamma_{-\beta u}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The positive eigenvalues of $D_\beta$ are $p_n=n^{-\beta}/Z(\beta)$.
Functional calculus gives
$D_\beta^{-iu}E_{mn}D_\beta^{iu}=(p_m/p_n)^{-iu}E_{mn}$,
which is exactly \eqref{eq:rh-modular-matrix-units}.
Equivalently,
$D_\beta^{-iu}=Z(\beta)^{iu}e^{i\beta uH}$, and the scalar factors cancel
in conjugation. The equality for all bounded $a$ follows directly from these
unitary conjugations, without extending the unbounded operator $H$ to all
vectors. In the Hilbert--Schmidt standard representation,
$\Delta=L_{D_\beta}R_{D_\beta}^{-1}$; hence conjugation by $\Delta^{-iu}$
on left multiplication implements the same formula.
This also identifies the sign with
\cite[\S2.1, footnote 3; \S4.1, (29), (36)--(42)]{connesRovelli1994}.
\end{proof}

On bounded observables this real flow is defined for every real $u$.
The cancellation just proved concerns the automorphism, not the thermal
expectations: the latter retain $1/Z(\beta)$. In particular, differentiating
the absolutely and locally uniformly convergent Dirichlet series gives
\begin{equation}
 \langle H\rangle_\beta=-\frac{\zeta'(\beta)}{\zeta(\beta)},\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}_\beta(H)=\frac{d^2}{d\beta^2}\log\zeta(\beta).
 \label{eq:rh-energy-cumulants}
\end{equation}
Indeed the first two derivatives insert $-\log n$ and $(\log n)^2$;
quotient differentiation subtracts the square of the first moment.
The local Laurent expansion $\zeta(1+\epsilon)=\epsilon^{-1}+\gamma_E+O(\epsilon)$
therefore yields
$\langle H\rangle_{1+\epsilon}=\epsilon^{-1}-\gamma_E+O(\epsilon)$ and
$\operatorname{Var}_{1+\epsilon}(H)=\epsilon^{-2}+O(1)$.
These are temperature-parameter divergences with an explicit variable.
Bost--Connes also construct KMS states for $0<\beta\le1$; the divergent
ordinary trace at $1$ is not their definition in that range
\cite[\S7]{bostConnes1995}.

\subsection{Explicit observables retaining the zeta denominator}

Define the real Dirichlet character
$\chi_3(n)=0,1,-1$ for $n\equiv0,1,2\pmod3$, respectively.
\begin{proposition}[A bounded arithmetic observable]
In \eqref{eq:rh-bc-representation},
\begin{equation}
 A_3=\frac{e(1/3)-e(-1/3)}{i\sqrt3},\qquad
 A_3=A_3^*,\quad \|A_3\|=1,\qquad
 \varphi_\beta(A_3)=\frac{L(\beta,\chi_3)}{\zeta(\beta)}\quad(\beta>1).
 \label{eq:rh-a3-observable}
\end{equation}
The meromorphic continuation of this expectation has a pole of order $m-k$
at a zeta zero of multiplicity $m$ when the numerator has zero order $k<m$.
When $k\ge m$ the singularity is removable.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $e(r)^*=e(-r)$, the operator is self-adjoint. Its $n$th eigenvalue is
$2\sin(2\pi n/3)/\sqrt3=\chi_3(n)$, proving the norm and all matrix entries.
The trace is the absolutely convergent sum
$\zeta(\beta)^{-1}\sum_n\chi_3(n)n^{-\beta}$.
For the pole statement write locally
$\zeta(s)=(s-\rho)^m a(s)$ and
$L(s,\chi_3)=(s-\rho)^k b(s)$ with $a(\rho)b(\rho)\ne0$.
Their ratio is $(s-\rho)^{k-m}b(s)/a(s)$.
This explicitly records possible cancellation instead of asserting that a
single observable must detect every zero.
\end{proof}

The full root-of-unity trace provides a second check. For $\Re s>1$ put
$S_j(s)=\sum_{n\equiv j\ (3)}n^{-s}$. Then
$S_0=3^{-s}\zeta(s)$,
$S_1+S_2=(1-3^{-s})\zeta(s)$ and
$S_1-S_2=L(s,\chi_3)$. Solving this two-by-two linear system gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\operatorname{Tr}(e(1/3)e^{-sH})}{\zeta(s)}
 =\frac{3^{1-s}-1}{2}
   +\frac{i\sqrt3}{2}\frac{L(s,\chi_3)}{\zeta(s)}.
 \label{eq:rh-e3-trace}
\end{equation}
In contrast, even--odd decomposition gives
$\operatorname{Tr}(e(1/2)e^{-sH})/\zeta(s)=2^{1-s}-1$.
Here the zeta factor cancels exactly. These two computations identify what
information the respective observable retains.

\begin{proposition}[Finite-prime projections and the reciprocal zeta trace]
For a finite set of primes $S$, define
$P_S=\prod_{p\in S}(1-\mu_p\mu_p^*)$ in the represented BC algebra.
As $S$ increases through all primes, $P_S$ converges strongly to the
rank-one projection $P_1$ onto $\C\varepsilon_1$, but not in operator norm.
For real $\beta>1$,
\begin{equation}
 \varphi_\beta(P_S)=\prod_{p\in S}(1-p^{-\beta}),\qquad
 \varphi_\beta(P_1)=\frac1{\zeta(\beta)}.
 \label{eq:rh-prime-projectors}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The range projection $\mu_p\mu_p^*$ has eigenvalue $1$ precisely when $p\mid n$;
these projections commute. Thus $P_S\varepsilon_n$ is $\varepsilon_n$ when
no prime in $S$ divides $n$, and is zero otherwise. Each $n>1$ eventually
vanishes, while $n=1$ survives. For $a\in\ell^2$, dominated convergence in
$\sum_n|a_n|^2$ proves strong convergence. A prime $q\notin S$ satisfies
$(P_S-P_1)\varepsilon_q=\varepsilon_q$, so $\|P_S-P_1\|=1$.
Inclusion--exclusion, or finite removal of Euler factors from the absolutely
convergent Dirichlet series, gives
\[
 \sum_{(n,\prod_{p\in S}p)=1}n^{-\beta}
 =\zeta(\beta)\prod_{p\in S}(1-p^{-\beta}).
\]
Finally $\operatorname{Tr}(P_1e^{-\beta H})=1$. The normal Gibbs state extends
to $B(\mathcal H)$, so its expectation at this strong limit is defined.
This argument asserts membership of $P_1$ in the represented von Neumann
closure; it does not assert a norm-convergent infinite product in the BC
$C^*$-algebra.
\end{proof}

Meromorphic continuation of the last scalar function has a pole at every
zeta zero, with that zero's multiplicity. Finite products are entire in $s$;
their limit above is justified on $\Re s>1$, not automatically near those
poles. This is an explicit limiting question to investigate.
Likewise the energy characteristic function has the exact trace
$\operatorname{Tr}(D_\beta e^{-itH})=\zeta(\beta+it)/\zeta(\beta)$ for
real $t$, $\beta>1$. Both coordinates of its complex zeta argument are specified.

\subsection{Cooling and the spectral-support formulation}

The zeros are also present in a published spectral construction, not just in
continued scalar expectations \cite[\S4.2--4.4]{ccmEndomotives}.
Let $\mathcal A$ be the BC groupoid algebra of functions with finite support
in $k$ and finite level in $u\in\widehat\Z$ (factoring through some
$\Z/N\Z$). Its groupoid consists of
$(k,u)\in\Q_+^*\times\widehat\Z$ with $ku\in\widehat\Z$;
composition is $(k_1,k_2u)(k_2,u)=(k_1k_2,u)$.
For $x=\int x(t)U_tdt$ in the analytic crossed-product algebra
$\widehat{\mathcal A}_\beta$, the source's coordinate map is
\begin{equation}
 f(k,u,\lambda)=\int_\R x(t)(k,u)\lambda^{it}dt,
 \qquad
 \theta_a(x)=\int_\R a^{it}x(t)U_tdt\quad(a>0).
 \label{eq:rh-crossed-fourier}
\end{equation}
The analytic strip is $I_\beta=\{z:0\le\Im z\le\beta\}$, with $\beta>1$.
In the source definition, the finitely many coefficient functions extend
holomorphically to that strip and have Schwartz boundary behavior.

Cooling evaluates representations, followed by trace:
\begin{equation}
 \pi:\widehat{\mathcal A}_\beta\longrightarrow
 C(\widetilde\Omega_\beta,\mathcal L^1),\quad
 \pi(x)(\varepsilon,H)=\pi_{\varepsilon,H}(x),\qquad
 \delta=\operatorname{Trace}\circ\pi.
 \label{eq:rh-cooling-map}
\end{equation}
Here $\widetilde\Omega_\beta\simeq\widehat\Z^*\times\R_+^*$ and
$\mathcal L^1$ denotes trace-class operators. In degree zero,
\begin{equation}
 \delta(x)(u,\lambda)=\sum_{n\ge1}f(1,nu,n\lambda).
 \label{eq:rh-cooling-coordinate-sum}
\end{equation}
This formula can be checked directly: in the representation at $(u,\lambda)$,
the Hamiltonian has $n$th diagonal entry $\log n+\log\lambda$, and the
$n$th diagonal entry of $x(t)$ is $x(t)(1,nu)$. Taking the trace of the
integrated representation gives exactly \eqref{eq:rh-cooling-coordinate-sum}.

The construction restricts to the trace-kernel cyclic submodule
$\widehat{\mathcal A}_{\beta,0}^{\natural}$ before taking
\[
 \delta:\widehat{\mathcal A}_{\beta,0}^{\natural}
 \longrightarrow\mathcal S^{\natural}(\widetilde\Omega_\beta),
 \qquad D(\mathcal A,\varphi)=\operatorname{coker}\delta.
\]
The cokernel is in cyclic modules. This specifies the category and the
discarded trace component; it is not an unrestricted algebra quotient.
For the trivial character projector $p_{\mathbf1}$, put
$M=HC_0(p_{\mathbf1}D(\mathcal A,\varphi))$. Here
$\operatorname{Hol}(I_\beta)$ denotes the source's holomorphic multiplier
algebra of $\mathcal S(I_\beta)$: multiplication by $h$ must preserve that
Schwartz strip space. It does not mean all holomorphic functions with
unrestricted growth. The one-dimensional module $\C_z$ uses evaluation at
$z$. The source's spectral theorem
\cite[Theorem 4.16, (4.36)]{ccmEndomotives} gives, in the open critical strip,
\begin{equation}
 M\otimes_{\operatorname{Hol}(I_\beta)}\C_z\ne0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \zeta(-iz)=0.
 \label{eq:rh-ccm-spectral-support}
\end{equation}
For $\rho=\sigma+i\gamma$ the coordinate and inverse are
\begin{equation}
 z=i\rho=-\gamma+i\sigma,\qquad \rho=-iz.
 \label{eq:rh-ccm-zero-coordinate}
\end{equation}
Thus a nonzero fibre with $0<\Im z<1$ and $\Im z\ne1/2$ is exactly an
off-critical zeta zero in these coordinates. This is a genuine target for
the investigation. No such fibre has been exhibited here.

The step to investigate on the fluid side is the image of its geometric or
quantum observables under a concrete correspondence into this arithmetic
construction. The metric map \eqref{eq:rh-bkls-metric}, modular map
\eqref{eq:rh-modular-matrix-units}, and cooling map
\eqref{eq:rh-cooling-map} have now been specified. They do not yet constitute
a constructed map from a singular fluid to a nonzero off-critical fibre.
This reports the current extent of the calculation, not a theorem excluding
such a map.

\subsection{A direct arithmetic test independent of the fluid route}

Use $\widehat f(z)=\int_\R f(u)e^{-iuz}du$ and
$f^\star(u)=\overline{f(-u)}$. For $f,g\in C_c^2(\R)$ define
\begin{equation}
 W(f,g)=\sum_{\rho}m_\rho
       \overline{\widehat f(\overline{\gamma_\rho})}
       \widehat g(\gamma_\rho),\qquad
 \gamma_\rho=\frac{\rho-1/2}{i}.
 \label{eq:rh-weil-convention}
\end{equation}
The sum is over distinct nontrivial zeros and $m_\rho$ is their multiplicity.
This is conjugate-linear in its first slot, reversing the slots of
\cite[\S1.2]{alpogeFurman2026}; diagonal values agree.
Twice integrating by parts gives $\widehat f(z)=O((1+|\Re z|)^{-2})$
uniformly for $|\Im z|\le1/2$. The zero count is $O(T\log T)$, so the
displayed sum converges absolutely. Functional-equation pairing
$\gamma\leftrightarrow\overline\gamma$ shows it is Hermitian.

\begin{proposition}[Finite negative-certificate test]
Let $G_{ij}=W(f_i,f_j)$ for a finite family and $a\in\C^d$. A rigorously
established value $a^*Ga<0$ refutes RH. If $\widetilde G$ approximates $G$
with $|G_{ij}-\widetilde G_{ij}|\le e_{ij}$, it suffices to establish
\begin{equation}
 \Re(a^*\widetilde G a)+\sum_{i,j}e_{ij}|a_i||a_j|<0.
 \label{eq:rh-matrix-certificate}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Sesquilinearity gives $a^*Ga=W(\sum a_if_i,\sum a_if_i)$.
Under RH each $\gamma_\rho$ is real and this last expression is
$\sum m_\rho|\widehat{\sum a_if_i}(\gamma_\rho)|^2\ge0$.
The contrapositive proves the first statement. The entrywise triangle
inequality bounds the error by the displayed sum, proving the second.
All errors, including the analytic integration and omitted tails, must be
included in $e_{ij}$. No positivity assumption is used to compute $G$.
\end{proof}

For explicit evaluation put $h_{ij}=f_j*f_i^\star$.
Then $\widehat h_{ij}(z)=\widehat f_j(z)
\overline{\widehat f_i(\overline z)}$. The complete explicit formula is
\begin{align}
 W(f_i,f_j)={}&\widehat h_{ij}(i/2)+\widehat h_{ij}(-i/2)
 +\int_\R\widehat h_{ij}(t)
 \left[\frac{1}{2\pi}\Re\psi\left(\frac14+\frac{it}{2}\right)
       -\frac{\log\pi}{2\pi}\right]dt \notag\\
 &-\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}
       \{h_{ij}(\log n)+h_{ij}(-\log n)\}.
 \label{eq:rh-full-weil-test}
\end{align}
The finite-prime terms, gamma factor, and both pole terms are all retained
\cite{weil1952,alpogeFurman2026}. Compact support makes the arithmetic sum
finite, but does not remove the other terms.

\subsection{A completely specified compact-support calibration}

Let $b_4=\mathbf1_{[-1/2,1/2]}^{*4}$ and
$B=b_4*b_4=\mathbf1_{[-1/2,1/2]}^{*8}$. Thus $b_4\in C_c^2(\R)$,
$B\in C_c^6(\R)$, and their supports are $[-2,2]$ and $[-4,4]$.
The repeated convolution, or successive integration of the indicator,
gives the exact truncated-power formula
\begin{equation}
 B(x)=\frac1{7!}\sum_{k=0}^8(-1)^k\binom8k(x+4-k)_+^7,
 \qquad B(0)=\frac{151}{315}.
 \label{eq:rh-b8-polynomial}
\end{equation}
For completeness, the convolution of $m$ indicators of $[0,1]$ is the
first derivative of the volume
$m!^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^m(-1)^k\binom mk(x-k)_+^m$ of the clipped simplex
$\{u_i\in[0,1],\sum u_i\le x\}$; the volume formula follows by
inclusion--exclusion on the inequalities $u_i>1$.
Differentiating once (to obtain the density), and translating by $m/2$,
proves \eqref{eq:rh-b8-polynomial} for $m=8$.

Set $f_T(u)=e^{-iTu}b_4(u)$ for real $T$. Its autocorrelation is
$h_T(x)=e^{-iTx}B(x)$, and
$\widehat h_T(z)=\operatorname{sinc}((z+T)/2)^8$, where
$\operatorname{sinc}w=\sin(w)/w$ with value $1$ at zero.
Define $M(w)=(\sinh(w/2)/(w/2))^8$, also filled in continuously at zero.
An equivalent real-space form of \eqref{eq:rh-full-weil-test} is
\begin{align}
 Q(T):=W(f_T,f_T)={}&2\Re M(1/2+iT)
       -(\gamma_E+\log(4\pi))B(0)\notag\\
 &-\int_0^4\frac{e^{x/2}B(x)\cos(Tx)-B(0)}{\sinh x}\,dx
       +B(0)\log\coth2\notag\\
 &-2\sum_{\substack{n=p^k\le e^4\\p\ \text{prime},\ k\ge1}}
       \frac{\log p}{\sqrt n}B(\log n)\cos(T\log n).
 \label{eq:rh-b8-weil-integral}
\end{align}
To verify the constants, substitute
$F(y)=y^{-1/2}h_T(\log y)$ in
\eqref{eq:local-explicit}--\eqref{eq:arch-place} and then put $y=e^x$.
The archimedean integrand becomes
$(e^{x/2}B(x)\cos(Tx)-B(0))/\sinh x$.
For $x>4$, $B(x)=0$; its exact contribution after the minus sign is
$B(0)\int_4^\infty dx/\sinh x=B(0)\log\coth2$.
The two Mellin-pole terms give $2\Re M(1/2+iT)$.
This proves \eqref{eq:rh-b8-weil-integral}, including its nonzero tail.

The executable implementation uses the exact polynomial on each interval
$[j,j+1]$, $j=0,1,2,3$. To remove the apparent singularity at $0$ analytically,
write $a_\pm=1/2\pm iT$ and $\operatorname{sinhc}(z)=\sinh(z)/z$.
Then
\[
 \frac{e^{x/2}\cos(Tx)-1}{x}
 =\frac12\sum_{a\in\{a_+,a_-\}}
     a e^{ax/2}\operatorname{sinhc}(ax/2).
\]
Thus the numerator divided by $x$ is evaluated using the polynomial
$(B(x)-B(0))/x$ plus this entire function; division is by
$\operatorname{sinhc}(x)$, nonzero on the integration interval.
This is an identity of analytic functions near $[0,1]$, not the deletion
of a small interval near zero.

\[
 C_d(x)=\frac{B(x-d)+B(x+d)}2,\qquad d=|i-j|/4.
\]
Replacing a correlation by its even part leaves the explicit formula
unchanged: every term pairs its arguments with their negatives.
Hence $G_{ij}=W(g_i,g_j)$ is real symmetric Toeplitz. For each $d$ the
formula is \eqref{eq:rh-b8-weil-integral} with $B\cos(Tx)$ replaced by
$C_d$, endpoint $4+d$, value at zero $C_d(0)$, pole term
$2M(1/2)\cosh(d/2)$, and tail
$C_d(0)\log\coth((4+d)/2)$. The prime sum extends to $e^{4+d}$;
the largest endpoint here is $5$.

Interval $LDL^{\mathsf T}$ elimination gives five strictly positive pivots,
approximately
\[
 (2.50408478,\ 1.50545884,\ 1.48555915,\ 1.36380903,\ 0.02280375)
 \times10^{-8}.
\]
The last pivot is enclosed by
$[2.2803752561005,2.2803752561006]\times10^{-10}$.
The exact recurrences used are
\[
 D_j=G_{jj}-\sum_{k<j}L_{jk}^2D_k,\qquad
 L_{ij}=\frac{G_{ij}-\sum_{k<j}L_{ik}L_{jk}D_k}{D_j},\quad L_{jj}=1.
\]
All inputs are enclosing balls, each divisor has a positive lower bound,
and interval arithmetic encloses each rational-operation recurrence.
Thus the actual pivots are positive and the exact five-by-five matrix is
positive definite. This rules out negative witnesses in this particular
five-dimensional space, not in the complete test space. The numerical trust
boundary is FLINT/Arb's enclosure arithmetic and integration implementation;
this execution is not a Lean verification of that implementation.

\subsection{Finite periodic observables with controlled numerator cancellation}

The earlier $A_3$ is a character observable. A different finite-level
observable gives a numerator that can be kept uniformly away from zero.
For an integer $q\ge2$ define
\begin{equation}
 B_q=\frac1q\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}(e^{-2\pi i j/q}-1)e(j/q),\qquad
 b_q(n)=\mathbf1_{n\equiv1\pmod q}-\mathbf1_{n\equiv0\pmod q}.
 \label{eq:rh-periodic-observable}
\end{equation}
The finite geometric-series identity shows that $B_q\varepsilon_n
=b_q(n)\varepsilon_n$. Thus $B_q=B_q^*$ and $\|B_q\|=1$.
It is an actual finite Fourier sum in the BC algebra, not a
postulated unbounded observable.

\begin{proposition}[A quantitative finite-level pole detector]
For $\Re s=\sigma>0$ the grouped series
\begin{equation}
 D_q(s)=1+\sum_{k\ge1}\bigl((qk+1)^{-s}-(qk)^{-s}\bigr)
 \label{eq:rh-periodic-numerator}
\end{equation}
converges absolutely and locally uniformly, and
\begin{align}
 |D_q(s)-1|&\le |s|q^{-1-\sigma}\zeta(1+\sigma),\label{eq:rh-periodic-bound}\\
 \left|D_q(s)-1-\sum_{k=1}^N
 ((qk+1)^{-s}-(qk)^{-s})\right|
 &\le\frac{|s|q^{-1-\sigma}}{\sigma N^\sigma}.
 \label{eq:rh-periodic-tail}
\end{align}
The Gibbs expectation of $B_q$ has meromorphic continuation
$F_q(s)=D_q(s)/\zeta(s)$. On the explicitly specified region
\[
 \mathcal R=\{s:\Re s\ge1/4,\ |s|\le101\}
\]
one has
\begin{equation}
 |D_{256}(s)-1|\le505/1024,\qquad
 |D_{256}(s)|\ge519/1024.
 \label{eq:rh-periodic-region}
\end{equation}
Consequently $F_{256}$ has a pole at every zeta zero in $\mathcal R$,
with exactly the zero's multiplicity and with no numerator cancellation.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For real $x>0$ powers use the real logarithm. The fundamental theorem
of calculus gives
\[
 (qk+1)^{-s}-(qk)^{-s}
 =-s\int_{qk}^{qk+1}x^{-s-1}\,dx .
\]
Its absolute value is at most $|s|(qk)^{-1-\sigma}$.
Summing proves \eqref{eq:rh-periodic-bound} and local uniform convergence.
The integral comparison
$\sum_{k>N}k^{-1-\sigma}\le N^{-\sigma}/\sigma$
proves \eqref{eq:rh-periodic-tail}.
For real $\beta>1$ the absolutely convergent trace may be grouped by
residue class, proving $\varphi_\beta(B_q)=D_q(\beta)/\zeta(\beta)$.
The grouped holomorphic numerator supplies the stated continuation.

For $\sigma\ge1/4$, $\zeta(1+\sigma)\le1+1/\sigma\le5$ and
$256^{1+\sigma}\ge256^{5/4}=1024$. This proves both bounds in
\eqref{eq:rh-periodic-region}. If
$\zeta(s)=(s-\rho)^m a(s)$ with $a(\rho)\ne0$, then
$F_{256}(s)=(s-\rho)^{-m}D_{256}(s)/a(s)$, proving the pole order.
\end{proof}

The comparison with special-function software uses the exact identity
\[
 D_q(s)=q^{-s}\{\zeta(s,1/q)-\zeta(s)\}.
\]
It follows by residue-class grouping for $\Re s>1$ and then continuation;
the equal simple-pole residues at $s=1$ cancel. Here $\zeta(s,a)$
is Hurwitz's zeta function, with the convention of
\cite[\S25.11(i), (25.11.1)--(25.11.3)]{apostolDLMF25}.
In particular $D_2(s)=(1-2^{1-s})\zeta(s)$ and
$F_2(s)=1-2^{1-s}$: numerator cancellation really does occur for a
poorly chosen modulus. The bound above supplies a choice for which
it cannot occur in the stated region.

As $q\to\infty$, $D_q\to1$ locally uniformly on $\Re s>0$.
Thus the continued $F_q$ converge to $1/\zeta$ away from its poles.
These are continuations of real-temperature expectations, not assertions
that complex temperature defines a positive state. The source of the
operator is the BC representation \eqref{eq:rh-bc-representation};
the quantitative estimates here are direct deductions with their proofs,
not claims of a new literature priority.

\subsection{A centered prime projector through the actual cooling map}

For a prime $p$ use just one of the projections already constructed:
\begin{equation}
 J_p=\frac{p}{p-1}(1-\mu_p\mu_p^*)-1,\qquad
 j_p(n)=
 \begin{cases}1/(p-1),&p\nmid n,\\-1,&p\mid n.\end{cases}
 \label{eq:rh-centered-prime}
\end{equation}
The same formula defines a locally constant function $j_p(u)$ on
$\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ of additive Haar mean zero.
For $a>0$ put
\begin{equation}
 g_a(\lambda)=\frac{e^{-(\log\lambda)^2/(4a)}}{\sqrt{4\pi a}},
 \quad
 x_a(t)(k,u)=\mathbf1_{k=1}j_p(u)\frac{e^{-at^2}}{2\pi}.
 \label{eq:rh-gaussian-cooling-input}
\end{equation}
The coefficient functions $e^{-at^2}$ are Schwartz on each closed
finite horizontal strip: every derivative is a polynomial times the
Gaussian, and $|e^{-a(v+iw)^2}|=e^{-av^2+aw^2}$.
Thus this is an element of the source's
$\widehat{\mathcal A}_\beta$ for each fixed $\beta>1$.
Its groupoid function is
$f_a(k,u,\lambda)=\mathbf1_{k=1}j_p(u)g_a(\lambda)$ by Gaussian
Fourier inversion. Its dual trace is zero since $\int j_p(u)\,du=0$.
These statements use the explicit source maps, not only an analogy with
temperature \cite[\S4.4, BC groupoid representation and dual trace]{ccmEndomotives}.

Since a unit $u\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}^*$ does not change divisibility
by $p$, \eqref{eq:rh-cooling-coordinate-sum} gives
\begin{equation}
 C_{p,a}(\lambda)=\delta(x_a)(u,\lambda)
       =\sum_{n\ge1}j_p(n)g_a(n\lambda).
 \label{eq:rh-prime-cooled-function}
\end{equation}
In particular this cooled function is already in the trivial unit-character
sector. It is independent of $u$.

\begin{proposition}[The Mellin transform of the cooled prime observable]
The Mellin integral of \eqref{eq:rh-prime-cooled-function} is entire and
\begin{equation}
 \int_0^\infty C_{p,a}(\lambda)\lambda^s\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}
   =e^{as^2}\frac{1-p^{1-s}}{p-1}\zeta(s).
 \label{eq:rh-cooled-zeta-detector}
\end{equation}
Inside $0<\Re s<1$, its zeros are exactly those of $\zeta(s)$,
with the same multiplicities.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Substituting $v=\log\lambda$ in the Gaussian integral yields
$\int g_a(\lambda)\lambda^s d^*\lambda=e^{as^2}$.
For $\Re s>1$ absolute Fubini is valid since $|j_p(n)|\le1$ and
$\sum n^{-\sigma}<\infty$. Therefore the left side equals
$e^{as^2}\sum_n j_p(n)n^{-s}$. Splitting off multiples of $p$ gives
\[
 \sum_n j_p(n)n^{-s}
 =\left(\frac{1-p^{-s}}{p-1}-p^{-s}\right)\zeta(s),
 \]
which is the right side of \eqref{eq:rh-cooled-zeta-detector}.

To justify the Mellin integral everywhere, extend $g_a$ by zero on
$(-\infty,0]$. This is a Schwartz function on the real line: each
derivative on $(0,\infty)$ is a finite sum of
$\lambda^{-r}$ times a polynomial in $\log\lambda$ times $g_a$,
which tends to zero faster than any prescribed power at both endpoints.
The sequence $j_p(n)$ is periodic of mean zero; express it by its
finite nonconstant Fourier modes. Poisson summation of each mode
gives only frequencies $(k+j/p)/\lambda$, $j=1,\ldots,p-1$.
Repeated integration by parts in the Fourier transform of $g_a$
then bounds each sum by $O(\lambda^{r-1})$ for every integer $r\ge2$.
The sums over $k$ converge absolutely, since
$\sum_k|k+j/p|^{-r}<\infty$. At infinity, for any $B>1$,
\[
 |C_{p,a}(\lambda)|\le
 \sup_{x>0}|x^B g_a(x)|\,\zeta(B)\lambda^{-B}.
 \]
Both estimates are locally uniform in the Mellin parameter on compact
vertical strips and remain so after insertion of powers of $\log\lambda$.
They prove that the integral is entire. Analytic continuation now proves
\eqref{eq:rh-cooled-zeta-detector} for all $s$; the apparent pole at
$s=1$ is removable.
Finally $e^{as^2}$ never vanishes, while
$p^{1-s}=1$ implies $\Re s=1$, proving the zero and multiplicity claim.
\end{proof}

There is an exact distinction of maps, with no information discarded
silently: $\varphi_\beta(J_p)=(1-p^{1-\beta})/(p-1)$ has its zeta
factor cancelled by Gibbs division, whereas cooling followed by Mellin
transformation retains that factor in \eqref{eq:rh-cooled-zeta-detector}.
Also $C_{p,a}$ is an \emph{image} under $\delta$, so its class in
$\operatorname{coker}\delta$ is zero. The scalar zero detector just
constructed is not itself a nonzero spectral fibre.

For numerical work the case $p=2$ has explicit error bounds.
Here $J_2=-e(1/2)$ and $C_{2,a}(\lambda)=
\sum_{n\ge1}(-1)^{n+1}g_a(n\lambda)$.
Use the ordinary Fourier transform
$\mathcal Fg(\xi)=\int_\mathbb Rg(x)e^{-2\pi i\xi x}dx$.
Poisson summation gives the exact alternative
\[
 C_{2,a}(\lambda)=-\lambda^{-1}
   \sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\mathcal Fg_a((k+1/2)/\lambda).
 \]
Consequently
\[
 |C_{2,a}(\lambda)|\le
 \frac{2(2^r-1)\zeta(r)}{(2\pi)^r}
 \|g_a^{(r)}\|_1\lambda^{r-1}\quad(r\ge2).
 \]
For $r=2$ one may use
$B_2(a)=e^a(1+a^{-1}+\sqrt{1+(2a)^{-1}})$ as an upper bound for
$\|g_a''\|_1$. Indeed, with $v=\log x$,
\[
 g_a''(x)=x^{-2}g_a(x)
  \left(\frac{v^2}{4a^2}+\frac{v}{2a}-\frac1{2a}\right).
 \]
Changing variables and completing the square gives a Gaussian of mean
$-2a$, variance $2a$, and total mass $e^a$. The triangle inequality
and Cauchy--Schwarz bound its three displayed terms by
$1+(2a)^{-1}$, $\sqrt{1+(2a)^{-1}}$, and $(2a)^{-1}$, respectively.

For $\sigma=\Re s>0$, $b>\max(1,\sigma)$, $0<\epsilon<R$, and
$N\epsilon\ge1$, the lower tail, upper tail, and omitted terms in the
middle integral are respectively bounded by
\begin{align}
 E_{\rm low}&=\frac{B_2(a)\epsilon^{\sigma+1}}{4(\sigma+1)},&
 E_{\rm high}&=\zeta(b)e^{ab^2}R^{\sigma-b},\notag\\
 E_{\rm middle}&=\frac{e^a}{2\epsilon}
 \operatorname{erfc}\left(\frac{\log(N\epsilon)-2a}{2\sqrt a}\right)
 \frac{R^\sigma-\epsilon^\sigma}{\sigma}.
 \label{eq:rh-gaussian-errors}
\end{align}
The first is obtained by integrating $B_2(a)\lambda/4$.
For the second, bound $\lambda^{\sigma-b}\le R^{\sigma-b}$ and apply
absolute Fubini to the nonnegative majorant.
For the third, $g_a$ decreases on $[1,\infty)$, so
$\sum_{n>N}g_a(n\lambda)\le\lambda^{-1}\int_{N\lambda}^\infty g_a(x)dx$.
Completing the square evaluates this last integral as
\[
 \frac{e^a}{2}
 \operatorname{erfc}\left(\frac{\log(N\lambda)-2a}{2\sqrt a}\right);
\]
bound it uniformly with $\lambda\ge\epsilon$ and integrate
$\lambda^{\sigma-1}$. Thus a finite quadrature with these three errors
can evaluate the detector without a zero table.

\subsection{Heat and Burgers equations from a fixed BC state}
\label{sec:rh-bc-heat}

There is an explicit fluid equation associated with the represented
BC Hamiltonian. Fix $\beta>1$ and keep the Gibbs state $\varphi_\beta$
fixed. On $\ell^2(\mathbb N)$ put
\begin{equation}
 F_\beta(\tau,x)=
 \varphi_\beta\!\left(e^{-\tau H^2}e^{ixH}\right)
 =\frac1{\zeta(\beta)}
   \sum_{n\ge1}n^{-\beta}e^{-\tau(\log n)^2+ix\log n}.
 \label{eq:rh-bc-heat}
\end{equation}
For real $\tau\ge0,x$, both displayed factors are bounded functional
calculus observables in $B(\ell^2(\mathbb N))$. This specifies the algebra:
no membership assertion in the original BC $C^*$-algebra is required.
For $\operatorname{Re}\tau>0$ the series and every derivative converge
locally uniformly for complex $x$, since a negative quadratic in $\log n$
dominates every linear term and every power of $\log n$. At $\tau=0$
the same conclusion holds for $x$ in compact subsets of
$\beta+\operatorname{Im}x>1$, including one-sided $\tau$ derivatives.
Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \partial_\tau F_\beta=\partial_x^2F_\beta,\qquad
 F_\beta(0,x)=\frac{\zeta(\beta-ix)}{\zeta(\beta)}.
 \label{eq:rh-bc-heat-initial}
\end{equation}
The sign follows from differentiating each summand:
both derivatives introduce $-(\log n)^2$.

Here is the complete logarithmic change of variables
(Cole--Hopf \cite{hopf1950,cole1951}). Fix $\nu\ne0$. For any nonvanishing heat solution
$F_\tau=\nu F_{xx}$ on a simply connected coordinate patch, set
$L=\log F$ and $u=-2\nu L_x$. Then
$L_\tau=\nu(L_{xx}+L_x^2)$, and differentiating this equality gives
\[
 u_\tau=-2\nu^2L_{xxx}-4\nu^2L_xL_{xx},\quad
 uu_x=4\nu^2L_xL_{xx},\quad
 \nu u_{xx}=-2\nu^2L_{xxx}.
\]
Thus $u_\tau+uu_x=\nu u_{xx}$; in
\eqref{eq:rh-bc-heat} the viscosity is $\nu=1$.
Conversely integrate $L_x=-u/(2\nu)$. Burgers' equation says that
$L_\tau-\nu(L_{xx}+L_x^2)$ is independent of $x$; subtracting its
time integral makes $e^L$ a heat solution. The inverse has a constant
nonzero multiplicative freedom. The forward map loses that amplitude,
but not the zeros: if $F=(x-x_0)^m a(x)$ with $a(x_0)\ne0$, then
$u=-2\nu m/(x-x_0)-2\nu a'/a$.

This is a concrete fixed-state observable-to-complex-fluid map.
The parameter $\tau$ changes the observable, not the Gibbs temperature
or the modular automorphism. The relation to the completed zeta function
will now be given explicitly.

\subsection{The Hermite cooling seed and the completed zeta function}
\label{sec:rh-hermite-cooling}

Use the Mellin measure $d\lambda/\lambda$ and the real Fourier convention
$\widehat f(y)=\int_{\mathbb R}f(x)e^{-2\pi ixy}dx$. Following the
arithmetic summation construction in \cite[\S6.2]{connes2026}, define
\begin{equation}
 h(x)=\frac{\pi}{2}x^2(2\pi x^2-3)e^{-\pi x^2},\quad
 S(\lambda)=\sum_{n\ge1}h(n\lambda),\quad
 k(v)=e^{v/2}S(e^v).
 \label{eq:rh-hermite-seed}
\end{equation}
These definitions retain all constants. In particular, $\xi(s)$ below
means $\frac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$.

The seed $h$ is Fourier self-dual. Indeed the Gaussian is self-dual,
and differentiating its transform gives
\[
 \widehat{x^2e^{-\pi x^2}}
   =\left(\frac1{2\pi}-y^2\right)e^{-\pi y^2},\qquad
 \widehat{x^4e^{-\pi x^2}}
   =\left(y^4-\frac{3y^2}{\pi}+\frac3{4\pi^2}\right)e^{-\pi y^2}.
\]
Insert the coefficients $\pi^2$ and $-3\pi/2$ to get $\widehat h=h$.
In particular $h(0)=\int_{\mathbb R}h(x)dx=0$.
Poisson summation applied to this Schwartz function therefore gives
$S(\lambda)=\lambda^{-1}S(\lambda^{-1})$.
It follows that $k(v)=k(-v)$. Gaussian decay of the series for
$\lambda\ge1$, followed by this equality, gives double-exponential
decay of $k$ at both ends, together with all its derivatives.
Also $k(v)>0$: for $v\ge0$ every summand has $n e^v\ge1$ and
$2\pi n^2e^{2v}-3>0$, and for negative $v$ use evenness.

The precise BC input is the groupoid function
$f(q,u,\lambda)=\boldsymbol1_{q=1}h(\lambda)$, constant in $u$.
The cooling formula of \cite[\S4.4]{ccmEndomotives}
is exactly $f\mapsto S$. Moreover its dual trace is
$\int_0^\infty h(\lambda)d\lambda=0$.
In logarithmic coordinates $h(e^v)=O(e^{2v})$ at $-\infty$ and is
super-exponentially decreasing at $+\infty$. Its Fourier transform
and all weighted derivatives are Schwartz on each closed strip
$0\le\operatorname{Im}w\le\beta$, so this is an allowed smooth
crossed-product input for every fixed $\beta>1$ in that convention.

The elementary integral
$\int_0^\infty x^{s+j-1}e^{-\pi x^2}dx
 =\frac12\pi^{-(s+j)/2}\Gamma((s+j)/2)$ gives, for $\operatorname{Re}s>-2$,
\begin{equation}
 {\cal M}h(s)=\frac{s(s-1)}8\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2).
 \label{eq:rh-hermite-mellin}
\end{equation}
The apparent singularities at $s=0$ are removable.
For $\operatorname{Re}s>1$ absolute Fubini thus gives
\begin{equation}
 \int_0^\infty S(\lambda)\lambda^s\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}
 =\zeta(s){\cal M}h(s)=\frac{\xi(s)}4.
 \label{eq:rh-hermite-completion}
\end{equation}
Poisson summation above makes the left side entire in $s$, so this
identity continues to all $s$. This proves the completion, including
the factor $1/4$.
With the printed seed of \cite[\S6.2, (15)]{connes2026}, its preceding
Fourier-transform display therefore needs a factor $4$ on the integral.
Equivalently one can use $4h$ as seed. This scalar correction does not
move zeros.

There is also a direct fixed-Gibbs-observable version of the same map.
For $\lambda>0$ define
$A_{\beta,\lambda}=e^{\beta H}h(\lambda e^H)$ by its diagonal
eigenvalues $n^\beta h(n\lambda)$. Their Gaussian decay makes this a
bounded trace-class operator in the represented von Neumann algebra.
Thus, pointwise in $\lambda$,
\begin{equation}
 S(\lambda)=\operatorname{Tr}h(\lambda e^H)
 =\zeta(\beta)\varphi_\beta(A_{\beta,\lambda}).
 \label{eq:rh-gibbs-hermite}
\end{equation}
The trace-summation step is exactly that of
\cite[\S4.3]{ccmEndomotives}.
This gives an explicit observable through which the fixed BC state
feeds the completed heat kernel. The $\beta$ counterweight cancels
the Gibbs weight. The observables commute with $H$ and are
modular-fixed; their parameter $\lambda$ is not modular time.
Take the expectation before the Mellin integral: the absolute
termwise exchange proved above applies only on $\operatorname{Re}s>1$.

\subsection{Newman evolution, its exact conjugations, and cooling}
\label{sec:rh-newman-maps}

Retain the conventions of Rodgers--Tao \cite[\S1]{rodgersTao2020}:
\begin{align}
 \Phi(u)&=\sum_{n\ge1}
 (2\pi^2n^4e^{9u}-3\pi n^2e^{5u})e^{-\pi n^2e^{4u}},\notag\\
 H_t(z)&=\int_0^\infty e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)\cos(zu)\,du,\qquad
 H_0(z)=\frac18\xi(1/2+iz/2).
 \label{eq:rh-newman-definition}
\end{align}
Substituting $x=n e^{2u}$ in \eqref{eq:rh-hermite-seed} proves
$k(2u)=\Phi(u)/2$. Since $k$ is even, the exact composite map is
\begin{equation}
 h\ \longmapsto\ S(\lambda)\ \longmapsto\
 k(v)=e^{v/2}S(e^v)\ \longmapsto\
 H_t(z)=\frac12\int_{\mathbb R}
 e^{tv^2/4}k(v)e^{izv/2}dv .
 \label{eq:rh-cooling-newman-map}
\end{equation}
The inverse coordinate substitutions are $\lambda=e^v$, $v=2u$,
$s=1/2+iz/2$ and $z=-2i(s-1/2)$. The scalar changes are displayed,
not absorbed into an unnamed normalization. Double-exponential decay
justifies all complex $(t,z)$ derivatives under the integral; hence
\[
 \partial_tH_t=-\partial_z^2H_t.
\]
Putting $\tau=-t$ makes $F(\tau,z)=H_{-\tau}(z)$ a forward heat
solution, with $u=-2F_z/F$ solving the viscosity-one Burgers equation.
Equivalently $v=2H_z/H$ obeys $v_t+v v_z=-v_{zz}$ in Newman time.

The completed arithmetic coordinate has a different, explicit
viscosity. Put
\begin{equation}
 X_t(s)=8H_t(-2i(s-1/2)).
 \quad X_0=\xi,\qquad \partial_tX_t=\tfrac14\partial_s^2X_t.
 \label{eq:rh-completed-heat}
\end{equation}
Thus $w=-\frac12X_s/X$ satisfies
$w_t+w w_s=\frac14 w_{ss}$. The chain rule
$\partial_z=(i/2)\partial_s$ proves the factor $1/4$.
On $0<\operatorname{Re}s<1$ the factor
$Q(s)=\frac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)$ is nonzero. Therefore
$Z_t=X_t/Q$, with inverse $X_t=QZ_t$, obeys the exact conjugated equation
\begin{equation}
 \partial_tZ_t=\frac14\left[
 \partial_s^2Z_t+2A(s)\partial_s Z_t+
 (A'(s)+A(s)^2)Z_t\right],\quad Z_0=\zeta,
 \label{eq:rh-completion-drift}
\end{equation}
where
$A(s)=1/s+1/(s-1)-\frac12\log\pi+\frac12\psi(s/2)$.
This follows by twice differentiating $QZ_t$.
It records precisely the gamma-factor drift and potential that relate
completed heat evolution to the Dirichlet function in
\eqref{eq:rh-bc-heat-initial}.

Heat can also be compared before and after arithmetic cooling, not
merely declared identical or different. Write
$(Cg)(\lambda)=\sum_{n\ge1}g(n\lambda)$ and
$(Mg)(\lambda)=(\log\lambda)^2g(\lambda)$. Direct subtraction yields
\begin{equation}
 [M,C]g(\lambda)=
 -2\log\lambda\sum_{n\ge1}(\log n)g(n\lambda)
 -\sum_{n\ge1}(\log n)^2g(n\lambda).
 \label{eq:rh-cooling-commutator}
\end{equation}
For $g=h$, Mellin transformation initially on $\operatorname{Re}s>1$
turns this into
$\zeta''{\cal M}h+2\zeta'({\cal M}h)'$, since $M$ becomes
$\partial_s^2$. The Newman deformation is output multiplication
$e^{tM/4}$, followed by the coordinate changes in
\eqref{eq:rh-cooling-newman-map}; the commutator gives its correction
relative to input multiplication.
The distinction has a concrete domain manifestation: for real $t>0$,
$e^{t(\log\lambda)^2/4}h(\lambda)$ grows at $\lambda\downarrow0$
and is not Mellin integrable there, whereas
$e^{t(\log\lambda)^2/4}S(\lambda)$ still decays at both ends.
Even the infinitesimal input $Mh$ changes the trace, because
\[
 \int_0^\infty(\log\lambda)^2h(\lambda)d\lambda
 =({\cal M}h)''(1)
 =\frac{2-\gamma_E-2\log2-\log\pi}{8}\ne0.
\]
These computations specify the map and its domain, not a claim of
disconnection. They also prevent treating output heat automatically
as an evolution of a trace-zero input or of the cyclic cokernel.

\subsection{Zeros as Burgers poles and their collision coordinates}
\label{sec:rh-newman-collision}

The zero dynamics needed here are not restricted to an assumed
all-real-zero regime. Polymath \cite[\S3, Proposition~3.1]{polymath2019}
proves them for real $t_0$, allowing complex and multiple zeros.
For a simple zero $z_j(t)$, differentiating $H_t(z_j(t))=0$ and using
backward heat gives
\begin{equation}
 z_j'(t)=\frac{H_t''(z_j(t))}{H_t'(z_j(t))}
 =2\sum_{k\ne j}^{\prime}\frac1{z_j(t)-z_k(t)}.
 \label{eq:rh-zero-motion}
\end{equation}
For completeness, the second equality follows as follows.
$H_t$ is even and, for real $t$, positive at the origin.
Its order is at most one: the positive kernel satisfies
$\Phi(u)\le6\pi^2e^{9u-\pi e^{4u}}$ for $u\ge0$, by the
geometric-series estimate below. For fixed real $t$, absorb
$\max(t,0)u^2+9u$ into $\pi e^{4u}/2+C_t$.
For $|z|\le R$ the remaining integral is bounded by a fixed
constant times $\exp(\sup_{u\ge0}(Ru-\pi e^{4u}/4))$.
For $R>\pi$ this supremum is
$\frac R4(\log(R/\pi)-1)$, proving
$\log\max_{|z|\le R}|H_t(z)|=O_t(R\log R)$.
Pair its nonzero zeros as $z_{-k}=-z_k$. Hadamard factorization
then gives a locally uniformly convergent paired product
$H_t(z)=H_t(0)\prod_k'(1-z/z_k)$.
The paired logarithmic derivative converges locally away from the
zeros, since a pair contributes $2z/(z^2-z_k^2)$ and
$\sum_k|z_k|^{-2}<\infty$. At a simple $z_j$, subtract
$1/(z-z_j)$ from $H_t'/H_t$ and take the limit.
Taylor expansion shows the limit is $H_t''(z_j)/(2H_t'(z_j))$.
The prime in \eqref{eq:rh-zero-motion} pairs $k$ with $-k$, except
that the term $k=-j$ is kept separately. It is not an unregularized sum.

The local collision law has equally explicit coordinates. Suppose
$H_{t_0}(z)=a_m(z-z_0)^m+O((z-z_0)^{m+1})$, $a_m\ne0$.
Taylor expansion in time, with
$\partial_t^\ell H=(-1)^\ell\partial_z^{2\ell}H$, shows that after
$z-z_0=\sqrt{2(t-t_0)}\,y$ the leading polynomial is
\[
 a_m[2(t-t_0)]^{m/2}\operatorname{He}_m(y),\qquad
 \operatorname{He}_m(y)=
 \sum_{\ell=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}
 \frac{m!(-1)^\ell y^{m-2\ell}}{2^\ell\ell!(m-2\ell)!}.
\]
On bounded $y$ sets the error divided by the displayed prefactor is
$O(|t-t_0|^{1/2})$. The Hermite polynomial has simple real roots:
its Gaussian derivative expression and Rolle's theorem give real
roots, and its second-order differential equation excludes a repeated
root by uniqueness. On small disjoint circles around those roots,
Rouche's theorem and then the inverse function theorem give
$z=z_0+\sqrt{2(t-t_0)}\,y_j+O(|t-t_0|)$.
For $m=2$ these are $z_0\pm\sqrt{2(t-t_0)}+O(|t-t_0|)$.
The exact polynomial heat solution
$(z-z_0)^2-2(t-t_0)$ illustrates the leading law, but is not substituted
for the arithmetic $H_t$.

The pole interpretation now has a precise counterexample criterion.
De Bruijn and Newman give a finite threshold $\Lambda$ such that
$H_t$ has only real zeros exactly for $t\ge\Lambda$;
Rodgers--Tao prove $\Lambda\ge0$
\cite[\S1]{rodgersTao2020}. Thus a certified nonreal zero at any
specified $t\ge0$ would prove $\Lambda>t\ge0$ and refute classical RH.
At $t=0$ the coordinate of $\rho=\sigma+i\gamma$ is
$z=2\gamma+i(1-2\sigma)$, so off-critical is exactly off-real.
In forward Burgers time $\tau=-t$, this target is $\tau\le0$.
A pole somewhere is not the target: even the real critical-line zeros
give poles of the logarithmic derivative. This identifies which
singularity, coordinate and time must be located. The explicit
BC-observable route of \S\ref{sec:rh-bc-heat} is retained alongside
the earlier vacuum-to-Navier--Stokes construction.

\subsection{Certified local zero motion from the theta integral}
\label{sec:rh-newman-certificates}

The code \texttt{verify\_newman\_heat.py} evaluates
\eqref{eq:rh-newman-definition} directly, without zeta values or zero
tables. Here are full bounds for its truncations. They hold for
$0\le t\le1/4$, $|\operatorname{Im}z|\le1$, and $0\le j\le4$
derivatives with respect to $z$. On $0\le u\le2$ keep $1\le n\le N$,
where $N=8$. Every theta summand is positive for real $u\ge0$ and is
at most $2\pi^2n^4e^{9u}e^{-\pi n^2e^{4u}}$. Since
$|\partial_z^j\cos(zu)|\le u^j e^{|\operatorname{Im}z|u}$, the
discarded part of the integral over $[0,2]$ is bounded by
\begin{equation}
 E_n=64\pi^2e^{21-\pi N^2}
 \left(\frac{N^3}{2\pi}+\frac{3N}{4\pi^2}
                  +\frac{3}{8\pi^3N}\right).
 \label{eq:rh-theta-n-tail}
\end{equation}
Indeed $u^j\le16$ there, the interval length is $2$, and the other
exponentials contribute at most $e^{1+2+18}$. The function
$x^4e^{-\pi x^2}$ is decreasing for $x\ge N$, so its sum for $n>N$
is bounded by its integral from $N$ to infinity. Two integrations by
parts, followed by
$\int_N^\infty e^{-\pi x^2}dx\le e^{-\pi N^2}/(2\pi N)$,
give the parenthesis in \eqref{eq:rh-theta-n-tail}.

For the remaining $u\ge2$ tail, use
$n^4\le16^{n-1}$ (induction) and
$n^2-1\ge3(n-1)$ to obtain
\[
 \sum_{n\ge1}n^4e^{-\pi n^2e^{4u}}
 \le\frac{e^{-\pi e^{4u}}}{1-16e^{-3\pi}}
 <3e^{-\pi e^{4u}}.
\]
For $u\ge2$,
$4\log u+u^2/4+10u\le29u^2/4\le8u^2\le e^{4u}
\le(\pi/2)e^{4u}$.
The middle exponential inequality follows at $u=2$ and persists
because $4-2/u>0$ is the logarithmic derivative of $e^{4u}/u^2$.
Consequently the integrand tail, including all the stated derivatives,
is at most $6\pi^2e^{-(\pi/2)e^{4u}}$.
Set $v=e^{4u}$ and bound $1/v\le e^{-8}$ to get
\begin{equation}
 E_u=3\pi e^{-8}\exp\left(-\frac\pi2e^8\right).
 \label{eq:rh-theta-u-tail}
\end{equation}
Outward-rounded values are $E_n<3.272678\,10^{-74}$ and
$E_u<8.425984\,10^{-2037}$.

Arb integrates the retained finite sum on eight subintervals of
$[0,2]$. It then adds $E_n+E_u$ to every required value or derivative.
Floating-point quadrature proposes the following decimal-rational
endpoints; their proof uses only the complete interval evaluations.

\toprule
$t$ & unique simple real root in this interval & local speed\\
\midrule
$0$ & $[28.269450279469,\ 28.269450287469]$ & $-0.5796306\pm4.70\,10^{-8}$\\

At each pair of endpoints, the complete $H_t$ enclosures have opposite

The intermediate value theorem gives existence, and the derivative

arithmetic heat flow really moves a certified zero at time zero.

\subsection{An explicit arithmetic lift and its Mellin singularities}
\label{sec:rh-mobius-heat-lift}

The preceding commutator does not prevent an arithmetic lift in a
larger function class. It tells us to construct that lift explicitly.
For real $t$ set
\begin{equation}
 B_t(\lambda)=e^{t(\log\lambda)^2/4}S(\lambda),\qquad
 g_t(\lambda)=\sum_{n\ge1}\mu(n)B_t(n\lambda).
 \label{eq:rh-mobius-heat-lift}
\end{equation}
For each fixed $\lambda>0$ the latter series converges absolutely,
as do all its derivatives locally in $(t,\lambda)$: $B_t(x)$
decreases faster than every power as $x\to\infty$, uniformly on
compact $t$ intervals. Absolute double summation and the divisor
identity $\sum_{d\mid m}\mu(d)=\boldsymbol1_{m=1}$ prove
$Cg_t=B_t$ and $g_0=h$. The same calculation proves uniqueness among
smooth functions that decrease faster than every power at infinity
and for which the indicated sums converge absolutely.
This is an actual inverse, not an unsupported existence assertion.

For $\sigma>1$, Tonelli's theorem applied to the absolute majorant
$\sum_n|B_t(n\lambda)|$ gives the integrable bound
$\zeta(\sigma)\int_0^\infty|B_t(\lambda)|\lambda^\sigma
d\lambda/\lambda$. Mellin transformation is therefore justified and
yields
\begin{equation}
 {\cal M}g_t(s)=\frac{X_t(s)}{4\zeta(s)}
 \quad(\operatorname{Re}s>1),\qquad
 {\cal M}\dot g_0(s)=\frac{\xi''(s)}{16\zeta(s)}.
 \label{eq:rh-mobius-mellin-lift}
\end{equation}
The second formula follows by differentiating
\eqref{eq:rh-completed-heat}; both quotients specify meromorphic
continuations, not continued positive Gibbs states.

There is a certified, explicit loss of smooth Mellin-strip membership
in this lift. Let $a$ be the unique root in the first interval of
\S\ref{sec:rh-newman-certificates} and $\rho=1/2+ia/2$.
Then $\xi(\rho)=0$, $\xi'(\rho)\ne0$ and $\xi''(\rho)\ne0$:
indeed $H_0'(a)=i\xi'(\rho)/16$ and
$H_0''(a)=-\xi''(\rho)/32$, while the two derivative enclosures
exclude zero. Since $Q(\rho)\ne0$, $\zeta$ has a simple zero at $\rho$.
Thus ${\cal M}\dot g_0$ has the nonzero residue
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname*{Res}_{s=\rho}{\cal M}\dot g_0(s)
 =\frac{\xi''(\rho)}{16\zeta'(\rho)}
 =\frac{Q(\rho)}{8i}\,a'(0).
 \label{eq:rh-lift-residue}
\end{equation}
A Mellin transform holomorphic on a strip containing $\rho$ cannot
have this residue. More precisely, among the rapid-decay-at-infinity
lifts just specified, the lift is uniquely $g_t$. Its pointwise
derivative cannot have a holomorphic Mellin transform on a strip
containing $\rho$ and connected to $\operatorname{Re}s>1$.
For example, fix $\beta>1$ and require differentiability in every
weighted $L^1$ seminorm
$\int_0^\infty |g(\lambda)|\lambda^\sigma d\lambda/\lambda$,
$0<\sigma<\beta$. Mellin transformation is then differentiable
and holomorphic on that strip (use two adjacent weights to control
logarithmic derivatives); the nonzero residue excludes such a path.
Formula \eqref{eq:rh-mobius-heat-lift} still realizes it in
the larger pointwise class, and
\eqref{eq:rh-mobius-mellin-lift} states exactly the extra singularity.
For sufficiently small nonzero $t$, $X_t(\rho)\ne0$ as well, by its
nonzero time derivative at zero. This demonstrates a moving-zero
mechanism and an exact change of function space, not a counterexample
to RH: the certified $\rho$ itself is on the critical line.

\subsection{Finite certificates for Newman collisions and nonreal zeros}
\label{sec:rh-newman-finite-tests}

The general zero-motion statement of \cite[\S3]{polymath2019}
includes complex and multiple zeros. It gives a direct two-variable
target, with no real-zero assumption in the calculation:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal F(t,x)=(H_t(x),H_t'(x)),\qquad
 D\mathcal F(t,x)=
 \begin{pmatrix}-H_t''(x)&H_t'(x)\\-H_t'''(x)&H_t''(x)\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:rh-collision-jacobian}
\end{equation}
At a real double zero with $H_t''(x)\ne0$, the determinant is
$-(H_t''(x))^2$, so the system is nonsingular. A certified solution
with $t_c>0$ gives nonreal zeros at times just below $t_c$, still
positive, by the collision expansion proved above. Hence it gives
$\Lambda>0$ and disproves RH. The derivative bounds in
\S\ref{sec:rh-newman-certificates} cover $\mathcal F$ and its
Jacobian on the displayed time range; interval Newton can therefore
certify this system without a new unbounded integral representation.
No such positive-time solution is reported here.

A complementary scalar test follows from the classical Jensen
criterion; see \cite[\S2 and \S4.2]{farmer2020jensen} for its statement
and for the loss of detecting power under repeated differentiation.
At a real center $x$, use the degree-two polynomial
\begin{equation}
 J^2_{t,x}(w)=H_t(x)+2H_t'(x)w+H_t''(x)w^2,\qquad
 L_1(t,x)=H_t'(x)^2-H_t(x)H_t''(x).
 \label{eq:rh-centered-laguerre}
\end{equation}
Here is the required necessity directly. If all zeros $r_j$ of
$H_t$ are real, its paired Hadamard product gives, away from them,
\[
 -\left(\frac{H_t'}{H_t}\right)'(x)
   =\sum_j\frac{1}{(x-r_j)^2}\ge0.
\]
The sum converges locally away from the zeros since the entire
function has order one; the exponential factor in a genus-one
product has logarithm of degree at most one and disappears after
two derivatives. Multiplication by $H_t(x)^2$ proves $L_1\ge0$.
At a zero the same assertion follows by continuity, or directly
from $L_1=H_t'^2$. Consequently a rigorously negative value at any
$t\ge0$ refutes RH by the de Bruijn--Newman threshold theorem.
This is a finite rejection certificate, not an assertion that a
bounded collection of nonnegative values characterizes RH.

Write $a_j$ for approximations to $H_t^{(j)}(x)$ with errors at
most $e_j$, $j=0,1,2$. Expanding the two products and using the
triangle inequality proves
\begin{align}
 |L_1-(a_1^2-a_0a_2)|&\le E,\\
 E&=2|a_1|e_1+e_1^2+|a_0|e_2+|a_2|e_0+e_0e_2.
 \label{eq:rh-laguerre-error}
\end{align}
Thus $a_1^2-a_0a_2+E<0$ suffices. The theta integral gives
$H_t=I_0$, $H_t'=-I_1$, $H_t''=-I_2$, where $I_0,I_2$ use the
cosine with weights $1,u^2$ and $I_1$ uses $u\sin(xu)$.
In particular $L_1=I_1^2+I_0I_2$. At $x=0$ both cosine integrals
are positive, so that center cannot supply a negative witness.
For a real, nonvanishing multiplier $q$ on an interval, differentiation
also gives the exact correction
\[
 L_1(qH)=q^2L_1(H)-q^2H^2(\log|q|)''.
\]
An $x$-dependent rescaling must retain that correction.
The polynomial $Q_t(z)=(z-a)^2+b^2-2t$ satisfies the same backward
heat equation and has $L_1(t,a)=-2(b^2-2t)$; this checks the sign
and detects its nonreal pair, but is not a zeta example.

The bounded implementation \texttt{verify\_centered\_laguerre.py}
evaluates the full three derivative integrals at exactly
$t\in\{0,1/20,1/5\}$ and $x\in\{20,24,28,\ldots,60\}$.
All 33 resulting interval enclosures are strictly positive, with
no inconclusive enclosure. For example,
$L_1(1/5,60)=5.021267296249904426999643023181472271150519
\times10^{-18}$ with absolute error at most $5.43\times10^{-61}$.
The polynomial control gives the exact negative values
$-2,-9/5,-6/5$ at its center at the same times. An independent
implementation re-evaluates nine of the positive points from the
Hermite seed with nine summands and sixteen panels, and reconstructs
all 33 signs from the saved enclosing derivative balls. These are
pointwise calibration results, not positivity on the intervals
between the points.

Finally, a candidate nonreal zero admits a small contour certificate.
Fix $t\ge0$, $0<r<|\operatorname{Im}z_0|$, and a proved bound
$M_2\ge\sup_{|z-z_0|\le r}|H_t''(z)|$. Taylor's formula along
the line segment from $z_0$ gives a remainder at most $M_2r^2/2$.
If
\begin{equation}
 |H_t(z_0)|+M_2r^2/2<r|H_t'(z_0)|,
 \label{eq:rh-local-rouche}
\end{equation}
then on the circle the remainder after subtracting
$H_t'(z_0)(z-z_0)$ is strictly smaller than that linear function.
Rouch\'e's theorem gives exactly one zero in the disk, which lies
strictly off the real axis. This is the local version of the
boundary/winding method used in \cite[\S8.3]{polymath2019}.

\subsection{Connes's exact arithmetic heat kernel and its Newman map}
\label{sec:rh-connes-heat-tests}

We use the Gaussian and explicit-formula conventions of
\cite[\S2--4, equations (5), (9), (13), (25)]{connesHeat2024}.
For $a>0$, put
\begin{align}
 F_a(e^y)&=(4\pi a)^{-1/2}e^{-y^2/(4a)},\qquad
 \widehat F_a(\gamma)=e^{-a\gamma^2},\\
 \Psi(a)&=\frac1{\sqrt{\pi a}}\sum_{n\ge2}
       \Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}e^{-(\log n)^2/(4a)},\\
 W_{\mathbb R}(F_a)&=(\log(4\pi)+\gamma_E)F_a(1)\notag\\
 &\quad+\int_0^\infty
 [2F_a(e^y)-2e^{-y/2}F_a(1)]
 \frac{e^{y/2}}{e^y-e^{-y}}\,dy,\\
 Z(a)&=2e^{a/4}-W_{\mathbb R}(F_a)-\Psi(a).
 \label{eq:rh-connes-arithmetic-heat}
\end{align}
Here $\Lambda(n)$ is the von Mangoldt function, not the Newman
constant, and $\gamma_E$ is Euler's constant. The integrand in the
archimedean term has a removable limit at zero: its numerator is
$F_a(1)y+O(y^2)$ and its denominator is $2y+O(y^3)$.
The series and integral converge absolutely at infinity, uniformly
on compact positive $a$ intervals, as do all $a$ derivatives.

Apply the unrestricted explicit formula to
$f_a(x)=x^{-1/2}F_a(x)$. Its Mellin transform is
$\widetilde f_a(s)=e^{a(s-1/2)^2}$, by the elementary Gaussian
integral. The two pole terms are each $e^{a/4}$, and the finite-place
terms combine into $\Psi$. Thus, without imposing RH,
\begin{equation}
 Z(a)=\sum_{\rho}e^{a(\rho-1/2)^2},
 \label{eq:rh-heat-all-zeros}
\end{equation}
where the sum is over nontrivial zeros with multiplicity. Its
absolute local convergence follows from $0<\operatorname{Re}\rho<1$
and the zero count $O(T\log T)$: the summands are bounded on positive
compact $a$ intervals by a constant times $e^{-c(\operatorname{Im}\rho)^2}$.
This also proves convergence after any number of $a$ derivatives.
It distinguishes the unconditional arithmetic definition from the
self-adjoint trace interpretation used under RH in the cited paper.

There is an exact transform relation to the Newman evolution, not
just common terminology. For $\tau>0$, the kernel $k$ of
\S\ref{sec:rh-hermite-cooling} gives
\begin{align}
 H_{-\tau}(z)
 &=\frac12\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{\tau}}
   \int_{\mathbb R}k(v)F_{1/\tau}(e^v)e^{izv/2}\,dv\notag\\
 &=\frac1{\sqrt{4\pi\tau}}\int_{\mathbb R}
       e^{-(z-y)^2/(4\tau)}H_0(y)\,dy.
 \label{eq:rh-connes-newman-gaussian}
\end{align}
For the first equality substitute the definition of $F_{1/\tau}$.
For the second insert the Fourier integral for $H_0$ and evaluate
the inner Gaussian integral: the multiplier is
$e^{-\tau(v/2)^2}$. Fubini is justified by integrability of $k$ and
Gaussian decay; the identity extends to complex $z$ by dominated
holomorphic continuation. This gives the reciprocal width
$a=1/\tau$ in logarithmic kernel coordinates and the forward heat
time $\tau=-t$ in Newman coordinates. Evaluating that Gaussian on
the fixed zero spectrum gives $Z(a)$; multiplying the theta kernel
and Fourier transforming gives $H_{-\tau}$. The formulas specify
both operations and their shared kernel exactly.

Two finite arithmetic matrices now give possible rejection
certificates. For any $a_i>0$, $a>0$ and nonnegative integers $i,j$,
form
\begin{equation}
 K_{ij}=Z(a_i+a_j),\qquad
 M_{ij}=(-1)^{i+j}Z^{(i+j)}(a).
 \label{eq:rh-connes-heat-matrices}
\end{equation}
If RH holds, write $\rho=1/2+i\gamma$. For every finite real vector
$c$ the absolutely convergent sums give
\[
 c^TKc=\sum_\gamma\left(\sum_i c_i e^{-a_i\gamma^2}\right)^2\ge0,
 \qquad
 c^TMc=\sum_\gamma e^{-a\gamma^2}
                  \left(\sum_i c_i\gamma^{2i}\right)^2\ge0.
\]
Therefore a certified negative quadratic form evaluated by
\eqref{eq:rh-connes-arithmetic-heat}, including its full prime sum,
would disprove RH. This proves a finite test criterion; it is not
a conditional construction of the desired example. No such
negative matrix is reported here.

The prime term must not be replaced by heat asymptotics. For
$0<a\le\log6/8$ and $n\ge2$, $\log n\le\sqrt n$ and
\[
 e^{-(\log n)^2/(4a)}
 \le e^{-(\log2)^2/(4a)}(n/2)^{-2},
\]
because for $n\ge3$ the factorization
$\log^2 n-\log^2 2=\log(n/2)\log(2n)$ is at least
$\log6\log(n/2)\ge8a\log(n/2)$; $n=2$ is exact.
The inequality $\log n\le\sqrt n$ follows by maximizing
$\log x/\sqrt x$, whose maximum on $x\ge1$ is $2/e<1$.
Consequently
\begin{equation}
 0\le\Psi(a)\le
 4\left(\frac{\pi^2}{6}-1\right)
 \frac{e^{-(\log2)^2/(4a)}}{\sqrt{\pi a}}.
 \label{eq:rh-heat-prime-flatness}
\end{equation}
Indeed $\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}\le1$ and the comparison series is
$\sum_{n\ge2}4n^{-2}$. This reproduces the cited bound and proves
$\Psi(a)=O(a^A)$ for every fixed $A>0$. Thus power-series heat
coefficients cannot distinguish the complete arithmetic expression
from one with its prime term omitted. Exact finite-$a$ tests retain it.
