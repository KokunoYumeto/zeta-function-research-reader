\section{The retained cover as an exact Weil test algebra}
\label{sec:retained-weil-algebra}

The inverse-fibre construction supplies the relation $r^2=-2s$.
We now realize that relation on the actual test space of the Weil form,
calculate every finite polynomial channel and its inverse domain, and
identify precisely which pairing evaluates a zeta-zero witness. This is
a transport of the retained mechanism, not a replacement by a generic
zero search. We use the Fourier and Weil conventions of
\eqref{eq:rh-weil-convention}; compare Weil\cite{weil1952} and
Alp\"oge--Furman\cite[Sections 1.1--1.2 and 2]{alpogeFurman2026}.
The elementary module calculations below are proved here in full;
no novelty is claimed for polynomial division, compactly supported
ordinary differential equations, or the resulting linear algebra.

\subsection{The arithmetic-coordinate operator and its full form}
Put $\mathcal E=C_c^\infty(\R;\C)$ and retain the variable $u$ in
the test domain and $s$ in the spectral domain. Define
\[
 (Tf)(u)=-i f'(u),\qquad
 \widehat f(s)=\int_\R f(u)e^{-ius}\,du.
\]
Integration by parts, with no endpoint term because $f$ has compact
support, gives $\widehat{Tf}(s)=s\widehat f(s)$ for every complex $s$.
If $\operatorname{supp}f\subset[-L,L]$, repeated integration by parts
gives arbitrarily rapid decay in $|\Re s|$ in each fixed horizontal
strip. Consequently every polynomial in $T$ acts on $\mathcal E$
without enlarging support, and all polynomially weighted zero sums
below converge absolutely. This uses the usual zero-count bound
$N(T)=O(T\log T)$, not RH.

\begin{proposition}[The coordinate is symmetric for the actual Weil form]
For $f,g\in\mathcal E$,
\begin{equation}
 W(Tf,g)=W(f,Tg).
 \label{eq:retained-T-symmetric}
\end{equation}
For a polynomial $p$, let $p^\#(s)=\overline{p(\bar s)}$.
Then $W(p(T)f,g)=W(f,p^\#(T)g)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $s_\rho=(\rho-1/2)/i$, exactly as in
\eqref{eq:rh-weil-convention}. The contribution to the first expression is
\[
 m_\rho\overline{\bar s_\rho\widehat f(\bar s_\rho)}
                 \widehat g(s_\rho)
 =m_\rho\overline{\widehat f(\bar s_\rho)}
                 s_\rho\widehat g(s_\rho),
\]
which is the contribution to the second expression. Absolute convergence
permits the equality of sums. Iteration and conjugate-linearity in the
first slot prove the polynomial statement. No $s_\rho$ was assumed real.
\end{proof}

Here is also the exact arithmetic expression for these weighted entries.
For $h=g*f^\star$, where $f^\star(u)=\overline{f(-u)}$, let
$\mathcal L(h)$ denote the right side of
\eqref{eq:rh-full-weil-test}, with correlation $h$.
For integers $j,k\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
 W(T^j f,T^k g)=\mathcal L\bigl((-i)^{j+k}h^{(j+k)}\bigr).
 \label{eq:retained-differentiated-correlation}
\end{equation}
Indeed $(Tf)^\star=T(f^\star)$ by differentiating the reflection
and conjugating $-i$. Derivatives commute with convolution and
$(T^k g)*(T^j f)^\star=T^{j+k}(g*f^\star)$.
Applying the explicit formula proves the identity, including both
pole terms, the gamma term and every prime-power term. Thus a finite
branch calculation gives explicit correlations for the full arithmetic
form; it does not authorize dropping its archimedean or prime contributions.

\subsection{Branch multiplication and the two different pairings}
On $\mathcal E^2$ in the ordered basis $(1,r)$ set
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal R(f_0,f_1)=(-2Tf_1,f_0),\qquad
 \mathcal R^2=-2\operatorname{diag}(T,T),\qquad
 \pi(f_0,f_1)=f_0.
 \label{eq:retained-test-root}
\end{equation}
These are the same coefficients as
\eqref{eq:retained-arithmetic-matrices}, now on a concrete test-function
module with a specified domain. Fourier transformation on each component
intertwines this operator with multiplication by the matrix
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-2s\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.

The componentwise arithmetic form is
\[
 \mathcal W(U,V)=W(f_0,g_0)+W(f_1,g_1),\qquad
 U=(f_0,f_1),\quad V=(g_0,g_1).
\]
The algebra trace with the real structure $s^*=s$, $r^*=r$
instead gives the form
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal B(U,V)=2W(f_0,g_0)-4W(f_1,Tg_1).
 \label{eq:retained-branch-trace-form}
\end{equation}
To derive the coefficients, multiply
$(q_0^\#+r q_1^\#)(p_0+r p_1)$, replace $r^2$ by $-2s$, and
take the algebra trace, which is twice the even coefficient. The result
is $2q_0^\#p_0-4s q_1^\#p_1$; evaluating by the Weil distribution
gives \eqref{eq:retained-branch-trace-form}. This derivation retains
the involution: choosing $r^*=-r$ would change the odd sign and is
a different specified real structure, not a silent convention change.

\begin{proposition}[Exact adjoint and trace comparison]
Both forms are Hermitian. For $\mathcal W$ the adjoint identity is
\[
 \mathcal W(\mathcal R U,V)
 =\mathcal W\left(U,
       \begin{pmatrix}0&I\\-2T&0\end{pmatrix}V\right).
\]
For $\mathcal B$, the operator $\mathcal R$ is symmetric:
$\mathcal B(\mathcal R U,V)=\mathcal B(U,\mathcal R V)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Hermiticity follows from that of $W$ and
\eqref{eq:retained-T-symmetric}. Direct substitution gives
\[
 \mathcal W(\mathcal R U,V)
 =-2W(f_1,Tg_0)+W(f_0,g_1).
\]
Similarly each of $\mathcal B(\mathcal R U,V)$ and
$\mathcal B(U,\mathcal R V)$ equals
$-4W(f_1,Tg_0)-4W(f_0,Tg_1)$.
\end{proof}

The distinction has an explicit fibre calculation. At a real positive
spectral parameter $s=\lambda$, the algebra trace matrix is
$\operatorname{diag}(2,-4\lambda)$, whereas the componentwise evaluation
form has matrix $I_2$. Thus the former already has a negative odd
direction over a real positive parameter. For example, at $\lambda=1$
the vector $(0,1)$ has trace value $-4$ and componentwise value $1$.
This is a calculation in the fibre algebra, not a claim of a zeta zero
at $1$. It proves why a negative branch-trace value alone is not a
negative Weil value. The exact route to an arithmetic witness is the
scalar channel calculated next; no relation between the structures is
discarded by this comparison.

\subsection{Every finite polynomial channel: range, kernel and lifts}
Every $P(r)\in\C[r]$ has a unique expression
\[
 P(r)=A(s)+rB(s),\qquad s=-r^2/2.
\]
If $P(r)=\sum_j p_jr^j$, the coefficients are exactly
$A(s)=\sum_n p_{2n}(-2s)^n$ and
$B(s)=\sum_n p_{2n+1}(-2s)^n$.
The induced scalar channel is
\begin{equation}
 L_P=\pi P(\mathcal R),\qquad
 L_P(f_0,f_1)=A(T)f_0+C(T)f_1,\qquad C(s)=-2sB(s).
 \label{eq:retained-polynomial-channel}
\end{equation}
This follows by multiplying $\mathcal R$ into the two components,
not by identifying the two sheets.

\begin{proposition}[Constructive channel classification]
Let $P\ne0$. Choose polynomials $d,a,c,u,v$ satisfying
\[
 A=da,\qquad C=dc,\qquad ua+vc=1.
\]
They can be obtained by the Euclidean algorithm; retain the chosen
nonzero constant factor in $d$ and its corresponding factors in
$a,c,u,v$. Then
\begin{align}
 \operatorname{im}L_P&=d(T)\mathcal E,
 &\ker L_P&=\{(-c(T)h,a(T)h):h\in\mathcal E\}.
 \label{eq:retained-channel-kernel}
\end{align}
The unique preimage $h\in\mathcal E$ of an element $d(T)h$ gives
the explicit section $(u(T)h,v(T)h)$ of $L_P$ onto its image.
All these maps preserve any common compact support interval.
For $P=0$, the channel is zero, its image is zero and its kernel is
all of $\mathcal E^2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
First $p(T)$ is injective on $\mathcal E$ for every nonzero polynomial
$p$: $p(s)\widehat f(s)=0$ implies $\widehat f=0$ away from finitely
many points, hence identically by analyticity, and Fourier uniqueness
gives $f=0$. Now the displayed section maps to
$d(T)(ua+vc)(T)h=d(T)h$, proving the image claim. A kernel vector
satisfies $a(T)f_0+c(T)f_1=0$ by injectivity of $d(T)$. Put
$h=-v(T)f_0+u(T)f_1$. Commutativity of polynomial differential operators
gives
\[
 c(T)h=-(vc+ua)(T)f_0=-f_0,\qquad
 a(T)h=(vc+ua)(T)f_1=f_1.
\]
Conversely $(-c(T)h,a(T)h)$ maps to zero. If both components of
this parameterized vector vanish, $(ua+vc)(T)h=h=0$, proving
uniqueness of the kernel parameter. Derivatives preserve support.
\end{proof}

The image condition is also fully explicit; it is not an unspecified
solvability assumption. Write
$d(s)=d_0\prod_j(s-\lambda_j)^{k_j}$ with $d_0\ne0$ and distinct
$\lambda_j$. Then
\begin{equation}
 d(T)\mathcal E
 =\left\{g\in\mathcal E:
 \int_\R u^\ell e^{-i\lambda_j u}g(u)\,du=0
 \quad(0\le\ell<k_j,\ \hbox{every }j)\right\}.
 \label{eq:retained-channel-moments}
\end{equation}
These conditions are equivalently
$\widehat g^{(\ell)}(\lambda_j)=0$ because differentiating the
Fourier integral inserts $(-iu)^\ell$.

Here is the inverse proving sufficiency, including the endpoint behavior.
For one repeated factor, set
\begin{equation}
 [(T-\lambda)^{-k}g](u)
 =\frac{i^k e^{i\lambda u}}{(k-1)!}
   \int_{-\infty}^u (u-v)^{k-1}e^{-i\lambda v}g(v)\,dv.
 \label{eq:retained-compact-primitive}
\end{equation}
To verify the equation, conjugate $T-\lambda$ by $e^{i\lambda u}$:
it becomes $-i\partial_u$. Differentiating the $k$-fold primitive
$k$ times gives $g$, with $(-i)^ki^k=1$.
If $g$ is supported in $[a,b]$, the expression vanishes for $u<a$.
For $u>b$, expand $(u-v)^{k-1}$: every coefficient is a constant
multiple of one of the $k$ weighted moments in
\eqref{eq:retained-channel-moments}, so it vanishes there too.
Smoothness follows from the integral and from smooth compact support
of $g$; the differential equation and the same derivative formulas
show matching of all derivatives at $a,b$.
Necessity follows by Fourier transformation of
$g=(T-\lambda)^kf$. For distinct factors, apply
\eqref{eq:retained-compact-primitive} successively and divide by
$d_0$. After division by one factor the Fourier transform still has
the required vanishing orders at the other roots, since the removed
factor is nonzero there. This proves sufficiency for the full product.
Injectivity already proved makes the result independent of the order
of successive divisions.

In particular $T^{-1}g=i\int_{-\infty}^u g(v)\,dv$ exists in
$\mathcal E$ exactly when $\int g=0$. Therefore
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal R^{-1}(g_0,g_1)
   =(g_1,-\tfrac12 T^{-1}g_0),\qquad
 \operatorname{dom}\mathcal R^{-1}
   =\{(g_0,g_1):\textstyle\int g_0=0\}.
 \label{eq:retained-root-inverse-domain}
\end{equation}
Both compositions give the identity on their exact domains by
\eqref{eq:retained-test-root}. The factor $-1/2$ and the lost
zero-moment condition have not been absorbed into a convention.

For a concrete polynomial family tied to the same root operator, take
$P(r)=r(r^2+2\lambda)$. Then $A=0$,
$C(s)=4s(s-\lambda)$, and
\[
 L_P(f_0,f_1)=4T(T-\lambda)f_1.
\]
The kernel is exactly $\mathcal E\oplus0$. For $\lambda\ne0$,
the image consists of tests whose Fourier transforms vanish at $0$
and $\lambda$. At $\lambda=0$ it instead requires a double zero at
$0$, equivalently $\int g=\int ug=0$.
The inverse of the nonzero component is
$\tfrac14(T-\lambda)^{-1}T^{-1}g$, with the repeated-factor
formula when $\lambda=0$. This exhibits the collision of two image
conditions without losing their multiplicity.

\subsection{Explicit finite jet interpolation with retained support}
Let $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_q$ be distinct complex numbers,
let $k_j\ge1$, and put $D=\sum_j k_j$ and
$d(s)=d_0\prod_j(s-\lambda_j)^{k_j}$ with $d_0\ne0$.
There is an exact $\C[s]$-module isomorphism
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal E/d(T)\mathcal E
 \longrightarrow
 \bigoplus_{j=1}^q\C[s]/(s-\lambda_j)^{k_j},\qquad
 [f]\longmapsto
 \left[\sum_{\ell<k_j}
     \frac{\widehat f^{(\ell)}(\lambda_j)}{\ell!}
                  (s-\lambda_j)^\ell\right]_j.
 \label{eq:retained-finite-jet-isomorphism}
\end{equation}
The module action on the left is through $T$.
Its kernel statement is exactly
\eqref{eq:retained-channel-moments}. We prove surjectivity by an
explicit construction inside any prescribed nonempty open interval $I$.

Choose $u_0\in I$ and $\epsilon>0$ so
$[u_0-\epsilon,u_0+\epsilon]\subset I$ and
$\epsilon\max_j|\lambda_j|<\log2$ (the latter is automatic if
all nodes are zero). Put
\[
 b(v)=\begin{cases}\exp(-1/(1-v^2)),&|v|<1,\\0,&|v|\ge1,
       \end{cases}
 \quad B=\int_{-1}^1b(v)\,dv>0,\quad
 \phi_\epsilon(u)=\frac{b((u-u_0)/\epsilon)}{\epsilon B}.
\]
Every derivative of $b$ on $(-1,1)$ is its exponential times a
rational function with a finite-order pole at the endpoints, so all
derivatives tend to zero there; hence $b$ is smooth on $\R$.
The constants $\epsilon$ and $B$ are retained in the construction.
Writing $\Phi(s)=\widehat{\phi_\epsilon}(s)$, we have
\[
 \Phi(s)=e^{-iu_0s}B^{-1}\int_{-1}^1b(v)e^{-i\epsilon vs}\,dv,
 \qquad
 \left|B^{-1}\int_{-1}^1b(v)e^{-i\epsilon v\lambda_j}\,dv-1\right|
 \le e^{\epsilon|\lambda_j|}-1<1.
\]
Thus $\Phi(\lambda_j)\ne0$ at every node, with a direct lower bound.

For prescribed jets let
$H_j(s)=\sum_{\ell<k_j}h_{j,\ell}(s-\lambda_j)^\ell$ and
$M_j(s)=\prod_{a\ne j}(s-\lambda_a)^{k_a}$.
Let $A_j(s)$ be the Taylor polynomial of degree $k_j-1$ at
$\lambda_j$ of $H_j(s)/(M_j(s)\Phi(s))$.
Its denominator is nonzero near that point, so its coefficients
are obtained by ordinary finite power-series division. Define
\begin{equation}
 Q(s)=\sum_{j=1}^qM_j(s)A_j(s),\qquad
 f=Q(T)\phi_\epsilon.
 \label{eq:retained-explicit-Hermite-lift}
\end{equation}
At $\lambda_j$, every summand other than the $j$th vanishes to
order at least $k_j$, and the $j$th satisfies
$M_jA_j\Phi=H_j+O((s-\lambda_j)^{k_j})$.
Therefore $\widehat f=Q\Phi$ has exactly the prescribed truncated
jets. Its support is inside $I$, and $\deg Q\le D-1$.
This proves surjectivity in
\eqref{eq:retained-finite-jet-isomorphism}, not merely an existence
claim for an unspecified interpolant. Injectivity on the quotient
and the polynomial module action follow from the already proved
kernel and $\widehat{Tf}=s\widehat f$.
For a nonzero constant $d$, both sides are the zero module, with
an empty direct sum on the right.

\subsection{Arithmetic evaluation jets and the original spectral coordinate}
The following map records every zeta-zero multiplicity without imposing RH:
\begin{equation}
 J:\mathcal E\longrightarrow
 \prod_{\rho}\C^{m_\rho},\qquad
 (Jf)_{\rho,j}=\frac{\widehat f^{(j)}(s_\rho)}{j!},
 \quad 0\le j<m_\rho.
 \label{eq:retained-arithmetic-jets}
\end{equation}
Here one factor is used for each distinct nontrivial zero.
It is an injective linear map. To prove this, suppose $Jf=0$ and
$\widehat f\not\equiv0$. Choose $s_0$ where $\widehat f(s_0)\ne0$.
Compact support in $[-L,L]$ gives
$|\widehat f(s)|\le\|f\|_1e^{L|\Im s|}$.
Jensen's formula on a disc of radius $2R$ about $s_0$ therefore bounds
the number of zeros in the radius-$R$ disc, counted with multiplicity,
by $2LR/\log2+O(1)$: each such zero contributes at least $\log2$
to the Jensen sum, and the boundary logarithm is at most
$2LR+L|\Im s_0|+\log\|f\|_1$.
Radii with boundary zeros follow by taking decreasing slightly larger
admissible radii. But $Jf=0$ requires zeros of order at least
$m_\rho$ at every $s_\rho$. Since
$|\Im s_\rho|<1/2$, the Riemann--von Mangoldt count
$N(T)=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi}-\frac{T}{2\pi}+O(\log T)$
places $\gg R\log R$ of these required zeros in that disc for large
$R$, a contradiction. The cited source recalls this count in
Section~1.1\cite{alpogeFurman2026}. Fourier uniqueness now gives
$f=0$. This proof uses multiplicities, not an assumption that all
zeros are simple, and claims no surjectivity onto arbitrary jet data.
The finite interpolation construction proves that the image is
dense for the product topology on the displayed product: every
basic neighborhood specifies only finitely many jet coordinates,
and those can be matched exactly. This topology statement does not
control an infinite weighted Weil sum or identify the topology of
a different arithmetic quotient.

In the factorial coordinates of \eqref{eq:retained-arithmetic-jets},
the exact intertwining formula is
\begin{equation}
 (JTf)_{\rho,j}
   =s_\rho(Jf)_{\rho,j}+(Jf)_{\rho,j-1},
 \qquad (Jf)_{\rho,-1}:=0.
 \label{eq:retained-jet-jordan}
\end{equation}
It follows by applying the product rule to $s\widehat f(s)$ and
dividing by $j!$. Thus the $m_\rho$-jet carries precisely
$S_\rho=s_\rho I+N_\rho$, where $N_\rho$ has ones immediately
below the diagonal and zeros elsewhere. On two-component jets the
branch operator is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-2S_\rho\\I&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
whose square is $-2\operatorname{diag}(S_\rho,S_\rho)$.
This retains both the arithmetic coordinate and the nilpotent jet
information. It makes no unproved identification with the topology
of the closed Connes quotient; that quotient has its own specified
source construction in the preceding section.

The Weil form evaluates only the zeroth coordinates of these jets,
with the original multiplicity weights and conjugate-zero pairing:
\[
 W(f,g)=\sum_\rho m_\rho
   \overline{\widehat f(\bar s_\rho)}\widehat g(s_\rho).
\]
Higher jets and the branch trace are not silently substituted into
this expression. For any finite family $U_j\in\mathcal E^2$ and
specified polynomial channels $P_j$, put $h_j=L_{P_j}U_j$.
Then, exactly,
\begin{equation}
 c^*\bigl[W(h_i,h_j)\bigr]c
   =W\left(\sum_j c_jh_j,\sum_j c_jh_j\right).
 \label{eq:retained-genuine-witness-pullback}
\end{equation}
Each entry can be evaluated by
\eqref{eq:retained-differentiated-correlation} and the complete
explicit formula. Thus a certified negative value in this transported
family would be an actual negative Weil value, while the fibre trace
example above is not such a certificate. The present results supply
the exact map, every finite-channel kernel and the inverse-domain
conditions; they do not exhibit a negative arithmetic value.
