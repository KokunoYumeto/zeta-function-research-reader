\section{The entire inverse fibre and its incompressible coefficient heat flow}
\label{sec:inverse-fibre-heat}

We continue the original polynomial construction of
Section~\ref{sec:retained-mechanism}, with the same coordinates and
human-source provenance\cite{tao2026jacobian,speyer2026jacobian,poplett2026alias}.
The complete inverse map is retained below to verify its dynamical lift;
no finite inverse point is discarded.

\subsection{Every finite inverse point, including the excluded roots}

Retain the original map, with the identity renaming of its third spatial
coordinate from $t$ to $w$:
\begin{align}
 F_1&=(1+xy)^3w+y^2(1+xy)(4+3xy),\notag\\
 F_2&=y+3x(1+xy)^2w+3xy^2(4+3xy),\notag\\
 F_3&=2x-3x^2y-x^3w. \label{eq:ifh-original}
\end{align}
The target coordinates are $(a,b,c)$. All algebraic statements first hold
over $\C$. Real parameters and real roots give precisely the real points.
For the determinant, on $x\ne0$ set
\[
 v=1+xy,\qquad h=2-3xy-x^2w,\qquad
 A=v^2+v-v^3h,\qquad B=4v+2-3v^2h.
\]
Direct substitution gives $F=(A/x^2,B/x,xh)$ and
\[
 \det\frac{\partial(x,v,h)}{\partial(x,y,w)}=-x^3.
\]
The other determinant is $x^{-3}$ times
\[
 \det\begin{pmatrix}
 -2A&2v+1-3v^2h&-v^3\\
 -B&4-6vh&-3v^2\\h&0&1
 \end{pmatrix}=2.
\]
Indeed expansion gives
\[
 (4-6vh)(-2v^2-2v+3v^3h)
 +(2v+1-3v^2h)(4v+2-6v^2h)=2.
\]
Their product is $-2$. The original Jacobian determinant is a polynomial;
agreement on $x\ne0$ proves
\begin{equation}
 \det DF=-2\quad\hbox{everywhere}. \label{eq:ifh-det}
\end{equation}

For fixed $(a,b,c)$ define
\[
 P_{a,b,c}(r)=cr^3-2r^2+br-2a,\qquad
 \alpha=\tfrac12 P_r(r).
\]
For each simple root, the entire inverse point is
\begin{equation}
 H(r,\alpha,c)=
 \left(\alpha^{-1},\ r-\alpha,\
       5\alpha^2-3r\alpha-c\alpha^3\right).
 \label{eq:ifh-chart}
\end{equation}
Here and below $\alpha\ne0$ is part of the domain. To prove both directions,
start with $x\ne0$, set $\alpha=1/x$, $r=y+1/x$, and solve $F_3=c$.
This gives exactly \eqref{eq:ifh-chart}. Before either other target equation
is imposed, direct substitution gives
\begin{equation}
 F\circ H=
 \left(r^2+r\alpha-cr^3,\ 4r+2\alpha-3cr^2,\ c\right).
 \label{eq:ifh-target-chart}
\end{equation}
The second equation equals $b$ precisely when
$2\alpha=3cr^2-4r+b=P_r(r)$. After imposing that identity,
$2(F_1-a)=P(r)$. Thus the formulas give every point with $x\ne0$,
and recovering $r=y+1/x$ proves injectivity of the parametrization.
A multiple root has $\alpha=0$ and gives no finite point in this chart.

The excluded hyperplane is handled without division:
\[
 F(0,y,w)=(w+4y^2,y,0).
\]
It contributes exactly $(0,b,a-4b^2)$ when $c=0$, and contributes no
point when $c\ne0$. These statements exhaust the whole fibre.

For completeness the multiplicities are explicit. If $c\ne0$, put
\[
 \mathscr D=4(b^2-cb^3+18abc-16a-27a^2c^2).
\]
Substitution of $(c,-2,b,-2a)$ in the cubic discriminant gives this
formula. If $\mathscr D\ne0$, there are three simple complex roots and
three finite complex inverse points. If $\mathscr D=0$ and there is a
double root and a simple root, the entire fibre is the single point
corresponding to the simple root. The remaining case is a triple root.
Comparing coefficients of $c(r-r_*)^3$ gives exactly
\begin{equation}
 r_*=\frac{2}{3c},\qquad
 b=\frac4{3c},\qquad a=\frac4{27c^2}. \label{eq:ifh-triple}
\end{equation}
All three root multiplicities then belong to the excluded $\alpha=0$
locus, and the original finite fibre is empty.
These are conclusions about this displayed polynomial and its inverse
formulas, not about any other coordinate model.

When $c=0$, the degree is exactly two, since its leading coefficient
remains $-2$. Its discriminant is $\delta=b^2-16a$.
If $\delta\ne0$, its two simple roots and the separate $x=0$ point
give three complex points. If $\delta=0$, its double root supplies no
chart point and the separate point is the whole fibre.
For real parameters, a real cubic with $\mathscr D>0$ has three real
simple roots, while $\mathscr D<0$ has one real simple root and a
nonreal conjugate pair. This follows, for example, from
$c^4\prod_{i<j}(r_i-r_j)^2$: it is positive for three distinct real
roots and negative for one real root and a conjugate pair.
In the double-root cubic case both the double and simple root are real,
because a nonreal multiple root would require its conjugate with the
same multiplicity. The real quadratic case has two real chart points
when $\delta>0$ and none when $\delta<0$, always retaining the additional
real boundary point.

\subsection{The coefficient heat flow and every time constant}

Let $\tau$ denote forward heat time. The complete target flow is
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_\tau(a,b,c)=(a+2\tau,\ b+6c\tau,\ c).
 \label{eq:ifh-target-flow}
\end{equation}
It is a group of affine maps with determinant one:
\[
 D\Phi_\tau=
 \begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&6\tau\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \Phi_{\tau+s}=\Phi_\tau\circ\Phi_s.
\]
For $P(\tau,r)=P_{\Phi_\tau(a,b,c)}(r)$,
\begin{equation}
 P_\tau=6cr-4=P_{rr}. \label{eq:ifh-heat}
\end{equation}
On a simple root the implicit function theorem applies because $P_r\ne0$;
differentiating $P(\tau,r(\tau))=0$ gives
$r'=-P_{rr}/P_r$. No derivative formula is assigned to a multiple root.

Two further identities determine this coefficient motion exactly:
\begin{align}
 \frac{d}{d\tau}(b-3ca)&=0,\notag\\
 \frac{d}{d\tau}\mathscr D
 &=(2\partial_a+6c\partial_b)\mathscr D
 =-8(3bc-4)^2. \label{eq:ifh-discriminant-flow}
\end{align}
Indeed differentiating the displayed polynomial $\mathscr D$ gives
$4(48bc-18c^2b^2-32)$, which equals the last expression.
Writing $A_0=3bc-4$ at $\tau=0$ and integrating the exact square gives
\[
 \mathscr D(\tau)=\mathscr D(0)
 -8A_0^2\tau-144A_0c^2\tau^2-864c^4\tau^3.
\]
For real $c\ne0$ this is strictly decreasing: the integral of the
nonnegative square over every interval of positive length is positive,
since $A_0+18c^2\tau$ cannot vanish throughout such an interval.
Its cubic leading term gives opposite infinite limits at the two ends
of the real line. Thus each such real heat orbit meets the discriminant
locus exactly once. For $c=0$, the derivative is instead the constant
$-128$, with the degree-two interpretation above still required.

For $c\ne0$, the exact coordinate substitution $r=z+2/(3c)$ gives
\[
 P=cz^3+\left(b-\frac4{3c}\right)z+K,\qquad
 K=\frac{2b}{3c}-\frac{16}{27c^2}-2a.
\]
The original coefficient $c$ is retained, and $K$ is constant under
\eqref{eq:ifh-target-flow}. If the orbit passes through
\eqref{eq:ifh-triple} at $\tau_c$, its polynomial is exactly
\[
 P=c z\bigl(z^2+6(\tau-\tau_c)\bigr).
\]
For $\tau\ne\tau_c$, the central root has
$\alpha=3c(\tau-\tau_c)$; either outer root has
$\alpha=-6c(\tau-\tau_c)$. These explicit denominators record the
escape of every chart inverse point at the triple-root time.
At that time the finite fibre is empty, as proved above.

Newman time is the specific reversal $t=-\tau$. Consequently
\[
 \mathcal P(t,r)=P_{a_0-2t,b_0-6ct,c}(r),\qquad
 \mathcal P_t=-\mathcal P_{rr}.
\]
For $b_0=c=0$,
\[
 \mathcal P(t,r)=-2\bigl(r^2-2(t-a_0/2)\bigr).
\]
Thus $a_0=-1/4$ gives the model collision time $-1/8$ in this
Newman convention, while its forward heat time is $+1/8$.
Neither number specifies the Newman constant of the zeta family:
the polynomial here has its own explicitly fixed initial datum.

\subsection{A global polynomial incompressible lift}

Write $J=DF$ and define all three inverse columns, with cyclic indices,
\[
 W_i=J^{-1}e_i
 =-\tfrac12\nabla F_{i+1}\times\nabla F_{i+2}.
\]
The scalar triple products and \eqref{eq:ifh-det} prove $JW_i=e_i$.
Commutation of mixed derivatives proves $\operatorname{div}W_i=0$.
Each column is polynomial. Moreover $W_iF_j=\delta_{ij}$, and
$[W_i,W_j]F_k=0$ for every $k$; invertibility of $J$ proves
$[W_i,W_j]=0$.

The exact heat lift is
\begin{equation}
 U=2W_1+6F_3W_2,\qquad JU=(2,6F_3,0)^{\mathsf T}.
 \label{eq:ifh-U}
\end{equation}
It is a polynomial vector field on the whole original $\R^3$.
Its ordinary Euclidean divergence is zero, since
\[
 \operatorname{div}U
 =2\operatorname{div}W_1+6F_3\operatorname{div}W_2+6W_2F_3=0.
\]
A global vector potential is also explicit:
\begin{equation}
 U=\curl\left(-F_2\nabla F_3-\tfrac32F_3^2\nabla F_1\right).
 \label{eq:ifh-potential}
\end{equation}
Expanding the curl gives
$-\nabla F_2\times\nabla F_3-3F_3\nabla F_3\times\nabla F_1$,
which is exactly \eqref{eq:ifh-U}.

The complete component formula can be kept polynomial without an inverse
denominator. With the same original $h=2-3xy-x^2w$, put
$Q=3h(1+xy)-2$. Then
\begin{align}
 U_x&=x^3(Q^2-3h),\notag\\
 U_y&=x(Q^2-Q-3h),\notag\\
 U_w&=3Q+(3h+3xy-7)Q^2+(21-9xy)h-9h^2.
 \label{eq:ifh-U-components}
\end{align}
These identities follow either by expanding \eqref{eq:ifh-U}, or by
substituting the inverse chart into the differential calculation below
and using $F_3=xh$. They hold on $x\ne0$ and then everywhere by polynomial
identity. At $x=0$ they give $U=(0,0,2)$, so the separate inverse point
has exactly the required boundary evolution.

Let $\varphi_\tau$ be the maximal local flow of $U$. Smooth polynomial
coefficients give local existence and uniqueness. Differentiating
$F(\varphi_\tau(q))$ using \eqref{eq:ifh-U}, and using uniqueness for the
affine target ODE, proves
\begin{equation}
 F\circ\varphi_\tau=\Phi_\tau\circ F
 \label{eq:ifh-flow-intertwining}
\end{equation}
on every common interval of definition. This identity includes the time
variable, not only a matching of fibres.
Differentiating it in the original initial point gives
\begin{equation}
 D\varphi_\tau(q)=
 J(\varphi_\tau(q))^{-1}D\Phi_\tau(F(q))J(q),
 \qquad \det D\varphi_\tau=1.
 \label{eq:ifh-material}
\end{equation}
Both determinants of $J$ are $-2$, so their ratio and
$\det D\Phi_\tau=1$ give the last equality without a limit argument.
For a retained initial covector $\xi_0$, its transported covector is
\[
 (D\varphi_\tau(q))^{-\mathsf T}\xi_0
 =J(\varphi_\tau(q))^{\mathsf T}
   (D\Phi_\tau)^{-\mathsf T}J(q)^{-\mathsf T}\xi_0.
\]
This is the full inverse-transpose material dictionary in these
coordinates.

There is an exact scalar operator identity as well. Define
\[
 \Pi(q,r)=F_3(q)r^3-2r^2+F_2(q)r-2F_1(q).
\]
Using $UF_1=2$, $UF_2=6F_3$, $UF_3=0$ gives, on the entire product
of the original space with the root coordinate,
\begin{equation}
 U\Pi=6F_3r-4=\partial_r^2\Pi.
 \label{eq:ifh-scalar-operator}
\end{equation}
Thus $\Pi(\varphi_\tau(q),r)$ obeys the exact forward heat equation
in the displayed auxiliary variable $r$. This variable remains the
inverse-root coordinate; it is not relabelled as a Euclidean spatial
diffusion coordinate of the three-dimensional fluid.

\subsection{The full inverse-chart dynamics and its preserved area form}

In \eqref{eq:ifh-chart}, $(r,\alpha,c)$ are actual coordinates on $x\ne0$,
with inverse $(y+1/x,1/x,F_3)$. Direct determinants of
\eqref{eq:ifh-chart} and \eqref{eq:ifh-target-chart} give
\[
 \det DH=-\alpha,\qquad \det D(F\circ H)=2\alpha.
\]
They multiply consistently with $\det J=-2$.
Put $q_r=3cr-2$, where the subscript is a name for this polynomial,
not a derivative. The complete lifted ODE in this chart is
\begin{equation}
 r'=-\frac{q_r}{\alpha},\qquad
 \alpha'=3c-\frac{q_r^2}{\alpha},\qquad c'=0.
 \label{eq:ifh-root-ode}
\end{equation}
Indeed $P_{rr}=2q_r$, $P_r=2\alpha$ give the first equation.
Differentiating
$\alpha=(3cr^2-4r+b)/2$ along $b'=6c$ gives
$\alpha'=q_r r'+3c$, which is the second equation.
Differentiating each component of $H$ then yields exactly $U\circ H$.
For example
$x'=-\alpha'/\alpha^2=-3cx^2+q_r^2x^3$ and
$y'=r'-\alpha'=(q_r^2-q_r)x-3c$.
For the third component the unaltered expression is
\[
 w'=-3\alpha r'
 +(10\alpha-3r-3c\alpha^2)\alpha',
\]
which gives \eqref{eq:ifh-U-components} after
$c=xh$, $q_r=3h(1+xy)-2$ are substituted.

The heat invariant $I=b-3ca$ becomes the specific polynomial
\[
 I(r,\alpha,c)=4r+2\alpha-6cr^2-3cr\alpha+3c^2r^3.
\]
Its two derivatives are
\[
 I_\alpha=-q_r,\qquad I_r=q_r^2-3c\alpha.
\]
Consequently, for each fixed real $c$, the nondegenerate two-form
\[
 \omega_c=\alpha\,dr\wedge d\alpha\quad(\alpha\ne0)
\]
satisfies
$\iota_{(r',\alpha')}\omega_c=dI$.
Indeed its contraction is
$\alpha r'\,d\alpha-\alpha\alpha'\,dr
 =I_\alpha\,d\alpha+I_r\,dr$.
This states the exact Hamiltonian convention and proves the claimed
Hamiltonian correspondence.

The preserved signed volume can also be checked directly:
\[
 \partial_r(\alpha r')+\partial_\alpha(\alpha\alpha')
 =\partial_r(-q_r)+\partial_\alpha(3c\alpha-q_r^2)=0.
\]
The Euclidean volume of the original source is
$|\alpha|\,dr\,d\alpha\,dc$ in this chart. On either component of
$\alpha\ne0$, the absolute value has a fixed sign relative to $\alpha$,
so this same calculation proves its preservation. The density tending
to zero at $\alpha=0$ describes a boundary of this inverse chart;
$x=1/\alpha$ shows that boundary is not a finite original coordinate point.

\subsection{The exact positive-viscosity fluid equation and escape}

On the target $\R^3_{a,b,c}$ with Euclidean metric, standard orientation,
and any retained viscosity $\nu>0$, set
\[
 v(a,b,c)=(2,6c,0),\qquad p=0,\qquad f=0.
\]
Every term of its stationary Navier--Stokes equation can be computed:
$\operatorname{div}v=0$,
$(v\cdot\nabla)v=(2\partial_a+6c\partial_b)v=0$, and
$\Delta v=0$. Its oriented curl is $(-6,0,0)$.
Its material flow is exactly \eqref{eq:ifh-target-flow}.

On the original source set $g=J^{\mathsf T}J$, using the standard
source orientation. This positive metric has $\det g=4$ and volume
$2\,dx\,dy\,dw$. In a local $F$ chart it is precisely the Euclidean
target metric. The Levi--Civita connection pulls back under this local
isometry: this follows from its metric compatibility and zero torsion,
which characterize that connection uniquely. The nonpositive vector
diffusion operator is
\[
 \mathcal L_g Y=\bigl[-(d\delta_g+\delta_gd)Y^\flat_g\bigr]^\sharp_g.
\]
The Hodge Laplacian commutes with local metric pullback, including an
orientation-reversing pullback, because the two orientation signs in
the codifferential cancel. In Euclidean coordinates this operator is
the componentwise Laplacian. Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{div}_gU=0,\qquad
 \nabla^g_UU=0,\qquad \mathcal L_gU=0.
\]
These prove the complete intrinsic stationary unforced
positive-viscosity Navier--Stokes equation with constant pressure.
Its vorticity retains the orientation sign:
\[
 \curl_g U=6W_1.
\]
In fact $\det J=-2$ makes
$*_gF^*\eta=-F^*(*_{\rm target}\eta)$.
Applying this to $d(v^\flat)$ negates the pullback of
$\curl v=-6e_1$, giving the displayed result.

There are explicit real finite-time escaping material trajectories.
On the initial fibre $(-1/4,0,0)$, for $\tau<1/8$ take
\[
 \gamma_\pm(\tau)=
 \left(\pm(1-8\tau)^{-1/2},
       \mp\tfrac32(1-8\tau)^{1/2},
       \tfrac{13}{2}(1-8\tau)\right).
\]
Substitution in the full map gives
$F(\gamma_\pm(\tau))=(-1/4+2\tau,0,0)$.
Differentiation and $J^{-1}$ prove $\gamma_\pm'=U(\gamma_\pm)$.
Their first coordinate escapes to infinity at $\tau=1/8$; their
$g$-speed is $|v|=2$ throughout. Hence their length from $\tau=0$
to the endpoint is $1/4$.
Their times approaching $1/8$ form a metric Cauchy sequence because
the remaining curve length tends to zero. It has no point limit:
a smooth positive Riemannian metric induces the ordinary coordinate
topology locally, while $x$ diverges. Thus the metric is incomplete.
The separate inverse point $(0,0,-1/4+2\tau)$ remains a finite
trajectory throughout. The coefficient heat collision and its
escaping branches have all been retained by these formulas.

\subsection{Its exact force in the original Euclidean metric}

The same polynomial $U$, now using the original Euclidean metric
and its component Laplacian, has stationary Navier--Stokes triples
precisely of the form
\[
 p=q,\qquad f=(U\cdot\nabla)U-\nu\Delta U+\nabla q .
\]
Substitution proves sufficiency; rearrangement of the momentum equation
proves necessity. Thus every pressure adjustment has been retained.

The force has the following exact curl on the original coordinate axis:
\begin{equation}
 \curl f(0,0,w)=(0,0,108\nu w).
 \label{eq:ifh-euclidean-force}
\end{equation}
Here is a direct derivative certificate from the full component formula.
Its transverse polynomial terms of degree at most three are
\[
 U_x=10x^3+R_x,\quad
 U_y=6x-12x^2y-18wx^3+R_y,\quad
 U_w=2-54xy-18wx^2+R_w,
\]
where the remainders are defined by these exact differences and
\[
 R_x,R_y\in(x,y)^5\R[x,y,w],\qquad
 R_w\in(x,y)^4\R[x,y,w].
\]
These ideal memberships follow by expanding
\eqref{eq:ifh-U-components}; thus no unspecified coefficient enters a
derivative of the required order. On the axis,
\[
 U=2e_3,\qquad
 DU=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\6&0&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}=:N .
\]
The derivative of the convective acceleration there is
$N^2+2\partial_w DU=0$, so its curl vanishes.
For the diffusive part,
\[
 \Delta\curl U(0,0,w)=(0,0,-108w):
\]
the third component is
$\partial_x\Delta U_y-\partial_y\Delta U_x=-108w$,
from $-18wx^3$, and the first two components vanish from the displayed
terms and remainder ideals. Commuting derivatives and using
$\curl\nabla q=0$ proves \eqref{eq:ifh-euclidean-force}, also
distributionally wherever a pressure is interpreted as a distribution.

Thus this exact same Euclidean velocity cannot have a rapidly decaying
smooth force for any pressure choice: its force curl is unbounded on
the axis for $\nu>0$. The source field is also a nonzero polynomial,
hence has infinite Euclidean $L^2$ norm. To see the latter fact directly,
a nonzero leading homogeneous component of a polynomial is bounded
away from zero on some open patch of the unit sphere. The full polynomial
then has a positive multiple of its leading radial power on the
corresponding cone for all sufficiently large radii. Radial integration
diverges. A nonzero constant component already gives the same conclusion
at degree zero. Here the field is nonconstant, as
$U_y=6x$ to first order at the origin proves, and a polynomial periodic
in every unit coordinate direction must be constant by the one-variable
polynomial difference identity. It therefore does not define the
required periodic velocity either.

These calculations identify both fluid realizations completely:
the target shear and its intrinsic lift are exact unforced viscous
solutions, while the original Euclidean lift requires the precisely
computed nondecaying force. The finite-time material escape belongs
to the incomplete metric realization. No classical Euclidean
Navier--Stokes counterexample or off-critical zeta zero is asserted.
