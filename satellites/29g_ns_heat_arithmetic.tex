\section{The source radial heat profile and its exact arithmetic transform}
\label{sec:ns-source-heat-arithmetic}

This section starts from the integral in Lemma A.6, equations
(A.32)--(A.38), of the preserved 165-page edition of
\emph{Finite Time Blowup for Navier--Stokes}~\cite{openaiNS2026release}.
It concerns that explicit heat-exterior profile, not an independent
verification of the manuscript's complete nonlinear construction.
The integral, all dilation coefficients, and the construction's parameter
range \(0<h<1/100\) are retained.

\subsection{The integral, its differential equation, and the physical field}

Fix \(0<h<1/100\), and put \(A=1/2+h\). Define, on the real
nonnegative half-line,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nh-profile}
 H_h(x)=\frac1{\Gamma(1+h)}
       \int_0^\infty e^{-v}v^h(1+xv)^{-h}\,dv .
\end{equation}
Every power in this formula uses its positive real base. For \(j\ge0\),
with \((h)_0=1\) and \((h)_j=h(h+1)\cdots(h+j-1)\), differentiation
under the integral gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nh-jets}
 H_h^{(j)}(x)=\frac{(-1)^j(h)_j}{\Gamma(1+h)}
 \int_0^\infty e^{-v}v^{h+j}(1+xv)^{-h-j}\,dv .
\end{equation}
Indeed, the absolute value of each differentiated integrand on \(x\ge0\)
is bounded by a constant times \(e^{-v}v^{h+j}\), which is integrable.
Dominated convergence also gives the right endpoint derivatives and their
continuity. Thus \(H_h\) is smooth on \([0,\infty)\), with
\[
 H_h(0)=1,\qquad H_h^{(j)}(0)=(-1)^j(h)_j(1+h)_j .
\]
This statement requires no convergent Taylor series at zero.

The exact source equation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nh-ode}
 x^2H_h''(x)+[1+2(1+h)x]H_h'(x)+h(1+h)H_h(x)=0 .
\end{equation}
Here is a direct proof retaining all its coefficients. Set
\[
 F(v)=e^{-v}v^h(1+xv)^{-h},\qquad
 B(v)=e^{-v}v^{1+h}(1+xv)^{-1-h}.
\]
Differentiation gives
\[
 B'(v)=F(v)\left\{\frac{1+h}{(1+xv)^2}
                         -\frac v{1+xv}\right\}.
\]
Substitution from \eqref{eq:nh-jets} into the left side of
\eqref{eq:nh-ode} gives \(h\Gamma(1+h)^{-1}\int_0^\infty B'(v)\,dv\):
the remaining factor reduces exactly by
\[
 (1+h)\left[1-\frac{2xv}{1+xv}
                   +\frac{x^2v^2}{(1+xv)^2}\right]
       -\frac v{1+xv}
 =\frac{1+h}{(1+xv)^2}-\frac v{1+xv}.
\]
Both boundary values of \(B\) vanish, by \(h>0\) at zero and
exponential decay at infinity. This proves the equation, including \(x=0\)
by right continuity.

Use a distinct symbol \(\sigma=r^2/2\) for the physical radial variable,
so that it is not confused with the Mellin argument below. The manuscript's
heat exterior is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nh-physical}
 K(r,t)=c_\infty \sigma^{-A}H_h(2\tau/\sigma),\qquad
 \sigma=r^2/2,\quad \tau=1-t,\quad c_\infty>0 ,
\end{equation}
on \(r>0,\ t<1\). Its swirl term must be retained. To derive its
physical equation, write \(Z=2\tau/\sigma\) and
\(W(\sigma,\tau)=\sigma^{-A}H_h(Z)\). Direct differentiation yields
\begin{align*}
 W_\sigma&=\sigma^{-A-1}[-AH_h-ZH_h'],\\
 W_{\sigma\sigma}
 &=\sigma^{-A-2}[A(A+1)H_h+2(A+1)ZH_h'+Z^2H_h''],\\
 \partial_t W&=-2\sigma^{-A-1}H_h'.
\end{align*}
Since \(\partial_r=r\partial_\sigma\),
\[
 (\partial_{rr}+r^{-1}\partial_r-r^{-2})W
 =2\sigma W_{\sigma\sigma}+2W_\sigma-\frac{W}{2\sigma}
 =2\sigma^{-A-1}
   [Z^2H_h''+(2A+1)ZH_h'+(A^2-1/4)H_h].
\]
The identities \(2A+1=2(1+h)\) and \(A^2-1/4=h(1+h)\), together
with \eqref{eq:nh-ode}, prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nh-swirl-heat}
 \partial_tK=(\partial_{rr}+r^{-1}\partial_r-r^{-2})K .
\end{equation}
The equation concerns this explicitly defined exterior field; it does not
replace the nonlinear convection, pressure, or stress corrections of the
full construction.

\subsection{Two exact dilation differences and their derivative bounds}

Define
\begin{align}
 \phi_h(x)&=H_h(x)-2^hH_h(2x),\label{eq:nh-phi}\\
 \psi_h(x)&=\phi_h(x)-\tfrac12\phi_h(x/2)\notag\\
 &=(1+2^{h-1})H_h(x)-2^hH_h(2x)-\tfrac12H_h(x/2).
 \label{eq:nh-psi}
\end{align}
All three source evaluations and coefficients in the last line are retained.
We first prove the all-order bounds needed for every subsequent integral
and inverse series.

For \(a>0\) put
\[
 I_a(\varepsilon)=\int_0^\infty e^{-v}
                  \left(\frac v{v+\varepsilon}\right)^a\,dv .
\]
For \(0<\varepsilon\le1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nh-I-bound}
 0\le1-I_a(\varepsilon)
 \le\varepsilon[1+a+a\log(1/\varepsilon)].
\end{equation}
To prove it, integrate
\[
 0\le1-(1+\varepsilon/v)^{-a}
       \le\min(1,a\varepsilon/v).
\]
The second inequality follows by integrating
\(a(1+w)^{-a-1}\le a\). On \(0<v<\varepsilon\) use the bound \(1\);
on \(\varepsilon<v<1\) use \(a\varepsilon/v\); on \(v>1\) use the
latter bound with \(e^{-v}/v\le e^{-v}\). Their integrals give
\eqref{eq:nh-I-bound}.

Let \(c_j=(-1)^j(h)_j/\Gamma(1+h)\). Equation \eqref{eq:nh-jets}
has the exact form
\[
 H_h^{(j)}(x)=c_jx^{-h-j}I_{h+j}(1/x),
\]
and differentiation of \eqref{eq:nh-phi} therefore gives the cancellation
\[
 \phi_h^{(j)}(x)
 =c_jx^{-h-j}\{I_{h+j}(1/x)-I_{h+j}(1/(2x))\}.
\]
Using \eqref{eq:nh-I-bound} in both terms, and then differentiating
\eqref{eq:nh-psi}, proves, for every \(j\ge0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nh-all-tail}
 |\phi_h^{(j)}(x)|+|\psi_h^{(j)}(x)|
 \le C_{h,j}x^{-1-h-j}(1+\log x)\qquad(x\ge1).
\end{equation}
At zero their derivatives are bounded by \eqref{eq:nh-jets}. In particular
\(\phi_h,\psi_h\), and every derivative of each, are integrable.
An ordinary substitution, now justified by that integrability, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nh-mean-zero}
 \int_0^\infty\psi_h(x)\,dx
 =\int_0^\infty\phi_h(x)\,dx
       -\tfrac12\,2\int_0^\infty\phi_h(y)\,dy=0 .
\end{equation}
Their endpoint values are
\[
 \phi_h(0)=1-2^h,\qquad \psi_h(0)=\tfrac12(1-2^h).
\]

We will also need a lower-order asymptotic with a signed leading term.
For fixed \(a>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nh-I-asymptotic}
 I_a(\varepsilon)=1-a\varepsilon\log(1/\varepsilon)+O_a(\varepsilon).
\end{equation}
The intervals \(v<\varepsilon\) and \(v>1\) contribute \(O_a(\varepsilon)\)
to \(1-I_a\). On the middle interval Taylor's formula on \(0\le w\le1\)
gives
\(1-(1+w)^{-a}=aw+O_a(w^2)\). Hence its contribution is
\[
 a\varepsilon\int_\varepsilon^1\frac{e^{-v}}v\,dv
       +O_a\left(\varepsilon^2\int_\varepsilon^1v^{-2}\,dv\right)
 =a\varepsilon\log(1/\varepsilon)+O_a(\varepsilon).
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:nh-I-asymptotic}, rather than assuming an asymptotic
expansion under a nonuniform integral. Substitution first into \(H_h\),
then into the two differences, yields
\begin{align}
 H_h(x)&=\frac{x^{-h}}{\Gamma(1+h)}
       -\frac{h\,x^{-1-h}\log x}{\Gamma(1+h)}
       +O_h(x^{-1-h}),\notag\\
 \phi_h(x)&=-\frac{h\,x^{-1-h}\log x}{2\Gamma(1+h)}
       +O_h(x^{-1-h}),\notag\\
 \psi_h(x)&=a_hx^{-1-h}\log x+O_h(x^{-1-h}),\qquad
 a_h=\frac{h(2^h-1)}{2\Gamma(1+h)}>0 .
 \label{eq:nh-signed-tail}
\end{align}

\subsection{The arithmetic trace, including every endpoint derivative}

For \(x>0\), define the genuine arithmetic dilation sum
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nh-S}
 S_h(x)=\sum_{n\ge1}\psi_h(nx).
\end{equation}
After \(j\) differentiations its \(n\)-th term is
\(n^j\psi_h^{(j)}(nx)\). On every compact subinterval of \((0,\infty)\),
\eqref{eq:nh-all-tail} bounds it, apart from finitely many terms, by
\(C n^{-1-h}(1+\log n)\). This is summable, so all differentiated
series converge locally uniformly and \(S_h\) is smooth there. For \(x\ge1\),
the same argument gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nh-S-all-tail}
 S_h^{(j)}(x)=O_{h,j}(x^{-1-h-j}(1+\log x)).
\end{equation}
Using the uniform remainder in \eqref{eq:nh-signed-tail} and summing
absolutely further proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nh-S-signed-tail}
 S_h(x)=a_h\zeta(1+h)x^{-1-h}\log x+O_h(x^{-1-h}).
\end{equation}
Indeed \(\sum n^{-1-h}\log n<\infty\), and the sum of the individual
remainders is bounded by \(C_hx^{-1-h}\zeta(1+h)\).

We prove the needed endpoint summation formula explicitly. Let
\(B_m(v)\) be the Bernoulli polynomials defined by \(B_0=1\),
\(B_m'=mB_{m-1}\), and \(\int_0^1B_m(v)\,dv=0\) for \(m\ge1\);
write \(B_m=B_m(0)\). In particular \(B_1(v)=v-1/2\).
For \(m\ge2\) integration of \(B_m'\) shows \(B_m(1)=B_m(0)\).
Uniqueness of this defining recursion proves
\(B_m(1-v)=(-1)^mB_m(v)\), so \(B_m(0)=0\) for odd \(m\ge3\).
Let \(\widetilde B_m(v)=B_m(\{v\})\); the values at the integers
are immaterial inside integrals.

Suppose \(f\) and its derivatives through order \(2M\) are integrable,
have the indicated one-sided values at zero, and tend to zero at infinity
through order \(2M-1\). Summing integration by parts over the intervals
\([nx,(n+1)x]\) gives
\[
 \sum_{n\ge1}f(nx)
 =x^{-1}\int_0^\infty f(v)\,dv-\frac{f(0)}2
       +\int_0^\infty\widetilde B_1(v/x)f'(v)\,dv .
\]
For example, the integral on one interval equals the half-sum of its
endpoint values minus \(x^{-1}\) times its integral. For every subsequent
integration the identity
\(\frac{d}{dv}\widetilde B_m(v/x)=
m x^{-1}\widetilde B_{m-1}(v/x)\) holds between endpoints.
The endpoint values match for \(m\ge2\), and the odd endpoint constants
vanish as just proved. Repeating these integrations gives
\begin{align}
 \sum_{n\ge1}f(nx)
 &=x^{-1}\int_0^\infty f(v)\,dv-\frac{f(0)}2
   -\sum_{k=1}^M\frac{B_{2k}}{(2k)!}
           f^{(2k-1)}(0)x^{2k-1}+R_M(f,x),\label{eq:nh-EM}\\
 |R_M(f,x)|&\le
 \frac{\|B_{2M}\|_{L^\infty[0,1]}}{(2M)!}
 x^{2M-1}\int_0^\infty|f^{(2M)}(v)|\,dv .\notag
\end{align}
The remainder is the negative of
\(x^{2M-1}(2M)!^{-1}\int\widetilde B_{2M}(v/x)f^{(2M)}(v)\,dv\);
its displayed bound justifies the infinite endpoint limit as well.

For every fixed \(j\ge0\), apply this formula to
\[
 f_j(v)=v^j\psi_h^{(j)}(v),\qquad
 x^j S_h^{(j)}(x)=\sum_{n\ge1} f_j(nx).
\]
Each derivative \(f_j^{(k)}\) is a finite sum of constant multiples of
\(v^{j-\ell}\psi_h^{(j+k-\ell)}(v)\), with
\(0\le\ell\le\min(j,k)\). It is bounded near zero and
\(O(v^{-1-h-k}(1+\log v))\) at infinity, so every integrability
and boundary assumption of \eqref{eq:nh-EM} holds.
Integrating by parts \(j\) times, with every boundary term zero by these
same estimates, gives
\[
 \int_0^\infty f_j(v)\,dv
   =(-1)^j j!\int_0^\infty\psi_h(v)\,dv=0.
\]
At the origin
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nh-fj-endpoint}
 f_j^{(r)}(0)=
 \begin{cases}
  0,&r<j,\\[2pt]
  \dfrac{r!}{(r-j)!}\psi_h^{(r)}(0),&r\ge j.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Thus, after division by \(x^j\), every nonzero term of
\eqref{eq:nh-EM} has a nonnegative power of \(x\). Choose
\(2M-1>j\); its remainder tends to zero after this division.
This proves that \(S_h^{(j)}\) is bounded at zero for every \(j\) and,
more precisely, has a finite limit. With
\(C_{h,j}\) denoting that limit,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nh-S-jets-zero}
 C_{h,0}=-\frac{\psi_h(0)}2=\frac{2^h-1}{4},\qquad
 C_{h,j}=-\frac{B_{j+1}}{j+1}\psi_h^{(j)}(0)\quad(j\ge1).
\end{equation}
For even \(j\ge2\) the last expression is zero. The formula follows
from \eqref{eq:nh-fj-endpoint}: the possible constant term has
\(2k-1=j\), and its coefficient is
\(-B_{j+1}j!/(j+1)!\).

These are actual smooth endpoint jets, not merely unrelated limits.
For \(0<a<x\), the fundamental theorem of calculus gives
\(S_h^{(j)}(x)-S_h^{(j)}(a)=
\int_a^x S_h^{(j+1)}(v)\,dv\). Boundedness permits \(a\downarrow0\),
and continuity of the limiting derivative proves that extending each
\(S_h^{(j)}\) by \(C_{h,j}\) preserves the derivative relation.
Induction proves \(S_h\in C^\infty([0,\infty))\). In particular
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nh-S-zero}
 S_h(x)=\frac{2^h-1}{4}+O_h(x)\qquad(x\downarrow0).
\end{equation}
The endpoint constant is positive. It is not a scalar Weil value.

\subsection{The exact Mellin detector and its removable points}

Use the Mellin convention
\[
 \mathcal M f(s)=\int_0^\infty f(x)x^{s-1}\,dx,\qquad
 x^{s-1}=\exp((s-1)\log x),
\]
where \(s\in\mathbb C\) and \(\log x\) is real.
For \(0<\Re s<h\), absolute interchange in \eqref{eq:nh-profile}
and substitution \(w=xv\) give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nh-MH}
 \mathcal M H_h(s)=
 G_h(s):=\frac{\Gamma(s)\Gamma(h-s)\Gamma(1+h-s)}
                    {\Gamma(h)\Gamma(1+h)} .
\end{equation}
For completeness, the inner integral is
\(\int_0^\infty w^{s-1}(1+w)^{-h}dw\).
In the absolutely convergent product integral
\(\Gamma(s)\Gamma(h-s)\), substitute
\((a,b)=(ry,r(1-y))\), \(r>0,\ 0<y<1\); its Jacobian is \(r\).
The result is \(\Gamma(h)\int_0^1y^{s-1}(1-y)^{h-s-1}dy\).
Substitution \(w=y/(1-y)\) proves that the inner integral is
\(\Gamma(s)\Gamma(h-s)/\Gamma(h)\). The remaining \(v\)-integral
is \(\Gamma(1+h-s)\), proving \eqref{eq:nh-MH}.

For \(a>0\), substitution gives exactly
\(\mathcal M[f(a\,\cdot)](s)=a^{-s}\mathcal M f(s)\).
Consequently, initially on \(0<\Re s<h\),
\begin{align}
 \mathcal M\phi_h(s)&=(1-2^{h-s})G_h(s),\label{eq:nh-Mphi}\\
 \mathcal M\psi_h(s)&=(1-2^{s-1})(1-2^{h-s})G_h(s).
 \label{eq:nh-Mpsi}
\end{align}
The bounds already proved make the left sides holomorphic on
\(0<\Re s<1+h\), with locally uniform logarithmic moments justifying
all complex derivatives. On the right, the only gamma pole in that open
strip is at \(s=h\). The factor \(1-2^{h-s}\) removes it, since
\[
 \lim_{s\to h}(1-2^{h-s})\Gamma(h-s)=-\log2.
\]
The identity theorem therefore proves \eqref{eq:nh-Mphi} and
\eqref{eq:nh-Mpsi} throughout the enlarged strip, without interchanging
a divergent source integral. In particular their removable values are
\begin{align}
 \mathcal M\phi_h(h)&=-\frac{\log2}{\Gamma(1+h)},&
 \mathcal M\psi_h(h)&=
 -\frac{(1-2^{h-1})\log2}{\Gamma(1+h)},\label{eq:nh-values-h}\\
 \mathcal M\phi_h(1)&=
 \frac{1-2^{h-1}}{h(h-1)},&
 \mathcal M\psi_h(1)&=0.\label{eq:nh-values-one}
\end{align}
The last equality agrees with the direct mean-zero calculation.

On the nonempty strip \(1<\Re s<1+h\), the sum defining \(S_h\)
can be integrated termwise absolutely: the sum of the absolute Mellin
integrals is
\(\zeta(\Re s)\int_0^\infty|\psi_h(x)|x^{\Re s-1}dx<\infty\).
It follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nh-detector}
 \mathcal M S_h(s)=\zeta(s)(1-2^{s-1})(1-2^{h-s})
       \frac{\Gamma(s)\Gamma(h-s)\Gamma(1+h-s)}
                    {\Gamma(h)\Gamma(1+h)} .
\end{equation}
Equations \eqref{eq:nh-S-zero} and \eqref{eq:nh-S-all-tail} make its
left side holomorphic on \(0<\Re s<1+h\). The right side's point at
\(h\) is removable as above, and its point at \(1\) is removable because
\(\zeta(s)\) has residue \(1\) there and
\(1-2^{s-1}=-(s-1)\log2+O((s-1)^2)\).
The identity therefore holds on the full open strip. Its values are
\begin{align}
 \mathcal M S_h(h)
 &=-\frac{\zeta(h)(1-2^{h-1})\log2}{\Gamma(1+h)},\notag\\
 \mathcal M S_h(1)
 &=\frac{(1-2^{h-1})\log2}{h(1-h)}>0 .
 \label{eq:nh-detector-removable}
\end{align}
Here \(\Gamma(h-1)/\Gamma(1+h)=1/[h(h-1)]\) supplies the second
value; no sign or scale is suppressed.

For fixed \(h\), throughout
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nh-zero-domain}
 h<\Re s<1
\end{equation}
the multiplier of \(\zeta(s)\) in \eqref{eq:nh-detector} is holomorphic
and nowhere zero. The dilation factors can vanish only on the lines
\(\Re s=h\) and \(\Re s=1\), respectively. The gamma functions
have no zeros, and their poles do not lie in \eqref{eq:nh-zero-domain}.
If a point \(s_0\) in that strip is a zero of order \(m\) of \(\zeta\),
write \(\zeta(s)=(s-s_0)^m a(s)\), \(a(s_0)\ne0\); multiplication
by the nonvanishing holomorphic factor preserves this order. Division
proves the converse. Thus the detector preserves every zero and its
exact multiplicity on \eqref{eq:nh-zero-domain}. The formula itself
does not supply a new zero.

The absolute Mellin strip is exactly \(0<\Re s<1+h\).
Sufficiency was proved above. Necessity follows from the positive
nonzero endpoint constant \eqref{eq:nh-S-zero} at zero and the positive
leading term \eqref{eq:nh-S-signed-tail} at infinity: the absolute
integral diverges for \(\Re s\le0\) at zero and for
\(\Re s\ge1+h\) at infinity. This is the domain of the defining
integral, not a denial of the meromorphic continuation supplied by
\eqref{eq:nh-detector}.

\subsection{All three inverse maps and their convergence}

The source kernel is recovered by the following explicit series:
\begin{align}
 \phi_h(x)&=\sum_{j\ge0}2^{-j}\psi_h(x/2^j),\label{eq:nh-inv-one}\\
 H_h(x)&=-\sum_{k\ge1}2^{-kh}\phi_h(x/2^k),\label{eq:nh-inv-two}\\
 \psi_h(x)&=\sum_{n\ge1}\mu(n)S_h(nx).\label{eq:nh-inv-three}
\end{align}
The first finite sum, through \(j=J\), telescopes exactly to
\[
 \phi_h(x)-2^{-J-1}\phi_h(x/2^{J+1}).
\]
The term subtracted tends to zero because \(\phi_h\) is bounded near
zero. The negative second finite sum, through \(k=K\), equals
\[
 H_h(x)-2^{-Kh}H_h(x/2^K);
\]
its last term tends to zero because \(h>0\) and \(H_h(0)=1\).
For derivative order \(\ell\), the terms of these two series have the
additional factors \(2^{-j\ell}\) and \(2^{-k\ell}\), respectively.
All source derivatives are bounded on bounded intervals, so the series
and every differentiated series converge uniformly on such intervals.
Their remainders vanish there as well. Thus the two inverse identities
hold smoothly, including at zero. Boundedness at zero also makes the
inverses unique: iterating either corresponding homogeneous dilation
equation gives its value times \(2^{-J}\), respectively \(2^{-Jh}\),
at a shrinking argument, and hence zero.

For the arithmetic inverse, at fixed \(x>0\) the double series is
absolutely bounded by
\[
 \sum_{a,b\ge1}|\psi_h(abx)|
 =\sum_{k\ge1}d(k)|\psi_h(kx)|
 \le C_x\sum_{k\ge1}d(k)k^{-1-h}(1+\log k)<\infty.
\]
To verify the last convergence directly, set \(k=ab\), use
\(\log(ab)=\log a+\log b\), and factor into products of
\(\sum a^{-1-h}\) and \(\sum a^{-1-h}\log a\), both convergent
by the integral test. Since \(|\mu(a)|\le1\), exchanging sums is
legitimate and gives
\[
 \sum_{a\ge1}\mu(a)S_h(ax)
 =\sum_{k\ge1}\psi_h(kx)\sum_{a\mid k}\mu(a)=\psi_h(x).
\]
The divisor coefficient is \(1\) for \(k=1\), while for \(k>1\) it is
\(\prod_{p\mid k}(1-1)=0\), by the defining squarefree expansion of
\(\mu\). For derivative order \(\ell\), the double-series terms have
the factor \((ab)^\ell\psi_h^{(\ell)}(abx)\); the same majorant works
by \eqref{eq:nh-all-tail}, locally uniformly in \(x>0\).
Thus \eqref{eq:nh-inv-three} is also an equality of smooth functions
with all derivatives, and is an actual inverse on this decay class.
The three steps preserve the original source through the arithmetic
sum; they are not merely identities between formal series.

\subsection{The logarithmic Fourier map and its heat domain}

For \(0<c<1+h\) define the logarithmic input
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nh-log-input}
 g_{h,c}(u)=e^{cu}S_h(e^u),\qquad u\in\mathbb R,
\end{equation}
and use the same minus-Fourier convention as the earlier sections.
The change of variable \(x=e^u\) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nh-fourier}
 \mathscr Fg_{h,c}(s)=\mathcal M S_h(c-is).
\end{equation}
This is an exact coordinate map, including its scale and vertical line.
The domain of absolute convergence of the defining Fourier integral
is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nh-fourier-strip}
 -c<\Im s<1+h-c .
\end{equation}
Indeed \(\Re(c-is)=c+\Im s\); the exact Mellin strip just proved
therefore supplies both necessity and sufficiency.

The real input \(g_{h,c}\) is a Schwartz function, despite the finite
absolute complex strip. To check every seminorm, differentiate
\(e^{cu}S_h(e^u)\) repeatedly. Each derivative is a finite linear
combination of \(e^{cu}x^jS_h^{(j)}(x)\), \(x=e^u\), with
\(0\le j\le k\) for derivative order \(k\).
At \(u\to-\infty\), every \(S_h^{(j)}\) is bounded by the complete
endpoint proof, so these derivatives are \(O_{h,c,k}(e^{cu})\).
At \(u\to+\infty\), \eqref{eq:nh-S-all-tail} bounds them by
\[
 O_{h,c,k}((1+u)e^{-(1+h-c)u}).
\]
Multiplication by any power of \(|u|\) preserves boundedness and
decay at both ends. This proves all Schwartz seminorm estimates.
Fourier inversion recovers \(g_{h,c}\). To check the inversion directly
on this domain, two integrations by parts give
\(\mathscr Fg_{h,c}(s)=O((1+|s|)^{-2})\) on the real axis:
the boundary terms vanish and \(g_{h,c}''\) is integrable.
Insert the factor \(e^{-\varepsilon s^2}\), \(\varepsilon>0\),
in the inverse integral. Absolute Fubini and the Gaussian integral
identify it with
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}
 \frac{e^{-(u-v)^2/(4\varepsilon)}}{\sqrt{4\pi\varepsilon}}\,
 g_{h,c}(v)\,dv.
\]
This tends to \(g_{h,c}(u)\): split at \(|u-v|=\delta\), use
continuity on the inner interval, and use boundedness of \(g_{h,c}\)
and the vanishing Gaussian tail outside it. On the Fourier side the
integrable bound just proved permits \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\)
by dominated convergence. Thus, explicitly on the real axis,
\[
 g_{h,c}(u)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
               \mathcal M S_h(c-is)e^{isu}\,ds,\qquad
 S_h(x)=x^{-c}g_{h,c}(\log x).
\]
Together with \eqref{eq:nh-inv-one}--\eqref{eq:nh-inv-three}, these
are inverse coordinates all the way back to \(H_h\).

The earlier arithmetic heat convention multiplies a logarithmic input
by \(e^{tu^2/4}\), corresponding to generator
\(-\partial_s^2/4\) on its Fourier transform. On this actual input
the domain must be checked, not inferred from its Schwartz property.
Equation \eqref{eq:nh-S-zero} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nh-left-asymptotic}
 g_{h,c}(u)\sim \frac{2^h-1}{4}e^{cu}\qquad(u\to-\infty).
\end{equation}
For any complex \(t\) with \(\Re t>0\) and any complex \(s\),
the absolute integrand
\(\lvert e^{tu^2/4}g_{h,c}(u)e^{-isu}\rvert\)
is therefore bounded below on a sufficiently negative half-line by
a positive constant times
\[
 \exp\bigl((\Re t)u^2/4+(c+\Im s)u\bigr).
\]
Its integral diverges. In particular the source input does not belong
to the superexponentially decreasing two-sided heat domain used for
the theta input. This explicit endpoint obstruction concerns that
defining integral, not every possible analytic continuation.

For \(\Re t<0\), on the other hand, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nh-negative-heat}
 F_{h,c}(s,t)=\int_{\mathbb R}
 e^{tu^2/4}g_{h,c}(u)e^{-isu}\,du .
\end{equation}
On any compact set of such \((s,t)\), there is a uniform negative
quadratic coefficient in \(u\). Every differentiated integrand is
bounded by an integrable Gaussian times an exponential-linear factor
and a fixed polynomial in \(|u|\), by the preceding endpoint estimates.
Dominated differentiation proves joint holomorphy, entirety in \(s\),
and the exact equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nh-spectral-heat}
 \partial_tF_{h,c}(s,t)=-\tfrac14\partial_s^2F_{h,c}(s,t).
\end{equation}
Both sides equal the integral with multiplier \(u^2/4\).
As \(t\) tends to zero through \(\Re t<0\), dominated convergence
on each compact substrip of \eqref{eq:nh-fourier-strip} gives the
boundary value \eqref{eq:nh-fourier}, with every \(s\)-derivative.
On \(\Re t=0\) absolute convergence is again exactly
\eqref{eq:nh-fourier-strip}, since the heat multiplier has modulus one.
No entire-time heat continuation is asserted.

The constructions above give an invertible, source-specific route from the
actual radial heat exterior to an arithmetic transform with exactly the
zeta zero multiplicities on \eqref{eq:nh-zero-domain}. They establish
neither a new off-critical-line zero nor a negative Weil witness. The
physical exterior equation \eqref{eq:nh-swirl-heat} and the valid
spectral equation \eqref{eq:nh-spectral-heat} have both been proved with
their actual domains; the full nonlinear Navier--Stokes construction
has not been replaced by either one.
