\section{The complete radial coefficient recursion and its finite source dependence}
\label{sec:ns-finite-radial-recursion}

The preceding second-coefficient cancellation extends to every fixed radial
order. We derive the exact recursion from the released source
\cite[(4.2), (5.2)--(5.6), Lemma 5.1]{openaiNS2026release}, including
the pressure equation and the radial cutoff commutator. The resulting
finiteness is in correction order at a fixed Taylor degree. It is not
convergence of the uncut formal series, a finite approximation to the
whole field, or a replacement of its force by zero.

\subsection{Coefficients, source data and the exact derivative operators}

Keep \(A=1/2+h\), \(D=1/2-h\), \(k=1+h\), \(b=-k\),
\(c=-A\), \(e=-2A\), \(\lambda_n=2nh\), \(d=1-\eta^2\)
and \(L=1-2h\eta^2\). The physical variables and their inverse are
\eqref{nsr:coordinates}; in particular \(X=\sigma/(\nu q)\),
\(\sigma=r^2/2\), and \(z=\sqrt\nu q^D\eta\).
For a smooth profile \(f\), let \([X^m]f=\partial_X^mf(0,\eta)/m!\).
Define functions of \(\eta\) by
\[
 \phi_{n,m}=[X^m]\varphi_n,\quad u_{n,m}=[X^m]U_n,\quad
 \pi_{n,m}=[X^m]\Pi_n,\quad v_{n,l}=[X^l]V_n\quad(l\geq1).
\]
Every negative correction index means zero. The source axis conditions are
\[
 \phi_{0,0}=\varphi,\quad u_{0,0}=U=4\eta+j_0,\quad
 \pi_{0,0}=\Pi_*(\eta),\qquad
 \phi_{n,0}=u_{n,0}=\pi_{n,0}=0\quad(n\geq1),\quad V_n(0,\eta)=0.
\]
Here \(\varphi\) is exactly \eqref{nsr:profiles}. The pressure datum is
the fixed source function
\[
 \Pi_*(\eta)=-\int_0^\infty\frac{E_o(X,\eta)^2}{2X}\,dX
\]
from source (4.31), pp.~35--36, with its already chosen exterior profile.
It is retained as that datum, not replaced by zero or a freely chosen
constant. Its parameter analyticity and the existence of all the source
profiles are imported source conclusions; the coefficient identities below
are direct consequences proved here.

On functions of \(\eta\), set
\[
 t_\beta f=\frac{-\beta f+D\eta f'}L,\qquad
 z_\beta f=\frac{2\beta\eta f+df'}L,
\]
where primes in these two formulas mean \(\eta\) derivatives. The full
source operators, which also differentiate \(X\), are
\[
 T_a=L^{-1}(-a+D\eta\partial_\eta+X\partial_X),\qquad
 Z_a=L^{-1}(2a\eta+d\partial_\eta-2\eta X\partial_X).
\]
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{nfr:operators}
 [X^m]T_af=t_{a-m}[X^m]f,\qquad
 [X^m]Z_af=z_{a-m}[X^m]f,\qquad
 [X^m]Z_{a-D}Z_af=z_{a-D-m}z_{a-m}[X^m]f.
\end{equation}
Indeed \([X^m]X\partial_Xf=m[X^m]f\); parameter differentiation
commutes with finite radial differentiation. This proves all three
identities, including the order of composition. We abbreviate
\[
 t_{a,n,m}=t_{a+\lambda_n-m},\quad z_{a,n,m}=z_{a+\lambda_n-m},\quad
 z^{[2]}_{a,n,m}=z_{a+\lambda_n-D-m}z_{a+\lambda_n-m}.
\]
The notation names operators, not scalar factors.

\subsection{All coupled coefficient equations with their finite sums}

All sums below have nonnegative \(i,j,a_0,b_0\) with the displayed
constraints; thus they are finite. Put \(\omega_{n,l}=[X^l]\Omega_n\).
Incompressibility gives
\begin{equation}\label{nfr:V}
 v_{n,m+1}=-\frac{z_{c,n,m}u_{n,m}}{m+1}.
\end{equation}
For \(n,m\geq0\), the angular, axial and pressure recursions are
\begin{align}
 2(m+1)(m+2)\phi_{n,m+1}
 &=t_{b,n,m}\phi_{n,m}-z^{[2]}_{b,n-1,m}\phi_{n-1,m}\notag\\
 &\quad+\sum_{\substack{i+j=n\\a_0+b_0=m}}
 \left((b_0+1)v_{i,a_0+1}\phi_{j,b_0}
                +u_{i,a_0}z_{b,j,b_0}\phi_{j,b_0}\right),
 \label{nfr:phi}\\
 2(m+1)^2u_{n,m+1}
 &=t_{c,n,m}u_{n,m}+z_{e,n,m}\pi_{n,m}
                -z^{[2]}_{c,n-1,m}u_{n-1,m}\notag\\
 &\quad+\sum_{\substack{i+j=n\\a_0+b_0=m}}
 \left(b_0v_{i,a_0+1}u_{j,b_0}
                +u_{i,a_0}z_{c,j,b_0}u_{j,b_0}\right),
 \label{nfr:U}\\
 (m+1)\pi_{n,m+1}
 &=C^{-2}\sum_{\substack{i+j=n\\a_0+b_0=m}}
                       \phi_{i,a_0}\phi_{j,b_0}
                  -\tfrac12\omega_{n-1,m+1}.
 \label{nfr:pressure}
\end{align}
Every term in the pressure source is specified by
\begin{align}
 \omega_{n,m+1}
 &=t_{0,n,m+1}v_{n,m+1}
       -2(m+2)(m+1)v_{n,m+2}
       -z^{[2]}_{0,n-1,m+1}v_{n-1,m+1}\notag\\
 &\quad+\sum_{\substack{i+j=n\\a_0+b_0=m}}
 \left((b_0+\tfrac12)v_{i,a_0+1}v_{j,b_0+1}
                  +u_{i,a_0}z_{0,j,b_0+1}v_{j,b_0+1}\right).
 \label{nfr:Omega}
\end{align}
For a negative first index \(\omega\) is zero. At \(n=0\), these
identities use the source's leading stress-zero equations (4.7), (4.13)
on the inner interval, so that no positive-order existence result is
being applied to order zero.

Here is a coefficient proof of every factor. Integrating
\(\partial_XV_n=-Z_{c+\lambda_n}U_n\) with \(V_n(0)=0\)
proves \eqref{nfr:V}. Applying \([X^m]\) to
\(2(X\varphi_n''+2\varphi_n')\) and \(2(XU_n''+U_n')\)
gives respectively \(2(m+1)(m+2)\phi_{n,m+1}\) and
\(2(m+1)^2u_{n,m+1}\), where these primes mean \(X\)
derivatives. A term \(v_{i,a_0+1}X^{a_0+1}\) times
\(\varphi_j'+\varphi_j/X\) supplies
\((b_0+1)v_{i,a_0+1}\phi_{j,b_0}X^{a_0+b_0}\).
The same product with \(U_j'\) has factor \(b_0\).
Equation \eqref{nfr:operators} supplies the remaining angular and axial
operators and all ordered \(U_iZ\) products. The coefficient of
\(X^m\) in \(\Omega_{n-1}/X\) is \(\omega_{n-1,m+1}\),
giving \eqref{nfr:pressure}. Finally
\(V_j'-V_j/(2X)\) has coefficient
\((b_0+1/2)v_{j,b_0+1}\) at degree \(b_0\), and
\([X^{m+1}](-2XV_n'')=-2(m+2)(m+1)v_{n,m+2}\).
These give precisely \eqref{nfr:Omega}, with its shifted axial viscosity.
Products of Taylor coefficients here follow from finite Leibniz rules.
No infinite Taylor expansion is substituted into a differential equation.

\subsection{Finite correction support at every radial degree}

\begin{theorem}\label{nfr:support}
For the full sequence of source profiles and every \(m\geq0\),
\[
 \phi_{n,m}=u_{n,m}=\pi_{n,m}=v_{n,m+1}=0\quad(n>m).
\]
At degree zero the stronger \(\pi_{n,0}=0\) holds for every \(n>0\).
The same zero identities hold after every fixed parameter derivative.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At \(m=0\) the axis data and \eqref{nfr:V} prove the assertions.
Assume they hold at all degrees at most \(m\). In each product on
the right of \eqref{nfr:phi}, a nonzero first factor requires
\(i\leq a_0\), and a nonzero second factor requires \(j\leq b_0\).
Their sum has \(n=i+j\leq a_0+b_0=m\). The time term vanishes
for \(n>m\), and the shifted viscous term for \(n>m+1\).
Thus \(\phi_{n,m+1}=0\) if \(n>m+1\).
For \eqref{nfr:U} the products have the same bound; its pressure term
vanishes when \(n>m\) by the degree-\(m\) pressure assertion.
The other two terms have the just stated bounds. Therefore
\(u_{n,m+1}=0\) for \(n>m+1\), and \eqref{nfr:V} gives
\(v_{n,m+2}=0\) for the same indices.

Now use the newly proved axial coefficient in \eqref{nfr:Omega}.
The first term and both product sums vanish for \(n>m\).
The radial viscous term vanishes for \(n>m+1\), and the shifted
axial viscous term also vanishes for \(n>m+1\).
Hence \(\omega_{n,m+1}=0\) for \(n>m+1\).
The product in \eqref{nfr:pressure} vanishes for \(n>m\), whereas
\(\omega_{n-1,m+1}\) vanishes for \(n>m+2\).
This already gives \(\pi_{n,m+1}=0\) for \(n>m+2\), but the
remaining boundary index cancels exactly. Put
\(\beta=c+\lambda_m-m\). In \eqref{nfr:U} at \(n=m+1\),
the pressure, time and product terms are zero by the induction assumption.
Therefore
\[
 u_{m+1,m+1}=-\frac{z_{\beta-D}z_\beta u_{m,m}}{2(m+1)^2},\quad
 v_{m,m+1}=-\frac{z_\beta u_{m,m}}{m+1},\quad
 v_{m+1,m+2}=-\frac{z_{\beta-2D}u_{m+1,m+1}}{m+2}.
\]
The last identity uses \(\lambda_{m+1}-(m+1)=\lambda_m-m-2D\).
All terms of \(\omega_{m+1,m+1}\) except its two viscosity terms
have already vanished. Since \(c-D=-1\), its remaining inner
axial exponent is \(\lambda_m-m-1=\beta-D\). Substitution gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 \omega_{m+1,m+1}
 &=-2(m+1)(m+2)v_{m+1,m+2}
       -z_{\beta-2D}z_{\beta-D}v_{m,m+1}\\
 &=-\frac{z_{\beta-2D}z_{\beta-D}z_\beta u_{m,m}}{m+1}
   +\frac{z_{\beta-2D}z_{\beta-D}z_\beta u_{m,m}}{m+1}=0.
 \end{split}
\]
The identical ordered compositions, not any commutation of distinct
operators, give this cancellation. Hence \(\omega_{n,m+1}=0\)
for all \(n>m\). Equation \eqref{nfr:pressure} now gives
\(\pi_{n,m+1}=0\) for \(n>m+1\), closing the strong induction.
The new axial coefficient was computed before the new pressure coefficient,
and both viscosity terms were retained. All identities are smooth in
\(\eta\), so their fixed parameter derivatives are zero.
\end{proof}

This is also a constructive finite algorithm. Start with the three
specified axis functions and \eqref{nfr:V}. At degree \(m+1\), compute
\(\phi,u\) for \(0\leq n\leq m+1\), then \(v\), then
\(\omega\) for \(0\leq n\leq m+1\), and finally \(\pi\)
for \(0\leq n\leq m+1\). The intermediate
\(\omega_{m+1,m+1}\) is the proved zero just calculated.
Only additions, products, parameter
derivatives, division by \(L\), and the displayed positive integer
denominators occur. The source choice \(0<h<1/100\) gives
\(L\geq1-2h>0\) on the real interval. For each finite degree the
algorithm therefore returns uniquely determined smooth coefficient
functions, from the original axis data with no new choice.

\subsection{The highest correction is an explicit axial derivative}

At \(n=j\), \(m=j-1\), the time and product terms in
\eqref{nfr:phi} vanish. Since
\(b+\lambda_{j-1}-(j-1)=-k-2D(j-1)\), it follows that
\begin{equation}\label{nfr:diagonal-step}
 \phi_{j,j}=-\frac{
 z_{-k-(2j-1)D}z_{-k-(2j-2)D}\phi_{j-1,j-1}}
 {2j(j+1)}\quad(j\geq1).
\end{equation}
Iterating this exact equality, with the rightmost operator applied first,
proves
\begin{equation}\label{nfr:diagonal}
 \phi_{j,j}=\frac{(-1)^j}{2^j j!(j+1)!}
 z_{-k-(2j-1)D}z_{-k-(2j-2)D}\cdots z_{-k}\varphi .
\end{equation}
The denominator is the product of \(2\ell(\ell+1)\) for
\(1\leq\ell\leq j\). Empty products at \(j=0\) mean the identity.
For the physical source trace \(b_1=2q^{-k}\varphi/C\), repeated
use of the physical axial derivative in \eqref{nsr:coordinates} gives
\[
 \partial_z^{2j}b_1=\frac2{C\nu^j}q^{-k-2jD}
 z_{-k-(2j-1)D}\cdots z_{-k}\varphi.
\]
This proves the complete axial meaning of the highest correction,
including its sign, amplitude, factorials and viscosity.

The strong pressure assertion also supplies the highest axial coefficient.
At \(n=j,m=j-1\) in \eqref{nfr:U}, the pressure term now vanishes
along with the time and product terms. The identical induction gives
\begin{equation}\label{nfr:axial-diagonal}
 u_{j,j}=\frac{(-1)^j}{2^j(j!)^2}
 z_{-A-(2j-1)D}\cdots z_{-A}U .
\end{equation}
Here the denominator is \(\prod_{\ell=1}^j2\ell^2\).
Writing \(w_0=\sqrt\nu q^{-A}U\), the \(n=j\) summand of
the physical coefficient \([\sigma^j]u_z\) is consequently
\(\rho_j(-1)^j\partial_z^{2j}w_0/(2^j(j!)^2)\).
This follows by the same \(2j\) physical chain rules, retaining
the factor \(\nu^{1/2-j}\).

\subsection{Exact coefficients of the actual cutoff-summed fields}

Put \(\rho_0(q)=1\) and \(\rho_n(q)=\chi(c_nq)\) for \(n>0\),
with precisely the increasing source scales in Proposition 5.5.
The final spatial and temporal cutoffs are one in the central slab near
terminal time used in Section~\ref{sec:ns-actual-second-radial}.
All assertions in this subsection concern that slab. At each preterminal
axis point the annular wave and mean terms vanish in a neighborhood;
the actual field there is the cutoff-summed axisymmetric base. Its
locally finite sum and the smooth extensions from the source permit
termwise finite radial and parameter differentiation.

Write the actual coefficients as
\[
 b_m=[\sigma^m]B\ (m\geq1),\qquad
 w_m=[\sigma^m]u_z\ (m\geq0),\qquad
 a_l=[\sigma^l](ru_r)\ (l\geq1).
\]
These are ordinary coefficients, not derivatives without factorials.
Theorem~\ref{nfr:support} and source (5.1), (5.45) give the exact finite
formulas
\begin{align}
 b_m&=\frac2{C\nu^{m-1}}\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}
       \rho_n q^{-k-m+1+\lambda_n}\phi_{n,m-1},
       \label{nfr:actual-B}\\
 w_m&=\nu^{1/2-m}\sum_{n=0}^{m}
       \rho_n q^{-A-m+\lambda_n}u_{n,m},
       \label{nfr:actual-U}\\
 a_l&=\nu^{1-l}\sum_{n=0}^{l-1}q^{\lambda_n-l}
       \left(\rho_n v_{n,l}
          -\frac{2\eta}{L}\,q\rho_n'(q)\frac{u_{n,l-1}}l\right).
       \label{nfr:actual-V}
\end{align}
To check the last expression rather than suppress its commutator, the
source primitive is \(F_n(X)=\int_0^XU_n(x)\,dx\). Its coefficient
at \(X^l\) is \(u_{n,l-1}/l\). Source (5.45) reads
\(ru_{r,n}^{\rm cut}=q^{\lambda_n}
(\rho_nV_n-2\eta q\rho_n'F_n/L)\) at viscosity one.
The physical viscosity map multiplies \(ru_r\) by \(\nu\)
and replaces \(X\) by \(\sigma/(\nu q)\), giving
\eqref{nfr:actual-V}. Similarly \(u_z\) scales by \(\sqrt\nu\),
whereas \(B=ru_\theta=2\sigma q^{-k}\varphi/C\), yielding the
other two formulas. Every omitted correction index has a proved zero
coefficient; no remainder has been estimated away.

In particular the \(n=j\) summand of \(b_{j+1}\) is precisely
\begin{equation}\label{nfr:physical-diagonal}
 \rho_j(q)\frac{(-1)^j}{2^j j!(j+1)!}\partial_z^{2j}b_1.
\end{equation}
The derivative acts on \(b_1\), not on the cutoff. The factors for
\(j=1,2,3\), respectively, are
\[
 -\frac14,\qquad \frac1{48},\qquad -\frac1{1152}.
\]
All lower-index contributions remain in \eqref{nfr:actual-B}.

The nonlinear fluxes retain their full ordered products. Since the
actual field is axisymmetric in this neighborhood,
\(P=(ru_r)B\) and \(Q=u_zB\), and therefore
\begin{equation}\label{nfr:fluxes}
 p_l=[\sigma^l]P=\sum_{a=1}^{l-1}a_a b_{l-a},\qquad
 q_l=[\sigma^l]Q=\sum_{a=0}^{l-1}w_a b_{l-a}.
\end{equation}
An empty sum is zero. Outside the neighborhood the original angular
averages, not these product expressions, continue to define the full
functions. Their axis coefficients agree with \eqref{nfr:fluxes}.

\subsection{Eventual exact disappearance of each fixed angular-force coefficient}

Let \(f_j=[\sigma^j]F\), with \(F=\langle rf_\theta\rangle\).
The full physical equation gives, everywhere on the central preterminal slab,
\begin{equation}\label{nfr:force}
 f_j=\partial_tb_j+(j+1)p_{j+1}+\partial_zq_j
       -\nu\{2j(j+1)b_{j+1}+\partial_{zz}b_j\},\qquad j\geq1.
\end{equation}
Equations \eqref{nfr:actual-B}--\eqref{nfr:fluxes} make this an exact
finite expression, including all cutoff derivatives, with indices at most
\(j\). It does not require replacing the force by an auxiliary estimate.

\begin{theorem}\label{nfr:force-eventual}
For each integer \(J\geq1\), every coefficient \(f_j\),
\(1\leq j\leq J\), and all its fixed \((z,t)\) derivatives vanish
identically on the open part of the central slab where
\[
                0<q<(2c_J)^{-1}.
\]
This is a degree-dependent statement about the angular-force trace.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The source scales are increasing and \(\chi=1\) on \([0,1/2]\),
so on this open region all \(\rho_n\) for \(1\leq n\leq J\)
are one and all their positive parameter derivatives are zero. Every
coefficient in \eqref{nfr:force} is consequently the same as for the
uncut sum through correction order \(J\).

To verify that no high-order nonlinear term survives this comparison,
fix \(j\leq J\). At radial degree \(j-1\) of the source angular
equation, each transport product has correction index at most \(j-1\)
by Theorem~\ref{nfr:support} and \(a_0+b_0=j-1\) in
\eqref{nfr:phi}. The time term has that same bound. The radial
diffusion term uses \(\phi_{n,j}\), and the shifted axial viscosity
uses \(\phi_{n-1,j-1}\); both have index at most \(j\).
Each coefficient at these finitely many correction indices is zero
by \eqref{nfr:phi}. Multiplication by the original physical powers
and the angular factor \(r\), or equivalently the coefficient
derivation of \eqref{nfr:force}, proves \(f_j=0\).
The cutoff commutator in \eqref{nfr:actual-V} is zero on this region,
and the annular corrections and final-cutoff derivatives give no axis
term there. Finally a smooth function identically zero on an open
parameter domain has all its parameter derivatives zero on that domain.
\end{proof}

For a compact interval \(|\eta|\leq\eta_c<1\), one can impose the
same explicit localization bound \(q<q_{\rm loc}\) as in
Section~\ref{sec:ns-actual-second-radial}; take
\(q<\min(q_{\rm loc},(2c_J)^{-1})\) for the theorem there.
No positive bound uniform in \(J\) follows: the source has
\(c_J\longrightarrow\infty\). Nor does a zero Taylor coefficient
prove that the full force vanishes off the axis. In particular this
does not convert the forced source theorem into an unforced one.

\subsection{Exact finite source formulas in all arithmetic residue--value coordinates}

Use the unchanged \(W_h\), \(c_{h,m}\), and \(E_m\) from
Section~\ref{sec:ns-hidden-mellin-diffusion}, with
\(\mathfrak B=\mathcal M\mathcal A_hB\). Define
\(\widetilde B_m=E_m(\mathfrak B)\) and similarly for \(P,Q,F\).
The previously proved Mellin continuation and \eqref{nfr:actual-B} give
\begin{equation}\label{nfr:arithmetic}
 \widetilde B_m=\frac{2c_{h,m}}{C\nu^{m-1}}
       \sum_{n=0}^{m-1}\rho_nq^{-k-m+1+2nh}\phi_{n,m-1}.
\end{equation}
For odd \(m\) this is the residue at \(-m\); for even \(m\) it
is the regular continued value there. In both cases the inverse is
the exact division \(b_m=\widetilde B_m/c_{h,m}\), with the nonzero
denominator already proved from the zeta functional equation.
Thus a trivial zeta zero retains its even input coefficient through
a regular value; it does not erase that coefficient.

The full nonlinear arithmetic evolution is still
\[
 \partial_t\widetilde B_j+\partial_z\widetilde Q_j
       -\nu\partial_{zz}\widetilde B_j-\widetilde F_j
  =(j+1)\frac{c_{h,j}}{c_{h,j+1}}
       (2\nu j\widetilde B_{j+1}-\widetilde P_{j+1}).
\]
Every entry now has the finite source formulas above. On the
degree-\(J\) domain of Theorem~\ref{nfr:force-eventual},
\(\widetilde F_j=0\) for \(j\leq J\) by the proved zero
coefficient, not by deletion of a force term from the equation.
The nonlinear fluxes and axial diffusion persist. There is no assertion
that a finite set of \(\widetilde B_j\) is an autonomous system.
The full functions and their flat remainders remain in the invertible
arithmetic transform. This supplies exact higher source data for the
ongoing arithmetic dynamics; it supplies neither a nontrivial zeta
zero nor a sign assertion about the quadratic Weil form.
