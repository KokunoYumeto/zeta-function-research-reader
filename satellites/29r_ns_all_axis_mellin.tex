\section{All source axis derivatives and their exact arithmetic representatives}
\label{sec:ns-all-axis-mellin}

The actual axial velocity has a nonzero axis value. To carry it alongside
the angular momentum we first extend the original arithmetic transform
without removing that value. We then compute every axial derivative of
both original source traces by Lagrange inversion and insert the results
into the radial residue--value maps. The original source choices and
cutoff-summed remainder are retained throughout. The imported existence
theorem is not strengthened by the calculation.

\subsection{The nonzero axis value is a residue at zero}

Let $\mathcal D=C_c^\infty([0,\infty);\mathbb C)$, where compact support
may meet zero; no vanishing condition is imposed there. Use exactly
\[
 \phi_a(x)=a(x)-2^ha(2x),\qquad
 \psi_a(x)=\phi_a(x)-\tfrac12\phi_a(x/2),\qquad
 G_a(x)=\sum_{n\geq1}\psi_a(nx),\quad x>0.
\]
The fixed source range is $0<h<1/100$. The multiplier remains
\[
 W_h(s)=\zeta(s)(1-2^{s-1})(1-2^{h-s}).
\]

\begin{theorem}\label{nax:extension}
The formula above extends to an injective linear map
$\mathcal A_h:\mathcal D\to\mathcal D$. It agrees with the previous
map on $\mathcal D_0$. For every $j\geq0$,
\[
 G_a^{(j)}(0)=W_h(-j)a^{(j)}(0),\qquad
 G_a(0)=\frac{2^h-1}{4}a(0).
\]
On its exact range its inverse is the three series
\eqref{ndm:inverse}. Meromorphically on $\mathbb C$,
\begin{equation}\label{nax:mellin-product}
 \mathcal M G_a(s)=W_h(s)\mathcal M a(s),\qquad
 \operatorname{Res}_{s=0}\mathcal M G_a(s)
                  =\frac{2^h-1}{4}a(0).
\end{equation}
Together with the negative-odd residues and negative-even regular values,
the residue at zero recovers every input Taylor coefficient.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $\operatorname{supp}a\subset[0,R]$, then $\psi_a$ is supported
in $[0,2R]$, so the sum and all its derivatives are locally finite at
$x>0$ and vanish for $x>2R$. Substitution gives
$\int_0^\infty\psi_a=0$ even when $a(0)\ne0$. Now
\[
 \psi_a(0)=\tfrac12(1-2^h)a(0).
\]
Euler--Maclaurin with its integral remainder
\cite[\S2.10(i)]{olverDLMF2}, applied to this smooth compact function,
gives $G_a(x)=-\psi_a(0)/2+O(x)$ as $x\downarrow0$.
This yields the stated constant, including its sign. For $j\geq1$ put
$f_j(v)=v^j\psi_a^{(j)}(v)$. Then
\[
 x^jG_a^{(j)}(x)=\sum_{n\geq1}f_j(nx),\qquad
 \int_0^\infty f_j=(-1)^jj!\int_0^\infty\psi_a=0.
\]
Every boundary term in the $j$ integrations by parts contains a positive
power of $v$ at zero, regardless of $a(0)$. Moreover
$f_j^{(r)}(0)=0$ for $r<j$ and
$f_j^{(r)}(0)=r!\psi_a^{(r)}(0)/(r-j)!$ for $r\geq j$.
The proof in Section~\ref{sec:ns-axis-arithmetic-jet} therefore applies
unchanged to every positive derivative: choose $2M-1>j$, divide its
Euler--Maclaurin remainder by $x^j$, and bound it by
\[
 \frac{\|\widetilde B_{2M}\|_\infty}{(2M)!}
 x^{2M-1-j}\|f_j^{(2M)}\|_1.
\]
The resulting limits and the fundamental theorem of calculus prove
smoothness up to zero, not just an asymptotic Taylor series. The Bernoulli
coefficient is exactly $\zeta(-j)(1-2^{-j-1})(1-2^{h+j})$ for $j\geq1$.
For $j=0$, $\zeta(0)=-1/2$ gives the same formula.

For completeness the inverse does not require $a(0)=0$. At each $x>0$
M\"obius inversion is a finite divisor calculation. The second inverse
sum telescopes with remainder $2^{-L-1}\phi_a(x/2^{L+1})$; the third
telescopes with remainder $2^{-Kh}a(x/2^K)$. Both tend to zero because
the recovered functions are bounded at zero and $h>0$. Their $j$th
derivatives have the extra factors $2^{-(L+1)j}$ and $2^{-Kj}$, proving
smooth local convergence after recovery of $\psi_a$. These identities
prove both inverse compositions on the actual range and hence injectivity.

Both compact smooth functions $a,G_a$ have their original Mellin integrals
on $\Re s>0$. For $\Re s>1$, absolute integration and summation are allowed since
$\psi_a$ is bounded at zero and compactly supported, and
$\sum n^{-\Re s}<\infty$. Scaling in the two differences proves
$\mathcal M G_a=\zeta(s)(1-2^{s-1})(1-2^{h-s})\mathcal M a$ there.
Taylor subtraction in \eqref{ndm:continuation}, now starting its finite
sum at $n=0$, proves meromorphic continuation of both sides, with only
possible simple endpoint poles at $0,-1,-2,\ldots$. The identity theorem
extends the product equality. Since $W_h(0)=(2^h-1)/4\ne0$, the residue
at zero is precisely $W_h(0)a(0)$. The previously proved functional
equation calculation supplies the other nonzero factors $c_{h,n}$.
\end{proof}

Write $E_0=\operatorname{Res}_{s=0}$ and $c_{h,0}=(2^h-1)/4$;
for $n\geq1$ retain $E_n,c_{h,n}$ from
Section~\ref{sec:ns-hidden-mellin-diffusion}. The exact coordinate map is
\begin{equation}\label{nax:coordinates}
 E_n(\mathcal M\mathcal A_h a)=c_{h,n}a_n,\qquad
 a_n=\frac{E_n(\mathcal M\mathcal A_h a)}{c_{h,n}},\quad n\geq0.
\end{equation}
This recovers the nonzero axial velocity itself at $n=0$; it does not
replace that velocity by its derivative or by an artificially centered one.

\subsection{Explicit localization, complete remainders, and the germ map}

For $\epsilon>0$ put
\[
 e(v)=\begin{cases}e^{-1/v},&v>0,\\0,&v\leq0,\end{cases}
 \qquad
 \kappa_\epsilon(\sigma)=
 \frac{e(2-\sigma/\epsilon)}
 {e(2-\sigma/\epsilon)+e(\sigma/\epsilon-1)}.
\]
The denominator is positive for all $\sigma\geq0$. Each derivative
of $e$ from the right at zero vanishes, by exponential decay against
every polynomial in $1/v$. Thus $\kappa_\epsilon$ is smooth, equals
one on $[0,\epsilon]$, and vanishes on $[2\epsilon,\infty)$.
On a compact preterminal parameter patch, choose $2\epsilon$ inside
the actual common axis neighborhood. Keep the selected source cutoffs
$\rho_j(z,t)$ in $W_\rho,G_\rho$, and form $a_W,a_G,a_B$ as in
Section~\ref{sec:ns-diagonal-generator}. The sums are locally finite
in $(z,t)$ and polynomial in $\sigma$, so these inputs belong to
$\mathcal D,\mathcal D,\mathcal D_0$, respectively.
All their $(z,t)$ derivatives have the same compact support. Hence
the differentiated proof of Theorem~\ref{nax:extension} commutes with
those derivatives on compact patches.

In particular the exact selected angular coordinates are
\begin{equation}\label{nax:angular-shift}
 E_n(\mathcal M\mathcal A_h a_B)
   =c_{h,n}\rho_{n-1}
       \frac{(-1)^{n-1}\partial_z^{2n-2}b_1}
            {2^{n-1}(n-1)!n!},\quad n\geq1.
\end{equation}
For the selected axial channel they are
\begin{equation}\label{nax:axial-channel}
 E_j(\mathcal M\mathcal A_h a_W)
   =c_{h,j}\rho_j
       \frac{(-1)^j\partial_z^{2j}w_0}{2^j(j!)^2},\quad j\geq0.
\end{equation}
The shift in \eqref{nax:angular-shift} comes from the actual factor
$B=\sigma G$, not from a convention for residues.

Let $\mathcal F\subset\mathcal D$ be the functions flat at zero.
The injectivity and full inverse of $\mathcal A_h$ induce an exact
linear bijection
\begin{equation}\label{nax:flat-quotient}
 \mathcal D/\mathcal F\ \longrightarrow\
 \mathcal A_h\mathcal D/\mathcal A_h\mathcal F,
 \qquad [a]\longmapsto[\mathcal A_h a],
\end{equation}
with inverse induced by \eqref{ndm:inverse}. If two radial localizations
are one near zero, their representatives differ by a function zero
near zero, so they give the same class in \eqref{nax:flat-quotient} and
the same coordinates \eqref{nax:coordinates}. Conversely equality of
all those coordinates means that the input difference is flat, since
every $c_{h,n}$ is nonzero. This proves exactly the cutoff independence
being used. For analytic input germs, flatness implies equality on a
neighborhood by convergence of their Taylor series. For smooth inputs
the full remainder is instead retained by the original inverse; the
quotient is not a replacement for that inverse.

Now let $B_{\rm act}$ be the original compact angular average and
$Z_{\rm act}=\langle u_z\rangle$ the compact axial average. Define
the latter at the axis by its smooth extension. Indeed angular averaging
the Cartesian Taylor polynomial of $u_z$ kills every odd radial degree
and produces a polynomial in $r^2=2\sigma$. Taylor's differentiated
remainder, taken to arbitrary even order, gives the same smooth extension
for every finite $\sigma$ derivative. Compact spatial support gives
compact support in $\sigma$ on each compact parameter patch. Define
the full, unestimated differences
\[
 R_B=B_{\rm act}-a_B,\qquad R_W=Z_{\rm act}-a_W.
\]
Both are in $\mathcal D$, and $R_B\in\mathcal D_0$. Thus
\[
 \mathcal M\mathcal A_hB_{\rm act}
 =\mathcal M\mathcal A_h a_B+\mathcal M\mathcal A_hR_B
\]
and the identical equality holds for $Z_{\rm act}$. The original full
source coefficients of Section~\ref{sec:ns-finite-radial-recursion}
remain in the second summand. At the first differing angular coefficient,
\begin{equation}\label{nax:first-remainder}
 [\sigma^2]R_B=\frac{q^{-2-h}}{2\nu C}\mathcal H(\eta),\quad
 \mathcal H=\phi\left[
 \frac{h-3-j_0\eta}{L}
 -\frac{\Lambda H_*^2}{H_*^2+\sigma_*^2}\right]<0.
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal H$ is the function called $H$ in
Section~\ref{sec:ns-actual-second-radial}. Indeed the other term in
the complete $b_2$ is exactly the selected
$-\rho_1\partial_{zz}b_1/4$. Positivity of $\phi,L$ and
$h-3-j_0\eta\leq h-3+j_0<0$ proves the strict sign on $|\eta|<1$.
Consequently the omitted regular Mellin value is exactly
$E_2(\mathcal M\mathcal A_hR_B)=d_hq^{-2-h}\mathcal H/(2\nu C)$.
It has not been absorbed into a normalization or silently set to zero.

Localization derivatives also remain in the differential equation.
For any smooth $B$ and $L_B=2\sigma\partial_{\sigma\sigma}+\partial_{zz}$,
\[
 L_B(\kappa_\epsilon B)-\kappa_\epsilon L_BB
       =4\sigma\kappa_\epsilon'\partial_\sigma B
          +2\sigma\kappa_\epsilon''B.
\]
This follows by the product rule, with $\kappa_\epsilon$ independent
of $z,t$. The terms are supported away from the axis but are part of
the full transformed function. Likewise all nonlinear fluxes use
$B_{\rm act}=a_B+R_B$ and $Z_{\rm act}=a_W+R_W$ with their cross
products; no closed selected-diagonal Navier--Stokes equation is asserted.

\subsection{The exact inverse coordinate and all physical axial derivatives}

Retain $A=1/2+h$, $D=1/2-h$, $k=1+h$, $\tau=1-t>0$ and
\[
 x=\frac{z}{\sqrt\nu\,\tau^D},\qquad
 x=\eta(1-\eta^2)^{-D},\qquad q=\frac{\tau}{1-\eta^2}.
\]
The inverse has its genuine holomorphic germ at zero: the forward
map has derivative one, and the chosen logarithm of $1-\eta^2$
is zero there. Its convergence can also be seen directly. For
$|x|\leq1/8$, iteration of $\eta\mapsto x(1-\eta^2)^D$ on
$|\eta|\leq1/4$ stays inside that disk and has derivative bounded
by $1/30$. It converges uniformly to the holomorphic inverse.
Oddness follows from uniqueness. For a holomorphic function $F$ and
$n\geq1$, the Lagrange formula is
\begin{equation}\label{nax:lagrange}
 [x^n]F(\eta(x))=
 \frac1n[u^{n-1}]F'(u)(1-u^2)^{Dn}.
\end{equation}
This is \cite[Corollary~5.4.3, (5.65)]{stanleyEC2Lagrange} in the
actual coordinates. To prove it here, change variables in the coefficient
residue by $x=f(u)=u(1-u^2)^{-D}$; the residue becomes
$\operatorname{Res}F(u)f'(u)f(u)^{-n-1}\,du$.
The residue of $(Ff^{-n})'$ is zero, giving
$n^{-1}\operatorname{Res}F'f^{-n}\,du$, precisely
\eqref{nax:lagrange}. For $n=0$ the answer is separately $F(0)$.

Write the actual axial trace as
\[
 w_0(z,t)=\sqrt\nu\,\tau^{-A}\mathcal W(x),\qquad
 \mathcal W(x)=(1-\eta(x)^2)^A(4\eta(x)+j_0)
             =\sum_{n\geq0}\mathsf a_nx^n.
\]
Then $\mathsf a_0=j_0$, $\mathsf a_1=4$, and for $j\geq1$
\begin{equation}\label{nax:axial-coefficients}
 \begin{split}
 \mathsf a_{2j}&=\frac{j_0(-1)^jA}{j}
                      { (2j-1)D\choose j-1},\\
 \mathsf a_{2j+1}&=\frac{4(-1)^j}{j}
                      {2jD\choose j-1}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Every generalized binomial denotes the finite polynomial
$\binom{\beta}{m}=\prod_{l=0}^{m-1}(\beta-l)/m!$.
For the even coefficients apply \eqref{nax:lagrange} to
$j_0(1-u^2)^A$. Its derivative is $-2Aj_0u(1-u^2)^{A-1}$,
and $A-1=-D$ gives the first formula. For the odd part, the
identity $A+D=1$ gives
$\eta(1-\eta^2)^A=x(1-\eta^2)$ exactly. Its coefficient at
$x^{2j+1}$ is $-4[x^{2j}]\eta^2$; applying
\eqref{nax:lagrange} to $u^2$ gives the second formula.
Finally the physical chain rule gives every scale and factorial:
\begin{equation}\label{nax:physical-derivatives}
 \partial_z^n w_0(0,t)
   =n!\nu^{(1-n)/2}\tau^{-A-nD}\mathsf a_n,\qquad n\geq0.
\end{equation}

For the unchanged source angular germ put
\[
 d(u)=1-u^2,\quad L(u)=1-2hu^2,\quad
 H_*(u)=Du+d(u)(4u+j_0),\quad
 \zeta_*(u)=-\frac{L(u)H_*(u)}{H_*(u)^2+\sigma_*^2},
\]
\[
 \phi(u)=\exp\left(\Lambda\int_0^u\zeta_*(v)\,dv\right),\qquad
 b_1(z,t)=\frac2C\tau^{-k}d(\eta(x))^k\phi(\eta(x)).
\]
These are the selected original axis data, not freely chosen substitutes
\cite[(B.1)--(B.3), (B.12)]{openaiNS2026release}. Their denominator
at zero is $j_0^2+\sigma_*^2>0$. Applying
\eqref{nax:lagrange} after product differentiation proves
\begin{equation}\label{nax:angular-derivatives}
 \begin{split}
 \partial_z^n b_1(0,t)
  ={}&\frac2C\nu^{-n/2}\tau^{-k-nD}(n-1)!\\
 &\cdot[u^{n-1}]\phi(u)d(u)^{k-1+nD}
       \bigl(\Lambda d(u)\zeta_*(u)-2ku\bigr),\qquad n\geq1.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The constant is separately $b_1(0,t)=2\tau^{-k}/C$. The required
finite coefficients are constructive: invert the rational denominator
as a Taylor series with its nonzero constant and solve
\[
 \phi_0=1,\qquad
 (m+1)\phi_{m+1}=\Lambda\sum_{l=0}^m(\zeta_*)_l\phi_{m-l}.
\]
No integration oracle or truncation of the source equation is needed.

\subsection{Insertion into the original arithmetic coordinates}

At $z=0$, $q=\tau$. For $j\geq1$, the exact selected axial coordinate
\eqref{nax:axial-channel} is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{nax:explicit-arithmetic}
 \begin{split}
 E_j(\mathcal M\mathcal A_h a_W)(0,t)
  ={}&c_{h,j}\rho_j(\tau)\,
  \frac{j_0A(2j)!}{2^j(j!)^2j}
     { (2j-1)D\choose j-1}\\
 &\cdot\nu^{1/2-j}\tau^{-A-2jD}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
At $j=0$ the corresponding actual axial residue, with no selected/full
difference at the axis, is
\begin{equation}\label{nax:zeroth-actual}
 \operatorname{Res}_{s=0}
  \mathcal M\mathcal A_h Z_{\rm act}(s,0,1-\tau)
   =\frac{2^h-1}{4}\sqrt\nu\,j_0\tau^{-A}.
\end{equation}
Every physical axial derivative of this residue is exactly $c_{h,0}$
times \eqref{nax:physical-derivatives}. The angular coordinates are
obtained by substituting \eqref{nax:angular-derivatives} into
\eqref{nax:angular-shift}, retaining the shift and the source $\rho_j$.
All lower profile-order contributions remain explicit through
\eqref{nfr:actual-B}--\eqref{nfr:actual-U} and $R_B,R_W$ above.
This is an exact source-to-arithmetic propagation. The growing residue
in \eqref{nax:zeroth-actual} is at the fixed endpoint $s=0$, not a newly
located zero; no conclusion about a sign of the complete Weil form is
drawn from this linear coordinate.
