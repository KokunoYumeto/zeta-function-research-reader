\textbf{Purpose and result.}

control from the total displacement of a finite zero packet. This chapter

of its original theta source and the moments of its original polynomial
relations. Their quotient is the determinant of the actual least-norm

below calculates the individual outgoing and incoming source maps as well

of the original quotient volumes. It supplies no growing-degree arithmetic

\subsection[\hspace{0.4em}Original arithmetic objects and the strip of real tilts]{Original arithmetic objects and the strip of real tilts}

The original spaces and transforms are
\[
 \begin{split}
 V&=\{\phi\in\mathcal S(\R):\phi\text{ is even},\quad
                     \phi(0)=\int_\R\phi(x)\,dx=0\},\\
 \mathscr B&=\{F\in C^\infty(\R_{>0}):
       \sup_{x>0}x^b|D^jF(x)|<\infty
           \text{ for every }b\in\mathbb Z,\ j\ge0\},\\
 D&=-x\partial_x,\qquad
 \Theta\phi(x)=\sum_{n\ne0}\phi(nx)=2\sum_{n\ge1}\phi(nx),\\
 \mathcal MF(s)&=\int_0^\infty F(x)x^{s-1}\,dx.
 \end{split}
\]
The series and its Euler derivatives converge uniformly at large
$x$ with every power of decay. For small $x$, Poisson summation
with Fourier kernel $e^{-2\pi ixy}$ gives
$\Theta\phi(x)=x^{-1}\Theta\widehat\phi(1/x)$; both zero terms
vanish because $\phi(0)=\widehat\phi(0)=0$.
The Fourier transform preserves the stated two zero constraints
and evenness. This formula proves every small-$x$ seminorm after
Euler differentiation, hence $\Theta:V\to\mathscr B$.
It also commutes with $D$ on these spaces by termwise differentiation.
Mellin transforms of elements of $\mathscr B$ are entire, and
integration by parts gives $\mathcal M(DF)(s)=s\mathcal MF(s)$;
both endpoint terms vanish with their original coefficients.

Let $h$ be a monic, nonempty packet of actual nontrivial zeros of
\[
 g(s)=2\xi(s)=s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s),
\]
with their complete orders. Assume the packet is stable under
$\rho\mapsto1-\bar\rho$. Set $d=\deg h$ and retain
\[
 v_h=g/h,\quad E_h=\C[s]/(h),\quad
 \upsilon_h=[v_h]_h\in E_h^\times,\quad
 w_h(t)=\frac{|v_h(1/2+it)|^2}{2\pi}.
 \tag{TVB.1}
\]
Here the jet of an entire function $f$ means its unique polynomial
Hermite remainder of degree less than $d$: in the local factor
$\C[t]/(t^{m_\rho})$, it is
$\sum_{j<m_\rho}f^{(j)}(\rho)t^j/j!$, with $t=s-\rho$.
The coprime factors of $h$ give the isomorphism to their product
by monic division and Bezout's identity, which supplies the
unique global remainder. The constant $v_h(\rho)$ is nonzero
at each selected zero because its full order was divided out.
If the local jet is $\sum a_jt^j$, its inverse coefficients are
$b_0=a_0^{-1}$ and
$b_n=-a_0^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^n a_jb_{n-j}$ for $n<m_\rho$.
Multiplication verifies this inverse in every local factor,
and the product isomorphism gives the inverse of $\upsilon_h$.

The original $F_h$ has a construction within $\mathscr B$.
For $F\in\mathscr B$ with $\mathcal MF(\rho)=0$, define
\[
 S_\rho F(x)=x^{-\rho}\int_x^\infty F(y)y^{\rho-1}\,dy
           =-x^{-\rho}\int_0^x F(y)y^{\rho-1}\,dy.
\]
The equality is the vanishing Mellin value. For any desired
power bound at infinity choose $A>\Re\rho$ with
$|F(y)|\le C_Ay^{-A}$ for $y\ge1$; the first integral is bounded
by $C_Ax^{-A}/(A-\Re\rho)$ there. At zero choose $B$ with
$B+\Re\rho>0$ and $|F(y)|\le C_By^B$ for $0<y\le1$;
the second integral is bounded by $C_Bx^B/(B+\Re\rho)$.
The exact identity $(D-\rho)S_\rho F=F$ now gives all Euler
derivative bounds inductively, so $S_\rho F\in\mathscr B$.
Mellin integration by parts proves
$\mathcal M(S_\rho F)=\mathcal MF/(s-\rho)$, with its removable
value at $s=\rho$ supplied by the derivative of the numerator.
For the original seed
$\phi_0=(4\pi^2x^4-6\pi x^2)e^{-\pi x^2}\in V$,
direct substitution $y=\pi x^2$ gives
\[
 \mathcal M\phi_0(s)=\tfrac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2),
 \qquad \Re s>-2.
\]
For $\Re s>1$, absolute convergence permits the two-sided theta
sum to be integrated termwise, yielding
$\mathcal M(\Theta\phi_0)=2\zeta(s)\mathcal M\phi_0(s)=g(s)$;
both sides continue to the same entire function. Apply $S_\rho$
successively to $\Theta\phi_0$ over the full list of roots of $h$,
including each repeated occurrence. At every stage the next
vanishing Mellin value holds by its remaining order. This constructs
$F_h\in\mathscr B$ with $\mathcal MF_h=g/h$ and
$h(D)F_h=\Theta\phi_0$; the latter also follows by Mellin
injectivity, which is Fourier injectivity after $x=e^y$.
Fix $k\ge1$, set $c=k/2$, $S=s_1+\cdots+s_k$, and put
$I=(h(s_1),\ldots,h(s_k))\subset\C[s_1,\ldots,s_k]$.
Let $\chi$ be the original monic generator of
$\{P\in\C[S]:P(S)\in I\}$. Its degree is $q\ge1$ and
\[
 C=\C[S]/(\chi),\qquad A=M_S:C\longrightarrow C.
 \tag{TVB.2}
\]
The coordinate basis of $C$ is always $1,S,\ldots,S^{q-1}$.
The polynomial exists because multiplication by $S$ on the finite
algebra $\C[s_1,\ldots,s_k]/I$ has an annihilating polynomial.
Division by its least-degree monic annihilator proves that its entire
annihilating ideal is $(\chi)$. In particular the injection
\[
 \eta:C\longrightarrow(E_h^{\otimes k})^{\mathfrak S_k},\qquad
 [P]\longmapsto\upsilon_h^{\otimes k}[P(S)]_I
 \tag{TVB.3}
\]
has exactly zero kernel: multiplication by the displayed unit is
invertible and the preceding annihilator ideal describes the other
factor's kernel. The original tensor source map is
\[
 \mathcal V P=P(D_1+\cdots+D_k)F_h^{\otimes k},\qquad
 D=-x\partial_x,\qquad \mathcal MF_h=v_h.
\]
On the original Mellin line its scalar observation has squared norm
\[
 \|\mathcal VP\|^2=\int_\R |P(c+iu)|^2m_k(u)\,du,
 \qquad m_k=w_h^{*k},\quad
 \int m_k=\mu_h^k,\quad\mu_h=\int w_h.
 \tag{TVB.4}
\]
Indeed the tensor Mellin--Plancherel density is
$\prod_iw_h(t_i)\,d^kt$. In coordinates
$u=\sum_i t_i$, $y=(t_1,\ldots,t_{k-1})$, the inverse is
$(y,u)\mapsto(y_1,\ldots,y_{k-1},u-\sum y_i)$.
With $(y,u)$ in this order its determinant is $1$; with $(u,y)$
its determinant is $(-1)^{k-1}$. Integrating the original density
over $y$ gives (TVB.4), with no scalar divisor.

For every compact substrip $|\Re\theta|\le r<\pi/2$, all integrals
\[
 M_h(\theta)=\int e^{\theta t}w_h(t)\,dt,\qquad
 Z(\theta)=M_h(\theta)^k=\int e^{\theta u}m_k(u)\,du
 \tag{TVB.5}
\]
and all their polynomially weighted derivatives converge locally
uniformly. Here is an elementary sufficient bound. Summation by parts
for the alternating Dirichlet series, whose partial sums have modulus
at most one, gives $|\eta(s)|\le |s|/\Re s$ when $\Re s>0$.
On $\Re s=1/2$, the denominator in
$\zeta(s)=\eta(s)/(1-2^{1-s})$ has modulus at least $\sqrt2-1$.
For $0<\varphi<\pi/2$, rotate the defining gamma integral to the
ray with argument $\operatorname{sgn}(t)\varphi$. The large arc
vanishes by $e^{-R\cos\varphi}$ and the small arc by the positive
real part $1/4$ of its exponent. Its absolute value is bounded by
\[
 |\Gamma(1/4+it/2)|
 \le\Gamma(1/4)(\cos\varphi)^{-1/4}e^{-\varphi|t|/2}.
\]
For sufficiently large $|t|$, the monic polynomial $h$ satisfies
$|h(1/2+it)|\ge C_h(1+|t|)^d$ with $C_h>0$.
The two factors $s,s-1$, the preceding zeta bound and the gamma
bound consequently give
\[
 w_h(t)\le C_{h,\varphi}(1+|t|)^{6-2d}e^{-\varphi|t|}
\]
outside a compact interval. On that interval $v_h$ is entire, since
the selected orders are complete. Choose $r<\varphi<\pi/2$ and
absorb every fixed polynomial in the remaining exponential decay.
This proves the asserted domination and differentiation under the
integral. Fubini gives the exact product $M_h^k$ in (TVB.5).
The nonzero entire function $v_h$ has a discrete real-line zero set;
therefore $w_h>0$ almost everywhere. Its convolutions are positive
almost everywhere (in fact at every $u$ for $k\ge2$), since their
fibre integrands are positive off a finite union of measure-zero
inverse images of this discrete set. Thus the norm of every nonzero
polynomial is positive.

In what follows $\theta$ is real, $|\theta|<\pi/2$, and the source
pairing, conjugate-linear in its first argument, is exactly
\[
 \langle P,Q\rangle_\theta
 =\int_\R\overline{P(c+iu)}Q(c+iu)e^{\theta u}m_k(u)\,du.
 \tag{TVB.6}
\]
The map $f(u)\mapsto e^{\theta u/2}f(u)$ is an onto isometry from
$L^2(e^{\theta u}m_k\,du)$ to $L^2(m_k\,du)$, with inverse
$f\mapsto e^{-\theta u/2}f$. It commutes with multiplication by
$c+iu$. This specifies the exact Hilbert comparison for the tilt.
The polynomial algebra, its reduction and $\eta$ are unchanged.
When the target of $\mathcal V$ is observed at this tilt, its declared
tensor norm is
\[
 \|F\|_{k,\theta}^2=\int_{\R^k}
 e^{\theta\sum_i t_i}|\mathcal M_{\rm unit}F(t_1,\ldots,t_k)|^2\,d^kt,
\]
where $\mathcal M_{\rm unit}$ is the original Mellin--Plancherel map
with a factor $(2\pi)^{-1/2}$ in each coordinate. The map
$f(t)\mapsto e^{\theta\sum_i t_i/2}f(t)$ is an onto isometry from
this weighted Mellin Hilbert space to the original one, with the
opposite exponential as inverse. The restriction to $\mathcal V P$
is exactly (TVB.6), by the same sum-fibre integral as (TVB.4).
Thus every tilted exterior norm below has a specified target norm;
at $\theta=0$ it is the original tensor-source norm.

\subsection[\hspace{0.4em}The source relation and its Laplace observation]{The source relation and its Laplace observation}

Write $\mathcal P_j=\C[S]_{\le j}$, with $\mathcal P_{-1}=0$.
For $N\ge q-1$, put $n=N+1$, $m=N-q+1$. Literal monic division
gives the exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow\mathcal P_{m-1}
 \xrightarrow{M_\chi}\mathcal P_N
 \xrightarrow{T_N}C\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad M_\chi Q=\chi Q,\quad T_NP=[P]_\chi.
 \tag{TVB.7}
\]
Its kernel is precisely the indicated image, and its first $q$
monomial columns prove surjectivity. Denote that kernel by
$\mathcal D_N=\chi\mathcal P_{m-1}$.

This sequence has an explicit map to the original theta boundaries.
To see every sign, choose the quotients $Q_i$ from successive monic
division, in the order $s_1,\ldots,s_k$, so that
\[
 \chi(S)=\sum_{i=1}^k h(s_i)Q_i(s_1,\ldots,s_k).
 \tag{TVB.8}
\]
The remainder is zero by the definition of $\chi$ and uniqueness of
the tensor remainder basis. Put
$\phi_0(x)=(4\pi^2x^4-6\pi x^2)e^{-\pi x^2}$.
It is even, vanishes at zero and has integral zero. Direct gamma
integration of its two terms gives
$\mathcal M(\Theta\phi_0)=g$, hence
$h(D)F_h=\Theta\phi_0$ by Mellin injectivity on the original
rapidly decreasing source. For every polynomial $P$, an actual
primitive of $\mathcal V(\chi P)$ in the tensor complex
$(V\xrightarrow{\Theta}\mathscr B)^{\otimes k}$ is
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^k(-1)^{i-1}Q_i(D_1,\ldots,D_k)P(D_1+\cdots+D_k)
 \bigl(F_h^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes\phi_0
             \otimes F_h^{\otimes(k-i)}\bigr).
 \tag{TVB.9}
\]
The preceding $i-1$ factors have cochain degree one. The tensor
differential therefore contributes $(-1)^{i-1}$ at the $i$th
factor, cancelling the written sign and yielding (TVB.8).
Euler differentiation preserves the source conditions and commutes
with $\Theta$, so all terms have the indicated domains. Conversely
the full jet of $\mathcal VP$ is $\eta T_NP$; injectivity in
(TVB.3) shows that the admitted polynomial source has no further
zero-jet elements. In particular every boundary vector in (TVB.7)
retains its positive source norm before the quotient sends it to
represented zero.

For any polynomial $P$, define its Laplace observation by
\[
 \mathscr L(P)(\theta)=\int P(c+iu)e^{\theta u}m_k(u)\,du.
\]
Differentiation under (TVB.5) proves the typed intertwining
$\mathscr L(SP)=(c+i\partial_\theta)\mathscr L(P)$.
Coefficientwise conjugation gives the exact Gram observation
\[
 Z_\chi
 =\overline\chi(c-i\partial_\theta)\chi(c+i\partial_\theta)Z
 =\int|\chi(c+iu)|^2e^{\theta u}m_k(u)\,du>0.
 \tag{TVB.10}
\]
Thus the two signs and both polynomial factors arise from
$\langle M_\chi1,M_\chi1\rangle_\theta$.
This is a map on source functions, with no replacement of the
higher contracted ideal by a power of $\chi$.

\subsection[\hspace{0.4em}Both monic determinants and their exact determinant-line map]{Both monic determinants and their exact determinant-line map}

Set
\[
 \mathfrak D_j=\det[Z^{(a+b)}]_{a,b=0}^{j-1},\quad
 \mathfrak B_j=\det[Z_\chi^{(a+b)}]_{a,b=0}^{j-1},\quad
 \mathfrak D_0=\mathfrak B_0=1.
 \tag{TVB.11}
\]
All nonempty determinants are positive by polynomial positivity.
The coefficient map from $1,S,\ldots,S^{j-1}$ to
$1,u,\ldots,u^{j-1}$ is explicitly
\[
 S^a=(c+iu)^a=\sum_{b=0}^a\binom ab c^{a-b}i^b u^b.
 \tag{TVB.12}
\]
Its determinant is $i^{j(j-1)/2}$ and its Gram determinant
multiplier is exactly one. Therefore $\mathfrak D_j$ is also
the original $S$-monomial Gram determinant. The same calculation
applies to the relation-weight pairing
$\langle P,Q\rangle_{\chi,\theta}=\langle\chi P,\chi Q\rangle_\theta$
and $\mathfrak B_j$. This proves the coordinate correspondence
without changing the original monic polynomial.

Let $p_j(S;\theta)$ be the monic source orthogonal polynomials,
and $r_j(S;\theta)$ the monic polynomials for this relation-weight
pairing. Retain their original squared norms
\[
 \omega_j=\|p_j\|_\theta^2=\mathfrak D_{j+1}/\mathfrak D_j,
 \qquad\nu_j=\|\chi r_j\|_\theta^2
               =\mathfrak B_{j+1}/\mathfrak B_j.
 \tag{TVB.13}
\]
The equations follow by triangular monic Gram elimination, whose
diagonal change is one. In particular $\omega_0=M_h(\theta)^k$.
The full mass has not been divided out.

Give $C$ its written coordinate Euclidean pairing only when
specifying adjoints. Let $M_N$ be the original monomial source
Gram, and $J_N$ its reduction matrix. The minimum lift is
\[
 K_N=J_NM_N^{-1}J_N^*,\quad G_N=K_N^{-1},\quad
 R_N=M_N^{-1}J_N^*G_N:C\longrightarrow\mathcal P_N.
 \tag{TVB.14}
\]
Here the last expression specifies polynomial coefficients.
It satisfies $T_NR_N=I$, $\im R_N\perp\mathcal D_N$ and
$R_N^*R_N=G_N$ in the original source pairing. Indeed the first
equality is matrix multiplication, and pairing with any vector
in $\ker J_N$ gives zero. Every other lift is $R_Nx+B$ for
$B\in\mathcal D_N$, so its squared norm is
$x^*G_Nx+\|B\|^2$. This proves the minimum assertion and
uniqueness.

Use the source basis
$1,S,\ldots,S^{q-1},\chi,\chi S,\ldots,\chi S^{m-1}$.
Its change from the $n$ source monomials is triangular with
diagonal one. Replacing its first $q$ columns by $R_N[1],
\ldots,R_N[S^{q-1}]$ adds boundary columns and again has
determinant one. The resulting Gram is the direct sum of $G_N$
and the relation monomial Gram. Consequently
\[
 V_N:=\det G_N=\frac{\mathfrak D_{N+1}}{\mathfrak B_m},
 \qquad D_N^{\rm CV}:=\det K_N=V_N^{-1}.
 \tag{TVB.15}
\]
More precisely the map
$\det C\otimes\det\mathcal P_{m-1}\to\det\mathcal P_N$
sends the written coordinate wedges to the wedge of these quotient
lifts followed by $M_\chi$ of the relation monomials; its coordinate
determinant is $1$. Putting the kernel wedge first instead contributes
the explicit sign $(-1)^{qm}$. Its norm identity is
$\det G_N\,\mathfrak B_m=\mathfrak D_n$.
Composing the wedge with $\mathcal V^{\otimes n}\Alt_n$
multiplies its squared norm by exactly $n!$: expanding the unscaled
alternating tensor sum gives $n!$ copies of the Gram determinant.
The original tensor target is $\mathscr B^{\widehat\otimes kn}$
in cochain degree $kn$.

\subsection[\hspace{0.4em}Toda derivatives and the full curvature operators]{Toda derivatives and the full curvature operators}

For either positive real $u$-weight in (TVB.11), write $Q_j(u)$
for its real monic orthogonal polynomials and $h_j$ for their
norms. Orthogonality and leading coefficients prove
\[
 uQ_j=Q_{j+1}+b_jQ_j+a_jQ_{j-1},\qquad
 a_j=h_j/h_{j-1}\ (j\ge1),\quad a_0=0.
\]
For $i\le j-2$, differentiating $\langle Q_i,Q_j\rangle=0$
shows that $\partial_\theta Q_j$ is orthogonal to $Q_i$, because
$uQ_i$ and $\partial_\theta Q_i$ have degree at most $j-1$.
For $i=j-1$ the same identity gives its coefficient $-a_j$.
Thus $\partial_\theta Q_j=-a_jQ_{j-1}$ and $h_j'=b_jh_j$.
To compute $b_j'$, differentiate $h_jb_j=\langle Q_j,uQ_j\rangle$.
The two polynomial derivative terms contribute $-2a_jh_j$.
The derivative of the measure contributes
$\|uQ_j\|^2=h_j(a_{j+1}+b_j^2+a_j)$.
After subtracting $h_j'b_j$ this gives $b_j'=a_{j+1}-a_j$.
The monic Gram determinant is $\prod_{j<n}h_j$, hence
\[
 (\log\mathfrak D_n)''
 =\frac{\omega_n}{\omega_{n-1}}\quad(n\ge1),\qquad
 (\log\mathfrak B_m)''=\frac{\nu_m}{\nu_{m-1}}\quad(m\ge1).
 \tag{TVB.16}
\]
Both $0$th logarithms have second derivative zero.
Multiplication by the square of the determinant proves each
Hankel--Toda equation with coefficient one.
Set
\[
 \alpha_{N+1}=\omega_{N+1}/\omega_N,\quad
 \beta_m=\nu_m/\nu_{m-1}\ (m\ge1),\quad\beta_0=0,
 \qquad\ell_N=\log V_N.
\]
Then $\ell_N''=\alpha_{N+1}-\beta_m$.

There is a finer exact source-map identity. Let $X=(S-c)/i$,
which is multiplication by the real variable $u$ in (TVB.6).
Let $P_N$ project to $\mathcal P_N$ and $P_{\mathcal D_N}$ to
$\mathcal D_N$ in that pairing. Differentiating $T_NR_N=I$
gives $R_N'x\in\mathcal D_N$. Differentiating orthogonality
against each fixed boundary polynomial gives
\[
 R_N'=-P_{\mathcal D_N}XR_N=:-I_N,
 \qquad O_N:=(1-P_N)XR_N.
 \tag{TVB.17}
\]
Every derivative is an actual boundary with the primitive
(TVB.9). Differentiating its norm, including the derivative of
the measure, gives
\[
 G_N'=R_N^*XR_N,\quad
 G_N''=R_N^*X^2R_N-2R_N^*XP_{\mathcal D_N}XR_N.
\]
The projection to $\im R_N$ is $R_NG_N^{-1}R_N^*$ and,
together with $P_{\mathcal D_N}$, sums to $P_N$.
Subtracting the square of the first derivative therefore proves
\[
 G_N''-G_N'G_N^{-1}G_N'=O_N^*O_N-I_N^*I_N.
 \tag{TVB.18}
\]
This is an equality of maps $C\to C^{\mathrm{anti}*}$, where
$C^{\mathrm{anti}*}$ consists of conjugate-linear functionals on $C$.
For example $O_N^*O_N$ sends $x$ to the functional
$y\mapsto\langle O_Ny,O_Nx\rangle_\theta$; this assignment is
complex-linear in $x$. The fixed coordinate Euclidean pairing
identifies that antidual with the written matrix coordinates.

Write $b_j=[p_j]_\chi$, and
$a=b_N^*G_Nb_N$, $d_+=b_{N+1}^*G_Nb_{N+1}$.
The canonical next boundary has the particularly exact factorization
\[
 \mathfrak b_N:=p_{N+1}-R_Nb_{N+1}=\chi r_m,
 \qquad \nu_m=\omega_{N+1}+d_+.
 \tag{TVB.19}
\]
To prove it, $\chi r_m$ is monic of degree $N+1$ and orthogonal
to $\mathcal D_N$. Subtract $p_{N+1}$; the difference lies in
$\mathcal P_N\cap\mathcal D_N^\perp$ and has reduction
$-b_{N+1}$. Uniqueness of $R_N$ proves the first equality;
the two terms in its definition are orthogonal, giving the second.
The coefficient of $p_N$ in $R_Nx$ is $b_N^*G_Nx/\omega_N$.
The leading coefficient of $X$ as an $S$-polynomial is $1/i=-i$.
Consequently the full maps are
\[
 O_Nx=-i\,p_{N+1}\frac{b_N^*G_Nx}{\omega_N},\qquad
 I_Nx=-i\,\chi r_{m-1}\frac{b_{N+1}^*G_Nx}{\nu_{m-1}}
 \quad(m\ge1),\qquad I_N=0\ (m=0).
 \tag{TVB.20}
\]
For the second equation, $X\chi r_j$ is an old boundary if
$j\le m-2$. For $j=m-1$ its coefficient of $\chi r_m$ is
$-i$. Moving $X$ across the pairing conjugates this coefficient
to $i$, and $\langle\chi r_m,R_Nx\rangle
=-b_{N+1}^*G_Nx$ by (TVB.19). Division by the original norm
$\nu_{m-1}$ gives exactly the second sign in (TVB.20).

Both maps have rank at most one. For $N\ge q$, put
$\delta_N=V_N/V_{N-1}$. The rank-one calculation in the next
section gives $a/\omega_N=1-\delta_N$ and
$\delta_N=\omega_N/\nu_{m-1}$. Taking traces in (TVB.20) gives
the separate, positive source costs
\[
 \begin{split}
 \tr(G_N^{-1}O_N^*O_N)&=\alpha_{N+1}(1-\delta_N),\\
 \tr(G_N^{-1}I_N^*I_N)&=\beta_m-\alpha_{N+1}\delta_N
                   =\frac{\nu_m-\omega_{N+1}}{\nu_{m-1}}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{TVB.21}
\]
Their difference is $\alpha_{N+1}-\beta_m$ as required.
Thus the individual positive map costs each retain the common
quantity $\alpha_{N+1}\delta_N$; assigning them separately
the two Toda coefficients would lose this quantity.
At $N=q-1$, the outer cost is $\alpha_q$ and the inner cost is
zero. If a single notation for the cost identities is wanted at
this endpoint, define only the scalar convention
$\delta_{q-1}^{\rm end}:=\det K_{q-2}/\det K_{q-1}=0$;
the included column has leverage $a/\omega_{q-1}=1$.
Together with $\beta_0=0$ the cost identities are
$\alpha_q(1-\delta_{q-1}^{\rm end})=\alpha_q$ and
$\beta_0-\alpha_q\delta_{q-1}^{\rm end}=0$.
This convention is not a quotient involving $V_{q-2}$ and does
not introduce an inverse for $K_{q-2}$ or a norm $\nu_{-1}$.

$Q=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&Y\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$
for a unique map $Y:C_-\to C_+$. Block multiplication proves
\[
 \mathscr S:\operatorname{Hom}(C_-,C_+)
       \longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(C_-,C_+),\quad
 \mathscr S(Y)=BY-YD^{\rm op}=\mathsf C,
 \qquad Q-\mathsf P=
 \begin{pmatrix}0&Y\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{TVB.42}
\]
This morphism is invertible with its full original nilpotent parts:
\[
 \mathscr S^{-1}(Z)=\int_0^\infty e^{-tB}Ze^{tD^{\rm op}}\,dt.
 \tag{TVB.43}
\]
Indeed every selected eigenvalue has real part greater than $c$
and every remaining one has real part at most $c$. On a full
block at $\lambda$ the first exponential is exactly
$e^{-t\lambda}\sum_{j<\ell_\lambda}(-t)^j(B-\lambda I)^j/j!$,
with its corresponding projector, and the second has the same
formula with positive $t$ on its remaining blocks. Summing these
finite expressions gives a polynomial times a strictly decaying
exponential for their product in (TVB.43). This proves convergence
in the original operator norm. Differentiating the integrand gives
$-B e^{-tB}Ze^{tD^{\rm op}}+e^{-tB}Ze^{tD^{\rm op}}D^{\rm op}$.
Its integral has lower endpoint $Z$ and zero upper endpoint,
proving the inverse identity. If $BY-YD^{\rm op}=0$, the same
product with $Z=Y$ is constant and decays to zero, hence $Y=0$.
This proves uniqueness as well. The space $C_-$ is nonzero:
otherwise $C_+=C$ and $L=\tr\mathcal H_N=0$, contradicting $L>0$.
Together with $C_+\ne0$ this makes the unit sphere in
$\operatorname{Hom}(C_-,C_+)$ nonempty. The actual smallest singular
value $s_{B,D}>0$ of $\mathscr S$ in the original Hilbert--Schmidt
pairing exists by compactness of its unit sphere and injectivity.
Consequently
$\|\mathsf C\|_{\mathrm{HS},G_N}
 \ge s_{B,D}\|Q-\mathsf P\|_{\mathrm{HS},G_N}$.
No bound on $s_{B,D}$ independent of the arithmetic inputs is used.

Retaining the phase and coupling in (TVB.41) gives the finite
strengthening
\[
 \log\frac{V_{N-1}}{V_{N+1}}
 \ge2\operatorname{arsinh}\left(
 \frac{\sqrt{L^2+\psi_N^2+\|\mathsf C\|_{\mathrm{HS},G_N}^2}}
      {\sqrt{\alpha_{N+1}}}\right).
 \tag{TVB.44}
\]
It follows by subtracting only the nonnegative imbalance and
compression-determinant terms and applying the same inverse
$\sinh$ as in (TVB.35). Summation over the disjoint indices
$N=N_0+2j$ has the same exact endpoints as (TVB.36), with this
degree-dependent square root in each summand. If $C_+=0$, use
(TVB.32) directly; no positive-eigenline compression is asserted
when $\epsilon_N=0$. At $N=q-1$, the scalar term instead uses
the separate endpoint expression (TVB.38). These identities supply
no asymptotic estimate and change no arithmetic class.
