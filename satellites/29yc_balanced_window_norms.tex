\subsection[\hspace{0.4em}Original measure and constants]{Original measure and constants}\label{balanced-window-original-measure-and-constants}

Fix a monic finite divisor h of \(g=2\xi\), removing its selected zeros with their full orders, and put \(d=\deg h\). On the original line \(S=k/2+iu\), use

\[
\begin{aligned}
w_h(t)&=\frac{|(g/h)(1/2+it)|^2}{2\pi},\qquad m_{h,k}=w_h^{*k},\\
\omega_{h,k,j}&=\inf_{\substack{P\text{ monic}\\\deg P=j}}
\int |P(k/2+iu)|^2m_{h,k}(u)\,du.
\end{aligned}
\]

The mass is \(\mu_h^k\), not one. The original note proves, for \(a=\pi/2\) and \(B=42+2d\),

\[
m_{h,k}(u)\ge c_h\vartheta_h^{k-3}
\exp[-a(|u|+k-3)](1+|u|+k-3)^{-B},\qquad k\ge3,
\]

where \(c_h=b_hc_h^{(1)}>0\), \(b_h=\min_{|v|\le1}(w_h*w_h)(v)>0\), and \(\vartheta_h=\int_{[-1,1]}w_h>0\). Fix \(0<b<a\) and retain both actual finite exponential moments \(M_+=\int\exp(bt)w_h(t)\,dt\) and \(M_-=\int\exp(-bt)w_h(t)\,dt\). Define \(X=\max(1,M_+,M_-)\) and \(K=\max(1,\vartheta_h^{-1})\). None of these definitions normalizes the source.

\subsection[\hspace{0.4em}Exact preceding estimate]{Exact preceding estimate}\label{balanced-window-exact-preceding-estimate}

The monic Lebesgue minimum, allowing any leading coefficient of modulus one, is

\[
\ell_n(L)=\frac{2L^{2n+1}}{2n+1}
\left[\frac{2^n(n!)^2}{(2n)!}\right]^2.
\]

This includes the original leading phase \(i^n\) under \(S=k/2+iu\). For integers \(n\ge0\), \(r\ge1\), \(k\ge3\) and any \(L>0\), the trial-monomial exponential upper bound and interval Legendre lower bound give

\[
\begin{aligned}
A_{h,k,n,r}&:=\left(\frac{\omega_{h,k,n+r}}{\omega_{h,k,n}}\right)^{1/(2r)}\\
&\le\left[
\frac{(2(n+r))!b^{-2(n+r)}(M_+^k+M_-^k)
\exp[a(L+k-3)](1+L+k-3)^B}
{c_h\vartheta_h^{k-3}\ell_n(L)}\right]^{1/(2r)}.
\end{aligned}\tag{BV.1}
\]

For the balanced-window theorem take integers \(n\ge k\ge3\) and \(n/2\le r\le n\), and set \(L=n\). Then

\[
A_{h,k,n,r}\le\mathrm{Cbal}_h n,\tag{BV.2}
\]
\[
\mathrm{Cbal}_h=256\exp(\pi)\max(1,3/c_h)XK
\max(1,b^{-3})6^{B/3}.\tag{BV.3}
\]

\subsubsection[\hspace{0.4em}Proof of the uniform constant]{Proof of the uniform constant}\label{balanced-window-proof-of-the-uniform-constant}

The central-binomial bound gives \(\ell_n(n)\ge2n^{2n+1}/[(2n+1)4^n]\). Also \((2(n+r))!\le(4n)^{2(n+r)}\) and \(M_+^k+M_-^k\le2X^k\). Combining these before taking roots cancels the factor two. The factorial, Legendre and \(c_h\) factors after the 2r-th root are at most

\[
n\cdot4\cdot8^{n/r}\left[\frac{2n+1}{c_hn}\right]^{1/(2r)}
\le256n\max(1,3/c_h).
\]

Here \(n/r\le2\), \((2n+1)/n\le3\), and \(0<1/(2r)\le1\). The remaining factors obey

\[
\begin{aligned}
X^{k/(2r)}&\le X,\\
\vartheta_h^{-(k-3)/(2r)}&\le K,\\
b^{-(n+r)/r}&\le\max(1,b^{-3}),\\
\exp[a(n+k-3)/(2r)]&\le\exp(2a)=\exp(\pi).
\end{aligned}
\]

Finally \(1+n+k-3\le2n\) and \(2r\ge n\). The function \(\log(2x)/x\) decreases for \(x\ge3\), since its derivative is \([1-\log(2x)]/x^2<0\). Hence

\[
(1+n+k-3)^{B/(2r)}\le(2n)^{B/n}\le6^{B/3}.
\]

Multiplication proves BV.2-BV.3. This proof works for the original arithmetic density without an evenness hypothesis. The constants depend on fixed h and b only, never n,k,r.

\subsection[\hspace{0.4em}Composition with the exact consecutive-window product]{Composition with the exact consecutive-window product}\label{balanced-window-composition-with-the-exact-consecutive-window-product}

This paragraph assumes the separately reviewed exact-window theorem for the original canonical quotient family, including its nonnegative contractions and zero-contraction cases. Write \(V_N=\det G_N>0\) and \(\Lambda_N=\omega_N/V_N\). Its simple product corollary is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\min_{n\le N<n+r}\epsilon_N
&\le\left(\frac{\Lambda_{n+r}}{\Lambda_n}\right)^{1/(2r)}\\
&=A_{h,k,n,r}\left(\frac{V_n}{V_{n+r}}\right)^{1/(2r)}.
\end{aligned}\tag{BV.4}
\]

Do not infer BV.4 from BV.1 alone. It uses the original rank-two Toda identities and the retained source/quotient maps. If every allowance on the window has the exterior lower bound \(L_{h,k}\), BV.2-BV.4 imply

\[
\log\frac{V_n}{V_{n+r}}\ge2r\log\left[\frac{L_{h,k}}{\mathrm{Cbal}_hn}\right].\tag{BV.5}
\]

This holds even if the right side is negative; canonical volume monotonicity independently gives a nonnegative left side.

For the packet consisting exactly of the hypothetical off-line quartet \(1/2\pm\delta\pm i\gamma\), with fixed \(\delta,\gamma>0\) and common multiplicity \(m\ge1\), retain

\[
\begin{aligned}
q=q_k&=[1+k(m-1)](k+1)^2,\\
L_{h,k}&=2\delta[1+k(m-1)](k+1)
\left\lfloor\frac{(k+1)^2}{4}\right\rfloor,\\
q&\ge k\ge3,\qquad L_{h,k}/q\ge\delta k/2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Take \(n=q\) and \(r=q-1\). Since \(q\ge3\), \(q/2\le q-1\le q\); the degrees \(q,\ldots,2q-2\) are admitted and the norm endpoint is 2q-1, not 2q. Define the central two-volume cost

\[
C_k=\log\frac{V_q}{V_{2q-1}}.
\]

Then

\[
C_k\ge2(q-1)\log\left[\frac{\delta k}{2\mathrm{Cbal}_h}\right].\tag{BV.6}
\]

Canonical monotonicity gives \(V_{q-1}\ge V_q\) and \(V_{2q-1}\ge V_{2q}\). Consequently each outer cost dominates this same central cost:

\[
\log\frac{V_{q-1}}{V_{2q-1}}\ge C_k,\qquad
\log\frac{V_q}{V_{2q}}\ge C_k.
\]

For the original four-volume budget \(B_k=\log[V_{q-1}V_q/(V_{2q-1}V_{2q})]\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
B_k&\ge2C_k\ge4(q-1)\log\left[\frac{\delta k}{2\mathrm{Cbal}_h}\right],\\
\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{B_k}{q_k\log k}&\ge4.
\end{aligned}\tag{BV.7}
\]

Indeed \(q_k\ge(k+1)^2\) tends to infinity, so \((q_k-1)/q_k\) tends to one; fixed positive \(\delta/(2\mathrm{Cbal}_h)\) contributes only \(O_h(q_k)\). The diagonal choice \(r=q\) also yields \(\log(V_q/V_{2q})\ge2q\log[\delta k/(2\mathrm{Cbal}_h)]\), but the overlapping shorter window proves the stronger four-volume threshold.

The supplied note's older threshold 2 remains correct under its weaker sinh endpoint estimate. BV.7 is a strengthened synthesis using BV.4, not a correction to that valid implication. No upper estimate on \(B_k\) or \(C_k\) follows. A separately proved \(\limsup B_k/(q_k\log k)<4\) would contradict this quartet; no such upper estimate and no RH proof is asserted here.
