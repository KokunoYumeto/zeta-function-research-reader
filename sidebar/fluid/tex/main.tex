\section{The exact target}
\label{sec:target}
We retain the dimensional viscosity $\nu>0$. The unknowns are
$u:\R^3\times[0,\infty)\to\R^3$ and
$p:\R^3\times[0,\infty)\to\R$, with prescribed data
$u^0:\R^3\to\R^3$ and $f:\R^3\times[0,\infty)\to\R^3$.
The sign convention is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns}
\partial_tu_i+\sum_{j=1}^3u_j\partial_{x_j}u_i
=\nu\sum_{j=1}^3\partial_{x_j}^2u_i-\partial_{x_i}p+f_i,
\qquad \sum_{i=1}^3\partial_{x_i}u_i=0,
\qquad u(x,0)=u^0(x).
\end{equation}
These are the equations and alternatives in Fefferman's official statement,
including its appended pressure-periodicity correction\cite{Fefferman}.
No change of spatial metric is implicit in the Euclidean Laplacian above.

For the whole-space alternatives, the prescribed initial data must satisfy,
for every multi-index $\alpha\in\mathbb N_0^3$ and every $K\in\mathbb N_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:schwartz-data}
|\partial_x^\alpha u^0(x)|\le C_{\alpha K}(1+|x|)^{-K}.
\end{equation}
The prescribed force must satisfy for every such $\alpha,K$ and
$m\in\mathbb N_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:schwartz-force}
|\partial_x^\alpha\partial_t^mf(x,t)|
\le C_{\alpha m K}(1+|x|+t)^{-K}\quad(x\in\R^3,t\ge0).
\end{equation}
The required solution is smooth on the whole closed time half-space and has
one finite bound $C$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy}
\int_{\R^3}|u(x,t)|^2\,dx<C\quad\hbox{for every }t\ge0.
\end{equation}
For the periodic alternatives, the domain is exactly $\R^3/\Z^3$:
$u^0$, $f$, $u$, and $p$ are invariant under $x\mapsto x+e_j$,
$j=1,2,3$. Here $e_j$ are the original unit coordinate vectors. The force
condition is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:periodic-force}
|\partial_x^\alpha\partial_t^mf(x,t)|
\le C_{\alpha m K}(1+|t|)^{-K}.
\end{equation}
In particular a spatially affine pressure cannot be inserted into a periodic
counterexample while preserving the corrected target.

Alternative A asserts existence of a smooth solution with
\eqref{eq:energy} for every divergence-free datum satisfying
\eqref{eq:schwartz-data}, with $f=0$. Alternative B asserts smooth periodic
existence for every smooth divergence-free periodic datum, with $f=0$.
Alternative C asks for data satisfying \eqref{eq:schwartz-data} and a force
satisfying \eqref{eq:schwartz-force} for which no global smooth solution with
\eqref{eq:energy} exists. Alternative D asks for smooth periodic data and a
force satisfying \eqref{eq:periodic-force} for which no global smooth periodic
solution exists. All four alternatives retain $\nu>0$ and dimension three.
A velocity expression that is singular is therefore not by itself a
counterexample: its prescribed data and force and the nonexistence conclusion
must belong to one of these exact alternatives.
