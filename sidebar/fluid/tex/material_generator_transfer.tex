\section{The full spatial material generator of the inverse-fibre flow}
\label{sec:material-generator}

Keep the original real coordinates $q=(x,y,w)$, polynomial $F$, Jacobian
$J=DF$, and vector field $U=2W_1+6F_3W_2$ from
Section~\ref{sec:inverse-fibre-heat}. That section proves $U\Pi=\Pi_{rr}$
on the product with the inverse-root variable $r$. Here we calculate the
actual three-dimensional spatial diffusion, covectors, vorticity,
pressure, function spaces and time changes of this same flow.

\subsection{The maximal flow and its complete differential}

Use $\xi=(\xi_1,\xi_2,\xi_3)$ for the original initial point and
$X_\tau(\xi)$ for the maximal solution of
\[
 \partial_\tau X_\tau(\xi)=U(X_\tau(\xi)),\qquad X_0(\xi)=\xi.
\]
Local existence at every finite $\xi$ follows directly from the integral
equation: on a closed ball, $U$ and $DU$ are bounded, so for sufficiently
short times $Y\mapsto\xi+\int_0^\tau U(Y(s))\,ds$ preserves the ball and
is a contraction in the supremum norm. Its fixed point solves the ODE;
the same Lipschitz estimate gives uniqueness. Differentiating the integral
equation gives the variational equation, and successive differentiation
gives smooth dependence on time and initial point. Continuation over
overlapping such neighborhoods defines the maximal flow.

Let $\mathcal D_\tau$ be the open set of initial points whose trajectory
exists throughout the interval joining $0$ to $\tau$. Openness follows
by covering that finite trajectory segment with the local neighborhoods
just constructed. Uniqueness gives the inverse $X_{-\tau}$, so
$X_\tau:\mathcal D_\tau\to\mathcal D_{-\tau}$ is a diffeomorphism.
We do not replace either domain by all of $\R^3$.
Define
\[
 A_\tau=D_\xi X_\tau,\quad C_\tau=A_\tau^{-1},\quad
 B_\tau=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&6\tau\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Differentiating $F\circ X_\tau=\Phi_\tau\circ F$ gives the exact formulas
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mg-A}
 A_\tau=J(X_\tau)^{-1}B_\tau J(\xi),\qquad
 C_\tau=J(\xi)^{-1}B_\tau^{-1}J(X_\tau),\qquad \det A_\tau=1.
\end{equation}
Both determinants of $J$ are the retained value $-2$, and $\det B_\tau=1$,
which proves the determinant assertion. Direct differentiation of the
ODE also gives $\partial_\tau A_\tau=DU(X_\tau)A_\tau$.

The material metric and its inverse are therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mg-metric}
 g_\tau=A_\tau^{\mathsf T}A_\tau,\quad
 K_\tau=g_\tau^{-1}=C_\tau C_\tau^{\mathsf T},\quad
 d\mathrm{vol}_{g_\tau}=d\xi_1\,d\xi_2\,d\xi_3.
\end{equation}
In particular the full tensor is
\[
 K_\tau=J(\xi)^{-1}B_\tau^{-1}J(X_\tau)J(X_\tau)^{\mathsf T}
 B_\tau^{-\mathsf T}J(\xi)^{-\mathsf T}.
\]
Every matrix retains its evaluation point; a constant metric or a scalar
multiple of the identity has not been substituted for this tensor.

\subsection{Scalar diffusion, function spaces and energy}

For a scalar $f(\tau,q)$ put $\widehat f=f\circ X_\tau$.
The time and spatial chain rules are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mg-chain}
 \partial_\tau\widehat f=((\partial_\tau+U\cdot\nabla_q)f)\circ X_\tau,
 \qquad
 (\nabla_qf)\circ X_\tau=C_\tau^{\mathsf T}\nabla_\xi\widehat f.
\end{equation}
With repeated indices summed from $1$ to $3$ and $\epsilon_{123}=1$,
the cofactor formula in this index convention gives
\[
 (C_\tau)_{ik}=\tfrac12\epsilon_{iab}\epsilon_{k\ell m}
 (A_\tau)_{\ell a}(A_\tau)_{mb},\qquad
 \partial_{\xi_i}(C_\tau)_{ik}=0.
\]
The first equality uses $\det A_\tau=1$. For the second, differentiate
the first equality. The derivative of the first factor contains
$\partial_i\partial_aX^\ell$, whose symmetry in $i,a$ makes its
contraction with $\epsilon_{iab}$ zero. The derivative of the second
factor similarly vanishes by symmetry in $i,b$.
Consequently the complete spatial diffusion operator is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mg-diffusion}
 (\Delta_qf)\circ X_\tau=\mathcal L_\tau\widehat f,\qquad
 \mathcal L_\tau h=\partial_{\xi_i}
                    ((K_\tau)_{ij}\partial_{\xi_j}h).
\end{equation}
Indeed after composition $\partial_{q_k}=C_{ik}\partial_{\xi_i}$;
applying it twice and using the cofactor divergence formula proves
\eqref{eq:mg-diffusion}. The first-order coefficient
$\partial_{\xi_i}(K_\tau)_{ij}$ is part of this formula.
For every retained $\nu>0$ we have equality of full operators,
\[
 ((\partial_\tau+U\cdot\nabla_q-\nu\Delta_q)f)\circ X_\tau
 =(\partial_\tau-\nu\mathcal L_\tau)\widehat f.
\]

For an open $D\Subset\mathcal D_\tau$, change of variables gives
\begin{align}
 \int_{X_\tau(D)}|f|^2\,dq&=\int_D|\widehat f|^2\,d\xi,\notag\\
 \int_{X_\tau(D)}|\nabla_q f|^2\,dq
 &=\int_D(\nabla_\xi\overline{\widehat f})^{\mathsf T}
                       K_\tau\nabla_\xi\widehat f\,d\xi .
 \label{eq:mg-dirichlet}
\end{align}
For compact smooth $h$, integration by parts also gives
$-\int_D\overline h\,\mathcal L_\tau h
=\int_D(\nabla\overline h)^{\mathsf T}K_\tau\nabla h$.
The continuous positive-definite matrix $K_\tau$ on $\overline D$
has a strictly positive minimum eigenvalue and a finite maximum eigenvalue.
These bounds depend on $D,\tau$ and are not asserted uniform at escape.

Define $H^1_0(D)$ by completion of $C_c^\infty(D)$ in the ordinary
$H^1$ norm, and use the analogous definition on $X_\tau(D)$.
The two eigenvalue bounds and \eqref{eq:mg-dirichlet} show that
composition by $X_\tau$ extends to a bounded invertible map between
these spaces, an isometry for the displayed material Dirichlet norm,
and a unitary map of their $L^2$ spaces. The exact Dirichlet operator
domains are carried bijectively as follows:
\[
 \{f\in H^1_0(X_\tau(D)):\Delta_qf\in L^2\}
 \ \xrightarrow{\ f\mapsto f\circ X_\tau\ }\
 \{h\in H^1_0(D):\mathcal L_\tau h\in L^2\}.
\]
Both derivative conditions are distributional. To prove the assertion,
test a distributional derivative against a compact smooth function,
use \eqref{eq:mg-dirichlet} in bilinear form, and change variables.
The inverse change of variables proves the converse. Completion of
compact smooth functions supplies the specified boundary conditions.

For a real phase $\varphi$, a material covector $\ell=d_\xi\widehat\varphi$
corresponds to the physical covector $C_\tau^{\mathsf T}\ell$.
Its exact principal diffusive quadratic form is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mg-symbol}
 \nu\,\ell^{\mathsf T}K_\tau\ell
       =\nu\,|C_\tau^{\mathsf T}\ell|^2.
\end{equation}
Thus stretching of this actual covector and its diffusion cost are
related by equality, including the original viscosity.

\subsection{Vectors, covectors, pressure and vorticity}

For a physical vector $V$ use material vector coefficients
$v=C_\tau(V\circ X_\tau)$. Their metric dual is
$v^{\flat_{g_\tau}}=g_\tau v=A_\tau^{\mathsf T}(V\circ X_\tau)$.
The exact divergence and connection formulas are
\[
 (\operatorname{div}_q V)\circ X_\tau=\partial_{\xi_i}v^i,\qquad
 (\Gamma_\tau)^k_{ij}=(C_\tau)_{k\ell}\partial_{\xi_i}\partial_{\xi_j}X^\ell_\tau.
\]
The first is the proved cofactor product rule. To prove the connection
formula, differentiate $A_\tau v$ in a material direction and multiply
by $C_\tau$. Its lower indices are symmetric; differentiating
$g=A^{\mathsf T}A$ proves metric compatibility, identifying precisely
the Levi--Civita connection. Consequently, if $z=C_\tau(Z\circ X_\tau)$,
\[
 C_\tau\bigl(((Z\cdot\nabla_q)V)\circ X_\tau\bigr)=\nabla^{g_\tau}_zv,
 \qquad C_\tau((\nabla_qp)\circ X_\tau)=K_\tau\nabla_\xi\widehat p.
\]
The full vector diffusion is
\begin{align}
 \mathscr L_\tau v
 &:=C_\tau\mathcal L_\tau(A_\tau v)
   =C_\tau((\Delta_qV)\circ X_\tau)\notag\\
 &=\mathcal L_\tau v+
  2(K_\tau)_{ij}C_\tau(\partial_iA_\tau)\partial_jv
   +C_\tau(\mathcal L_\tau A_\tau)v .
 \label{eq:mg-vector-diffusion}
\end{align}
The last equality is the full product rule, using the symmetry of $K$;
$\mathcal L_\tau$ acts componentwise on each displayed matrix or vector.
This also equals the nonpositive Hodge vector Laplacian of $g_\tau$:
the orientation-preserving local isometry $X_\tau$ transports $d$
and the metric codifferential to their Euclidean versions, whose
negative Hodge Laplacian is the Cartesian component Laplacian.

Put $E_\tau=C_\tau\partial_\tau A_\tau$ and
$u=C_\tau(U\circ X_\tau)$. Differentiating $C_\tau A_\tau=I$ gives
\[
 \partial_\tau v+E_\tau v
 =C_\tau\bigl(((\partial_\tau+U\cdot\nabla_q)V)\circ X_\tau\bigr).
\]
Therefore the entire Navier--Stokes residual of any smooth $V,p,f$
is carried to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mg-momentum}
 \partial_\tau v+E_\tau v+\nabla^{g_\tau}_{v-u}v
  +K_\tau\nabla_\xi\widehat p-\nu\mathscr L_\tau v-\mathfrak f,
 \qquad \mathfrak f=C_\tau(f\circ X_\tau).
\end{equation}
This expression is $C_\tau$ times the composed original residual,
by adding the proved time and connection identities. Its vanishing is
equivalent to vanishing of that residual because $C_\tau$ is invertible.

For the actual stationary $U$ we have exactly $u(\tau,\xi)=U(\xi)$:
$J(\xi)U(\xi)=(2,6F_3(\xi),0)$, $F_3(X_\tau)=F_3(\xi)$,
and $B_\tau$ fixes this vector; \eqref{eq:mg-A} therefore gives
$A_\tau U(\xi)=U(X_\tau)$. The material vector coefficients are constant
in time, while $g_\tau,E_\tau$ and both diffusion operators vary.
Physical velocity and vorticity norms keep these changes:
\[
 |U(X_\tau)|^2=u^{\mathsf T}g_\tau u,\qquad
 |\omega(X_\tau)|^2=W^{\mathsf T}g_\tau W,\quad
 W=C_\tau(\omega\circ X_\tau),\quad\omega=\curl_qU.
\]

Choose any smooth pressure $p$ and the actual full force
$f=(U\cdot\nabla)U-\nu\Delta U+\nabla p$ already computed in
Section~\ref{sec:inverse-fibre-heat}. Put
$\beta=X_\tau^*(U^\flat)=g_\tau u$ and $\Omega=d_\xi\beta$.
In the original Euclidean coordinates,
\[
 (\partial_\tau+\mathcal L_U)U^\flat
 =\nu\Delta_qU^\flat+f^\flat-d_q(p-|U|^2/2).
\]
Here $\mathcal L_U$ means Lie derivative, distinct from the scalar
operator $\mathcal L_\tau$. Its extra covector term is
$(DU)^{\mathsf T}U=\nabla_q(|U|^2/2)$, proving the displayed identity.
Differentiating $A_\tau^{\mathsf T}(U\circ X_\tau)$ shows that
pullback of this Lie derivative is the ordinary material time derivative.
Hence, with $\Delta_H=d\delta+\delta d$ nonnegative by convention,
\begin{align}
 \partial_\tau\beta
 &=-\nu\Delta_{H,g_\tau}\beta+g_\tau\mathfrak f
       -d_\xi(\widehat p-u^{\mathsf T}g_\tau u/2),\notag\\
 \partial_\tau\Omega
 &=-\nu\Delta_{H,g_\tau}\Omega+d_\xi(g_\tau\mathfrak f).
 \label{eq:mg-vorticity-form}
\end{align}
The second line follows by applying $d_\xi$: both $d^2=0$ and
$d\Delta_H=\Delta_Hd$ follow from the displayed definition of $\Delta_H$.
This commutation is at each fixed metric time and does not differentiate
the time-dependent metric codifferential.

Equivalently, the contravariant vorticity obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mg-vorticity-vector}
 \partial_\tau W=\nu\mathscr L_\tau W+\curl_{g_\tau}\mathfrak f.
\end{equation}
Indeed taking Cartesian curl and using $\operatorname{div}U=0$ gives
$(\partial_\tau+U\cdot\nabla)\omega
=DU\,\omega+\nu\Delta\omega+\curl f$.
The time product rule for $C_\tau$ cancels precisely the stretching
term, since $E_\tau W=C_\tau DU(X_\tau)A_\tau W$.
The orientation-preserving isometry transports the force curl as
$C_\tau((\curl f)\circ X_\tau)=\curl_{g_\tau}\mathfrak f$.
In material coordinates this last vector is
$\epsilon_{ijk}\partial_{\xi_j}((g_\tau)_{k\ell}\mathfrak f^\ell)$;
its volume denominator is one because $\det g_\tau=1$.
Pressure disappears from this curl identity by $d^2=0$, with the
full pressure still present in \eqref{eq:mg-momentum}.

\subsection{Accumulated time keeps its acceleration and diffusion costs}

Define an explicit time change, with its full inverse,
\[
 \rho(s)=\frac{1-e^{-8s}}8,\quad \sigma(s)=\rho'(s)=e^{-8s},
 \quad s\ge0,\qquad s=-\tfrac18\log(1-8\tau).
\]
It maps $[0,\infty)$ bijectively to $[0,1/8)$. The exact spatial map is
$Y_s=X_{\rho(s)}:\mathcal D_{\rho(s)}\to\mathcal D_{-\rho(s)}$,
and it satisfies $\partial_sY_s=\sigma(s)U(Y_s)$.
For a scalar material function $h$, on
$\{(s,\xi):s\ge0,\ \xi\in\mathcal D_{\rho(s)}\}$ the complete chain rule is
\[
 [(\partial_\tau-\nu\mathcal L_\tau)h]_{\tau=\rho(s)}
 =\sigma(s)^{-1}
  (\partial_s-\nu\sigma(s)\mathcal L_{\rho(s)})h(\rho(s),\xi).
\]
Thus this time change transports the diffusion coefficient as displayed.

For the actual stationary fields set
$\widetilde U(s,q)=\sigma(s)U(q)$ and
$\widetilde p(s,q)=\sigma(s)^2p(q)$.
The velocity remains divergence-free. At the same viscosity $\nu>0$
its exact force is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mg-time-force}
 \widetilde f=\sigma' U+\sigma^2f
                         +\nu(\sigma^2-\sigma)\Delta_qU.
\end{equation}
The time derivative gives $\sigma'U$, convection and pressure give
their old values multiplied by $\sigma^2$, and diffusion gives
$-\nu\sigma\Delta U$; inserting the original complete $f$ proves the
formula. The previously computed axis values
$\curl U=(0,0,6)$, $\Delta\curl U=(0,0,-108w)$ and
$\curl f=(0,0,108\nu w)$ give the exact resulting curl
\[
 \curl\widetilde f(s,0,0,w)
 =(0,0,108\nu e^{-8s}w-48e^{-8s}).
\]
It remains unbounded in $w$ at every finite $s$ for every pressure
choice and $\nu>0$. The corresponding actual trajectory is
\[
 Y_s(1,-3/2,13/2)
 =(e^{4s},-\tfrac32e^{-4s},\tfrac{13}{2}e^{-8s}).
\]
It escapes as $s\to\infty$ in this coordinate. These maps preserve
the original escape at $\tau=1/8$, its spatial location at infinity,
the full spatial diffusion and the exact force. They do not establish
an admissible finite-energy Euclidean Navier--Stokes singularity.
