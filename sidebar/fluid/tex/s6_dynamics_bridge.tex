\subsection{The retained full gauge--scalar equations}

Use the source real state space and metric
\[
 \mathcal E=\operatorname{Herm}_3(\C)_0\oplus\C^3,
 \qquad
 \langle(\phi,c),(\psi,d)\rangle
   =\tr(\phi\psi)+2\operatorname{Re}(c^*d).
\]
Here the subscript means trace zero. Retain signature $(+---)$, source
time $\tau$, the gauge action $(\Phi,b)\mapsto(g\Phi g^*,gb)$ for $g\in SU(3)$,
and the potential
\begin{equation}\label{eq:s6dyn-potential}
 V=\frac{m^2}{2}s+\frac{\Gamma}{4}s^2
       -\kappa(\det\Phi-b^*\Phi b),\qquad
 s=\tr\Phi^2+2b^*b.
\end{equation}
The scalar kinetic density is
$\tfrac12\tr(D_\mu\Phi D^\mu\Phi)+(D_\mu b)^*D^\mu b$.
We use the identically zero connection $\mathcal A_\mu=0$ and retain
$Z_{\rm tf}=Z-\tr(Z)I_3/3$. Set $a=m^2+\Gamma s$. Direct variation gives
\begin{align}
 dV(\delta\Phi,\delta b)
 &=\tr\!\left([a\Phi-\kappa(\operatorname{adj}\Phi-bb^*)_{\rm tf}]
                   \delta\Phi\right)\notag\\
 &\quad+2\operatorname{Re}\!\left[((aI_3+\kappa\Phi)b)^*
                   \delta b\right].\label{eq:s6dyn-variation}
\end{align}
The determinant derivative used here is the polynomial identity
$d(\det\Phi)[H]=\tr((\operatorname{adj}\Phi)H)$, which holds also for
singular matrices by polynomial continuation from the invertible ones.
Integration by parts with the displayed kinetic metric therefore gives
\begin{align}
 \Box\Phi&=-a\Phi+\kappa(\operatorname{adj}\Phi-bb^*)_{\rm tf},\notag\\
 \Box b&=-(aI_3+\kappa\Phi)b,\qquad
 \Box=\partial_\tau^2-\sum_{j=1}^3\partial_{x_j}^2.
 \label{eq:s6dyn-full}
\end{align}
The matter current paired with an anti-Hermitian connection variation is
\[
 J_\mu=[\Phi,\partial_\mu\Phi]
       +b(\partial_\mu b)^*-(\partial_\mu b)b^*.
\]
For the fields used below, this matrix is zero, so its trace-free projection
is zero as well. The Yang--Mills field strength of the zero connection is
zero. Thus all connection equations, including Gauss, hold for the full
variational system, not only for variations restricted to the scalar ansatz.

Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:s6dyn-sector}
 \Phi=\operatorname{diag}(h+k,h-k,-2h),\qquad b=\beta e_3,
 \qquad h,k,\beta\in\R.
\end{equation}
All commutators in $J_\mu$ vanish because the matrices are diagonal;
the remaining two products are equal because $\beta$ and its derivatives
are real. Further,
\begin{align*}
 s&=6h^2+2k^2+2\beta^2,\\
 \det\Phi-b^*\Phi b&=2h(k^2+\beta^2-h^2),\\
 \operatorname{adj}\Phi-bb^*
 &=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(-2h(h-k),-2h(h+k),h^2-k^2-\beta^2\bigr).
\end{align*}
The last trace is $-3h^2-k^2-\beta^2$. Removing exactly one third of it
in \eqref{eq:s6dyn-full}, taking half the sum and difference of the first
two diagonal entries, and using the third component of $b$, gives
\begin{align}
 \Box h&=-ah-\kappa h^2+\frac{\kappa}{3}(k^2+\beta^2),\notag\\
 \Box k&=-(a-2\kappa h)k,\qquad
 \Box\beta=-(a-2\kappa h)\beta,\notag\\
 a&=m^2+\Gamma(6h^2+2k^2+2\beta^2).
 \label{eq:s6dyn-three}
\end{align}
Conversely these equations give every component of
\eqref{eq:s6dyn-full}; all omitted entries are zero. This proves the exact
invariant sector. For homogeneous fields the kinetic density is
$3\dot h^2+\dot k^2+\dot\beta^2$, with Euler--Lagrange weights $6,2,2$.

\subsection{The source oscillator, including its parameter interval}

Retain the source hypotheses
\[
 0<m^2<\frac{\kappa^2}{27\Gamma},\qquad \Gamma>0,\quad\kappa>0,
 \qquad d_0=\sqrt{\kappa^2-24\Gamma m^2},\qquad
 h_0=\frac{\kappa+d_0}{24\Gamma}.
\]
Choose $h_0<h<\kappa/(6\Gamma)$ and define
\begin{align}
 \Delta(h)&=1-\frac{6\Gamma h}{\kappa},&
 a(h)&=\frac{m^2+12\Gamma h^2}{\Delta(h)},\notag\\
 R^2(h)&=\frac{3a(h)h}{\kappa}+3h^2,&
 \omega^2(h)&=a(h)-2\kappa h
   =\frac{m^2-2\kappa h+24\Gamma h^2}{\Delta(h)}.
 \label{eq:s6dyn-parameters}
\end{align}
Take the positive roots $R,\omega$. The square root $d_0$ is real and
$0<d_0<\kappa$; in particular $0<h_0<\kappa/(12\Gamma)$, so the interval

$R^2$. The numerator of $\omega^2$ factors as
\[
 24\Gamma\left(h-\frac{\kappa-d_0}{24\Gamma}\right)(h-h_0),
\]
so $\omega^2>0$. Direct substitution gives the three exact identities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:s6dyn-three-identities}
 a=m^2+\Gamma(6h^2+2R^2),\qquad
 -ah-\kappa h^2+\frac{\kappa R^2}{3}=0,\qquad
 \omega^2=a-2\kappa h.
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:s6dyn-oscillator}
 h(\tau)=h,\qquad k(\tau)=R\cos(\omega\tau),\qquad
 \beta(\tau)=R\sin(\omega\tau),\qquad\mathcal A_\mu=0
\end{equation}
solves the full equations for every real $\tau$. Indeed its squared radius
is $R^2$, its last two second derivatives are $-\omega^2$ times the original
coordinates, and its first second derivative is zero; these are precisely
\eqref{eq:s6dyn-three}. The observable $\|b\|^2=R^2\sin^2(\omega\tau)$
is gauge invariant and nonconstant. In the source polar convention
$k=r\cos(2\theta)$, $\beta=-r\sin(2\theta)$, this same trajectory is
$r=R$, $\theta=-\omega\tau/2$, preserving the original minus sign.

Its conserved energy density, obtained by adding its kinetic and potential
densities without rescaling, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:s6dyn-source-energy}
 E(h)=R^2\omega^2+m^2(3h^2+R^2)+\Gamma(3h^2+R^2)^2
                 -2\kappa h(R^2-h^2).
\end{equation}
On the source flat torus of volume $\mathcal V_3$, the total energy is
$\mathcal V_3E(h)$. This volume and this scalar energy are not identified
with the fluid volume or kinetic energy in the map below.

\subsection{A coefficient map with two nonparallel wave directions}

The target is exactly $\T^3=\R^3/\Z^3$, with coordinates $(x_1,x_2,x_3)$,
Euclidean metric, standard orientation, and volume one. Choose and retain
\[
 n\in\mathbb N_{>0},\qquad \lambda=2\pi n,\qquad
 \nu>0,\quad\eta>0,\quad\sigma>0.
\]
Here $\tau=\sigma t$ is the stated source-to-fluid time dictionary and
$\eta$ converts scalar amplitude to fluid velocity; these are independent
parameters. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:s6dyn-modes}
 B_1=(\sin(\lambda x_3),\cos(\lambda x_3),0),\qquad
 B_2=(0,\sin(\lambda x_1),\cos(\lambda x_1)).
\end{equation}
Both fields are unit-periodic. Their wave directions are $e_3$ and $e_1$.
For example
$\partial_3(B_1)_2=-\lambda\sin(\lambda x_3)$ and
$\partial_3(B_1)_1=\lambda\cos(\lambda x_3)$; inserting these in the
oriented curl gives $\curl B_1=\lambda B_1$. The same calculation with
$\partial_1(B_2)_3=-\lambda\sin(\lambda x_1)$ and
$\partial_1(B_2)_2=\lambda\cos(\lambda x_1)$ gives
$\curl B_2=\lambda B_2$. Componentwise differentiation further proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:s6dyn-mode-identities}
 \operatorname{div}B_j=0,\quad\Delta B_j=-\lambda^2B_j,\quad
 |B_j|^2=1,\quad
 B_1\cdot B_2=c(x):=\cos(\lambda x_3)\sin(\lambda x_1).
\end{equation}
The mean of $c$ is zero, by integration of either sine or cosine over the
unit interval. Therefore $(B_1,B_2)$ is an orthonormal pair in real
$L^2(\T^3;\R^3)$, despite its nonzero pointwise inner product.

Put $d(t)=e^{-\nu\lambda^2t}$. For each finite $t$, the map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:s6dyn-linear-map}
 L_t:\R^2\longrightarrow C^\infty(\T^3;\R^3),\qquad
 L_t(K,B)=\eta d(t)(KB_1+BB_2)
\end{equation}
has kernel zero and image $\operatorname{span}_{\R}\{B_1,B_2\}$.
Its inverse on that image and its inner product are exactly
\begin{align}
 K&=\frac{\int_{\T^3}L_t(K,B)\cdot B_1\,dx}{\eta d(t)},&
 B&=\frac{\int_{\T^3}L_t(K,B)\cdot B_2\,dx}{\eta d(t)},\notag\\
 \langle L_t(K,B),L_t(K',B')\rangle_{L^2}
 &=\eta^2d(t)^2(KK'+BB').\label{eq:s6dyn-inverse}
\end{align}
These statements follow by the two orthogonality integrals, not by a
dimension count. On the source oscillator circle $K^2+B^2=R^2$, its image
is the circle of $L^2$ radius $\eta d(t)R$ in that two-dimensional plane.
The original $h$ is retained as a fixed parameter defining this circle;
the construction does not identify the full three-coordinate state space
or the full gauge configuration space with this plane.

There is also an exact flow relation on this image. Define
$J_EB_1=B_2$ and $J_EB_2=-B_1$ on the two-dimensional image. The
orthogonality already proved gives $J_E^*=-J_E$. For any trajectory
$q(\tau)=(k(\tau),\beta(\tau))$ with
$q'=\omega(-\beta,k)$, direct differentiation of \eqref{eq:s6dyn-linear-map}
gives
\[
 \frac{d}{dt}L_t(q(\sigma t))
   =(-\nu\lambda^2I+\sigma\omega J_E)L_t(q(\sigma t)).
\]
Thus the full coefficient evolution, time conversion, and damping
are included in the map; it is not only a correspondence of time slices.

\subsection{Exact pressure, force, energy, and regularity}

For readability write $K(t)=k(\sigma t)$ and $B(t)=\beta(\sigma t)$.
Then $K'=-\sigma\omega B$, $B'=\sigma\omega K$, and $K^2+B^2=R^2$.
Define the following complete periodic data and solution:
\begin{align}
 u(x,t)&=\eta d(t)(K(t)B_1(x)+B(t)B_2(x)),\notag\\
 p(x,t)&=-\eta^2d(t)^2K(t)B(t)c(x),\notag\\
 f(x,t)&=\eta\sigma\omega d(t)(-B(t)B_1(x)+K(t)B_2(x)),\notag\\
 u^0(x)&=\eta R B_1(x).
 \label{eq:s6dyn-ns-solution}
\end{align}
These satisfy the full three-dimensional classical system
\eqref{eq:ns}, with the stated positive viscosity and its original sign.
To prove this, \eqref{eq:s6dyn-mode-identities} first gives
$\operatorname{div}u=0$, $\curl u=\lambda u$, and $\Delta u=-\lambda^2u$.
The component identity
\[
 ((u\cdot\nabla)u)_i
 =\partial_i\frac{|u|^2}{2}-(u\times\curl u)_i
\]
follows by expanding the two Levi--Civita symbols in the cross product.
Here its cross product is zero. Since
\[
 \frac{|u|^2}{2}
   =\frac{\eta^2d(t)^2R^2}{2}+\eta^2d(t)^2K(t)B(t)c(x),
\]
the first term has zero spatial gradient and
$(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=0$ exactly. This pressure is spatially
nonconstant whenever $KB\ne0$: $\partial_1c=\lambda\cos(\lambda x_3)
\cos(\lambda x_1)$ is not the zero function. Thus the nonlinear
interaction has been calculated, including its nonzero pressure.
Finally $d'=-\nu\lambda^2d$ and the two oscillator derivatives give
$\partial_tu=\nu\Delta u+f$, completing every component of the equation.
At $t=0$ the displayed initial condition follows from $(K,B)=(R,0)$.

The fluid kinetic energy and its exact balance are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:s6dyn-fluid-energy}
 \mathcal E_{\rm fluid}(t)=\frac12\int_{\T^3}|u|^2dx
       =\frac{\eta^2R^2}{2}e^{-2\nu\lambda^2t},\qquad
 \mathcal E_{\rm fluid}'(t)
       +\nu\int_{\T^3}|\nabla u|^2dx=0.
\end{equation}
Indeed integration of $u\cdot f$ gives
$\eta^2\sigma\omega d^2(-KB+BK)=0$. Integration by parts on the
periodic cube gives
$\int|\nabla u|^2=-\int u\cdot\Delta u=\lambda^2\eta^2d^2R^2$,
which proves the balance and agrees with differentiating the energy.
The force does zero \emph{integrated} work; no pointwise zero-work claim
is made, since
$u\cdot f=\eta^2\sigma\omega d^2(K^2-B^2)c(x)$.

All fields in \eqref{eq:s6dyn-ns-solution} are smooth for $t\ge0$.
For each spatial multi-index $\alpha$ and each nonnegative integer $r$,
differentiation of the finite trigonometric expressions gives
\[
 \sup_{x\in\T^3}|\partial_x^\alpha\partial_t^rf(x,t)|
 \le C_{\alpha r}e^{-\nu\lambda^2t},
\]
with a finite constant depending on the retained parameters. For every
integer $N\ge0$, $(1+t)^Ne^{-\nu\lambda^2t}$ is bounded on $[0,\infty)$:
it is continuous on $[0,1]$, and for $t\ge1$ the exponential series
$e^{\nu\lambda^2t}\ge(\nu\lambda^2t)^{N+1}/(N+1)!$ gives decay of
its product with $(1+t)^N$. Thus the force satisfies precisely the
periodic decay condition \eqref{eq:periodic-force}, and the initial data
are smooth, divergence-free, and periodic. The construction gives an
explicit global solution for these admissible data. Its decaying fluid
energy \eqref{eq:s6dyn-fluid-energy} and the constant source energy
\eqref{eq:s6dyn-source-energy} show exactly that this map preserves the
displayed differential equations with the displayed force dictionary;
it is not an energy-preserving identification of the two systems.

\subsection{Gauge equivalence and the exact translation quotient}

The coefficient map above uses the marked diagonal sector. It does not
silently identify gauge-equivalent source matrices with a single fixed
coordinate velocity. This issue has an exact resolution for the fixed
$h$ circle used here. For two states \eqref{eq:s6dyn-sector} with this
same $h>0$, full $SU(3)$ gauge equivalence implies
\[
  \beta'^2=\beta^2,\qquad
 2h(k'^2-h^2)=\det\Phi'=\det\Phi=2h(k^2-h^2),
\]
so $k'^2=k^2$. Conversely every independent change of signs of
$k,\beta$ is a gauge transformation. In fact
\[
 g_b=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,-1),\qquad
 g_s=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\1&0&0\\0&0&-1\end{pmatrix}
\]
are unitary matrices of determinant one. The first sends
$(k,\beta)$ to $(k,-\beta)$, and the second sends it to $(-k,-\beta)$;
their product realizes $(-k,\beta)$. These statements remain valid when
one coordinate is zero. This proves both directions of the orbit
classification within the fixed-$h$ sector, with no generic-orbit
exception.

For a torus translation $T_a v(x)=v(x+a)$, define
\[
 a_1=(0,0,1/(2n)),\qquad a_2=(1/(2n),0,0).
\]
Then
\[
 T_{a_1}B_1=-B_1,\quad T_{a_1}B_2=B_2,\qquad
 T_{a_2}B_1=B_1,\quad T_{a_2}B_2=-B_2.
\]
Consequently the $SU(3)$ orbit relation just calculated corresponds
exactly under $L_t$ to the orbit relation generated by $T_{a_1},T_{a_2}$
on its image. Their action on this image factors through
$(\Z/2\Z)^2$; the translations themselves need not have order two on
the whole torus. The map therefore descends to a bijection between the
fixed-$h$ source circle modulo the stated gauge equivalence and the
image circle modulo these translations. The inverse is supplied by
\eqref{eq:s6dyn-inverse} followed by the sign orbit.

For entire trajectories, a constant sign transformation also transforms
the derivatives $(K',B')$ with the same signs. Applying
\eqref{eq:s6dyn-ns-solution} using these transformed derivatives in
$f=\eta d(K'B_1+B'B_2)$ intertwines all three fields $(u,p,f)$ with
the corresponding fixed torus translation. A single sign reversal
changes the orientation of the source oscillator; it is not to be
inserted into $K'=-\sigma\omega B$, $B'=\sigma\omega K$ while
retaining those first-order equations. The second-order source equations
are unchanged. This proves equivariance of the full construction without
discarding its orientation data.

\subsection{What the fixed curl sector actually evolves}

Let $E_\lambda$ be the real smooth periodic fields satisfying
$\operatorname{div}v=0$ and $\curl v=\lambda v$, for the same
$\lambda=2\pi n>0$. Let $P$ be the flat torus orthogonal projection
onto divergence-free fields, including the constant fields. In Fourier
coordinates its symbol is $P_j=I-jj^{\mathsf T}/|j|^2$ for
$j\in\Z^3\setminus\{0\}$ and $P_0=I$. This definition proves
$P\nabla q=0$ for every smooth periodic scalar $q$.
For any time-dependent field that remains in $E_\lambda$, the component
identity used above gives
\[
 P((u\cdot\nabla)u)=0,\qquad\Delta u=-\lambda^2u.
\]
Projection of the full Navier--Stokes equation therefore yields the
\emph{exact} evolution
\begin{equation}\label{eq:s6dyn-linear-sector}
 \partial_tu+\nu\lambda^2u=Pf,\qquad
 u(t)=e^{-\nu\lambda^2t}u(0)
      +\int_0^t e^{-\nu\lambda^2(t-s)}Pf(s)\,ds.
\end{equation}
The second identity follows by differentiating
$e^{\nu\lambda^2t}u(t)$ and integrating; no nonlinear estimate is
substituted for an equality. Necessarily $Pf(s)\in E_\lambda$ for a
solution that stays in that space. Conversely a smooth force with this
property and an initial field in $E_\lambda$ gives a velocity in
$E_\lambda$ by this formula. The complete pressure reconstruction is
\[
 \widehat q(0,t)=0,\qquad
 \widehat q(j,t)=\frac{j\cdot\widehat f(j,t)}
                         {2\pi i|j|^2}\quad(j\ne0),\qquad
 p=q-\frac{|u|^2}{2}+c_0(t).
\]
Here $c_0$ is any smooth real function. Smoothness of $f$ gives rapid
decay of its Fourier coefficients, including every time derivative
on finite intervals, so this series defines a smooth real $q$.
Its reality follows from the conjugate symmetry of $\widehat f$.
Multiplication of its coefficient by $2\pi i j$ proves
$\nabla q=(I-P)f$. Substitution then gives the full equation, since
$Pf+\nabla(|u|^2/2)+\nabla p-f=0$.

There are only finitely many Fourier frequencies in $E_\lambda$.
Indeed $\Delta u=-\lambda^2u$ gives
$(4\pi^2|j|^2-\lambda^2)\widehat u(j)=0$; hence $|j|=n$, a finite
set of integer triples, and $\widehat u(0)=0$. Every spatial derivative
norm is consequently bounded by a fixed finite sum of absolute values
of these coefficients. Smooth prescribed forcing on a finite closed
time interval has bounded coefficients there. Formula
\eqref{eq:s6dyn-linear-sector} bounds every coefficient and all its
time derivatives on that interval, by differentiation of the finite
scalar integrals. Thus this entire exact invariant sector has no
finite-time singularity under smooth prescribed forcing. This establishes
the behavior of this particular image, not an obstruction to maps whose
images contain interacting curl eigenvalues or an unbounded frequency
sequence.

\subsection{The September 8 Wilson-box vacuum gives a second exact map}

The later source extension
\path{ym_bulk_plaquette_extension.tex} retains an open cubic spatial
graph with vertices $\{-L,\ldots,L\}^3$, $L\in\Z$, $L\ge2$, all
$N=3(2L)(2L+1)^2$ positive links and all $M=3(2L)^2(2L+1)$ elementary
faces. The exact configuration space is the compact connected manifold
\[
 \mathcal C_L=SU(2)^N,
 \qquad\dim_{\R}\mathcal C_L=3N.
\]
The following symbols are the \emph{Wilson source} parameters: its
$\kappa=2g_{\rm YM}^2/a$ is a kinetic coefficient, with $g_{\rm YM}>0$,
and its $a>0$ is a
lattice spacing. They are not the scalar cubic coefficient or $a(h)$
of \eqref{eq:s6dyn-parameters}. Retain
\[
 b=\frac1{2g_{\rm YM}^2a},\qquad
 H=-\kappa\Delta+V,\qquad
 V=b\sum_p(2-W_p),\qquad
 W_p=\tr(U_{e_1}U_{e_2}U_{e_3}^{-1}U_{e_4}^{-1}),
\]
with the oriented boundary of each square as in the source. The metric
on each factor is bi-invariant with orthonormal
$T_j=-i\sigma_j/2$ and inner product $-2\tr(T_jT_k)=\delta_{jk}$.
Haar probability measure is its volume measure up to the fixed total
volume factor; this factor does not affect integration by parts.

The lowest eigenfunction $\psi$ may be chosen smooth, strictly positive,
and $L^2$ normalized. To record the analytical ingredients explicitly,
the bounded smooth real potential and compact manifold give a Rayleigh
minimizer: a bounded-energy minimizing sequence has bounded $H^1$ norm,
its subsequence converges strongly in $L^2$ by compact Sobolev embedding,
and weak lower semicontinuity gives the minimum. Replacing it by its
absolute value preserves its $L^2$ norm and does not increase the
Dirichlet integral. The weak Euler--Lagrange equation and elliptic
regularity give a smooth nonnegative eigenfunction. The strong maximum
principle applied after a sufficiently large constant shift makes it
strictly positive. Its eigenvalue $E$ is the minimum. Uniqueness up to
scale also follows directly: if another real ground eigenfunction is
$\psi F$, subtracting $F$ times the equation for $\psi$ and integrating
against $F\psi$ gives $\int\psi^2|\nabla F|^2=0$; thus $F$ is constant.
The real and imaginary parts give the corresponding complex uniqueness.
This argument retains the actual interacting potential, with every link
and face present.

Set $\phi=\log\psi$ and $\rho=\psi^2$. Expanding the Laplacian of
$\psi F$ proves the ground-state transform
\[
 \psi^{-1}(H-E)(\psi F)
  =-\kappa\bigl(\Delta F+\nabla\log\rho\cdot\nabla F\bigr).
\]
The forward heat drift is $\beta_{\rm W}=\kappa\nabla\log\rho$.
Define its negative as the potential velocity
$u_{\rm W}=-2\kappa\nabla\phi=-\beta_{\rm W}$.
To fix the geometric diffusion sign, define
\[
 \Delta_{\rm rough}Y=\sum_j\left(
 \nabla_{E_j}\nabla_{E_j}Y-
 \nabla_{\nabla_{E_j}E_j}Y\right),\qquad
 \mathcal D Y=\Delta_{\rm rough}Y-\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}Y.
\]
The commutation of covariant derivatives on the gradient gives
\[
 \Delta_{\rm rough}\nabla q
   =\nabla\Delta q+\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}\nabla q,
 \qquad \mathcal D\nabla q=\nabla\Delta q.
\]
For completeness, the bi-invariant connection on each factor is
$\nabla_XY=[X,Y]/2$ on invariant fields. The Koszul formula gives this
identity from invariance of the metric. Substitution in the definition
of curvature gives sectional curvature $|[X,Y]|^2/4$ for orthonormal
$X,Y$. Since $[T_j,T_k]=\epsilon_{jk\ell}T_\ell$, this curvature is
$1/4$, and summing its two transverse directions gives
$\operatorname{Ric}=g/2$. Mixed factors have zero curvature, so on the
product $\mathcal D=\Delta_{\rm rough}-I/2$.

The original ground-state equation, divided by its positive $\psi$,
now yields
\[
 \kappa(\Delta\phi+|\nabla\phi|^2)=V-E.
\]
The symmetry of the Hessian gives
$\nabla_{\nabla\phi}\nabla\phi=\nabla|\nabla\phi|^2/2$.
Taking a gradient of the preceding identity therefore proves the exact
stationary viscous equation
\begin{align}
 \nabla_{u_{\rm W}}u_{\rm W}-\kappa\mathcal D u_{\rm W}
 &=2\kappa^2\nabla(|\nabla\phi|^2+\Delta\phi)\notag\\
 &=2\kappa\nabla V
 =-\frac{2}{a^2}\nabla\sum_pW_p.
 \label{eq:s6dyn-wilson-burgers}
\end{align}
The last coefficient uses $2\kappa b=2/a^2$. This is an exact forced
potential Burgers equation on the retained configuration manifold.
The velocity sign is essential: the forward drift itself satisfies
$\nabla_{\beta_{\rm W}}\beta_{\rm W}+\kappa\mathcal D\beta_{\rm W}
=2\kappa\nabla V$.

Its incompressible component can be computed exactly on this same
manifold. If $\operatorname{div}u_{\rm W}=-2\kappa\Delta\phi$ vanished,
then integration against $\phi$ would give
$0=\int\phi\Delta\phi=-\int|\nabla\phi|^2$, so $\phi$ and $\psi$
would be constant by connectedness. The ground-state equation would
then force $V$ to be constant. It is not: all identity links give
$V=0$, whereas replacing one strictly interior link by $-I$ leaves
all other links at the identity and changes precisely its four incident
face traces from $2$ to $-2$, giving $V=16b>0$. Therefore this actual
vacuum velocity is not divergence-free. Moreover for every smooth
divergence-free vector field $Y$,
\[
 \langle u_{\rm W},Y\rangle_{L^2}
   =2\kappa\int\phi\operatorname{div}Y=0.
\]
By continuity this holds for the $L^2$ closure of those fields, so the
orthogonal incompressible projection of $u_{\rm W}$ is exactly zero.
The force in \eqref{eq:s6dyn-wilson-burgers} is also a gradient and has
zero such projection. This proves both the differential-equation
correspondence and what its specific incompressible projection retains.
It does not erase the interacting vacuum, replace the graph by three
coordinates, or exclude different maps from excited states or currents.
