\section{The report's Boussinesq coordinates, with the original scales retained}
\label{sec:wba-bouss-scales}

The incoming report, Section 6, writes the base gradient as $-e_0$,
whereas Section 9.4 gives the original datum
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wba-original-datum}
 \theta_{\mathrm{in}}(x)=-\frac{A_0}{\lambda_0}
 \sin(\lambda_0x_2)\chi_0(x),\qquad
 A_0\geq1,\quad\lambda_0=\left\lceil\frac{\sqrt{A_0}}2\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
Here $\chi_0:\mathbb R^2\to[0,1]$ is smooth, radial, compactly supported
in $B_{R_0}$, equals one on $B_2$, and $R_0>2$.
These formulas use different coordinates. The primary Boussinesq manuscript,
Lemma 1.2 and equations (1.6)--(1.8), explicitly gives the dilation between
them. We prove it here and retain both objects. The primary manuscript calls
its time-dilation constant $\nu$; in this section its exact value is denoted
$\upsilon=\sqrt{A_0}$, so that it cannot be mistaken for physical viscosity.
Set $\mu=\lambda_0$. Both constants are strictly positive.

\subsection{An invertible map of fields, forces and both diffusion operators}

On $\mathbb R_X^2\times[0,\widetilde T)$ let
$\widetilde\theta,\widetilde u,\widetilde p,
\widetilde f_\theta,\widetilde f_u$ be smooth fields. Fix constants
$\widetilde\kappa,\widetilde\nu\geq0$, and define their residuals by
\begin{align}
 \widetilde E_\theta={}&\partial_\tau\widetilde\theta+
 \widetilde u\cdot\nabla_X\widetilde\theta
 -\widetilde\kappa\Delta_X\widetilde\theta-\widetilde f_\theta,\\
 \widetilde E_u={}&\partial_\tau\widetilde u+
 (\widetilde u\cdot\nabla_X)\widetilde u+
 \nabla_X\widetilde p-\widetilde\theta e_2
 -\widetilde\nu\Delta_X\widetilde u-\widetilde f_u.
\end{align}
Define $X=\mu x$, $\tau=\upsilon t$, $T=\widetilde T/\upsilon$, and
evaluate every tilded expression below at $(\mu x,\upsilon t)$:
\begin{align}\label{eq:wba-scale-map}
 \theta&=\frac{\upsilon^2}{\mu}\widetilde\theta,&
 u&=\frac{\upsilon}{\mu}\widetilde u,&
 p&=\frac{\upsilon^2}{\mu^2}\widetilde p,\\
 f_\theta&=\frac{\upsilon^3}{\mu}\widetilde f_\theta,&
 f_u&=\frac{\upsilon^2}{\mu}\widetilde f_u,&
 \kappa&=\frac{\upsilon}{\mu^2}\widetilde\kappa,
 \qquad\nu=\frac{\upsilon}{\mu^2}\widetilde\nu.
\end{align}
Let $E_\theta,E_u$ be the residuals in the original $(x,t)$ coordinates,
with the last two diffusion constants in \eqref{eq:wba-scale-map}.
Then the following are identities of fields, without assuming that a residual
vanishes:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wba-residual-intertwining}
 E_\theta(x,t)=\frac{\upsilon^3}{\mu}
 \widetilde E_\theta(\mu x,\upsilon t),\quad
 E_u(x,t)=\frac{\upsilon^2}{\mu}
 \widetilde E_u(\mu x,\upsilon t),\quad
 \operatorname{div}_x u=\upsilon\operatorname{div}_X\widetilde u.
\end{equation}
The map is a bijection of these field tuples and, in particular, of their
zero-residual subsets. Its inverse is obtained by evaluating at
$(x,t)=(X/\mu,\tau/\upsilon)$ and multiplying the five fields respectively
by $\mu/\upsilon^2$, $\mu/\upsilon$, $\mu^2/\upsilon^2$,
$\mu/\upsilon^3$, $\mu/\upsilon^2$; the inverse diffusion constants are
$\widetilde\kappa=\mu^2\kappa/\upsilon$,
$\widetilde\nu=\mu^2\nu/\upsilon$.

\begin{proof}
The chain rule gives
\[
 \partial_t\theta=\frac{\upsilon^3}{\mu}\partial_\tau\widetilde\theta,
 \qquad u\cdot\nabla_x\theta=
 \frac{\upsilon}{\mu}\widetilde u\cdot
 \upsilon^2\nabla_X\widetilde\theta,
 \qquad\Delta_x\theta=\upsilon^2\mu\Delta_X\widetilde\theta.
\]
Multiplication of the last expression by
$\kappa=\upsilon\widetilde\kappa/\mu^2$ gives the required factor
$\upsilon^3/\mu$. For the vector equation,
\[
 \partial_tu=\frac{\upsilon^2}{\mu}\partial_\tau\widetilde u,
 \quad(u\cdot\nabla_x)u=\frac{\upsilon^2}{\mu}
 (\widetilde u\cdot\nabla_X)\widetilde u,
 \quad\nabla_xp=\frac{\upsilon^2}{\mu}\nabla_X\widetilde p,
 \quad\Delta_xu=\upsilon\mu\Delta_X\widetilde u.
\]
Thus all its terms, including $\theta e_2$ with the unchanged laboratory
vector $e_2=(0,1)$, acquire $\upsilon^2/\mu$.
Taking the trace of the derivative of $u$ proves the divergence identity.
Every listed inverse multiplier is nonzero, and substitution in either
order returns each original field and coefficient. This proves both
directions, including the operators, domains and forces.
\end{proof}

For the original datum, define
$\widetilde\chi_0(X)=\chi_0(X/\mu)$ and
$\widetilde\theta_{\mathrm{in}}(X)=-\sin(X_2)\widetilde\chi_0(X)$.
Then \eqref{eq:wba-scale-map} gives exactly
\eqref{eq:wba-original-datum}, since $\upsilon^2=A_0$ and
$\mu=\lambda_0$. The tilded cutoff equals one on $B_{2\mu}$ and is
supported in $B_{\mu R_0}$. Support is therefore transported rather than
discarded. For a compact streamfunction, the exact formulas are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wba-stream-scale}
 \psi=\frac{\upsilon}{\mu^2}\widetilde\psi(\mu x,\upsilon t),
 \qquad u=J\nabla_x\psi,
 \qquad\omega=\Delta_x\psi
       =\upsilon\widetilde\omega(\mu x,\upsilon t),
 \qquad J(a,b)=(-b,a).
\end{equation}
These follow by one and two spatial differentiations; no inverse Laplacian
at zero frequency is needed.

For every spatial multi-index $\gamma$ and integer $b\geq0$,
\begin{align}\label{eq:wba-force-jets}
 \partial_x^\gamma\partial_t^bf_\theta
 &=\upsilon^{3+b}\mu^{|\gamma|-1}
 (\partial_X^\gamma\partial_\tau^b\widetilde f_\theta)(\mu x,\upsilon t),\\
 \partial_x^\gamma\partial_t^bf_u
 &=\upsilon^{2+b}\mu^{|\gamma|-1}
 (\partial_X^\gamma\partial_\tau^b\widetilde f_u)(\mu x,\upsilon t).
\end{align}
The change of variables $dX=\mu^2dx$ also proves, at corresponding times,
\begin{align}
 \|\theta\|_{L^2_x}&=\frac{\upsilon^2}{\mu^2}
 \|\widetilde\theta\|_{L^2_X},&
 \|u\|_{L^2_x}&=\frac{\upsilon}{\mu^2}
 \|\widetilde u\|_{L^2_X},\\
 \|\nabla_x\theta\|_\infty&=\upsilon^2
 \|\nabla_X\widetilde\theta\|_\infty,&
 \|\omega\|_\infty&=\upsilon\|\widetilde\omega\|_\infty.
\end{align}
Positive finite factors preserve boundedness, full divergence to infinity,
and divergent limsup with their different quantifiers. These identities
transfer such properties of specified fields; they do not assert the
global iteration estimates for unspecified fields.

\subsection{The phase, shear and feedback map in the original variables}

Retain a finite collection of layers at each time. For each layer $j$, let
$w_j,\Omega_j,\Theta_j$ be scalar functions, $\lambda_j>0$,
$\zeta_j\ne0$, and $e_j=\zeta_j/|\zeta_j|$. Let
$e_0=R_\alpha e_2$, with $R_\alpha$ counterclockwise rotation. In the
original variables the background arrays are
\begin{align}\label{eq:wba-original-background}
 G_{<j}&=-A_0e_0-\sum_{1\leq i<j}w_i A_i e_i,
 &A_i&=-\lambda_i|\zeta_i|\Theta_i,\\
 D_{<j}&=\dot\alpha J+
 \sum_{1\leq i<j}w_i\Omega_i Je_i\otimes e_i.
\end{align}
Here $(a\otimes b)c=a(b\cdot c)$. The unit-coordinate source formulas
are related by the exact identities
\begin{align}\label{eq:wba-layer-map}
 \lambda_j&=\mu\widetilde\lambda_j,&
 \zeta_j(t)&=\widetilde\zeta_j(\upsilon t),&
 w_j(t)&=\widetilde w_j(\upsilon t),\\
 \Theta_j(t)&=\frac{\upsilon^2}{\mu}\widetilde\Theta_j(\upsilon t),&
 \Omega_j(t)&=\upsilon\widetilde\Omega_j(\upsilon t),&
 \alpha(t)&=\widetilde\alpha(\upsilon t),\\
 A_j(t)&=\upsilon^2\widetilde A_j(\upsilon t),&
 G_{<j}(t)&=\upsilon^2\widetilde G_{<j}(\upsilon t),&
 D_{<j}(t)&=\upsilon\widetilde D_{<j}(\upsilon t).
\end{align}
In particular the phase is exactly
$\lambda_j\zeta_j(t)\cdot x
=\widetilde\lambda_j\widetilde\zeta_j(\tau)\cdot X$.
Substitution into
\[
 \dot\zeta_j=-D_{<j}^{T}\zeta_j,\qquad
 \dot\Theta_j=-\frac{J\zeta_j\cdot G_{<j}}
 {\lambda_j|\zeta_j|^2}\Omega_j,\qquad
 \dot\Omega_j=\lambda_j\zeta_{j,1}\Theta_j
\]
gives the source equations with respective common factors
$\upsilon$, $\upsilon^3/\mu$, and $\upsilon^2$.
This proves the dynamics in both coordinates. It also explains why the
source's initial interpolation of duration one has duration
$1/\sqrt{A_0}$ for \eqref{eq:wba-original-datum}.

We now prove the feedback identities without discarding any shear. Put
$s_i=w_i\Omega_i$. Transposition of \eqref{eq:wba-original-background}
and $J^T=-J$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wba-covector-components}
 \dot\zeta_j=\dot\alpha J\zeta_j
 -\sum_{i<j}s_i e_i(Je_i\cdot\zeta_j),\qquad
 \dot\zeta_{j,1}=-\dot\alpha\zeta_{j,2}
 -\sum_{i<j}s_i e_{i,1}(Je_i\cdot\zeta_j).
\end{equation}
On the open set $\zeta_{j,2}\ne0$ define
\[
 \mathcal F_j=-\frac{\sum_{i<j}s_i e_{i,1}(Je_i\cdot\zeta_j)}
 {\zeta_{j,2}}.
\]
Then $\dot\zeta_{j,1}=(\mathcal F_j-\dot\alpha)\zeta_{j,2}$.
For a prescribed differentiable function $Z$, the identity
$\dot\alpha=\mathcal F_j-\dot Z/\zeta_{j,2}$ implies
$d(\zeta_{j,1}-Z)/dt=0$ on that open set. Matching the values at one
time therefore gives equality throughout the connected interval.
This is an exact local feedback identity. It does not assert that its
denominator stays nonzero in a constructed infinite cascade.
Under \eqref{eq:wba-layer-map},
$\mathcal F_j(t)=\upsilon\widetilde{\mathcal F}_j(\upsilon t)$,
so this identity too retains the original time scale.

On any time interval choose continuous angle lifts with
$e_j=(\sin\phi_j,\cos\phi_j)$, and write
$\rho_j=\log|\zeta_j|$, $d_{ij}=\phi_j-\phi_i$.
Since $\partial_\phi e_j=-Je_j$, differentiation gives
\[
 \dot\zeta_j=|\zeta_j|(\dot\rho_j e_j-\dot\phi_j Je_j).
\]
The exact inner products are
$e_i\cdot e_j=\cos d_{ij}$,
$Je_i\cdot e_j=-\sin d_{ij}$, and
$e_i\cdot Je_j=\sin d_{ij}$.
Taking inner products of \eqref{eq:wba-covector-components} with
$e_j$ and $Je_j$, and dividing by the positive $|\zeta_j|$, proves
\begin{align}\label{eq:wba-angle-equations}
 \dot\rho_j&=\sum_{i<j}s_i\sin d_{ij}\cos d_{ij},\\
 \dot\phi_j&=-\dot\alpha-\Sigma_j,
 \qquad\Sigma_j=\sum_{i<j}s_i\sin^2d_{ij},\qquad
 \dot d_{ij}=\Sigma_i-\Sigma_j.
\end{align}
Conversely these two equations for $\rho_j,\phi_j$, inserted into the
displayed derivative of $\zeta_j=e^{\rho_j}e_j$, recover both components
of \eqref{eq:wba-covector-components}, because $(e_j,Je_j)$ is an
orthonormal basis. This proves the exact covector--angle correspondence,
including its dynamics and all older shears. Common rotation cancels
from the explicit relative-angle derivative, but the amplitudes and
angles still satisfy a coupled system.

Finally, the affine-core derivative is
$S_j=\Omega_jJe_j\otimes e_j$. Its trace is
$\Omega_j e_j\cdot Je_j=0$ and its square is
$\Omega_j^2Je_j(e_j\cdot Je_j)\otimes e_j=0$.
Its image equals $\operatorname{span}(Je_j)$ when $\Omega_j\ne0$,
whereas $S_j=0$ when $\Omega_j=0$. Thus the incoming report's
unqualified ``rank one'' is corrected to rank one precisely for a
nonzero amplitude and rank zero at its return endpoint.

\subsection{All initial-angle cases in the report's illustrative subsystem}

The report's equation (6.10) divides by $\sin\phi(0)$, so its endpoint
cases must be restored. Keep exactly its equations
\[
 \frac{d\phi}{d\tau}=\sin\phi,\qquad
 \frac{d\log B}{d\tau}=\cos\phi,\qquad B(0)=B_0>0,
 \qquad\phi(0)=\phi_0\in\mathbb R.
\]
For $\phi_0=n\pi$, uniqueness gives $\phi(\tau)=n\pi$ and direct
integration gives $B(\tau)=B_0e^{(-1)^n\tau}$.
For $\phi_0\notin\pi\mathbb Z$, set $z_0=\tan(\phi_0/2)$.
It is finite and nonzero. Choose the unique integer $n$ for which
$\phi_0=2\arctan z_0+2n\pi$. Then on the whole real time line the
exact formulas are
\[
 z(\tau)=e^\tau z_0,\qquad
 \phi(\tau)=2\arctan z(\tau)+2n\pi,\qquad
 B(\tau)=B_0\frac{e^\tau(1+z_0^2)}{1+e^{2\tau}z_0^2}.
\]
Indeed $\phi'=2z/(1+z^2)=\sin\phi$ and
$B'/B=1-2z^2/(1+z^2)=(1-z^2)/(1+z^2)=\cos\phi$.
The positive denominator proves smoothness and $B>0$ for finite time.
The scalar ODE for $\phi$ has a globally Lipschitz right side; uniqueness
follows by applying the integral inequality for its difference and
iterating to the factorial bound. The linear equation $B'=B\cos\phi$
then has a unique solution by the same argument.
Finally $\sin\phi=2z/(1+z^2)$ proves
$B(\tau)=B_0\sin\phi(\tau)/\sin\phi_0$ in exactly the nonstationary
cases where that quotient is defined. Thus neither the constant-angle
solutions nor their distinct exponential amplitudes have been omitted.
