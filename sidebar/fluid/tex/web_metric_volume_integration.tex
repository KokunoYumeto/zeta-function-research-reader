\section{Exact metric realization and the circle volume lift}
\label{sec:ml-realization}

This section proves the metric and volume maps needed by the rational
cylinder reduction. It does not assert existence of a nonzero solution to
the nonlinear reduced equations. All constructions below are explicit maps
on the stated data, and all residual identities hold for arbitrary smooth
data in their domains, including data with nonzero residual.

\subsection{Domains, conventions, and the source ordering}
Let $N$ be a smooth, Hausdorff, second-countable manifold of dimension
$n\geq2$. Let $v,w\in\Gamma(TN)$ be smooth and pointwise linearly independent,
and let $\theta,\eta\in\Omega^1(N)$. The independence requirement is pointwise
on all of $N$; the same results apply on any open independent locus without
extending a formula across its rank-changing boundary. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ml-H}
 H=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}
  :=\begin{pmatrix}\theta(v)&\theta(w)\\\eta(v)&\eta(w)\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad A(X):=\begin{pmatrix}\theta(X)\\\eta(X)\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
No rescaling of $v,w$, their order, or the given one-forms is made. A
Riemannian metric has positive definiteness as part of its definition.
When a volume form is prescribed, $N$ is additionally oriented and
$\mu\in\Omega^n(N)$ is smooth and everywhere positive for that orientation.
The metric volume form is positive for the same orientation. Divergence is
defined by $\mathcal L_X\mu=(\operatorname{div}_\mu X)\mu$.
For a two-form $\omega$, the contraction convention is
$(\iota_X\omega)(Y)=\omega(X,Y)$.

The primary 2024 post defines $\theta=h(v,\cdot)$ and writes $\lambda$ for
$h(w,\cdot)$, corresponding to $\eta$ here. Its displayed equation (7)
orders the rows as $(\lambda,\theta)$ while ordering the columns as $(v,w)$.
Thus its displayed matrix is $\Pi_{\rm swap}H$, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ml-source-order}
 \Pi_{\rm swap}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \det(\Pi_{\rm swap}H)=-\det H.
\end{equation}
For an independent pair realized by a positive metric, $\det H>0$, so
the displayed $\Pi_{\rm swap}H$ is not a positive definite Gram matrix. Simultaneously
reordering both arguments would give $\Pi_{\rm swap}H\Pi_{\rm swap}^T$, which is a Gram matrix in
the order $(w,v)$. Equation \eqref{eq:ml-source-order} identifies the exact
permutation; the correct duality map remains the one proved below.
The incoming report uses the correctly ordered $H$.
See \url{https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2024/09/08/a-possible-approach-to-finite-time-blowup-for-the-euler-equations/}.

\subsection{All realizing metrics, globally}
\begin{proposition}[Complete realization on the independent locus]
\label{prop:ml-metric}
There exists a smooth Riemannian metric $h$ on $N$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ml-duality}
 h(v,X)=\theta(X),\qquad h(w,X)=\eta(X)\quad(X\in TN)
\end{equation}
exactly when $H$ is symmetric positive definite at every point. In this
case put $D=\operatorname{span}(v,w)$, $E=\ker\theta\cap\ker\eta$, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ml-projection}
 P(X)=(v\ \ w)H^{-1}A(X),\qquad Q(X)=X-P(X).
\end{equation}
Then $P$ and $Q$ are smooth complementary projections with images $D$ and
$E$. The full set of metrics satisfying \eqref{eq:ml-duality} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ml-family}
 h_k(X,Y)=A(X)^TH^{-1}A(Y)+k(QX,QY),
\end{equation}
where $k$ ranges over the smooth positive definite metrics on $E$.
For $n=2$, $E$ is the zero bundle and the first term is the unique metric.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If \eqref{eq:ml-duality} holds, its restriction to $(v,w)$ gives the Gram
matrix of the independent pair. Symmetry follows from symmetry of $h$;
for $(s,t)\ne(0,0)$ its quadratic form equals
$h(sv+tw,sv+tw)>0$. This proves necessity, including $b=c$.

Conversely suppose $H=H^T>0$. The map $A:TN\to N\times\mathbb R^2$
has rank two because its restriction to $D$ is the invertible matrix $H$.
In local coordinates its kernel is a smooth rank-$(n-2)$ subbundle:
an invertible two-column minor allows solving two components smoothly in
terms of the remaining components. Thus $E$ is a smooth vector bundle.
For $X\in TN$, $A(PX)=HH^{-1}A(X)=A(X)$, so $QX\in E$;
also $Pv=v$, $Pw=w$, and $P|_E=0$. These equalities prove
$P^2=P$, $Q^2=Q$, $PQ=QP=0$, and $TN=D\oplus E$.
The entries of $H^{-1}$ are smooth because $\det H$ never vanishes,
so both projections are smooth globally.

For completeness, a global positive metric on $E$ can be constructed as
follows. Choose a locally finite trivializing cover and a smooth partition
of unity subordinate to it, and give each local trivialization its
coordinate Euclidean quadratic form. Their partition-weighted sum is
smooth. At a point, at least one nonzero partition weight multiplies a
strictly positive quadratic form on any nonzero vector in that fibre;
the other summands are nonnegative. This produces a smooth positive $k$.
This construction uses only the stated smooth-manifold hypotheses, which
give a locally finite refinement and smooth partitions of unity.

Formula \eqref{eq:ml-family} is smooth and symmetric. If $X\ne0$, then
either $A(X)\ne0$, making its first quadratic term positive, or
$A(X)=0$ and $QX=X\ne0$, making the second term positive. Moreover
$A(v)=He_1$, $A(w)=He_2$, $Qv=Qw=0$. Hence
$h_k(v,X)=e_1^TA(X)=\theta(X)$ and
$h_k(w,X)=e_2^TA(X)=\eta(X)$, proving sufficiency.

To prove exhaustion, any realizing $h$ has $h(D,E)=0$ by its two prescribed
dualities. Its restriction to $D$ is $H$ in the given frame $(v,w)$.
Writing $X=PX+QX$, $Y=PY+QY$ therefore gives
\eqref{eq:ml-family} with $k=h|_E$. Conversely every such $k$ was already
shown to work. If $n=2$, $E$ has rank zero, leaving no free term.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact compatibility with a prescribed volume}
\begin{proposition}[Dimension two and dimensions at least three]
\label{prop:ml-volume}
Retain $H=H^T>0$ and the specified orientation and positive $\mu$.
If $n=2$, a metric satisfying \eqref{eq:ml-duality} and
$d\operatorname{vol}_h=\mu$ exists exactly when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ml-2d-condition}
 \mu(v,w)^2=\det H=\theta(v)\eta(w)-\theta(w)\eta(v).
\end{equation}
If $n\geq3$, such a metric always exists. Explicitly choose any $k_0>0$
on $E$, form $h_0=h_{k_0}$, define the positive smooth ratio $q$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ml-q}
 \mu=q\,d\operatorname{vol}_{h_0},\qquad m=n-2,
\end{equation}
and set $k=q^{2/m}k_0$ in \eqref{eq:ml-family}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $n=2$, the ordered frame $(v,w)$ is either positive or negative for
the specified orientation on each connected component. Let
$\varepsilon=\operatorname{sgn}\mu(v,w)\in\{1,-1\}$, which is locally
constant because $\mu(v,w)$ never vanishes. The metric volume of this
frame is exactly $\varepsilon\sqrt{\det H}$. Thus its equality to
$\mu(v,w)$ is equivalent to \eqref{eq:ml-2d-condition}, because both
have the sign $\varepsilon$. Equality on this frame is equality of the
two top-degree forms. The metric was unique in
Proposition~\ref{prop:ml-metric}, proving both directions without changing
the orientation or setting any determinant to a normalized value.

For $n\geq3$, choose any local frame $e_1,\ldots,e_m$ of $E$. In the
unchanged frame $(v,w,e_1,\ldots,e_m)$, the metrics $h_0$ and $h_k$ have
matrices $\operatorname{diag}(H,K_0)$ and
$\operatorname{diag}(H,q^{2/m}K_0)$, respectively. Consequently
\[
 \frac{\det\operatorname{diag}(H,q^{2/m}K_0)}
      {\det\operatorname{diag}(H,K_0)}
 =\frac{(\det H)q^{2m/m}\det K_0}{(\det H)\det K_0}=q^2.
\]
Both metric volume forms have the specified orientation and their positive
ratio is therefore $q$. It follows from \eqref{eq:ml-q} that
$d\operatorname{vol}_{h_k}=\mu$. The function $q^{2/m}$ is globally smooth
and positive, so the metric is global. No trivialization of $E$ was
required globally.
\end{proof}

The assertion in the incoming report that the local construction does
not freely prescribe volume requires this dimensional refinement:
with an everywhere independent pair the complete same-manifold
obstruction is \eqref{eq:ml-2d-condition} in dimension two, whereas there
is no prescribed-positive-volume obstruction in dimensions at least three.
Changing $k$ can change acceleration as a vector. The exact acceleration
relation, including the inverse metric, is proved next.

\subsection{Dualization of each reduced acceleration}
For a realizing metric $h$, let $\nabla$ denote its Levi-Civita
connection and let $\flat_h:TN\to T^*N$ and
$\sharp_h:T^*N\to TN$ be its mutually inverse metric dualities.
Equations \eqref{eq:wb-accel} and \eqref{eq:wb-polarized} have already
proved the full acceleration and polarization identities for this
metric, with the same contraction convention. We now keep their inverse
metric maps explicit while varying the complement metric.

For a prescribed scalar $\varphi\in C^\infty(N)$ define, with all
coefficients retained,
\begin{align}
 B_0&=\varphi\eta+\iota_vd\theta+\tfrac12d[\theta(v)],\label{eq:ml-B0}\\
 B_1&=-4\varphi\theta+\iota_vd\eta+\iota_wd\theta
                   +\tfrac12d[\theta(w)+\eta(v)],\label{eq:ml-B1}\\
 B_2&=-3\varphi\eta+\iota_wd\eta+\tfrac12d[\eta(w)].\label{eq:ml-B2}
\end{align}
The exact vector residuals are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ml-residuals}
 \begin{split}
 \varphi w+\nabla_vv&=\sharp_h B_0,\\
 -4\varphi v+\nabla_vw+\nabla_wv&=\sharp_h B_1,\\
 -3\varphi w+\nabla_ww&=\sharp_h B_2.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
These are identities for arbitrary smooth data in the domain of
Proposition~\ref{prop:ml-metric}. In particular vanishing of the three
one-form residuals is equivalent to vanishing of the three vector
residuals, for each realizing $h$, since $\sharp_h$ is an isomorphism.
The residuals $B_j$ are independent of $k$; their vector representatives
are related for any two realizing metrics by the explicit fibrewise map
$\sharp_{h_{k_2}}\flat_{h_{k_1}}$. Together with the volume construction
this proves the exact realization relation for the reduced system,
including its two divergence constraints
$\operatorname{div}_\mu v=0$ and $\operatorname{div}_\mu w=2\varphi$.
It does not assume these constraints or $B_j=0$ have been solved.

\subsection{The circle lift as a map of metric and volume data}
\label{subsec:ml-circle}
Here the base metric $h$ is fixed; it may be any smooth Riemannian metric,
including the metric already realizing the given pair. The construction
in this subsection needs no pair of independent fields and works in
every base dimension. Keep the given $\mu$ and define $f>0$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ml-f}
 \mu=f\,d\operatorname{vol}_h.
\end{equation}
Fix a circle period $L>0$ without normalizing it. Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ml-lift-metric}
 \widehat N=N\times(\mathbb R/L\mathbb Z),\qquad
 \pi:\widehat N\to N,\quad\pi(y,s)=y,\qquad
 \widehat h=\pi^*h+(f\circ\pi)^2ds\otimes ds.
\end{equation}
The form $ds$ is the global invariant one-form induced from $\mathbb R$;
the coordinate $s$ itself is only local modulo $L$.
The product orientation is specified by a positive base form followed
by $ds$. Define the injective linear lift
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ml-J}
 \mathcal J:\Gamma(TN)\longrightarrow\Gamma(T\widehat N),\qquad
 (\mathcal JX)_{(y,s)}=(X_y,0).
\end{equation}
Its image consists precisely of the horizontal vector fields invariant
under all circle translations. In coordinates the coefficients of a
translation-invariant horizontal field depend only on $y$, yielding
the inverse of $\mathcal J$. For functions and differential forms the
map is the ordinary pullback $\pi^*$, which is also injective:
restriction to the slice $y\mapsto(y,s_0)$ is a left inverse.

\begin{proposition}[Volume and first-order operators of the lift]
\label{prop:ml-circle-first}
The data \eqref{eq:ml-lift-metric} satisfy
\begin{align}
 d\operatorname{vol}_{\widehat h}&=\pi^*\mu\wedge ds,
                                                        \label{eq:ml-lift-vol}\\
 (\mathcal JX)^{\flat_{\widehat h}}&=\pi^*(X^{\flat_h}),
 \qquad\operatorname{grad}_{\widehat h}\pi^*F
        =\mathcal J(\operatorname{grad}_hF),             \label{eq:ml-lift-dual}\\
 \operatorname{div}_{\widehat h}\mathcal JX
        &=\pi^*(\operatorname{div}_\mu X),              \label{eq:ml-lift-div}\\
 \widehat\nabla_{\mathcal JX}\mathcal JY
        &=\mathcal J(\nabla_XY),                       \label{eq:ml-lift-connection}\\
 [\mathcal JX,\mathcal JY]&=\mathcal J[X,Y],\qquad
 d\pi^*\alpha=\pi^*d\alpha,\qquad
 \iota_{\mathcal JX}\pi^*\alpha=\pi^*(\iota_X\alpha).
                                                        \label{eq:ml-pullback}
\end{align}
For a time-dependent field $X(t)$ the lift commutes with $\partial_t$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take positively oriented local coordinates $y^1,\ldots,y^n$ on $N$.
Write $h=(h_{ij})$, $h^{-1}=(h^{ij})$, and $D_h=\det(h_{ij})>0$.
The matrix of $\widehat h$ in the unchanged coordinates $(y,s)$ is
$\operatorname{diag}(h_{ij},f^2)$, with determinant $D_hf^2$.
Its positive volume is therefore
$f\sqrt{D_h}\,dy^1\wedge\cdots\wedge dy^n\wedge ds$, proving
\eqref{eq:ml-lift-vol} with its orientation and coefficient.
Pairing $(X,0)$ with $(Y,a\partial_s)$ gives $h(X,Y)$, proving the
duality identity. Pairing $(\operatorname{grad}_hF,0)$ with the same
arbitrary vector gives $dF(Y)$, proving the gradient identity.

If $\mu=\rho\,dy^1\wedge\cdots\wedge dy^n$, expand its Lie derivative
using $\mathcal L_X dy^j=dX^j$ and the product rule. Only the coefficient
of $dy^1\wedge\cdots\wedge dy^n$ survives, giving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ml-div-coord}
 \operatorname{div}_\mu X
     =\rho^{-1}\partial_i(\rho X^i)
     =\operatorname{div}_hX+X(\log f),
 \qquad\rho=f\sqrt{D_h}.
\end{equation}
The lifted field has zero $s$ component, so the identical divergence
calculation with density $\rho$ in $(y,s)$ proves
\eqref{eq:ml-lift-div}.

For clarity, all potentially nonzero lifted Christoffel coefficients are
computed from
$\widehat\Gamma^A_{BC}=\tfrac12\widehat h^{AD}
(\partial_B\widehat h_{CD}+\partial_C\widehat h_{BD}
-\partial_D\widehat h_{BC})$:
\begin{align}
 \widehat\Gamma^k_{ij}&=\Gamma^k_{ij},&
 \widehat\Gamma^s_{ij}&=0,&
 \widehat\Gamma^k_{is}&=\widehat\Gamma^k_{si}=0,\notag\\
 \widehat\Gamma^s_{is}&=\widehat\Gamma^s_{si}=f^{-1}\partial_i f,&
 \widehat\Gamma^k_{ss}&=-f h^{kj}\partial_jf,&
 \widehat\Gamma^s_{ss}&=0.                            \label{eq:ml-Christoffel}
\end{align}
For instance the middle coefficient in the second line equals
$\tfrac12h^{kj}(-\partial_j f^2)=-f h^{kj}\partial_jf$;
the first equals $\tfrac12 f^{-2}\partial_i f^2$.
The other equalities follow because there are no mixed metric entries,
$\partial_s h_{ij}=\partial_s f=0$, and the horizontal inverse block
is $h^{-1}$. Since $\mathcal JX$ and $\mathcal JY$ have zero vertical
components, \eqref{eq:ml-Christoffel} proves
\eqref{eq:ml-lift-connection}, including its zero vertical component.

The coordinate formula for a bracket contains only derivatives of the
horizontal coefficients, proving the bracket identity. Pulling back a
form replaces its coefficient functions by functions of $y$ and retains
the differentials $dy^i$; taking $d$ differentiates those coefficients
in the same $y$ directions, proving the exterior-derivative identity.
Evaluation of the alternating forms on arbitrary product tangent vectors
proves the contraction identity. The map $\mathcal J$ is fixed in time,
and its coordinate coefficients are those of $X$, proving commutation
with $\partial_t$.
\end{proof}

The warping in \eqref{eq:ml-lift-metric} equals the determinant construction
in Section 3 of the primary 2017 paper: defining
$D_\mu(h)$ by $d\operatorname{vol}_h=\sqrt{D_\mu(h)}\mu$ gives
$D_\mu(h)=f^{-2}$ and thus $D_\mu(h)^{-1}=f^2$.
Our circle has retained period $L$, while that source takes $L=1$.
Its Section 4 proves a metric realization by another complement;
\eqref{eq:ml-family} provides the full family in the intrinsic kernel
complement. See Sections 3--4 of
\url{https://arxiv.org/html/1707.07807v2}.

In particular set
$(\widehat v,\widehat w,\widehat\theta,\widehat\eta,
\widehat\varphi)=(\mathcal Jv,\mathcal Jw,
\pi^*\theta,\pi^*\eta,\pi^*\varphi)$. Equations
\eqref{eq:ml-lift-dual} and \eqref{eq:ml-pullback} give the exact
identities $\widehat B_j=\pi^*B_j$ for all three residuals
\eqref{eq:ml-B0}--\eqref{eq:ml-B2};
\eqref{eq:ml-lift-connection} gives the corresponding lift of each vector
residual, with coefficients $1,-4,-3$ unchanged. The divergence residuals
lift as
\[
 \operatorname{div}_{\widehat h}\widehat v
       =\pi^*(\operatorname{div}_\mu v),\qquad
 \operatorname{div}_{\widehat h}\widehat w-2\widehat\varphi
       =\pi^*(\operatorname{div}_\mu w-2\varphi).
\]
Injectivity of pullback and of $\mathcal J$ proves both directions of
each zero-residual equivalence. Thus the volume obstruction in dimension
two has an explicit exact bridge to dimension three. This statement
constructs the bridge for every input tuple; it is not an existence claim
for a tuple with the nonlinear residuals zero.

\subsection{Time-dependent dynamics and energy}
Fix a time interval $I\subset\mathbb R$ and smooth
$U:I\times N\to TN$, $p:I\times N\to\mathbb R$, and
$F:I\times N\to TN$, with the vector fields based at $y\in N$.
Define the momentum residual, without discarding pressure or force, by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ml-Euler-residual}
 \mathcal E_{h}(U,p,F)
    =\partial_tU+\nabla_UU+\operatorname{grad}_hp-F.
\end{equation}
The lifted fields are
$\widehat U=\mathcal JU$, $\widehat p=\pi^*p$,
$\widehat F=\mathcal JF$. Proposition~\ref{prop:ml-circle-first} proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ml-dynamics}
 \mathcal E_{\widehat h}(\widehat U,\widehat p,\widehat F)
   =\mathcal J\mathcal E_h(U,p,F),\qquad
 \operatorname{div}_{\widehat h}\widehat U
   =\pi^*\operatorname{div}_\mu U.
\end{equation}
Hence $\mathcal J$ is a bijection between the base smooth solution pairs
of the weighted-volume Euler system
$\mathcal E_h=0$, $\operatorname{div}_\mu U=0$ and the lifted smooth
Euler solution pairs whose velocity is horizontal and whose velocity,
pressure, and specified force are circle-invariant with the given lifts.
This is a statement about solution sets, proved by the two residual
identities and the explicit inverses on their images. It neither assumes
existence for arbitrary initial data nor changes the base divergence
constraint from $\operatorname{div}_\mu$ to $\operatorname{div}_h$.
Indeed those two divergences differ by the explicit term $U(\log f)$.

If $N$ is compact without boundary, the energy and its balance are
also exact. For any smooth $X,Y$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ml-energy-norm}
 \int_{\widehat N}\widehat h(\mathcal JX,\mathcal JY)
              d\operatorname{vol}_{\widehat h}
   =L\int_N h(X,Y)\,\mu.
\end{equation}
This follows by \eqref{eq:ml-lift-vol}, the unchanged horizontal inner
product, and $\int_{\mathbb R/L\mathbb Z}ds=L$.
Let $E_\mu(U)=\tfrac12\int_Nh(U,U)\mu$.
For any smooth solution of the base system in \eqref{eq:ml-dynamics},
metric compatibility and time independence of $h,\mu$ give
\[
 \frac{dE_\mu}{dt}
  =-\int_N U\!\left(\tfrac12h(U,U)+p\right)\mu
      +\int_Nh(F,U)\mu
  =\int_Nh(F,U)\mu.
\]
For the final equality, let $G=\tfrac12h(U,U)+p$ and use
$\operatorname{div}_\mu(GU)=U(G)+G\operatorname{div}_\mu U=U(G)$.
Its integral is zero: $\mathcal L_{GU}\mu=d(\iota_{GU}\mu)$ because
$d\mu=0$ in top degree, and Stokes' theorem has no boundary term.
The lifted kinetic energy is $LE_\mu$ and its work balance is multiplied
by the same $L$. No energy coefficient has been normalized away.

\subsection{Second-order operators: the exact additional terms}
\label{subsec:ml-second}
The lift preserves the horizontal connection in
\eqref{eq:ml-lift-connection}, but tracing over all lifted directions adds
terms. Define
$Z=\operatorname{grad}_h\log f$. The scalar Laplacian convention here is
$\Delta_h F=\operatorname{div}_h\operatorname{grad}_hF$, whose Euclidean
expression is $\sum_i\partial_i^2F$. By the gradient and divergence
identities already proved,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ml-scalar-Laplace}
 \Delta_{\widehat h}\pi^*F
   =\pi^*\big(\Delta_hF+h(Z,\operatorname{grad}_hF)\big).
\end{equation}
Indeed $\operatorname{div}_\mu\operatorname{grad}_hF
=\operatorname{div}_h\operatorname{grad}_hF
+(\operatorname{grad}_hF)(\log f)$ by
\eqref{eq:ml-div-coord}; the last term is the displayed inner product.

For vector fields use the positive-trace rough operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ml-rough-definition}
 \mathscr L_hX=\sum_{a=1}^n
    \left(\nabla_{e_a}\nabla_{e_a}X-
                        \nabla_{\nabla_{e_a}e_a}X\right),
\end{equation}
in any local $h$-orthonormal frame. This is the metric trace of the
second covariant derivative, so its definition is independent of frame.
Set $e_0=f^{-1}\partial_s$ on the lift and lift each $e_a$ horizontally.
The Christoffel formulas prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ml-unit-vertical}
 \widehat\nabla_{\mathcal JX}e_0=0,\qquad
 \widehat\nabla_{e_0}\mathcal JX=h(Z,X)e_0,\qquad
 \widehat\nabla_{e_0}e_0=-\mathcal JZ.
\end{equation}
For example the first equality follows from differentiating $f^{-1}$:
$-f^{-2}X(f)\partial_s+f^{-1}X(\log f)\partial_s=0$.
The second is $f^{-1}X(\log f)\partial_s$.
The third is $f^{-2}(-f\operatorname{grad}_hf)=-Z$, since
$\partial_s f=0$.
The horizontal trace in \eqref{eq:ml-rough-definition} is exactly
$\mathcal J\mathscr L_hX$. The remaining term is
\[
 \widehat\nabla_{e_0}\big(h(Z,X)e_0\big)
   -\widehat\nabla_{-\mathcal JZ}\mathcal JX
   =-h(Z,X)\mathcal JZ+\mathcal J(\nabla_ZX),
\]
because $e_0[h(Z,X)]=0$. Thus the full vector formula is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ml-rough-lift}
 \mathscr L_{\widehat h}\mathcal JX
  =\mathcal J\left(\mathscr L_hX+\nabla_ZX-h(Z,X)Z\right).
\end{equation}

The form version can be calculated without an unspecified viscosity
convention. Define the codifferential on differential forms by
$\delta_h\alpha=-\sum_a\iota_{e_a}\nabla_{e_a}\alpha$ and the
nonnegative Hodge Laplacian by
$\Delta_h^{\mathrm H}=d\delta_h+\delta_hd$.
For every $k$-form $\alpha$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ml-codiff}
 \delta_{\widehat h}\pi^*\alpha
       =\pi^*(\delta_h\alpha-\iota_Z\alpha),\qquad
 \Delta_{\widehat h}^{\mathrm H}\pi^*\alpha
       =\pi^*(\Delta_h^{\mathrm H}\alpha-\mathcal L_Z\alpha).
\end{equation}
Here contractions of zero-forms are zero. To prove the first identity,
the horizontal frame terms agree with the base terms by
\eqref{eq:ml-lift-connection} and
$\widehat\nabla_{\mathcal Je_a}e_0=0$. For $k\geq1$ and horizontal
$Y_1,\ldots,Y_{k-1}$, the vertical contribution before its minus sign is
\[
 (\widehat\nabla_{e_0}\pi^*\alpha)
       (e_0,\mathcal JY_1,\ldots,\mathcal JY_{k-1})
 =\alpha(Z,Y_1,\ldots,Y_{k-1}).
\]
The derivative of the initially zero evaluation is zero, the derivative
of its first slot is $-Z$, and derivatives of the other slots are
vertical and still give zero by alternation. Evaluations with an
additional vertical slot are zero. This proves the first formula on
all tangent arguments. Applying $d$, commuting it with pullback, and
also applying the first identity to $d\alpha$ gives
$\Delta^{\mathrm H}_{\widehat h}\pi^*\alpha
=\pi^*(\Delta_h^{\mathrm H}\alpha-d\iota_Z\alpha-\iota_Zd\alpha)$.
The equality $d\iota_Z+\iota_Zd=\mathcal L_Z$ follows in coordinates
by differentiating the coefficients of $\alpha$ and of $Z$: its
coefficient for indices $i_1,\ldots,i_k$ is
$Z^j\partial_j\alpha_{i_1\cdots i_k}
+\sum_{r=1}^k(\partial_{i_r}Z^j)
  \alpha_{i_1\cdots i_{r-1}j i_{r+1}\cdots i_k}$,
which is the coordinate definition of the Lie derivative. This proves
the second formula, including its sign. For functions it is consistent
with \eqref{eq:ml-scalar-Laplace} since
$\Delta_h^{\mathrm H}F=-\Delta_hF$ under the specified conventions.

These identities supply an exact viscous bridge with all added terms.
For example retain a dimensional viscosity $\nu>0$ and define
\[
 \mathcal V_h(U,p,F)
 =\partial_tU+\nabla_UU+\operatorname{grad}_hp
                           -\nu\mathscr L_hU-F.
\]
Equation \eqref{eq:ml-rough-lift} yields, for every input triple,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ml-viscous-residual}
 \mathcal V_{\widehat h}(\mathcal JU,\pi^*p,\mathcal JF)
 =\mathcal J\left(\mathcal V_h(U,p,F)
              -\nu[\nabla_ZU-h(Z,U)Z]\right).
\end{equation}
Accordingly the explicitly corrected force
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ml-viscous-force}
 \widehat F_{\rm corr}
 =\mathcal J\left(F-\nu[\nabla_ZU-h(Z,U)Z]\right)
\end{equation}
gives the exact identity
