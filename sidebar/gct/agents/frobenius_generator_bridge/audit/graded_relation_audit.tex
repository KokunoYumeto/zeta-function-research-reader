\subsection{Exact tensor-path cancellation and its categorical obstruction}
\label{subsec:fgb-path-cancellation}

This construction retains the source-correct ordered states
$(T,T_1,T_2,Q)$, with coefficient entries
\[
 \alpha=[7]-[3],\qquad \beta=[8],\qquad
 \gamma=[6],\qquad \delta=-[3],\qquad
 C=q^{10}-q^4-q^{-4}+q^{-10}.
\]
Write $\mathbb Z\{w\}$ for a free rank-one graded module whose basis
has weight $w$, and $\Pi$ for parity reversal. All tensor products in
this subsection are over $\mathbb Z$ until a coefficient extension is
specified. Define even graded free modules with the indicated named bases:
\begin{align*}
 A&=\bigoplus_{a\in\{-6,-4,4,6\}}\mathbb Z a_a,&
 G&=\bigoplus_{g\in\{-5,-3,-1,1,3,5\}}\mathbb Z g_g,\\
 B&=\bigoplus_{b\in\{-7,-5,-3,-1,1,3,5,7\}}\mathbb Z b_b,&
 J&=\bigoplus_{j\in\{-2,0,2\}}\mathbb Z j_j.
\end{align*}
Every subscript is its basis vector's weight. In particular
$\operatorname{ch}A=[7]-[3]$ exactly: the weights $-2,0,2$ of $[3]$
cancel those same weights of $[7]$, leaving precisely the four displayed
weights. The other characters are $\operatorname{ch}G=[6]$,
$\operatorname{ch}B=[8]$, and
$\operatorname{sch}(\Pi J)=-[3]$.
These equalities retain the original Laurent coefficients and do not
replace the source vectors or their rescalings.

Put $P=\mathbb Z\{-1\}\oplus\mathbb Z\{1\}$ and define the
zero-differential super module
\[
 \mathcal C=C^+\oplus\Pi C^-,\qquad
 C^+=\mathbb Z\{-10\}\oplus\mathbb Z\{10\},\qquad
 C^-=\mathbb Z\{-4\}\oplus\mathbb Z\{4\}.
\]
Thus $\operatorname{ch}P=[2]$ and
$\operatorname{sch}\mathcal C=C$, with every original exponent and sign
retained. The two length-two source paths have coefficient objects
\[
 E=A\otimes G,\qquad \Pi O=\Pi(B\otimes J).
\]
The tensor order here is fixed by the named path bases: $e_{a,g}=a_a\otimes
g_g$ labels $T\to T_1\to Q$, and $o_{b,j}=b_b\otimes j_j$ labels
$T\to T_2\to Q$. Their weights are $a+g$ and $b+j$ respectively.
Consequently the source relation is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fgb-path-euler}
 \operatorname{ch}E-\operatorname{ch}O
 =[6]([7]-[3])-[8][3]=[2]C.
\end{equation}
The finite basis calculation below proves this identity and constructs
the stronger chain maps that its scalar statement alone does not supply.

For every weight $w$, list the pairs $(a,g)$ and $(b,j)$ with sum $w$
in increasing lexicographic order. Write the corresponding basis lists
as $e_{w,1},\ldots,e_{w,r_w}$ and $o_{w,1},\ldots,o_{w,s_w}$.
Set $m_w=\min(r_w,s_w)$. The complete count and unmatched basis table is
\[
\begin{array}{r|rrr|c|c}
 w&r_w&s_w&m_w&\text{unmatched }(a,g)&\text{unmatched }(b,j)\\\hline
 11&1&0&0&(6,5)&--\\
 9&2&1&1&(6,3)&--\\
 7&2&2&2&--&--\\
 5&2&3&2&--&(7,-2)\\
 3&2&3&2&--&(5,-2)\\
 1&3&3&3&--&--\\
 -1&3&3&3&--&--\\
 -3&2&3&2&--&(-1,-2)\\
 -5&2&3&2&--&(-3,-2)\\
 -7&2&2&2&--&--\\
 -9&2&1&1&(-4,-5)&--\\
 -11&1&0&0&(-6,-5)&--
\end{array}
\]
For example the weight-nine even list is
$((4,5),(6,3))$ and its odd list is $((7,2))$; the rule pairs
$(4,5)$ with $(7,2)$ and leaves $(6,3)$. This also fixes which vector
survives whenever the weight has more than one path. The twelve rows
exhaust all sums in the four displayed finite sets, and
$\sum r_w=\sum s_w=24$, $\sum m_w=20$.

Form the cochain complex $\Gamma^0=E$, $\Gamma^1=O$, zero in other
degrees, with the integral, weight-zero differential
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fgb-cancel-differential}
 d(e_{w,k})=o_{w,k}\quad(1\leq k\leq m_w),\qquad
 d(e_{w,k})=0\quad(k>m_w).
\end{equation}
It satisfies $d^2=0$ because its image lies in the final cochain degree.
Give $P\otimes C^+$ cochain degree zero and $P\otimes C^-$ degree one,
both with zero differential. A projection $\pi:\Gamma\to P\otimes
\mathcal C$ kills every matched basis vector, and on unmatched vectors
is the following explicit map. Here $p_u$ has weight $u$, $c_v^+$ has
weight $v\in\{-10,10\}$, and $c_v^-$ has weight
$v\in\{-4,4\}$:
\begin{align*}
 e_{6,5}&\longmapsto p_1\otimes c_{10}^+,&
 e_{6,3}&\longmapsto p_{-1}\otimes c_{10}^+,\\
 e_{-4,-5}&\longmapsto p_1\otimes c_{-10}^+,&
 e_{-6,-5}&\longmapsto p_{-1}\otimes c_{-10}^+,\\
 o_{7,-2}&\longmapsto p_1\otimes c_4^-,&
 o_{5,-2}&\longmapsto p_{-1}\otimes c_4^-,\\
 o_{-1,-2}&\longmapsto p_1\otimes c_{-4}^-,&
 o_{-3,-2}&\longmapsto p_{-1}\otimes c_{-4}^-.
\end{align*}
Let $\iota$ send each displayed target basis vector back to its stated
unmatched source vector. Both maps preserve weight and cochain degree.
They commute with differentials: the unmatched vectors have zero
differential, and the projection kills the image of $d$. They satisfy
$\pi\iota=1$ on every target basis vector.

Define $h:\Gamma^1\to\Gamma^0$ by
$h(o_{w,k})=e_{w,k}$ for $k\leq m_w$, and zero on unmatched vectors;
put $h=0$ on $\Gamma^0$. On a matched even vector $hd=1$ and $dh=0$;
on a matched odd vector $dh=1$ and $hd=0$; on every unmatched vector
$dh+hd=0$. Since $\iota\pi$ is zero on matched vectors and identity
on unmatched vectors, these basis calculations prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fgb-path-contraction}
 1-\iota\pi=dh+hd,\qquad
 h^2=0,\qquad \pi h=0,\qquad h\iota=0.
\end{equation}
They are identities over $\mathbb Z$, not just after passing to a field.
Thus $\Gamma$ is chain homotopy equivalent to $P\otimes\mathcal C$.
More explicitly it is chain isomorphic to their direct sum with the
twenty two-term complexes
$\mathbb Z\{w\}\xrightarrow{1}\mathbb Z\{w\}$ indexed by the
matched pairs. This proves both the cohomology and the precise
contractible part, and proves \eqref{eq:fgb-path-euler} by taking the
alternating weight ranks of this actual direct-sum decomposition.

There is also an exact comparison without introducing a differential:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fgb-stable-isomorphism}
 E\oplus(P\otimes C^-)
 \ \cong\ O\oplus(P\otimes C^+)
 \quad\text{as ordinary graded free modules.}
\end{equation}
At weight $w$, concatenate the lexicographic path list with the target
basis list displayed above, and map its $k$th vector to the $k$th
vector of the other side. The table shows that the two list lengths
are equal at every weight. The inverse uses the reverse ordered list
map, so this is a proved integral isomorphism with all its components
specified. Its total rank is $28$ on each side.

\paragraph{Frobenius-equivariant realization and its retained grading.}
Fix a prime power $Q$, a prime $\ell$ not dividing $Q$, and a field
$L=\mathbb Q_\ell(\tau)$ with a chosen root $\tau^2=Q$. The letter
$q$ remains the formal weight parameter above; it is not renamed $Q$.
There is a continuous one-dimensional $L$-representation $\mathcal L$
of $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb F}_Q/\mathbb F_Q)$ on which
the chosen geometric Frobenius acts by $\tau$. Indeed $\tau$ is an
$\ell$-adic unit. In each finite group
$(\mathcal O_L/\mathfrak m_L^n)^\times$ its reduction has finite order,
so the homomorphism from the integers sending $1$ to this reduction
extends to $\widehat{\mathbb Z}$. These homomorphisms are compatible
as $n$ varies; their inverse limit gives the stated continuous unit
character. Identify the generator $1$ with geometric Frobenius, thereby
fixing the Frobenius convention as well as the chosen square root.

Send a weight-$w$ basis line to $\mathcal L^{\otimes w}$, using dual
powers for $w<0$, and extend by direct sums and tensor products.
Send a weight-preserving integral matrix to its scalar extension to $L$.
This defines a tensor functor to finite-dimensional continuous
Frobenius representations, since weights add under tensor product and
$\tau^{u+v}=\tau^u\tau^v$ exactly. The functor preserves parity and
the Koszul symmetry when applied to super modules. It sends all maps
$d,\pi,\iota,h$ above to Frobenius-equivariant maps: on each nonzero
matrix entry its source and target Frobenius eigenvalues are the same
$\tau^w$. Therefore \eqref{eq:fgb-path-contraction} remains an identity
of Frobenius-equivariant complexes. The statement also holds after
two-periodization, with even and odd cochain degrees identified modulo
two, retaining the stated parity on every basis vector.

One may index the even arrow bases by the usual cell labels
\[
 a=2i-6\ (i\in\{0,1,5,6\}),\quad
 g=2j-5\ (0\leq j\leq5),\quad
 b=2k-7\ (0\leq k\leq7),\quad
 j'=2l-2\ (0\leq l\leq2).
\]
These are respectively the centered cell-weight labels of
$\mathbb A^0\sqcup\mathbb A^1\sqcup\mathbb A^5\sqcup\mathbb A^6$,
$\mathbb P^5$, $\mathbb P^7$, and $\mathbb P^2$, with the last
coefficient given odd parity. The maps constructed here are maps of
their explicitly specified weight lines. They are not asserted to be
induced by algebraic correspondences between those schemes. In
particular two-periodization forgets actual differences of even
cohomological degree; matching weights does not prove an isomorphism
preserving the original integer-valued geometric cohomological degrees.

\paragraph{What the chain comparison does and does not implement.}
Take the four-by-four tensor-functor matrix on quadruples of graded
super modules whose nonzero coefficients are $A$ in position $(1,0)$,
$B$ in $(2,0)$, $G$ in $(3,1)$, and $\Pi J$ in $(3,2)$. Give every
arrow coefficient zero differential. Its square has the single
coefficient $E\oplus\Pi O$ in position $(3,0)$, so its super rank is
$24\mid24$. The proposed shortest divided-square coefficient
$P\otimes\mathcal C$ has super rank $4\mid4$. Thus these coefficient
objects are not isomorphic, even after forgetting weight. Viewed as
zero-differential complexes they cannot be quasi-isomorphic, because
their cohomology is the same respective underlying objects. The
discrepancy persists after two-periodization. These facts are proved
by the displayed unequal ranks; no general assertion of unrelatedness
is drawn from them.

The complex $\Gamma$ remedies the endpoint comparison by adding the
specific nonzero differential \eqref{eq:fgb-cancel-differential}.
That differential is extra relation data. It is not the tensor
differential of the four zero-differential arrow objects. Indeed a
tensor or differential-graded composition must obey
$d(v\circ u)=d(v)\circ u+(-1)^{\deg v}v\circ d(u)$.
If both original arrows are closed then their composite is closed,
whereas the twenty specified composites have nonzero image under
\eqref{eq:fgb-cancel-differential}. Hence this particular construction
cannot assert a natural isomorphism of the original generator functors
$\mathcal N^2\cong P\otimes\mathcal D$. It supplies the complete
stable isomorphism \eqref{eq:fgb-stable-isomorphism} and the complete
endpoint chain homotopy equivalence instead. A larger construction may
change or enrich the arrow data, but must prove its new composition
maps and compatibility; they are not inferred here.

Finally $\delta=-q^2-1-q^{-2}$ cannot be the weight character of an
ordinary graded finite-dimensional vector space: each such character's
coefficient is a nonnegative dimension, while these three coefficients
are $-1$. After the positive real evaluation $q=\sqrt Q$, the value
$-Q-1-Q^{-1}$ also cannot be a point count or a trace on an even direct
sum of Tate lines with positive Frobenius eigenvalues. The actual
odd object $\Pi J$ constructed above realizes the negative signs
exactly by supertrace. This obstruction is restricted to the stated
even positive-coefficient model; it does not exclude general mixed
cohomology, signed correspondences, or Frobenius representations with
other eigenvalues.
