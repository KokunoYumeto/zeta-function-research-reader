\section{The common heat space and the two moving quotient coordinates}
\label{ccb:continuation}
The full continuation in Appendices~\ref{sec:cq-source}--\ref{sec:cq-tests}
uses $\cqE=\mathcal H_{\leq1}$ for the entire functions of order at
most one. This is the same underlying function set denoted by
$\mathcal H$ in Section~\ref{ccb:bridge}. Its source topology is
$\tau$, its original relation is $N_{\rm ar}=N_{\rm C}$, and its
original quotient is $Q_{\rm ar}=Q_{\rm C}$. These equalities identify
the unchanged source objects. The symbol $\E$ elsewhere in this
manuscript still denotes the weighted smooth Fourier-kernel space,
and $\A=T\E$ retains its transported topology. In this section write
$N_t^{\A},Q_t^{\A}$ for the earlier Fourier relation and quotient,
and $N_t^{\mathcal H},Q_t^{\mathcal H}$ for those in
\eqref{eq:cq-Qt}. Thus every coordinate in the comparison has a
specified source and target. The topology called $\kappa$ in the
continuation is its compact-convergence topology; the positive
physical energy scale $\kappa=2g^2/a$ remains the separate real
parameter of Section~\ref{sec:xi4-packet}.

\begin{theorem}[Equality of the heat maps on the common representatives]
\label{ccb:common-heat}
On $C=\A\cap\mathcal H$, the two globally defined maps
\[
 U_t^{\A}f=T\bigl(e^{tv^2/4}T^{-1}f\bigr),\qquad
 U_t^{\mathcal H}f=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
              \frac{(-t/4)^k}{k!}\partial_s^{2k}f
\]
agree as entire functions, for every $t\in\C$. In particular $C$
is invariant under each map and its inverse. Give $C_t=C$ the
topology induced by
\[
 C_t\longrightarrow\A\times(\mathcal H,\tau_t),\qquad f\longmapsto(f,f).
\]
Then $U_t:C_0\longrightarrow C_t$ is a linear homeomorphism with
inverse $U_{-t}$; the parameter in the inverse indicates the function
map from $C_t$ back to $C_0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take $f=T\phi\in C$. For $|t|\leq T_0$ and $|s|\leq R_0$,
the absolute value of the Fourier integrand is bounded by
$4|\phi(v)|\exp(T_0v^2/4+R_0|v|)$. Its integral is finite by
the defining seminorms of $\E$. The sum of the absolute values of
the time-exponential terms has this same integrable bound. Absolute
integration of the series therefore gives, at every $(t,s)$,
\begin{align*}
 U_t^{\A}f(s)
 &=4\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(t/4)^k}{k!}
                   \int_{\R}v^{2k}\phi(v)e^{isv}\,dv\\
 &=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-t/4)^k}{k!}\partial_s^{2k}f(s).
\end{align*}
The second identity uses $(iv)^{2k}=(-1)^kv^{2k}$, and the
same weighted bounds justify every spatial derivative. The series
on the right is precisely the convergent $\gamma$-series proved in
Lemma~\ref{thm:cq-heat}; convergence in $\gamma$ implies convergence
at every $s$. Hence the two maps agree with all constants and signs
as displayed. Their common output is in both underlying spaces.
Applying this conclusion at $-t$ proves invariance under the inverse.

The product map $U_t^{\A}\times U_t^{\mathcal H}$ is a
homeomorphism from $\A\times(\mathcal H,\tau)$ to
$\A\times(\mathcal H,\tau_t)$: the first assertion is the proved
Fourier heat group, and the second is the exact definition of
$\tau_t$. The pointwise equality just proved maps the first
diagonal onto the second diagonal. Restricting the product map and
its continuous inverse proves the claimed homeomorphism.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Simultaneous moving quotient correspondence]
\label{ccb:simultaneous}
Put
\[
 \mathscr K_t=N_t^{\A}\cap N_t^{\mathcal H},\qquad
 \mathscr R_t=C_t/\mathscr K_t.
\]
Then $\mathscr K_t=U_t\mathscr K_0$, and the following maps have the stated types:
\begin{align}
 \overline U_t &: \mathscr R_0\longrightarrow\mathscr R_t,
       & [f]_{\mathscr K_0}&\longmapsto[U_tf]_{\mathscr K_t},\label{ccb:relation-heat}\\
 J_t &: \mathscr R_t\longrightarrow Q_t^{\A}\times Q_t^{\mathcal H},
       & [g]_{\mathscr K_t}&\longmapsto([g]_{N_t^{\A}},[g]_{N_t^{\mathcal H}}).
       \label{ccb:simultaneous-map}
\end{align}
The first map is a homeomorphism and the second a continuous
injection. Its two coordinate images and kernels are exactly
\begin{align*}
 \operatorname{im}\pi_{\A,t}&=\{[g]_{N_t^{\A}}:g\in C\},&
 \ker\pi_{\A,t}&=(N_t^{\A}\cap\mathcal H)/\mathscr K_t,\\
 \operatorname{im}\pi_{\mathcal H,t}&=\{[g]_{N_t^{\mathcal H}}:g\in C\},&
 \ker\pi_{\mathcal H,t}&=(\A\cap N_t^{\mathcal H})/\mathscr K_t.
\end{align*}
The first image is dense in $Q_t^{\A}$. For
$B_t=M_s-(t/2)\partial_s$, the induced continuous operator on
$\mathscr R_t$ satisfies the complete coordinate identity
\begin{equation}
 J_t([B_tg]_{\mathscr K_t})=
 \bigl([B_tg]_{N_t^{\A}},[B_tg]_{N_t^{\mathcal H}}\bigr).
 \label{ccb:simultaneous-operator}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $f\in \mathscr K_0$, both definitions of the moving relation put $U_tf$
in $\mathscr K_t$. Conversely, $g\in \mathscr K_t$ lies in both function spaces;
Theorem~\ref{ccb:common-heat} identifies both inverse images as the
same $U_{-t}g$. Each moving-relation definition puts this inverse
in its original relation, so it belongs to $\mathscr K_0$. This proves the
equality, including both inclusions. The homeomorphism of $C_0$
and $C_t$ consequently descends to the homeomorphism
\eqref{ccb:relation-heat}, with inverse $[g]\mapsto[U_{-t}g]$.

The two coordinate quotient maps are continuous on the product
and therefore on its diagonal. Their joint kernel on $C_t$ is
exactly $\mathscr K_t$. The quotient universal property gives continuity
and injectivity of $J_t$. Testing its first coordinate for zero
means precisely $g\in N_t^{\A}$ in addition to $g\in C$; testing
the second means $g\in N_t^{\mathcal H}$. This proves both kernel
identities and the displayed image formulas, without an assumption
about a topology on the image of the injection. Density follows
from the cutoff proof in Section~\ref{ccb:bridge}: $C$ is dense in
$\A$, and its image under the continuous surjective quotient map
is dense in $Q_t^{\A}$.

At time zero, multiplication $M_s$ is continuous on both spaces,
preserves $C$, and preserves both closed original relations.
It therefore preserves $\mathscr K_0$. Conjugation by the proved
homeomorphism gives a continuous operator on $C_t$ preserving
$\mathscr K_t$. In both coordinates its entire-function formula is
$U_tM_sU_{-t}=M_s-(t/2)\partial_s$. Passing to representatives
proves \eqref{ccb:simultaneous-operator} in both quotient coordinates.
\end{proof}

This also gives the exact map from the earlier fixed-second-coordinate
presentation \eqref{ccb:heat-correspondence} to the present one. If
$\mathcal U_t^{\mathcal H}:Q_{\rm C}\to Q_t^{\mathcal H}$ is the
source heat homeomorphism, then
\begin{equation}
 (\operatorname{id}_{Q_t^{\A}}\times\mathcal U_t^{\mathcal H})
                 j_t=J_t\overline U_t.
 \label{ccb:old-new-correspondence}
\end{equation}
Both sides send $[f]_{\mathscr K_0}$ to
$([U_tf]_{N_t^{\A}},[U_tf]_{N_t^{\mathcal H}})$; this proves the
identity with its domain, codomain and inverse coordinate change.

\paragraph{The actual fourth coefficient in the same function space.}
The transported coefficient retains its complete formula
\begin{align*}
 K_t(s)=T_DZ_t(s)
 ={}&-\frac{59}{275184}Z_t(s+3)-\frac1{336}Z_t'(s+3)\\
 &+\frac1{1296}Z_t(s+9/2)+\frac3{132496}Z_t(s+13/2).
\end{align*}
The resulting map is explicitly
\[
 C\longrightarrow\mathscr R_t\xrightarrow{\ J_t\ }
 Q_t^{\A}\times Q_t^{\mathcal H},\qquad
 f\longmapsto[f]_{\mathscr K_t}
 \longmapsto([f]_{N_t^{\A}},[f]_{N_t^{\mathcal H}}),
\]
applied to the actual $f=K_t$. Its joint kernel is
$\mathscr K_t$, as proved above. For this application $K_t$
lies in $C$: $Z_t=U_t\Xi\in C$, and the four displayed operations
preserve both underlying function spaces. On $\A$, a translation
by $h$ multiplies the kernel by $e^{ihv}$ and differentiation
multiplies it by $iv$; every seminorm remains finite by Leibniz's
rule and the weights already defining $\E$. On $\mathcal H$, the
translation bound is, for $b=a/2^{p-1}$,
\[
 \|f(\,\cdot+h)\|_{p,a}\leq e^{a|h|^p}\|f\|_{p,b},
\]
because $|s+h|^p\leq2^{p-1}(|s|^p+|h|^p)$; differentiation has
the bound \eqref{eq:cq-cauchy}. Thus $T_D:C\to C$ is a well-defined
linear map on the common underlying representatives. The formula
does not require that $T_D$ preserve either quotient relation.
Its exact inverse on $\A$, the entire jet quotients and the retained
analytic-unit recovery were proved earlier; the present calculation
places the actual coefficient in both moving quotient coordinates.
The incidence factor remains $a_{ef}K_t$ in the original
fourth-order covariance, so these same linear maps retain $a_{ef}$.
