\subsection*{The retained six-channel map and the physical angular projection}

The preceding complete imported note defines
$K_\nu=\mathcal F_3^{-1}LJ_\nu D$, its adjoint, range projection
$P_\nu=K_\nu K_\nu^*$, generator $A=s^2/4$ and all their domains.
Its $J_\nu$ is the displayed radial Fourier isometry; the original
polynomial differential $J=DS$ remains the different explicitly
defined matrix in the earlier appendices. Composition here uses
the physical velocity as common data, through the exact inverses
$u=K_\nu h+w$ and $(X,D)\mapsto u\circ X$ already proved; neither
map's coordinates are silently substituted for the other's.
All six angular functions, their Gram factors and viscosity constants
are retained exactly as in the imported equations (1)--(25).

Use the physical angular-momentum projection
$\Pi u=\langle r u_\theta\rangle$ of the preceding Mellin appendix,
with $\sigma=r^2/2$. Its full Hilbert target is
\[
 \mathcal B=L^2\left((0,\infty)\times\R,
                   \frac{2\pi}{2\sigma}\,d\sigma\,dz;\C\right),
 \qquad
 \|B\|_{\mathcal B}^2=2\pi\int_\R\int_0^\infty
                       \frac{|B(\sigma,z)|^2}{2\sigma}d\sigma dz.
\]
Jensen's inequality on each circle and $d\sigma=r\,dr$ prove
\[
 \|\Pi u\|_{\mathcal B}^2
 =2\pi\int|\langle u_\theta\rangle|^2r\,dr\,dz
 \leq\int|u_\theta|^2r\,d\theta\,dr\,dz\leq\|u\|_V^2.
\]
Thus the formula defines a bounded map on the full physical $L^2$
space, by Fubini, without a pointwise trace assumption at the axis.
The exact section of \eqref{am:section} extends to an isometry
$\mathcal R:\mathcal B\to V$ because
\[
 (\mathcal RB)(x)=\frac{B(\sigma,z)}r e_\theta,
 \qquad \|\mathcal RB\|_V^2=\|B\|_{\mathcal B}^2,
 \qquad \Pi\mathcal RB=B.
\]
Its divergence is zero in distributions: its pairing with a gradient
has the angular factor $\partial_\theta\phi$, whose integral over
the circle is zero; absolute integrability follows from the $L^2$
bound and the compactly supported smooth test gradient.
For the inner products linear in the first argument, polar integration
also gives
$\langle u,\mathcal RB\rangle_V
 =\langle\Pi u,B\rangle_{\mathcal B}$.
Consequently $\mathcal R^*=\Pi$ and
\[
 C=\mathcal R\Pi=C^*=C^2
\]
is the orthogonal projection onto all axisymmetric azimuthal fields.
This gives both its full range and its kernel, and the norm retains
the complete $2\pi/(2\sigma)$ weight.

\subsection*{Rotation and reflection prove that the six channels have zero mean swirl}

Let $R_\alpha$ be rotation by $\alpha$ about $e_3$, and let
$S=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,1)$. For any orthogonal matrix $O$ define
the unitary polar-vector action
\[
 (T_Ou)(x)=O u(O^{-1}x).
\]
On the Fourier-coordinate copy $\widehat V=L^2(d\xi;\C^3)$ define
$(\widehat T_Og)(\xi)=O g(O^{-1}\xi)$.
Substitution $x=Oy$, with the absolute Jacobian one, in the original
Fourier integral proves
$\mathcal F_3T_O=\widehat T_O\mathcal F_3$, first on integrable square
integrable fields and then on $L^2$ by density and unitarity.
For $O=R_\alpha$ or $O=S$, both $Y_0$ and $Y_1$ are invariant,
$L(O\omega)=O L(\omega)O^{-1}$, and both branch matrices in $D$
commute with $O$. Substituting these three identities into the full
formula for $K_\nu$ proves
\begin{equation}
 T_OK_\nu h=K_\nu(Oh),
 \qquad (Oh)(s)=O h(s).
 \label{sc:equivariance}
\end{equation}
The exact mean rotation operator is the bounded integral
$\mathcal Z=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_0^{2\pi}T_{R_\alpha}d\alpha$.
Equation \eqref{sc:equivariance} gives
\[
 \mathcal ZK_\nu h=K_\nu(h_3e_3),
 \qquad T_S\mathcal ZK_\nu h=\mathcal ZK_\nu h.
\]
For a smooth vector field fixed by all rotations, its radial,
azimuthal and axial components are functions of $(r,z)$.
At $x=(r,0,z)$ reflection invariance under $S$ makes its second
component zero. Rotation then gives zero azimuthal component
everywhere. Equivalently, for every smooth $u$,
\begin{equation}
 C u=\tfrac12(I-T_S)\mathcal Z u.
 \label{sc:Cformula}
\end{equation}
Indeed at $(r,0,z)$ the right side retains precisely the azimuthal
component of $\mathcal Zu$, and its value is
$\langle u_\theta\rangle e_\theta$. Rotation transports the identity
over each circle. The formula extends to all $L^2$ fields by density
and boundedness. Applying it to the preceding reflection identity proves
\begin{equation}
 CK_\nu=0,\qquad \Pi K_\nu=0,
 \qquad CP_\nu=P_\nu C=0.
 \label{sc:annihilation}
\end{equation}
For the second identity use $\Pi C=\Pi$; the last follows by taking
adjoints as well. This is an exact operator computation, not a
nonidentity assertion about two coordinate presentations.
It does not say that $K_\nu h$ has zero azimuthal component at each
angle: nonconstant angular components can remain before averaging.

\subsection*{An exact three-component state space, energy and heat domains}

Set
$V_{\rm rem}=V_\sigma\cap\ker K_\nu^*\cap\ker\Pi$ and
$Q_\nu^{\rm rem}=I-P_\nu-C$ on $V_\sigma$. Orthogonality in
\eqref{sc:annihilation} makes this the projection onto $V_{\rm rem}$.
The complete correspondence is
\begin{align}
 u&\longmapsto(h,B,v)=(K_\nu^*u,\Pi u,Q_\nu^{\rm rem}u),
 &u&=K_\nu h+\mathcal RB+v,\label{sc:triple}\\
 \|u\|_V^2&=\|h\|_H^2+\|B\|_{\mathcal B}^2+\|v\|_V^2.
 \label{sc:energy}
\end{align}
The domain is $V_\sigma$ and the target is exactly
$H\oplus\mathcal B\oplus V_{\rm rem}$. Applying $K_\nu^*$,
$\Pi$ and $Q_\nu^{\rm rem}$ to the displayed inverse proves
surjectivity and both inverse identities. Pairwise orthogonality
proves the energy equality. On real physical velocities the first
component obeys the imported branch conjugation (21), while $B,v$
are real; the projections commute with the corresponding conjugations.
The original two-component remainder is exactly
\begin{equation}
 w=(I-P_\nu)u=\mathcal RB+v.
 \label{sc:oldremainder}
\end{equation}
Its inverse coordinates are $B=\Pi w$ and $v=(I-C)w$.

Let $B_\nu=-\nu\Delta$ with the full Fourier domain in imported
(23). Orthogonal-coordinate actions $T_O$ commute with its radial
Fourier multiplier and preserve its domain. Hence \eqref{sc:Cformula}
proves that $C$ commutes with $B_\nu$, all its nonnegative integer
powers, and the heat multipliers, with preservation of their domains.
One may also see domain preservation by applying the angular projection
at each Fourier radius before integrating its weighted squared norm.
Define the exact swirl generator and domain by
\begin{align*}
 G_\nu&=\Pi B_\nu\mathcal R,\\
 \operatorname{Dom}(G_\nu)
 &=\left\{B\in\mathcal B:
    \int_{\R^3}\nu^2|\xi|^4
       |\mathcal F_3(\mathcal RB)(\xi)|^2d\xi<\infty\right\}.
\end{align*}
This preserves the actual spatial and Fourier domain, including the
axis; no unrestricted formal half-line boundary condition replaces it.
On this domain $B_\nu\mathcal R=\mathcal RG_\nu$ and
$\|G_\nu B\|_{\mathcal B}=\|B_\nu\mathcal RB\|_V$,
by invariance of the range and the isometry.
For smooth axis-regular compactly supported $B$, the original
cylindrical vector Laplacian gives explicitly
\[
 G_\nu B=-\nu(2\sigma B_{\sigma\sigma}+B_{zz}).
\]
Indeed its azimuthal component is
$(\partial_{rr}+r^{-1}\partial_r-r^{-2}+\partial_{zz})(B/r)$;
multiplying this by $r$ gives the displayed expression using
$\partial_r=r\partial_\sigma$. The exact form integral is
\begin{align*}
 \nu\|\nabla\mathcal RB\|_V^2
 &=2\pi\nu\int\left[
   \left|B_\sigma-\frac{B}{2\sigma}\right|^2
       +\frac{|B|^2}{4\sigma^2}
       +\frac{|B_z|^2}{2\sigma}\right]d\sigma dz\\
 &=2\pi\nu\int\left[|B_\sigma|^2
                         +\frac{|B_z|^2}{2\sigma}\right]d\sigma dz.
\end{align*}
For the second equality the retained cross term is
$-\operatorname{Re}(B_\sigma\overline B)/\sigma$.
Integration of $(|B|^2)'/\sigma$ cancels precisely the displayed
$|B|^2/(2\sigma^2)$ terms; the boundary value $|B|^2/\sigma$
vanishes at zero by $B=O(\sigma)$ and at infinity by compact support.
Thus both expressions account for the same full spatial derivatives.

The corresponding heat action on $\mathcal B$ is explicitly
$E_\nu(t)=\Pi e^{-tB_\nu}\mathcal R$, $t\geq0$.
Range invariance and $\Pi\mathcal R=I$ prove the semigroup law,
strong continuity and the intertwiner
$e^{-tB_\nu}\mathcal R=\mathcal RE_\nu(t)$.
Its generator is $-G_\nu$ on exactly the stated domain, by conjugating
the Fourier difference quotient through the isometry. This establishes
the heat correspondence at the axis as well as on $r>0$.

\subsection*{The actual blowup belongs to an explicit orthogonal component}

\[

   =\sqrt\nu\,\tau^{-1/2-h}(e_0+R_*(\tau)),
 \qquad r_{\nu,\tau}=\sqrt{2\nu X_{\rm in}\tau}.
\]
This value is independent of $\theta$ by the definition of

$|R_*(\tau)|\leq e_0/2$, it proves
\begin{equation}
 \|\mathcal RB(1-\tau)\|_\infty
       \geq\frac{\sqrt\nu e_0}{2}\tau^{-1/2-h},
 \qquad
 \|w(1-\tau)\|_\infty
       \geq\frac{\sqrt\nu e_0}{2}\tau^{-1/2-h}.
 \label{sc:componentgrowth}
\end{equation}
For the second inequality, $\Pi w=B$ gives
$|B|/r=|\langle w_\theta\rangle|

No growth assertion about $K_\nu h$ alone is necessary or inferred.

\]
