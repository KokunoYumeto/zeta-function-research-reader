The shared letters are retained through the following explicit product
space and maps. Subscripts in this paragraph identify the original
coordinate being evaluated: $a_{\rm lat}=a$ in the Hamiltonian,
$b_{\rm mag}=b$ in its potential, and $(a_{\rm pol},b_{\rm pol},c_{
\rm pol})=(a,b,c)$ in the polynomial target. Put
\[
\mathcal P_L=(0,\infty)_{a_{\rm lat}}\times(0,\infty)_g,
\quad \mathcal X_L=\mathcal P_L\times\C^3_{x,y,w},
\quad \mathcal Y_L=\mathcal P_L\times\C^3_{a_{\rm pol},b_{\rm pol},c_{\rm pol}}.
\]
The exact morphism is
\[
\widehat F_L:\mathcal X_L\longrightarrow\mathcal Y_L,
\qquad (a_{\rm lat},g,q)\longmapsto(a_{\rm lat},g,F(q)).
\]
Its parameter projection is unchanged, its polynomial projection is
the full $F$ in \eqref{hc:F}, and its derivative in this coordinate
order is $\operatorname{diag}(I_2,DF(q))$. These assertions follow by
differentiating each displayed component. The arithmetic projection
$\widehat\sigma(a_{\rm lat},g,q)=F_1(q)/2$ therefore satisfies
$\widehat\sigma=\tfrac12\operatorname{pr}_{a_{\rm pol}}\widehat F_L$.
In particular the lattice spacing is a retained parameter of the map,
while the polynomial coordinate is evaluated by its explicit component;
neither coordinate is set equal to a constant or erased.

The physical parameter chart and its globally defined positive-real inverse are
\begin{align*}
\chi:\mathcal P_L&\longrightarrow(0,\infty)_\kappa\times(0,\infty)_\xi,
&\chi(a_{\rm lat},g)&=(2g^2/a_{\rm lat},1/(4g^4)),\\
\chi^{-1}(\kappa,\xi)&=(1/(\kappa\sqrt\xi),(4\xi)^{-1/4}),
& b_{\rm mag}&=\kappa\xi.
\end{align*}
All roots here are the positive real roots. Indeed
$g^2=1/(2\sqrt\xi)$, so $2g^2/a_{\rm lat}=\kappa$ and
$1/(2g^2a_{\rm lat})=\kappa\xi$; substitution in the other direction
returns the original $a_{\rm lat},g$. Thus this is an invertible
parameter chart retaining both physical parameters. In particular a
fixed-$\kappa$ coupling path has
$a_{\rm lat}(\xi)=1/(\kappa\sqrt\xi)$ and
$g(\xi)=(4\xi)^{-1/4}$; it does not silently hold the lattice spacing
fixed. The source's finite-box coefficient has the displayed dependence
on these parameters and makes no continuum-limit assertion.

On the original generators the Pauli trace identity gives
$-\operatorname{tr}(T_iT_j)/2=\delta_{ij}/4$.
Consequently the orthonormal frame for that metric is exactly
$(2T_1,2T_2,2T_3)$ and its left derivatives are $(2X_1,2X_2,2X_3)$.
Their sum of squares is $4\sum_iX_i^2$, proving
$-\Delta^c=4E$ and the original electric coefficient $\kappa/4$.
This records the metric map with its factor, as well as the parameter map.
