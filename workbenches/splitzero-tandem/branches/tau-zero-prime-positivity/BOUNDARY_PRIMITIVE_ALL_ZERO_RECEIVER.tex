 M_f(s)=\int_{\mathbb R}f(v)e^{-(s-1/2)v}\,dv,

Let $\rho$ range over the distinct zeros of the actual completed
function $\xi$, with multiplicities $m_\rho$. Retain

 B(f,g)=\sum_\rho m_\rho

\subsection{Convergence on the original zero multiset}

Here are the analytic estimates needed below. From the original kernel,
for $u\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\tag{BPZ3}
 \Phi(u)=\sum_{n\ge1}
 (2\pi^2n^4e^{9u}-3\pi n^2e^{5u})e^{-\pi n^2e^{4u}}>0,
 \quad
 \Phi(u)\le C e^{9u-(\pi/2)e^{4u}},
 \quad C=2\pi^2\sum_{n\ge1}n^4e^{-(\pi/2)n^2}<\infty.
\end{equation}
Positivity follows termwise from $2\pi n^2e^{4u}-3>0$.
For the upper bound discard the negative term and use
$n^2e^{4u}\ge(n^2+e^{4u})/2$. The constant series converges by comparison
with a Gaussian integral. For each $\alpha>0$, the elementary square
inequality $R u\le\alpha R^2+u^2/(4\alpha)$ therefore proves
\begin{equation}\tag{BPZ4}
 |H_0(z)|\le C_\alpha e^{\alpha|z|^2},\qquad
 C_\alpha=\int_0^\infty\Phi(u)e^{u^2/(4\alpha)}du<\infty,
 \qquad H_0(0)>0.
\end{equation}
The same bounds on compact sets justify every complex derivative of
the integral, so it is entire. Its kernel is real and its cosine even;
the retained affine coordinate gives the conjugation and reflection
symmetries of $\xi$. Its Euler product and nonvanishing gamma factor
exclude zeros in $\Re s>1$, and reflection excludes $\Re s<0$.
Also $\xi(0)=\xi(1)=1/2$, from the residue of $\zeta$ at one and
reflection. Thus every zero used here satisfies
$0\le\Re\rho\le1$ and $\rho(\rho-1)\ne0$.
No zero-free theorem on the two vertical boundary lines is required.

For completeness (BPZ4) supplies a sufficient polynomial zero count.
Choose a circle radius $r\in(2R,3R)$ on which $H_0$ has no zero, for
$R\ge1$. Factoring its finitely many interior zeros leaves a zero-free
holomorphic factor in that disc. The harmonic mean-value property for
the logarithm of its modulus, and
the circle mean of $\log|re^{i\theta}-a|=\log r$ for $|a|<r$, give
\[
 \sum_{|a|<r}\operatorname{ord}_a(H_0)\log\frac r{|a|}
 =\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log|H_0(re^{i\theta})|d\theta
   -\log H_0(0).
\]
The circle identity follows by expanding
$\log(1-ae^{-i\theta}/r)$ and integrating its real part.
Every zero with $|a|\le R$ contributes at least $\log2$; (BPZ4)
bounds the right side by a constant plus $9\alpha R^2$.
Using the actual affine $z=-2i(s-1/2)$ proves
\begin{equation}\tag{BPZ5}
 \sum_{|\Im\rho|\le R}m_\rho\le C_0(1+R)^2
 \quad(R\ge0)
\end{equation}
for some finite constant $C_0$. Only finiteness of such a constant is
used below, not an unevaluated sign or arithmetic estimate.

Repeated integration by parts in (BPZ1), with zero endpoint terms
because $f$ is smooth and compactly supported, gives for every integer
$N\ge0$
\begin{equation}\tag{BPZ6}
 \sup_{0\le\sigma\le1}|M_f(\sigma+i\gamma)|
       \le C_{f,N}(1+|\gamma|)^{-N}.
\end{equation}
For $|\gamma|\ge1$, integrate $e^{-i\gamma v}$ by parts $N$ times;
the integrals of the derivatives of $f(v)e^{-(\sigma-1/2)v}$
are uniformly bounded on the compact support and $0\le\sigma\le1$.
For $|\gamma|\le1$ use the original integral. Equations (BPZ5)--(BPZ6)
prove absolute convergence of (BPZ2), by summing over dyadic height
intervals. They also prove every normal convergence assertion below.
Replacing $\rho$ by $1-\bar\rho$ proves
$B(g,f)=\overline{B(f,g)}$, so $Q(f)$ is real.

\subsection{The exact compactly supported boundary filter}

Define
\begin{equation}\tag{BPZ7}
 T=\frac{d^2}{dv^2}-\frac14,\qquad
 E(f)=(M_f(0),M_f(1)),\qquad \mathcal T_0=\ker E.
\end{equation}
Direct integration by parts twice gives, for every $s\in\mathbb C$,
\begin{equation}\tag{BPZ8}
 M_{Tg}(s)=\bigl((s-1/2)^2-1/4\bigr)M_g(s)
          =s(s-1)M_g(s).
\end{equation}
In particular $T\mathcal T\subset\mathcal T_0$.
The converse has a compact inverse with all constants fixed:
\begin{equation}\tag{BPZ9}
 G(v)=-e^{-|v|/2},\qquad
 T^{-1}f(v)=(G*f)(v)
      =-\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-|v-u|/2}f(u)du
                    \quad(f\in\mathcal T_0).
\end{equation}
Indeed $G''-G/4=\delta_0$ distributionally: away from zero it is
zero, $G$ is continuous, and its first derivative has jump one.
Convolution with $f$ is smooth, since every derivative may be
transferred to the compactly supported smooth factor, and $T(G*f)=f$.
If $\operatorname{supp} f\subset[a,b]$, its value for $v>b$ is
$-e^{-v/2}M_f(0)=0$ and for $v<a$ is $-e^{v/2}M_f(1)=0$.
It is thus compactly supported in the same interval.
Finally a compactly supported solution of $Tg=0$ is a combination
of $e^{v/2}$ and $e^{-v/2}$, hence zero. This proves that
\begin{equation}\tag{BPZ10}
 T:\mathcal T\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal T_0
\end{equation}
is a linear bijection, commuting with real translations and $\#$.
For convolution, differentiation under the integral gives the exact
identities $T(f*g)=(Tf)*g=f*(Tg)$; it is not asserted that
$T(f*g)=(Tf)*(Tg)$.

There is also an explicit right inverse of the endpoint map.
Choose $b\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$ nonnegative and positive on an
interval, put $h_0(v)=e^{v/2}$, $h_1(v)=e^{-v/2}$, and form
$C_{ij}=\int b h_i h_j$. Its quadratic form is
$\int b|c_0h_0+c_1h_1|^2$, which is positive for nonzero
$(c_0,c_1)$: a linear combination of these two independent exponential
functions cannot vanish on an interval unless both coefficients vanish.
For $w\in\mathbb C^2$ set $R(w)=b\sum_j(C^{-1}w)_j h_j$.
Then $ER=I$. Consequently the complete exact sequence and a specified
projection are
\begin{equation}\tag{BPZ11}
 0\longrightarrow\mathcal T\xrightarrow{T}\mathcal T
   \xrightarrow{E}\mathbb C^2\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \Pi=I-RE,\quad \operatorname{im}\Pi=\mathcal T_0.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Every off-line zero gives a negative primitive test}

This section proves the exact equivalence, without assuming a missing
positivity estimate:
\begin{equation}\tag{BPZ12}
 \boxed{\quad Q|_{\mathcal T_0}\ge0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Re\rho=\tfrac12\text{ for every zero of the actual }\xi.\quad}
\end{equation}
The reverse implication follows immediately and absolutely from
(BPZ2): when $1-\bar\rho=\rho$, each term is
$m_\rho|M_f(\rho)|^2\ge0$, for every test including primitive tests.
We prove the other direction by constructing a negative test from any
actual off-line zero, not by assuming finite interpolation controls
an omitted tail.

Choose such a zero $\rho_*$ in the right half of the strip, using
reflection if necessary. Write $c_*=\rho_*-1/2$, so $\Re c_*>0$.
Choose an even nonnegative smooth bump $g$ of integral one supported in
$[-\delta,\delta]$, with
$\delta|c_*|<\log(3/2)$. For example a positive multiple of
$\exp(-1/(1-(v/\delta)^2))$ on that interval, extended by zero,
has these properties; each derivative tends to zero at its endpoints,
because exponential decay dominates every power of
$(1-(v/\delta)^2)^{-1}$. Then
\[
 |M_g(s)-1|\le e^{\delta|s-1/2|}-1<\tfrac12
 \quad\text{at }s=\rho_*,\ 1-\bar\rho_*.
\]
Put $f=Tg$. Equations (BPZ8)--(BPZ10) prove
$f\in\mathcal T_0$, $f\ne0$, and both transformed values at the
selected pair are nonzero. Set
\begin{equation}\tag{BPZ13}
 W_\rho=\overline{M_f(1-\bar\rho)}M_f(\rho),\qquad
 K(a)=B(f_a,f)=\sum_\rho m_\rho W_\rho
                    e^{(\rho-1/2)a}\quad(a\in\mathbb R).
\end{equation}
Here $W_{\rho_*}\ne0$. The formula follows from
$M_{f_a}(s)=e^{-(s-1/2)a}M_f(s)$, with the conjugation in the
first slot retained. The same formula proves exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{BPZ14}
 Q(f_a)=Q(f),\qquad E(f_a)=0.
\end{equation}
For the first identity the two exponential factors cancel at each
zero; for the second, each of the two zero endpoint moments is
multiplied by an exponential.

The function $K$ is unbounded on $[0,\infty)$. Here is a proof which
keeps the full infinite multiset. Absolute summability of $m_\rho
|W_\rho|$ and $\Re\rho\le1$ permit integrating its series against
$e^{-wa}$ when $\Re w>1/2$, giving
\begin{equation}\tag{BPZ15}
 \int_0^\infty e^{-wa}K(a)da
       =\sum_\rho\frac{m_\rho W_\rho}{w-(\rho-1/2)}.
\end{equation}
The series on the right is a meromorphic function of $w$ on the
whole complex plane. Indeed on any compact set avoiding its poles,
the finitely many bounded-height denominators stay uniformly away
from zero, while all other denominators have modulus at least
$|\Im\rho|/2$. The previously proved rapid summability gives normal
convergence. At $w=c_*$ its residue is
$m_{\rho_*}W_{\rho_*}\ne0$; distinct zeros have distinct poles, and
the actual multiplicity is already in this residue.

If $K$ were bounded on the positive half-line, its Laplace integral
would instead be holomorphic on $\Re w>0$, by locally dominated
differentiation under the integral. The half-plane with the discrete
pole set removed is connected: any segment joining two points meets
only finitely many poles in a compact neighborhood and can be
detoured by small arcs. The identity theorem therefore extends (BPZ15)
from $\Re w>1/2$ to that punctured half-plane. A holomorphic left
side cannot equal the right side near its nonzero-residue pole at
$c_*$. This contradiction proves the stated unboundedness.

If $Q(f)<0$, the required primitive test is already $f$.
Otherwise choose $a\ge0$ with $|K(a)|>Q(f)$, which exists by that
unboundedness, and define the actual compact test
\begin{equation}\tag{BPZ16}
 h=f_a-\frac{\overline{K(a)}}{|K(a)|}f.
\end{equation}
Hermitian symmetry, conjugate linearity in the first slot and
(BPZ14) give
\begin{equation}\tag{BPZ17}
 E(h)=0,\qquad Q(h)=2Q(f)-2|K(a)|<0.
\end{equation}
All sums in this equality converge absolutely. This proves (BPZ12)
for every zero and every multiplicity and also supplies the full
negative-test construction. No finite zero cutoff has been substituted.

\subsection{The actual boundary correction on this entire detecting space}

Retain the supplied endpoint coordinates $(A,B)=(M_f(0),M_f(1))$.
The two endpoint forms and the exact holonomy correction are
\begin{equation}\tag{BPZ18}
\begin{gathered}
 P_e(f,f)=2\Re(\overline A B),\qquad
 \overline P_e(f,f)=\tfrac12(|A|^2+|B|^2)
                         +\tfrac13\Re(\overline A B),\\
 K_e(f,f)=\tfrac12(|A|^2+|B|^2)
                         -\tfrac53\Re(\overline A B).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
For every test in $\mathcal T_0=T\mathcal T$, each of these three
expressions vanishes. Thus the supplied complete explicit formula
restricts, with every term retained, to
\begin{equation}\tag{BPZ19}
 Q(f)=\mathcal A_\infty(f,f)-\mathcal P_{\rm fin}(f,f)
                  \quad(f\in\mathcal T_0),
\end{equation}
before and after the holonomy endpoint average.
For a test written in its exact inverse coordinates $f=Tg$, this is
also an explicit prime-power calculation. Put $k=g^\#*g$ and
$\widehat g(\gamma)=\int g(v)e^{-i\gamma v}dv$. Differentiation and
the convolution identities after (BPZ10) give
$(Tg)^\#*(Tg)=T^2k$ and
$\widehat{Tg}(\gamma)=-(\gamma^2+1/4)\widehat g(\gamma)$.
The complete formula is therefore
\begin{equation}\tag{BPZ23}
\begin{aligned}
 Q(Tg)={}&\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
  (\gamma^2+1/4)^2|\widehat g(\gamma)|^2
  [\Re\psi(1/4+i\gamma/2)-\log\pi]d\gamma\\
 &-\sum_{p,\ell\ge1}(\log p)p^{-\ell/2}
   \left[k^{(4)}-\tfrac12k''+\tfrac1{16}k\right](\ell\log p)\\
 &-\sum_{p,\ell\ge1}(\log p)p^{-\ell/2}
   \left[k^{(4)}-\tfrac12k''+\tfrac1{16}k\right](-\ell\log p).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
All prime sums are finite because $k$ and its derivatives have compact
support; the integral is the original convergent archimedean form on
$Tg$. This derives every multiplier and sign in the remaining
arithmetic expression, rather than replacing it by endpoint positivity.
The archimedean term and prime sum are the original ones in the
supplied \texttt{eq:Weil}; no inequality for their difference has been
introduced. In particular the exact primitive witness (BPZ16)--(BPZ17)
to any off-line zero is unchanged by that endpoint operation.

This is a complete description of what that boundary correction can
and cannot detect. The codimension-two filter removes all its endpoint
contribution but preserves an all-zero-complete test space. The
remaining object is the actual quadratic form (BPZ19) on the precisely
constructed range (BPZ10), with its negative witnesses and translation
coefficients specified above. Positivity of the endpoint average is
not a calculation of this remaining quadratic form.

\subsection{The same construction under the complete tau lattice map}

For any complex vector space $V$ define its synchronized carrier
\[
 G_L(V)=\{(0,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}\cup(V\times\{1_L\}),
 \quad(v,\lambda)+(w,\mu)=(v+w,\lambda\vee\mu).
\]
It is a $G_L(\mathbb C)$-semimodule under
$(c,\alpha)(v,\lambda)=(cv,\alpha\wedge\lambda)$.
Every linear map $A:V\to W$ lifts to
$A^\uparrow(v,\lambda)=(Av,\lambda)$; direct substitution proves
additivity, scalar compatibility and preservation of every zero label.
Its full comparison is
\begin{equation}\tag{BPZ20}
 \Phi_V(v,\lambda)=(v,1_K\otimes\lambda)
       \in V\times K_L,\qquad K_L=K\otimes_{\mathbb B}L,
 \qquad
 (A\times I)\Phi_V=\Phi_W A^\uparrow.
\end{equation}
The target uses its componentwise $\mathbb C\times K_L$ action.
The zero/nonzero map $K\to\mathbb B$ is a semiring homomorphism;
tensoring it with $L$ gives a retraction of
$\lambda\mapsto1_K\otimes\lambda$. This proves injectivity and
retention of the entire bounded distributive lattice, without replacing
it by its Boolean characters.

Apply these exact lifts to $T,T^{-1},E,R,\Pi$ and every translation.
Equation (BPZ11) becomes an explicit split family of linear maps with
\begin{equation}\tag{BPZ21}
 (E^\uparrow)^{-1}(G_L(0))=G_L(\mathcal T_0),\qquad
 (E^\uparrow)^{-1}(e)=\mathcal T_0\times\{1_L\},\qquad
 (E^\uparrow)^{-1}(\tau)=\{\tau\}.
\end{equation}
The top-supported primitive tests map to supported zero in the endpoint
space, not to external absence. The inverse and projection maps remain
valid there with the same labels.

The spectral pairing itself has the exact lifted value
\begin{equation}\tag{BPZ22}
 B^L((f,\lambda),(g,\mu))=(B(f,g),\lambda\wedge\mu),\qquad
 \Phi_{\mathbb C}B^L
       =(B(f,g),\iota_L(\lambda\wedge\mu)).
\end{equation}
If its amplitude is nonzero then both inputs are nonzero, hence both
labels are top, so the stated element belongs to $G_L(\mathbb C)$.
Sesquilinearity follows from that of $B$, distributivity of meet over
join, and the lifted conjugation $(c,\alpha)^*=(\bar c,\alpha)$.
The same rule applies to every endpoint form and the retained terms
in (BPZ19). On a nonzero primitive test their zero amplitudes have label
top, namely $e$; the negative amplitude in (BPZ17) has that same label
and is still negative after the injective $\Phi_{\mathbb C}$ map.
Thus the complete boundary filter, the all-zero receiver and the
negative-test construction commute with the original full map.
Localizing at supported zero would erase these amplitudes; that
localization is not performed anywhere in this argument.
