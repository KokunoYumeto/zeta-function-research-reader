\subsection{The actual Hilbert-space representation and all four relations}

Use $\mathbb N=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}$ and its original orthonormal
basis $\xi_n$ in $\mathcal H=\ell^2(\mathbb N)$. For
$r\in\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$ and $m\ge1$, set
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR1}
 E(r)\xi_n=e^{2\pi i n r}\xi_n,\qquad
 S_m\xi_n=\xi_{mn},\qquad
 S_m^*\xi_n=
 \begin{cases}\xi_{n/m}&m\mid n,\\0&m\nmid n.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The phase is independent of the chosen representative of $r$.
$E(r)$ is unitary and $S_m$ is an isometry, since the indicated
basis vectors remain orthonormal. Taking inner products with
each basis vector proves the adjoint formula. Consequently
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR2}
\begin{gathered}
 E(r)E(t)=E(r+t),\quad E(0)=I,\quad E(r)^*=E(-r),\\
 S_m^*S_m=I,\qquad
 S_mS_n=S_{mn},\qquad S_m^*S_n^*=S_{mn}^*,\\
 S_mS_n^*=S_n^*S_m\quad(\gcd(m,n)=1).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The first line is multiplication of phases. The second follows
by multiplication or division of the basis index. For the last
line, its left side is nonzero exactly when $n\mid k$ on input
$\xi_k$, and then gives $\xi_{mk/n}$. Its right side is nonzero
exactly when $n\mid mk$. Coprimality makes these conditions
identical, with the same output. This proves the source relations
(c1), both parts of (c2), and (c3), without changing the basis.

Choose a rational representative of $r$. Its $m$ inverse images
under multiplication by $m$ are $(r+j)/m$, $0\le j<m$,
modulo $\mathbb Z$. The finite root sum is
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR3}
 \frac1m\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}e^{2\pi i n j/m}
       =\begin{cases}1&m\mid n,\\0&m\nmid n.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
If the ratio is one all $m$ terms are one; otherwise multiplying
the geometric sum by $1-e^{2\pi i n/m}$ gives zero and that
factor is nonzero. Therefore both sides of the exact source
relation (c4) act on $\xi_n$ by the same scalar:
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR4}
 S_mE(r)S_m^*
       =\frac1m\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}E((r+j)/m),\qquad
 (S_mE(r)S_m^*)\xi_n=
 \begin{cases}e^{2\pi i(n/m)r}\xi_n&m\mid n,\\0&m\nmid n.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Bounded operators agreeing on the complete basis agree on all
of $\mathcal H$. In particular $P_m=S_mS_m^*$ projects onto
the actual multiples of $m$. The covariance formulas are also
direct basis identities:
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR5}
 E(r)S_m=S_mE(mr),\quad
 S_m^*E(r)=E(mr)S_m^*,\quad S_m^*E(r)S_m=E(mr).
\end{equation}

For the original trace weight let $N\xi_n=n\xi_n$, with
domain $\{x:\sum n^2|x_n|^2<\infty\}$, and let
$H=\log N$. The diagonal unitary group and its exact covariance
are
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR6}
 N^{iu}S_mN^{-iu}=m^{iu}S_m,\qquad
 N^{iu}E(r)N^{-iu}=E(r)\quad(u\in\mathbb R).
\end{equation}
Indeed the first phase on $\xi_n$ is $(mn)^{iu}n^{-iu}=m^{iu}$;
the second identity is commutation of diagonal operators.
These are the original time-evolution factors in
\texttt{funBC.tex}, lines 865--869. No parameter of the trace
weight is rescaled below.

\subsection{Locally finite matrices with every original lattice label}

Let $L$ be any nontrivial bounded distributive lattice and retain
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR7}
\begin{gathered}
 S=G_L(\mathbb C)=\{(a,\lambda):a\in\mathbb C,\lambda\in L,
                               \ a\ne0\Rightarrow\lambda=1_L\},\\
 (a,\lambda)+(b,\mu)=(a+b,\lambda\vee\mu),\quad
 (a,\lambda)(b,\mu)=(ab,\lambda\wedge\mu),\\
 \tau=(0,0_L),\quad e=(0,1_L),\quad 1_S=(1,1_L),\quad
 \widehat c=(c,1_L),\qquad(a,\lambda)^*=(\overline a,\lambda).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Let $\mathscr M_L$ consist of matrices indexed by $\mathbb N^2$
whose rows and columns contain only finitely many entries different
from $\tau$. Finiteness is measured using the actual split zero,
not just the complex amplitude. Matrix addition, multiplication
and adjoint make this a unital involutive semiring. For each
product entry its sum is finite. A product row is contained in
the union of finitely many rows of the second factor, and a
product column in the union of finitely many columns of the
first; this proves closure. Triple products have finite sets
of contributing paths, so associativity follows from finite
distributivity. The identity has $1_S$ on the diagonal and
$\tau$ elsewhere.

The lift of the specified generators is
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR8}
 \widehat E(r)_{ij}=
   \begin{cases}\widehat{e^{2\pi i j r}}&i=j,\\\tau&i\ne j,
   \end{cases}
 \qquad
 \widehat S_m{}_{ij}=
   \begin{cases}1_S&i=mj,\\\tau&i\ne mj.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Both are locally finite. Their adjoints use (HCR7) entrywise
and transpose. All the identities (HCR2) and (HCR5) lift exactly:
each nonzero entry calculation has a unique contributing path,
with the same phase and label, and all missing paths give
$\tau$. This includes both adjoint relations in (c2).
One is not applying an additive map that changes every complex
zero to $\tau$: that proposed map fails on $1+(-1)$, since
$\widehat1+\widehat{-1}=e\ne\tau$. Formula (HCR8) specifies
the lifts and all subsequent sums are calculated in $S$.

The full coefficient injection is retained entry by entry.
Let $K$ be the original tropical semifield with zero,
$K_L=K\otimes_{\mathbb B}L$, and
$\iota_L(\lambda)=1_K\otimes\lambda$.
The map $\beta:K\to\mathbb B$ taking a nonzero element
to one and zero to zero is a semiring homomorphism:
a tropical sum is zero exactly when both terms are
zero, and a product is zero exactly when a factor is.
Consequently $\rho_L=\beta\otimes\operatorname{id}_L$
is the stated retraction, after $\mathbb B\otimes_{\mathbb B}L=L$.
The exact matrix map is
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR9}
\begin{gathered}
 \Phi_S(a,\lambda)=(a,\iota_L(\lambda))\in\mathbb C\times K_L,
       \qquad\rho_L\iota_L=\operatorname{id}_L,\\
 \Phi_{\mathscr M}:\mathscr M_L\hookrightarrow
     \mathscr M(\mathbb C)\times\mathscr M(K_L),\qquad
 ((a_{ij},\lambda_{ij}))\longmapsto
                    ((a_{ij}),(\iota_L\lambda_{ij})).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Here all matrix algebras use finite rows and columns, and the
$K_L$ involution is the identity on coefficients followed by
transpose. Finite sums and products are preserved because
$\iota_L$ preserves the lattice semiring operations. The
retraction recovers each label, proving injectivity. Its image
consists exactly of pairs whose every support entry belongs to
$\iota_L(L)$ and whose nonzero amplitude entries have support
$1_{K_L}$, with the original finite row and column condition.
No infinite lattice join is required.

\subsection{The exact missing support in relation (c4)}

Define the two actual split matrices and the defect by
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR10}
\begin{gathered}
 A_m(r)=\widehat S_m\widehat E(r)\widehat S_m^*,\qquad
 R_m(r)=\widehat{1/m}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\widehat E((r+j)/m),\\
 (M_m)_{nn}=\begin{cases}\tau&m\mid n,\\e&m\nmid n,\end{cases}
       \qquad (M_m)_{ij}=\tau\quad(i\ne j).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The scalar multiplication in the average multiplies every
entry by $\widehat{1/m}$; it preserves all displayed labels.
The original root sum (HCR3) now gives
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR11}
\begin{gathered}
 A_m(r)_{nn}=
 \begin{cases}(e^{2\pi i(n/m)r},1_L)&m\mid n,\\\tau&m\nmid n,
 \end{cases}\quad
 R_m(r)_{nn}=
 \begin{cases}(e^{2\pi i(n/m)r},1_L)&m\mid n,\\e&m\nmid n,
 \end{cases}\\
 \boxed{A_m(r)+M_m=R_m(r).}
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Every off-diagonal entry is $\tau$. On the diagonal each
Fourier summand has top label. Their finite sum therefore
has top label even where the complex sum vanishes. This
proves the entire equation rather than only its amplitude.

The correction is minimal and its entire set of alternatives
is explicit. If $A_m(r)+D=R_m(r)$, amplitude equality forces
every amplitude of $D$ to be zero. Off-diagonal entries must
be $\tau$. At a nonmultiple of $m$, its label must be top;
at a multiple it may be any original label. Thus
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR12}
 D=M_m+\operatorname{diag}(d_n),\qquad
 d_n=\begin{cases}(0,\lambda_n),\ \lambda_n\in L&m\mid n,\\
                    \tau&m\nmid n.
       \end{cases}
\end{equation}
In the entrywise order on zero-amplitude labels, $M_m$ is
the unique least such correction. Also $M_m^2=M_m$,
$M_mA_m(r)=A_m(r)M_m=0_{\mathscr M}$,
$M_m\widehat S_m=0_{\mathscr M}$ and
$\widehat S_m^*M_m=0_{\mathscr M}$, by their disjoint
allowed indices. Hence the compression of the repaired
relation recovers $\widehat E(r)$ exactly.

Let $P_m^K$ be the diagonal $K_L$ matrix with entry $1_{K_L}$
at multiples of $m$ and zero elsewhere, and $C_m^K$ its
complementary diagonal indicator. The complete map is
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR13}
\begin{gathered}
 \Phi_{\mathscr M}(A_m(r))=(S_mE(r)S_m^*,P_m^K),\quad
 \Phi_{\mathscr M}(M_m)=(0,C_m^K),\\
 \Phi_{\mathscr M}(R_m(r))=(S_mE(r)S_m^*,I_K),\qquad
                         P_m^K+C_m^K=I_K.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Thus the full coefficient map itself exhibits the missing
support and its exact correction; equality in the complex
coordinate alone is not equality of the original matrices.

\subsection{Exact full-label corner and common-label receivers}

Let $\mathscr D_L\subset\mathscr M_L$ be the diagonal
semiring, identified with $S^{\mathbb N}$, and put
$\widehat P_m=\widehat S_m\widehat S_m^*$.
There are exact maps retaining all entries that they receive:
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR14}
\begin{gathered}
 \sigma_m(D)=\widehat S_m^*D\widehat S_m,
          \qquad(\sigma_mD)_{nn}=D_{mn,mn},\\
 \rho_m^{\mathrm{sp}}(D)=\widehat S_mD\widehat S_m^*,\qquad
 \sigma_m\rho_m^{\mathrm{sp}}=\operatorname{id},\qquad
 \rho_m^{\mathrm{sp}}\sigma_m(D)=\widehat P_mD.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The first map is a unital semiring homomorphism.
The second is a unital isomorphism onto the genuine
corner $\widehat P_m\mathscr D_L$ with its unit
$\widehat P_m$ and additive zero $0_{\mathscr M}$.
All assertions follow entrywise from selection or insertion
of the indices divisible by $m$; in particular every
original selected label is unchanged.

Its exact completed presentation is
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR15}
\begin{gathered}
 \mathscr C_m=\{D+M_m:D\in\widehat P_m\mathscr D_L\},\qquad
 C_m(D)=D+M_m,\\
 0_{\mathscr C_m}=M_m,\qquad
 1_{\mathscr C_m}=\widehat P_m+M_m=R_m(0),\qquad
 C_m^{-1}(X)=\widehat P_mX.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Addition and multiplication on this displayed set are inherited
from matrices, but its additive zero is the stated $M_m$.
For supported $D,F$, their products with $M_m$ vanish and
$M_m^2=M_m$, so
$(D+M_m)(F+M_m)=DF+M_m$ and
$(D+M_m)+(F+M_m)=D+F+M_m$.
These calculations prove every semiring law, the unit and
the mutually inverse maps. This completion preserves every
label at a multiple of $m$ and inserts exactly the forced
top zero at a nonmultiple. In particular
$C_m(\rho_m^{\mathrm{sp}}\widehat E(r))=R_m(r)$ is (c4)
in this exact completed presentation, with its zero stated.
The inverse is compression, not an unidentified projection.

For the common-label diagonal ring put
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR16}
 Z=\operatorname{diag}(e,e,\ldots),\qquad
 \mathscr D^{\mathrm{top}}=
    \{\operatorname{diag}((a_n,1_L)):a_n\in\mathbb C\},\qquad
 C(D)=D+Z.
\end{equation}
This is a ring under its inherited diagonal operations,
with additive zero $Z$, unit $I$ and additive inverse
$\operatorname{diag}((-a_n,1_L))$. It is canonically
isomorphic to the ring of complex sequences. The displayed
$C:\mathscr D_L\to\mathscr D^{\mathrm{top}}$ is onto and
unital; finite pointwise operations give
$C(D+F)=C(D)+C(F)$ and $C(DF)=C(D)C(F)$.
Its kernel congruence is exactly equality of every complex
amplitude; the complete fibers retain arbitrary labels
where those amplitudes vanish. In particular
$C(A_m(r))=R_m(r)$ and $C(M_m)=Z$.
This receiver proves exactly how the classical diagonal
relation is obtained and specifies the data that it forgets.

For the whole matrix algebra, the amplitude receiver has
the equally exact universal description
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR17}
 \mathscr M_L/\langle Z=0_{\mathscr M}\rangle
       \simeq\mathscr M(\mathbb C),\qquad
 ((a_{ij},\lambda_{ij}))\longmapsto(a_{ij}).
\end{equation}
The matrix $Z$ is central idempotent, and $ZA$ is the
zero-amplitude matrix with precisely the labels of $A$.
Thus imposing $Z=0$ kills every zero-amplitude matrix.
Every complex row-and-column-finite matrix has a preimage
by placing top labels at nonzero entries and $\tau$ at
zeros. Two preimages with the same amplitudes are congruent:
add the coefficientwise amplitude negative of the second,
with its same labels; both relevant sums have zero amplitude
and hence vanish in the quotient. Conversely the amplitude
map kills $Z$ and distinguishes unequal amplitudes.
This proves the quotient and its universal property.
It receives all four original Bost--Connes relations.

Inverting $Z$ is a different map. Its idempotent corner
$Z\mathscr M_L$ is the semiring of locally finite $L$-valued
support matrices. There (HCR13) becomes the two different
support indicators $P_m$ and $I$, with their nonzero
complementary correction for $m>1$. Thus supported-zero
inversion does not itself restore (c4) with the correction
omitted. Both exact connecting maps have now been calculated.

\subsection{Every residue projector and its complete supported product}

For $q\ge1$ and $a\in\{1,\ldots,q\}$, with $a=q$
representing residue zero, define
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR18}
 P_{q,a}=\frac1q\sum_{k=0}^{q-1}
            e^{-2\pi i ak/q}E(k/q),\qquad
 P_{q,a}\xi_n=\mathbf1_{n\equiv a\ (q)}\xi_n.
\end{equation}
The second formula is (HCR3) applied to $n-a$ and proves
the first is exactly the orthogonal residue projection.
Its split Fourier sum, sparse projection and missing-support
diagonal are
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR19}
\begin{gathered}
 Q_{q,a}=\widehat{1/q}\sum_{k=0}^{q-1}
       \widehat{e^{-2\pi i ak/q}}\,\widehat E(k/q),\\
 (\Pi_{q,a})_{nn}=
       \begin{cases}1_S&n\equiv a\ (q),\\\tau&n\not\equiv a\ (q),\end{cases}
 \quad
 (B_{q,a})_{nn}=
       \begin{cases}\tau&n\equiv a\ (q),\\e&n\not\equiv a\ (q),\end{cases}\\
 \boxed{Q_{q,a}=\Pi_{q,a}+B_{q,a}.}
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
All off-diagonal entries are $\tau$. The nonselected
diagonal Fourier sums have zero amplitude and top label,
so the last formula follows directly, not by replacing
those entries with $\tau$.

The complete projector laws in the ambient split algebra are
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR20}
\begin{gathered}
 Q_{q,a}^2=Q_{q,a},\qquad
 Q_{q,a}Q_{q,b}=Z\quad(a\ne b),\qquad
 \sum_{a=1}^qQ_{q,a}=I,\\
 \Pi_{q,a}^2=\Pi_{q,a},\qquad
 \Pi_{q,a}\Pi_{q,b}=0_{\mathscr M}\quad(a\ne b),\qquad
 \sum_{a=1}^q\Pi_{q,a}=I.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
For $a\ne b$, at each index at least one raw diagonal
amplitude is zero, but both labels are top, so their
product is $e$ at every index. At a given index exactly
one summand in the raw sum is $1_S$ and the others are
$e$; their sum is $1_S$. These calculations prove all
raw identities, including the case $q=1$ where no distinct
pair exists. The sparse identities are disjoint indicators.
The matrices $Q_{q,a}$ are orthogonal in the common-label
ring (HCR16), whose zero is $Z$; their ambient product
must still be recorded as the displayed $Z$, not $\tau$.
For $q\ge2$, making that one distinct product equal to the
ambient zero imposes $Z=0$, and (HCR17) gives the exact
resulting quotient.

The mixed original labels can be tested on every coefficient
vector $v_n=(c_n,\lambda_n)$ with $c\in\ell^2(\mathbb N)$
and $c_n\ne0\Rightarrow\lambda_n=1_L$. The indicated
operators preserve that carrier and have the unchanged
bounded Hilbert-space amplitudes. Their exact actions are
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR21}
\begin{aligned}
 (Q_{q,a}v)_n&=
 \begin{cases}(c_n,\lambda_n)&n\equiv a\ (q),\\(0,\lambda_n)&n\not\equiv a\ (q),\end{cases}\\
 (\Pi_{q,a}v)_n&=
 \begin{cases}(c_n,\lambda_n)&n\equiv a\ (q),\\\tau&n\not\equiv a\ (q).
 \end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Thus the missing-support term retains each original
$\lambda_n$, even when it is neither top nor bottom.
More generally any locally finite matrix product on a
finite vector has at output $i$ the exact label
$\bigvee_j(\lambda_{ij}\wedge\lambda_j)$ over its finite
contributing row. The complete map sends this to the
same finite sum of products in $K_L$ and recovers it
by $\rho_L$. This proves naturality of all mixed labels,
not just of the binary masks of the generators.
Any bounded lattice homomorphism $L\to L'$ preserves
the finite formulas and the carrier; applying it
entrywise commutes with all displayed constructions.

For completeness the coefficient images are
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR22}
 \Phi_{\mathscr M}(\Pi_{q,a})=(P_{q,a},P_{q,a}^K),\quad
 \Phi_{\mathscr M}(B_{q,a})=(0,(P_{q,a}^{\mathrm{comp}})^K),\quad
 \Phi_{\mathscr M}(Q_{q,a})=(P_{q,a},I_K).
\end{equation}
The two support masks are complementary, disjoint diagonal
indicators. This identifies their images without subtraction
inside $K_L$.

The residue receiver is compatible with the actual dilation:
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR23}
\begin{gathered}
 S_mP_{q,a}S_m^*=P_{mq,ma},\qquad
 \widehat S_m\Pi_{q,a}\widehat S_m^*=\Pi_{mq,ma},\\
 \widehat S_mQ_{q,a}\widehat S_m^*+M_m=Q_{mq,ma}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
At an output index divisible by $m$, all three equalities
test $n/m\equiv a\pmod q$. At other indices the sparse
expressions are zero and the final raw expression needs
exactly $M_m$. This proves the complete equations.
If $q\mid Q$, refining the residue classes gives
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR24}
 P_{q,a}=\sum_{\substack{1\le b\le Q\\b\equiv a\ (q)}}P_{Q,b},\quad
 \Pi_{q,a}=\sum_{b\equiv a\ (q)}\Pi_{Q,b},\quad
 Q_{q,a}=\sum_{b\equiv a\ (q)}Q_{Q,b}.
\end{equation}
The classes partition the selected progression. In the
raw equation there are $Q/q\ge1$ summands, all with
top diagonal label; their amplitude sum is the same
indicator and every label remains top. Thus refinement
also preserves the exact split sums.

Compression also has an exact non-coprime formula. Put
$d=\gcd(m,q)$ and $q'=q/d$. If $d\nmid a$, the congruence
$mn\equiv a\pmod q$ has no solution. If $d\mid a$, let
$b\in\{1,\ldots,q'\}$ be the unique representative with
$(m/d)b\equiv a/d\pmod{q'}$; it exists because $m/d$ is
invertible modulo $q'$, including $b=1$ when $q'=1$.
Then
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR25}
\begin{aligned}
 S_m^*P_{q,a}S_m
    &=\begin{cases}0&d\nmid a,\\P_{q',b}&d\mid a,\end{cases}\\
 \widehat S_m^*\Pi_{q,a}\widehat S_m
    &=\begin{cases}0_{\mathscr M}&d\nmid a,\\\Pi_{q',b}&d\mid a,\end{cases}\\
 \widehat S_m^*Q_{q,a}\widehat S_m
    &=\begin{cases}Z&d\nmid a,\\Q_{q',b}&d\mid a.\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The diagonal amplitude test is the displayed congruence;
compression of a raw matrix retains top label at every
new diagonal index, whereas compression of a sparse
matrix retains a label only at a selected index. This
proves the three different zero cases exactly.

\subsection{The trace of the original weight is exactly the Hurwitz series}

For $s\in\mathbb C$ with $\sigma=\operatorname{Re}s>1$,
use $x^{-s}=\exp(-s\log x)$ for positive real $x$, with
its real logarithm. Then
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR26}
 W_s=N^{-s}=e^{-sH},\quad W_s\xi_n=n^{-s}\xi_n,
 \qquad \|W_s\|_1=\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-\sigma}<\infty.
\end{equation}
The absolute values of the diagonal eigenvalues are
the singular values, proving the trace norm formula.
For any bounded diagonal $D\xi_n=d_n\xi_n$, finite
basis truncation and the tail bound
$\|D\|\sum_{n>K}n^{-\sigma}\to0$ prove
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR27}
 \operatorname{Tr}(DW_s)=\sum_{n\ge1}d_nn^{-s}.
\end{equation}
Every series here is absolutely convergent. The selected
indices for $P_{q,a}$ are precisely $a+qj$, $j\ge0$,
including $q,2q,\ldots$ when $a=q$. Thus the actual
Hurwitz receiver is
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR28}
\boxed{\begin{gathered}
 \zeta(s,\alpha)=\sum_{j=0}^\infty(j+\alpha)^{-s}\quad(\alpha>0),\\
 \operatorname{Tr}(P_{q,a}N^{-s})
  =\sum_{j=0}^\infty(a+qj)^{-s}
  =q^{-s}\zeta(s,a/q),\qquad 1\le a\le q,\quad\sigma>1.
\end{gathered}}
\end{equation}
Absolute convergence of the Hurwitz series follows from
the finite initial term and the bound $(j+\alpha)^{-\sigma}
\le j^{-\sigma}$ for $j\ge1$. Additivity of real logarithms
of positive factors proves the factor $q^{-s}$ exactly.
All these series are locally uniformly convergent in this
half-plane by a common majorant $\sum n^{-1-\varepsilon}$
on each compact subset; hence the stated receiver is
holomorphic there.

The complete split trace on these particular matrices
can also be recorded without an unproved infinite join.
Let $\widehat W_s=\operatorname{diag}(\widehat{n^{-s}})$
and let $\operatorname{tr}_K$ mean the sum of the first
$K$ diagonal entries. Then
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR29}
\begin{gathered}
 T_K=\sum_{\substack{1\le n\le K\\n\equiv a\ (q)}}n^{-s},\\
 \operatorname{tr}_K(Q_{q,a}\widehat W_s)=(T_K,1_L)
                                      \quad(K\ge1),\\
 \operatorname{tr}_K(\Pi_{q,a}\widehat W_s)=
     \begin{cases}\tau&K<a,\\(T_K,1_L)&K\ge a.
     \end{cases}
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The first equality retains top labels even on unselected
indices. In the second, the first selected index is $a$.
After that index, the support of the finite trace is
constant top and the amplitude converges absolutely.
Therefore the amplitude limit with that retained label
is $(q^{-s}\zeta(s,a/q),1_L)$ for both sequences.
Under the full map it is
$(q^{-s}\zeta(s,a/q),1_{K_L})$.
For $q>1$ the analogous weighted trace of $B_{q,a}$
has eventual value $e$, although the two preceding
limits agree because addition of $e$ leaves a top-labelled
element fixed. Thus this scalar trace equality does not
identify their different operator support matrices.
No infinite trace on arbitrary labelled matrices, or
join not present in $L$, has been assumed.

The original additive-character trace and its exact
finite transforms are
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR30}
\begin{gathered}
 F_{q,k}(s)=\operatorname{Tr}(E(k/q)N^{-s})
           =\sum_{n\ge1}e^{2\pi i kn/q}n^{-s},\\
 q^{-s}\zeta(s,a/q)
       =\frac1q\sum_{k=0}^{q-1}e^{-2\pi i ak/q}F_{q,k}(s),\\
 F_{q,k}(s)=q^{-s}\sum_{a=1}^q
                 e^{2\pi i ka/q}\zeta(s,a/q).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The first transform is the trace of (HCR18). For the
inverse, $E(k/q)=\sum_a e^{2\pi i ka/q}P_{q,a}$ on
each basis vector, and (HCR27) gives its trace. Finite
character sums and absolute convergence justify every
interchange. In particular the finite cyclic group
algebra acts faithfully: if a combination of $E(k/q)$
vanishes, its values at $n=1,\ldots,q$ and (HCR3)
recover each coefficient as zero.

Dilation retains the Hurwitz shift and changes the
trace factor by exactly its original degree:
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR31}
\begin{gathered}
 N^{-s}S_m=m^{-s}S_mN^{-s},\qquad
 S_m^*N^{-s}S_m=m^{-s}N^{-s},\\
 \operatorname{Tr}(P_{mq,ma}N^{-s})
   =m^{-s}\operatorname{Tr}(P_{q,a}N^{-s})
   =(mq)^{-s}\zeta(s,a/q).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The operator identities follow on $\xi_n$ from
$(mn)^{-s}=m^{-s}n^{-s}$, and the trace identity also
follows directly by the selected series. Refinement
(HCR24), with $Q=hq$, similarly gives
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR32}
 q^{-s}\zeta(s,a/q)
    =Q^{-s}\sum_{j=0}^{h-1}\zeta(s,(a+jq)/Q).
\end{equation}
The representatives $a+jq$ lie in $1,\ldots,Q$ and
partition the original residue class, preserving all
offsets and both original factors.

\subsection{Finite multiplicative characters and every constant in their trace}

We prove the character identity rather than assuming its
factor. For a finite abelian group $G$, every character
of a subgroup $B$ extends to $G$. To prove it in one
step, choose $g\notin B$ and let $m$ be the least
positive integer with $g^m\in B$. Each element of
$\langle B,g\rangle$ is uniquely $bg^j$ with $0\le j<m$.
For a character $\chi$ of $B$, choose one of the $m$
unit complex roots $z$ of $z^m=\chi(g^m)$ and set
$\chi'(bg^j)=\chi(b)z^j$. Unique representatives make
it well defined, and carrying an exponent through $m$
uses exactly the identity $z^m=\chi(g^m)$, proving
multiplicativity. These are exactly its $m$ extensions.
Starting from the trivial subgroup shows both that
$|\widehat G|=|G|$ and that subgroup characters extend.
For a nonidentity element of order $d$, its cyclic
character with value $e^{2\pi i/d}$ therefore extends
and separates it from the identity. Multiplying all
characters by such a separating character permutes
the finite character set and forces its sum at that
element to vanish. At the identity all summands are
one. Consequently
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR33}
 \frac1{|G|}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat G}
            \overline{\chi(a)}\chi(b)=\mathbf1_{a=b}
                         \qquad(a,b\in G).
\end{equation}
The conjugate is exactly $\chi(a)^{-1}$, so the tested
element is $ba^{-1}$. This fixes both the conjugation
and the numerical factor.

Take $G=(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times$, of cardinality
$\varphi(q)$. Extend each character to all integers
by zero on nonunits. The extension is completely
multiplicative, since a product is a unit modulo $q$
exactly when both factors are units. When $q=1$,
the unique character is identically one and
$\varphi(1)=1$. For $\gcd(a,q)=1$, (HCR33) gives
the following equality for every $n\ge1$, including
nonunit residues where both sides vanish:
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR34}
 \mathbf1_{n\equiv a\ (q)}
    =\frac1{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi\bmod q}
                                \overline{\chi(a)}\chi(n).
\end{equation}
Define the bounded diagonal operators and their
absolutely convergent original series by
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR35}
\begin{gathered}
 D_\chi\xi_n=\chi(n)\xi_n,
 \qquad D_\chi=\sum_{b=1}^q\chi(b)P_{q,b},\\
 L(s,\chi)=\sum_{n\ge1}\chi(n)n^{-s}
                       =\operatorname{Tr}(D_\chi N^{-s}),\quad\sigma>1,\\
 P_{q,a}=\frac1{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi\bmod q}
                            \overline{\chi(a)}D_\chi
                         \quad(\gcd(a,q)=1).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The norm is at most one and the series is majorized by
$\sum n^{-\sigma}$. The last line is (HCR34) on each
basis vector. Taking its trace and using the original
factor from (HCR28) proves
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR36}
 \boxed{\zeta(s,a/q)=\frac{q^s}{\varphi(q)}
       \sum_{\chi\bmod q}\overline{\chi(a)}L(s,\chi),
        \qquad\gcd(a,q)=1,\quad\operatorname{Re}s>1.}
\end{equation}
The factor $q^s$ converts the residue trace to the
Hurwitz function; it is not part of the residue
projection or of its original trace weight.

This finite operator decomposition has an exact
common-label split receiver. Set
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR37}
 \widetilde D_\chi=\operatorname{diag}((\chi(n),1_L))_{n\ge1}
                       \in\mathscr D^{\mathrm{top}}.
\end{equation}
The entries on nonunit residues are $e$, not $\tau$.
The corresponding identities, including all those
zeros, are
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR38}
 \widetilde D_\chi=\sum_{b=1}^q\widehat{\chi(b)}\,Q_{q,b},
 \qquad
 Q_{q,a}=\widehat{1/\varphi(q)}
       \sum_{\chi\bmod q}\widehat{\overline{\chi(a)}}
                          \,\widetilde D_\chi
                      \quad(\gcd(a,q)=1).
\end{equation}
Their amplitudes are (HCR35); every diagonal label
on each side is top, including when $\chi(b)=0$,
because $\widehat0=e$. This proves equality of
the full matrices. Substituting (HCR18) into the
ordinary identity for $D_\chi$ also gives the exact
additive-to-multiplicative Fourier coefficients
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR39}
 D_\chi=\frac1q\sum_{k=0}^{q-1}
       \left(\sum_{b=1}^q\chi(b)e^{-2\pi i bk/q}\right)E(k/q).
\end{equation}
Only two finite sums have been interchanged. The
inner sum is retained as written, so this formula
also covers imprimitive characters without inserting
an unwarranted primitive-character factor.

\subsection{Euler products and the exact modulus-two specialization}

Absolute convergence and unique prime factorization
give the Euler product with all original local factors:
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR40}
 L(s,\chi)=\prod_{p\ \mathrm{prime}}
                  (1-\chi(p)p^{-s})^{-1},\qquad\sigma>1.
\end{equation}
For a finite set of primes, expand each factor in its
absolutely convergent geometric series. Complete
multiplicativity and unique factorization identify
the product with the sum over integers supported on
those primes. As the finite set exhausts all primes,
each integer appears, and the majorant
$\sum n^{-\sigma}<\infty$ makes the omitted sum tend
to zero. This proves the product and its limit. The
same proof with the constant-one character gives
$\zeta(s)=\prod_p(1-p^{-s})^{-1}$.

For $q=2$, the sole character is one on odd integers
and zero on even integers. Its factor at the prime
2 is exactly one, while every other factor is the
corresponding zeta factor. Thus
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR41}
\boxed{\begin{gathered}
 L(s,\chi_0)=\prod_{p\ne2}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}
                           =(1-2^{-s})\zeta(s),\\
 \operatorname{Tr}(P_{2,1}N^{-s})=(1-2^{-s})\zeta(s),\qquad
 \zeta(s,\tfrac12)=(2^s-1)\zeta(s),\qquad\sigma>1.
\end{gathered}}
\end{equation}
The same constants follow by separating the even
indices $2j$ from all positive indices, justified
by absolute convergence. The sign can also be
checked directly from
\begin{equation}\tag{HCR42}
 P_{2,1}=\tfrac12(I-E(1/2)),\quad
 P_{2,2}=\tfrac12(I+E(1/2)),\quad
 \operatorname{Tr}(E(1/2)N^{-s})=(2^{1-s}-1)\zeta(s).
\end{equation}
At index $n$, the phase is the original $(-1)^n$,
so these are the odd, even and signed traces with
their actual orientations. Equations (HCR28),
(HCR30), (HCR36) and (HCR41) are proved in their
common absolutely convergent domain. They identify
the Hurwitz shifts with explicit trace receivers
of the represented endomotive operators; they
assert no additional zero-location result.
