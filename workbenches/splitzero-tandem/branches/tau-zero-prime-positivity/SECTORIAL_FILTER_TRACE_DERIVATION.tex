\subsection{1. The original functions and the exact differential
map}\label{the-original-functions-and-the-exact-differential-map}

Put \[
 Z=-2i(s-\tfrac12),\qquad r=s-\tfrac12,\qquad
 \mu(s)=s(s-1),\qquad
 A(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2),\qquad C(s)=\frac{\mu(s)A(s)}2.
 \tag{SFT1}
\] The expressions \(I_t(s)\) and \(H_t(s)\) below mean the actual
source functions evaluated at the displayed \(Z\); no time change is
made. The original-zeta receivers are \[
 f_t(s)=\frac{I_t(s)}{A(s)},\qquad
 z_t(s)=\frac{16H_t(s)}{\mu(s)A(s)},\qquad
 W_t(s)=\mu(s)A(s)z_t(s)=16H_t(s).
 \tag{SFT2}
\] ESH15 states, and the computation below verifies, that \[
 \mathcal B_t=\mu+\frac t2+tr\,\partial_s+
                  \frac{t^2}4\partial_s^2
            =\left(r+\frac t2\partial_s\right)^2-\frac14,
 \qquad
 \mathcal B_t I_t=W_t.
 \tag{SFT3}
\] Indeed \(Z-2t\partial_Z=-i(2r+t\partial_s)\). Squaring the operator
differentiates \(r\), so its expansion contributes \(t/2\) in SFT3. Thus
\(16\mathcal N_t=\mathcal B_t\), with the factor sixteen and the
original endpoint polynomial both retained.

Define \[
 q_A=\frac{A'}A=-\frac12\log\pi+\frac12\psi_\Gamma(s/2),
 \quad q_C=\frac{C'}C=q_A+\frac{2s-1}{s(s-1)},
 \quad \nabla_A=\partial_s+q_A,\quad
 \nabla_C=\partial_s+q_C.
 \tag{SFT4}
\] The exact map on the original functions is \[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \mathcal P_t f=
 \frac1{\mu A}\mathcal B_t(Af)
 =\frac1\mu\left[
    \left(\mu+\frac t2\right)f+
    tr(f'+q_Af)+
    \frac{t^2}4\{f''+2q_Af'+(q_A'+q_A^2)f\}
             \right].}
 \tag{SFT5}
\] Here \(q_A'=\psi_\Gamma'(s/2)/4\). The equation
\(\mathcal P_t f_t=z_t\) is an equality of meromorphic functions,
including the endpoint and Gamma germs. The multiplier \(C\) is used
only to name its full logarithmic derivative; it has not replaced
\(f_t\) or \(z_t\).

\subsection{2. The original heat equations and their time-dependent
intertwiner}\label{the-original-heat-equations-and-their-time-dependent-intertwiner}

Since \(-\partial_Z^2=\partial_s^2/4\), the source heat equations give
\[
 \partial_t f_t=\frac14\nabla_A^2 f_t,\qquad
 \partial_t z_t=\frac14\nabla_C^2 z_t.
 \tag{SFT6}
\] For the second equation, multiply through by \(\mu A\), which is
independent of \(t\); for the first, multiply through by \(A\). Each
equality is valid as a meromorphic equality at exceptional points. This
proves the equations for the original receivers without dropping their
connection terms.

The elementary commutators are \[
 [\partial_s^2,\mu]=2+4r\partial_s,\qquad
 [\partial_s^2,tr\partial_s]=2t\partial_s^2.
\] Consequently \[
 \partial_t\mathcal B_t
   =\frac12+r\partial_s+\frac t2\partial_s^2
   =\frac14[\partial_s^2,\mathcal B_t].
 \tag{SFT7}
\] Applying the product rule to an arbitrary time-dependent function
proves the operator identity \[
 \left(\partial_t-\frac14\partial_s^2\right)\mathcal B_t
   =\mathcal B_t
       \left(\partial_t-\frac14\partial_s^2\right).
\] Conjugating the two sides with the displayed full factors yields \[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \left(\partial_t-\frac14\nabla_C^2\right)\mathcal P_t
  =\mathcal P_t
       \left(\partial_t-\frac14\nabla_A^2\right).}
 \tag{SFT8}
\] In SFT8 the time derivative on the left differentiates the
coefficients of \(\mathcal P_t\) as well as its argument. SFT7 proves
this convention and every time-dependent contribution explicitly.

\subsection{3. Fixed-time domains, endpoint germs and the augmented
inverse}\label{fixed-time-domains-endpoint-germs-and-the-augmented-inverse}

Let \(\mathcal E=\mathcal O(\mathbb C)\). The embedded spaces used at
each nonzero time are \[
 \mathcal H_A=A^{-1}\mathcal E\subset\mathcal M(\mathbb C),
 \qquad
 \mathcal H_C=(\mu A)^{-1}\mathcal E
                  \subset\mathcal M(\mathbb C).
 \tag{SFT9}
\] The first consists of entire \(f\) for which \(Af\) is entire;
equivalently, \(f\) has a zero of order at least one at each
\(0,-2,-4,\ldots\). The second consists of meromorphic \(z\) for which
\(\mu Az\) is entire. Its members are holomorphic away from \(s=1\), can
have at most a simple pole there, and have a zero of order at least one
at each \(-2,-4,\ldots\). At \(s=0\) they are holomorphic without a
required zero.

To verify these assertions and preserve the exceptional units, write
\(h=s-s_0\). At the Gamma poles, \[
 A(-2m+h)=h^{-1}a_m(h),\qquad
 a_m(0)=\frac{2(-1)^m\pi^m}{m!}\ne0,\qquad m=0,1,\ldots.
 \tag{SFT10}
\] At zero and one the full endpoint products are \[
 \begin{aligned}
  A(h)&=h^{-1}a_0(h), &a_0(0)&=2,&
  \mu(h)A(h)&=(h-1)a_0(h),\\
  A(1+h)&=a_1^{\mathrm{end}}(h),&
  a_1^{\mathrm{end}}(0)&=1,&
  \mu(1+h)A(1+h)&=h(1+h)a_1^{\mathrm{end}}(h).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{SFT11}
\] For \(m\ge1\), \(\mu(-2m)=2m(2m+1)\ne0\). Thus the units in SFT10--11
give every asserted local order. They also prove that SFT5 is a
well-defined complex-linear map
\(\mathcal H_A\longrightarrow\mathcal H_C\). We have not inferred a ring
or spectrum map from its linearity.

Fix \(t\ne0\), an arbitrary target \(z\in\mathcal H_C\), and arbitrary
\(u_0,u_1\in\mathbb C\). Set \(W=\mu Az\in\mathcal E\). There is a
unique entire \(I\) satisfying \(\mathcal B_tI=W\), \(I(1/2)=u_0\),
\(I'(1/2)=u_1\), namely \[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 I(\tfrac12+r)=e^{-r^2/t}\left[
 u_0\cosh(r/t)+t u_1\sinh(r/t)
 +\frac4t\int_0^r
     \sinh((r-v)/t)e^{v^2/t}W(\tfrac12+v)\,dv
                         \right].}
 \tag{SFT12}
\] The integral may be taken along any path from zero to \(r\). For
fixed \(r\) its integrand is entire in \(v\); expanding the hyperbolic
sine into its two exponentials writes the integral as a sum of entire
primitives multiplied by entire functions of \(r\). This proves both
path independence and entire dependence on \(r\).

For a direct verification, put \(I=e^{-r^2/t}J\). Applying
\(r+(t/2)\partial_s\) once gives \(e^{-r^2/t}(t/2)J'\), and applying it
twice gives \(e^{-r^2/t}(t^2/4)J''\). Thus the equation is exactly \[
 J''-t^{-2}J=\frac4{t^2}e^{r^2/t}W(\tfrac12+r).
 \tag{SFT13}
\] The homogeneous solution with data \(u_0,u_1\) is the first two terms
in SFT12. Differentiating the integral twice gives its forcing term
\(4e^{r^2/t}W/t^2\); at zero the integral and its first derivative both
vanish. This proves SFT12. If two solutions share their data, their
difference solves the homogeneous equation in SFT13 with both data zero;
its power series recursion forces every coefficient to vanish. This
proves uniqueness.

Returning to the original function is the exact operation \(f=I/A\). Its
initial data are \[
 u_0=A(\tfrac12)f(\tfrac12),\qquad
 u_1=A(\tfrac12)
       [f'(\tfrac12)+q_A(\tfrac12)f(\tfrac12)].
 \tag{SFT14}
\] It follows that the augmented map, with inverse SFT12 and \(f=I/A\),
is a complex-linear bijection \[
 \mathcal H_A\ \longrightarrow\
          \mathcal H_C\oplus\mathbb C^2,\qquad
 f\longmapsto(\mathcal P_tf,u_0,u_1).
 \tag{SFT15}
\] The unaugmented kernel is exactly \[
 \ker\mathcal P_t=
 \left\{A(s)^{-1}e^{-r^2/t}
       [a\cosh(r/t)+b\sinh(r/t)]:a,b\in\mathbb C\right\}.
 \tag{SFT16}
\] The formula specifies the full original-function kernel, including
\(A^{-1}\).

The actual cosine source is even in \(Z\), hence satisfies
\(I(s)=I(1-s)\). It is not necessary, and is generally false, that
\(f(s)=f(1-s)\). Define the exact even domains by \[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal H_A^{\mathrm{ev}}
   &=\{f\in\mathcal H_A:(Af)(s)=(Af)(1-s)\},\\
 \mathcal H_C^{\mathrm{ev}}
   &=\{z\in\mathcal H_C:(\mu Az)(s)=(\mu Az)(1-s)\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{SFT17}
\] The operator \(\mathcal B_t\) preserves evenness in \(r\). For even
\(W\), the solution SFT12 is even precisely when \(u_1=0\): reflection
gives another solution with data \(u_0,-u_1\), and uniqueness proves
both implications. Thus the actual even augmented map is the bijection
\[
 \mathcal H_A^{\mathrm{ev}}
       \longrightarrow \mathcal H_C^{\mathrm{ev}}\oplus\mathbb C,
       \qquad f\longmapsto(\mathcal P_tf,u_0).
 \tag{SFT18}
\]

At \(t=0\), SFT5 is the identity on meromorphic functions. This does not
extend SFT15 to \(t=0\). On the fixed domains of SFT9 its image is the
proper subspace \(\mathcal H_A\subset\mathcal H_C\), and the equation
for \(I\) becomes \(\mu I=W\). In addition, the actual initial function
\(\zeta\) is not in \(\mathcal H_A\): \(A\zeta\) has poles at zero and
one. It does belong to \(\mathcal H_C\), since \(\mu A\zeta=16H_0\) is
entire. The sectorial limit to the original \(\zeta\) in \(0<\Re s<1\)
therefore has a genuine exceptional-domain change at zero time. Neither
SFT12 nor local uniform convergence of the entire \(f_t\) at the pole
\(s=1\) is claimed at that time.

\subsection{4. The inverse together with the original heat
equation}\label{the-inverse-together-with-the-original-heat-equation}

Let \(D_t\subset\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}\) be a simply connected time
domain. Suppose \(W(t,s)\) is jointly holomorphic on
\(D_t\times\mathbb C\) and obeys \(\partial_tW=\partial_s^2W/4\). Use
SFT12 with holomorphic initial functions \(u_0(t),u_1(t)\). Then \(I\)
obeys the same heat equation if and only if \[
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
  t^2u_0'(t)+(\tfrac t2-\tfrac14)u_0(t)
      &=W(t,\tfrac12),\\
  t^2u_1'(t)+(\tfrac{3t}2-\tfrac14)u_1(t)
      &=\partial_sW(t,\tfrac12).
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{SFT19}
\] For necessity, evaluate \(\mathcal B_tI=W\) at \(r=0\) and use
\(I''=4I_t\); differentiating that equation once in \(s\) and evaluating
at \(r=0\) gives the second line. In that differentiated equation the
coefficient of \(I'\) is \(3t/2-1/4\), since differentiating \(trI'\)
contributes the additional \(tI'\).

For sufficiency, let \(R=I_t-I''/4\). SFT7 and the heat equation for
\(W\) give \(\mathcal B_tR=0\). The two equations SFT19 and the same
evaluated identities give \(R(t,1/2)=0\) and \(\partial_sR(t,1/2)=0\).
Uniqueness in SFT13 implies \(R=0\) for each \(t\). This establishes
sufficiency without assuming an unproved uniqueness statement for the
sectorial heat equation at \(t=0\).

Choose one holomorphic branch of \(t^{1/2}\) on \(D_t\), a base point
\(t_*\), and write \[
 g_0(t)=t^{1/2}e^{1/(4t)},\qquad
 g_1(t)=t^{3/2}e^{1/(4t)},\qquad
 W_0(t)=W(t,\tfrac12),\quad
 W_1(t)=\partial_sW(t,\tfrac12).
\] The complete solutions of SFT19 are \[
 u_j(t)=g_j(t)^{-1}
     \left[\kappa_j+
       \int_{t_*}^t\frac{g_j(v)W_j(v)}{v^2}\,dv\right],
 \qquad j=0,1.
 \tag{SFT20}
\] The integrands are holomorphic on the simply connected domain, so the
displayed primitives are well-defined. Their logarithmic derivatives
give \(g_0'/g_0=1/(2t)-1/(4t^2)\) and \(g_1'/g_1=3/(2t)-1/(4t^2)\),
proving the formula exactly.

The difference between any two heat-compatible inverses of the same
\(W\) is therefore \[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Delta I(t,s)
 &=t^{-1/2}e^{-(r^2+1/4)/t}
       [\kappa_0\cosh(r/t)+\kappa_1\sinh(r/t)]\\
 &=t^{-1/2}
     \left[\frac{\kappa_0-\kappa_1}{2}e^{-s^2/t}
           +\frac{\kappa_0+\kappa_1}{2}e^{-(s-1)^2/t}\right].
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{SFT21}
\] If the original even domain is imposed, \(\kappa_1=0\). Thus the free
data are two constants on the unrestricted heat-compatible domain and
one constant on the actual even domain. They are not two arbitrary
functions of time.

For ESH's two lateral continuations the jump is \[
 I_+-I_-=2i\sqrt{\pi/t}
              [e^{-s^2/t}+e^{-(s-1)^2/t}].
 \tag{SFT22}
\] On positive real \(t\) with its positive square root this is SFT21
with \(\kappa_0=4i\sqrt\pi\), \(\kappa_1=0\). Division by the full
\(A(s)\) gives the original-function jump. These constants describe the
difference of the specified continuations; arbitrary choices of the
constants are not asserted to satisfy those continuations' original
integral/asymptotic conditions.

\subsection{5. The multiplicative, logarithmic and divisor
maps}\label{the-multiplicative-logarithmic-and-divisor-maps}

Fix \(t\ne0\) and \(f\in\mathcal H_A\) such that neither \(f\) nor
\(z=\mathcal P_tf\) is identically zero. Put \(I=Af\),
\(W=\mathcal B_tI=\mu Az\). The meromorphic functions \[
 L=\frac{I'}I=\frac{f'}f+q_A,\qquad
 G_t(f)=\frac{\mathcal B_tI}{I}
       =\mu+\frac t2+trL+\frac{t^2}4(L'+L^2),
 \qquad
 \Lambda_t(f)=\frac{G_t(f)}{\mu}
 \tag{SFT23}
\] retain every derivative and endpoint factor. They satisfy the exact
identity \[
 \boxed{z=\Lambda_t(f)\,f.}
 \tag{SFT24}
\] The quotient is defined meromorphically through the isolated zeros
and poles. It depends on \(f\); it is not a fixed multiplier which is a
unit everywhere.

Logarithmic differentiation, first where the functions are nonzero and
then meromorphically, gives \[
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \frac{z'}z
   &=\frac{f'}f+\frac{\Lambda_t(f)'}{\Lambda_t(f)}
     =\frac{f'}f+\frac{G_t(f)'}{G_t(f)}-\frac{2s-1}{s(s-1)},\\
 \frac{z'}z+q_C
   &=\frac{f'}f+q_A+\frac{G_t(f)'}{G_t(f)}.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{SFT25}
\] The last line is the explicit map between the full two logarithmic
receivers. In particular it does not equate those receivers merely
because the additive filter kernel is finite-dimensional.

The exact divisor equality is \[
 \operatorname{div}z
   =\operatorname{div}f+\operatorname{div}\Lambda_t(f).
 \tag{SFT26}
\] For a proof with all exceptional orders present, fix any \(s_0\) and
let \[
 d_I=\operatorname{ord}_{s_0}I,\quad
 d_W=\operatorname{ord}_{s_0}W,\quad
 e_A=\operatorname{ord}_{s_0}A,\quad
 e_\mu=\operatorname{ord}_{s_0}\mu.
\] The local quotient definitions give \[
 \operatorname{ord}_{s_0}f=d_I-e_A,\quad
 \operatorname{ord}_{s_0}z=d_W-e_\mu-e_A,\quad
 \operatorname{ord}_{s_0}\Lambda_t(f)=d_W-d_I-e_\mu.
 \tag{SFT27}
\] All these identities follow by writing each function as its power of
\(h=s-s_0\) times a holomorphic unit. SFT10--11 give \(e_A=-1\) at
\(0,-2,-4,\ldots\), zero elsewhere, and \(e_\mu=1\) at zero and one,
zero elsewhere. For example: \[
 \begin{array}{c|ccc}
 s_0&\operatorname{ord}f&\operatorname{ord}z&
           \operatorname{ord}\Lambda_t(f)\\ \hline
 0&d_I+1&d_W&d_W-d_I-1\\
 1&d_I&d_W-1&d_W-d_I-1\\
 -2m,\ m\ge1&d_I+1&d_W+1&d_W-d_I\\
 \text{other points}&d_I&d_W&d_W-d_I .
 \end{array}
 \tag{SFT28}
\] The full germs, not only their orders, are \(f=I/A\), \(z=W/(\mu A)\)
and \(\Lambda=W/(\mu I)\), with the units of SFT10--11. In particular
\(z(0)=-W(0)/2\) when \(W(0)\ne0\), and the residue of \(z\) at one is
\(W(1)\). These statements include their signs.

Let \(\Omega\) be a bounded region with positively oriented piecewise
smooth boundary \(\Gamma\), on which the relevant meromorphic functions
have no zeros or poles. Assume \(R\) is meromorphic on a neighborhood of
\(\overline\Omega\), has no pole on \(\Gamma\), and has only finitely
many poles inside. For every point \(a\) inside let \[
 m_a=\operatorname{ord}_a z-\operatorname{ord}_a f,\qquad
 L_\Lambda=\Lambda'/\Lambda.
\] If \(R\) is holomorphic throughout the region, the exact
finite-contour trace correction is \[
 \sum_{a\in\Omega}m_aR(a)
       =\frac1{2\pi i}\int_\Gamma R(s)L_\Lambda(s)\,ds.
 \tag{SFT29}
\] The sums are finite on this compact region. To include poles of
\(R\), write at each test pole \(b\) \[
 R(b+h)=\sum_{k=-M_b}^{\infty}r_{b,k}h^k,\qquad
 L_\Lambda(b+h)=\frac{m_b}{h}
                      +\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\ell_{b,j}h^j.
\] Its residue is exactly \[
 \operatorname{Res}_{s=b}(RL_\Lambda)
    =m_b r_{b,0}
       +\sum_{k=1}^{M_b}r_{b,-k}\ell_{b,k-1}.
 \tag{SFT30}
\] Thus SFT29 becomes the sum over non-test-pole divisor points,
together with the full SFT30 contributions at test poles. This includes
a divisor/test collision: \(m_br_{b,0}\) is the finite-part term and the
other displayed summands are the required holomorphic-logarithm jets.
The proof is multiplication of the convergent Laurent series followed by
the residue theorem. No passage to an infinite zero sum, no vanishing of
a boundary at infinity, and no unrestricted rational-test domain is
implicit in SFT29--30.

\subsection{6. A one-dimensional additive kernel can change arbitrarily
large
divisors}\label{a-one-dimensional-additive-kernel-can-change-arbitrarily-large-divisors}

The following exact example lies in the even domains SFT17 and keeps the
same original-function filter output. Fix \(t\ne0\) and an integer
\(N\ge1\), and set \[
 h_t(s)=e^{-(s-1/2)^2/t}\cosh((s-1/2)/t),\qquad
 p_N(s)=(s-\tfrac12)^{2N}.
\] Define the two original receivers and their common target by \[
 f_1(s)=\frac{h_t(s)p_N(s)}{A(s)},\qquad
 f_2(s)=\frac{h_t(s)[p_N(s)+1]}{A(s)},\qquad
 z(s)=\frac{\mathcal B_t[h_t p_N](s)}{\mu(s)A(s)}.
 \tag{SFT31}
\] Both \(f_1,f_2\) lie in \(\mathcal H_A^{\mathrm{ev}}\), and
\(\mathcal B_th_t=0\), so \(\mathcal P_tf_1=\mathcal P_tf_2=z\). This
common target is not identically zero: if \(\mathcal B_t(h_tp_N)=0\),
the one-dimensional even kernel forces \(h_tp_N=c h_t\) for a constant
\(c\). On the nonempty open set where \(h_t\ne0\) this says the
nonconstant polynomial \(p_N=c\), a contradiction.

Their exact quotient and divisor difference are \[
 \frac{f_2}{f_1}=\frac{p_N+1}{p_N},\qquad
 \operatorname{div}f_2-\operatorname{div}f_1
   =\sum_{j=0}^{2N-1}
       [\tfrac12+e^{(2j+1)\pi i/(2N)}]
                     -2N[\tfrac12].
 \tag{SFT32}
\] The \(2N\) displayed zeros are distinct; none is the center. The
quotient identity proves the formula even if a displayed point also
happens to be a zero of \(h_t/A\), since that factor occurs on both
sides with the same order. Its signed degree on a contour enclosing all
the displayed points is zero, its signed count on a small contour about
the center is \(-2N\), and the total variation of its divisor
multiplicities is \(4N\). As \(N\) is arbitrary, retaining only the
dimension of the additive kernel gives no bound on the number or total
multiplicity of changed zeros. This is a fixed-time example; it does not
assert that these two polynomial choices are the particular ESH lateral
families or jointly heat-compatible as \(t\) varies. Those actual
families have the specified jump SFT22. Their divisor correction must be
calculated from their actual quotient, using SFT23--30.

For any two nonzero choices \(f_1,f_2=f_1+k\) of the same inverse with
\(k\in\ker\mathcal P_t\), the general exact formulas are \[
 \frac{f_2}{f_1}=1+\frac{k}{f_1},\qquad
 \frac{f_2'}{f_2}-\frac{f_1'}{f_1}
      =\partial_s\log\!\left(1+\frac{k}{f_1}\right),\qquad
 \operatorname{div}f_2-\operatorname{div}f_1
      =\operatorname{div}\!\left(1+\frac{k}{f_1}\right).
 \tag{SFT33}
\] The logarithmic notation means the single-valued meromorphic
logarithmic derivative; no globally chosen logarithm is assumed.

\subsection{7. Check of the actual upper lateral
decomposition}\label{check-of-the-actual-upper-lateral-decomposition}

This section independently checks the constants and domains of the root
calculation, without replacing its fuller local zero-motion argument.
For positive real \(t\), define \[
 F_+(t,a)=-i\int_0^\infty e^{-tv^2+iav}\,dv,\qquad
 E_0(t,s)=e^{-s^2/t},\quad E_1(t,s)=e^{-(s-1)^2/t}.
 \tag{SFT34}
\] The integral is entire in \(a\); comparison with ESH3, with square
root \(+i\sqrt t\), gives the specified upper \(q\)-lateral value. The
erfc reflection identity, or the full Fourier Gaussian integral, gives
\[
 F_+(t,a)+F_+(t,-a)
        =-i\sqrt{\pi/t}\,e^{-a^2/(4t)}.
\] Set \[
 J_t(s)=L_t(s)-2F_+(t,2s)+2F_+(t,2(s-1)).
\] Since \(1+iZ=2s\) and \(1-iZ=2(1-s)\), substitution into ESH4 and
reflection yield exactly \[
 I_+(t,s)=J_t(s)+2i\sqrt{\pi/t}\,E_1(t,s),\qquad
 I_-(t,s)=J_t(s)-2i\sqrt{\pi/t}\,E_0(t,s).
 \tag{SFT35}
\] In particular the oriented symmetry defect is \[
 J_t(1-s)-J_t(s)
       =2i\sqrt{\pi/t}\,[E_1(t,s)-E_0(t,s)].
 \tag{SFT36}
\] This follows from the actual evenness of \(I_+\), or directly from
the reflection formula. Thus \(J_t\) is not asserted to be the original
even source.

On every compact set in \(\Im s>0\), the integral for every right time
derivative of \(F_+(t,2s)\), including \(t=0\), is dominated by a
constant times \(v^{2n}e^{-2\delta v}\), where \(\delta>0\) is a lower
bound for \(\Im s\). The same holds for \(s-1\). At zero time direct
integration gives \(F_+(0,2s)=1/(2s)\). Hence \[
 J_0(s)=L_0(s)-\frac1s+\frac1{s-1}=A(s)\zeta(s)
       \quad(\Im s>0).
 \tag{SFT37}
\] For the equality on the whole upper half-plane, first use ESH1 on its
overlap with \(0<\Re s<1\), where the rational terms coincide, and then
the identity theorem; the functions have no poles in the upper
half-plane. Every right time derivative exists there locally uniformly
by the displayed domination. These observations give boundedness of
\(J_t\) on each such compact set for sufficiently small \(t\ge0\).

Take a compact set \(K\) contained in the strict upper wedge \[
 \Im s>\max(|\Re s|,|\Re(s-1)|).
 \tag{SFT38}
\] Continuity and compactness give a \(\delta_K>0\) such that \[
 |E_0(t,s)|\ge e^{\delta_K/t},\qquad
 |E_1(t,s)|\ge e^{\delta_K/t}
       \quad(s\in K,\ t>0).
\] Indeed their real exponents are respectively \((\Im s)^2-(\Re s)^2\)
and \((\Im s)^2-(\Re(s-1))^2\), divided by \(t\). For all sufficiently
small positive \(t\), the single exponential appearing in each line of
SFT35 strictly dominates the uniformly bounded \(J_t\). The reverse
triangle inequality therefore proves that both \(I_+\) and \(I_-\) have
no zeros on \(K\). The factor \(A\) has no zero or pole in this wedge,
so the same assertion holds for \(f_+,f_-\).

In contrast, on a sufficiently small closed disk about any isolated
original-zeta zero inside the wedge, the full factor \(\mu A\) is
holomorphic and nonvanishing and \(z_t\to\zeta\) uniformly as \(t\to0\).
The latter assertion follows directly from the entire filtered \(H_t\)
and SFT2. On a disk boundary without a zero, Rouché's theorem then
preserves the number of zeros, including multiplicity, of \(z_t\) for
sufficiently small \(t\). The threshold depends on the disk or compact
set. Nothing here asserts one uniform positive-time threshold for the
entire unbounded zero set.

Both the actual quotient correction and this compact-set calculation
show why the kernel dimension alone does not specify the zero trace.
They provide exact maps and an actual domain of different divisor
behavior. They supply no sign theorem for a full Weil pairing and no RH
conclusion.

\subsection{8. A global rational-test receiver retaining genus-two
terms}\label{a-global-rational-test-receiver-retaining-genus-two-terms}

At any fixed positive real \(t\), each of \(I_+(t,\cdot)\),
\(I_-(t,\cdot)\) and \(J_t\) is an entire function of order at most two.
The same bound holds at a fixed nonzero time on a specified analytic
sectorial branch. Here is a direct bound, so that the trace domain does
not rest on an assumed zero density. The elementary bound
\(\psi(e^{4u})\le K e^{-\pi e^{4u}}\) for \(u\ge0\) follows by summing
its defining positive exponential series. For fixed complex \(t\), the
factor \(|t|u^2+u\) can be bounded above by \((\pi/2)e^{4u}+K_t\). The
residual density therefore bounds \(L_t(s)\) by a constant times
\(\int_0^\infty\exp(-(\pi/2)e^{4u}+2|s-1/2|u)\,du\). For \(b\ge0\), \[
 bu-\frac{\pi}{4}e^{4u}
 \le\frac b4\log(1+b/\pi)\qquad(u\ge0);
\] this follows by maximizing the left side, with an interior maximum
when \(b\ge\pi\) and a boundary maximum otherwise. The unused factor
\(\exp(-(\pi/4)e^{4u})\) is integrable. Thus \[
 |L_t(s)|\le K_t
   \exp\!\left(\frac{2|s-1/2|}{4}
                    \log(1+2|s-1/2|/\pi)\right).
 \tag{SFT39}
\] The theta density defining \(H_t\) has the additional factors
\(e^{9u}\), \(e^{5u}\) and powers of \(n\). Summing those powers against
\(e^{-\pi n^2e^{4u}}\) gives the same bound with a different constant
and exponent linear in \(u\), which can again be absorbed into part of
\(e^{4u}\). Hence \(W_t=16H_t\) is also entire of order at most one.

For \(t>0\), the integral SFT34 gives the explicit bound \[
 |F_+(t,a)|\le \int_0^\infty e^{-tv^2+|a|v}\,dv
       \le \sqrt{\pi/t}\,e^{|a|^2/(4t)}.
 \tag{SFT40}
\] Reflection gives the same order bound for \(F_-\). For other fixed
nonzero branch values, ESH3 and
\(\left|\int_0^z e^{-v^2}dv\right|\le |z|e^{|z|^2}\), obtained along the
straight segment, give a bound \(\exp(K_t(1+|a|^2))\). SFT35 and these
bounds prove the stated order-at-most-two assertion for all the raw
entire functions. Multiplication by \(1/A\), an entire function of order
at most one by the Gamma product below, proves the same bound for each
original entire raw \(f\). The filtered original \(z=W/(\mu A)\) is
meromorphic with at most a simple pole at one.

For a nonzero entire \(I\) of order at most two, let
\(m_0=\operatorname{ord}_0 I\), and let its nonzero zeros be \(\rho\),
with multiplicities \(m_\rho\). The finite-order Hadamard factorization,
with all its polynomial terms retained, has the form \[
 I(s)=s^{m_0}e^{a_I s^2+b_I s+c_I}
          \prod_{\rho\ne0}
             E_2(s/\rho)^{m_\rho},\qquad
 E_2(w)=(1-w)e^{w+w^2/2}.
 \tag{SFT41}
\] Here is a self-contained proof of the precise classical Hadamard
factorization used in SFT41. Remove the zero at the origin by writing
\(I_0(s)=I(s)/s^{m_0}\), so \(I_0(0)\ne0\). Fix \(\varepsilon=1/4\). The
order bound implies
\(\log^+M_{I_0}(R)\le K_\varepsilon(1+R^{2+\varepsilon})\). The
circle-mean identity for \(\log|s-\rho|\), obtained by expanding
\(\log(1-w)\) when \(|w|<1\), is \[
 \frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
      \log|r e^{i\theta}-\rho|\,d\theta
       =\log\max(r,|\rho|).
\] Removing the finitely many zeros inside a circle from \(I_0\), and
applying the mean-value property to the logarithm of the remaining
zero-free function, gives Jensen's identity. In particular, using
circles of radius at least \(2R\), each zero with \(|\rho|\le R\)
contributes at least \(\log2\). It follows that the multiplicity-counted
number \(n(R)\) of nonzero zeros satisfies \[
 n(R)\le K(1+R^{2+\varepsilon}).
\] Radii passing through a zero may be approached by radii not passing
through zeros; the same bound follows by continuity and monotonicity of
the counts.

Summing dyadic annuli now gives
\(\sum_{\rho\ne0}m_\rho|\rho|^{-3}<\infty\). For \(|w|\le1/2\), \[
 \log E_2(w)=-\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}\frac{w^k}{k},
 \qquad |\log E_2(w)|\le K|w|^3.
\] Consequently \(\Pi(s)=\prod_{\rho\ne0}E_2(s/\rho)^{m_\rho}\)
converges locally normally and has exactly the prescribed zeros and
multiplicities. The quotient \(I_0/\Pi\) is an entire zero-free
function. Its logarithmic derivative has an entire primitive on
\(\mathbb C\); choosing the additive constant to recover its nonzero
value at zero proves that \(I_0/\Pi=e^Q\) for an entire \(Q\).

It remains to prove that \(Q\) is a polynomial of degree at most two,
since an arbitrary entire logarithm would not suffice for the trace
argument. For large \(R\), exclude from \([R,2R]\) all intervals
\((|\rho|-R^{-4},|\rho|+R^{-4})\) for the zeros with \(|\rho|\le4R\).
Their total length, even when overcounted with multiplicity, is at most
\(2R^{-4}n(4R)=O(R^{-2+\varepsilon})<R\). There exists a remaining
radius \(r\in[R,2R]\). On its entire circle every such zero satisfies
\(|s-\rho|\ge R^{-4}\).

Because the nonzero zero set is discrete, its moduli have a positive
lower bound unless it is empty, in which case the following estimates
are immediate. The finitely many moduli below one can be bounded
separately. For all other zeros with \(|\rho|\le4R\), \[
 \log|1-s/\rho|\ge-\log4-5\log R,
 \qquad
 \Re\left(\frac{s}{\rho}+\frac{s^2}{2\rho^2}\right)
      \ge-\frac{2R}{|\rho|}-\frac{2R^2}{|\rho|^2}.
\] The zero count, summed in dyadic annuli up to \(4R\), gives \[
 \sum_{|\rho|\le4R}\frac{m_\rho}{|\rho|}
       \le K(1+R^{1+\varepsilon}),\qquad
 \sum_{|\rho|\le4R}\frac{m_\rho}{|\rho|^2}
       \le K(1+R^\varepsilon).
\] The finite small-modulus contributions are included in the constants.
Thus the logarithm of the modulus of the small-zero part of \(\Pi\) is
bounded below by \(-K R^{2+\varepsilon}\log R\). For the tail,
\(|\rho|>4R\) gives \(|s/\rho|\le1/2\), and summing the displayed
logarithmic series bound and dyadic tail estimate yields \[
 \log|\Pi_{\mathrm{tail}}(s)|
       \ge-K r^3\sum_{|\rho|>4R}
                           \frac{m_\rho}{|\rho|^3}
       \ge-KR^{2+\varepsilon}.
\] On these selected circles it follows that \[
 \Re Q(s)=\log|I_0(s)|-\log|\Pi(s)|
          \le K R^{2+\varepsilon}\log R.
\] Let this upper bound be \(M_R\), increasing it if necessary. Write
the entire power series \(Q(s)=\sum_{n\ge0}q_ns^n\). The nonnegative
function \(M_R-\Re Q(r e^{i\theta})\) has average \(M_R-\Re q_0\). Its
\(n\)-th Fourier coefficient for \(n\ge1\) is \(-q_n r^n/2\). Bounding
the modulus of that coefficient by the average gives \[
 |q_n|\le\frac{2(M_R-\Re q_0)}{r^n}
            \le K_n R^{2+\varepsilon-n}\log R.
\] Letting \(R\to\infty\), with \(\varepsilon=1/4\), proves \(q_n=0\)
for every \(n\ge3\). Therefore \(Q=a_Is^2+b_Is+c_I\), as asserted in
SFT41. This proves the needed factorization and its polynomial degree
without an assumed genus-one replacement.

Logarithmic differentiation now yields the locally normally convergent
series away from the zeros \[
 \frac{I'}I=
       2a_I s+b_I+\frac{m_0}{s}
       +\sum_{\rho\ne0}m_\rho
          \left[\frac1{s-\rho}+\frac1\rho+\frac{s}{\rho^2}\right].
 \tag{SFT42}
\] The bracket is \(O_K(|\rho|^{-3})\) for \(s\) in a fixed compact set
\(K\); for example its exact expression is \(s^2/[\rho^2(s-\rho)]\). The
coefficients \(a_I,b_I,c_I\) depend on the actual raw branch or
function. They are not set to zero or identified between branches.

For completeness, the reciprocal-Gamma product gives \[
 \frac1{A(s)}
   =\pi^{s/2}\frac{s}{2}e^{\gamma s/2}
       \prod_{n=1}^{\infty}
           \left(1+\frac{s}{2n}\right)e^{-s/(2n)}.
\] Consequently the exact original raw logarithmic derivative is \[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \frac{f'}f
  =\frac{I'}I-q_A
  =\frac{I'}I+\frac{\log\pi+\gamma}{2}+\frac1s+
       \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
          \left[\frac1{s+2n}-\frac1{2n}\right].}
 \tag{SFT43}
\] This is an equality of meromorphic functions, retaining the pi
factor, the Euler constant, all Gamma poles and their complete
derivative contribution. The series in SFT43 converges normally off its
poles.

Let \(R\) be a rational function satisfying \(R(s)=O(s^{-3})\) at
infinity, and first assume that every finite pole of \(R\) avoids the
divisors under discussion. Let \(T_R\) be its finite pole set. The
signed original divisor traces, with zeros positive and poles negative,
are absolutely convergent: \[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathscr T_I(R)&=\sum_{a\in\mathbb C}
                      \operatorname{ord}_a(I)\,R(a),\\
 \mathscr T_f(R)&=\mathscr T_I(R)
                          +\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}R(-2m),\\
 \mathscr T_z(R)&=\mathscr T_W(R)
                          +\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}R(-2m)-R(0)-R(1).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{SFT44}
\] The zero at zero from \(1/A\) and the endpoint at zero from \(\mu\)
remain separately displayed in the last line. Coincident zeros of \(I\)
or \(W\) contribute their full multiplicities in addition to these fixed
factors.

The exact test-residue formulas are \[
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \mathscr T_I(R)&=-\sum_{b\in T_R}
                    \operatorname{Res}_{s=b}
                          \left(R(s)\frac{I'(s)}{I(s)}\right),\\
 \mathscr T_f(R)&=-\sum_{b\in T_R}
                    \operatorname{Res}_{s=b}
                          \left(R(s)\left[\frac{I'}I-q_A\right]\right),\\
 \mathscr T_z(R)&=-\sum_{b\in T_R}
                    \operatorname{Res}_{s=b}
                    \left(R(s)\left[\frac{W'}W-q_A
                                    -\frac1s-\frac1{s-1}\right]\right).
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{SFT45}
\] To prove the first, insert SFT42 on small circles around each test
pole. Local normal convergence permits integration term by term. For
each zero \(\rho\ne0\), the rational function \[
 R(s)\left[\frac1{s-\rho}+\frac1\rho+\frac{s}{\rho^2}\right]
\] is \(O(s^{-2})\) at infinity and therefore has zero residue there.
Its pole at \(\rho\) contributes \(R(\rho)\), and the sum of its
residues at all test poles is \(-R(\rho)\). The term \(m_0/s\) gives the
same conclusion for the zero at zero. The retained polynomial
\(2a_Is+b_I\), multiplied by \(R\), is also \(O(s^{-2})\), so its finite
test residues sum to zero. Absolute convergence of the zero sum follows
from SFT41. This proves the formula without asserting that an arbitrary
growing contour has vanishing boundary integral.

For the second formula, use SFT43. Each Gamma summand, including the
term at zero, contributes \(R(-2m)\) by the same finite-pole residue
argument; the constants are retained in the logarithmic derivative and
have zero total test residue after multiplication by \(R\). The normally
convergent series and absolutely convergent Gamma trace justify
interchanging sum and contour integration. For the third, apply the same
reasoning to \(W\), subtracting the two exact endpoint logarithmic
derivatives as displayed. Thus the full original factors prove every
line of SFT45.

If a test pole coincides with a divisor point, define its trace value
using \(\operatorname{FP}_{s=a}R(s)\), the constant coefficient of the
Laurent expansion, and use this value only at the finitely many
colliding points. For any of the functions \(g=I,f,z\), the complete
extension is \[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \mathscr T_g^{\mathrm{FP}}(R)
     =-\sum_{b\in T_R}\operatorname{Res}_{s=b}
                         \left(R(s)\frac{g'(s)}{g(s)}\right)
       +\sum_{b\in T_R}
            \operatorname{ord}_b(g)\operatorname{FP}_{s=b}R(s).}
 \tag{SFT46}
\] To verify the collision correction, consider a single divisor term
\(m/(s-a)\). If \(a\notin T_R\), its pole lies outside the test set and
contributes \(-mR(a)\) to the summed test residues, as above. If
\(a\in T_R\), all finite poles of \(R(s)/(s-a)\) are test poles, so
their residue sum is zero. The added term \(m\,\operatorname{FP}_aR\)
inserts precisely the required trace value. The constant and linear
compensating terms in SFT42 have zero summed test residue. Summing
proves SFT46, also for negative orders at poles. The local jet
contributions at each test pole are exactly SFT30; SFT46 does not
discard them.

For the global filter receiver, assume explicitly that \(I=Af\) is
nonzero entire of order at most two and that \(W=\mathcal B_tI\) is not
identically zero. Cauchy's derivative estimate on circles of radius one
bounds the maximum moduli of \(I'\) and \(I''\) by constant multiples of
the maximum modulus of \(I\) on the disk of radius \(R+1\).
Multiplication by the polynomial coefficients in \(\mathcal B_t\) does
not increase this order bound. Thus \(W\) also has order at most two.
Combining SFT24--27 with SFT45--46 now gives the global exact filter
receiver \[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \mathscr T_z^{\mathrm{FP}}(R)-\mathscr T_f^{\mathrm{FP}}(R)
     =-\sum_{b\in T_R}\operatorname{Res}_{s=b}
                         \left(R(s)\frac{\Lambda_t(f)'}{\Lambda_t(f)}\right)
       +\sum_{b\in T_R}
        [\operatorname{ord}_b z-\operatorname{ord}_b f]
                                    \operatorname{FP}_{s=b}R(s).}
 \tag{SFT47}
\] It applies, in particular, to each actual \(f_+\) and \(f_-\) with
the common filtered \(z_t\), preserving their different \(I\)'s, factors
\(G_t(f)\), and branch-specific polynomial terms from SFT42. Every
infinite sum in this statement is absolutely convergent on the stated
cubic-decay rational domain.

The growth bound alone does not imply actual order two, quadratic zero
density, or divergence for a test of order \(O(s^{-2})\). It does mean
that the genus-one/Cauchy domain cannot be transferred using only that
bound: \(\sum m_\rho|\rho|^{-2}\) need not follow, and the retained term
\(2a_Is\) can give a nonzero residue at infinity when multiplied by an
\(O(s^{-2})\) test. This failure of a uniform quadratic-decay version of
SFT45 has an exact example: \[
 I(s)=e^{s^2},\qquad R(s)=s^{-2},\qquad
 \mathscr T_I(R)=0,\qquad
 -\operatorname{Res}_{s=0}
       \left(R(s)\frac{I'(s)}{I(s)}\right)=-2.
 \tag{SFT48}
\] The missing term is the contribution at infinity of the retained
polynomial \(2s\): its product with \(R\) is \(2/s\), whose infinity
residue is \(-2\). There are no zeros or divisor/test collisions in this
example, so the discrepancy does not arise from an ambiguous zero sum.
It concerns the full class of order-at-most-two entire functions; it
does not assert that a particular actual lateral branch has this same
coefficient. The cubic domain above is a proved receiver without an
additional zero-density or polynomial-coefficient assertion.

\subsection{9. Support and coefficient
data}\label{support-and-coefficient-data}

All displayed maps act on complex function spaces with the original
coefficients and local germs recorded. If these function components are
accompanied by the programme's labels, the map is the displayed function
map together with the identity on the label set. The coefficient
semiring itself has its identity morphism, whose prime-ideal pullback is
the identity. No new spectrum map is inferred from the complex-linearity
of \(\mathcal P_t\), and neither the supported zero nor the unsupported
element is identified with the other by any formula here.

This note's new mathematical content is the exact augmented inverse
SFT12--20, the multiplicative and local trace receiver SFT23--30, the
unbounded divisor-change example SFT31--32, and the original-function
global receiver and exact test-domain example SFT39--48. The source ESH
supplies the actual sectorial continuations and jump. Their full
original factors, domains and time dependence have been retained
throughout.
