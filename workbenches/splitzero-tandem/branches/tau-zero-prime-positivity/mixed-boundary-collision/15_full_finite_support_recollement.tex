

\section{The whole finite support spectrum and its relative cohomology}
\label{sec:full-finite-support}
The coefficient source is the original globalization note and the full lattice extension \cite{globalization,support}. The complete construction and proofs follow.
\subsection{The original semiring and its exact topology}

Let $L$ be a finite nontrivial bounded distributive lattice. Its bottom
and top are $0_L$ and $1_L$. For a nonzero ring $R$, retain the semiring
\[
 \G_L(R)=\{z_\lambda=(0,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}
     \cup\{\widehat a=(a,1_L):a\in R\}\subset R\times L.
 \tag{FL1}\label{FL:eq:semiring}
\]
The overlap is $e=z_{1_L}=\widehat0$, and the additive identity is
$\tau=z_{0_L}\ne e$. Addition is coefficient addition and lattice
join; multiplication is coefficient multiplication and lattice meet.
In particular
\[
 z_\lambda+z_\mu=z_{\lambda\vee\mu},\quad
 z_\lambda z_\mu=z_{\lambda\wedge\mu},\quad
 \widehat a+z_\lambda=\widehat a,\quad
 \widehat a z_\lambda=z_\lambda.
 \tag{FL2}\label{FL:eq:operations}
\]
Every coefficient and every support label remains present.

\begin{proposition}[All strata and all opens]\label{FL:prop:ordinal-spectrum}
Put $S=\SpecLat L$ and $X=\Spec R$. The prime spectrum of
$\G_L(R)$ is the disjoint union $Y=S\sqcup X$ with primes
\[
 P_{\mathfrak a}=\{z_\lambda:\lambda\in\mathfrak a\},\qquad
 Q_{\mathfrak p}=\{z_\lambda:\lambda\in L\}
                   \cup\{\widehat a:a\in\mathfrak p\}.
 \tag{FL3}\label{FL:eq:all-primes}
\]
Here $\mathfrak a$ is a prime lattice ideal and $\mathfrak p$ a ring
prime. The order in either stratum is its original prime-inclusion
order, and $P_{\mathfrak a}\subsetneq Q_{\mathfrak p}$ for every pair.
The opens of $Y$ are exactly the opens of $S$ and the sets
$S\cup U$ with nonempty open $U\subset X$. In particular
$D(e)=S$ and $V(e)=X$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
An ideal containing a supported element $\widehat a$ contains $e$
because $\widehat a e=e$, and then contains every $z_\lambda$ because
$ez_\lambda=z_\lambda$. Its supported coefficients form a ring ideal:
closure under negatives is multiplication by $\widehat{-1}$. Its
primality is exactly primality of that ring ideal. An ideal with no
supported element consists of zero-coefficient labels from a proper
lattice ideal. Addition gives join closure and multiplication by
$z_\mu$ for $\mu\le\lambda$ gives downward closure. Its primality is
exactly the meet-prime condition. Conversely these constructions give
ideals and verify the corresponding primality conditions, proving
\eqref{FL:eq:all-primes}. A pure support prime omits $e$ whereas every
arithmetic prime contains the whole zero-coefficient lattice. This
proves every stated inclusion. Finally the basic opens are
\[
 D(z_\lambda)=\{P_{\mathfrak a}:\lambda\notin\mathfrak a\},\qquad
 D(\widehat a)=S\cup D_R(a).
 \tag{FL4}\label{FL:eq:basic-opens}
\]
Unions of these give exactly the asserted topology and both stratum
identifications.
\end{proof}

For completeness, the support spectrum here is nonempty and $T_0$.
Choose a maximal proper lattice ideal $I$, possible because $L$ is
finite and nontrivial. If $a,b\notin I$ but $a\wedge b\in I$,
maximality gives $u\vee a=1_L$ and $v\vee b=1_L$ for some $u,v\in I$.
Distributivity expands $(u\vee a)\wedge(v\vee b)$ as a join of four
elements of $I$, including $a\wedge b$, a contradiction. Thus $I$
is prime. Distinct prime ideals differ on some label $\lambda$, and
the basic open $D(z_\lambda)$ separates them, proving $T_0$.

More generally, take any nonempty finite $T_0$ space $S$ and any
nonempty irreducible space $X$ and give $Y=S\triangleright X$ the
same topology. Write $j:S\hookrightarrow Y$ for the open inclusion
and $i:X\hookrightarrow Y$ for the closed inclusion. The applications
below use $X=\Spec\mathbb Z$. No semiring additive-module action is
implicitly imposed on the abelian sheaves: the receiving category
throughout is the category of abelian sheaves on this whole topology.

We use the specialization convention that $s\le t$ means that $s$
generalizes $t$. Thus opens in a finite $T_0$ space are lower sets,
the smallest neighbourhood of $s$ is $\mathord\downarrow s$, and a
sheaf has a restriction map from its stalk at $t$ to its stalk at $s$
when $s\le t$. This fixes all cochain orientations below.

\subsection{Sheaves, universal coupling, and derived support}

Let $c_X B$ denote the constant sheaf on the irreducible space $X$:
its value on every nonempty open is $B$ and every such restriction is
the identity. Let $c_S B$ denote the usual constant sheaf on $S$;
its value on an open $V\subset S$ is one copy of $B$ for each connected
component of $V$. For an abelian sheaf $\mathcal A$ on $X$ define
\[
 g\mathcal A=\varinjlim_{\varnothing\ne U\subset X}\mathcal A(U).
 \tag{FL5}\label{FL:eq:generic}
\]
The nonempty opens form a filtered restriction system because $X$ is
irreducible. The functor $g$ is exact. Indeed a sheaf epimorphism lifts
a section on some nonempty member of an open cover, which suffices for
its generic germ, and a generic germ with zero image becomes zero on
a smaller nonempty open, where the kernel sheaf gives exactness.
There are adjunctions
\[
 g\dashv c_X,\qquad c_S\dashv\Gamma(S,-).
 \tag{FL6}\label{FL:eq:constant-adjunctions}
\]
The first expresses that compatible maps $\mathcal A(U)\to B$ are
exactly maps from their colimit; the second follows because a map from
constant sections is determined by their global images and then
sheafification. Both constant-sheaf functors are exact, as is seen on
stalks. In particular $c_X$ preserves injectives, and applying it to
an injective resolution of $B$ proves
\[
 R\Gamma(X,c_XB)=B[0].
 \tag{FL7}\label{FL:eq:constant-acyclic}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Full gluing over a finite support space]
\label{FL:thm:finite-gluing}
An abelian sheaf on $Y$ is exactly a triple
\[
 \mathcal F=(\mathcal A,\mathcal B,\phi),\qquad
 \phi:\mathcal A\longrightarrow c_X\Gamma(S,\mathcal B),
 \tag{FL8}\label{FL:eq:triple}
\]
with $\mathcal A$ a sheaf on $X$ and $\mathcal B$ a sheaf on $S$.
Its sections on $S\cup U$ are $\mathcal A(U)$ and its sections on an
open $V\subset S$ are $\mathcal B(V)$. Restriction from $S\cup U$ to
$V$ is $\phi_U$ followed by restriction from $S$ to $V$.
A morphism $(a,b)$ satisfies
$\phi'a=c_X\Gamma(S,b)\phi$. Exactness is detected on the two
components. The elementary functors are
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
 i^*\mathcal F=\mathcal A,&i_*\mathcal A=(\mathcal A,0,0),&
 i^!\mathcal F=\ker\phi,\\
 j^*\mathcal F=\mathcal B,&j_!\mathcal B=(0,\mathcal B,0),&
 j_*\mathcal B=(c_X\Gamma(S,\mathcal B),\mathcal B,\mathrm{id}).
\end{array}
 \tag{FL9}\label{FL:eq:functors}
\]
Moreover the exact left adjoint of $i^*$ is
\[
 U_S\mathcal A=
 (\mathcal A,c_Sg\mathcal A,\epsilon_S),
 \tag{FL10}\label{FL:eq:universal}
\]
where $\epsilon_S$ sends a section to the constant support section
with its original generic germ. This is the universal coupling to
the entire support space.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Starting with a sheaf on $Y$, set
$\mathcal A(U)=\mathcal F(S\cup U)$ for nonempty $U$ and
$\mathcal B=j^*\mathcal F$. Any pair of nonempty arithmetic opens
intersects. Consequently the sheaf gluing condition for the opens
$S\cup U_a$ is exactly that for the corresponding opens $U_a$ of $X$.
These values therefore form a sheaf $\mathcal A$. Restriction to all
of $S$ gives $\phi$. The mixed open $S\cup U$ is the smallest open
of $Y$ containing $U$, so the resulting sheaf is precisely $i^*\mathcal F$.

Conversely a triple gives the displayed restrictions. A cover of a
mixed open has mixed members covering its arithmetic part. Sections
on those members glue in $\mathcal A$; compatibility with any member
lying wholly in $S$ is exactly its prescribed restriction from
$\Gamma(S,\mathcal B)$. Covers lying wholly in $S$ use the sheaf
axiom for $\mathcal B$. This proves the sheaf axiom and the inverse
classification. The morphism equation follows from restriction to
$S$. Kernels and cokernels are computed on the two strata: they are
the inverse-image sheaves on $X$ and $S$, and stalkwise exactness on
these disjoint strata is exactness on $Y$. The induced cokernel arrow
is obtained by composing to $\Gamma(S,\coker b)$; no exactness of
$\Gamma(S,-)$ is being assumed.

The formulas for $i^*,i_*,i^!,j^*,j_!,j_*$ follow by the Hom
adjunctions: a map from $i_*\mathcal C$ lands in $\ker\phi$, and a
map to $j_*\mathcal D$ has its arithmetic part forced by its support
part. The putative $j_!$ has zero sections on every mixed open. This
is a sheaf because a cover of a mixed open has a mixed member whose
intersection with $S$ is all of $S$, forcing any compatible support
section to vanish. It has the required left adjunction to $j^*$.

For the final adjunction, a map $U_S\mathcal C\to\mathcal F$ with
arithmetic part $a$ has its support map uniquely determined by the
composite $\mathcal C\xrightarrow a\mathcal A\xrightarrow\phi
c_X\Gamma(S,\mathcal B)$: apply successively the two adjunctions in
\eqref{FL:eq:constant-adjunctions}. Conversely that map satisfies the
gluing equation. Thus $U_S\dashv i^*$. Exactness of $U_S$ follows
from exactness of $g,c_S$, and the componentwise exactness already
proved. All assertions follow without dropping a support point.
\end{proof}

The distinction from a single open point is that $\Gamma(S,-)$, and
hence $j_*$, can have higher derived functors. They must be retained.
We use cohomological degrees, so $B[-1]$ has $B$ in degree one.

\begin{theorem}[Derived gluing including support higher cohomology]
\label{FL:thm:derived-finite}
For $\mathcal F=(\mathcal A,\mathcal B,\phi)$ there are natural formulas
\[
 Ri^!\mathcal F\simeq
 \operatorname{Cone}\bigl(\mathcal A\longrightarrow
          c_XR\Gamma(S,\mathcal B)\bigr)[-1],
 \tag{FL11}\label{FL:eq:derived-closed}
\]
\[
 R\Gamma(Y,\mathcal F)=R\Gamma(X,\mathcal A),\qquad
 R\Gamma_X(Y,\mathcal F)\simeq
 \operatorname{Cone}\bigl(R\Gamma(X,\mathcal A)
                \longrightarrow R\Gamma(S,\mathcal B)\bigr)[-1].
 \tag{FL12}\label{FL:eq:derived-global}
\]
The maps are induced by the actual restriction to $S$ in
\eqref{FL:eq:triple}. The localization triangle is
\[
 i_*Ri^!\mathcal F\longrightarrow\mathcal F
 \longrightarrow Rj_*\mathcal B\longrightarrow i_*Ri^!\mathcal F[1].
 \tag{FL13}\label{FL:eq:localization}
\]
Here $R\Gamma_X(Y,-)$ denotes derived sections supported in the closed
arithmetic stratum. The formula does not replace $R\Gamma(S,\mathcal B)$
by its zeroth cohomology.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The functors $i^*$ and $j^*$ are exact. Their right adjoints $i_*$ and
$j_*$ preserve injectives. Also $i^*$ preserves injectives because
its left adjoint $U_S$ is exact, and $j^*$ preserves injectives because
$j_!$ is exact.
If $\mathcal I$ is injective on $Y$, write
$\mathcal B_I=j^*\mathcal I$. Extend the identity support map
$j_!\mathcal B_I\to\mathcal I$ along the monomorphism
$j_!\mathcal B_I\to j_*\mathcal B_I$. Injectivity supplies a map
$j_*\mathcal B_I\to\mathcal I$ right inverse to the restriction
$\mathcal I\to j_*\mathcal B_I$: on $S$ its composite is the identity,
and on $X$ the gluing equation forces the identity on
$c_X\Gamma(S,\mathcal B_I)$. Thus
\[
 0\to i_*i^!\mathcal I\to\mathcal I\to j_*j^*\mathcal I\to0
\]
is split exact. Apply this to an injective resolution of $\mathcal F$.
Its support restriction is an injective resolution of $\mathcal B$;
the corresponding $j_*$ complex computes $Rj_*\mathcal B$.
The kernel complex computes $i_*Ri^!\mathcal F$, proving
\eqref{FL:eq:localization}. Restriction to $X$ turns the $j_*$ complex
into $c_X\Gamma(S,j^*\mathcal I^\bullet)$, which is exactly
$c_XR\Gamma(S,\mathcal B)$. This proves
\eqref{FL:eq:derived-closed}. Global sections on $Y$ are the sections
of $\mathcal A$ on $X$, and $i^*$ preserves injectives, proving the
first formula in \eqref{FL:eq:derived-global}. Applying global sections
to \eqref{FL:eq:derived-closed} and using
\eqref{FL:eq:constant-acyclic} proves the second. At degree zero the
kernel consists precisely of sections restricting to zero on the
open stratum, which verifies the asserted support interpretation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite cochains with their complete orientation}

For the next calculation, identify a finite $T_0$ space with its poset
under the convention above. A sheaf $\mathcal B$ is equivalently the
diagram of stalks $B_s=\mathcal B(\mathord\downarrow s)$ and restriction
maps $r_{t,s}:B_t\to B_s$ for $s\le t$. Indeed a section on a lower
open is a compatible family of these germs, since the smallest
neighbourhoods cover it, and sheaf gluing recovers exactly such families.

\begin{theorem}[The full finite cochain resolution]\label{FL:thm:finite-cochains}
For a sheaf of rational vector spaces on a finite $T_0$ space $S$,
$R\Gamma(S,\mathcal B)$ is represented by
\[
 C^n(S,\mathcal B)=\prod_{s_0<\cdots<s_n}B_{s_0},
 \tag{FL14}\label{FL:eq:cochains}
\]
with differential
\[
 (dc)_{s_0<\cdots<s_{n+1}}
 =r_{s_1,s_0}c_{s_1<\cdots<s_{n+1}}
   +\sum_{a=1}^{n+1}(-1)^a
       c_{s_0<\cdots<\widehat{s_a}<\cdots<s_{n+1}}.
 \tag{FL15}\label{FL:eq:cochain-differential}
\]
For constant coefficients $\RQ$ every restriction is the identity,
so the degree-zero differential is
$(db)_{s<t}=b_t-b_s$. No chain with a repeated point is included.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Computing with rational sheaves gives the same derived sections as
computing with their underlying abelian sheaves. Indeed the forgetful
functor is right adjoint to sheafwise rationalization
$\RQ\otimes_{\mathbb Z}-$; rationalization is exact because it is
localization of abelian groups. Thus an injective rational sheaf
remains injective after forgetting its rational scalar action.
For $t\in S$ and a rational vector space $M$, let $I_t(M)$ be the
sheaf with value $M$ on an open containing $t$ and zero otherwise.
This is direct image from the one-point subspace. Its stalk at $q$
is $M$ if $t\le q$ and zero otherwise. It is right adjoint to the
exact stalk functor at $t$. Since every rational vector space is
injective, $I_t(M)$ is an injective sheaf.

Define the sheaf
\[
 \mathcal C^n=\prod_{s_0<\cdots<s_n}I_{s_n}(B_{s_0}).
\]
Its value on a lower open $V$ is the product over all chains contained
in $V$, since having $s_n\in V$ forces every preceding point into
$V$. The formula \eqref{FL:eq:cochain-differential} therefore defines a
sheaf differential. Expanding $d^2$ cancels every twice-deleted face
with the opposite order of deletion; the two terms using a restriction
cancel because $r_{s_2,s_0}=r_{s_1,s_0}r_{s_2,s_1}$.
The augmentation $\mathcal B\to\mathcal C^0$ is the family of germs.

At a stalk $q$, the augmented complex is that on
$\mathord\downarrow q$, which has maximal point $q$.
Its contracting map from degree zero to the augmentation is evaluation
at $q$. For $n\ge1$, its contracting map from degree $n$ to degree
$n-1$ is
\[
 (hc)_{s_0<\cdots<s_{n-1}}=
 \begin{cases}
 (-1)^n c_{s_0<\cdots<s_{n-1}<q},&s_{n-1}<q,\\
 0,&s_{n-1}=q.
 \end{cases}
\]
The identity $dh+hd=1$ follows by pairing the faces omitting one of
$s_0,\ldots,s_{n-1}$, which cancel, leaving only the face omitting
$q$. In degree zero the additional term is the restriction of the
value at $q$, so that identity includes the augmentation. This proves
stalkwise exactness of the augmented complex. The sheaves
$\mathcal C^n$ are finite products of injectives and vanish above the
maximum strict-chain length. They give an injective resolution, whose
global sections are precisely \eqref{FL:eq:cochains}. This proves the
claimed derived computation with all its signs.
\end{proof}

For an order-preserving map $f:S'\to S$, the induced pullback on
constant-coefficient cochains is
\[
 (f^*c)_{t_0<\cdots<t_n}=
 \begin{cases}
 c_{f(t_0)<\cdots<f(t_n)},&f(t_0),\ldots,f(t_n)\text{ are distinct},\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{FL16}\label{FL:eq:cochain-map}
\]
At degree zero it is the usual coordinate pullback. This commutes
with $d$. If the image chain has no repetitions it is immediate from
\eqref{FL:eq:cochain-differential}. If it has a repetition, terms deleting
either member of an adjacent equal pair have the same value and
opposite signs; all other terms still have a repetition and are zero.
This proves the identity in that case as well. The construction is
functorial and gives the map induced by restriction on the injective
cochain resolutions and their augmentations. More explicitly, at a
point $t\in S'$ it pulls cochains on $\mathord\downarrow f(t)$ back
to cochains on $\mathord\downarrow t$, by the displayed formula.
These maps commute with restriction to smaller minimal opens, so
define a sheaf cochain map from the inverse-image resolution to the
resolution on $S'$. Its augmentation is the identity on constant
coefficients. This identifies its global cochain map with the actual
derived pullback. Equivalently it is the
map on the cochains of strict chains after removing repeated chains;
the cancellation just proved verifies that removal directly.

\subsection{The universal arithmetic sheaf for every finite support}

Take $X=\Spec\mathbb Z$, so its generic stalk is $\RQ$. Define the
actual sheaf on the whole ordinal-sum space
\[
 \mathcal U_S=U_S\mathcal O_X
   =(\mathcal O_X,\RQ_S,\text{generic constant restriction}).
 \tag{FL17}\label{FL:eq:arithmetic-object}
\]
The morphism sends each original regular integer-valued rational
section to its same rational generic value at every support point.
In particular it is diagonal on disconnected support components.

We will use $R\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)=\mathbb Z[0]$, including its
vanishing in higher degrees. Here is a direct verification in this
case. On a nonempty open $U=D(N)$, regular functions are the rational
numbers whose denominator has prime factors only among those dividing
$N$. The quotient sheaf $\PP_X=\RQ_X/\mathcal O_X$ has
\[
 \PP_X(U)=\bigoplus_{p\in U\text{ closed}}\RQ/\mathbb Z_{(p)}.
 \tag{FL18}\label{FL:eq:principal-parts}
\]
The rational section map onto this direct sum is surjective: represent
each of finitely many prescribed $p$ classes by $a_p/p^{n_p}$ and
sum them; each other summand is integral at that prime. Its kernel
is precisely $\mathcal O_X(U)$. Formula \eqref{FL:eq:principal-parts}
is a sheaf, since opens of this Noetherian space are quasi-compact
and compatible finite-support sections glue with finite support.
Restrictions discard the removed primes and are surjective, so
$\PP_X$ is flasque. The rational constant sheaf is also flasque.
Their exact sequence is therefore an acyclic resolution of
$\mathcal O_X$. The surjectivity just proved at $U=X$ makes its
global-sections complex have only $\mathbb Z$ in degree zero, proving
the claimed calculation. The acyclicity of flasque sheaves follows
from the usual resolution argument: local lifts modulo a flasque
kernel extend and glue by successive extension over open sets, so a
flasque resolution has an exact global-sections complex in positive
degrees. This argument applies to both displayed sheaves.

\begin{theorem}[A complete relative complex for the full support lattice]
\label{FL:thm:whole-relative-complex}
Put $C^\bullet=C^\bullet(S,\RQ)$ with the exact differential
\eqref{FL:eq:cochain-differential}. The relative complex of
$\mathcal U_S$ is represented by
\[
 K_S^0=\mathbb Z,\qquad K_S^n=C^{n-1}\ (n\ge1),\qquad
 d^0(z)=(z)_{s\in S},\quad d^n=d_C^{n-1}\ (n\ge1).
 \tag{FL19}\label{FL:eq:relative-complex}
\]
These signs identify it with the mapping fibre in
\eqref{FL:eq:derived-global}. Explicitly use fibre coordinates
$(a,c)\in A^n\oplus C^{n-1}$ with differential
$(a,c)\mapsto(d_Aa,f(a)-d_Cc)$; this is a model of
$\operatorname{Cone}(f)[-1]$. In support degree $n-1$ multiply this
coordinate by $(-1)^{n-1}$ to obtain the displayed positive
support differential. Let $c$ be the number
of connected components of $S$. Then
\[
 H_X^0(Y,\mathcal U_S)=0,\qquad
 H_X^1(Y,\mathcal U_S)=\RQ^c/\Delta\mathbb Z,
 \qquad
 H_X^n(Y,\mathcal U_S)=H^{n-1}(S,\RQ)\quad(n\ge2).
 \tag{FL20}\label{FL:eq:relative-groups}
\]
On the arithmetic stratum, the entire derived object is
\[
 Ri^!\mathcal U_S\simeq
 [\mathcal O_X\longrightarrow c_XC^0\longrightarrow
 c_XC^1\longrightarrow\cdots],
 \tag{FL21}\label{FL:eq:relative-sheaf-complex}
\]
with $\mathcal O_X$ in degree zero, the displayed generic constant
map in degree zero, and the same support differential thereafter.
In particular
\[
 R^1i^!\mathcal U_S=(\RQ^c)_X/\Delta\mathcal O_X,
 \qquad R^ni^!\mathcal U_S=c_XH^{n-1}(S,\RQ)\quad(n\ge2).
 \tag{FL22}\label{FL:eq:relative-cohomology-sheaves}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{FL:thm:finite-cochains} computes the actual support derived
sections, and \eqref{FL:eq:arithmetic-object} sends a rational number
to its constant zero-cochain. Substitute these maps and
$R\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)=\mathbb Z[0]$ into
Theorem~\ref{FL:thm:derived-finite}. The indicated sign change gives
exactly \eqref{FL:eq:relative-complex} and
\eqref{FL:eq:relative-sheaf-complex}; it does not change their maps on
degree-zero constants.
The kernel of $d_C^0$ consists of functions equal along every
comparable pair, hence constant on each connected component of the
comparability graph. Those graph components are exactly the connected
components of the finite space: a union of graph components is open
and closed, whereas a partition into two open-and-closed sets cannot
separate a comparable pair. Thus that kernel is $\RQ^c$.
The constant integer map is injective since $S$ is nonempty. Taking
cohomology now gives \eqref{FL:eq:relative-groups}. Stalkwise the original
local domain embeds into its generic field, and the same calculation
of the kernel and higher differentials gives
\eqref{FL:eq:relative-cohomology-sheaves}. This proves the derived
formulas and all connecting maps from the actual sheaf data.
\end{proof}

There is a canonical diagonal inclusion of the one-point relative
complex
\[
 [\mathbb Z\hookrightarrow\RQ]\longrightarrow K_S^\bullet,
 \tag{FL23}\label{FL:eq:diagonal-inclusion}
\]
which is the identity on $\mathbb Z$, the all-points constant map
on $\RQ$, and zero in higher degrees. At the sheaf level this is
$[\mathcal O_X\hookrightarrow\RQ_X]\to Ri^!\mathcal U_S$.
Its quotient is the shifted reduced support complex
\[
 \widetilde C^0=C^0/\Delta\RQ,\qquad
 \widetilde C^n=C^n\ (n\ge1),
 \quad\text{placed starting in relative degree one}.
 \tag{FL24}\label{FL:eq:reduced-support-complex}
\]
Thus the original principal parts and the complete remaining support
complex are linked by an explicit short exact sequence of complexes.
For any specified support point $s$, evaluation of $C^0$ at $s$,
zero in positive support degrees, and the identity on $\mathbb Z$
give a retraction of \eqref{FL:eq:diagonal-inclusion}. It is a cochain
map because the target has no positive support terms. This splits
the complexes once that point is chosen. The diagonal inclusion
itself requires no chosen point.

\subsection{Every Boolean branch and every deletion map}

Let $I$ be a nonempty finite set of cardinality $r$ and take
$L=\mathcal P(I)=\mathbb B^r$. Its prime lattice ideals are
$\mathfrak a_b=\{A\subset I:b\notin A\}$ for $b\in I$.
Indeed a meet-prime ideal must omit some atom; it cannot omit two
distinct atoms because their intersection is empty, and any ideal
omitting the atom $\{b\}$ must contain every other atom. Hence these
are all primes. They are incomparable, so $S_I$ is discrete.

\begin{theorem}[The complete Boolean relative object]
\label{FL:thm:boolean-relative}
For $Y_I=S_I\triangleright\Spec\mathbb Z$ the universal arithmetic
coupling has
\[
 Ri^!\mathcal U_I=[\mathcal O_X\xrightarrow\Delta\RQ_X^I],
 \qquad H_X^1(Y_I,\mathcal U_I)=\RQ^I/\Delta\mathbb Z,
 \tag{FL25}\label{FL:eq:boolean-relative}
\]
and all other relative cohomology groups vanish. Its exact canonical
sequence is
\[
 0\longrightarrow\RQ/\mathbb Z
 \xrightarrow{\ d\ }\RQ^I/\Delta\mathbb Z
 \xrightarrow{\ q\ }\RQ^I/\Delta\RQ\longrightarrow0,
 \quad d(a+\mathbb Z)=[(a)_{b\in I}],\quad q([v])=v+\Delta\RQ.
 \tag{FL26}\label{FL:eq:boolean-sequence}
\]
For a chosen branch $b\in I$, the branch map is
$\beta_b([v])=v_b+\mathbb Z$ and a splitting of the sequence is
\[
 v+\Delta\RQ\longmapsto[v-v_b\mathbf1_I].
 \tag{FL27}\label{FL:eq:branch-splitting}
\]
In particular the explicit branch coordinates are
\[
 \RQ^I/\Delta\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{\sim}
 \RQ/\mathbb Z\oplus\RQ^{I\setminus\{b\}},\qquad
 [v]\longmapsto(v_b+\mathbb Z,(v_a-v_b)_{a\ne b}).
 \tag{FL28}\label{FL:eq:branch-coordinates}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For discrete $S_I$, the support complex is $\RQ^I$ in degree zero
and zero elsewhere. Theorem~\ref{FL:thm:whole-relative-complex} gives
\eqref{FL:eq:boolean-relative}. In \eqref{FL:eq:boolean-sequence}, the
first map is injective because a diagonal vector is an integer
diagonal exactly when its coefficient is an integer. The kernel of
the second consists exactly of rational diagonal vectors modulo
integer diagonals; the second map is surjective. This proves the
sequence. Changing a representative $v$ by a rational diagonal
changes $v_b$ by the same rational, so
$v-v_b\mathbf1_I$ is unchanged. Its class projects to
$v+\Delta\RQ$, proving the splitting. Coordinate evaluation is well
defined modulo integers and is a left inverse of $d$. Finally an
inverse to \eqref{FL:eq:branch-coordinates} sends
$(a+\mathbb Z,(u_c)_{c\ne b})$ to the class of the vector with entry
$a$ at $b$ and entry $a+u_c$ at $c$; changing $a$ by an integer changes
the vector by an integer diagonal. These checks prove every formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Actual branch, deletion, and duplication morphisms]
\label{FL:prop:deletions}
For a map of nonempty finite sets $f:J\to I$, inverse image of subsets
is a bounded lattice homomorphism $\mathcal P(I)\to\mathcal P(J)$.
It induces the original semiring map
$\G_{\mathcal P(I)}(\mathbb Z)\to\G_{\mathcal P(J)}(\mathbb Z)$,
with unchanged supported coefficients, and the corresponding map of
full spectra is
\[
 f_Y:Y_J\to Y_I,\qquad f_Y|_{S_J}=f,\quad f_Y|_X=\mathrm{id}_X.
 \tag{FL29}\label{FL:eq:branch-topology-map}
\]
The pullback of universal arithmetic sheaves is the universal sheaf
on $Y_J$. Its induced relative map is exactly
\[
 \RQ^I/\Delta\mathbb Z\longrightarrow\RQ^J/\Delta\mathbb Z,
 \qquad [v]\longmapsto[(v_{f(b)})_{b\in J}].
 \tag{FL30}\label{FL:eq:branch-cohomology-map}
\]
For a nonempty subset $J\subset I$ this is the deletion projection,
with the exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow\RQ^{I\setminus J}
 \longrightarrow\RQ^I/\Delta\mathbb Z
 \longrightarrow\RQ^J/\Delta\mathbb Z\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{FL31}\label{FL:eq:deletion-kernel}
\]
The first map inserts zeros on $J$. For arbitrary $f$, the kernel
is $\RQ^{I\setminus f(J)}$ by the same insertion, and the cokernel is
$\RQ^J/f^*\RQ^I$; it consists of the differences between duplicated
coordinates in each fibre of $f$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The preimage operation preserves bottom, top, intersections, and
unions, so \eqref{FL:eq:operations} verifies the semiring map. The
inverse image of the lattice prime at $b\in J$ is the lattice prime
at $f(b)$; arithmetic primes contract to themselves. This proves
\eqref{FL:eq:branch-topology-map} on the entire spectrum, not only on
one stratum. A mixed open pulls back to the mixed open with the same
arithmetic part; the constant rational support data pulls back by
the same coordinate map. Thus it identifies the universal sheaves
and gives the map of relative complexes that is the identity on
$\mathbb Z$ and $f^*$ on degree one. This proves
\eqref{FL:eq:branch-cohomology-map}.

For inclusion of $J$, a kernel element restricts to one common integer
on $J$. Subtract its integer diagonal to give zero on $J$, uniquely
because $J$ is nonempty; all remaining coordinates are free rationals.
Surjectivity is extension of a vector by zeros outside $J$. This proves
\eqref{FL:eq:deletion-kernel}. The same reasoning uses $f(J)$ in the
general case. Since $\Delta\mathbb Z\subset f^*\RQ^I$, taking the
quotient of the target gives the stated cokernel. More explicitly it
is $\bigoplus_{a\in f(J)}\RQ^{f^{-1}(a)}/\Delta\RQ$.
\end{proof}

Deleting all support points is a different endpoint: the remaining
space is $X$, its relative complex is $\mathbb Z[0]$, and the map of
complexes from \eqref{FL:eq:boolean-relative} is the identity in degree
zero and zero in degree one. Its relative degree-one target is zero.
The kernel formula \eqref{FL:eq:deletion-kernel} was stated only for
nonempty $J$ and is not extended incorrectly to this endpoint.

\subsection{A finite lattice whose support has a cycle}

Let $P$ have six elements
\[
 v_1,v_2,v_3,e_{12},e_{23},e_{31},
\]
and exactly the strict relations
\[
 v_1<e_{12},\ v_2<e_{12},\ v_2<e_{23},\ v_3<e_{23},\
 v_3<e_{31},\ v_1<e_{31}.
 \tag{FL32}\label{FL:eq:triangle-relations}
\]
These are the vertices and edges of the boundary of a triangle ordered
by face inclusion; there is no filled triangular face. Set
$L=\Down(P)$, with union as join and intersection as meet.

\begin{proposition}[The eighteen-element lattice and its exact spectrum]
\label{FL:prop:triangle-lattice}
The lattice $L$ has exactly eighteen elements. For any finite poset
$P$, the map
\[
 p\longmapsto\mathfrak a_p=\{D\in\Down(P):p\notin D\}
 \tag{FL33}\label{FL:eq:poset-primes}
\]
is a homeomorphism $P\to\SpecLat\Down(P)$ when $P$ has its lower-set
topology. It preserves the stated specialization order: $p\le q$
if and only if $\mathfrak a_p\subseteq\mathfrak a_q$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A downset in the triangle poset first chooses a subset $V$ of its
three vertices and then an arbitrary subset of those edges whose
two endpoints lie in $V$. For $|V|=0,1,2,3$, the numbers of choices
are respectively $1,3,3\cdot2,2^3$. Their sum is
$1+3+6+8=18$.

For a general finite $P$, each displayed $\mathfrak a_p$ is a proper
lattice ideal and is prime: membership in a union is downward-ideal
closure, and if $p$ belongs to both downsets it belongs to their
intersection. To show exhaustion, let $h:\Down(P)\to\{0,1\}$ be the
indicator of the complement of a prime ideal. It preserves union
and intersection by the ideal and prime conditions. Every nonempty
downset is the union of the principal downsets of its members.
Choose a minimal $p$ for which $h(\mathord\downarrow p)=1$; one exists
because $h(P)=1$. If $h(\mathord\downarrow q)=1$, then
$h(\mathord\downarrow p\cap\mathord\downarrow q)=1$. Decomposing that
intersection into principal downsets yields some $r\le p,q$ with
$h(\mathord\downarrow r)=1$. Minimality forces $r=p$, so $p\le q$.
It follows that for every downset $D$, $h(D)=1$ exactly when $p\in D$.
This proves exhaustion and uniqueness. The basic spectral open at a
downset $D$ is exactly $\{\mathfrak a_p:p\in D\}$, which proves the
homeomorphism. Finally if $p\le q$, absence of $p$ from a downset
implies absence of $q$, proving the ideal inclusion. Conversely if
$p\not\le q$, the downset $\mathord\downarrow q$ belongs to
$\mathfrak a_p$ but not $\mathfrak a_q$, disproving inclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Every triangle support cochain and every relative group]
\label{FL:thm:triangle-cohomology}
For $S=\SpecLat L$ with the lattice of
Proposition~\ref{FL:prop:triangle-lattice}, the full constant-rational
support complex is
\[
 C^0=\RQ^6\xrightarrow{D}C^1=\RQ^6,
 \qquad C^n=0\quad(n\ge2),
 \tag{FL34}\label{FL:eq:triangle-complex}
\]
where the degree-zero coordinates are
$(a_1,a_2,a_3,b_{12},b_{23},b_{31})$, the degree-one coordinates are
in exactly the order of \eqref{FL:eq:triangle-relations}, and
\[
 D(a,b)=(b_{12}-a_1,b_{12}-a_2,b_{23}-a_2,
                b_{23}-a_3,b_{31}-a_3,b_{31}-a_1).
 \tag{FL35}\label{FL:eq:triangle-differential}
\]
Its cohomology is $H^0(S,\RQ)=\RQ$ by constant sections and
$H^1(S,\RQ)=\RQ$ by the quotient map
\[
 \omega(c)=c_1-c_2+c_3-c_4+c_5-c_6.
 \tag{FL36}\label{FL:eq:cycle-functional}
\]
The actual relative complex over $\Spec\mathbb Z$ is
\[
 \mathbb Z\xrightarrow{z\mapsto(z,z,z,z,z,z)}\RQ^6
 \xrightarrow{D}\RQ^6
 \quad\text{in degrees }0,1,2.
 \tag{FL37}\label{FL:eq:triangle-relative-complex}
\]
It has precisely
\[
 H_X^1(Y,\mathcal U_S)=\RQ/\mathbb Z,\qquad
 H_X^2(Y,\mathcal U_S)=\RQ,
 \qquad H_X^n(Y,\mathcal U_S)=0\ (n\ne1,2).
 \tag{FL38}\label{FL:eq:triangle-relative-groups}
\]
At the sheaf level $R^1i^!\mathcal U_S=\PP_X$ and
$R^2i^!\mathcal U_S=\RQ_X$, with no other cohomology sheaves.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The six points and the six relations in \eqref{FL:eq:triangle-relations}
are all strict chains of lengths zero and one. There is no strict
chain of length two. Theorem~\ref{FL:thm:finite-cochains} therefore gives
exactly \eqref{FL:eq:triangle-complex} and
\eqref{FL:eq:triangle-differential}. Its kernel forces equal values at
every incident vertex and edge, hence forces all six values equal.
Substitution gives $\omega D=0$.

Conversely let $c\in\RQ^6$ satisfy $\omega(c)=0$. Put
\[
\begin{aligned}
 a_1&=0,& b_{12}&=c_1,& a_2&=c_1-c_2,\\
 b_{23}&=c_1-c_2+c_3,&a_3&=c_1-c_2+c_3-c_4,\\
 b_{31}&=c_1-c_2+c_3-c_4+c_5.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{FL39}\label{FL:eq:triangle-preimage}
\]
The first five equations of $D(a,b)=c$ hold by direct substitution;
the sixth holds because $\omega(c)=0$. Thus $\operatorname{im}D$
is exactly $\ker\omega$. The functional $\omega$ is surjective,
as is seen on $(1,0,0,0,0,0)$, proving the support cohomology and
fixing its generator and orientation.

Insert this complete support complex into
Theorem~\ref{FL:thm:whole-relative-complex}. The resulting relative
differentials are precisely \eqref{FL:eq:triangle-relative-complex}.
Its first cohomology consists of a rational constant six-vector
modulo an integer constant six-vector, identified by $q\mapsto q+\mathbb Z$.
Its second cohomology is the quotient by $D$, identified by
\eqref{FL:eq:cycle-functional}. Its degree-zero kernel is zero, and
there are no further terms. The sheaf-level calculation has the
same support matrix, with the initial diagonal
$\mathcal O_X\hookrightarrow\RQ_X$; its first quotient is
$\RQ_X/\mathcal O_X=\PP_X$, and its second is $\RQ_X$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Maps retaining or deleting the actual cycle}

\begin{proposition}[Support maps and their relative chain maps]
\label{FL:prop:support-morphisms}
For an order-preserving map $f:S'\to S$, the map
$f_Y:S'\triangleright X\to S\triangleright X$ that is $f$ on support
and the identity on $X$ is continuous. Its inverse image takes
$\mathcal U_S$ to $\mathcal U_{S'}$. On relative complexes its map is
the identity on $\mathbb Z$ and the cochain pullback
\eqref{FL:eq:cochain-map} in all support degrees. If the support spaces
are spectra of downset lattices, this is the actual spectral map
induced by the lattice homomorphism $D\mapsto f^{-1}(D)$ and the
coefficient-preserving map of the original semirings.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The preimage of a support open is a support open because $f$ is
order preserving. The preimage of $S\cup U$ is $S'\cup U$, proving
continuity on every open. The arithmetic sheaf is unchanged and
the inverse image of a constant rational sheaf on support is the
constant rational sheaf. The generic-value map stays constant with
the same value, proving the sheaf identification. Its maps on the
relative resolutions are exactly those displayed, by the natural
augmentations, restriction maps, and the cochain computation in
Theorem~\ref{FL:thm:finite-cochains}. The cochain identity was checked
including collapsed chains after \eqref{FL:eq:cochain-map}.
Finally $f^{-1}$ preserves unions and intersections and both bounds.
The preimage of the prime at $s'$, applied to a downset $D$, tests
$s'\notin f^{-1}D$, which is exactly $f(s')\notin D$. Thus its
spectral map is $f$, while supported coefficients are unchanged.
This proves the last assertion on both strata.
\end{proof}

For the triangle, delete the maximal element $e_{31}$ to obtain the
lower open subposet
\[
 T=\{v_1,v_2,v_3,e_{12},e_{23}\}.
\]
Its support cochains are $\RQ^5\to\RQ^4$, with the first four
coordinates of \eqref{FL:eq:triangle-differential}. The explicit
preimage formulas for these four coordinates are the first five
assigned quantities in \eqref{FL:eq:triangle-preimage}; hence that
differential is surjective and its kernel is the rational constants.
The relative groups for $T$ are therefore $\RQ/\mathbb Z$ in degree
one and zero in degree two. The actual inclusion map
$T\triangleright X\to S\triangleright X$ pulls back the relative
complex by deleting $b_{31}$ from degree one and $c_5,c_6$ from
degree two. It induces the identity on the principal-parts
$\RQ/\mathbb Z$ and the zero map from the original cycle $\RQ$.
There is no claim that $T\triangleright X$ is an open subset of the
whole space: it is the indicated continuous subspace embedding,
and the maps are the proved pullbacks of pairs.

The map $S\to\{*\}$ gives a map from the triangle full space to the
one-point full space. Its relative pullback is the canonical diagonal
inclusion \eqref{FL:eq:diagonal-inclusion}. Choosing any point
$*\to S$ gives the evaluation retraction. Thus the ordinary
principal-parts contribution is retained with exact section and
retraction maps, while the new degree-two support cycle is kept in
the kernel of each branch evaluation. These statements specify the
full morphisms, not merely a comparison of dimensions.

\subsection{Scope and proof provenance}

The original coefficient/support definition and its complete prime
classification were read in the existing programme sources
\texttt{01\_spectra.tex}, the theorem
\texttt{thm:rebuilt-full-support}, and
\texttt{08\_full\_stalks.tex}, formulas (TL1)--(TL5). They are
reproved in Proposition~\ref{FL:prop:ordinal-spectrum} to fix the topology
and prevent a change of support object. The earlier independent
\path{split_prime_recollement_pairing.tex}, formulas (RP3)--(RP12)
and (RP21)--(RP25), treated a single extra point and its principal
parts. The present proofs retain the entire finite support and the
higher derived support sections, and do not infer them from that
one-point calculation.

The extra groups in \eqref{FL:eq:relative-groups} and
\eqref{FL:eq:triangle-relative-groups} are actual derived invariants of
the original full-support prime topology and the specified universal
arithmetic coupling. Their receiving category is the abelian-sheaf
category of that topology. They have not been given an incompatible
additive-group semiring-module action. The proofs construct no
Frobenius endomorphism and assign no weight to the new cycle; such
an endomorphism and its trace would have to be computed from actual
maps on the displayed complexes. No Riemann-hypothesis implication
is asserted here.
