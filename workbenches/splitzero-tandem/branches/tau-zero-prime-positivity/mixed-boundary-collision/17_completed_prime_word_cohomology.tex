\section{The completed prime-word cohomology and its exact image}
\label{sec:completed-prime-word}
The quotient (AQ3) now permits an actual image calculation, rather
than a proposed regularity assumption. The source is the prime-word
subspace of the relative operator (AQ2), with its physical valuation
index $m\in\mathbb Z$. We compute its finite-support and rapid-decay
cohomology, the map between them, and the full relative object
recording the classes that this map kills. This is a local comparison
built from the split relative-prime operator. Its construction is not
identified with global arithmetic compactly supported cohomology.

\subsection{Three exact coefficient complexes}
Keep $E,G_N,k,p,F_p,B_p$ from (AQ1), with their original factors.
For an arbitrary invertible complex matrix $B$ on $E$, let
\[
 \begin{array}{lll}
 \Phi=E^{(\mathbb Z)},&
 \mathscr S=\mathscr S(\mathbb Z;E),&
 \Pi=E^{\mathbb Z},\\
 (sv)_m=v_{m-1},&& d_B=sI-B.
 \end{array}
 \tag{PC1}
\]
The first is finite-support sequences, the second is exactly (AQ11),
and the third is all sequences, with no decay imposed. The differential
is $(d_Bv)_m=v_{m-1}-Bv_m$ in each case. Put each complex in degrees
zero and one. Its degree action is $p^{k/2}s$ on both terms; this
commutes with $d_B$, so it induces an action on cohomology. For $B=B_p$
this is the actual tensor relative-prime degree in (AQ4).

The finite complex has the exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow\Phi\xrightarrow{d_B}\Phi
 \xrightarrow{\Psi_B}E\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \Psi_B(v)=\sum_mB^mv_m.
 \tag{PC2}
\]
Surjectivity and the kernel equality are (AQ3) and (AQ5). Injectivity
of $d_B$ follows directly: for a nonzero finite sequence, take its
largest nonzero index $m$; then $(d_Bv)_{m+1}=v_m\ne0$.
Consequently its cohomology is $E$ in degree one and zero in degree
zero. The degree action on that cohomology is $p^{k/2}B$ by (AQ4).

At the other endpoint the all-sequence complex has
\[
 H^0(\Pi\xrightarrow{d_B}\Pi)
   =\{(B^{-m}v)_{m\in\mathbb Z}:v\in E\}\simeq E,
 \qquad H^1(\Pi\xrightarrow{d_B}\Pi)=0.
 \tag{PC3}
\]
The homogeneous recurrence is exactly $v_m=B^{-1}v_{m-1}$, proving
the kernel formula in both directions. For a prescribed sequence
$w$, choose $v_0=0$, solve
$v_m=B^{-1}(v_{m-1}-w_m)$ successively for $m\ge1$, and solve
$v_{m-1}=w_m+Bv_m$ successively for $m\le0$. This produces a
two-sided sequence with $d_Bv=w$ and proves surjectivity. The degree
action on its kernel again corresponds to $p^{k/2}B$, since
$(sv)_m=B^{-(m-1)}v=B^{-m}Bv$. Thus the full algebraic receiver
retains every original eigenvalue, in its stated cohomological degree.

\subsection{Rapid sequences and smooth division on the original circle}
The Fourier series map is
\[
 \mathcal F:\mathscr S\xrightarrow{\sim}C^\infty(\mathbb T;E),
 \qquad (\mathcal Fv)(z)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}z^mv_m,
 \quad |z|=1.
 \tag{PC4}
\]
For a rapid sequence each differentiated series converges absolutely
and uniformly, since multiplication of its $m$th coefficient by any
power of $m$ still gives an absolutely summable sequence. Conversely
the coefficient formula
$v_m=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_0^{2\pi}f(e^{i\theta})e^{-im\theta}\,d\theta$
and integration by parts any number of times give every seminorm in
(AQ11). The Fourier coefficients determine the smooth function: for
example convolution with the finite Fejer kernels, whose coefficients
are $1-|m|/(M+1)$ for $|m|\le M$, tends uniformly to a continuous
periodic function. This follows directly from their nonnegativity,
integral one, and the formula
$(M+1)^{-1}|\sum_{j=0}^Me^{ij\theta}|^2$, which tends uniformly to
zero outside any neighbourhood of zero. Thus zero coefficients force
the function to be zero, and the two displayed operations are inverse.
These estimates also prove continuity in both smooth and sequence
seminorms. In these exact coordinates, $d_B$ is multiplication by
$zI-B$.

Let $E_\lambda$ be the generalized eigenspace of $B$ at $\lambda$,
$P_\lambda$ its spectral projection, and
$N_\lambda=(B-\lambda I)|_{E_\lambda}$ with nilpotence index
$d_\lambda$. The decomposition and its projections are algebraic:
Bezout's identity for the pairwise coprime powers
$(t-\lambda)^{d_\lambda}$ gives their Chinese remainder idempotents,
evaluated at $B$. This proves the direct-sum decomposition and does
not require semisimplicity or distinct eigenvalues. Put
\[
 E_\circ=\bigoplus_{|\lambda|=1}E_\lambda,
 \quad E_{\rm off}=\bigoplus_{|\lambda|\ne1}E_\lambda,
 \quad P_\circ=\sum_{|\lambda|=1}P_\lambda.
 \tag{PC5}
\]
For $z=e^{i\theta}$, write $\partial_z=(iz)^{-1}\partial_\theta$.
This is a specified complex differential operator on smooth functions
on the circle; it is not an assumption of a holomorphic extension.
Define the complete jet receiver
\[
 \mathcal J_B(f)=
 \sum_{|\lambda|=1}\sum_{j=0}^{d_\lambda-1}
   \frac{N_\lambda^j}{j!}
   (\partial_z^j P_\lambda f)(\lambda)\ \in E_\circ.
 \tag{PC6}
\]
The sum of finitely many derivative evaluations is continuous. For
a constant function $f=v\in E_\circ$, it has value $v$, so it is
surjective.

\begin{theorem}[The entire rapid-decay cohomology]
\label{thm:rapid-prime-cohomology}
There is an exact sequence of complex vector spaces and continuous maps
\[
 0\longrightarrow C^\infty(\mathbb T;E)
 \xrightarrow{zI-B} C^\infty(\mathbb T;E)
 \xrightarrow{\mathcal J_B}E_\circ\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{PC7}
\]
The first map has closed range. Consequently the complex on
$\mathscr S$ in (PC1) has $H^0=0$, $H^1=E_\circ$; no off-circle
eigenspace or unit-circle Jordan block is omitted from this assertion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Away from the finitely many unit-circle eigenvalues, $zI-B$ is
invertible. A smooth function in its kernel vanishes on that dense
open set and hence everywhere, proving injectivity.

On a primary component with $|\lambda|\ne1$, the inverse
$\sum_{j=0}^{d_\lambda-1}(z-\lambda)^{-j-1}N_\lambda^j$
is smooth on the entire circle. There is therefore no cokernel
contribution from that component.

For $|\lambda|=1$, a smooth component $f_\lambda$ has a local finite
expansion
\[
 f_\lambda(z)=\sum_{r=0}^{d_\lambda-1}a_r(z-\lambda)^r
           +(z-\lambda)^{d_\lambda}g(z),
 \quad a_r=\frac{\partial_z^rf_\lambda(\lambda)}{r!},
 \tag{PC8}
\]
with $g$ smooth. To justify this for smooth, possibly nonholomorphic
data, use a real coordinate $t=\theta-\theta_0$. The quotient
$(z(t)-\lambda)/t$ is smooth and nonzero at zero. The fundamental
theorem of calculus gives a smooth quotient by $t$ for every smooth
function vanishing at zero. Repeating this division gives the finite
expansion; applying $\partial_z$ successively identifies its
coefficients as the displayed derivatives.

Multiplication of (PC8) by the finite inverse above has, at the
negative power $(z-\lambda)^{-l}$, exactly the coefficient
\[
 \sum_{r=0}^{d_\lambda-l}N_\lambda^{r+l-1}a_r
  =N_\lambda^{l-1}\sum_{r=0}^{d_\lambda-1}N_\lambda^r a_r,
 \qquad 1\le l\le d_\lambda.
 \tag{PC9}
\]
Terms added on the right have powers at least $d_\lambda$ and are
zero. The coefficient for $l=1$ is precisely the $\lambda$ component
of (PC6); all other negative coefficients are its displayed multiples.
Thus if (PC6) vanishes, the inverse applied to $f_\lambda$ has a
smooth extension at $\lambda$. It is smooth elsewhere already and
solves $(zI-B)g=f$ on the whole circle. Conversely, if $f=(zI-B)g$
with smooth $g$, its inverse away from the point is that smooth
function. A nonzero highest negative Laurent power in (PC9) is
unbounded in some linear coordinate as $t\to0$; hence all negative
coefficients must vanish, including $l=1$. This proves that the
kernel of (PC6) is exactly the range. Surjectivity was proved after
(PC6), completing exactness. The range is closed as the kernel of a
continuous map to a finite-dimensional space.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The comparison image and every lost class}
For a finite Laurent polynomial $f(z)=\sum z^mv_m$, the terminating
Taylor formula on each primary block, including negative powers,
gives
\[
 \mathcal J_B(f)=P_\circ\sum_mB^mv_m=P_\circ\Psi_B(v).
 \tag{PC10}
\]
For negative powers this is exactly the inverse-block binomial
calculation (AQ13). Hence the inclusion of finite sequences into
rapid sequences induces the fully evaluated cohomology map
\[
 \boxed{H^1(\Phi\xrightarrow{d_B}\Phi)=E
       \xrightarrow{\ P_\circ\ }
       H^1(\mathscr S\xrightarrow{d_B}\mathscr S)=E_\circ.}
 \tag{PC11}
\]
Its image is the entire right side, its kernel is $E_{\rm off}$, and
its cokernel is zero. Its degree action is the actual
$p^{k/2}B|_{E_\circ}$. Every eigenvalue on the image has absolute
value $p^{k/2}$. This proves a local purity-circle image statement for
these specified complexes. It does not assert the arithmetic
integrality of these eigenvalues or Deligne's purity at every
embedding of an algebraic coefficient field.

The relative object of this completion is also completely determined.
Let $C_\Phi,C_{\mathscr S}$ denote the two complexes in (PC1).
The homotopy fibre of their actual inclusion has
\[
 H^1\bigl(\operatorname{Cone}(C_\Phi\to C_{\mathscr S})[-1]\bigr)
       =E_{\rm off},\qquad H^j=0\quad(j\ne1).
 \tag{PC12}
\]
Indeed the long exact sequence of the explicit cone uses the
cohomology already proved, and its only nonzero portion is
$0\to H^1\to E\xrightarrow{P_\circ}E_\circ\to H^2\to0$.
The last map is surjective, so the assertion follows with its exact
kernel. This retains the removed classes as a derived object with
their unchanged original degree action $p^{k/2}B|_{E_{\rm off}}$.
The finite, rapid, and all-sequence constructions (PC2), (PC7), and
(PC3) therefore specify both which receiver retains each class and
its cohomological degree. No class is declared nonexistent merely
because one completion does not receive it.

For the actual arithmetic family (AQ1) with $\delta>0$, odd $k$
implies $2a-k\ne0$ for every original root. Thus (AQ7) gives
\[
 E_\circ=0,\qquad E_{\rm off}=E,
 \quad H^1(C_{\mathscr S})=0,
 \quad H^1\bigl(\operatorname{Cone}(C_\Phi\to C_{\mathscr S})[-1]\bigr)=E.
 \tag{PC13}
\]
This is a calculation for the displayed hypothetical off-line family.
The finite source and the all-sequence kernel still contain all $q$
dimensions and their original phases. It is not a contradiction
unless another proved geometric map requires a nonzero such class to
survive precisely the comparison (PC11).

At $\delta=0$, keep every repeated root and nilpotent coefficient of
(AQ15). All eigenvalues of $B_p$ have modulus one, including any
coincident phases at the selected prime. Hence $E_\circ=E$, and
(PC11) is the identity on the original $q$-dimensional coefficient
algebra. The receiver (PC6) retains derivatives through order $k$
on the size-$k+1$ blocks of (AQ15). When different blocks have the
same exponential eigenvalue, their direct sum is the primary space
used in (PC6); no block is merged into a single vector or discarded.
The full relative object (PC12) is then acyclic.

\subsection{What has been supplied for the global purity problem}
The construction uses the additional arithmetic prime through its
relative principal parts, the actual $p$-to-one multiplication, and
the resulting $k$-fold degree action. Its comparison is now a proved
map with a computed image, kernel, and full collision specialization.
The point at which Deligne's method would require additional global
information is explicit: a geometric compact-to-ordinary map must
be related to this local completion by actual chain maps, retaining
the two pole classes, the Archimedean contribution, and the original
observation and conductor maps. The affine comparison (AQ17), whose
image was calculated as zero, is not substituted for those maps.
Equations (PC11)--(PC13) prevent a false shortcut: choosing a
completion that keeps only a purity circle proves the stated image
calculation, but does not by itself prove that a class from the
original global trace has to survive it. The exact relative object
where such a class can reside is (PC12), not an unrecorded error term.
