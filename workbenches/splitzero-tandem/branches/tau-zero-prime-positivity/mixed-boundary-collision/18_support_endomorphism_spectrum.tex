\section{The exact spectrum of support operations and transfers}


\subsection{The full support object and the endomorphism to be computed}

Let $L$ be a finite nontrivial bounded distributive lattice,
$S=\operatorname{SpecLat}L$, $X=\Spec\Z$, and
$Y=S\triangleright X$. The opens of $Y$ are the opens of $S$ and
the sets $S\cup U$ with nonempty open $U\subset X$. This is the
entire spectrum of the original semiring
\[
 \G_L(\Z)=\{(0,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}
       \cup\{(a,1_L):a\in\Z\}.
 \tag{SE1}\label{eq:original-space}
\]
The supported zero $e=(0,1_L)$ and the additive identity
$\tau=(0,0_L)$ remain distinct; $D(e)=S$ and $V(e)=X$.
We use the original specialization order: $s\le t$ means that $s$
generalizes $t$, so finite opens are lower sets.

For completeness, the passage from the full lattice to this finite
space loses no lattice element. An element $j\ne0$ is join
irreducible if $j=a\vee b$ forces $j=a$ or $j=b$. Distributivity
makes it join prime: if $j\le a\vee b$, then
$j=(j\wedge a)\vee(j\wedge b)$, so $j\le a$ or $j\le b$.
Every lattice element is the join of the join irreducibles below
it, by induction in the finite order. Each $j$ defines the prime
ideal $I_j=\{\lambda:j\not\le\lambda\}$. Conversely, the complement
of a prime ideal is a nonempty finite filter; its meet $j$ generates
it, and primeness makes $j$ join prime and nonzero. Thus these are
exactly all prime ideals. Consequently
\[
 L\longrightarrow\Down(S),\qquad
 \lambda\longmapsto D(\lambda)=\{I_j:j\le\lambda\}
\]
is an isomorphism: joins and meets give union and intersection,
injectivity follows from the join decomposition, and a downset of
join irreducibles is obtained from the join of its elements.
For the last assertion, a join prime below that finite join lies
below one member and hence lies in the downset. An order map on
$S$ therefore gives an actual lattice map on this original $L$
by inverse image of downsets. No Boolean replacement of $L$ is made.

The receiving category in this note is abelian sheaves on the whole
space $Y$. A sheaf is given by
\[
 (\mathcal A,\mathcal B,\phi),\qquad
 \phi:\mathcal A\longrightarrow c_X\Gamma(S,\mathcal B),
 \tag{SE2}\label{eq:gluing}
\]
where $c_X$ is the constant sheaf on the irreducible arithmetic space.
Its mixed-open sections are $\mathcal A(U)$ and its support-open
sections are $\mathcal B(V)$; the map $\phi$ gives their restrictions.
This receiving category is not silently replaced by additive-group
modules over the split semiring sheaf. That latter action kills its
additively idempotent support stalks, as proved in (RP17)--(RP20) of
\path{split_prime_recollement_pairing.tex}.

Let $\Lambda$ be a finite free abelian group and set
$V=\Lambda\otimes_{\Z}\Q$. The actual universal coefficient sheaf is
\[
 \mathcal U_{S,\Lambda}
   =(\mathcal O_X\otimes_{\Z}\Lambda,V_S,
                 \text{constant generic restriction}).
 \tag{SE3}\label{eq:universal-coefficients}
\]
Every point of the support receives the same original generic value.
For $\Lambda=\Z$ this is the previously constructed universal
arithmetic coupling, with no branch selected.

For constant rational coefficients define the full strict-chain complex
\[
 C^q(S,\Q)=\prod_{s_0<\cdots<s_q}\Q,
 \quad
 (dc)_{s_0<\cdots<s_{q+1}}
   =\sum_{a=0}^{q+1}(-1)^a
         c_{s_0<\cdots<\widehat{s_a}<\cdots<s_{q+1}}.
 \tag{SE4}\label{eq:support-complex}
\]
Its exact relative arithmetic complex is
\[
 K_{S,\Lambda}^0=\Lambda,\qquad
 K_{S,\Lambda}^{q+1}=C^q(S,\Q)\otimes_\Q V,\qquad
 d^0(\lambda)=(\lambda)_{s\in S},\quad d^{q+1}=d_C\otimes1.
 \tag{SE5}\label{eq:relative-complex}
\]
The proof that this is the actual closed-support derived complex is
given in (FL8)--(FL22) of \path{finite_support_recollement.tex}:
full sheaf gluing gives the mapping fibre
$\Cone(R\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X\otimes\Lambda)
\to R\Gamma(S,V_S))[-1]$; the first term is $\Lambda[0]$.
The support complex is the global-sections complex of an injective
resolution: its degree-$q$ sheaf
is the product over strict chains of the direct image of the first
stalk from the chain's last point. At a point $s$, append the maximal
point $s$ to chains in $\mathord\downarrow s$ with sign $(-1)^q$;
this contracts its augmented stalk complex. Thus no support degree
has been omitted in \eqref{eq:relative-complex}. The signs shown
are obtained from the usual shifted cone by multiplying its support
coordinate in degree $q+1$ by $(-1)^q$.

\subsection{The exact cone endomorphism and its gluing constraint}

An order-preserving map $f:S\to S$ induces an actual map
$f_Y:Y\to Y$, equal to the identity on $X$ and to $f$ on $S$.
For support spectra of downset lattices it is induced by
$D\mapsto f^{-1}(D)$ on the lattice and the unchanged integer
coefficient on \eqref{eq:original-space}.
The pullback cochain map is
\[
 (f^*c)_{s_0<\cdots<s_q}=
 \begin{cases}
 c_{f(s_0)<\cdots<f(s_q)},&\text{the image points are distinct},\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{SE6}\label{eq:finite-pullback}
\]
It commutes with $d$: on a chain with an adjacent pair of equal image
points, the two terms deleting that pair cancel, and all other terms
retain a repetition; on a chain without such a repetition it is the
usual alternating-face identity. The maps are functorial under
composition, including noninjective maps.

\begin{theorem}[An endomorphism of the actual sheaf and its cone]
\label{thm:cone-action}
Let $A\in\operatorname{End}_{\Z}(\Lambda)$ and let $A_V$ be its
rational extension. The maps $A$ on the arithmetic coefficients
and $A_V$ on the pulled-back constant support sheaf define a morphism
$f_Y^{-1}\mathcal U_{S,\Lambda}\to\mathcal U_{S,\Lambda}$.
On the exact relative complex its endomorphism is
\[
 T_{A,f}^0=A,\qquad
 T_{A,f}^{q+1}=f^*\otimes A_V.
 \tag{SE7}\label{eq:actual-cone-action}
\]
For a general compatible cochain diagram $u:A^\bullet\to B^\bullet$,
with endomorphisms $a,b$ satisfying $bu=ua$, the corresponding
endomorphism of $\Cone(u)[-1]$ is exactly $(a,b)$ on its two entries.
There is no extra scalar in this construction.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a regular coefficient section, taking its generic value and then
applying $A_V$ gives the generic value of its original image under $A$.
Its support restriction is constant, and pullback by any $f$ preserves
that constant section. This is precisely the commutative gluing square
in \eqref{eq:gluing}, so it constructs the sheaf morphism.
The degree-zero map of \eqref{eq:relative-complex} therefore commutes
with the constant generic inclusion. In higher degrees
$A_V$ commutes with restrictions and $f^*$ commutes with $d$, proving
\eqref{eq:actual-cone-action} is its chain endomorphism.

For the general statement use the shifted-cone convention
$R^n=A^n\oplus B^{n-1}$ with
$d_R(x,y)=(d_Ax,u(x)-d_By)$. Applying $(a,b)$ before or after this
differential gives the same result exactly when $bu=ua$.
There is no multiplication other than $a$ and $b$ in either formula.
Changing to the positive support-differential convention of
\eqref{eq:relative-complex} conjugates both endomorphisms by the stated
sign change and introduces no scalar.
\end{proof}

The absolute and relative receivers have an exact comparison as
well. Let $i:X\hookrightarrow Y$ be the arithmetic inclusion. Its
restriction functor has exact left adjoint
\[
 U_S(\mathcal A)=
 (\mathcal A,c_S\mathcal A_\eta,\text{generic constant restriction}),
\]
where $\eta$ is the original arithmetic generic point, not a point
of $S$. To check the adjunction, a map on the arithmetic component
uniquely forces the support map: under
$\operatorname{Hom}(c_S\mathcal A_\eta,\mathcal B)
=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal A_\eta,\Gamma(S,\mathcal B))$
it is its generic restriction followed by the target gluing map.
Both the generic-stalk functor and the constant-sheaf functor are
exact, which proves exactness of $U_S$. Thus $i^*$ preserves
injectives. Since $\Gamma(Y,\mathcal F)=\Gamma(X,i^*\mathcal F)$
by the triple description, injective resolutions give the exact
equivariant identity
\[
 \bigl(R\Gamma(Y,\mathcal U_{S,\Lambda}),T_{A,f}\bigr)
 \simeq\bigl(\Lambda[0],A\bigr).
 \tag{SE7a}\label{eq:absolute-comparison}
\]
Here the arithmetic acyclicity was proved in (FL18)--(FL20) by the
rational-sections and principal-parts resolution. Consequently the
absolute rational receiver has exactly the coefficient eigenvalues.
The new relative receiver is the actual cone
\eqref{eq:relative-complex}; its additional degrees, possible zero
blocks, and coefficient/support factors are calculated below. This
gives both sides of the comparison, rather than substituting an
absolute cohomology identity for the relative calculation.

\begin{proposition}[When rescaling is a proved operation]
\label{prop:rescaling}
Over a coefficient field, replacing only the support endomorphism
$b$ by $\lambda b$ while retaining $a$ defines an endomorphism of the
same cone exactly when
\[
 (\lambda-1)ua=0.
 \tag{SE8}\label{eq:scaling-constraint}
\]
In particular for the universal scalar arithmetic action $a=n\ne0$
and its injective generic map, it forces $\lambda=1$.
Multiplying both $a$ and $b$ by a scalar is instead a genuine new
coefficient operation whenever that scalar preserves their coefficient
objects. On the original scalar lattice $\Z$, such a scalar is an
integer; an invertible scalar preserving this lattice in both
directions is $1$ or $-1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The new compatibility condition is $\lambda bu=ua$.
Subtract the original equality $bu=ua$ to obtain exactly
\eqref{eq:scaling-constraint}. The injectivity and nonzero scalar
case gives the stated conclusion. Simultaneous scalar multiplication
commutes with both differentials and preserves the equality.
For a scalar preserving $\Z$, its value on $1$ must be an integer;
if its inverse preserves $\Z$ too, both it and its reciprocal are
integers, so it is $\pm1$.
\end{proof}

Thus a one-dimensional coefficient representation with an assigned
endomorphism $\lambda$ would multiply the eigenvalues by $\lambda$
because its tensor product does so. That is an additional specified
operation. Neither the new prime topology nor the cone shift supplies
that representation or an unproved Tate twist.

\subsection{All eigenvalues and the nilpotent part of a finite support map}

\begin{theorem}[The complete eventual-periodicity decomposition]
\label{thm:eventual-periodicity}
For every self-map $f$ of the finite support poset there are integers
$N\ge0$ and $m\ge1$ such that $f^{N+m}=f^N$. Consequently, on every
support cochain group and every rational support cohomology group,
\[
 F^N(F^m-I)=0,\qquad F=f^*.
 \tag{SE9}\label{eq:annihilating-polynomial}
\]
Choose $k\ge N$ divisible by $m$ and set $S_\infty=f^N(S)$.
Then $r=f^k:S\to S_\infty$ is a retraction of the inclusion
$a:S_\infty\hookrightarrow S$. The restriction $f_\infty$ is an
order automorphism of finite order dividing $m$. In each cohomological
degree,
\[
 H^q(S,\Q)=
 \underbrace{r^*H^q(S_\infty,\Q)}_{\text{periodic part}}
 \ \oplus\
 \underbrace{\ker(a^*:H^q(S,\Q)\to H^q(S_\infty,\Q))}_{\text{nilpotent part}}.
 \tag{SE10}\label{eq:periodic-decomposition}
\]
On the first part $f^*$ is intertwined with $f_\infty^*$ by $r^*$;
on the second $(f^*)^N=0$. The nonzero complex eigenvalues are roots
of unity, with no nontrivial Jordan blocks. Every remaining Jordan
block has eigenvalue zero and size at most $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every orbit of a finite function eventually reaches a cycle. Take $N$
at least the maximum number of steps before a cycle and let $m$ be
a common multiple of all cycle lengths. This proves the equality of
functions. Their pullbacks are functorial by
\eqref{eq:finite-pullback}, so the same polynomial identity holds
already on cochains and hence on cohomology.
The stable image consists of precisely the periodic points. There
$f$ is a permutation with inverse $f^{m-1}$. Both are order preserving,
so the restricted map is an order automorphism. The map $r=f^k$ fixes
every stable point and takes every point into the stable image,
giving $ra=\mathrm{id}$.

On cochains and cohomology, $E=r^*a^*=F^k$ is idempotent.
The identities $a^*r^*=I$ and $E^2=E$ give exactly the direct sum
in \eqref{eq:periodic-decomposition}. Its image is the finite-order
part, since $F^m=I$ there. To verify the nilpotence bound, note that
every $F^N v$ lies in this periodic image: the polynomial identity
makes it fixed by $F^m$, and $E$ acts as the identity on such a vector.
If $Ev=0$, then $F^Nv=EF^Nv=F^NEv=0$.
Over $\C$, the polynomial $x^m-1$ has distinct roots, since its
derivative $mx^{m-1}$ has no common root with it. It therefore
diagonalizes the periodic part with roots of unity as eigenvalues.
The other part is annihilated by $x^N$, which gives the stated zero
eigenvalues and Jordan bound.
\end{proof}

This decomposition retains both the stable image and its exact
complement, with explicit projection and connecting maps. It is not
an instruction to replace the full support by its periodic points.

It also computes all multiplicities, rather than only bounding the
possible eigenvalues. In any fixed support degree, put $H=H^q(S,\C)$,
$E=F^k$ as above, and $N_0=F|_{\ker E}$. For every $m$th root of
unity $\zeta$, define the exact projector
\[
 \Pi_\zeta=\frac1m\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\zeta^{-j}F^jE.
 \tag{SE10a}\label{eq:periodic-projectors}
\]
On a periodic eigenvector of eigenvalue $\eta$, the coefficient is
$m^{-1}\sum_j(\eta/\zeta)^j$, which is one for $\eta=\zeta$
and zero otherwise; on $\ker E$ it vanishes. Thus the multiplicity
of $\zeta$ is exactly $\operatorname{rank}\Pi_\zeta$, computable
from the finite cochain kernels and quotients in
\eqref{eq:support-complex}. Put $d_a=\dim\ker N_0^a$, so $d_0=0$
and $d_a=\dim\ker E$ for $a\ge N$. The number of nilpotent Jordan
blocks of size exactly $a\ge1$ is
\[
 b_a=2d_a-d_{a-1}-d_{a+1}.
 \tag{SE10b}\label{eq:nilpotent-block-count}
\]
Indeed a nilpotent block of size $b$ contributes $\min(a,b)$ to
$d_a$. Its first difference is one exactly for $a\le b$, and
its second difference in the displayed order is one exactly for
$a=b$. Summing over all blocks proves the formula. Therefore the
finite cochain matrices, the recurrent retraction, and these formulas
specify every eigenvalue and every Jordan block over $\C$, starting
from the original rational cohomology action.

Nonzero nilpotent blocks really occur. Let $S$ be the disjoint union
of three copies of the six-point triangle face poset. Map the first
copy isomorphically to the second, the second to the third, and every
point of the third to one of its vertices. This is order preserving.
Each triangle has rational $H^1=\Q$, with the six-incidence alternating
cycle coordinate from (FL35)--(FL39). The induced action is
\[
 F(a_1,a_2,a_3)=(a_2,a_3,0)
 \quad\text{on }H^1(S,\Q)=\Q^3.
 \tag{SE11}\label{eq:nilpotent-example}
\]
It is a single zero-eigenvalue Jordan block of size three, because
$F^3=0$ and $F^2\ne0$. The constant map on the third triangle kills
its one-cochains under \eqref{eq:finite-pullback}, which verifies
the last coordinate without a homotopy assumption.

\begin{theorem}[Combining support and coefficient spectra]
\label{thm:combined-spectrum}
Let $A$ be any endomorphism of a finite-dimensional complex coefficient
space $V$, and let $F=f^*$ act on a support cohomology group $H$.
Write $V=V_0\oplus V_\times$ for the generalized zero eigenspace and
the sum of its nonzero generalized eigenspaces. Write
$H=H_{\mathrm{nil}}\oplus H_{\mathrm{per}}$ as in
\eqref{eq:periodic-decomposition}. Then the exact zero-eigenvalue
part of $A\otimes F$ is
\[
 (V_0\otimes H_{\mathrm{per}})\oplus(V\otimes H_{\mathrm{nil}}),
 \tag{SE12}\label{eq:combined-zero}
\]
and its complementary invertible part is
$V_\times\otimes H_{\mathrm{per}}$. On that part the eigenvalues
are exactly $\lambda\zeta$, with multiplicity, where $\lambda$ is
a nonzero coefficient eigenvalue and $\zeta$ a support root of unity.
The coefficient Jordan-block sizes are retained on each support
eigenspace. In particular
\[
 |\lambda\zeta|=|\lambda|.
 \tag{SE13}\label{eq:modulus-comparison}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Both displayed decompositions are invariant. On $H_{\mathrm{per}}$
choose the eigenspace decomposition provided by
Theorem~\ref{thm:eventual-periodicity}. Each summand with eigenvalue
$\zeta$ gives the operator $\zeta A$ on $V$. This has precisely the
coefficient Jordan blocks with their eigenvalues multiplied by
$\zeta$. On $V\otimes H_{\mathrm{nil}}$, the $N$th power is zero
because $(A\otimes F)^N=A^N\otimes F^N$. On
$V_0\otimes H_{\mathrm{per}}$, a power annihilating $A|_{V_0}$
annihilates the tensor. On the remaining summand both factors are
invertible. This proves the exact generalized-zero space and every
assertion about the complementary spectrum.
\end{proof}

For completeness, the sizes of the combined zero blocks are also
fixed by the original matrices. Set $A_0=A|_{V_0}$ and
$N_0=F|_{H_{\mathrm{nil}}}$. The rank of the $a$th power on the
zero-eigenvalue space \eqref{eq:combined-zero} is exactly
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(A_0^a)\dim H_{\mathrm{per}}
  +\operatorname{rank}(A^a)\operatorname{rank}(N_0^a),
 \qquad a\ge0.
 \tag{SE12a}\label{eq:combined-nilpotent-ranks}
\]
This follows from the two invariant direct summands in that formula,
invertibility of $F^a$ on its periodic part, and
$\operatorname{rank}(B\otimes C)=\operatorname{rank}(B)
\operatorname{rank}(C)$. To see the last equality, choose bases in
which the image and kernel of each map have coordinate bases;
their tensor map has exactly the products of the nonzero image basis
vectors as an image basis. Subtract
\eqref{eq:combined-nilpotent-ranks} from the dimension of
\eqref{eq:combined-zero} and apply
\eqref{eq:nilpotent-block-count} to obtain every combined zero block.

Consequently the statement that every eigenvalue has modulus one can
change upon passing to the full support object: it acquires a failure
if a nonzero nilpotent support part is retained. Its nonzero periodic
eigenvalues do not acquire a new modulus. They retain the actual
coefficient moduli by \eqref{eq:modulus-comparison}. A coefficient
eigenvalue off the unit circle remains off it on every nonzero
periodic support channel. If that channel is annihilated by the
support map, the exact annihilated space is
\eqref{eq:combined-zero}, rather than a corrected unit-modulus mode.
If a supplied coefficient action is $p^{w/2}B$, its nonzero combined
eigenvalues are $p^{w/2}\beta\zeta$; removing that scalar would be
the specified coefficient operation $p^{-w/2}$, not a consequence
of cohomological degree.

\subsection{Relative degrees and their actual Euler determinant factors}

For a bounded finite-dimensional complex $C^\bullet$ with chain
endomorphism $T$, fix the convention
\[
 \mathcal E(C,T;t)=
 \prod_q\det(1-tH^q(T))^{(-1)^{q+1}}.
 \tag{SE14}\label{eq:euler-determinant}
\]
This rational function retains its stated convention throughout.

\begin{proposition}[Cone determinant and degree change]
\label{prop:cone-determinant}
For a compatible map $(A^\bullet,a)\to(B^\bullet,b)$ and its actual
relative cone $(R,T)$ from Theorem~\ref{thm:cone-action},
\[
 \mathcal E(R,T;t)=\mathcal E(A,a;t)/\mathcal E(B,b;t).
 \tag{SE15}\label{eq:cone-determinant}
\]
A shift $C[-1]$ inverts $\mathcal E(C,T;t)$ and retains the same
endomorphisms on the shifted cohomology spaces. More specifically,
the actual relative cohomology has equivariant exact sequences
\[
 0\to\coker(H^{q-1}A\to H^{q-1}B)\to H^qR
   \to\ker(H^qA\to H^qB)\to0.
 \tag{SE16}\label{eq:relative-eigen-subquotients}
\]
Thus its characteristic polynomial is the product of those of the
two indicated actual subquotients. The degree shift changes the
Euler exponent; kernels, cokernels, and possible extensions determine
which eigenvalues and Jordan data the cone retains.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For an invariant short exact sequence of finite-dimensional vector
spaces, a basis adapted to its subspace makes the endomorphism
upper triangular by blocks, so its determinant $\det(1-tT)$ is the
product of those on the subspace and quotient. Apply this first to
boundaries inside cycles and then to cycles inside cochains. All
boundary factors cancel in the alternating product, proving that
\eqref{eq:euler-determinant} can also be computed on cochains.
The cochains of $R$ are $A^q\oplus B^{q-1}$ with the diagonal
endomorphism $(a,b)$, so their alternating product is exactly
\eqref{eq:cone-determinant}. The shift assertion follows by replacing
$q$ by $q+1$. The long exact cohomology sequence of the cone, with
the maps induced by $u$, gives \eqref{eq:relative-eigen-subquotients}.
Its maps commute with $T$ by $bu=ua$, and the same block determinant
argument proves the characteristic-polynomial assertion.
\end{proof}

For the actual lattice complex \eqref{eq:relative-complex}, its first
relative group is
\[
 D_{S,\Lambda}=V^c/\Delta\Lambda,
 \qquad c=|\pi_0(S)|,
 \tag{SE17}\label{eq:relative-lattice-group}
\]
and its higher groups are $H^q(S,\Q)\otimes V$ in relative degree
$q+1$ for $q\ge1$. On \eqref{eq:relative-lattice-group},
\[
 [v]\longmapsto[(A_Vv_{\bar f(b)})_{b\in\pi_0(S)}],
 \tag{SE18}\label{eq:relative-lattice-action}
\]
where $\bar f$ is the actual map of connected components. Its
canonical torsion subgroup is $\Delta(V/\Lambda)$, acted on by $A$;
the rational quotient is $V^c/\Delta V$, acted on by
$\overline{f^*}\otimes A_V$. Indeed torsion in
\eqref{eq:relative-lattice-group} means that some integer multiple
of the vector is an integer-lattice diagonal, forcing it to be a
rational diagonal. This proves the assertion and retains that torsion.

After rationalizing the complex, and only in that receiver, the
torsion is killed. Its Euler determinant is then
\[
 \mathcal E(K_{S,\Lambda}\otimes\C,T_{A,f};t)
 =\prod_{q\ge0}
 \det\left(1-t(A_\C\otimes\widetilde f_q^*)\right)^{(-1)^q},
 \tag{SE19}\label{eq:relative-support-determinant}
\]
where degree zero is reduced support cohomology
$\widetilde H^0(S,\C)=H^0(S,\C)/\Delta\C$, and in positive degrees
$\widetilde H^q=H^q$. This is exactly the relative shift in
\eqref{eq:relative-complex}. Formula \eqref{eq:relative-support-determinant}
does not encode the discarded $V/\Lambda$; its group and Hilbert
receivers are treated in the next section.

For Boolean support, let the eventual cycles of the component map
have lengths $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_b$. The transient coordinates give
zero eigenvalues, and the periodic coordinates give these cycles.
Thus the exact determinant in \eqref{eq:relative-support-determinant}
is
\[
 \frac{\prod_{a=1}^b\det(1-t^{\ell_a}A_\C^{\ell_a})}
      {\det(1-tA_\C)}.
 \tag{SE20}\label{eq:boolean-determinant}
\]
For a cycle of length $\ell$, its eigenvalues are the $\ell$th roots
of unity. Taking their product against each coefficient eigenvalue
gives $\prod_{\zeta^\ell=1}(1-t\lambda\zeta)=1-(t\lambda)^\ell$,
which proves the numerator. The diagonal coefficient space is
invariant with action $A_\C$, proving the denominator. In particular
with $r$ fixed Boolean branches and scalar coefficient $a$ it is
$(1-at)^{r-1}$.

For triangle support, its rational $H^1$ is one-dimensional, with
the exact alternating incidence generator (FL36). The identity map
acts there by $1$; reflection of the triangle reverses the generator
and acts by $-1$; the constant map acts by $0$ by
\eqref{eq:finite-pullback}. With scalar coefficient $a$, the respective
Euler factors are
\[
 (1-at)^{-1},\qquad(1+at)^{-1},\qquad1.
 \tag{SE21}\label{eq:triangle-determinants}
\]
The last value does not say that the relative degree-two space has
vanished: it is still $\Q$, with zero endomorphism. These examples
compute the actual spectral effect of the topology and its map.

\subsection{Exact scalar-branch maps and the degree action (FD1)--(FD9)}

Write $K_*$ for the one-support-point version of
\eqref{eq:relative-complex}. Its diagonal inclusion in $K_S$ sends
the generic coefficient to the constant zero-cochain and is the
identity on the arithmetic lattice. Evaluation at $s\in S$, zero
on positive support degrees, gives the branch map $\beta_s:K_S\to K_*$.
The actual intertwining identities are
\[
 T_{A,f}\Delta=\Delta T_{A,*},\qquad
 \beta_sT_{A,f}=T_{A,*}\beta_{f(s)}.
 \tag{SE22}\label{eq:branch-action}
\]
They follow by substituting a constant cochain and by evaluation in
\eqref{eq:finite-pullback}. If $s$ has period $m$, iteration gives
$\beta_sT_{A,f}^m=T_{A,*}^m\beta_s$.
There is also an equivariant retraction without choosing one branch:
average evaluation over the finite stable set $S_\infty$ in degree
zero of support, use zero in positive support degrees, and use the
identity on $\Lambda$. This is a chain map: its degree-one component
composed with $d^0$ is the original inclusion $\Lambda\to V$, and
every differential out of a positive degree of the target vanishes.
It retracts $\Delta$ because the average of a constant cochain is its
original value. The stable map permutes this finite set,
so the retraction commutes with the endomorphisms. Its rational
average is taken in $V$ and sends every diagonal lattice element
back to that same lattice element. Thus it splits off the actual
scalar relative complex equivariantly while retaining the entire
remaining support complex.

Now specialize to the scalar lattice $\Lambda=\Z$ and the positive
integer coefficient $n$. Set
\[
 D_S=\Q^c/\Delta\Z,\qquad
 T=nF,\quad (Fv)_b=v_{\bar f(b)},\qquad
 J=\bar f(\pi_0(S)),\quad r=|J|.
 \tag{SE23}\label{eq:full-degree-group}
\]
Then the exact group kernel and cokernel are
\[
 \ker T\cong(n^{-1}\Z/\Z)\oplus\Q^{c-r},\qquad
 \coker T\cong\Q^c/F\Q^c.
 \tag{SE24}\label{eq:noninvertible-degree}
\]
For the kernel, the coordinates of a representative indexed by $J$
must all equal a common $k/n$, with $k\in\Z$. Subtract that rational
diagonal and retain the unrestricted coordinates off $J$. This gives
the displayed isomorphism, independent of changing the representative
by an integer diagonal. The image is $F\Q^c/\Delta\Z$, because
$n\Q=\Q$ and the diagonal is included, which proves the cokernel.
On the canonical diagonal torsion subgroup $\Q/\Z$ the action is
exactly multiplication by $n$ for every support map.

\begin{theorem}[When the full counting pullback exists]
\label{thm:counting-action}
On finitely supported functions on $D_S$, consider the actual
pullback $P_T h(d)=h(Td)$. If $\bar f$ is not a permutation, it does
not map the vector $\delta_0$ to $\ell^2(D_S)$: its support is the
infinite kernel in \eqref{eq:noninvertible-degree}. Its exact target
is then the space of all functions on $D_S$, not a bounded counting
operator.

If $\bar f$ is a permutation, $T$ is surjective with exactly $n$
points in every fibre, and
\[
 P_T^*h(u)=\sum_{Td=u}h(d),\qquad P_T^*P_T=nI.
 \tag{SE25}\label{eq:finite-degree-Hilbert}
\]
The diagonal torsion subspace is reducing, and extension by zero
$J_S:\ell^2(\Q/\Z)\to\ell^2(D_S)$ satisfies
\[
 P_TJ_S=J_SP_n,\qquad P_T^*J_S=J_SP_n^*.
 \tag{SE26}\label{eq:scalar-Hilbert-branch}
\]
For the additive receiver
$\kappa_S(\delta_d)=d\otimes1\in\C^c/\Delta\C$,
\[
 \kappa_SP_T=\overline F^{-1}\kappa_S,
 \qquad \kappa_SP_T^*=n\overline F\kappa_S.
 \tag{SE27}\label{eq:additive-receiver}
\]
When $f=\mathrm{id}$ these are exactly (FD3)--(FD5) and (FD8)--(FD9)
of \path{16_full_support_degree_and_arithmetic_quotient.tex}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The failure in the first case follows from
$P_T\delta_0=\mathbf1_{\ker T}$ and the nonzero rational summand in
\eqref{eq:noninvertible-degree}. If $\bar f$ is a permutation, $F$
is a group automorphism and commutes with multiplication by $n$.
The latter is surjective with kernel $\Delta(n^{-1}\Z)/\Delta\Z$,
of order $n$. Group the counting sum by these fibres to obtain the
adjoint and norm formulas in \eqref{eq:finite-degree-Hilbert}.

The quotient of $D_S$ by its torsion is $\Q^c/\Delta\Q$; on it
$n\overline F$ is invertible. Thus $Td$ is torsion exactly when $d$
is torsion. Every fibre above torsion stays in that subgroup, and
every other fibre stays outside it. This proves both reducing and
the two pointwise identities \eqref{eq:scalar-Hilbert-branch}.

The pullback of a basis vector is the sum of its $n$ preimages.
After additive complexification every such preimage has the same
value $n^{-1}\overline F^{-1}(u\otimes1)$, because the differences
are torsion. Their sum proves the first identity in
\eqref{eq:additive-receiver}. The adjoint sends $\delta_d$ to
$\delta_{Td}$, proving the second. The complete kernel of
$\kappa_S$ consists of the linear span of
$\delta_0$ and $\delta_{a+b}-\delta_a-\delta_b$: this is the
universal presentation of the additive tensor product. It in
particular kills the entire diagonal torsion sector. This proves
the receiver statements without discarding that sector beforehand.
\end{proof}

For a nonpermutational component map there is still a precise map
on finitely supported functions: the pushforward
$T_*\delta_d=\delta_{Td}$. It obeys
\[
 \kappa_ST_*=(n\overline F)\kappa_S.
 \tag{SE28}\label{eq:pushforward-all-maps}
\]
Its algebraic dual is pullback on all functions, so the noninvertible
support map is retained in exact domains and codomains. For the
zero coefficient $n=0$, the kernel is all of the infinite group
$D_S$ and the same counting-pullback obstruction holds. The abelian
coefficient cone then has zero endomorphism; this statement does
not identify the original semiring elements $e$ and $\tau$.

For a prime $p$ and a permutational component map, write
$P_T=U_F P_p$, with $U_Fh(d)=h(Fd)$. The two factors commute and
$U_F$ is a finite-order unitary. Its normalized pullback is still
a pure isometry in the exact sense of (FD6): the $m$th range consists
of functions constant on cosets of the kernel of multiplication by
$p^m$, since $F^m$ is an automorphism. A vector in every such range
is constant on cosets of the infinite group
$\Delta(\Z[1/p]/\Z)$ and square summability forces zero. The
telescoping range projections therefore give the same bilateral
shift extension as in (FD7), with coefficient $\sqrt p$ and with
the support permutation retained. The norm factor is produced by
the proved $p$-element fibre, not by the relative cohomological shift.

\subsection{A proved covering operation that does produce a new multiplier}

The finite-self-map restriction in
Theorem~\ref{thm:eventual-periodicity} matters. Actual maps between
different finite support lattices can have nonunit degree on their
cohomology. The following construction gives all maps explicitly.

For $m\ge1$, let $P_m$ be the face poset of a cycle with $3m$ vertices
$v_i$ and $3m$ edges $e_i$, indexed modulo $3m$, with exactly
$v_i<e_i$ and $v_{i+1}<e_i$. It has no two-dimensional face. Put
$L_m=\Down(P_m)$ and define
\[
 q_m:P_m\to P_1,\qquad v_i\mapsto v_{i\bmod3},\quad
 e_i\mapsto e_{i\bmod3}.
 \tag{SE29}\label{eq:cycle-cover}
\]
It is an order map and an $m$-sheeted covering of the finite spaces:
the preimage of a minimal vertex open is $m$ disjoint vertex opens,
and that of $\{v_i,v_{i+1},e_i\}$ is $m$ disjoint copies of that
three-point minimal edge open. Inverse image of downsets gives a
bounded lattice homomorphism $L_1\to L_m$ and the corresponding
coefficient-preserving semiring and full spectral maps.

For the complete cochain complex of $P_m$, use zero-coordinates
$a_i,b_i$ and incidence coordinates
\[
 (Da)_{i,0}=b_i-a_i,\qquad(Da)_{i,1}=b_i-a_{i+1}.
\]
Its cycle functional is
\[
 \omega_m(c)=\sum_{i\bmod3m}(c_{i,0}-c_{i,1}).
 \tag{SE30}\label{eq:cover-cycle-coordinate}
\]
Its kernel is the image of $D$: set $a_0=0$, define successively
$b_i=c_{i,0}+a_i$ and $a_{i+1}=b_i-c_{i,1}$, and the closing equation
$a_{3m}=a_0$ is exactly $\omega_m(c)=0$. The kernel of $D$ itself
is the constant functions on all vertices and edges. Since
$\omega_m$ is surjective on the coordinate vector $c_{0,0}=1$,
these formulas prove $H^0(P_m,\Q)=\Q$ and $H^1(P_m,\Q)=\Q$ with
the exact stated orientation.

\begin{theorem}[Cover pullback, transfer, and their full gluing]
\label{thm:cover-degree}
The actual cochain pullback $q_m^*$ repeats every coordinate $m$
times. The transfer $\operatorname{Tr}_{q_m}$ sums the coordinates
over all lifts of each vertex, edge, and incidence chain. They are
cochain maps and satisfy
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}_{q_m}\,q_m^*=mI.
 \tag{SE31}\label{eq:cover-transfer-identity}
\]
On support cohomology their exact maps are
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &H^0&H^1\\ \hline
 q_m^*&1&m\\
 \operatorname{Tr}_{q_m}&m&1.
\end{array}
 \tag{SE32}\label{eq:cover-cohomology}
\]
The pullback of full universal arithmetic sheaves has arithmetic map
the identity. Extending the support transfer across the arithmetic
stratum forces its arithmetic map to be multiplication by $m$.
With that map it gives an actual sheaf transfer, and the composite
transfer after pullback is multiplication by $m$ on the entire
universal sheaf and its relative complex. On relative cohomology the
individual maps are
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &H_X^1=\Q/\Z&H_X^2=\Q\\ \hline
 q_m^*&1&m\\
 \operatorname{Tr}_{q_m}&m&1.
\end{array}
 \tag{SE33}\label{eq:cover-relative-cohomology}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every base vertex, edge, and incidence has exactly $m$ lifts. Each
lift of a face is the face of exactly one lifted incidence of the
fixed base type. Thus taking a differential and summing lifts commute,
proving the transfer is a cochain map; pullback is already
\eqref{eq:finite-pullback}. Summing the $m$ identical pulled-back
coordinates proves \eqref{eq:cover-transfer-identity} on every
cochain, not only on cohomology. Constants pull back unchanged and
transfer to $m$ times themselves. Formula
\eqref{eq:cover-cycle-coordinate} gives
$\omega_m(q_m^*c)=m\omega_1(c)$ and
$\omega_1(\operatorname{Tr}_{q_m}d)=\omega_m(d)$, proving
\eqref{eq:cover-cohomology}.

The full map $Y_m\to Y_1$ is the identity on $X$ and $q_m$ on
support. Pushforward on the support is exact because locally it is
a finite product over the covering sheets; on arithmetic sheaves
it is the identity. The full pushforward therefore is exact by the
two-component sheaf description. The support trace is the sheaf
map that sums these local sheets. A constant generic rational value
$a$ restricts on them to $m$ copies of $a$, whose trace is $ma$.
The gluing equation in \eqref{eq:gluing} consequently requires the
arithmetic image of $a$ to have generic value $ma$. The injective
map $\mathcal O_X\to\Q_X$ forces arithmetic multiplication by $m$.
This map satisfies the equation and therefore defines the full
sheaf transfer.

On relative complexes the pullback is the identity on the initial
$\Z$ and coordinate repetition thereafter. The transfer is $m$ on
that $\Z$ and the just-proved cochain trace thereafter. Their composite
is $m$ in every degree. A constant rational modulo the original
integer diagonal is sent to itself under pullback and multiplied
by $m$ under transfer, while the cycle coordinates are those already
computed. This proves \eqref{eq:cover-relative-cohomology}.
\end{proof}
