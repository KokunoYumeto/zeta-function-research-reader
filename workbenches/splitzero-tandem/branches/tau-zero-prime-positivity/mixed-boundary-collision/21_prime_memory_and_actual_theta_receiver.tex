\section{Prime boundary classes in the actual adelic theta receiver}
\label{sec:prime-memory-theta}

The preceding coefficient calculation can be carried through the
original rational-scaling relation and actual theta map. The
source here is Connes \cite{Connes1998}, Section III, equations
(6)--(19): its two moment conditions, weighted periodization norm,
and the factor $|x|^{1/2}$ are all retained. We compute an exact
kernel which would be lost by taking coinvariants before imposing
the two conditions. It lies in the marked relative group (CCF16).
This calculation uses original author TeX, not a PDF extraction.

Connes--Consani \cite{ConnesConsaniHochschild},
\texttt{h0coinv}, \texttt{propmapE}, and \texttt{propmapE1},
already prove the unconditioned spherical coinvariant and trace
comparison. The calculations below reprove its maps and compute
the extra moment-first kernel and its receiving relative classes.
Their \texttt{quotbruhatschwartz} supplies the real Laplacian
context, which is applied with its exact range at the end.

\subsection{The entire spherical coefficient module}

Write $K=\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{\times}$, $\Gamma=\mathbb Q^\times$,
$\Gamma_+=\mathbb Q_{>0}^{\times}$, and
$R=\mathbb C[\Gamma_+]$. A group-algebra basis element is denoted
$t_a$ for $a\in\Gamma_+$. Let $I=\ker(\operatorname{aug}:R\to\mathbb C)$.
For $a>0$ rational set
\[
 \eta_a=\bigotimes_p1_{p^{v_p(a)}\mathbb Z_p},\qquad
 (\alpha_a f)(x)=f(a^{-1}x),\qquad
 s(h)=\eta_1\otimes h.
 \tag{PM1}
\]
The local action and all rational coefficients are original ones.
Every $\mathbb Z_p^\times$-invariant local Schwartz function is
a unique finite linear combination of $1_{p^n\mathbb Z_p}$,
$n\in\mathbb Z$. In fact its values on valuation shells are
zero below a finite bound and constant above a finite bound;
successive differences give the coefficients of these nested
balls. Their independence follows by evaluating on successive
shells. Averaging a finite tensor over the compact unit groups
is still a finite tensor sum; only finitely many nonstandard
places occur. The global invariant space is therefore
\[
 V^K=\bigoplus_{a\in\Gamma_+}\eta_a\otimes\mathcal S(\mathbb R),
 \quad
 \mathcal J:R\otimes\mathcal S(\mathbb R)\xrightarrow{\sim}V^K,
 \qquad t_a\otimes h\longmapsto\alpha_a s(h).
 \tag{PM2}
\]
Indeed $\alpha_as(h)=\eta_a\otimes h(x/a)$, so the change
between the two displayed coordinates is invertible on every
summand. Rational positive scaling acts on $R$ by left
multiplication and leaves the second coordinate unchanged.
The element $-1\in\Gamma$ acts by $h(x)\mapsto h(-x)$.

Both moments of $\alpha_as(h)$ equal those of $h$. For the
integral this is the exact product $a\prod_p p^{-v_p(a)}=1$,
and evaluation at zero is unchanged. Consequently the moment
map on (PM2) is $\operatorname{aug}\otimes m$, where
\[
 m(h)=\left(h(0),\int_{\mathbb R}h(x)\,dx\right),\quad
 H=\mathcal S(\mathbb R)^{\rm even},\quad H_0=\ker(m|_H),
 \quad M_0=(V^K)^0.
 \tag{PM3}
\]
The superscript zero means both original moment conditions.
The odd functions have both moments zero and vanish under
$\{\pm1\}$ coinvariants: $h-(-h)=2h$. Averaging by
$\tfrac12(1+\alpha_{-1})$ is an exact projection, so all
coinvariant calculations reduce to the even summand without
losing a remaining odd class.

\subsection{Taking the moments first leaves two classes per prime}

Let $\Lambda=\Gamma_+\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb C
=\bigoplus_p\mathbb C\ell_p$, where $\ell_p$ records the
original rational prime generator. There is a canonical split
exact sequence of complex vector spaces
\[
 0\longrightarrow\Lambda\otimes\mathbb C^2
   \xrightarrow{\ b\ }(M_0)_\Gamma
   \xrightarrow{\ \Phi\ }H_0\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{PM4}
\]
The section is the actual unramified source $h\mapsto[s(h)]$.
In the original finite-place coordinates, the quotient map is
\[
 \Phi\left[\sum_a\eta_a\otimes h_a\right](x)
       =\frac12\sum_a\bigl(h_a(ax)+h_a(-ax)\bigr).
 \tag{PM5}
\]
The sum is finite. For $u\in\mathbb C^2$, choose any even
Schwartz function $h$ with $m(h)=u$. Then
\[
 b(\ell_p\otimes u)=[(\alpha_p-1)s(h)].
 \tag{PM6}
\]
The vector in brackets is in $M_0$ although $s(h)$ itself need
not satisfy the moments. Formula (PM6) is independent of the
lift: a difference of lifts belongs to $H_0$, so its difference
under $\alpha_p-1$ is one of the relations defining $(M_0)_\Gamma$.

Here is a full proof of exactness, including its injectivity.
The two even functions
\[
 h_0(x)=(1-2\pi x^2)e^{-\pi x^2},\qquad
 h_1(x)=2\pi x^2e^{-\pi x^2}
 \tag{PM7}
\]
have moments $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$. Thus $H=H_0\oplus
\operatorname{span}\{h_0,h_1\}$. In the even part of (PM2),
the moment kernel is exactly
\[
 R\otimes H_0\ \oplus\ I\otimes\mathbb C^2.
 \tag{PM8}
\]
Positive-rational coinvariants quotient a module by $I$ times
that module. They therefore turn (PM8) into
$H_0\oplus(I/I^2)\otimes\mathbb C^2$.
The canonical map $I/I^2\to\Lambda$ sends $t_a-1$ to
$\sum_pv_p(a)\ell_p$. Products satisfy
$t_{ab}-1=(t_a-1)+(t_b-1)+(t_a-1)(t_b-1)$, and
$t_{p^{-1}}-1\equiv-(t_p-1)\pmod{I^2}$. Hence the classes
$t_p-1$ span. Their independence follows by the derivations
$D_p(t_a)=v_p(a)$: each $D_p$ vanishes on $I^2$ and takes
value $\delta_{pq}$ on $t_q-1$. This proves $I/I^2\simeq\Lambda$.
It proves (PM4) and every map (PM5)--(PM6). Although (PM7)
was used to verify exactness, the resulting injection is
independent of those lifts and the section is already canonical.

The same calculation specifies the higher algebraic group
homology:
\[
 H_n(\Gamma,M_0)\simeq
       \bigwedge^{n+1}\Lambda\otimes\mathbb C^2
       \qquad(n\ge1).
 \tag{PM9}
\]
For completeness, on any finitely generated subgroup of
$\Gamma_+$ use the tensor product of the exact two-term
resolutions with differential $t_p-1$ for its prime generators.
The one-variable resolution is exact because multiplication
by $t_p-1$ is injective on its Laurent polynomial ring and
its quotient is evaluation at one. Tensoring over $\mathbb C$
preserves exactness, giving the finite Koszul resolution.
Tensoring this resolution with a trivial module annihilates
every differential, yielding the exterior powers of its
generator space. Increasing the finite set of generators
and taking the exact filtered union gives
$H_j(\Gamma_+,\mathbb C^2)=\bigwedge^j\Lambda\otimes\mathbb C^2$.
The even $V^K$ in (PM2) is a free $R$ module and hence has
zero higher group homology. The order-two factor has an exact
averaging projection over $\mathbb C$, so adds no higher group.
The long exact sequence of
$0\to M_0\to V^K\to\mathbb C^2\to0$ now proves (PM9),
and its degree-zero portion is exactly (PM4).
These are algebraic homology statements; no Hilbert completion
is hidden in them.

\subsection{The complete map to Connes's periodization}

On the trivial compact-character sector, represent an idele
class by real $x>0$ and finite components one. The original
periodization is
\[
 (\mathcal E f)(x)=x^{1/2}\sum_{q\in\mathbb Q^\times}f(qx).
 \tag{PM10}
\]
It is invariant under $\alpha_q$, by reindexing the sum. On
an unramified even source the finite conditions force
$q\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$, and therefore
\[
 \mathcal E f(x)=x^{1/2}\Theta\Phi[f](x),\qquad
 \Theta h(x)=2\sum_{n\ge1}h(nx).
 \tag{PM11}
\]
This holds first on each summand $\alpha_a s(h)$ of (PM2)
and then by linearity. The factor two is the sum over positive
and negative integers; the factor $x^{1/2}$ is the source's
specified measure comparison, not a new convention.

The map $\Theta$ is injective on $H$. Indeed, for fixed $x>0$,
rapid decay implies absolute convergence of
$\sum_{m,n\ge1}|h(mnx)|$: for $A>2$ it is bounded by
a constant times $x^{-A}\zeta(A)^2$. With the integer Möbius
function $\mu$, the exact divisor identity
$\sum_{d\mid r}\mu(d)=1$ for $r=1$ and zero otherwise gives
\[
 h(x)=\frac12\sum_{n\ge1}\mu(n)\Theta h(nx).
 \tag{PM12}
\]
The divisor identity follows by expanding
$\prod_{p\mid r}(1-1)$ for $r>1$ and its empty product for
$r=1$. This supplies a direct proof of injectivity with no
assumption about zeta zeros. Continuity gives $h(0)$ as well,
and evenness determines negative arguments.

For $h\in H_0$, both $h(0)$ and $\widehat h(0)=\int h$ vanish.
Poisson summation gives
\[
 \Theta h(x)=x^{-1}\Theta\widehat h(1/x).
 \tag{PM13}
\]
One proof in these exact conventions is to periodize
$t\mapsto h(xt)$ on $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$. Its uniformly
convergent Fourier series has coefficients
$x^{-1}\widehat h(n/x)$; evaluation at zero gives the equality
of the sums over all integers. Both zero-index terms vanish,
which gives (PM13). All derivatives are controlled by the
same Schwartz bounds. Thus $\Theta h$ decays to arbitrary
order at infinity and, by (PM13), at zero after multiplication
by any fixed power. In particular the original norm
\[
 \|f\|_\delta^2=
   \int_0^\infty |\Theta\Phi[f](x)|^2
          (1+\log^2x)^{\delta/2}\,x\,\frac{dx}{x}
 \quad(\delta>1)
 \tag{PM14}
\]
is finite. It is exactly Connes's norm in this sector, with
its original weight. Equation (PM12) proves the exact kernel
after algebraic coinvariants:
\[
 \ker\bigl(\mathcal E:(M_0)_\Gamma\to
               C^\infty(\mathbb R_{>0})\bigr)
       =b(\Lambda\otimes\mathbb C^2).
 \tag{PM15}
\]
The same kernel is killed by (PM14). Consequently the
moment-first quotient contains every prime boundary class,
while the original periodization seminorm explicitly removes
them. This is a calculated kernel with its unchanged
representatives, rather than an identification of those classes
with absent $\tau$.

\subsection{Their actual support map, scaling characters, and Fourier action}

Use the invariant marked relative space $D^K=D_V^K$ from
(CCF10). Unit averaging commutes with its projections, and
finite averaging on every local test proves exactness of
taking these invariants. Let
\[
 R_0=\operatorname{span}\{(\alpha_q-1)f:
            q\in\Gamma,\ f\in M_0\},\qquad
 D_{\Gamma,0}=D^K/\delta_VR_0.
 \tag{PM16}
\]
This is the stated quotient of the original relative group;
no action on $D^K$ is presumed when a rational prime fails
to preserve a local unramified line. The map $\delta_V$
nevertheless induces an exact injection
\[
 \Lambda\otimes\mathbb C^2\hookrightarrow D_{\Gamma,0},
 \qquad \ell_p\otimes u\longmapsto
             [\delta_V((\alpha_p-1)s(h))].
 \tag{PM17}
\]
The only nonzero marked component of this representative is
at $p$, where it is $[\alpha_ps(h)]_p$, the actual defect
(CCF33). Other primes see a unit and retain the standard
factor, so their quotient class is zero. This connects the
new classes to the original prime, not to an auxiliary index.

To prove injectivity of (PM17), suppose a representative
$v\in M_0$ of a class in the kernel of $\Phi$ satisfies
$\delta_Vv\in\delta_VR_0$. Then $v-r\in\ker\delta_V$ for
some $r\in R_0$. By (CCF11) its value is $s(h)$ with
$h\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R)$; since $v,r$ have both moments
zero, $h$ has both moments zero. In coinvariants its class
is $[s(h^{\rm even})]$, whose image under $\Phi$ is
$h^{\rm even}$. The class under consideration had image
zero, so $h^{\rm even}=0$. Its odd part is already an
order-two relation. Therefore $[v]=0$ in $(M_0)_\Gamma$,
which proves injectivity. The image of the full induced map
$(M_0)_\Gamma\to D_{\Gamma,0}$ is precisely (PM17), since
the unramified section of $H_0$ has $\delta_Vs(h)=0$.

Archimedean scaling commutes with every rational action and
with $\delta_V$. It therefore acts on all these groups.
Its exact action on the new prime classes is
\[
 \vartheta_\lambda b(\ell_p\otimes(u_0,u_1))
      =b(\ell_p\otimes(u_0,\lambda u_1)).
 \tag{PM18}
\]
Apply (CCF21) to the chosen lift $h$; (PM6) is independent of
the remaining zero-moment difference. This proves (PM18)
without choosing homogeneous Schwartz lifts. The section
$H_0\to(M_0)_\Gamma$ also commutes with $\vartheta_\lambda$,
so (PM4) is equivariantly split for this action.

Fourier inversion on the original adelic space satisfies
$\mathcal F\alpha_p=\alpha_{p^{-1}}\mathcal F$ and
$\mathcal Fs(h)=s(\widehat h)$. It induces a map on
$(M_0)_\Gamma$. In $I/I^2$ inversion sends $t_p-1$ to its
negative, while Fourier exchanges the two moments. Hence
\[
 \mathcal F b(\ell_p\otimes(u_0,u_1))
       =-b(\ell_p\otimes(u_1,u_0)).
 \tag{PM19}
\]
One may verify the sign directly by
$t_p^{-1}-1=-t_p^{-1}(t_p-1)$ and reducing modulo $I^2$.
Moreover $\Phi\mathcal F=\widehat{\phantom h}\,\Phi$,
by (PM2) and the same inversion of rational labels.
Together (PM13), (PM18), and (PM19) give the exact reflection
and both pole characters before and after periodization.
For example
$\mathcal E(\mathcal F f)(x)=\mathcal E f(1/x)$
for $f\in M_0$, including the retained factor $x^{1/2}$.

Finally the map to the original arithmetic image (CCF19)
sends the representatives (PM17) to zero because they are
in $\delta_VV$. Thus the complete receiving diagram has
three specified operations: passage to the split relative
group, rational-scaling relations imposed after the moment
conditions, and the arithmetic or theta receiver. The new
prime classes survive the first two operations as (PM17)
and have the exactly computed kernel in the third. Their
survival in the relative space and their removal by the
periodization norm are both part of the reconstructed
mathematics.

\subsection{The Laplacian range and the full missing moment-first module}

Retain the human source's differential operator
$H=x\partial_x$ and $\Delta=H(1+H)$, acting at the real
coordinate. The map $\Delta:H\to H_0$ is an isomorphism
\cite[\texttt{quotbruhatschwartz}]{ConnesConsaniHochschild};
in this sentence the first $H$ denotes the space of even
Schwartz functions from (PM3), and in the formulas below we
write that space explicitly to avoid confusion with the operator.
Here is a direct inverse in the original coordinate. For
$f\in H_0$ and $x>0$ set
\[
 u(x)=-\int_x^\infty\frac{f(t)}t\,dt,
 \qquad g(x)=-\frac1x\int_x^\infty u(t)\,dt.
 \tag{PM20}
\]
Extend both evenly. Since $f$ is even and $f(0)=0$,
$f(t)/t$ is smooth odd and vanishes to first order at zero.
Its primitive $u$ is smooth even and rapidly decreasing at
infinity. It obeys $Hu=f$. Integration by parts gives
$\int_0^\infty u=-\int_0^\infty f=0$. Thus near zero the
second expression equals $x^{-1}\int_0^xu(t)dt$, which is
smooth even; its expression as a tail proves rapid decay.
It satisfies $(1+H)g=u$, hence $\Delta g=f$.
The kernel of $H$ on Schwartz functions is zero, and the
kernel of $1+H$ there is zero as well, since its solutions
have $xg$ constant away from zero. This proves injectivity
and the full inverse claim.

In the even spherical adelic coordinates (PM2), $\Delta$
acts as $1\otimes\Delta$; it commutes with every rational
scaling. It follows that its entire range is $R\otimes H_0$.
The exact extra quotient before taking coinvariants is
\[
 M_0^{\rm even}/\Delta(V^{K,\rm even})
       \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ } I\otimes\mathbb C^2,
 \qquad \sum_at_a\otimes h_a\longmapsto
                         \sum_at_a\otimes m(h_a).
 \tag{PM21}
\]
The moment condition says exactly that the target has
augmentation zero. The kernel is $R\otimes H_0$, and
surjectivity follows using (PM7) coefficientwise. After
coinvariants the exact remaining quotient is
$(I/I^2)\otimes\mathbb C^2$, namely (PM4)'s prime classes.
Thus the real Laplacian theorem and the full adelic
moment-first space have an explicit comparison with a
calculated cokernel. The original theta image is unchanged
by this cokernel, because (PM15) is exactly its kernel.

On the positive arithmetic Hilbert quotient (CCF31)--(CCF32),
the same original operator has a fully determined realization.
On finite Schwartz channels integration by parts gives
\[
 H^*=-1-H,\qquad
 \Delta=-H^*H=(H+\tfrac12)^2-\tfrac14,
 \qquad
 \langle f,\Delta f\rangle
       =-\|(H+\tfrac12)f\|^2-\tfrac14\|f\|^2.
 \tag{PM22}
\]
The boundary terms $x\overline f g$ vanish at zero and
infinity. Each finite-place projection and marked mean
commutes with $H$, so the same calculation holds on all
zero-sum marked channels. This proves the exact sign and
the retained $1/4$ on the actual attained receiver.

Its closed realization and spectrum can also be calculated.
On the positive and negative half-lines, respectively, the maps
$f\mapsto e^{t/2}f(e^t)$ and $f\mapsto e^{t/2}f(-e^t)$
are unitary from additive $L^2$ to $L^2(\mathbb R,dt)$.
They send $H+1/2$ to $\partial_t$. The logarithmic Fourier transform here is explicitly
$(2\pi)^{-1/2}\int e^{-it\xi}h(t)\,dt$; it therefore sends $\Delta$ to multiplication by
$-\xi^2-1/4$, on its exact domain
\[
 \{h\in L^2(\mathbb R):
              (\xi^2+1/4)h(\xi)\in L^2(\mathbb R)\}.
 \tag{PM23}
\]
Real multiplication on this domain is selfadjoint: its
resolvents at nonreal parameters are the bounded multipliers
$(-\xi^2-1/4-z)^{-1}$, and the adjoint condition obtained by
testing compactly supported functions gives exactly the same
domain. Compact smooth functions away from $x=0$, in each
half-line and each finite channel, give a core by truncation
and smooth approximation in the multiplication graph norm.
Consequently its spectrum on any nonzero such arithmetic
channel is $(-\infty,-1/4]$; approximate functions supported
near a frequency show every value in that interval occurs
in the spectrum, while outside it the reciprocal is bounded.
Each level set has measure zero, so there is no nonzero
eigenvector at a single spectral value. For an orthogonal sum, its domain additionally requires the sum
of the integrals $\int(\xi^2+1/4)^2|h_a(\xi)|^2d\xi$ over all
channels to be finite. This follows directly by applying the
multiplication operator to the whole Hilbert direct sum. Its spectrum
is the same interval when at least one channel is nonzero.
This is a complete spectral calculation for the specified
adelic Haar receiver. The source's full zeta-jet receiver
and this Hilbert receiver remain connected by the explicit
source maps and kernels (PM4)--(PM21); no identification of
their spectral quotients is assumed.
