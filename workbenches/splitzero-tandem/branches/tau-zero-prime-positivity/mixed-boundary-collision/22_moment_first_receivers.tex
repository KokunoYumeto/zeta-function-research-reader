\section{All moment-first classes, higher cycles, and their actual completions}


\subsection{Original source and coefficient coordinates}

The source is Alain Connes, \emph{Trace formula in noncommutative
Geometry and the zeros of the Riemann zeta function},
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9811068}{arXiv:math/9811068}.
The supplied original author file \path{math_9811068.tex} was
read at lines 646--825: Section III, equations (6)--(19), including
the two moments, the weighted norm, its Haar normalization, the
periodization factor, and its exact scaling identity. This is an
independent derivation of (PM1)--(PM19) of the programme source
\path{PRIME_MEMORY_AND_ACTUAL_THETA_RECEIVER.tex}.
The new maps below also identify precisely what happens in the
marked Hilbert receiver, rather than identifying algebraic
coinvariants with a completed quotient.

Put $V=\mathcal S(\mathbb A_\Q)$, $K=\widehat\Z^{\times}$,
$\Gamma=\Q^{\times}$, $\Gamma_+=\Q_{>0}^{\times}$, and
$R=\C[\Gamma_+]$. Its basis is $t_a$ for positive rational $a$,
and $I=\ker\aug$ is its augmentation ideal. Use the actual action
and the actual unramified section
\[
 \alpha_af(x)=f(a^{-1}x),\qquad
 s(h)=h\otimes\bigotimes_p1_{\Z_p},\qquad
 \eta_a=\bigotimes_p1_{p^{v_p(a)}\Z_p}.
 \tag{MC1}\label{mc:eq:coordinates}
\]
Every $\Z_p^{\times}$-invariant local test is a unique finite
linear combination of $1_{p^n\Z_p}$, $n\in\Z$. Its shell values
vanish for sufficiently negative valuations and become constant
for sufficiently positive valuations; successive differences give
exactly the claimed coefficients. Independence follows by testing
successive shells. Expanding finite adelic tensors therefore gives
the exact isomorphism
\[
 J:R\otimes\mathcal S(\mathbb R)\xrightarrow{\sim}V^K,
 \qquad J(t_a\otimes h)=\alpha_as(h)
       =\eta_a\otimes h(x/a).
 \tag{MC2}\label{mc:eq:free-module}
\]
Compact-unit averaging is valid here without an integration
completion: a local test has an open stabilizer in its compact
unit group, so its orbit is finite. Every relevant averaging is
a finite average and remains an original adelic test. This also
proves exactness of invariants on the modules and marked quotients
used below. Positive rationals act on the first factor of
\eqref{mc:eq:free-module} by left multiplication. The element $-1$
acts on the second factor by real reflection.

Let
\[
 \begin{gathered} m(h)=\left(h(0),\int_{\mathbb R}h(x)\,dx\right),\\
 H=\mathcal S(\mathbb R)^{\mathrm{even}},\qquad H_0=\ker(m|_H),
 \quad M_0=\ker(m_{\mathbb A}:V^K\to\C^2).
 \end{gathered}\tag{MC3}\label{mc:eq:moments}
\]
Both adelic moments of $\alpha_as(h)$ equal $m(h)$: evaluation
is fixed, and the integral scales by
$a\prod_p p^{-v_p(a)}=1$. Hence the moment map in
\eqref{mc:eq:free-module} is exactly $\aug\otimes m$.
Odd functions already have both moments zero. Their coinvariant
classes vanish because reflection acts by $-1$ and the relation
is twice the vector. Averaging by $\tfrac12(1+\alpha_{-1})$ is
an exact projection, so the even reduction loses no surviving
coinvariant class.

\subsection{All moment-first coinvariants and their explicit inverse coordinates}

Write
\[
 \Lambda=\bigoplus_p\C\ell_p,
 \qquad \ell(a)=\sum_pv_p(a)\ell_p.
 \tag{MC4}\label{mc:eq:prime-vectors}
\]
The sums are finite, with the original prime exponents and signs.
Let $R_0$ be the span of $(\alpha_q-1)f$ with $q\in\Gamma$ and
$f\in M_0$. Then $(M_0)_\Gamma=M_0/R_0$.

\begin{theorem}[The exact canonical split sequence]
\label{mc:thm:coinvariants}
There is a canonical split exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow\Lambda\otimes\C^2
 \xrightarrow{b}(M_0)_\Gamma
 \xrightarrow{\Phi}H_0\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{MC5}\label{mc:eq:coinvariant-sequence}
\]
Its section is $h\mapsto[s(h)]$, and its injection is
\[
 b(\ell_p\otimes u)=[(\alpha_p-1)s(h)]
 \quad\text{for any }h\in H\text{ with }m(h)=u.
 \tag{MC6}\label{mc:eq:boundary-class}
\]
For a representative $f=\sum_a\alpha_as(h_a)\in M_0$, its
complete inverse coordinates are
\[
 \Phi[f]=\sum_a h_a^{\mathrm{even}},\qquad
 \beta[f]=\sum_a\ell(a)\otimes m(h_a),
 \qquad
 [f]=[s(\Phi[f])]+b(\beta[f]).
 \tag{MC7}\label{mc:eq:inverse-coordinates}
\]
In the original coordinates $f=\sum_a\eta_a\otimes g_a$,
the first formula is
$\Phi[f](x)=\tfrac12\sum_a(g_a(ax)+g_a(-ax))$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The even functions
\[
 u(x)=(1-2\pi x^2)e^{-\pi x^2},\qquad
 v(x)=2\pi x^2e^{-\pi x^2}
 \tag{MC8}\label{mc:eq:moment-lifts}
\]
have moments $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$, because
$\int e^{-\pi x^2}=1$ and $\int x^2e^{-\pi x^2}=1/(2\pi)$.
Thus $H=H_0\oplus\C u\oplus\C v$. The even moment kernel is
exactly the $R$-module
\[
 R\otimes H_0\ \oplus\ I\otimes\C^2.
 \tag{MC9}\label{mc:eq:moment-module}
\]
Taking positive-rational coinvariants quotients by $I$ times this
module and gives $H_0\oplus(I/I^2)\otimes\C^2$.
The map $t_a-1\mapsto\ell(a)$ identifies $I/I^2$ with
$\Lambda$. Indeed
$t_{ab}-1=(t_a-1)+(t_b-1)+(t_a-1)(t_b-1)$ and
$t_{p^{-1}}-1\equiv-(t_p-1)\pmod{I^2}$ show spanning by
the prime classes. The linear derivation $D_p(t_a)=v_p(a)$
vanishes on $I^2$ and takes value $\delta_{pq}$ on $t_q-1$,
proving independence. This proves the exact sequence.

Changing the lift in \eqref{mc:eq:boundary-class} changes it by
$(\alpha_p-1)s(h_0)$ with $h_0\in H_0$, an actual relation in
$R_0$. Thus $b$ is independent of the moment lifts.
The first formula of \eqref{mc:eq:inverse-coordinates} is augmentation
followed by even projection. Its moments are zero because $f\in M_0$.
For the second, applying $(\alpha_c-1)$ to a representative changes
the indicated sum by
$\ell(c)\otimes\sum_am(h_a)=0$. Reflection also leaves it
unchanged, since both moments of an odd function vanish. Thus
$\beta$ is defined on the stated coinvariants. It is zero on the
section $s(H_0)$ and takes $b(\ell_p\otimes u)$ to
$\ell_p\otimes u$. The just-proved direct sum therefore proves
the final equality and all canonicity claims.
\end{proof}

For comparison, quotienting $V^K$ by rational relations first gives
$H$; imposing its two moments then gives only $H_0$. The precise
defect is the kernel in \eqref{mc:eq:coinvariant-sequence}. More
explicitly, if $I_\Gamma V^K$ denotes the span of all
$(\alpha_q-1)f$ with $f\in V^K$, then its moments vanish and
\[
 I_\Gamma V^K/I_\Gamma M_0\simeq\Lambda\otimes\C^2,
 \qquad I_\Gamma M_0=R_0.
 \tag{MC10}\label{mc:eq:order-defect}
\]
This follows because augmentation is the complete quotient
$V^K\to H$ and its restriction to $M_0$ is $\Phi$.

\subsection{Higher pole classes with explicit cycles and actions}

\begin{theorem}[All higher algebraic group homology]
\label{mc:thm:homology}
For $n\ge1$ there is the canonical connecting isomorphism
\[
 H_n(\Gamma,M_0)\simeq
       \bigwedge^{n+1}\Lambda\otimes\C^2.
 \tag{MC11}\label{mc:eq:higher-homology}
\]
For distinct ordered primes $p_0,\ldots,p_n$ and a lift $h$ of
$u\in\C^2$, the class corresponding to
$\ell_{p_0}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ell_{p_n}\otimes u$ is represented
in the finite-prime Koszul complex by the exact cycle
\[
 \sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^i(\alpha_{p_i}-1)s(h)
       \otimes e_{p_0}\wedge\cdots\widehat{e_{p_i}}
                                      \cdots\wedge e_{p_n}.
 \tag{MC12}\label{mc:eq:explicit-cycles}
\]
The same formula for $n=0$ is \eqref{mc:eq:boundary-class}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a finite set of prime generators, tensor the two-term Laurent
polynomial resolutions with differential $t_p-1$. Each is exact
because this multiplication is injective and its cokernel is
evaluation at one. Tensor products over the coefficient field
preserve exactness. On a trivial module every differential becomes
zero, so the result in degree $j$ is its tensor with the $j$th
exterior power of the prime-generator space. The subgroups generated
by finite prime sets are cofinal among the finite subsets of
$\Gamma_+$, and every group-homology chain uses finitely many group
elements. Their exact filtered union therefore gives
$H_j(\Gamma_+,\C^2)=\bigwedge^j\Lambda\otimes\C^2$.

The module in \eqref{mc:eq:free-module} is free over $R$, and remains
free when restricted to any such subgroup. Hence its higher
positive-rational homology is zero. The order-two group is exact
under averaging over $\C$, so adds no positive-degree homology.
The long exact sequence of
$0\to M_0\to V^K\to\C^2\to0$ gives
\eqref{mc:eq:higher-homology}. The Koszul differential of
$s(h)\otimes e_{p_0}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{p_n}$ is exactly
\eqref{mc:eq:explicit-cycles}. Each coefficient has both moments zero,
and its next differential is zero because the Koszul differential
squares to zero. Changing $h$ by a zero-moment lift changes the
cycle by a boundary in the $M_0$ complex itself. This verifies the
stated cycle and its independence without an implicit lift choice.
\end{proof}

Let $\vartheta_\lambda$ scale the real coordinate by
$f(x_\infty,x_f)\mapsto f(\lambda^{-1}x_\infty,x_f)$.
It commutes with rational scaling and acts on the two moments by
$\operatorname{diag}(1,\lambda)$. Thus it acts on each right-hand
side of \eqref{mc:eq:coinvariant-sequence} and
\eqref{mc:eq:higher-homology} by the identity on the exterior prime
factor and that exact two-character action on $\C^2$.
The adelic Fourier transform satisfies
$\mathcal F\alpha_a=\alpha_{a^{-1}}\mathcal F$ and
$\mathcal Fs(h)=s(\widehat h)$. It exchanges the two moments.
Inversion on $\Gamma_+$ negates each generator of $\Lambda$.
Consequently its exact action on the higher prime module is
\[
 \mathcal F(\omega\otimes(u_0,u_1))
       =(-1)^{n+1}\omega\otimes(u_1,u_0)
 \quad\text{in degree }n\ge0.
 \tag{MC13}\label{mc:eq:higher-fourier}
\]
One can also check the sign on chains: inversion sends the Koszul
basis $e_p$ to $-t_p^{-1}e_p$, since
$t_p^{-1}-1=-t_p^{-1}(t_p-1)$. On trivial coefficients this is
exactly the stated exterior sign. Degree zero gives (PM19).

\subsection{The original periodization and its complete kernel}

In the compact-unit invariant sector an idele class has representative
with real coordinate $x>0$ and finite coordinates one. Connes's
actual map is
\[
 (\mathcal Ef)(x)=x^{1/2}\sum_{q\in\Q^\times}f(qx).
 \tag{MC14}\label{mc:eq:periodization}
\]
For $\alpha_as(h)$ the finite conditions force $q/a\in\Z$, so
the nonzero rational sum is precisely the sum of $h(nx)$ over
nonzero integers $n$. In particular it is absolutely convergent
for each $x>0$. By the finite expansion \eqref{mc:eq:free-module},
all of \eqref{mc:eq:periodization} is therefore
\[
 \mathcal Ef(x)=x^{1/2}\Theta\Phi[f](x),\qquad
 \Theta h(x)=2\sum_{n\ge1}h(nx).
 \tag{MC15}\label{mc:eq:theta}
\]
Reindexing its absolutely convergent rational sum proves invariance
under rational scaling. The two retained constants, $2$ and
$x^{1/2}$, have their original sources: sign summation and comparison
of the multiplicative and weighted measures.

The theta map on $H$ is injective. For fixed $x>0$ and $A>2$,
Schwartz decay bounds the double absolute sum
$\sum_{m,n\ge1}|h(mnx)|$ by $C_Ax^{-A}\zeta(A)^2$. Thus the
integer M\"obius identity can be rearranged to give
\[
 h(x)=\frac12\sum_{n\ge1}\mu(n)\Theta h(nx).
 \tag{MC16}\label{mc:eq:mobius}
\]
Indeed the coefficient of $h(rx)$ is $\sum_{n\mid r}\mu(n)$,
equal to one for $r=1$ and zero otherwise by expanding the product
$\prod_{p\mid r}(1-1)$. Continuity determines $h(0)$ and evenness
determines its negative arguments. This proves injectivity.

For $h\in H_0$, periodizing the Schwartz function $t\mapsto h(xt)$
on $\mathbb R/\Z$ gives a uniformly convergent Fourier series
with coefficients $x^{-1}\widehat h(n/x)$. Evaluation at zero
therefore yields Poisson summation. Both zero-index terms vanish
because both moments vanish, and hence
\[
 \Theta h(x)=x^{-1}\Theta\widehat h(1/x).
 \tag{MC17}\label{mc:eq:poisson}
\]
Schwartz bounds at infinity and this exact reflection imply decay
to every positive order at both endpoints, even after multiplying
by a fixed power. Thus the original Section III(7) norm in this
sector is finite and is exactly
\[
 \|f\|_\delta^2=
 \int_0^\infty|\Theta\Phi[f](x)|^2
        (1+\log^2x)^{\delta/2}\,x\,\frac{dx}{x},
 \qquad\delta>1.
 \tag{MC18}\label{mc:eq:original-norm}
\]
The source's Section III(14) Haar normalization gives compact-unit
volume one, so there is no extra compact-volume multiplier here.
The formula is a seminorm until its exact kernel is removed.
Equations \eqref{mc:eq:coinvariant-sequence}, \eqref{mc:eq:theta}, and
\eqref{mc:eq:mobius} prove
\[
 \ker\bigl(\mathcal E:(M_0)_\Gamma\to
                        C^\infty(\mathbb R_{>0})\bigr)
   =b(\Lambda\otimes\C^2).
 \tag{MC19}\label{mc:eq:theta-kernel}
\]
The same is the exact kernel of \eqref{mc:eq:original-norm}, because
a continuous theta function of zero weighted integral is zero.
Equivalently the kernel on $M_0$ before coinvariants is
$I_\Gamma V^K$, while the coinvariant relations imposed after
the moments are only $I_\Gamma M_0$. This verifies (PM15) with
the two original moments imposed at the stated stage.

\subsection{The full marked extension beyond the injected prime classes}

Use $D=D_V=\bigoplus_p V/V_p$ and $\delta:V\to D$ from
\path{connes_marked_quotient_verification.tex}, (MQ1)--(MQ4).
The compact-unit action preserves every $V_p$, so acts on $D$ and
commutes with $\delta$. Exact finite averaging identifies
$(D/\delta V)^K=D^K/\delta V^K$. Define the exact algebraic
receiver
\[
 D_{\Gamma,0}=D^K/\delta R_0.
 \tag{MC20}\label{mc:eq:marked-coinvariant-receiver}
\]
No rational action on $D$ is assumed in this definition. A rational
prime does not preserve its local unramified line; the formula
uses only the actual images under $\delta$ of existing relations
in $M_0$.

\begin{theorem}[The complete marked extension and all of its maps]
\label{mc:thm:marked-extension}
There is a canonical exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow\Lambda\otimes\C^2
 \xrightarrow{\delta b}D_{\Gamma,0}
 \longrightarrow (D/\delta V)^K\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{MC21}\label{mc:eq:full-marked-extension}
\]
The first map sends $\ell_p\otimes u$ to the class of
$\delta((\alpha_p-1)s(h))$ for any lift $m(h)=u$. Its only
nonzero marked component is the original prime $p$. The right-hand
map is the original arithmetic quotient. The induced map from
$(M_0)_\Gamma$ to $D_{\Gamma,0}$ has kernel exactly $s(H_0)$
and image exactly the displayed prime kernel.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Independence of the moment lift follows because the change belongs
to $\delta R_0$. At a prime $\ell\ne p$, rational $p$ is a
local unit and keeps the factor $1_{\Z_\ell}$, so the marked
class is zero; at $p$ the original standard term is itself zero
in $V/V_p$, leaving $[\alpha_ps(h)]_p$.

Suppose $f\in M_0$ maps to zero in $D_{\Gamma,0}$. Then
$\delta f=\delta r$ for some $r\in R_0$. The kernel of
$\delta$ is the original unramified $s(\mathcal S(\mathbb R))$,
by the arithmetic resolution, so $f-r=s(h)$. Both moments of
$h$ vanish because those of $f$ and $r$ do. Its odd part is a
reflection relation; its even part lies in $H_0$. This proves
that the induced kernel is exactly $s(H_0)$, whose injection
is already proved by $\Phi s=1$. Restricting to $\ker\Phi$
therefore proves injectivity of $\delta b$ and identifies the
whole induced image with the prime module.

Finally $\delta M_0=\delta V^K$. Indeed subtract from any
$f\in V^K$ an unramified $s(h)$ with the same two moments,
using \eqref{mc:eq:moment-lifts}. The difference belongs to $M_0$
and its marked image equals $\delta f$. Therefore the kernel
of the right-hand quotient in \eqref{mc:eq:full-marked-extension}
is exactly $\delta M_0/\delta R_0$, which is the prime image
just computed. Surjectivity and the remaining maps follow from
the displayed successive quotients.
\end{proof}

Thus the injected prime classes are the entire extra kernel over
the original mixed-place arithmetic receiver. All that receiver's
finite-place components, and its real odd coefficients as well,
remain in the right-hand term. Taking $K$ invariants does not
impose real evenness there: the rational relations in this theorem
were imposed on generic coefficient images, not on an assumed
rational action on the marked space.

\subsection{Their exact fate in the inherited marked Hilbert quotient}

Give $D$ the original norm from the orthogonal unramified
projections
$P_pf=1_{\Z_p}\otimes\int_{\Z_p}f(t,\cdot)\,dt$.
Let $\overline D^{,K}$ be the Hilbert completion of $D^K$.
It equals the invariant subspace of $\overline D$: averaging is
a bounded orthogonal projection and preserves the dense algebraic
tests. The marked diagonal closure and its faithful arithmetic
quotient were computed in (MQ15)--(MQ19).

\begin{theorem}[The prime kernel has zero inherited quotient norm]
\label{mc:thm:closure-relations}
In the original marked Hilbert norm,
\[
 \overline{\delta R_0}=\overline{\delta V^K}
          \quad\text{inside }\overline D^{,K}.
 \tag{MC22}\label{mc:eq:relation-closure}
\]
Consequently the separated Hilbert completion of
$D^K/\delta R_0$ in its inherited quotient seminorm is exactly
\[
 \overline D^{,K}/\overline{\delta V^K},
 \tag{MC23}\label{mc:eq:completed-receiver}
\]
the positive arithmetic mixed-place receiver. On its original
algebraic quotient, the radical is exactly
$\delta b(\Lambda\otimes\C^2)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
There is an explicit moment-preserving approximation. For the
functions $u,v$ in \eqref{mc:eq:moment-lifts}, put
\[
 u_n(x)=u(nx),\qquad v_n(x)=n^{-1}v(x/n),\qquad
 h_n=h-h(0)u_n-\left(\int h\right)v_n.
 \tag{MC24}\label{mc:eq:moment-approximation}
\]
For every even Schwartz $h$, the moments of $u_n$ and $v_n$
remain $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$, so $h_n\in H_0$. Moreover
$\|u_n\|_2=n^{-1/2}\|u\|_2$ and
$\|v_n\|_2=n^{-1/2}\|v\|_2$, hence $h_n\to h$ in $L^2$.
All approximants remain original Schwartz functions.

For a fixed prime, the exact defect norm (MQ28)--(MQ29) is
\[
 \bigl\|\delta((\alpha_p-1)s(h))\bigr\|^2
                   =(1-p^{-1})\|h\|_2^2.
 \tag{MC25}\label{mc:eq:boundary-norm}
\]
Thus the same marked vectors with $h_n$ converge to the vector
with $h$, while each approximating vector belongs to $\delta R_0$
because $s(h_n)\in M_0$. Every prime generator of
\eqref{mc:eq:full-marked-extension} therefore lies in
$\overline{\delta R_0}$. The exact sequence and
$\delta M_0=\delta V^K$ imply that every algebraic element of
$\delta V^K$ is a sum of an element of $\delta R_0$ and
finitely many such prime vectors. This proves one inclusion in
\eqref{mc:eq:relation-closure}; the other is immediate.

To identify the radical exactly, the faithful arithmetic quotient
calculation (MQ16)--(MQ18) gives
$D\cap\overline{\delta V}=\delta V$. Averaging shows
$D^K\cap\overline{\delta V^K}=\delta V^K$: an invariant
algebraic vector in the full closure has a lift $f\in V$, and
averaging that lift gives one in $V^K$. Its class modulo
$\delta R_0$ is precisely the kernel in
\eqref{mc:eq:full-marked-extension}. This proves both the exact
radical and \eqref{mc:eq:completed-receiver}.
\end{proof}

The approximation in \eqref{mc:eq:moment-approximation} does not
converge in the Schwartz topology in general: its first correction
concentrates at zero and its second spreads to large scale. It is
therefore not a relation erasing the original algebraic classes.
It proves their fate under the specific inherited $L^2$ quotient
operation. This is distinct from the exact theta kernel, though
both receivers kill the same prime module by the maps just proved.

\subsection{The positive weight constraint and a retained indefinite form}

On the prime module the actual operators are
\[
 T_\lambda(\ell_p\otimes(u_0,u_1))
       =\ell_p\otimes(u_0,\lambda u_1),\qquad
 F(\ell_p\otimes(u_0,u_1))
       =-\ell_p\otimes(u_1,u_0).
 \tag{MC26}\label{mc:eq:prime-operators}
\]
The second sign is the original Fourier inversion of the prime
generator in \eqref{mc:eq:higher-fourier}, not a chosen orientation.

\begin{proposition}[The complete positive obstruction and its receiver]
\label{mc:prop:positive-obstruction}
Every positive semidefinite Hermitian form on $D_{\Gamma,0}$
satisfying the original marked scaling identity
\[
 Q(T_\lambda z,T_\lambda z)=\lambda Q(z,z)
                       \quad(\lambda>0)
 \tag{MC27}\label{mc:eq:weight-identity}
\]
annihilates the entire prime module in
\eqref{mc:eq:full-marked-extension}. Hence it factors through that
actual arithmetic quotient. The inherited marked quotient has
exactly this radical, by Theorem~\ref{mc:thm:closure-relations}.
There is nevertheless an explicit nondegenerate Hermitian form
on the retained algebraic prime module:
\[
 J(z,w)=\sum_p\left(z_{p,0}\overline{w_{p,1}}
                       +z_{p,1}\overline{w_{p,0}}\right).
 \tag{MC28}\label{mc:eq:indefinite-form}
\]
It satisfies $J(T_\lambda z,T_\lambda w)=\lambda J(z,w)$
and $J(Fz,Fw)=J(z,w)$. It has signature $(1,1)$ at every
prime, retains both original characters, and is not a positive
realization.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a prime vector $z$ with coordinates $(u_0,0)$, its action is
fixed. Equation
\eqref{mc:eq:weight-identity} gives $Q(z,z)=\lambda Q(z,z)$,
so $Q(z,z)=0$ by taking $\lambda\ne1$. For the other coordinate
it gives $\lambda^2Q(z,z)=\lambda Q(z,z)$ and the same
conclusion. The Cauchy--Schwarz inequality for a positive
semidefinite Hermitian form then makes these null vectors
orthogonal to every vector. Finite sums give the full prime
module in the radical, proving factorization through the actual
quotient. No completion assumption is needed for this argument.

In \eqref{mc:eq:indefinite-form}, every vector has finite prime
support, so the sum is defined. Its local matrix is
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
which is invertible and has eigenvalues $1$ and $-1$.
Substituting \eqref{mc:eq:prime-operators} gives the two asserted
identities term by term; the two Fourier minus signs cancel.
For clarity, the form is an explicit additional construction
from the original moment coordinates. Its constant has not been
inferred from a cohomological degree. The whole family on one
prime compatible with \eqref{mc:eq:weight-identity} has matrix
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&c\\\overline c&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$:
the same scaling equation forces both diagonal entries to vanish.
Fourier invariance forces $c$ real, and nondegeneracy is precisely
$c\ne0$. Formula \eqref{mc:eq:indefinite-form} specifies the value
$c=1$ in the displayed original coordinates.
\end{proof}

Completing this last explicitly specified form with the positive
coordinate norm $\sum_p(|z_{p,0}|^2+|z_{p,1}|^2)$ gives
$\ell^2(\{\text{primes}\})\otimes\C^2$, with a bounded
invertible fundamental symmetry given by the same exchange matrix.
This is a concrete indefinite Hilbert-space form retaining the
prime classes. Its scaling has the two actual characters, and its
Fourier operator is the stated negative exchange. It is not the
inherited marked quotient completion \eqref{mc:eq:completed-receiver};
the exact maps and kernels relating that positive completion to
the algebraic objects were proved above.
