\section{The finite projective receiver of the full arithmetic jets}
\label{sec:projective-jet-receiver}

The new full-support reconstruction supplied with this calculation uses
finite projective modules to receive ordinary matrices. We prove that
construction here and apply it to the complete jet pairing above.
The scalar object is the original $S=G_L(\mathbb C)$ of version 11
\cite{support}; $L$ is any nontrivial bounded distributive lattice.
No choice of Boolean branch or replacement of an amplitude zero by
absence occurs in these maps.

For positive integers $n$ let $P_n$ have entries $1^\bullet$ on
the diagonal and $e$ off the diagonal. The free identity has $\tau$
off the diagonal. Multiplication in the original semiring gives
\[
 P_n^2=P_n,\qquad
 \mathcal V_n:=P_nS^n
 =\{(a,x):a=1_L,\ x\in\mathbb C^n\}
       \sqcup\{(a,0):a<1_L\}.
 \tag{JC1}
\]
The displayed coordinates identify $(a,0)$ with the constant vector
$z_a$, and $(1_L,x)$ with its fully supported coordinate vector.
To verify the image exactly, if one input coordinate has top support,
each row acquires top support through multiplication by $1^\bullet$
or $e$, and its amplitude is the original coordinate amplitude.
If all supports are lower, every entry in the output has the common
join of those supports and zero amplitude; the join is allowed to
be top. Conversely each displayed vector is fixed by $P_n$.
This proves both the image and idempotence and realizes $\mathcal V_n$
as a retract of the free module, hence as finite projective.

For a matrix $A\in M_{m,n}(\mathbb C)$ let $\widehat A$ have
all entries supported, including every zero. Then
\[
 P_m\widehat A P_n=\widehat A,\qquad
 \widehat A\widehat B=\widehat{AB},\qquad
 \widehat {I_n}=P_n,\qquad \widehat 0=eP_n.
 \tag{JC2}
\]
Each matrix entry is a nonempty sum of supported products, so its
amplitude is the ordinary matrix product and its support is top even
when the sum cancels. This proves every formula in (JC2).
Consequently all invertible ordinary matrices become automorphisms
of these actual projective modules, with inverse $\widehat{A^{-1}}$.

More precisely, for arbitrary $X\in M_{m,n}(S)$, the entry supports
of $P_mXP_n$ are all the join of the supports of every entry of $X$;
its amplitude matrix is the original amplitude matrix. Therefore
\[
 P_mM_{m,n}(S)P_n
 =\{\widehat A:A\in M_{m,n}(\mathbb C)\}
       \sqcup\{z_a\mathbf1_{m,n}:a<1_L\}.
 \tag{JC3}
\]
Every matrix in the displayed set occurs. In the square case this
is the exact semiring $G_L(M_n(\mathbb C))$, with its possibly
noncommutative amplitude multiplication and identity $P_n$.
The support-preserving maps in (JC3) are precisely the fully
supported ones: they act by $(1,x)\mapsto(1,Ax)$ and fix every
lower support. A lower constant matrix instead meets all input
supports with $a$ and cannot preserve top. Composition is exactly
ordinary matrix composition. The relative zero map is $\widehat0$.
The relative zero object is $eS\simeq L$, with zero-dimensional
arithmetic fibre; the unique support-preserving arrows to and from
it justify that description. This includes dimension zero without
identifying the object with the absent semimodule.

With lexicographic ordering of tensor coordinates,
$P_n\otimes P_m=P_{nm}$: a diagonal entry is $1^\bullet$ and
every other entry has one or two factors $e$, whose product is $e$.
The usual explicit tensor basis maps of finite free semimodules
identify $\mathcal V_n\otimes_S\mathcal V_m$ with the image of
this projector. Thus the projective realization also retains every
ordinary tensor operation.

For original positive Hermitian matrices $G,H$, give $\mathcal V_n$
the pairing which equals $(x^*Gy)^\bullet$ on the top fibre and
$z_{a\wedge b}$ whenever one input is in lower support.
Sesquilinearity follows by checking whether a join reaches top:
lower inputs have zero amplitude, and their joins and meets follow
distributivity in $L$; on the top fibre it is ordinary
sesquilinearity. Hence the actual adjoint identity is
\[
 \widehat A^{\dagger_{G,H}}=\widehat{G^{-1}A^*H}.
 \tag{JC4}
\]
Testing both top inputs proves the amplitudes, and a lower input
on either side gives the same meet of supports. No new metric
is substituted for the source or target metric.

The trace on a finite projective retract is computed by inclusion,
the endomorphism, and the retraction in its free ambient module.
It is independent of the retract presentation: if maps $U,V$ give
inverse projective identifications, the equality
$\operatorname{tr}(XY)=\operatorname{tr}(YX)$ follows by writing
both finite double sums over the commutative scalar semiring and
interchanging their indices. Applying that identity to the retract
maps proves invariance. For (JC2) it gives
\[
 \operatorname{tr}_S(\widehat A)
       =(\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb C}A)^\bullet,
 \quad
 p\operatorname{tr}_S(\widehat A)=\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb C}A,
 \quad
 \chi\operatorname{tr}_S(\widehat A)=1_L.
 \tag{JC5}
\]
The last equality holds even if the amplitude trace is zero.
After scalar extension by $p:S\to\mathbb C$, the projector is
$I_n$. After extension by localization $\chi:S\to L=S[e^{-1}]$,
it is the all-one matrix, whose image is the common-coordinate
copy of $L$; every $\widehat A$ becomes the identity on this image.
These follow by tensoring the two retract arrows, so identify the
actual base changes, including their trace maps.

Now take the full packet $A_Z$ of (FJ1)--(FJ3), of dimension
$d=\sum_\rho m_\rho$, with every local unit retained. Choose the
ordered monomial jet coordinates already used there. Its perfect
residue pairing has an invertible matrix $D_R$. Equation (JC2)
therefore transports its full dual isomorphism to the projective
corner, with inverse $\widehat{D_R^{-1}}$. The transported pairing
has the same full amplitude $R$ and value $z_{a\wedge b}$ on
lower supports. The prime matrix and the exact duality give
\[
 \widehat{D_R}\,\widehat{\overline{\Pi_p}}
  =\widehat{p(\Pi_p^{-1})^*}\,\widehat{D_R}.
 \tag{JC6}
\]
This is the lifted equality of the original matrices in (FJ4)--(FJ7),
proved by (JC2), and retains every higher jet and the original factor
$p$. In particular the inverse prime matrix is an actual projective
automorphism without assuming it is a free split automorphism.

The Jacobian map $J_g$ from (FJ8)--(FJ10) has ordinary kernel the
nilradical and image the socle. Its supported-zero kernel on the
top fibre is precisely that same kernel, and every lower support
is retained with zero amplitude. Thus the exact support-preserving
kernel and quotient are
\[
 \mathcal V(\operatorname{nil}A_Z)\hookrightarrow\mathcal V(A_Z)
 \longrightarrow\mathcal V(A_Z/\operatorname{nil}A_Z),
 \qquad (a,x)\longmapsto(a,[x]).
 \tag{JC7}
\]
Here $\mathcal V(W)$ is (JC1) with top fibre $W$; for $W=0$
it is $L$. The last map has exactly the displayed fibrewise kernel,
is onto on every fibre, and preserves all support transitions.
The full residue pairing is perfect before this quotient; the
ordinary arithmetic trace pairing factors through it by (FJ10).
After localization every object and map in (JC7) is $L$ with the
identity, including its fibrewise zero object. This describes the
entire degeneration rather than assigning that identity a complex
trace of one. The amplitude and support trace receivers are exactly
(JC5), with the actual connecting scalar maps stated there.
