\subsection{The original map and its evaluated source}

Let $\mathcal P$ be all rational primes, and put
\begin{equation}\tag{GSB1}
\begin{gathered}
G=\mathbb Q^\times,\qquad G_+=\mathbb Q_{>0}^\times,\qquad
T=\mathcal S(\mathbb R)\otimes\bigotimes_p'
 (\mathcal S(\mathbb Q_p),\mathbf1_{\mathbb Z_p}),\\
(U_g\xi)(x)=\xi(g^{-1}x),\qquad
E\xi(x)=\sum_{q\in\mathbb Q^\times}\xi(qx),\\
\Omega=\{0,1\}^{\{\infty\}\sqcup\mathcal P},\qquad
B=\operatorname{LC}(\Omega,\mathbb C),\qquad
b=\bigotimes_v\left(\xi_v\longmapsto
             (\xi_v(0),\int_{\mathbb Q_v}\xi_v(x)\,dx)\right).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
At infinity $\mathbb Q_\infty=\mathbb R$ with Lebesgue measure;
at $p$ the self-dual additive measure has
$\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb Z_p)=1$. The real Schwartz and finite
Bruhat spaces retain their original topologies. Every tensor,
coinvariant, group chain, and quotient in this section is algebraic;
no closure of a group-relation subspace is taken.

For $r\ge1$ retain the ordered tensor powers and maps
\begin{equation}\tag{GSB2}
\begin{gathered}
T_r=T^{\otimes r},\quad B_r=B^{\otimes r}
       =\operatorname{LC}(\Omega^r,\mathbb C),\quad
b_r=b^{\otimes r},\quad E_r=E^{\otimes r},\\
P_r=(\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes r},\quad
\epsilon_r=\epsilon^{\otimes r},\quad
\epsilon(f)=(f(\mathbf0),f(\mathbf1)),\\
M_r=\ker\epsilon_r,\quad J_r=\ker b_r,\quad
Z_r^\theta=\ker E_r,\quad K_r^\theta=J_r\cap Z_r^\theta.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The action on $T_r$ is the diagonal $U_g^{\otimes r}$, including
the original sign. The action on $B_r$, at a configuration
$\omega=(\epsilon_{v,i})$, is
\begin{equation}\tag{GSB3}
(D_g f)(\omega)=\prod_p p^{v_p(g)(m_\infty(\omega)-m_p(\omega))}
 f(\omega),\qquad m_v(\omega)=\sum_{i=1}^r\epsilon_{v,i}.
\end{equation}
The group acts trivially on $P_r$. The finite product in GSB3
retains all nonzero valuations of $g$.

The full target of $E$ is the endpoint-enlarged space $A$ of GBC3:
write $T_0=\{\xi:\xi(0)=0,\int\xi=0\}$,
$V=E(T_0)$, $\mathscr B=\bigcap_{\beta\in\mathbb R}
|\cdot|^\beta\mathcal S(C_{\mathbb Q})$, and
$W=\overline V$ in that topology. Then
\begin{equation}\tag{GSB4}
\begin{gathered}
\eta_0(x)=(1-2\pi x^2)e^{-\pi x^2},\quad
\eta_1(x)=2\pi x^2e^{-\pi x^2},\quad
\xi_i=\eta_i\prod_p\mathbf1_{\mathbb Z_p},\quad\theta_i=E\xi_i,\\
A=\mathscr B\oplus\mathbb C\theta_0\oplus\mathbb C\theta_1,
\quad E_r:T_r\longrightarrow A^{\otimes r},
\quad H=\mathscr B/W.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
In particular $E$ has not been replaced by an endpoint-free transform.
The original identities
$\theta_0(y)=y^{-1}\theta_1(y^{-1})-1$ and
$\theta_1(y)=y^{-1}\theta_0(y^{-1})+y^{-1}$ remain in this target.

GBC17--19 proves the two exact coefficient sequences
\begin{equation}\tag{GSB5}
\begin{array}{ccccccccc}
0&\longrightarrow&K_r^\theta&\longrightarrow&Z_r^\theta
 &\xrightarrow{b_r}&M_r&\longrightarrow&0\\
 &&\big\downarrow&&\big\downarrow&&\big\downarrow\\
0&\longrightarrow&J_r&\longrightarrow&T_r
 &\xrightarrow{b_r}&B_r&\longrightarrow&0.
\end{array}
\end{equation}
For completeness, the essential surjectivity in the first row retains
the original image. At $r=1$, BW6 says
$\ker\epsilon=\sum_p(D_p-I)B$. Given $\xi\in T_0$, lift the
finitely many coefficients in such an expression for $b\xi$ to $T$
and subtract the corresponding $(U_p-I)$ relations. This produces
$j\in\ker b$ with $Ej=E\xi$, since $EU_p=E$. Thus
$E(\ker b)=V$. Tensoring and expanding
$E(T)=V\oplus\mathbb C\theta_0\oplus\mathbb C\theta_1$ proves
$E_r(J_r)=E_r(T_r)\cap\ker(\pi^{\otimes r})$, where
$\pi(f+c_0\theta_0+c_1\theta_1)=(c_0,c_1)$.
For $c\in M_r$, lift it to $t\in T_r$; the identity
$\pi^{\otimes r}E_rt=\epsilon_rb_rt=0$ supplies $u\in J_r$
with $E_ru=E_rt$. Then $z=t-u\in Z_r^\theta$ lifts $c$.
All of these are finite algebraic operations on the original spaces.

Define
\begin{equation}\tag{GSB6}
\begin{gathered}
\mathcal Z_r=\{\omega\in\Omega^r:m_p(\omega)=m_\infty(\omega)
                                      \text{ for every }p\},\\
\mathcal E_r=\{\omega:\epsilon_{v,i}\text{ is independent of }v
                                          \text{ for every }i\},\\
\mathcal I_r=\{f\in\operatorname{LC}(\mathcal Z_r,\mathbb C):
                                     f|_{\mathcal E_r}=0\},\qquad
\mathcal V_{\rm pr}=\bigoplus_{p\in\mathcal P}\mathbb C\vartheta_p.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The set $\mathcal E_r$ has $2^r$ points and is contained in
$\mathcal Z_r$. BW13, including its finite-stage proof, gives
\begin{equation}\tag{GSB7}
H_j(G,B_r)=\operatorname{LC}(\mathcal Z_r,\mathbb C)
                     \otimes\Lambda^j\mathcal V_{\rm pr},\qquad
H_j(G,P_r)=\mathbb C^{\mathcal E_r}
                     \otimes\Lambda^j\mathcal V_{\rm pr}.
\end{equation}
The map between them is restriction to $\mathcal E_r$ in the first
factor and the identity in the second. This is a statement about
the actual maps: at every finite prime stage, projection to the
balanced coordinate lines commutes with evaluation at the constant
columns. These constant columns are balanced. The Koszul differential
is zero on those lines, proving the stated formula before the
filtered union over prime sets.

Restriction is onto: an arbitrary function on the finite set
$\mathcal E_r$ extends by assigning its value to the cylinder having
the indicated infinity column. Therefore the homology map in GSB7
is onto in every degree. The homology sequence of
$0\to M_r\to B_r\to P_r\to0$ consequently proves
\begin{equation}\tag{GSB8}
H_j(G,M_r)\ \xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\
\mathcal I_r\otimes\Lambda^j\mathcal V_{\rm pr}
\ \subset H_j(G,B_r)\qquad(j\ge0).
\end{equation}
Both injectivity and the entire image are part of this equality;
no kernel contributed by $H_{j+1}(G,P_r)$ remains because the
preceding map in GSB7 is onto.

\subsection{A split subobject of the full source with zero higher homology}

Let $\mathcal U=\{\pm1\}\times\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{\times}$
act through its actual ideles, the first factor at infinity. Define
\begin{equation}\tag{GSB9}
Q\xi=\frac12\int_{\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{\times}}
       (U_{(1,u)}\xi+U_{(-1,u)}\xi)\,du,\qquad Q_r=Q^{\otimes r},
\end{equation}
where the compact Haar measure in this averaging operator has total
mass one. This measure specifies an exact projection; it does not
alter any additive measure, original function, or arithmetic pairing.
For a Bruhat test this integral is a finite average: only finitely
many primes have non-reference factors, and on their compact support
unit translations factor through finite quotients by local constancy.
The real sign average is the displayed sum with its factor $1/2$.

The operators commute with every rational and idele dilation, since
the idele group is abelian. Every local unit and real sign preserves
evaluation and the original additive integral. Hence
\begin{equation}\tag{GSB10}
Q_r^2=Q_r,\qquad b_rQ_r=b_r,\qquad
E_rQ_r=\widetilde Q_rE_r,
\end{equation}
where $\widetilde Q_r$ is the same finite compact averaging on the
theta target. The last identity follows by applying a unit dilation
to the absolutely convergent original rational sum, or termwise
to the finite average. The target average exists on $E_r(T_r)$;
this is all that is needed to prove preservation of $\ker E_r$.
Thus $Q_r$ preserves $J_r,Z_r^\theta,K_r^\theta$. Each of these
modules, and $T_r$, splits as its image under $Q_r$ plus its image
under $I-Q_r$. The second summand is contained in $\ker b_r$.
These are actual equivariant retracts inside the full modules;
none of the complementary modules is identified with zero.

Write $R=\mathbb C[G_+]$ and $\mathcal H=\mathcal S_{\rm even}(\mathbb R)$.
The exact source isomorphism of ABR3--4 is
\begin{equation}\tag{GSB11}
\begin{split}
\Theta:R\otimes\mathcal H&\longrightarrow QT,\\
t_a\otimes h&\longmapsto
  \bigl[(x_\infty,x_f)\longmapsto
           h(x_\infty/a)\mathbf1_{a\widehat{\mathbb Z}}(x_f)\bigr],\\
U_g\Theta(t_a\otimes h)&=\Theta(t_{ga}\otimes h)\quad(g>0).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Here the balls $\mathbf1_{a\widehat{\mathbb Z}}$ form a basis of
the finite unit-invariant Bruhat space. Locally this follows by
writing a radial locally constant compact function as a finite
sum of nested ball indicators with coefficients given by its
successive shell-value differences. Globally, expand its finitely
many non-reference factors. Independence follows by evaluating a
finite proposed relation at its rational ideles $a$: the incidence
matrix for divisibility can be ordered triangularly with diagonal
one. These facts prove spanning and independence with vector
coefficients as well. The inverse to GSB11 sends
$g_a(x_\infty)\mathbf1_{a\widehat{\mathbb Z}}$ to
$t_a\otimes h_a$, $h_a(x)=g_a(ax)$. In particular the formula is
an exact map retaining the original coordinates in its target.

The diagonal tensor is again free over $R$. Its explicit description is
\begin{equation}\tag{GSB12}
\begin{split}
R\otimes\mathbb C[G_+^{r-1}]\otimes\mathcal H^{\otimes r}
 &\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }Q_rT_r,\\
t_a\otimes[t_{b_2},\ldots,t_{b_r}]\otimes
       (h_1\otimes\cdots\otimes h_r)
 &\longmapsto\Theta(t_a\otimes h_1)\otimes
       \bigotimes_{i=2}^r\Theta(t_{ab_i}\otimes h_i).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
On a basis tensor its inverse has $a=a_1$ and $b_i=a_i/a_1$.
Rational dilation acts on $t_a$ alone, which proves freeness.
The full sign $-1$ acts trivially here: finite balls are unit
invariant and every real factor is even.

For completeness the infinite-prime homology calculation on a free
$R$ module has no positive-degree term. Resolve the trivial module
over $R_F=\mathbb C[t_p^{\pm1}:p\in F]$ by the Koszul complex on
$(t_p-1)_{p\in F}$. Successive quotients are Laurent polynomial
rings, so each next multiplication is injective. The one-variable
complex is exact; its successive mapping cones prove exactness for
each finite $F$. The full $R$ is free over $R_F$. Extend and take
the filtered union: a cycle and a bounding relation occur at finite
stages, and filtered unions of vector spaces preserve exactness.
This gives a free resolution of the trivial $R$ module. Tensoring
with the free module GSB12 retains exactness in positive degrees.
The order-two sign subgroup has an exact invariant projection
$(I+U_{-1}^{\otimes r})/2$, so contributes no positive-degree
homology in characteristic zero. Therefore
\begin{equation}\tag{GSB13}
H_j(G,Q_rT_r)=0\qquad(j\ge1).
\end{equation}
No such vanishing for the complementary full-source summand has
been assumed or inferred.

\subsection{Injection and the complete image of the secondary map}

Let $\partial^\theta_{r,j}$ be the connecting homomorphism of the
top row of GSB5. Applying $Q_r$ to the coefficient modules leaves
both rows exact with the same $M_r$ and $B_r$: any lift can be
replaced by its $Q_r$ image using GSB10. The lower-row connecting map
\[
\delta^Q_{r,j}:H_j(G,B_r)\longrightarrow H_{j-1}(G,Q_rJ_r)
\]
is injective for $j\ge1$, by GSB13 and the exact homology sequence.
For a cycle $c$ in $M_r$, choose a chain lift $z$ in $Q_rZ_r^\theta$.
Both connecting maps are represented by $dz$, with the same
differential and sign. Consequently the square
\begin{equation}\tag{GSB14}
\begin{array}{ccc}
H_j(G,M_r)&\xrightarrow{\partial^\theta_{r,j}}&
                       H_{j-1}(G,K_r^\theta)\\
\big\downarrow&&\big\downarrow Q_r\ \text{then inclusion}\\
H_j(G,B_r)&\xrightarrow{\delta^Q_{r,j}}&H_{j-1}(G,Q_rJ_r)
\end{array}
\end{equation}
commutes. The left arrow is injective by GSB8 and the bottom
arrow is injective by GSB13. If the upper-right class were zero,
its lower-right image would be zero; injectivity then forces the
original class to be zero. This proves
\begin{equation}\tag{GSB15}
\boxed{\quad
\ker\partial^\theta_{r,j}=0,\qquad
\operatorname{im}\partial^\theta_{r,j}
 \cong\mathcal I_r\otimes\Lambda^j\mathcal V_{\rm pr}
\quad(r\ge1,\ j\ge1).\quad}
\end{equation}
The isomorphism is the original connecting map on the exact domain
GSB8, not a dimension count. Its image lies in the split spherical
summand of $H_{j-1}(G,K_r^\theta)$, even though that target retains
the entire original source. This evaluates both the whole kernel
and the whole image, without computing an unrelated complementary
summand of the target or replacing the image by an intersection
quotient left unevaluated.

Here are all the components and their idele action. For $0\le m\le r$,
\begin{equation}\tag{GSB16}
\begin{gathered}
\mathcal Z_{r,m}=\binom{[r]}m\times
                        \prod_p\binom{[r]}m,\qquad
\mathcal E_{r,m}=\{(A,A,A,\ldots):A\in\tbinom{[r]}m\},\\
\mathcal I_r=\bigoplus_{m=1}^{r-1}
  \{f\in\operatorname{LC}(\mathcal Z_{r,m},\mathbb C):
                                          f|_{\mathcal E_{r,m}}=0\},\\
U_a\partial^\theta_{r,j}(f\otimes\omega)
 =\left(|a_\infty|_\infty\prod_p|a_p|_p\right)^m
                      \partial^\theta_{r,j}(f\otimes\omega).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The first equality identifies the actual chosen coordinate sets at
every place. At $m=0,r$ the space is a singleton endpoint, so its
vanishing-at-endpoints subspace is zero. The last formula follows
by the original product of local integration factors and equivariance
of the connecting map: on a balanced configuration each $m_v=m$,
so that product is exactly the one displayed. No factor depending
on $j$ appears. Each interior component has countably infinite
dimension: finite cylinder stages give a countable spanning union,
and arbitrarily many distinct prime coordinates give arbitrarily
many independent endpoint-vanishing cylinders.

At the first mixed tensor $r=2$, encode a column of sum one by the
index of its entry equal to one. With
$X=\{1,2\}^{\{\infty\}\sqcup\mathcal P}$, the result is
\begin{equation}\tag{GSB17}
\operatorname{im}\partial^\theta_{2,1}
 \cong\{f\in\operatorname{LC}(X,\mathbb C):
        f(1,1,\ldots)=f(2,2,\ldots)=0\}
              \otimes\bigoplus_p\mathbb C\vartheta_p.
\end{equation}
Every idele acts on this entire image by its original modulus
$|a_\infty|_\infty\prod_p|a_p|_p$. Its modulus weight in the
convention $|a|^{w/2}$ is therefore $w=2$, not $w=1$.

\subsection{Exact representatives for every class and a nonzero witness}

Order primes increasingly. Represent a vector in GSB8 as a finite
sum of $f\otimes\vartheta_{p_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\vartheta_{p_j}$.
Choose a finite $F$ containing every coefficient coordinate and every
$p_i$. Extend each coefficient from the balanced finite patterns
by zero on the unbalanced patterns of $\Omega_F^r$; denote this
extension by $c$. Its endpoint values are zero. For each prime
appearing in the wedge, $(D_p-I)c=0$, so it is an actual Koszul
cycle. It restricts to the original $f$ on $\mathcal Z_r$.

Decompose $c$ into its finitely many pattern-cylinder indicators.
For a prime $q\in F$ use the exact moment sections
\begin{equation}\tag{GSB18}
\phi_{q,0}=\frac{q\mathbf1_{q\mathbb Z_q}-\mathbf1_{\mathbb Z_q}}{q-1},
\qquad
\phi_{q,1}=\frac{q(\mathbf1_{\mathbb Z_q}-\mathbf1_{q\mathbb Z_q})}{q-1}.
\end{equation}
Their evaluation/integral pairs are $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$:
in particular the first integral is $(q q^{-1}-1)/(q-1)=0$,
and the second is $q(1-q^{-1})/(q-1)=1$.
Use $\eta_0,\eta_1$ at infinity and the reference indicator at
all primes outside $F$. Tensor these sections in their original
factor order to obtain a lift $t_A\in Q_rT_r$ of each nonzero
pattern cylinder $c_A$.

Every such pattern differs from a constant-column endpoint: its
coefficient at every endpoint pattern is zero. Thus there are
$q\in F$ and a factor $i$ for which
$d=\epsilon_{\infty,i}-\epsilon_{q,i}$ is $1$ or $-1$.
Let $U_q^{[i]}$ mean rational dilation on that factor alone.
Then
\begin{equation}\tag{GSB19}
z_A=\frac{(U_q^{[i]}-I)t_A}{q^d-1},\qquad
b_rz_A=c_A,\qquad E_rz_A=0.
\end{equation}
The denominator is nonzero. Its numerator has boundary
$(q^d-1)c_A$ by the actual one-factor form of GSB3; applying
$E$ in that factor gives zero by rational reindexing. Thus
GSB19 is an explicit original theta-zero lift. Sum these lifts
with the original cylinder coefficients to obtain $z$ for $c$.
The complete connecting representative is
\begin{equation}\tag{GSB20}
\sum_{h=1}^j(-1)^{h-1}(U_{p_h}^{\otimes r}-I)z
 \otimes\vartheta_{p_1}\wedge\cdots
       \widehat{\vartheta_{p_h}}\cdots\wedge\vartheta_{p_j}.
\end{equation}
Each coefficient has zero boundary since the original $c$ is
balanced at those primes, and zero theta image since $E_rz=0$.
It lies in the required complex of $K_r^\theta$. Changing lifts
changes it by a boundary there. Refining $F$ represents the same
class: the finite-stage unbalanced terms contract in the Koszul
complex, as in BW15--16, while restriction retains the identical
$f$. Alternatively uniqueness follows directly from GSB8 and the
well-defined connecting map. Thus these are representatives for
the entire module GSB15, with all cylinder relations and every prime.

For the concrete tensor-two witness choose any prime $p$. Put
\begin{equation}\tag{GSB21}
\begin{gathered}
a_p=\eta_1\otimes\phi_{p,0}\otimes
                       \bigotimes_{\ell\ne p}\mathbf1_{\mathbb Z_\ell},\quad
d_p=\eta_0\otimes\phi_{p,1}\otimes
                       \bigotimes_{\ell\ne p}\mathbf1_{\mathbb Z_\ell},\\
c_p=\mathbf1_{\{\epsilon_{\infty,1}=1,\epsilon_{\infty,2}=0,
                    \epsilon_{p,1}=0,\epsilon_{p,2}=1\}},\quad
z_p=\frac{((U_p-I)a_p)\otimes d_p}{p-1},\\
\partial^\theta_{2,1}[c_p\otimes\vartheta_p]
       =[(U_p\otimes U_p-I)z_p]\ne0.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The other coordinates of $c_p$ are unrestricted. It is balanced
at its prime, vanishes on all four endpoints, and is nonzero on
$\mathcal Z_2$. Its class in GSB8 is nonzero because
$\vartheta_p\ne0$ and the coefficient restriction is nonzero.
Equation GSB15 then proves the final inequality. This is a proof
of nonvanishing in the original joint-kernel coinvariants, not
an inference from a nonzero chain or finite sample.

There is a finite exact inverse on the entire reached degree-one
image. In GSB12 write $Q_2k=\Theta_2(\sum_a t_a\otimes v_a)$,
where $\Theta_2$ denotes that displayed isomorphism, for a
representative of a reached class. Only finitely many coefficients
are nonzero. Its augmentation $\sum_a v_a$ is zero: connecting
representatives are sums of diagonal rational differences, and
changing a representative by a relation in $K_2^\theta$ preserves
this fact after applying $Q_2$. For integer $n$ define
\begin{equation}\tag{GSB21a}
L_n(t)=\begin{cases}
\sum_{h=0}^{n-1}t^h,&n>0,\\
0,&n=0,\\
-\sum_{h=n}^{-1}t^h,&n<0,
\end{cases}\qquad
L_p(a)=\left(\prod_{\ell<p}t_\ell^{v_\ell(a)}\right)
                           L_{v_p(a)}(t_p).
\end{equation}
The identity $(t-1)L_n(t)=t^n-1$ and telescoping the ordered
finite prime factorization give
$t_a-1=\sum_p(t_p-1)L_p(a)$, including all negative valuations.
Therefore define, with the original free-module coordinates,
\begin{equation}\tag{GSB21b}
v_p'=\Theta_2\!\left(\sum_aL_p(a)\otimes v_a\right),\qquad
\mathcal D(k)=\sum_p (b_2v_p')|_{\mathcal Z_2}\otimes\vartheta_p,
\qquad Q_2k=\sum_p(U_p^{\otimes2}-I)v_p'.
\end{equation}
These are finite sums for each input, with no restriction on which
primes an input may involve. Since $b_2Q_2k=0$, the coefficient
chain $\sum_pb_2v_p'\otimes\vartheta_p$ is a cycle in $B_2$.
The finite-stage Koszul restriction of BW15--16 sends its class to
exactly $\mathcal D(k)$. The ordinary connecting map
$\delta^Q_{2,1}$ sends it to $[Q_2k]$ in $H_0(G,Q_2J_2)$.
That map is injective, so the detected source is independent of
the difference decomposition and is unchanged under every
coinvariant relation from $K_2^\theta$. Naturality GSB14 now gives
the exact inverse identity
\begin{equation}\tag{GSB21c}
\mathcal D\,\partial^\theta_{2,1}=I
\quad\text{on }\mathcal I_2\otimes\mathcal V_{\rm pr},\qquad
\mathcal D((U_p\otimes U_p-I)z_p)
                         =c_p|_{\mathcal Z_2}\otimes\vartheta_p.
\end{equation}
This detects the entire computed image using finite source
coefficients. It does not claim that the whole target consists
of reached classes.

\subsection{The actual arithmetic receiver and every support label}

Define $q_A:A\to H$ by
$q_A(f+c_0\theta_0+c_1\theta_1)=[f]$. GBC4 proves
$q_AE=0$. With the relative-cone differential
$d(a,t)=(d_Aa+E_rt,-d_Tt)$, the mixed connecting class has
representative $(0,-dz)$ whenever $z$ is the theta-zero lift
GSB19. The canonical interior map of GBC13 is
$(a,t)\mapsto q_A^{\otimes r}a$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\tag{GSB22}
(0,-dz)\longmapsto0\quad\text{in }C_\bullet(G,H^{\otimes r})
\end{equation}
for every class just proved nonzero. Its image in this arithmetic
receiver is exactly zero. The construction establishes a nonzero
extension together with its annihilation by a specific existing
receiver. It does not infer that all other comparisons are zero,
and supplies no sign for the full original Weil pairing.

The relative-cone class itself is nonzero as well. To verify this
without confusing the source and target, compute full-group homology
by the prime Koszul complex on the exact diagonal-sign-even
summands. Put $I_r^A=\ker(\pi^{\otimes r})\subset A^{\otimes r}$.
The rational action on $I_r^A$ is trivial, so its Koszul differential
is zero. In the cone of $C_\bullet(G,J_r)\xrightarrow{E_r}
C_\bullet(G,I_r^A)$, if a cycle $(0,k)$ is a boundary then
\[
(0,k)=d(a,t)=(E_rt,-d_Tt).
\]
Thus $E_rt=0$ and $t$ is a chain in $J_r\cap\ker E_r=K_r^\theta$;
hence $k$ was already a boundary in that kernel complex.
Conversely a boundary there is visibly a cone boundary. This proves
\begin{equation}\tag{GSB22a}
\kappa_j:H_{j-1}(G,K_r^\theta)\longrightarrow
 H_j\operatorname{Cone}\bigl(C_\bullet(G,J_r)
                       \xrightarrow{E_r}C_\bullet(G,I_r^A)\bigr),
\qquad[k]\longmapsto[(0,k)]
\quad\text{is injective}.
