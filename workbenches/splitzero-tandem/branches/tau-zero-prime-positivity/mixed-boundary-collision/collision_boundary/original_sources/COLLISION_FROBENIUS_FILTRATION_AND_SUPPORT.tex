This calculation concerns the existing integral collision prism and
its actual operators. It constructs the coefficient maps into the
abelian-sheaf cohomology of the full Split-Zero spectrum. No numerical
range is used. The filtration computed here is a monodromy filtration
of specified nilpotent operators; it is not an assertion of arithmetic
purity or a proof of RH.

\section{CFF1: the original coefficient object}
Put \(O=\OO\), retain an arbitrary \(c\in O\), and retain the algebra
\[
 C=O[\epsilon,T]/(\epsilon^2,T^2-6\epsilon),\qquad
 1,\epsilon,T,z=\epsilon T.
 \tag{CFF1}
\]
Successive monic division proves that the displayed elements are an
\(O\)-basis. The nilradical is
\[
 M=O\epsilon\oplus OT\oplus Oz.
 \tag{CFF2}
\]
Indeed \(M^2=6O\epsilon\oplus Oz\), \(M^3=6Oz\), and \(M^4=0\), while
\(C/M=O\) is reduced. These statements follow from
\(\epsilon^2=0\), \(\epsilon T=z\), \(T^2=6\epsilon\),
\(Tz=0\), and \(\epsilon z=0\).

The original Frobenius lift and divided residue are
\[
 F(\epsilon)=0,\quad F(T)=9cz,\quad F|_O=\id,\qquad
 N(\epsilon)=0,\quad N(T)=-z/8,\quad N|_O=0 .
 \tag{CFF3}
\]
The first is an algebra map: the two relations map to zero.
Modulo 3 it is cubing on the generators and scalars. The second is
an \(O\)-derivation: its value on \(\epsilon^2\) is zero and its
value on \(T^2-6\epsilon\) is
\(-\epsilon T^2/4=-3\epsilon^2/2=0\). The original undivided residue
is \(D=3N\); this factor is retained throughout. In particular,
\[
 F^2=\eta\pi\text{ on }C,\qquad
 F_M^2=0,\quad N_M^2=0,\quad N_MF_M=F_MN_M=0,
 \tag{CFF4}
\]
where \(\pi:C\to O\) is augmentation and \(\eta:O\to C\) is the
scalar inclusion. For \(c\ne0\), \(F_M\) has image \(9cOz\); for
\(c=0\), its image is zero.

The pair \((C,(3+\epsilon))\), including this entire Frobenius family,
is the bounded prism proved in DP1--19 of
\texttt{DISTINGUISHED\_COLLISION\_PRISM\_DERIVATION.md}.
In particular, \(C/(3+\epsilon)=(O/9)[T]/T^2\) and the prism map
to \((C,(3))\) is \(F\), not the identity. Nothing below changes
the prism ideal or the parameter \(c\).

\section{CFF2: the actual cotangent Frobenius}
The two defining equations are a regular sequence in \(O[\epsilon,T]\):
\(\epsilon^2\) is regular in that polynomial ring, and modulo it
\(T^2-6\epsilon\) is monic in \(T\) and therefore regular. The
relative cotangent presentation in degrees \(-1,0\) is
\[
 L=[C f_1\oplus C f_2 \xrightarrow{A}
                 C\,d\epsilon\oplus C\,dT],\qquad
 A=\begin{pmatrix}2\epsilon&-6\\0&2T\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{CFF5}
\]
Its degree-minus-one cohomology has the free \(O\)-basis
\[
 H=\ker A=Ov_1\oplus Ov_2\oplus Ov_3,\quad
 v_1=(\epsilon,0),\quad v_2=(z,0),\quad v_3=(3T,z).
 \tag{CFF6}
\]
To check exhaustiveness, the lower equation for \((a,b)\) gives
\(Tb=0\), hence \(b=t z\) by comparison in CFF1. The upper equation
is \(\epsilon a=3t z\); the scalar coefficient of \(a\) is zero
and its \(T\)-coefficient is \(3t\). This is exactly CFF6.

Lift \(F\) to the polynomial ring by
\(\epsilon\mapsto0,\ T\mapsto9c\epsilon T\). Its images of the
relations before quotienting are
\[
 F(f_1)=0,\qquad F(f_2)=81c^2T^2f_1.
\]
Consequently the actual \(F\)-semilinear chain map has matrices
\[
 U=\begin{pmatrix}0&486c^2\epsilon\\0&0\end{pmatrix}
 \quad\text{in degree }-1,\qquad
 V=\begin{pmatrix}0&9cT\\0&9c\epsilon\end{pmatrix}
 \quad\text{in degree }0.
 \tag{CFF7}
\]
The rule on a vector is \(a\mapsto U F(a)\) or \(a\mapsto V F(a)\),
not ordinary multiplication by the displayed matrix. Directly,
\[
 AU=0=VF(A):
\]
the only possibly nonzero entries in either product are multiples
of \(\epsilon^2\) or \(\epsilon T^2=6\epsilon^2\).
The polynomial calculation proves that this is the induced
cotangent map, and also checks its chain identity.
The second coefficients of the three vectors in CFF6 are \(0,0,z\),
and \(F(z)=0\). Therefore
\[
                         F_H=0.
 \tag{CFF8}
\]

The divided Lie derivative induced by \(N\) on \(H\) is
\[
 N_Hv_1=N_Hv_2=0,\qquad N_Hv_3=-15v_2/8.
 \tag{CFF9}
\]
Here the factor 5 has not disappeared. On the polynomial relation
generators the derivation sends
\[
 N(f_1)=0,\qquad N(f_2)=-\tfrac32 f_1-\tfrac14\epsilon f_2.
\]
Applying its coefficient derivation to \(v_3=(3T,z)\) contributes
\(-3z/8\) in the first coordinate, and its action on the second
relation contributes \(-3z/2\). Their sum is \(-15z/8\).
The other two cycles map to zero. Thus CFF9 is the Lie action
of the actual divided residue, with \(D_H=3N_H\).

\section{CFF3: the old injection and its Frobenius defect}
Retain the existing \(C\)-linear map
\[
 \kappa:M\longrightarrow H,\qquad
 \epsilon\longmapsto3v_1,\quad
 T\longmapsto v_3-2v_2,\quad z\longmapsto3v_2.
 \tag{CFF10}
\]
It is injective by comparing the three free basis coefficients.
For linearity, the actions in \(H\) are
\[
 \epsilon v_1=\epsilon v_2=0,\quad \epsilon v_3=3v_2,\qquad
 Tv_1=v_2,\quad Tv_2=0,\quad Tv_3=18v_1.
\]
These identities check both generator actions in CFF10, hence
linearity for all of \(C\). Its cokernel is
\[
 H/\kappa M=(O/3)\{[v_1],[v_2]\},\qquad T[v_1]=[v_2],\
 T[v_2]=0,\ \epsilon[v_i]=0.
 \tag{CFF11}
\]
The residue comparison and the Frobenius defect are exactly
\[
 N_H\kappa=5\kappa N_M,\qquad
 \Delta_F:=\kappa F_M-F_H\kappa,\qquad
 \Delta_F(T)=27cv_2,\quad \Delta_F(\epsilon)=\Delta_F(z)=0.
 \tag{CFF12}
\]
For \(c\ne0\) this defect is nonzero in the free module \(H\).
It is not removed by inverting 3. For \(c=0\) it is zero.
Thus the existing injection is preserved as a map with its exact
defect, rather than being declared Frobenius-compatible.

\section{CFF4: differentials give a compatible integral receiver}
Set \(\Omega=H^0(L)=\Omega_{C/O}\). Use the actual elements
\[
 u=d\epsilon,\quad v=dT,\quad q=\epsilon dT,\quad
 w=T\,d\epsilon-2\epsilon dT .
 \tag{CFF13}
\]
Here \(q\) is a differential, not the earlier algebra quotient map.
As an \(O\)-module,
\[
 \Omega=Ou\oplus Ov\oplus Oq\oplus(O/3)w .
 \tag{CFF14}
\]
For a full presentation check, start with the eight basis elements
of \(C u\oplus C v\). Multiples of the two columns of CFF5 impose
\(\epsilon u=0,\ z u=0,\ Tv=3u,\ zv=0\), and
\(3(Tu-2\epsilon v)=0\). All remaining multiples of those columns
are consequences: multiplication of the latter relation by \(T\)
or \(\epsilon\) yields zero after these relations. Eliminating
\(\epsilon u,zu,Tv,zv\), and then writing \(Tu=w+2q\), gives
exactly CFF14, including the order-three generator.

The universal differential restricts to an \(O\)-linear map
\[
 d_M:M\longrightarrow\Omega,\qquad
 \epsilon\mapsto u,\quad T\mapsto v,\quad z\mapsto w+3q.
 \tag{CFF15}
\]
It is not asserted to be \(C\)-linear; its actual rule is Leibniz.
It is injective: a zero linear combination of its images has zero
\(u,v,q\) coefficients, forcing all three source coefficients to
vanish in the torsionfree ring \(O\).
In the cokernel, \(u=v=0,\ w=-3q,\ 3w=0\), giving the exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow M\xrightarrow{d_M}\Omega
   \xrightarrow{\rho}O/9\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 \rho(q)=1,\quad\rho(w)=-3,\quad\rho(u)=\rho(v)=0 .
 \tag{CFF16}
\]
No shorter exponent is substituted. Its extension class, using the
lift \(q\) of \(1\), is
\[
                   9q=3d_M(z),\qquad [3z]\in M/9M.
 \tag{CFF17}
\]
It has exact order 3, so CFF16 does not split as \(O\)-modules.
Indeed a section would provide a lift of 1 killed by 9, while
changing \(q\) by \(d_M(m)\) changes \(9q\) by \(9d_M(m)\);
the element \(3z\) is not in \(9M\).

The induced Frobenius and Lie actions on \(\Omega\) are
\[
 \begin{array}{c|rrrr}
       &u&v&q&w\\ \hline
 F_\Omega&0&27cq&0&0\\
 N_\Omega&0&-(w+3q)/8&0&0 .
 \end{array}
 \tag{CFF18}
\]
For Frobenius, \(F_\Omega(v)=d(9cz)=9c(w+3q)=27cq\)
because \(3w=0\), and all other values follow from semilinearity.
For the Lie action, \(N_\Omega(v)=d(-z/8)\);
the coefficient rule gives the other three zero values.
This proves
\[
 F_\Omega d_M=d_MF_M,\qquad N_\Omega d_M=d_MN_M,\qquad
 F_\Omega^2=N_\Omega^2=F_\Omega N_\Omega=N_\Omega F_\Omega=0.
 \tag{CFF19}
\]
The induced actions on the quotient \(O/9\) in CFF16 are both zero.
The nonzero Frobenius on \(\Omega\) for \(c\ne0\) also proves
that CFF7 is not a nullhomotopic cotangent chain map: a
nullhomotopic map induces zero on every cohomology group.

\section{CFF5: image/kernel monodromy and the integral middle piece}
For each specified square-zero operator \(N_E\), use the increasing
filtration
\[
 W_jE=0\ (j<-1),\quad W_{-1}E=\im N_E,\quad
 W_0E=\ker N_E,\quad W_jE=E\ (j\ge1).
 \tag{CFF20}
\]
It satisfies \(N_EW_j\subset W_{j-2}\) and
\[
 N_E:\operatorname{Gr}^W_1 E\xrightarrow{\sim}
                  \operatorname{Gr}^W_{-1}E .
 \tag{CFF21}
\]
The last statement is the first isomorphism theorem for the actual
map \(N_E\). It is an integral statement for this specified
filtration. It does not assert that every graded piece is free.

For \(M,\Omega,H\) the whole calculation is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
 E&W_{-1}E&W_0E&\operatorname{Gr}^W_0E\\ \hline
 M&Oz&O\epsilon\oplus Oz&O\epsilon\\
 \Omega&O(w+3q)&Ou\oplus Oq\oplus(O/3)w&Ou\oplus O/9\\
 H&3Ov_2&Ov_1\oplus Ov_2&Ov_1\oplus(O/3)[v_2].
\end{array}
 \tag{CFF22}
\]
The first two rows follow from CFF3 and CFF18. The last follows
from \(-15/8=3(-5/8)\), where \(-5/8\) is a unit in \(O\).
The \(O/9\) in the middle row has generator \([q]\) and relation
\([w]=-3[q]\). The top graded pieces are freely generated by
\([T],[v],[v_3]\); the bottom arrows CFF21 have respective
coefficients \(-1/8,-1/8,-5/8\) when the displayed bottom
generators \(z,w+3q,3v_2\) are used.

The map \(d_M\) is strict for these filtrations. On the bottom
and top graded pieces it is an isomorphism; on the middle piece
it is \(O\epsilon\to Ou\oplus O/9,\ \epsilon\mapsto u\).
To prove strictness directly, a vector \(a u+b v+t(w+3q)\)
lies in \(\ker N_\Omega\) exactly when \(b=0\), and lies in
\(\im N_\Omega\) exactly when \(a=b=0\).
Thus the full integral discrepancy of CFF16 is the middle
graded torsion, not a discarded contribution.

The map \(\kappa:(M,5N_M)\to(H,N_H)\) is also strict:
its intersections with \(Ov_1\oplus Ov_2\) and \(3Ov_2\)
are \(3Ov_1\oplus3Ov_2\) and \(3Ov_2\), respectively.
It is an isomorphism on the outer graded pieces and has the
two-dimensional \(O/3\) cokernel of CFF11 in the middle.
Its Frobenius defect CFF12 sends the top step into the bottom
step; hence it is invisible on the associated graded. All
three Frobenius operators are zero on their associated graded
pieces although \(F_M\) and \(F_\Omega\) are nonzero when \(c\ne0\).

After tensoring with \(\KK\), CFF20 is the centered monodromy
filtration of each retained square-zero operator: the only
nontrivial required graded isomorphism is CFF21. Rationalizing
does not authorize replacing CFF22. For example, saturating
\(3Ov_2\subset H\) replaces it by \(Ov_2\) and changes the
outer integral map to multiplication by \(-15/8\), whose
cokernel is \(O/3\). Saturation is a different, explicitly
comparable lattice, not the original image lattice.

\section{CFF6: all tensor powers and dual evaluation}
All tensors in this section are over \(O\), and all duals are
\(O\)-linear. No comultiplication of \(C\) or diagonal
\(C\)-module action is being assumed.
For \(k\ge1\), retain \(M^{\otimes k}\) with
\[
 F_k=F_M^{\otimes k},\qquad
 N_k=\sum_{i=1}^k1^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes N_M\otimes
                       1^{\otimes(k-i)} .
 \tag{CFF23}
\]
For a word in \(\{\epsilon,T,z\}^k\), \(N_k^r\) is the sum over
its \(r\)-element subsets of positions occupied by \(T\),
replacing those entries by \(z\), with coefficient
\[
                         r!(-1/8)^r .
 \tag{CFF24}
\]
This follows by expanding the \(r\)-th power in CFF23: repeated
positions give zero and the \(r!\) distinct orders of any
chosen subset give the same tensor word. It proves
\(N_k^{k+1}=0\) and \(N_k^k(T^{\otimes k})=
k!(-1/8)^k z^{\otimes k}\ne0\). In particular the length
of these nilpotent strings is not bounded independently of \(k\).
The original residue on the tensor power is \(D_k=3N_k\);
its \(r\)-th power has the additional factor \(3^r\).

Frobenius has the exact all-\(k\) formula
\[
 F_k(T^{\otimes k})=(9c)^kz^{\otimes k},\qquad
 F_k(\text{every other original word})=0.
 \tag{CFF25}
\]
Thus \(F_k^2=0\), \(F_kN_k=N_kF_k=0\), including \(c=0\).
Increasing tensor degree retains longer monodromy strings,
but does not turn this Frobenius into an invertible operator.
The maps \(d_M^{\otimes k}\) intertwine both operators with
their tensor counterparts on \(\Omega^{\otimes k}\), by
expanding each tensor summand. No injectivity assertion for
this tensor map is made by using the injection CFF15:
\(\Omega\) contains torsion and is not flat over \(O\).

On \(M^\vee=\operatorname{Hom}_O(M,O)\) the actual dual
nilpotent is \(N^\vee=-N_M^t\); it satisfies
\[
 (N^\vee\lambda)(m)+\lambda(N_Mm)=0 .
 \tag{CFF26}
\]
The ordinary Frobenius pullback \(F^t\lambda=\lambda\circ F_M\)
satisfies the different, contravariant identity
\[
 (F^t\lambda)(m)=\lambda(F_Mm).
 \tag{CFF27}
\]
There is no endomorphism \(F_{\rm dual}\) for which the
ordinary evaluation to \((O,\id)\) obeys
\((F_{\rm dual}\lambda)(F_Mm)=\lambda(m)\) for all \(m,\lambda\):
take \(m=\epsilon\), \(F_Mm=0\), and the coordinate functional
\(\lambda(\epsilon)=1\). This is an explicit failure of that
particular proposed duality, with the valid transpose map
CFF27 retained. With both factors acted on by \(F^t,F_M\),
the evaluation equals \(\lambda(F_M^2m)=0\).

More generally any map from \(M^{\otimes k}\), \(\Omega\), or
\(H\), intertwining these operators with an injective
Frobenius on a target, is zero: applying Frobenius twice
reduces its image to zero, and injectivity cancels those two
applications. In the opposite direction an intertwining
map from an invertible-Frobenius object is zero by
surjectivity of its squared Frobenius. These are exact Hom
calculations in the category of modules with an endomorphism.
The objects with their nilpotent Frobenius remain present.
For \(C\), the sequential colimit along \(F\) is exactly \(O\):
the augmentation induces this map, every \(m\in M\) dies
after two transitions, and constants survive unchanged.

\section{CFF7: the coefficient map to the whole spectrum}
Let \(L\) be any finite nontrivial bounded distributive
lattice and let
\[
 Y=\Spec G_L(\mathbb Z)=S\triangleright X,\qquad
 S=\SpecLat L,\quad X=\Spec\mathbb Z.
 \tag{CFF28}
\]
Here \(G_L(\mathbb Z)\) retains all \((0,\lambda)\) and all
\((a,1_L)\); addition uses join and multiplication uses meet.
Its two prime strata and all opens are FL1--4 of
\texttt{15\_full\_finite\_support\_recollement.tex}.
In particular \(D(e)=S,\ V(e)=X\), and \(e=(0,1_L)\ne
\tau=(0,0_L)\). No arithmetic prime or support stratum is
removed.

The receiving category here is abelian sheaves, with the
displayed constant \(O\)-action. For an \(O\)-module \(E\),
forget its additional coefficient-ring action, take its
constant sheaf \(c_XE\), and apply the actual exact universal
coupling of FL8--10:
\[
 \mathcal U(E)=U_S(c_XE)
       =(c_XE,c_SE,\Delta).
 \tag{CFF29}
\]
The generic germ of \(c_XE\) is \(E\), and \(\Delta\) is its
constant section on every component of \(S\). Thus every
stalk is the original \(E\). An \(O\)-linear map is the same
map on every stalk and commutes with these restrictions.
This constructs the required coefficient-forgetful and
constant-sheaf arrows; it uses no map from \(\mathbb C\)
to \(O\) or to \(\KK\). It imposes no additive semiring
module structure on these abelian sheaves.

The integral cochains on \(S\) are
\[
 C^n(S,E)=\prod_{s_0<\cdots<s_n}E,\qquad
 (db)_{s_0<\cdots<s_{n+1}}
 =b_{s_1<\cdots<s_{n+1}}+
   \sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(-1)^i
     b_{s_0<\cdots<\widehat{s_i}<\cdots<s_{n+1}}.
 \tag{CFF30}
\]
They compute \(R\Gamma(S,c_SE)\) also for the integral
and torsion coefficients here. To verify the extension
of FL14--15 beyond rational coefficients, use its
resolution by products of \(I_t(E)\), direct images
from one-point subspaces. The functor \(I_t\) is exact
(check its stalks) and preserves injectives (it is right
adjoint to the exact stalk functor). Applying \(I_t\)
to an injective resolution of \(E\), then taking global
sections, gives that same resolution of \(E\).
Thus \(I_t(E)\) is \(\Gamma\)-acyclic without requiring
that \(E\) be injective. The finite product terms are
acyclic, and the stalk contraction obtained by adjoining
the maximal point of a smallest open proves exactness
of the augmented resolution over any coefficient group.
This proves CFF30. The restrictions and alternating
signs are exactly those in FL15.

Let \(P_S=\Cone(O[0]\xrightarrow{\Delta}C^\bullet(S,O))[-1]\).
For an explicit sign convention, \(P_S^0=O\),
\(P_S^j=C^{j-1}(S,O)\) for \(j\ge1\), and its
differentials are \(-\Delta\) from degree 0 and
\(-d\) in the subsequent degrees. This is the
unmodified shifted-cone convention. Every term is
finite free over \(O\). FL11--12 and CFF30 now give
\[
 R\Gamma_X(Y,\mathcal U(E))=P_S\otimes_O E,\qquad
 R\Gamma(Y,\mathcal U(E))=E[0].
 \tag{CFF31}
\]
These are natural quasi-isomorphisms. In particular,
all maps CFF12 and CFF15--19 act on the exact cochain
model without collapsing any support label.

\section{CFF8: the integral extension in supported cohomology}
Since the terms of \(P_S\) are free, CFF16 gives the
short exact sequence of actual complexes
\[
 0\longrightarrow P_S\otimes M
 \xrightarrow{1\otimes d_M}P_S\otimes\Omega
 \xrightarrow{1\otimes\rho}P_S/9P_S\longrightarrow0 .
 \tag{CFF32}
\]
The resulting connecting map has a complete cochain
description. If \(\bar a\) is a degree-\(j\) cocycle
modulo 9, lift it to \(a\in P_S^j\) and write
\(d a=9b\). The lift to the middle complex is
\(a\otimes q\), whose differential is
\[
 9b\otimes q=3b\otimes d_M(z).
 \tag{CFF33}
\]
Hence the connecting homomorphism is exactly
\[
 \partial[\bar a]=[3b\otimes z]
       \in H^{j+1}(P_S\otimes M).
 \tag{CFF34}
\]
This is 3 times the ordinary mod-9 Bockstein followed
by the coefficient map \(1\mapsto z\). If the lift is
changed by \(9a'\), then \(b\) changes by \(da'\) and
the result changes by a boundary. Changing the cocycle
representative gives the same conclusion, or follows
directly from CFF32. Thus CFF34 is a proved map,
not a choice of a torsion-free substitute for CFF16.

Its exact image must also be retained. Since \(M\) is free
on \(\epsilon,T,z\), cohomology with that coefficient is
the direct sum of those three copies of \(H^{j+1}(P_S)\).
The long exact sequence for
\(0\to P_S\xrightarrow{9}P_S\to P_S/9P_S\to0\) gives
\[
 \im\beta_9=H^{j+1}(P_S)[9],\qquad
 \im\partial=\{(0,0,3x):x\in H^{j+1}(P_S)[9]\}.
 \tag{CFF34a}
\]
Here the three coordinates retain the original basis
\(\epsilon,T,\epsilon T\). Thus \(3\im\partial=0\), and
a class has nonzero image exactly when its Bockstein is
not killed by 3. A non-split coefficient extension does
not assert that this image is nonzero for every \(L\).

For the two-element lattice \(L=\{0_L,1_L\}\), its support
spectrum has one point and the actual relative complex is
\[
             P_S=[O\xrightarrow{-1}O]
             \quad\text{in degrees }0,1 .
 \tag{CFF34b}
\]
The homotopy \(-1:P_S^1\to P_S^0\) contracts it.
Every relative group and connecting map in CFF32 then
vanishes, while CFF16 remains non-split. The sheaf
extension also remains non-split even after forgetting \(O\):
an additive splitting at a stalk would lift \(1\in O/9\)
to an element of order 9, whereas the torsion of
\(\Omega\) is killed by 3. Ordinary cohomology still gives CFF16 itself
through \(R\Gamma(Y,\mathcal U(E))=E[0]\). These are the
exact comparison maps between the coefficient, ordinary,
and relative statements.

Frobenius on each nilpotent coefficient complex still
has square zero, and the residue maps still satisfy
the preceding formulas. On the whole coefficient
algebra \(C=O\oplus M\), the supported complex splits
as \(P_S\oplus(P_S\otimes M)\), and its squared
Frobenius is the identity on the first summand and
zero on the second. This is an exact result for every
finite support lattice simultaneously; it is not an
estimate on a growing finite range.

For the support carrier
\(G_L(E)=\{(v,1_L):v\in E\}\cup
\{(0,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}\), addition is coefficient
addition and lattice join. No multiplication on a
general coefficient module is imposed. An \(O\)-linear map
\(a:E\to E'\) also has the distinct lift
\[
 G_L(a)(v,1_L)=(a(v),1_L),\qquad
 G_L(a)(0,\lambda)=(0,\lambda).
 \tag{CFF35}
\]
It preserves addition by direct use of join, and
composition by evaluating the two displayed types
of element. In particular \(G_L(F_M)^2=G_L(0_M)\)
sends every top-supported vector to \(e\) and fixes
every lower zero label. It does not send them to
\(\tau\). CFF29 is the sheaf receiver, while CFF35 is
the coefficient-carrier map; these two constructions
are both specified and are not being identified.

The computed obstruction is therefore itself an object with
maps: the lowering defect CFF12 (nonzero when \(c\ne0\)),
the middle torsion in CFF22, and the supported Bockstein
CFF34 with its exact image CFF34a. The coefficient and
sheaf extension are non-split, whereas the relative
connecting map can vanish, including in CFF34b.
These specified distinctions remain part of any further
weight construction. The local Frobenius is nilpotent on
the collision pieces even after rationalization and in
every tensor degree. Its exact Hom vanishing in CFF6
means that those same pieces cannot be inserted by a
nonzero Frobenius-equivariant map into an object with
injective Frobenius while keeping this operator unchanged.
The sheaf comparison CFF29--34b nevertheless retains
the coefficient extension on every stalk and in ordinary
cohomology, with the stated relative image. A geometric or prismatic cohomology
comparison with a different Frobenius requires its actual
construction; the present calculation does not supply or
assume it. No sign of the full Weil form is inferred here.
