\section{Parameter holonomy, the reflected trace, and arithmetic scaling}
\label{sec:hh}
\newcounter{hh}\renewcommand{\thehh}{HH\arabic{hh}}
\newcommand{\HH}{\refstepcounter{hh}\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{\thehh.}\ }

\HH\textbf{Original arithmetic family and scope.}
We use precisely the convention of Brad Rodgers and Terence Tao,
\emph{The de Bruijn--Newman constant is non-negative},
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.05914v5}{arXiv:1801.05914v5},
author source equations \texttt{hoz}, \texttt{sas}, \texttt{phidef},
\texttt{htdef} and the sentence following \texttt{htdef}:
\begin{align}
 H_t(Z)&=\int_0^\infty e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)\cos(Zu)\,du,&
 H_0(Z)&=\tfrac18\xi_{\mathrm R}(\tfrac12+iZ/2),\notag\\
 \Phi(u)&=\sum_{n\ge1}
 (2\pi^2n^4e^{9u}-3\pi n^2e^{5u})e^{-\pi n^2e^{4u}},&
 \partial_tH_t&=-\partial_Z^2H_t .
 \tag{HH1}\label{hh:original}
\end{align}
Here $\xi_{\mathrm R}(s)=s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)/2$.
The subscript prevents confusion with the local coordinate $\xi$ below.
Fix an actual real zero $x_0$ of $H_0$ and retain its actual order
$m\ge1$ and every coefficient
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 H_0(x_0+\xi)=\sum_{n\ge m}a_n\xi^n,\qquad
 a_n=\partial_Z^nH_0(x_0)/n!,\quad a_m\ne0,\\
 c=a_{m+1}/a_m,\quad d=a_{m+2}/a_m,\quad b=2d-c^2 .
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{HH2}\label{hh:coeff}
\]
This describes each actual real zero without asserting that an actual
multiple zero exists. At $t=0$ the constructions below are original
completed-zeta jets and their reflected Weil trace contribution.
At $t\ne0$ they are the explicitly defined trace and multiplier on
the actual deformed $H_t$ cluster. An explicit formula for the original
zeta function is not asserted for these deformed fibres.

For completeness the analyticity used here follows directly from HH1.
On compact subsets of complex $(t,Z)$ every differentiated integrand
is bounded by a constant times
$e^{C u^2+Cu}(1+u)^N e^{-c_1e^{4u}}$ for $u\ge1$, with $c_1>0$.
To obtain this estimate, absorb polynomial factors of $n$ into
$e^{-\pi n^2e^{4u}/2}$, and sum the remaining Gaussian tail uniformly.
The series and its derivatives are uniformly convergent on bounded
$u$ intervals. The displayed bound is integrable and remains so
after any finite number of $t$ and $Z$ derivatives. Differentiation
under the integral thus proves joint holomorphy and the heat equation.
For real $t$ all local coefficients are real. These facts also prove
the uniform convergence on a small fixed root-isolating circle that
is used below.

\HH\textbf{All analytic branches, with their original coefficients.}
Define
\[
 P_n(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
       \frac{(-1)^jn!}{j!(n-2j)!}z^{n-2j}.
 \qquad
 H_{u^2}(x_0+uz)=\sum_{n\ge m}a_nu^nP_n(z).
 \tag{HH3}\label{hh:branches-source}
\]
The second equality is convergent for bounded $z$ and small $u$:
the heat equation gives
$[t^j\xi^\ell]H_t=(-1)^j(\ell+2j)!a_{\ell+2j}/(j!\ell!)$,
and the locally absolutely convergent double Taylor series can be
regrouped by $n=2j+\ell$.
This $P_n$ is the classical Hermite polynomial $H_n^{\rm Hermite}(z/2)$;
the explicit finite formula is also
\href{https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.5.E13}{DLMF 18.5.13},
Chapter 18 by T.H. Koornwinder, R. Wong, R. Koekoek and
R.F. Swarttouw. All zero properties used here have the following proof.
The generating identity $\sum P_n(z)w^n/n!=e^{zw-w^2}$ gives
\[
 P_n'=nP_{n-1},\quad P_{n+1}=zP_n-2nP_{n-1},\quad
 2P_n''-zP_n'+nP_n=0,\quad
 P_ne^{-z^2/4}=(-2)^n\partial_z^n e^{-z^2/4}.
 \tag{HH4}
\]
Integration by parts makes $P_n$ orthogonal to each polynomial of
degree less than $n$ for the positive weight $e^{-z^2/4}$.
If it had fewer than $n$ distinct real sign-changing roots, multiply
it by the product of those linear factors. The product has constant
nonzero sign away from finitely many points, contradicting this
orthogonality. Hence $P_m$ has exactly $m$ real simple roots
$r_1<\cdots<r_m$. Its parity gives $r_{\sigma j}=-r_j$, where
$\sigma j=m+1-j$.

Apply the analytic implicit-function theorem to
$H_{u^2}(x_0+uz)/(a_mu^m)$ at $(0,r_j)$.
It gives convergent germs $\theta_j(u)=uz_j(u)$, with
\begin{gather}
 H_{u^2}(x_0+\theta_j(u))=0,\qquad
 \theta_j(u)=r_ju+2cu^2+r_jbu^3+O(u^4),\notag\\
 \theta_j(-u)=\theta_{\sigma j}(u),\qquad
 \theta_j(\bar u)=\overline{\theta_j(u)} .
 \tag{HH5}\label{hh:theta}
\end{gather}
Here the coefficients beyond the displayed terms remain the unique
convergent coefficients specified by HH3; no polynomial replacement
of $H$ is made. To compute the displayed terms, at a root of $P_m$,
HH4 yields $P_{m+1}=-2P_m'$, $P_{m+1}'=0$,
$P_m''=r_jP_m'/2$, $P_{m+2}=-2r_jP_m'$.
Substitution of $z_j=r_j+A_ju+B_ju^2+O(u^3)$ gives
$A_j=2c$ and $B_j=r_jb$.
Complex conjugation and the change $u\mapsto-u$ give the last two
identities by uniqueness of the corresponding implicit germ.
Every difference $\theta_j-\theta_k$ equals
$u(r_j-r_k+O(u))$, and is nonzero for small $u\ne0$.
An isolating circle about $x_0$, followed by the argument principle
and uniform convergence, retains exactly $m$ zeros for small $t$.
These $m$ branches therefore exhaust the original local cluster.

\HH\textbf{The original algebra, its full unit, and its evaluation map.}
Put $\mathcal O=\mathbb C\{t\}$ and let $\mathcal K$ be its fraction field.
Define
\[
 W(t,\xi)=\prod_{j=1}^m(\xi-\theta_j(u)),\quad t=u^2,\qquad
 H_t(x_0+\xi)=U(t,\xi)W(t,\xi),\qquad
 \mathcal A=\mathcal O[\xi]/(W).
 \tag{HH6}\label{hh:original-algebra}
\]
The product is even in $u$ by HH5, so its coefficients are
convergent germs in $t$. Its coefficients are real, $W(0,\xi)=\xi^m$,
and $U$ is a holomorphic unit with
$U(0,\xi)=\sum_{j\ge0}a_{m+j}\xi^j$.
One can obtain the latter assertion without discarding any unit:
divide the jointly holomorphic function $H_{u^2}(x_0+\xi)$ successively
by $\xi-\theta_j(u)$. Holomorphic division by one such factor follows
from the integral of its $\xi$ derivative along a line segment.
At each remaining root the quotient vanishes for $u\ne0$, hence
also at $u=0$ by analyticity. All $m$ divisions are therefore valid.
The resulting quotient is even in $u$, since this is so away from
the zero set, and hence everywhere by analytic continuation.
It descends to $U(t,\xi)$, with $U(0,0)=a_m\ne0$.

In the original basis $1,\xi,\ldots,\xi^{m-1}$ set
\[
 V(u)_{jk}=\theta_j(u)^k\quad(1\le j\le m,\ 0\le k<m).
 \qquad V(-u)=P V(u),\quad
 (Py)_j=y_{\sigma j}.
 \tag{HH7}\label{hh:vandermonde}
\]
Its determinant is $\prod_{j<k}(\theta_k-\theta_j)$, nonzero off $u=0$.
For a coefficient column $f$, $V(u)f$ is its exact vector of values.
This retains every coefficient of every actual branch and the entire
original nonreduced central algebra
$\mathcal A/(t)=\mathbb C[\xi]/(\xi^m)$.

\HH\textbf{The exact Gauss--Manin connection and the unit contribution.}
The degree-zero Gauss--Manin connection here means differentiation
of functions along the finite etale root cover, on the punctured
disc. Its exact formula in $\mathcal A_{\mathcal K}$ is
\begin{align}
 \nabla f&=\partial_tf+q\,\partial_\xi f,&
 q&=-\frac{W_t}{W_\xi}
   =\frac{H_{\xi\xi}}{H_\xi}
   =\frac{W_{\xi\xi}}{W_\xi}+2\frac{U_\xi}{U}\pmod W, \notag\\
 \nabla f&=f'+\Gamma_{\mathcal A}f,&
 \Gamma_{\mathcal A}(t)&=
     V(u)^{-1}\frac1{2u}\partial_u V(u).
 \tag{HH8}\label{hh:connection}
\end{align}
Every inverse in this equation is taken in the etale algebra over
$\mathcal K$, where $W_\xi$ is invertible; reduction modulo $W$ is
performed after the indicated operations.
Indeed differentiating $W(t,\theta_j)=0$ gives
$\theta_j'=-W_t/W_\xi$. Differentiating the actual equation $H=0$
and using HH1 gives $\theta_j'=H_{\xi\xi}/H_\xi$.
Differentiating $H=UW$ twice in $\xi$ proves the last expression
at every simple root, and hence in the etale algebra.
The expression $\partial_t+q\partial_\xi$ annihilates $W$ modulo $W$,
so it is independent of polynomial representative.
In the original power basis the $k$th column is the coefficient
column of $k\xi^{k-1}q$ modulo $W$, including a zero column for $k=0$.
The chain rule applied to $Vf$ proves the matrix formula.
Its invariance under $u\mapsto-u$ follows from HH7, so it is
meromorphic in $t$. In particular the term $2U_\xi/U$ is part of
the actual connection and cannot be suppressed.

\HH\textbf{Complete loop holonomy.}
For a path in the punctured $t$ disc, choose its continuous square-root
lift with endpoints $u_0,u_1$. Horizontality is precisely
$(V(u)f)'=0$. Thus the exact coefficient transport and positive
once-around holonomy at a base point $t_0=u_0^2$ are
\[
 C_{1\gets0}=V(u_1)^{-1}V(u_0),\qquad
 M_{\mathcal A}(t_0)=V(u_0)^{-1}P V(u_0),\qquad
 M_{\mathcal A}^2=I.
 \tag{HH9}\label{hh:monodromy}
\]
These formulas are independent of the initial choice of square root.
A loop of winding number $n$ has holonomy $M_{\mathcal A}^n$,
because the square-root endpoint is $(-1)^nu_0$.
There are $r=\lfloor m/2\rfloor$ transpositions and
$\epsilon=m-2r\in\{0,1\}$ fixed branches. The multiplicities of
the holonomy eigenvalues $+1,-1$ are therefore $r+\epsilon,r$.
In particular a multiple real zero gives nonidentity parameter
holonomy, although its central reflected trace is nonnegative,
as will be calculated below. This holonomy is a meromorphic
automorphism of $\mathcal A_{\mathcal K}$; regularity on the original
integral lattice $\mathcal A$ has not been assumed.

\HH\textbf{A logarithmic lattice and the exact integral-lattice defect.}
Over the $u$ cover let
\[
 e_j=\prod_{k\ne j}\frac{\xi-\theta_k(u)}
                               {\theta_j(u)-\theta_k(u)}.
 \tag{HH10}
\]
Their evaluations are the coordinate unit vectors, so they are
horizontal idempotents. For each positive leading root $r_a>0$
choose its pair $(a,+),(a,-)$ with leading roots $r_a,-r_a$ and set
\[
 \mathbf e_a=e_{a,+}+e_{a,-},\qquad
 \mathbf f_a=u(e_{a,+}-e_{a,-}),\qquad
 \mathcal L=\bigoplus_{a=1}^r
  (\mathcal O\mathbf e_a\oplus\mathcal O\mathbf f_a)
       \ \oplus\ \mathcal O^\epsilon\mathbf e_0 .
 \tag{HH11}\label{hh:loglattice}
\]
Here $\mathbf e_0$ is the central idempotent if $m$ is odd; the last
summand is absent otherwise. All these elements are invariant under
$u\mapsto-u$ and so belong to $\mathcal A_{\mathcal K}$.
Products from different summands vanish,
$\mathbf e_a^2=\mathbf e_a$,
$\mathbf e_a\mathbf f_a=\mathbf f_a$,
$\mathbf f_a^2=t\mathbf e_a$ and
$1=\sum_a\mathbf e_a+\epsilon\mathbf e_0$.
They are a basis over $\mathcal K$ and define an $\mathcal O$ algebra.
They also give the explicit matrices
\[
 \nabla|_a=\partial_t+
       \begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1/(2t)\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 S_{\mathcal L}|_a=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix};
 \quad \nabla\mathbf e_0=0,\quad S_{\mathcal L}\mathbf e_0=\mathbf e_0 .
 \tag{HH12}\label{hh:logconnection}
\]
Indeed $\nabla e_j=0$ and $\partial_tu=u/(2t)$.
This proves regular singularity by exhibiting a logarithmic lattice,
with no claim that the original power basis has a simple-pole matrix.

For clarity the exact original-coordinate inclusion
$J:\mathcal A\hookrightarrow\mathcal L$ is
\begin{align}
 (Jf)_{a,\mathrm{even}}&=
   \frac{f(\theta_{a,+}(u))+f(\theta_{a,-}(u))}{2},&
 (Jf)_{a,\mathrm{odd}}&=
   \frac{f(\theta_{a,+}(u))-f(\theta_{a,-}(u))}{2u},\notag\\
 (Jf)_0&=f(\theta_0(u)).
 \tag{HH13}\label{hh:inclusion}
\end{align}
The two displayed numerators have the required parity, so these
are holomorphic germs in $t$. For $f=\xi^k$ they give the exact
matrix columns. The map is injective because it is injective off
zero and $\mathcal A$ is free over $\mathcal O$. Its determinant is
nonzero, so its cokernel has finite length. In particular
\[
 M_{\mathcal A}=J^{-1}S_{\mathcal L}J,\qquad
 \mathcal D_M=
   \frac{\mathcal A+M_{\mathcal A}\mathcal A}{\mathcal A}
   =\frac{J\mathcal O^m+S_{\mathcal L}J\mathcal O^m}
          {J\mathcal O^m}
   \ \subset\ \mathcal L/\mathcal A .
 \tag{HH14}\label{hh:lattice-defect}
\]
This is an exact finite-length object, with the exact sequence
$0\to\mathcal A\to\mathcal A+M_{\mathcal A}\mathcal A
\to\mathcal D_M\to0$.
The middle module is the smallest $M_{\mathcal A}$-stable lattice
containing $\mathcal A$: applying the involution interchanges its two
summands. Its rank is $m$, and it is free since a finite torsion-free
module over the discrete valuation ring $\mathcal O$ is free.
No assertion that this sum is an algebra is needed.
$\mathcal D_M=0$ is exactly the assertion that the loop preserves
the original lattice. Equations HH13--14 compute this defect from
the full actual coefficients even when it is not zero.

The scalar even and odd branch germs
\[
 b_a(t)=\frac{\theta_{a,+}(u)+\theta_{a,-}(u)}2,\qquad
 R_a(t)=\frac{\theta_{a,+}(u)-\theta_{a,-}(u)}{2u}
 \tag{HH15}
\]
satisfy $b_a(t)=2ct+O(t^2)$,
$R_a(t)=r_a(1+bt)+O(t^2)$ and $R_a(0)=r_a\ne0$.
They retain all higher coefficients, and the original coordinate is
exactly
\[
 \xi=\sum_a\bigl(b_a(t)\mathbf e_a+R_a(t)\mathbf f_a\bigr)
                     +\epsilon\theta_0(t)\mathbf e_0 .
 \tag{HH16}\label{hh:coordinate}
\]
The scalar $\theta_0$ is even in $u$ and is real for real $t$.
This provides the concrete relation between the auxiliary lattice
and the full original algebra without identifying their special fibres.

\HH\textbf{The reflected trace and its three exact inertias.}
For real $t$ define the conjugate-linear involution $\#$ by conjugating
coefficients and fixing $\xi$. Put
\[
 T_t(f,g)=\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal A_t}
                    (M_{f^\#g}),\qquad
 K_{\mathcal A}(t)_{k\ell}
       =\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal A_t}(M_{\xi^{k+\ell}})
       =\sum_{j=1}^m\theta_j(u)^{k+\ell}.
 \tag{HH17}\label{hh:trace}
\]
The last expression extends analytically to $t=0$. For nonzero $t$
it follows by diagonalizing the multiplication operators via $V$;
at zero direct multiplication on $\mathbb C[\xi]/\xi^m$ gives
$\operatorname{Tr}(M_f)=mf(0)$.
Thus $T_t(f,g)=f^*K_{\mathcal A}(t)g$ for real $t$, and the matrix
is real symmetric. For $t>0$ choose $u=\sqrt t$; every root is real.
For $t<0$ choose $u=i\sqrt{-t}$. HH5 then gives
$\overline{\theta_j(u)}=\theta_{\sigma j}(u)$; each noncentral
pair is nonreal for sufficiently small negative $t$. Consequently
\[
 K_{\mathcal A}(t)=V(u)^TV(u)
   =\begin{cases}
     V(u)^*V(u),&t>0,\\
     V(u)^*P V(u),&t<0,
    \end{cases}
 \qquad K_{\mathcal A}(0)=\operatorname{diag}(m,0,\ldots,0).
 \tag{HH18}\label{hh:gram}
\]
The first equality is a holomorphic bilinear identity for complex $t$;
the other two are the Hermitian identities for the indicated real
fibres. Congruence by the invertible $V$ proves the full inertia,
written as (positive, negative, zero):
\[
 \operatorname{inertia}T_t=
 \begin{cases}
  (m,0,0),&t>0,\\
  (1,0,m-1),&t=0,\\
  (r+\epsilon,r,0),&t<0 .
 \end{cases}
 \qquad \operatorname{rad}T_0=(\xi).
 \tag{HH19}\label{hh:inertia}
\]
On $\mathcal L$ the trace Gram matrix is exactly
\[
 K_{\mathcal L}(t)=
       \bigoplus_{a=1}^r\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&2t\end{pmatrix}
            \ \oplus\ (1)^\epsilon,\qquad
 K_{\mathcal A}(t)=J(t)^T K_{\mathcal L}(t)J(t).
 \tag{HH20}\label{hh:auxgram}
\]
For proof, $\#$ fixes $\mathbf e_a,\mathbf f_a$ for both real signs
of $t$; at negative $t$, conjugation of $u$ and exchange of the
idempotents cancel. The algebra relations give the traces $2,0,2t$.
The congruence follows first off zero by HH13 and then at zero
analytically. At $t=0$ the map on fibres induced by $J$ need not be
injective. Accordingly HH20 does not replace the retained algebra
$\mathbb C[\xi]/\xi^m$ by $r$ dual-number factors.

\HH\textbf{Flatness, loop isometry, and the half-plane metric defect.}
Differentiating the holomorphic identity $K=V^TV$ proves
\[
 K'=\Gamma_{\mathcal A}^T K+K\Gamma_{\mathcal A},\qquad
 C_{1\gets0}^T K(t_1)C_{1\gets0}=K(t_0).
 \tag{HH21}\label{hh:bilinear-flat}
\]
For real $t$ the connection matrix is real, so the first equality
also states compatibility with the Hermitian trace on each real
interval not containing zero.
At a fixed nonzero real $t$, HH9 and HH18 give
$M_{\mathcal A}^*K M_{\mathcal A}=K$, because $P$ is an isometry
both for $I$ and for $P$. Thus the loop is unitary for the positive
trace at $t>0$, and preserves the indefinite trace at $t<0$.

There is also an exact defect when moving between the two real
structures. Let $t_+>0,t_-<0$, choose the upper-half-plane path
whose square-root lift goes from $u_+=\sqrt{t_+}$ to
$u_-=i\sqrt{-t_-}$, and let $C=V(u_-)^{-1}V(u_+)$.
Then
\[
 K(t_+)-C^*K(t_-)C
     =V(u_+)^*(I-P)V(u_+)
     =2V(u_+)^*\Pi_-V(u_+),\qquad
 \Pi_-=(I-P)/2 .
 \tag{HH22}\label{hh:halfplane}
\]
This is a positive semidefinite matrix of rank $r$.
Indeed the first real fibre has value Gram matrix $I$, while the
second has value Gram matrix $P$; substitution gives the equation,
and $\Pi_-$ is the orthogonal projection on the antisymmetric
pair directions. Its image and kernel are therefore exact:
the defect vanishes precisely when the transported value vector
has equal values within each pair. HH21 still holds with transpose.
There is no inconsistency: HH22 measures the different endpoint
conjugations in the Hermitian forms.

\HH\textbf{The full-unit residue and the original Jacobian receiver.}
Introduce the entire deformed function and its exact arithmetic
coordinate
\[
 g_t(s)=16H_t(-2i(s-\tfrac12)),\quad
 s=\rho+i\xi/2,\quad \rho=\tfrac12+ix_0/2,\quad
 g_0(s)=2\xi_{\mathrm R}(s).
 \tag{HH23}\label{hh:g}
\]
For real $t$, reflection is $s\mapsto1-\bar s$, or
$\xi\mapsto\bar\xi$ on points. On coordinate algebras $\xi^\#=\xi$,
whereas $v^\#=-v$ for $v=s-\rho=i\xi/2$.
The raw residue pairing and Jacobian multiplier are
\begin{align}
 R_t(f,g)&=\frac{i}{32}\sum_{\text{cluster}}
       \operatorname{Res}_\xi
       \frac{f^\#(\xi)g(\xi)}
            {U(t,\xi)W(t,\xi)}\,d\xi,&
 \mathcal J_t&=M_{g_t'(s)}=-32iM_{H_\xi(t,x_0+\xi)},\notag\\
 R_t(f,\mathcal J_tg)&=T_t(f,g).
 \tag{HH24}\label{hh:residue}
\end{align}
The factor $i/32$ is exactly $ds/16=(i/32)d\xi$.
The pairing is well-defined modulo $W$, since changing a numerator
by a multiple of $W$ adds a holomorphic differential.
It is perfect on every fibre. At a simple root its weight
$i/(32H_\xi)$ is nonzero. At $t=0$ the matrix has entries
$(i/32)[\xi^{m-1-k-\ell}]U(0,\xi)^{-1}$, zero when
$k+\ell>m-1$ and equal to $i/(32a_m)$ on the antidiagonal.
Its determinant is therefore nonzero. This is a perfect
sesquilinear residue pairing; with these real-coordinate conventions
it is skew-Hermitian, not the Hermitian trace itself.

The last equality in HH24 follows by cancellation of the constants
and the identity
$H_\xi/H=W_\xi/W+U_\xi/U$. The unit term has no residue.
At each root of multiplicity $n$, the residue of
$f^\#g\,W_\xi/W$ is $n(f^\#g)$ evaluated at that root, exactly the
regular multiplication trace. This proves the receiver at every
fibre, including the original nonreduced central fibre.
In matrix form, for $t\ne0$ and $C_t=I$ or $P$ as in HH18,
\[
 R_t=\frac{i}{32}V^*C_t
       \operatorname{diag}\bigl(H_\xi(t,x_0+\theta_j)^{-1}\bigr)V,
 \qquad
 \mathcal J_t=V^{-1}
       \operatorname{diag}\bigl(-32iH_\xi(t,x_0+\theta_j)\bigr)V .
 \tag{HH25}\label{hh:residue-matrix}
\]
Their product is precisely $K_{\mathcal A}$, with all units retained.

The compatibility with the connection has its own exact unit-sensitive
formula. In the punctured algebra set
\[
 \kappa=-\frac{H_{\xi\xi\xi}}{H_\xi}
                +\left(\frac{H_{\xi\xi}}{H_\xi}\right)^2\pmod W.
 \tag{HH26}
\]
For horizontal $f,g$ on a real interval,
$\frac{d}{dt}R_t(f,g)=-R_t(f,M_\kappa g)$.
Indeed the values of $f,g$ are constant along each branch, while
$(\partial_t+q\partial_\xi)\log H_\xi
=-H_{\xi\xi\xi}/H_\xi+H_{\xi\xi}^2/H_\xi^2$ by HH1 and HH8.
Differentiating the weights in HH25 proves the identity. The
Jacobian satisfies $[\nabla,\mathcal J_t]=M_\kappa\mathcal J_t$ as
a multiplier; its covariant derivative cancels this residue variation and
recovers the trace compatibility HH21.

\HH\textbf{Exact specialization to the arithmetic jet and its Weil value.}
At $t=0$ the coordinate map
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \mathbb C[v]/(v^m)\longrightarrow\mathcal A_0,\qquad
 v\longmapsto i\xi/2,\qquad g_0(\rho+v)=v^m u_\rho(v),\\
 u_\rho(v)=16(-2i)^m\sum_{j\ge0}a_{m+j}(-2i)^jv^j
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{HH27}\label{hh:arithmetic-unit}
\]
is an isomorphism of the reflected algebras, including the full
analytic unit in the displayed local equation.
Consequently
\[
 T_0(f,g)=m\overline{f(0)}g(0),\qquad
 (\log u_\rho)''(0)=4(c^2-2d).
 \tag{HH28}
\]
The equality with the original local Weil contribution means exactly
the multiplicity-$m$ summand at the fixed reflected zero
$\rho=1-\bar\rho$ in the spectral side
$\sum_\rho m_\rho\overline{F(1-\bar\rho)}G(\rho)$.
The complete explicit formula retains its other zeros, its prime
terms, its Archimedean term and its endpoint terms; none is replaced
by the finite cluster. The original convention and criterion are
Alain Connes and Caterina Consani,
\emph{Weil positivity and trace formula, the archimedean place},
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.13771v1}{arXiv:2006.13771v1},
author source introduction and Appendix \texttt{mainprop};
the full original-map derivation is also given in HW19--31 of this
collection. The local equality here is proved independently by HH24
and HH27. It asserts no positivity of the remaining spectral packet.

\HH\textbf{Arithmetic scaling and its full deformed multiplier.}
For real $L$ define, without any change of original coordinate,
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 S_L(t)=M_{\exp(iL(x_0+\xi)/2)},\qquad
 D_L(u)=\operatorname{diag}(d_j),\\
 d_j=\exp(iL(x_0+\theta_j(u))/2),\qquad
 S_L=V^{-1}D_LV .
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{HH29}\label{hh:scaling}
\]
The multiplier defines a holomorphic algebra section: it can be
reduced modulo the monic $W$, or its entire convergent power series
can be applied to the holomorphic multiplication matrix of $\xi$.
At the arithmetic endpoint this is exactly
\[
 S_L(0)=e^{L(\rho-1/2)}\exp(LM_v)
  =e^{iLx_0/2}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{L^kM_v^k}{k!}.
 \tag{HH30}\label{hh:arithmetic-scaling}
\]
It includes the original nilpotent factor on every jet layer.
This is the shifted multiplicative arithmetic character convention
used in Connes--Consani's spectral realizations. Their paper
\emph{Hochschild homology, trace map and zeta cycles},
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.10419}{arXiv:2207.10419},
author source Remark \texttt{remtauto}, explicitly retains the
Jordan blocks at multiple critical zeros. HH30 derives the entire
finite matrix here, so no simplicity assumption is imported.

 S_L(t)^*K(t)S_L(t)=K(t).
 \tag{HH31}\label{hh:scaling-isometry}

 [\nabla,S_L]=\frac{iL}{2}M_qS_L,\qquad

 \tag{HH33}\label{hh:action-defect}

The first formula is the Leibniz rule and $\nabla\xi=q$;

\HH\textbf{A quantitative action defect containing the finite collision energy.}
For $t>0$ the actual trace $T_t$ is positive definite, so its
Hilbert--Schmidt operator norm is defined. The map $V$ is an isometry
onto $\mathbb C^m$ with its usual inner product. Therefore HH33 gives
the exact formula
\[
 \bigl\|[M_{\mathcal A},S_L]\bigr\|_{\mathrm{HS},T_t}^2
  =\sum_j|d_j-d_{\sigma j}|^2
  =8\sum_{a=1}^r
       \sin^2\!\left(\frac{L(\theta_{a,+}(u)
                                    -\theta_{a,-}(u))}{4}\right).
 \tag{HH34}\label{hh:hs-exact}
\]
There is one nonzero entry per row of $P D-DP$; a pair contributes
twice the same absolute square. The identity
$|e^{i\alpha}-e^{i\beta}|^2=4\sin^2((\alpha-\beta)/2)$ gives
exactly the factor $8$ and the denominator $4$ above.

For each fixed real $L$ its full second-order expansion is
\begin{align}
 \bigl\|[M_{\mathcal A},S_L]\bigr\|_{\mathrm{HS},T_t}^2
  ={}&2L^2m(m-1)t \notag\\
   &+m(m-1)\left(4L^2(2d-c^2)
                    -\frac{L^4}{3}(2m-3)\right)t^2+O(t^3).
 \tag{HH35}\label{hh:hs-expansion}
\end{align}
Here the remainder is a convergent-germ remainder for the fixed
actual root and fixed $L$. To prove every constant, HH3 has
$[z^{m-2}]P_m=-m(m-1)$ and
$[z^{m-4}]P_m=m(m-1)(m-2)(m-3)/2$, with missing coefficients
taken as zero. Newton's identities, or direct expansion of the
product, give
\[
 \sum_{a=1}^r r_a^2=m(m-1),\qquad
 \sum_{a=1}^r r_a^4=2m(m-1)(2m-3).
 \tag{HH36}
\]
For example the full fourth power sum is
$2m^2(m-1)^2-2m(m-1)(m-2)(m-3)
=4m(m-1)(2m-3)$; parity halves it.
The identities also hold for $m=1,2,3$ by the same product expansion.
Exact parity in HH5 improves the gap expansion to
$\theta_{a,+}-\theta_{a,-}=2r_au+2r_ab u^3+O(u^5)$.
Insert this in $\sin^2z=z^2-z^4/3+O(z^6)$ and multiply by $8$.
The contribution of pair $a$ is
$2L^2r_a^2t+(4L^2r_a^2b-L^4r_a^4/6)t^2+O(t^3)$.
Summing by HH36 proves HH35.
For $m\ge2$ and fixed $L\ne0$ this norm is strictly positive for all
sufficiently small $t>0$, despite both actions being isometries.
For $m=1$ or $L=0$ it is identically zero.

The finite energy term can be calculated from the same retained

\[
 E_{x_0}(t)=\sum_{j\ne k}
        \frac1{(\theta_j(u)-\theta_k(u))^2}
     =\frac{m(m-1)}{8t}-\frac{m(m-1)b}{4}+O(t).
 \tag{HH37}\label{hh:energy}
\]
For a complete verification, put
$A_j=\sum_{k\ne j}(r_j-r_k)^{-1}$ and
$B_j=\sum_{k\ne j}(r_j-r_k)^{-2}$.
Logarithmic differentiation of the product for $P_m$, followed by

$3(A_j^2-B_j)=P_m'''(r_j)/P_m'(r_j)

Thus $B_j=(m-1)/6-r_j^2/48$.
HH36 gives $\sum_jB_j=m(m-1)/8$.
Each gap equals $u(r_j-r_k)(1+bu^2)+O(u^4)$, so substitution gives

\HH\textbf{The full supported fixed locus of the actual endpoint action.}
Let $\Lambda$ be the original bounded distributive support lattice,
with every one of its labels retained; use $\Lambda$ here to

For the original arithmetic jet $A_0=\mathbb C[\xi]/(\xi^m)$ define
its supported module exactly as in the original lattice-split
construction:
\[
 \widehat A_0=
   \{(0,\lambda):\lambda\in\Lambda\}
       \ \cup\ \{(f,1_\Lambda):f\in A_0\},\qquad
 (f,\lambda)+(g,\mu)=(f+g,\lambda\vee\mu).
 \tag{HH39}\label{hh:supported-module}
\]
The scalars $(a,\lambda)\in G_\Lambda(\mathbb C)$ act by
$(a,\lambda)(f,\mu)=(af,\lambda\wedge\mu)$.
Nonzero amplitudes occur only at top support, so the operations
are closed and satisfy the semimodule axioms by distributivity
of the lattice and the original vector-space operations.
In particular actual absence $(0,0_\Lambda)$ and supported zero
$(0,1_\Lambda)$ remain distinct when the lattice is nontrivial.
Lift HH30 by
\[
 \widehat S_L(f,\lambda)=(S_L(0)f,\lambda).
 \tag{HH40}
\]
This is a semimodule automorphism: the matrix is complex linear and
invertible and the support coordinate is unchanged.
It is not asserted to be a unital algebra automorphism.

Set $\alpha=iL/2$, $N=M_\xi$, and
$\lambda_0=e^{iLx_0/2}$. If $\lambda_0\ne1$, the original
full-jet inverse is the finite expression
\[
 (S_L(0)-I)^{-1}
  =\frac1{\lambda_0-1}
      \sum_{j=0}^{m-1}
       \left[-\frac{\lambda_0}{\lambda_0-1}
                         (e^{\alpha N}-I)\right]^j .
 \tag{HH41}\label{hh:fixed-inverse}
\]
Indeed $N^m=0$, so $(e^{\alpha N}-I)^m=0$, and multiplication
by $S_L-I=(\lambda_0-1)I+\lambda_0(e^{\alpha N}-I)$

this terminating inverse. If $\lambda_0=1$ and $L\ne0$, instead
\[
 S_L(0)-I=\alpha N h(\alpha N),\qquad
 h(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{z^k}{(k+1)!},\qquad
 h(\alpha N)^{-1}
      =\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(I-h(\alpha N))^j .
 \tag{HH42}\label{hh:fixed-resonance}
\]
The product $\alpha Nh(\alpha N)$ is exactly $e^{\alpha N}-I$,
because its last potentially extra term contains $N^m=0$.
The constant term of $h$ is one; the same terminating geometric
identity proves its displayed inverse. Thus the full fixed
amplitude space and the full lifted fixed set are
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 F_L=\ker(S_L(0)-I)=
 \begin{cases}
   \{0\},&e^{iLx_0/2}\ne1,\\
   \mathbb C\xi^{m-1},&e^{iLx_0/2}=1,\ L\ne0,\\
   A_0,&L=0,
  \end{cases}\\[5pt]
 \operatorname{Fix}(\widehat S_L)=
 \{(0,\lambda):\lambda\in\Lambda\}
       \cup\{(f,1_\Lambda):f\in F_L\}.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{HH43}\label{hh:fixed-locus}
\]
For the middle case, multiplication by the unit in HH42 does
not change $\ker N$; writing the full polynomial of degree below

when the fixed amplitude is the entire one-dimensional algebra.
