\section{The full endpoint plane and the supported-zero action}

The original cosine endpoint test has a sine companion. This note
evaluates their complete reflected Weil matrix and carries that matrix
through the actual even-source prime receiver. Supported-zero
multiplication then has a fully determined metric defect: its difference
from the orthogonal projection onto its image is square-zero. The
defect vanishes at one explicitly derived positive time. Its analytic
family has a cohomology class supported at that time, lying over the
supported-zero prime of the original arithmetic base.

There are two time parameters, which are never identified in the proof:
the theta time is fixed at \(t=1/32\); \(T>0\) is the independent
integer-coefficient heat time of the supported-zero receiver. The
calculation at \(T=1/32\) is a stated specialization of the latter.

\subsection{The original reflected form on both source directions}
Keep the entire original density \(\Phi\), the original coordinates,
and the function \(H=H_{1/32}\) of AP2 in the complete appendix.
Define
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 h_c(x)&=e^{-8x^2}H(x)\cos(16x),&
 h_s(x)&=e^{-8x^2}H(x)\sin(16x),\\
 f_c&=(D^2-1/4)h_c,& f_s&=(D^2-1/4)h_s.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{TS1}
\]
The definitions of \(H\) and \(\Phi\), including both terms of
every theta summand, are the original Rodgers--Tao formulas
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.05914v5}{\texttt{phidef},
\texttt{htdef}, \texttt{hoz}, \texttt{sas}}. Their original
LaTeX and exact factors are used in AP2--3.

The Mellin convention remains
\(F_f(s)=\int f(x)e^{-(s-1/2)x}\,dx\).
Put \(w=s-1/2\), \(\sigma_1=1+itw\), \(\sigma_0=itw\),
and write out the two complete original-zeta terms
\[
\begin{split}
 Z_1(s)&=e^{t(w-i/(2t))^2}\sigma_1(\sigma_1-1)
       \pi^{-\sigma_1/2}\Gamma(\sigma_1/2)\zeta(\sigma_1),\\
 Z_0(s)&=e^{t(w+i/(2t))^2}\sigma_0(\sigma_0-1)
       \pi^{-\sigma_0/2}\Gamma(\sigma_0/2)\zeta(\sigma_0).
\end{split}\tag{TS2}
\]
Every exceptional product is continued as the displayed full product.
The Gaussian integration in AP3 proves, with no discarded factors,
\[
 F_c(s)=\frac{s(s-1)\sqrt{\pi t}}{16}(Z_1+Z_0),\qquad
 F_s(s)=\frac{s(s-1)\sqrt{\pi t}}{16i}(Z_1-Z_0).
 \tag{TS3}
\]
The sources are real, \(f_c\) is even and \(f_s\) is odd. Each
has Gaussian decay with all derivatives, and both transforms vanish
at the two prescribed endpoints \(0,1\). No assertion that these
are their only zeros is made here.

On their complex span \(V=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\{f_c,f_s\}\),
use the conjugate-linear-first form
\[
 \langle f,g\rangle_W
 =\sum_\rho m_\rho\overline{F_f(1-\overline\rho)}F_g(\rho).
 \tag{TS4}
\]
The sums converge absolutely by the same Gaussian bounds and original
zero count used in AP4--6. The reflected source is
\(f^\#(x)=\overline{f(-x)}\), so the complete source convolution
for TS4 is \(k_{f,g}=f^\#*g\). For any such convolution,
the original arithmetic receiver is
\[
\begin{split}
 P(k)&=\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}
                    [k(\log n)+k(-\log n)],\\
 A_\infty(k)&=-(\gamma_{\rm E}+\log\pi)k(0)
 +\int_0^\infty
 \frac{e^{-x}k(0)-\frac12e^{-x/4}[k(x/2)+k(-x/2)]}{1-e^{-x}}\,dx,\\
 \langle f,g\rangle_W&=A_\infty(k_{f,g})-P(k_{f,g}).
\end{split}\tag{TS5}
\]
Apply the finite-cutoff calculation AP6 to
\(A(s)=\overline{F_f(1-\overline s)}F_g(s)\). It retains
every \(A(-2m)\), the pole at one, and the matching Gamma boundary;
both endpoint terms vanish for these actual tests. This proves TS5
without assigning a value to an individually divergent trivial-zero
series. The source conventions are those of
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9811068v1}{Alain Connes,
Appendix II, (1)--(11), Theorems 1 and 6}; the Gaussian extension
and all boundary terms are proved in the appendix.

For the cross term, \(k_{c,s}=f_c*f_s\) is odd, and
\(k_{s,c}=-k_{c,s}\). Both prime legs cancel, the symmetric Gamma
numerator is zero, and \(k_{c,s}(0)=0\). Hence
\[
 G=\begin{pmatrix}K_c&0\\0&K_s\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 K_c=\langle f_c,f_c\rangle_W,\quad
 K_s=\langle f_s,f_s\rangle_W,
 \qquad 50<K_c,K_s<64.\tag{TS6}
\]
This is the original basis, without rescaling either source.

Here are the additional calculations proving the second bound.
If
\[
 U_H=(256x^2-1089/4)H-32xH'+H'',\qquad
 V_H=512xH-32H',
\]
then
\[
 f_c=e^{-8x^2}(U_H\cos16x+V_H\sin16x),\qquad
 f_s=e^{-8x^2}(U_H\sin16x-V_H\cos16x).\tag{TS7}
\]
Replacing \(H\) by the explicitly separated constant \(C=J_0\)
defines comparison sources; all residuals remain, as in AP7--18.
The two sine/cosine formulas have exactly the same absolute
envelopes \(A,E,B,E_1\). The odd autocorrelation is
\(k_s=-f_s*f_s\), not \(f_s*f_s\). Its exact comparison is
\[
\begin{split}
 k_{s,0}(x)=\frac{C^2\sqrt\pi}{128}e^{-4x^2}\bigl\{&
 (65536x^4-1622528x^2+1250369)\cos16x\\
 &+2048x(256x^2-1121)\sin16x\\
 &-e^{-16}(65536x^4-49664x^2+3137)\bigr\}.
\end{split}\tag{TS8}
\]
Indeed \(-h_{s,0}*h_{s,0}=C^2\sqrt\pi\,e^{-4x^2}
(\cos16x-e^{-16})/8\); apply \((D^2-1/4)^2\).
The comparison differs from AP14 only in the displayed opposite-frequency
term; that term remains in the finite computation.

The identical source bounds imply the identical residual estimates
\(d_0<0.303\), \(d_2<175\), \(E_\Gamma<6.1\), \(E_P<0.45\).
AP19--23 prove the full prime tail \(<10^{-21}\), exact Gamma

origin-interval bound. The 100-bit interval calculation
\texttt{certify\_pair.py} evaluates TS8 and the original moments,
with source-tail radius \(10^{-300}\) proved in the appendix.
For the sine comparison it gives
\[
 56.95<A_\infty(k_{s,0})-P_{s,0,64}<56.97.
\]
Adding the entire residual as in AP26 proves TS6. Thus for every
\((a,b)\ne(0,0)\),
\[
 50(|a|^2+|b|^2)
 <\langle af_c+bf_s,af_c+bf_s\rangle_W
 <64(|a|^2+|b|^2).\tag{TS9}
\]
In the unscaled exponential basis \(f_\pm=f_c\pm if_s\), the matrix is
\[
 G_\pm=\begin{pmatrix}K_c+K_s&K_c-K_s\\K_c-K_s&K_c+K_s\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{TS10}
\]
This follows by substituting \(a=z_++z_-\),
\(b=i(z_+-z_-)\); it retains the factor two in its eigenvalues
\(2K_c,2K_s\).

\subsection{The exact map into the original even prime source}
The original moment receiver uses \(H_{\rm ev}=\mathcal S(\mathbb R)^{\rm even}\),
with \(m(h)=(h(0),\int h)\). The odd source \(f_s\) is therefore
not an input to that receiver. The following exact arrow provides
the connection, retaining its domain:
\[
 U:V\longrightarrow H_{\rm ev},\qquad
 U(af_c+bf_s)=af_c+bf_s'.\tag{TS11}
\]
Set the actual constants
\[
 A=-J_2-1089C/4,\quad B=-48J_2-4868C,\quad
 R=-\frac{\sqrt{\pi/32}}{32}e^{-8}.
 \tag{TS12}
\]
Direct differentiation of TS7 gives \(f_s'(0)=B\).
Oddness gives \(f_s(0)=\int f_s=0\); decay gives
\(\int f_s'=0\). AP27 gives the two moments of \(f_c\).
Consequently
\[
 mU(af_c+bf_s)=M\binom ab,\qquad
 M=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\R&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 M^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1/R\\1/B&-A/(BR)\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{TS13}
\]
All three constants are strictly negative; \(\det M=-BR\ne0\).
This proves that TS11 is injective and provides its inverse on
its image from the two original moments. Its construction is not
asserted to preserve the form evaluated directly on \(Uf\): every
transported form below explicitly uses \(U^{-1}\).

For completeness the algebraic prime receiver itself is as follows.
Put \(\mathscr R=\mathbb C[\mathbb Q_{>0}^{\times}]\),
\(I=\ker\operatorname{aug}\),
\(M_0=\ker(\operatorname{aug}\otimes m)\), and \(Q_0=M_0/IM_0\).
The moment lifts
\((1-2\pi y^2)e^{-\pi y^2}\), \(2\pi y^2e^{-\pi y^2}\)
have moments \((1,0)\), \((0,1)\). Hence
\[
 Q_0\cong\ker m\ \oplus\ (I/I^2)\otimes\mathbb C^2,
 \qquad I/I^2\cong\bigoplus_p\mathbb C\ell_p.\tag{TS14}
\]
The identity \([t_{ab}-1]=[t_a-1]+[t_b-1]\) modulo \(I^2\)
and the independent valuations \(v_p\) prove the second isomorphism.
The first follows by writing
\(M_0=(\mathscr R\otimes\ker m)\oplus(I\otimes\mathbb C^2)\)
and multiplying by \(I\). In particular
put \(\Phi(\sum_a t_a\otimes h_a)=\sum_a h_a\) and
\(\beta(\sum_a t_a\otimes h_a)=
\sum_p\ell_p\otimes\sum_a v_p(a)m(h_a)\).
Both descend to the displayed quotient: multiplication by \(t_b-1\)
kills \(\Phi\), and its \(\beta\) value is
\(\sum_p\ell_p\otimes v_p(b)m(\Phi c)=0\) for \(c\in M_0\).
Then
\[
 \mathcal B_p:V\longrightarrow Q_0,\qquad
 \mathcal B_p f=[(t_p-1)\otimes Uf],\qquad
 (\Phi,\beta)\mathcal B_p f=(0,\ell_p\otimes mUf)
 \tag{TS15}
\]
is an isomorphism onto the complete two-dimensional boundary summand
at the original prime \(p\). Its inverse is TS13, followed by
\((a,b)\mapsto af_c+bf_s\). These are the same quotient maps as
\href{https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/zeta-function-research-reader/blob/ec73ac350d0cda3d95c0bd1361bede33166b4d34/workbenches/splitzero-tandem/continuations/20260923-original-zeta-return/NOTE.tex}{FR15--25};
the displayed proof fixes their action on this new source.

The transported positive form on this summand, in the unchanged
moment coordinates \((m_0,m_1)\), is exactly
\[
 Q_B(m_0,m_1)=K_c|m_1/R|^2
             +K_s\left|\frac{m_0-A m_1/R}{B}\right|^2.
 \tag{TS16}
\]
This uses the specified inverse of TS15. The original periodization
seminorm of this boundary summand remains zero because \(\Phi=0\).
Equation TS16 does not identify that seminorm with a positive norm;
it gives the explicit form brought from TS4 by the injective receiver.

\subsection{Supported zero gives a specified oblique projection}
Retain the original full bounded distributive lattice \(L\) and
\(G_L(\mathbb Z)\), with \(e=(0,1_L)\),
\(\tau=(0,0_L)\). Multiplication by \(e\) sends each supported
integer to \(e\) and fixes every zero label \(z_\lambda\).
On finite supported-integer coefficients it is therefore
\(a\mapsto(\sum_n a_n)\delta_0\). Under the actual coefficient
heat map
\[
 A_Ta(y)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}a_n
       \frac{e^{-(y-n)^2/(4T)}}{\sqrt{4\pi T}},\qquad T>0,
\]
the two moments are \((\sum_na_nk_T(n),\sum_na_n)\),
where \(k_T(n)=(4\pi T)^{-1/2}e^{-n^2/(4T)}\).
After multiplication by \(e\) they are
\((\kappa_T m_1,m_1)\), \(\kappa_T=(4\pi T)^{-1/2}\).
Every moment pair occurs already for an even coefficient vector:
\[
 a_0=\frac{m_0-k_T(1)m_1}{k_T(0)-k_T(1)},\qquad
 a_1=a_{-1}=\frac{k_T(0)m_1-m_0}{2[k_T(0)-k_T(1)]}.
 \tag{TS17}
\]
All other coefficients are zero. The denominator is positive;
substitution proves both moments. Thus the induced endomorphism
of the actual prime-boundary summand is well-defined, independently
of the chosen coefficient lift, and is
\[
 J_T=\begin{pmatrix}0&\kappa_T\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{TS18}
\]
No multiplication on the whole vector-space quotient is postulated.
On the source plane the exact conjugate is
\[
 E_T=M^{-1}J_TM=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\c(T)&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad c(T)=\frac{\kappa_T R-A}{B},\qquad E_T^2=E_T.
 \tag{TS19}
\]
The range is \(\mathbb C u_T\), \(u_T=(1,c(T))^T\), and
the kernel is \(\mathbb C(0,1)^T\). It acts as the identity on
its range and is not an invertible operator on the entire plane.
The unital algebra map \(\mathbb C[q]/(q^2-q)\to\operatorname{End}(V)\),
\(q\mapsto E_T\), is faithful, since the range and kernel are
both nonzero. It factors through neither \(q\mapsto0\) nor
\(q\mapsto1\). This states exactly how the supported-zero datum
survives in the actual linear representation.

For the positive metric \(G\) of TS6,
\[
 GE_T-E_T^*G=
 \begin{pmatrix}0&-K_sc(T)\\K_sc(T)&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \|E_T\|_G^2=1+\frac{K_s}{K_c}c(T)^2.
 \tag{TS20}
\]
The norm formula follows by maximizing
\((K_c+K_sc^2)|a|^2/(K_c|a|^2+K_s|b|^2)\), with equality
at \(b=0\). Since \(A,R<0\), there is exactly one positive
time at which this projection is self-adjoint:
\[
 \boxed{T_*=\frac{(R/A)^2}{4\pi},\qquad
 2.99\cdot10^{-15}<T_*<3\cdot10^{-15}.}\tag{TS21}
\]
The unique equation is \(\kappa_{T_*}=A/R>0\); the interval
is certified from the original moments. At the stated specialization
\(T=1/32\), the same certificate gives
\[
 -0.055923<c(T)<-0.055922,\qquad
 1.00248<\|E_T\|_G^2<1.00394.\tag{TS22}
\]
This is the actual transported action; no orthogonal projection
has been substituted for it.

\subsection{The square-zero metric defect and its exact algebra}
For real \(T>0\), the \(G\)-orthogonal projection onto the same
range is computed, with all metric factors, by
\[
 D_T=K_c+K_sc(T)^2,\qquad
 P_T=\frac1{D_T}
 \begin{pmatrix}K_c&K_sc(T)\\K_cc(T)&K_sc(T)^2\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{TS23}
\]
Indeed \(P_Tv=u_T(u_T^*Gv)/D_T\), so its range, identity on
that range, idempotence and self-adjointness follow directly.
Define the exact difference
\[
 N_T=E_T-P_T=\frac{K_sc(T)}{D_T}
 \begin{pmatrix}c(T)&-1\\c(T)^2&-c(T)\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{TS24}
\]
Because \(E_TP_T=P_T\) and \(P_TE_T=E_T\),
\(N_T^2=0\). For the unchanged frame
\[
 \mathcal F_T=[u_T,w_T],\qquad
 w_T=(-K_sc(T),K_c)^T,\qquad\det\mathcal F_T=D_T,
\]
one has
\[
 \mathcal F_T^{-1}N_T\mathcal F_T
   =\begin{pmatrix}0&-K_sc(T)\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{TS25}
\]
This proves \(N_T\ne0\) precisely for \(T\ne T_*\).
Moreover \(u_T\) and \(w_T\) are \(G\)-orthogonal, with
\(\|u_T\|_G^2=D_T\), \(\|w_T\|_G^2=K_cK_sD_T\).
TS25 therefore proves the exact identity
\[
 \|N_T\|_G^2=(K_s/K_c)c(T)^2,\qquad
 \|E_T\|_G^2=1+\|N_T\|_G^2.\tag{TS25a}
\]
For each such \(T\), the map
\[
 \mathbb C[\eta]/(\eta^2)\longrightarrow
       \mathbb C[I,N_T]\subset\operatorname{End}(V),
 \qquad a+b\eta\longmapsto aI+bN_T\tag{TS26}
\]
is an isomorphism: multiplicativity follows from \(N_T^2=0\),
and injectivity follows from trace and \(N_T\ne0\).
At \(T_*\) its evaluated image is \(\mathbb C I\) and its
kernel is exactly \((\eta)\). Thus the nilpotent is a computed
defect of this original supported-zero action and its actual metric.
It is not identified with the idempotent \(e\) itself.

\subsection{Cohomology and representation specialization}
Take the complex disc \(\Omega=\{|T-T_*|<T_*/2\}\), and
the branch of \(\sqrt T\) positive at \(T_*\). Retain TS19,
TS23 and TS24 as holomorphic formulas on this disc. TS23 is the
analytic continuation of the real orthogonal-projection formula;
it is not claimed to be a Hermitian projection at nonreal \(T\).

These formulas have no hidden denominator zero. In fact
\[
 0<A/B<1/16,
\]
since \(16(J_2+1089C/4)<48J_2+4868C\), and
\(c(T)=(A/B)[(T_*/T)^{1/2}-1]\). On \(\Omega\),
\(|c(T)|<(1+\sqrt2)/16<1/4\), whence
\(|K_sc(T)^2|<4<K_c\). Thus \(D_T\ne0\), and the
frame TS25 is holomorphic and invertible on the whole disc.

Let \(\mathcal O\) be the ring of holomorphic germs at \(T_*\).
The coefficient \(n(T)=-K_sc(T)\) has the complete factorization
\[
\begin{split}
 c(T)&=-\frac{R(T-T_*)}
 {B\sqrt{4\pi}\sqrt T\sqrt{T_*}(\sqrt T+\sqrt{T_*})},\\
 n(T)&=\frac{K_sR(T-T_*)}
 {B\sqrt{4\pi}\sqrt T\sqrt{T_*}(\sqrt T+\sqrt{T_*})}.
\end{split}\tag{TS27}
\]
The displayed unit and its sign are retained; in particular
\(n'(T_*)=K_sR/[2B\sqrt{4\pi}T_*^{3/2}]>0\).
The actual two-periodic complex with every term \(\mathcal O^2\)
and differential \(N_T\) has
\[
 H^j\cong\mathcal O/(n(T))\cong\mathcal O/(T-T_*)
 \quad\text{for every integer }j.\tag{TS28}
\]
To prove it, use the full frame TS25. An element \(a u_T+b w_T\)
is in the kernel exactly when \(n(T)b=0\). The germ ring is a
domain and \(n\ne0\), so the kernel is \(\mathcal O u_T\);
the image is \(n\mathcal O u_T\). Evaluation at \(T_*\)
gives the second isomorphism, whose kernel is exactly the principal
ideal proved in TS27. At any real \(T\ne T_*\) the specialized
complex is acyclic; at \(T_*\) its differential is zero and its
cohomology is \(\mathbb C^2\) in every degree. These assertions
compute specialization directly, without assuming cohomology
commutes with it.
The additional fibre class is explicit. The natural map from
\(H^j\otimes_{\mathcal O}\mathbb C\) gives the line
\(\mathbb C u_{T_*}\) in fibre cohomology, while the other
class is represented by \(w_{T_*}\). The free resolution
\(0\to\mathcal O\xrightarrow{n}\mathcal O\to
\mathcal O/(n)\to0\) shows that
\(\operatorname{Tor}_1^{\mathcal O}(\mathcal O/(n),\mathbb C)
=\mathbb C\). Thus the base-change sequence here is exactly
\[
 0\longrightarrow\mathbb C u_{T_*}
 \longrightarrow H^j(V_{T_*},N_{T_*})
 \longrightarrow\mathbb C[w_{T_*}]\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{TS28a}
\]
All three spaces and maps have been evaluated in the original frame.
The quotient map sends \(a u_{T_*}+b w_{T_*}\) to \(b\)
times the Tor generator in the displayed resolution by \(n\).
If instead the connecting map uses the coordinate \(T-T_*\),
its value is \(b\,n'(T_*)\); the complete positive coefficient
is the one given immediately after TS27.

There is also an exact algebra specialization. The map
\[
 \mathcal O[\eta]/(\eta^2)\longrightarrow
       \operatorname{End}_{\mathcal O}(\mathcal O^2),
 \quad \eta\longmapsto N_T\tag{TS29}
\]
is injective. Trace kills a scalar coefficient of a relation
\(aI+bN=0\); a nonzero entry of \(N\) then gives \(b=0\)
in the domain \(\mathcal O\). Its image is a free rank-two
algebra. In the frame TS25 the representation of its two basis
elements has columns \((1,0,0,1)^T\) and \((0,n,0,0)^T\).
Its cokernel is \(\mathcal O^2\oplus\mathcal O/(n)\):
subtract the first row from the fourth and read the four coordinates.
All two-column minors generate exactly \((n)=(T-T_*)\).
The abstract algebra fibre at \(T_*\) is still the dual-number
algebra; its evaluated representation has kernel \((\eta)\).
This is the exact failure of injectivity under that specialization.

\subsection{Where this class lies on the full split prime spectrum}
The preceding local object has an exact map to the original arithmetic
base. The source construction is the
\href{https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/zeta-function-research-reader/blob/d2f0020a7c446f429682925c1c682d99ba86a4cc/workbenches/splitzero-tandem/foundations/globalization-semiring/globalization_note.tex}{original globalization note,
\texttt{cor:prime-ideals} and \texttt{thm:zero-prime-not-irr}},
and its full lattice extension is
\href{https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/zeta-function-research-reader/blob/a2851f9eb352e7e4376bc629de86fa4ed9c1c434/workbenches/splitzero-tandem/foundations/split-support-geometry-v11/split_support_geometry_arithmetic_curve_v11.tex}{version 11,
\texttt{def:lattice-split} and \texttt{thm:lattice-prime-classification}}.
The needed statements are rederived here. For a ring \(R\) and the unchanged bounded distributive
lattice \(L\) with \(0_L\ne1_L\), define
\[
 G_L(R)=\{(r,1_L):r\in R\}\cup\{z_\lambda=(0,\lambda):\lambda\ne1_L\}.
 \tag{TS30}
\]
Addition uses the ring sum and joins of labels, multiplication uses
the ring product and meets. Nonzero amplitudes always have label
\(1_L\); cancellations remain \(e=z_{1_L}\), and
\(\tau=z_{0_L}\). For a domain \(R\), the ideal
\[
P_e^R=\{z_\lambda:\lambda\in L\}
\]
is prime, since a product has zero amplitude exactly when one factor
has zero amplitude. It is the principal ideal \((e)\):
\(e z_\lambda=z_\lambda\) and every product by \(e\) has zero amplitude.
For a ring prime \(\mathfrak p\), let \(P_{\mathfrak p}^R\)
be the inverse image of \(\mathfrak p\) under amplitude.
These operations stay in the displayed carrier: any nonzero sum
has a top join, and any nonzero product has two top input labels.
The ring and lattice identities prove associativity, distributivity,
additive identity \(\tau\), and multiplicative identity \((1,1_L)\)
coordinate by coordinate. A semiring ideal contains \(\tau\), is
closed under addition, and absorbs multiplication by every element;
primality means that membership of a product forces membership of a factor.

For completeness all remaining prime points retain the full lattice.
A prime ideal excluding \(e\) contains no top-supported ring element,
since multiplying such an element by \(-1\) and adding would put
\(e\) in that ideal. It is therefore
\(Q_J=\{z_\lambda:\lambda\in J\}\), where \(J\) is a
proper lattice ideal prime for meets. Conversely every such \(Q_J\)
is prime by the meet-prime property, with nonzero ring products
remaining nonzero. A prime containing \(e\) contains every zero
label and is exactly \(P_{\mathfrak p}^R\) for a ring prime;
the ring ideal and its primality follow from the original top-supported
ring operations. This proves the classification with no quotient of \(L\).

The germ ring \(\mathcal O\) has precisely the ring primes
\((0)\), \(\mathfrak m=(T-T_*)\): every nonzero germ factors
as a finite power of \(T-T_*\) times its nonvanishing holomorphic
unit. Hence
\[
 Q_J\subsetneq P_e^{\mathcal O}\subsetneq
 P_{\mathfrak m}^{\mathcal O},\qquad
 \operatorname{Ann}_{\mathcal O}H^j=(T-T_*).
 \tag{TS31}
\]
The first symbols range over the actual lattice prime ideals; they
are not all replaced by a single point. For the original two-element
lattice only, the chain is \((\tau)\subsetneq(e)\subsetneq
P_{\mathfrak m}\).

The original integer constants give a unital semiring map
\(G_L(\mathbb Z)\to G_L(\mathcal O)\), preserving every label.
Each nonzero integer is a unit in \(\mathcal O\). Its map on prime
spectra therefore satisfies exactly
\[
 Q_J\longmapsto Q_J,\qquad
 P_e^{\mathcal O}\longmapsto P_e^{\mathbb Z},\qquad
 P_{\mathfrak m}^{\mathcal O}\longmapsto P_e^{\mathbb Z}.
 \tag{TS32}
\]
The cohomology module TS28, considered by restriction along amplitude,
has support \(P_{\mathfrak m}^{\mathcal O}\) and thus lies over
the supported-zero prime \(P_e^{\mathbb Z}\). To prove the support
directly, localization at \(Q_J\) inverts the annihilating scalar
\(e\); localization at \(P_e^{\mathcal O}\) inverts the annihilating
nonzero germ \(T-T_*\). Both modules therefore vanish. At
\(P_{\mathfrak m}^{\mathcal O}\), every inverted scalar has unit
germ amplitude, so localization leaves \(\mathcal O/(n)\) unchanged.
No ordinary positive
integer prime lies in the image of this complex-germ chart: each is
a unit here. This specifies the chart and its arithmetic location;
it does not identify its new specialization point with an ordinary
integer prime or with the absent zero.

Restriction of the module to the integer base has a different support
