\section{Independent review of the unsplit integral collision at
3}\label{independent-review-of-the-unsplit-integral-collision-at-3}

23 September 2026. This review checks the specified integral algebra,
its quotient, its cotangent and ordinary de Rham groups, and the
completed divided-power comparison. The calculations below confirm
IEC43--50, including their constants and nonzero classes. They also give
the complete additive image of the divided-power map and distinguish the
cotangent map before and after tensoring with the quotient.

The reviewed local source is
\url{INTEGRAL_EIGHT_STATE_COLLISION_DERIVATION.md}, SHA256
DAD299495E93DB162931C5CCDDD86610F352BA046A9F13FB91A9FD1ABEA00FBE. Actual
reading used IEC1--29, IEC37--52, and the explicit Appendix A
computations, especially P3.10--P3.14. The divided-power basis and
differential were checked against the complete DC1--DC10 calculation in
\url{COLLISION_DIVIDED_CLASS_DERIVATION.md}, SHA256
1CB2179581412097BDA232AC97579F848AEF923217B9D0B63CB6D0C6E939BF9C. These
are local review locators, not claims that this review or every reviewed
edition is public. No new historical source-reading or novelty claim is
made.

\subsection{R3.1. The retained algebras and their integral
quotient}\label{r3.1.-the-retained-algebras-and-their-integral-quotient}

Put \(O=\mathbb Z_3\), and retain \(x=R-1\). The two algebras are \[
B=O[x,T]/(f,g),\qquad
f=x^2(x+3),\qquad g=T^2-3x(x+2),
\] \[
C=O[\epsilon,T]/(\epsilon^2,T^2-6\epsilon).
\tag{R3.1}
\] Their respective \(O\)-bases are \[
1,x,x^2,T,xT,x^2T,\qquad
1,\epsilon,T,\epsilon T.
\tag{R3.2}
\] Successive division by the monic equations proves both spanning and
uniqueness. In particular both rings are free over \(O\), so
cancellation of a nonzero scalar in a free module is permitted; division
of an arbitrary element by 3 is not.

The map \[
q:B\longrightarrow C,\qquad x\longmapsto\epsilon,\quad T\longmapsto T
\tag{R3.3}
\] is a surjective ring homomorphism. Substitution makes both defining
relations zero, and its images contain the displayed basis of \(C\).
Comparing coefficients in (R3.2) proves \[
J:=\ker q=Ox^2\oplus Ox^2T=x^2B.
\] Since \(x^3=-3x^2\) and \(x^4=9x^2\), \[
J^2=9J,\qquad
J/J^2=(O/9O)[x^2]\oplus(O/9O)[x^2T].
\tag{R3.4}
\] Thus this particular kernel is not square-zero. It is different from
the square-zero kernel of \(C\to O[\eta]/\eta^2\).

The reduction \(B/3B=\mathbb F_3[x,T]/(x^3,T^2)\) has the single maximal
ideal \((x,T)\). Every maximal ideal of \(B\) contracts to the maximal
ideal of \(O\), since \(B\) is finite and integral over \(O\).
Consequently \(B\) is local. Both \(x+1\) and \(x+2\) are units, as
their residues in its residue field are nonzero. This does not supply
the missing inverse of 3 or a root-factor decomposition of \(B\).

\subsection{R3.2. The two cotangent complexes and their degree-minus-one
map}\label{r3.2.-the-two-cotangent-complexes-and-their-degree-minus-one-map}

In \(O[x,T]\), the polynomial \(f\) is a nonzero divisor. Modulo \(f\),
the polynomial \(g\) is monic in \(T\), so multiplication by \(g\) is
injective by highest-degree coefficient comparison. Therefore \((f,g)\)
is a regular sequence. The same argument applies to
\(\epsilon^2,T^2-6\epsilon\). In the relation bases, the
regular-immersion cotangent complexes are \[
L_{B/O}=
\left[B\bar f\oplus B\bar g
\xrightarrow{J_B}B\,dx\oplus B\,dT\right],
\quad
J_B=
\begin{pmatrix}3x(x+2)&-6(x+1)\\0&2T\end{pmatrix},
\] \[
L_{C/O}=
\left[C\bar f_C\oplus C\bar g_C
\xrightarrow{J_C}C\,d\epsilon\oplus C\,dT\right],
\quad
J_C=
\begin{pmatrix}2\epsilon&-6\\0&2T\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{R3.5}
\] The degrees are \(-1,0\), and the matrices give images as columns.

Set \[
n_1=x(x+3),\quad n_2=T(x+3),\quad n_3=Tx(x+3).
\] Direct coefficient comparison gives \[
\operatorname{ann}_B(T)=On_3,\qquad
\operatorname{ann}_B(T^2)=On_1\oplus On_3.
\tag{R3.6}
\] For detail, in \(O[x]/f\), multiplication of \(a+bx+cx^2\) by \(x\)
gives \(ax+(b-3c)x^2\). Its kernel is \(On_1\). Multiplication by
\(x+2\) is invertible, and multiplication by 3 is injective. Writing an
element of \(B\) uniquely as \(p+Tq\), multiplication by \(T\) gives
\(Tp+3x(x+2)q\). Its zero equation forces \(p=0\) and \(q\in On_1\). The
same comparison for \(T^2\) proves the second equality.

The kernel of \(J_B\) has the \(O\)-basis \[
u_1=(n_1,0),\quad u_2=(n_3,0),\quad u_3=(n_2,n_3).
\tag{R3.7}
\] Indeed the second row gives \(b=cn_3\). Since \[
(x+1)n_3=n_3,\qquad T^2n_2=6n_3,
\] the first row is \(T^2(a-cn_2)=0\). Equation (R3.6) now proves both
existence and uniqueness of the displayed coordinates.

In \(C\), \(Tb=0\) forces \(b=c\epsilon T\). The other kernel equation
\(2\epsilon a=6b\) then forces the scalar coefficient of \(a\) to be
zero and its \(T\)-coefficient to equal \(3c\). Therefore the kernel of
\(J_C\) has the \(O\)-basis \[
v_1=\epsilon\bar f_C,\quad
v_2=\epsilon T\bar f_C,\quad
v_3=3T\bar f_C+\epsilon T\bar g_C.
\tag{R3.8}
\]

The polynomial lift of \(q\) sends \[
f\longmapsto(\epsilon+3)f_C,\qquad
g\longmapsto-3f_C+g_C.
\] It therefore gives the chain map \[
\bar f\longmapsto(\epsilon+3)\bar f_C,\qquad
\bar g\longmapsto-3\bar f_C+\bar g_C,\qquad
dx\longmapsto d\epsilon,\quad dT\longmapsto dT.
\tag{R3.9}
\] Differentiating the two preceding polynomial identities verifies the
chain condition. Substitution in (R3.7) yields \[
u_1\longmapsto9v_1,\qquad
u_2\longmapsto9v_2,\qquad
u_3\longmapsto3v_3-3v_2.
\tag{R3.10}
\] For the last coefficient, the \(\bar f_C\)-component is \[
(3T+\epsilon T)(\epsilon+3)-9\epsilon T
=9T-3\epsilon T.
\] In the integral target basis \(v_1,v_2,v_3-v_2\), the matrix is
diagonal with entries \(9,9,3\). Thus \[
\ker\!\left(H^{-1}(L_{B/O})\to H^{-1}(L_{C/O})\right)=0,
\] \[
\operatorname{coker}\!\left(H^{-1}(L_{B/O})\to H^{-1}(L_{C/O})\right)
\simeq(O/9O)^2\oplus O/3O.
\tag{R3.11}
\] This confirms IEC45--47. All basis changes used here have unit
determinant.

\subsection{R3.3. The entire differential module and the quotient
map}\label{r3.3.-the-entire-differential-module-and-the-quotient-map}

Use the following actual forms in \(\Omega^1_{B/O}\): \[
\begin{gathered}
a_0=dx,\ a_1=x\,dx,\ a_2=x^2dx,\ a_3=Tdx,\ 
a_4=xTdx,\ a_5=x^2Tdx,\\
b_0=dT,\ b_1=x\,dT,\ b_2=x^2dT,\ 
b_3=TdT,\ b_4=xTdT,\ b_5=x^2TdT,\\
c=a_3-2b_1.
\end{gathered}
\tag{R3.12}
\] Multiply \(df=(6x+3x^2)dx\) by all six basis elements of \(B\). The
resulting relations are exactly equivalent to \[
3a_1=3a_2=3a_4=3a_5=0.
\tag{R3.13}
\] The even relations before this reduction are \(6a_1+3a_2=0\),
\(-3a_2=0\), \(9a_2=0\); the odd relations replace \(a_1,a_2\) by
\(a_4,a_5\). The reduction uses only the unit 2.

The multiples of \(dg=-6(x+1)dx+2T\,dT\) by \(1,x,x^2\) eliminate \[
b_3=3(a_0+a_1)=3a_0,\quad
b_4=3(a_1+a_2)=0,\quad b_5=-6a_2=0.
\tag{R3.14}
\] Its multiples by \(T,xT,x^2T\) give, after division only by 2, \[
3(-a_3-a_4+2b_1+b_2)=0,\quad
3(a_4+a_5+b_2)=0,\quad
6a_5+9b_2=0.
\] Together with (R3.13), these are exactly \(3b_2=3c=0\). Every
relation multiple has now been included. Eliminating the three \(b_j\)
with \(j\ge3\), and replacing \(a_3\) by \(c+2b_1\), therefore proves
the direct decomposition \[
\Omega^1_{B/O}
=Oa_0\oplus Ob_0\oplus Ob_1
\oplus(O/3O)\{a_1,a_2,a_4,a_5,b_2,c\}.
\tag{R3.15}
\]

In \(C\), write \[
\alpha=d\epsilon,\quad \beta=dT,\quad
\upsilon=\epsilon dT,\quad
\kappa=T\,d\epsilon-2\epsilon dT.
\] Multiplying \(2\epsilon d\epsilon=0\) and \(2TdT-6d\epsilon=0\) by
the four basis elements gives exactly \[
\epsilon d\epsilon=\epsilon T\,d\epsilon=\epsilon T\,dT=0,\qquad
TdT=3d\epsilon,\qquad3\kappa=0.
\] The same coefficient eliminations, with no other relation remaining,
give \[
\Omega^1_{C/O}
=O\alpha\oplus O\beta\oplus O\upsilon\oplus(O/3O)\kappa.
\tag{R3.16}
\] The quotient sends \(a_0,b_0,b_1,c\) respectively to
\(\alpha,\beta,\upsilon,\kappa\), and kills the other five torsion
generators. Hence \[
0\longrightarrow(O/3O)\{a_1,a_2,a_4,a_5,b_2\}
\longrightarrow\Omega^1_{B/O}
\longrightarrow\Omega^1_{C/O}
\longrightarrow0
\tag{R3.17}
\] is exact. It splits as a sequence of \(O\)-modules using the
displayed generators; no algebra section is asserted.

\subsection{R3.4. Ordinary de Rham groups, with exact
representatives}\label{r3.4.-ordinary-de-rham-groups-with-exact-representatives}

Put \(w=dx\wedge dT\). The coefficient ideal of the exterior relations
in \(B\,w\) is \[
(3x(x+2),6(x+1),2T)=(3,T).
\] Here \(x+1\) and 2 are units. Thus \[
\Omega^2_{B/O}=\mathbb F_3[x]/(x^3)\,w,\qquad
\Omega^j_{B/O}=0\quad(j\ge3).
\tag{R3.18}
\] In the direct decomposition (R3.15), the differential on the basis
(R3.2) is \[
d1=0,\quad dx=a_0,\quad d(x^2)=2a_1,\quad dT=b_0,
\] \[
d(xT)=c+3b_1,\qquad d(x^2T)=2a_4+b_2.
\tag{R3.19}
\] For a general element of \(B\), its \(a_0,b_0,b_1\) coordinates force
the coefficients of \(x,T,xT\) to be zero if it is closed. The remaining
\(a_1\) and \(2a_4+b_2\) coordinates force the coefficients of
\(x^2,x^2T\) to belong to \(3O\). Consequently \[
H^0_{\mathrm{dR}}(B/O)
=O\cdot1\oplus O\cdot3x^2\oplus O\cdot3x^2T.
\tag{R3.20}
\] This is also its exact subring of closed elements. If \(A_0=3x^2\)
and \(A_1=3x^2T\), then \[
A_0^2=27A_0,\qquad A_0A_1=27A_1,\qquad A_1^2=243A_0.
\] These follow from \(x^4=9x^2\) and \(x^4T^2=81x^2\).

The next differential satisfies \[
da_0=db_0=da_1=da_2=dc=0,\quad db_1=w,
\] \[
da_4=-xw,\quad da_5=-x^2w,\quad db_2=2xw.
\tag{R3.21}
\] In particular \(dc=-3w=0\). The images span (R3.18), proving that
\(H^2=0\). Their full kernel is \[
Oa_0\oplus Ob_0\oplus O(3b_1)
\oplus(O/3O)\{a_1,a_2,c,2a_4+b_2\}.
\] To check this assertion directly, the coefficient of \(b_1\) must be
divisible by 3, the coefficient of \(a_5\) must vanish, and the pair of
coefficients on \(a_4,b_2\) must be a multiple of \((2,1)\). Quotienting
by (R3.19) kills \(a_0,b_0,a_1,2a_4+b_2\), and imposes \(c+3b_1=0\).
Therefore \[
H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(B/O)
=(O/3O)[x^2dx]\oplus(O/3O)[3x\,dT],
\qquad H^j_{\mathrm{dR}}(B/O)=0\ (j\ge2).
\tag{R3.22}
\] The direct quotient computation proves that both representatives are
nonzero with exact order 3.

For \(C\), the exterior relation module is \((O/3O)z_C\), where
\(z_C=d\epsilon\wedge dT\). In (R3.16), \[
d\upsilon=z_C,\quad d\kappa=-3z_C=0,\quad
d\alpha=d\beta=0.
\] Its closed degree-one module is \[
O\alpha\oplus O\beta\oplus O(3\upsilon)\oplus(O/3O)\kappa.
\] The image from \(C\) is generated by \[
\alpha,\quad\beta,\quad d(\epsilon T)=\kappa+3\upsilon.
\] Consequently \[
H^0_{\mathrm{dR}}(C/O)=O,\qquad
H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(C/O)=(O/3O)[3\epsilon\,dT],\qquad
H^j_{\mathrm{dR}}(C/O)=0\ (j\ge2).
\tag{R3.23}
\] Degree zero follows as well by comparing the free
\(\alpha,\beta,\upsilon\) coordinates of the derivative of an arbitrary
element of \(C\).

The map \(q\) on degree-zero cohomology is augmentation, with kernel
\(O(3x^2)\oplus O(3x^2T)\) and zero cokernel. In degree one it has the
exact sequence \[
0\longrightarrow(O/3O)[x^2dx]\longrightarrow
H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(B/O)\xrightarrow{q_*}
H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(C/O)\longrightarrow0,
\] \[
q_*[3x\,dT]=[3\epsilon\,dT].
\tag{R3.24}
\] For \(D=O[\eta]/\eta^2\), the unit 2 makes
\(\Omega^1_{D/O}=O\,d\eta\). Its derivative is onto, so
\(H^0_{\mathrm{dR}}(D/O)=O\) and all its positive-degree cohomology
vanishes. The further quotient \(C\to D\) therefore kills both
degree-one classes originating in \(B\).

\subsection{R3.5. Tensoring the cotangent complex changes the
map}\label{r3.5.-tensoring-the-cotangent-complex-changes-the-map}

The complexes in (R3.5) consist of free modules. Their derived tensor
with \(C\) is therefore computed by ordinary tensoring. Nevertheless
\(H^{-1}(L_{B/O}\otimes_B C)\) is not obtained by identifying its
elements with the three source cycles in (R3.7).

The kernel of the tensored differential \[
\begin{pmatrix}6\epsilon&-6(\epsilon+1)\\0&2T\end{pmatrix}
\] has basis \[
w_1=(\epsilon,0),\quad w_2=(\epsilon T,0),\quad
w_3=(T,\epsilon T).
\tag{R3.25}
\] Indeed the second equation gives \(b=s\epsilon T\). Writing \(a\) in
the basis of \(C\), the first equation then forces its scalar
coefficient to be zero and its \(T\)-coefficient to equal \(s\).

The maps induced by (R3.9) are \[
w_1\longmapsto3v_1,\qquad
w_2\longmapsto3v_2,\qquad
w_3\longmapsto v_3-2v_2.
\tag{R3.26}
\] This map is injective with cokernel \((O/3O)^2\). It is different
from (R3.11). The original cycles map into the tensored kernel by \[
u_1\longmapsto3w_1,\quad
u_2\longmapsto3w_2,\quad
u_3\longmapsto3w_3+w_2;
\] composing with (R3.26) recovers exactly (R3.10).

The conormal sequence exhibits the same distinction. From (R3.15),
tensoring \(\Omega^1_{B/O}\) with \(C\) removes exactly \(a_2,a_5,b_2\).
The conormal map is \[
J/J^2\longrightarrow\Omega^1_{B/O}\otimes_B C,\qquad
[x^2]\longmapsto2a_1,\quad[x^2T]\longmapsto2a_4.
\tag{R3.27}
\] Its kernel is \(3(J/J^2)\), which is \((O/3O)^2\), and its image is
the two independent classes \(a_1,a_4\).

For a completely explicit connecting map, choose the sign given by
evaluating lifted relations in \(J/J^2\). The relation
\(f_C=\epsilon^2\) lifts to \(x^2\), and \(g_C=T^2-6\epsilon\) lifts to
\(3x^2\) in \(B\). Thus \[
\partial v_1=-3[x^2],\quad
\partial v_2=-3[x^2T],\quad
\partial v_3=-6[x^2T].
\tag{R3.28}
\] For example \(x^3=-3x^2\) gives the first equality, and
\(3Tx^2+3Tx^3=-6Tx^2\) gives the last. Formula (R3.28) has image
\(3(J/J^2)\) and kernel precisely the lattice generated by (R3.26).
Together with (R3.27), this proves the exactness of \[
0\longrightarrow H^{-1}(L_{B/O}\otimes_B C)
\longrightarrow H^{-1}(L_{C/O})
\xrightarrow{\partial}J/J^2
\longrightarrow\Omega^1_{B/O}\otimes_B C
\longrightarrow\Omega^1_{C/O}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{R3.29}
\] Reversing the global connecting-map sign changes no kernel or image.

\subsection{R3.6. The completed divided-power map, its image and its
kernel}\label{r3.6.-the-completed-divided-power-map-its-image-and-its-kernel}

Use the exact divided-power basis \[
E_n=z^{2n}/n!,\qquad O_n=z^{2n+1}/n!.
\] The completed source \(\widehat P\) consists of restricted sums \[
\sum_{n\ge0}a_nE_n+\sum_{n\ge0}b_nO_n,\qquad
a_n,b_n\in O,\quad a_n\to0,\quad b_n\to0
\] in the 3-adic topology. Its multiplication is given by \[
E_iE_j=\binom{i+j}{i}E_{i+j},\quad
E_iO_j=\binom{i+j}{i}O_{i+j},\quad
O_iO_j=\frac{(i+j+1)!}{i!j!}E_{i+j+1}.
\tag{R3.30}
\] Put \(U=x(x+2)\). The candidate map is \[
\rho_B(E_n)=\frac{3^n}{n!}U^n,\qquad
\rho_B(O_n)=T\rho_B(E_n).
\tag{R3.31}
\] Each scalar belongs to \(O\), because \(v_3(n!)\le n/2\). Evaluation
\(z\mapsto T\) in \(B\otimes_O\mathbb Q_3\), using \(T^2=3U\), verifies
all three multiplication formulas. Both sides have integral coefficients
and \(B\) embeds in this fraction-field algebra, so this proves the
identities in \(B\). The images are uniformly bounded in the finite free
complete module \(B\); restricted sums therefore converge and give the
claimed continuous ring map.

More explicitly, \[
U^2=x^2,\qquad U^n=3^{n-2}x^2\quad(n\ge2).
\tag{R3.32}
\] The first equality uses \(x^4+4x^3+4x^2=x^2\). The recurrence uses
\(x^2U=x^4+2x^3=3x^2\). Thus \[
\rho_B(E_0)=1,\quad \rho_B(E_1)=6x+3x^2,\quad
\rho_B(E_n)=c_nx^2\ (n\ge2),\qquad
c_n=\frac{3^{2n-2}}{n!}.
\tag{R3.33}
\] For \(n=2\), \(c_2=9/2\). Every \(c_n\) with \(n\ge2\) belongs to
\(9O\): it holds directly at \(n=2\), and for \(n\ge3\) the bound
\(2n-2-v_3(n!)\ge 3n/2-2\) is stronger. Hence the entire image,
including completed sums, is \[
\operatorname{im}\rho_B
=O\{1,T,6x+3x^2,6Tx+3Tx^2,9x^2,9Tx^2\}.
\tag{R3.34}
\] The right side is a closed finite free lattice, contains every basis
image, and is generated by the images of \(E_0,O_0,E_1,O_1,2E_2,2O_2\),
so both inclusions are proved.

The integral basis change \(X=x+x^2/2,\ Y=x^2\) changes its even
nonconstant generators into \(6X,9Y\). Since 2 is a unit, the associated
quotient is \(O/3O\oplus O/9O\); the odd part gives the same pair.
Therefore \[
\operatorname{coker}_O\rho_B
\simeq(O/3O)^2\oplus(O/9O)^2.
\tag{R3.35}
\] This is an additive cokernel, not a quotient ring by the unital
image.

There is also an exact description of the kernel without replacing
restricted sums by finite sums. Put \[
d_n=\frac{c_n}{c_2}=\frac{2\cdot3^{2n-4}}{n!}\quad(n\ge2).
\] Here \(d_2=1\), every \(d_n\) is integral, and \(d_n\to0\). By
independent coefficient comparison in (R3.33), a restricted even sum
lies in the kernel exactly when \[
a_0=a_1=0,\qquad
a_2=-\sum_{n\ge3}d_na_n.
\] The odd sum satisfies the same three conditions on its \(b_n\).
Consequently \[
\ker\rho_B=
\left\{
\sum_{n\ge3}a_n(E_n-d_nE_2)
+\sum_{n\ge3}b_n(O_n-d_nO_2):
a_n,b_n\to0
\right\}.
\tag{R3.36}
\] Convergence and the uniqueness of the coefficients \(a_n,b_n\) prove
that this parametrization is exact. The kernel is an ideal because it is
the kernel of the proved ring map; (R3.36) describes its entire
underlying restricted module.

The composition with \(q\) sends \(U\) to \(2\epsilon\), whose square is
zero. It is therefore exactly the map \(\rho_C\) with images \[
1,\quad T,\quad6\epsilon,\quad6\epsilon T
\] on \(E_0,O_0,E_1,O_1\), and zero on the other basis vectors. In
particular its additive cokernel is \((O/3O)^2\), and \[
(\operatorname{im}\rho_B)\cap J=9J.
\tag{R3.37}
\] The last statement follows by setting the coefficients of
\(1,T,x,Tx\) in (R3.34) to zero.

\subsection{R3.7. The cochain map and the complete degree-one
image}\label{r3.7.-the-cochain-map-and-the-complete-degree-one-image}

The source differential is \[
dE_0=0,\qquad dE_n=2O_{n-1}dz\ (n\ge1),\qquad
dO_n=(2n+1)E_n dz.
\tag{R3.38}
\] Define the degree-one map by \(h\,dz\mapsto\rho_B(h)dT\). For
\(n\ge1\), \[
d\rho_B(E_n)
=\frac{2\cdot3^n}{(n-1)!}U^{n-1}(x+1)dx
=2\rho_B(O_{n-1})dT.
\tag{R3.39}
\] For the second equality, multiply the relation \(2TdT=6(x+1)dx\) by
the integral coefficient \(3^{n-1}U^{n-1}/(n-1)!\). Thus no equality was
inferred by dividing a torsion differential module by 3.

Leibniz then gives \[
d\rho_B(O_n)=\rho_B(E_n)dT+2T\rho_B(O_{n-1})dT
=(2n+1)\rho_B(E_n)dT.
\] The last equality uses \(T^2\rho_B(E_{n-1})=n\rho_B(E_n)\), which
follows directly from (R3.31). The case \(n=0\) is \(dT=dT\). These
identities prove compatibility with the degree-zero differential.
Compatibility with the zero degree-two source follows from \[
d\bigl(\rho_B(h)dT\bigr)=\rho_B(dh/dz)dT\wedge dT=0.
\] The finite target modules are complete and all these maps are
continuous, so the identities extend to restricted sums.

The distinguished class is \[
\theta_3=[E_1dz]=[z^2dz].
\] It has exact order 3: \(dO_1=3E_1dz\), while the \(E_1dz\)
coefficient of every exact form is divisible by 3. Its image is \[
\rho_{B,*}(\theta_3)
=[T^2dT]=[3x^2dT+6x\,dT]
=2[3x\,dT]\ne0.
\tag{R3.40}
\] The middle torsion term is zero by \(3b_2=0\) in (R3.15). Under
\(q_*\), this maps to \(2[3\epsilon dT]\ne0\), and the dual-number
quotient sends it to zero. Every asserted nonzero class has exact order
3 by (R3.22)--(R3.23).

The complete image, beyond this single representative, can also be
calculated. For \(n\ge2\), (R3.33) and \(3b_2=0\) give \[
\rho_B(E_n)dT=c_nb_2=0.
\] The \(n=0\) term is the exact form \(dT\). For the odd terms, \[
\rho_B(O_0)dT=TdT=3dx
\] is exact. For \(n=1\), use \(UR=2x\) and (R3.14): \[
\rho_B(O_1)dT=3UTdT=9UR\,dx=18x\,dx=0.
\] For \(n\ge2\), \(c_nx^2TdT=3c_nx^2R\,dx=-6c_nx^2dx=0\). Thus only the
\(E_1dz\) coefficient contributes to cohomology.

To specify the full kernel, let \(Q=c_0(\mathbb N_0,O)\), the module of
coefficient sequences tending to zero. Since 2 is a unit, every
restricted sum of \(O_n dz\) is the differential of the corresponding
restricted sum \(\frac12 E_{n+1}\). Therefore \[
H^1(\widehat P\xrightarrow{d}\widehat P\,dz)
\simeq Q/\{((2n+1)c_n)_{n\ge0}:(c_n)\in Q\}.
\tag{R3.41}
\] This is the actual module quotient; no closure of the image or
finite-support replacement is imposed.

Splitting off the coordinate \(n=1\) gives the exact direct
decomposition \[
H^1(\widehat P\xrightarrow d\widehat P\,dz)
=(O/3O)\theta_3\oplus\mathcal K,
\] \[
\mathcal K=
c_0(\mathbb N_0\setminus\{1\},O)\big/
\{((2n+1)c_n)_{n\ne1}:(c_n)_{n\ne1}\to0\}.
\tag{R3.42}
\] The induced map is multiplication by 2 from the first summand to
\((O/3O)[3x\,dT]\), and is zero on \(\mathcal K\). Hence its kernel is
exactly \(\mathcal K\), its image is exactly that one-dimensional
summand, and its cokernel is \[
(O/3O)[x^2dx].
\tag{R3.43}
\] In degree zero the PD complex has kernel \(O\), by coefficient
comparison in (R3.38). Its map to (R3.20) is the inclusion of constants,
with cokernel \(O(3x^2)\oplus O(3x^2T)\).

\subsection{R3.8. Scope of the Frobenius
comparison}\label{r3.8.-scope-of-the-frobenius-comparison}

The preceding maps are ring and ordinary cochain maps. They do not
become compatible with the specified monoidal Frobenius merely because
the class \(\theta_3\) survives.

For completeness, every coefficient-preserving Frobenius lift on \(B\)
has either \[
(\Phi(x),\Phi(T))=(-3,3),\quad(-3,-3),
\] or \[
\Phi_v(x)=\frac32v^2,\qquad \Phi_v(T)=3v,\qquad v\in N_B.
\tag{R3.44}
\] Here \(N_B=On_1\oplus On_2\oplus On_3\), with \(N_B^4=0\). One can
derive the list by writing \(\Phi(x)=3u,\Phi(T)=3v\), since
\(x^3=T^3=0\) in \(B/3B\). The two equations reduce, using
torsionfreeness, to \[
u^2(u+1)=0,\qquad v^2=u(3u+2).
\] If \(u\) is a unit, then \(u=-1\), and locality together with the
unit 2 gives \(v=1\) or \(v=-1\). Otherwise \(u+1\) is a unit, so
\(u^2=0\), \(v^2=2u\), and \(v^4=0\). This gives the last family and
proves its necessity. Conversely \(N_B^4=0\) proves that every displayed
\(v\) supplies a lift.

The nilradical description used here follows directly from evaluation at
\((x,T)=(0,0),(-3,3),(-3,-3)\). Their common kernel has exactly the
basis \(n_1,n_2,n_3\); multiplying those basis elements gives \[
n_1^2=n_1n_3=n_2n_3=n_3^2=0,\quad
n_2^2=18n_1,\quad n_1n_2=3n_3.
\] Thus the common kernel is nilpotent, and every nilpotent belongs to
it because the target \(O^3\) is reduced.

Compatibility with the source lift \(z\mapsto z^3\) would require
\(\Phi(T)=T^3\). The two constant lifts fail after evaluation at
\((0,0)\). In the third family, equality would force \(v=Tx(x+2)\), but
its evaluation at \((-3,3)\) is 9, whereas every nilpotent evaluates to
zero. Thus no lift in (R3.44) is compatible with this specified source
lift. The exact generator defects are \[
\Phi_v(T)-T^3=3v-T^3,\qquad
\delta_v(T)-\rho_B(\delta_{\widehat P}(z))=v-Tx(x+2).
\tag{R3.45}
\] This confirms IEC52 with its original coordinates and signs.

Each of these lifts nevertheless acts as zero on (R3.22). For
\(\Phi_v\), write \(X=3v^2/2,Y=3v\). Then \[
X^2dX=0,\qquad
3X\,dY=\frac{27}{2}v^2dv=\frac92\,d(v^3).
\] These are respectively zero and an explicit exact form over \(O\).
The two constant lifts annihilate every positive-degree differential.
This proves the claimed zero action without discarding either of the
nonzero classes before applying that action.

\subsection{Review outcome}\label{review-outcome}

No correction to the specified \(p=3\) module decompositions, IEC45--47
cotangent map, IEC44 de Rham quotient, or IEC48--50 divided-power class
map was found. The exact supplements are (R3.17), (R3.29),
(R3.34)--(R3.37), and (R3.41)--(R3.43). In particular the \(9,9,3\)
cotangent cokernel is the one before tensoring the source complex with
\(C\); after tensoring, the corresponding cokernel is \((O/3O)^2\). The
full divided-power cohomology image is exactly the \([3x\,dT]\) summand,
and the other class \([x^2dx]\) is its exact cokernel.

As an independent exact matrix check, multiplying both Jacobian columns
by all six basis elements, reducing with the original monic equations
over \(\mathbb Z\), and taking the Smith form gives nonnegative diagonal
entries \(1,1,2,6,6,6,6,6,6,0,0,0\). At 3 this confirms exactly three
free generators and six torsion generators of order 3. The proofs above
establish the generators, their maps, and all cohomology independently
of that computation.
