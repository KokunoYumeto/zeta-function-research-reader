\section{The external element and the endpoint maps in the adele-class
construction}\label{the-external-element-and-the-endpoint-maps-in-the-adele-class-construction}

This note defines an exact algebraic extension of the function space
used in Alain Connes's adele-class construction, and computes its
endpoint quotient, periodization kernel, support strata, and
stabilizers. The carrier extension is the programme's actual split-zero
extension: the external element \(\tau\) and the supported adele zero
\(e=0_A^\bullet\) are distinct throughout.

The human source is Alain Connes,
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9811068}{\emph{Trace formula in
noncommutative geometry and the zeros of the Riemann zeta function},
arXiv:math/9811068}, Section III, equations (1)--(19), and Section VI,
equations (1)--(16). The original author TeX was read at local source
lines 608--822 and 1574--1726. Section III supplies the Bruhat--Schwartz
subspace with two vanishing endpoints, its weighted orbital norm, and
its periodization map. Section VI supplies the local-zero fixed-point
calculation and carefully identifies its trace discussion there as
formal. The programme's support convention is
\href{https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/zeta-function-research-reader/blob/39ced92952a9c807d51500783b221beb141da3b6/workbenches/splitzero-tandem/tex/support_diagrams.tex\#L9}{\emph{Reconstruction
with changes of support index}, D1--D8}, specialized to its split
extension; the relevant definitions and maps are included below. All
assertions specific to the extended carrier are proved here.

\subsection{1. The carriers and the exact action
groups}\label{the-carriers-and-the-exact-action-groups}

Let \(k\) be a global field, \(A=A_k\) its adele ring, \(J=A^\times\)
its idele group, and \(C_k=J/k^\times\) its idele class group. Use the
usual local absolute values normalized for the product formula, and
write \[
|j|=\prod_v|j_v|_v\qquad(j\in J).
\tag{TC1}
\] Only finitely many factors differ from 1. The product formula gives
\(|q|=1\) for \(q\in k^\times\). Fix additive Haar measure \(dx\) on
\(A\); its scalar change law is \(d(jx)=|j|\,dx\). All integrals below
use this fixed measure, with no change of its scale.

Define the split-zero semiring \[
G(A)=\{\tau\}\sqcup\{a^\bullet:a\in A\},\qquad e=0_A^\bullet.
\tag{TC2}
\] Two supported elements add and multiply by their adele values. The
remaining laws are \[
x+\tau=x,\qquad x\tau=\tau x=\tau.
\tag{TC3}
\] Its unit is \(1_A^\bullet\). The supported part is closed under both
operations, but its zero \(e\) is not the additive identity of \(G(A)\).
The idele action is \[
j\cdot a^\bullet=(ja)^\bullet,\qquad j\cdot\tau=\tau.
\tag{TC4}
\] Composition and identities hold because they do so for adele
multiplication, while the external element is fixed. This action
preserves addition and the external identity, and commutes with scalar
multiplication. Multiplication by an idele is generally not a unital
ring endomorphism of the adele carrier; no such assertion is needed for
this group action.

There are equivariant semiring maps \[
p:G(A)\to A,\qquad p(\tau)=0_A,\quad p(a^\bullet)=a,
\] \[
\chi:G(A)\to\mathbb B,\qquad\chi(\tau)=0,\quad\chi(a^\bullet)=1,
\tag{TC5}
\] where \(\mathbb B\) has join as addition and meet as multiplication,
and the idele group acts trivially on it. Direct evaluation on two
supported elements and on any pair involving \(\tau\) verifies both
operations and both identities. The joint map is injective, with image
\[
\{(0_A,0)\}\cup(A\times\{1\}).
\tag{TC6}
\] Thus the arithmetic map identifies exactly \(\tau\) and \(e\), while
the support map distinguishes them.

As an additive monoid, \(G(A)\) is not a group. A supported element
cannot have an additive inverse to the external identity: its sum with
any supported element remains supported, and adding \(\tau\) leaves it
supported. The arithmetic projection is its universal additive-group
quotient. Indeed if \(h:G(A)\to V\) is an additive-monoid map to an
abelian group, then \(h(e)=h(e)+h(e)\), hence \(h(e)=0\). Its
restriction \(a\mapsto h(a^\bullet)\) is additive and sends the adele
zero to zero, so defines an additive-group map \(A\to V\). This map
factors \(h\) through \(p\), including at \(\tau\), and surjectivity of
\(p\) makes it unique. This states exactly which quotient recovers the
additive group; it does not equip the original semiring with an
additive-group Haar measure.

Taking only orbit sets gives \[
X_\tau:=G(A)/k^\times
\cong(A/k^\times)\sqcup\{[\tau]\}.
\tag{TC7}
\] No supported orbit contains \(\tau\), proving the disjoint
decomposition. Commutativity of the idele action with \(k^\times\) makes
(TC7) a \(C_k\)-set. The quotient map induced by \(p\) identifies
exactly the two fixed points \([\tau]\) and \([e]\). This is an
orbit-set assertion; addition on multiplicative orbit classes is not
being presumed well defined, and no topology on (TC7) has been chosen.

\subsection{2. An algebraic function-space extension and its
endpoints}\label{an-algebraic-function-space-extension-and-its-endpoints}

Let \(\mathcal S(A)\) be the original Bruhat--Schwartz space. Define the
complex vector space \[
\mathcal F_\tau=\mathcal S(A)\oplus\mathbb C_\tau.
\tag{TC8}
\] An element \((f,c)\) is the function on the set \(G(A)\) taking value
\(f(a)\) at \(a^\bullet\) and value \(c\) at \(\tau\). This is a
specified algebraic space of functions, not a claim that \(G(A)\)
already has a Bruhat--Schwartz theory. Its idele representation is \[
\widetilde U(j)(f,c)=(U(j)f,c),\qquad
(U(j)f)(a)=f(j^{-1}a).
\tag{TC9}
\] Scalar multiplication by an idele preserves the local Schwartz and
locally constant compact-support conditions, hence preserves
\(\mathcal S(A)\). The identity and composition laws in (TC9) follow by
substituting \(j^{-1}a\). On \(\tau\), the value remains \(c\) because
(TC4) fixes the point.

At this stage the acting group is \(J\), not \(C_k\): the maps
\(f(a)\mapsto f(q^{-1}a)\) for \(q\in k^\times\) need not be the
identity on the original test functions. Passing to the idele class
group requires a quotient or another proved factorization. The endpoint
representation below does factor, by the product formula.

Put \[
\mathcal S(A)_0=\left\{f\in\mathcal S(A):f(0_A)=0,
\quad\int_A f(a)\,da=0\right\}.
\tag{TC10}
\] This is exactly Connes's Section III, equation (6). Define \[
\mathcal E_\tau:\mathcal F_\tau\longrightarrow\mathbb C^3,
\qquad (f,c)\longmapsto
\left(f(0_A),\int_A f(a)\,da,c\right).
\tag{TC11}
\]

\textbf{Theorem TC1 (the three endpoint quotient).} The map (TC11) is
onto, with kernel \(\mathcal S(A)_0\oplus0\). It is an equivariant map
for the idele action on its source and the action \[
j\cdot(z_0,z_1,z_\tau)=(z_0,|j|z_1,z_\tau)
\tag{TC12}
\] on its target. Thus there is an exact sequence of complex
\(J\)-representations \[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal S(A)_0\longrightarrow\mathcal F_\tau
\xrightarrow{\mathcal E_\tau}
\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C(1)\oplus\mathbb C\longrightarrow0.
\tag{TC13}
\] The action on the final three-dimensional quotient factors through
\(C_k\).

\textbf{Proof.} The kernel is the three simultaneous vanishing
conditions, which are precisely \(f\in\mathcal S(A)_0\) and \(c=0\). To
prove surjectivity with actual functions, choose a nonnegative
elementary Bruhat--Schwartz function \(\psi\) with \(\psi(0_A)=1\) and
integral \(I>0\). Such a function is a product of nonnegative smooth
compactly supported factors equal to 1 at zero at the archimedean
places, together with characteristic functions of compact open additive
subgroups at the nonarchimedean places, equal to \(1_{\mathcal O_v}\) at
all but finitely many places. In a function field there are no
archimedean factors. Each factor has finite positive integral, and the
restricted-product measure gives finite positive total integral.

Choose a nonarchimedean place \(w\) where the chosen factor is the
characteristic function of a compact open additive subgroup \(K_w\).
There exists \(b_w\notin K_w\), since a local field has elements with
arbitrarily negative valuation. Let \(b\in A\) have component \(b_w\) at
\(w\) and zero elsewhere, and put \(\psi_b(a)=\psi(a-b)\). Then
\(\psi_b(0_A)=0\), and translation invariance of the fixed additive Haar
measure gives \(\int\psi_b=I\). Hence \[
h_0=\psi-\psi_b,\qquad h_1=I^{-1}\psi_b
\tag{TC14}
\] satisfy \((h_0(0),\int h_0)=(1,0)\) and \((h_1(0),\int h_1)=(0,1)\).
The three elements \((h_0,0),(h_1,0),(0,1)\) map to the coordinate basis
of \(\mathbb C^3\), proving surjectivity. This supplies a linear
section; it is not asserted to be an equivariant section.

Evaluation at zero gives \((U(j)f)(0)=f(0)\). Changing variables
\(a=jb\) gives \[
\int_A f(j^{-1}a)\,da=|j|\int_A f(b)\,db.
\tag{TC15}
\] The external coordinate is fixed by (TC9). These three equalities
prove equivariance (TC12). Finally \(|q|=1\) for \(q\in k^\times\), so
that subgroup acts trivially on every endpoint. \(\square\)

If only Connes's original two endpoints are imposed, the resulting
invariant subspace is \[
\mathcal D_\tau:=\mathcal S(A)_0\oplus\mathbb C_\tau.
\tag{TC16}
\] There is a split exact sequence of \(J\)-representations \[
0\longrightarrow\mathbb C_\tau
\longrightarrow\mathcal D_\tau
\xrightarrow{\pi}\mathcal S(A)_0\longrightarrow0,
\qquad\pi(f,c)=f.
\tag{TC17}
\] Its section is \(f\mapsto(f,0)\), which commutes with (TC9). Thus the
new fixed coordinate is a fully specified additional representation,
even though the original two constraints do not detect it.

\subsection{3. Periodization and the external
orbit}\label{periodization-and-the-external-orbit}

For \(f\in\mathcal S(A)\) and \(x\in J\), define the unweighted orbital
sum \[
(Pf)(x)=\sum_{q\in k^\times}f(qx).
\tag{TC18}
\] This sum converges absolutely. The needed convergence can be verified
directly from the test-function definition. In a function field, a
Bruhat--Schwartz function has compact support. The diagonal subgroup
\(k\) is discrete in \(A\), and its intersection with any compact set is
finite. To see the latter assertion without assuming it for an arbitrary
discrete subset, take a neighbourhood \(W\) of zero with
\(W\cap k=\{0\}\) and a smaller neighbourhood \(V\) with
\(V-V\subseteq W\). A compact set has a finite cover by translates of
\(V\), each containing at most one point of \(k\), since two such points
would have nonzero difference in \(W\cap k\). Applying this to
\(x^{-1}\operatorname{supp}(f)\) proves finiteness of the sum. A
neighbourhood \(W\) as used here follows from the product formula:
restrict every finite absolute value to at most 1 and require at one
place a value strictly below 1; no nonzero global element can satisfy
these inequalities.

In a number field, each Bruhat--Schwartz function is a finite sum of
products \(f_\infty g_f\), where \(f_\infty\) is a Schwartz function on
the finite-dimensional real vector space \(k_\infty\), and \(g_f\) is
locally constant with compact support on the finite adeles. For fixed
\(x\), the finite components of a \(q\) contributing to one product lie
in the compact set \(x_f^{-1}\operatorname{supp}(g_f)\). Differences of
any two such components lie in a fixed compact set \(K\). There is a
constant \(C\) such that \(\prod_{v\text{ finite}}|r_v|_v\le C\) for all
\(r_f\in K\): a compact subset of the finite adeles is contained in a
product of bounded local sets with integral coordinates outside finitely
many places. If two distinct contributing global elements had
sufficiently close archimedean coordinates, the product of the
archimedean absolute values of their nonzero difference would be
strictly less than \(C^{-1}\). The product formula for their difference
would then fail. Their archimedean coordinates are therefore uniformly
separated.

A uniformly separated subset of a real vector space of dimension \(d\)
has at most a constant times \((1+R)^d\) points in a ball of radius
\(R\): place disjoint balls of one fixed small radius around its points
and compare their Euclidean volumes with a ball enlarged by that radius.
On the other hand, for every integer \(N>0\), the Schwartz estimate and
the invertible linear map \(a_\infty\mapsto a_\infty x_\infty\) give a
bound \(|f_\infty(q_\infty x_\infty)|\le C_N(1+\|q_\infty\|)^{-N}\). The
locally constant compact-support factor is bounded. Summing over unit
radial shells and taking \(N>d+1\) therefore proves absolute
convergence. Finite sums of the elementary products preserve this
conclusion.

Absolute convergence permits reindexing by multiplication with
\(k^\times\). Thus \(Pf\) is a function on \(C_k\), and \[
P(U(j)f)(x)=(Pf)(j^{-1}x).
\tag{TC19}
\] The external extension is \[
P_\tau:\mathcal F_\tau\to\operatorname{Fun}(C_k,\mathbb C),
\qquad P_\tau(f,c)=Pf,
\tag{TC20}
\] where \(\operatorname{Fun}\) here means the vector space of functions
on the indicated set. Its exact kernel is \[
\ker P_\tau=(\ker P)\oplus\mathbb C_\tau,
\tag{TC21}
\] and its image is the image of \(P\). Both statements follow
immediately and in both directions from its defining equality
\(P_\tau(f,c)=Pf\). Restricting the source to (TC16) gives the same
statements with \(P\) restricted to \(\mathcal S(A)_0\). The extra
coordinate is therefore present in the source and explicitly in the
kernel of this observation map.

Connes's weighted map has the exact extension \[
(E_\tau(f,c))(x)=|x|^{1/2}\sum_{q\in k^\times}f(qx).
\tag{TC22}
\] The positive factor does not change the kernel or image comparison
with the original weighted map. If \(V(j)\xi(x)=\xi(j^{-1}x)\), then \[
E_\tau\widetilde U(j)=|j|^{1/2}V(j)E_\tau.
\tag{TC23}
\] Indeed \(|x|^{1/2}=|j|^{1/2}|j^{-1}x|^{1/2}\); substitute this and
(TC19). This is precisely the twist in Connes's Section III, equation
(19), with the external coordinate explicitly retained in the domain.

There is a different requirement if the same counting orbital sum is
demanded at the new point \(\tau\). Its proposed value is \[
\sum_{q\in k^\times}(f,c)(q\tau)
=\sum_{q\in k^\times}c.
\tag{TC24}
\] The group \(k^\times\) is infinite for every global field. A global
number field contains infinitely many nonzero rational integers; a
global function field contains all nonzero powers of any transcendental
element. A series on such a countable group with constant term \(c\)
converges only if \(c=0\): for an enumeration its terms must tend to
zero, and for the unordered finite-subset definition its increment on
any new singleton is always \(c\). If \(c=0\), the sum is zero. At the
old fixed point \(e\), the identical argument gives the independent
condition \(f(0_A)=0\). Consequently imposing the two original endpoint
constraints and convergence of (TC24) gives exactly the subspace
\(\mathcal S(A)_0\oplus0\). The projection in (TC17) identifies
\(\mathcal D_\tau/\mathbb C_\tau\) with this subspace by the inverse
section \(f\mapsto(f,0)\). This is the exact relation between the
vanishing-coordinate subspace and the quotient by the fixed-coordinate
line; neither operation proves that the external point was the same
point as \(e\).

\subsubsection{The algebraic rational-scaling quotient retains the
external
line}\label{the-algebraic-rational-scaling-quotient-retains-the-external-line}

There is a canonical representation of \(C_k\) before any completion,
obtained from coinvariants. Define \[
N_k=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}
\{U(q)f-f:q\in k^\times,\ f\in\mathcal S(A)\}.
\tag{TC24a}
\] Each such difference has value zero at zero. Its integral is
\((|q|-1)\int f=0\), by the product formula. Hence
\(N_k\subseteq\mathcal S(A)_0\). Commutativity of the idele group
implies \(U(j)(U(q)f-f)=U(q)U(j)f-U(j)f\), so \(N_k\) is invariant under
\(J\).

The coinvariant quotient of (TC8) is exactly \[
(\mathcal F_\tau)_{k^\times}
=\mathcal F_\tau/(N_k\oplus0)
\cong(\mathcal S(A)/N_k)\oplus\mathbb C_\tau.
\tag{TC24b}
\] Indeed the difference \(\widetilde U(q)(f,c)-(f,c)\) is
\((U(q)f-f,0)\), so the subspace generated by all these differences is
exactly \(N_k\oplus0\). Every element of \(k^\times\) now acts as the
identity, and the \(J\)-action therefore factors uniquely through
\(C_k\). Conversely any \(J\)-equivariant linear map from
\(\mathcal F_\tau\) to a representation on which \(k^\times\) acts
trivially kills every displayed difference, so factors uniquely through
(TC24b). This proves the full universal property of the quotient.

Since \(N_k\subseteq\mathcal S(A)_0\), (TC13) descends to the exact
sequence of \(C_k\)-representations \[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal S(A)_0/N_k
\longrightarrow(\mathcal F_\tau)_{k^\times}
\longrightarrow\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C(1)\oplus\mathbb C
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{TC24c}
\] The endpoint map is well defined because it kills \(N_k\); its kernel
is the quotient of its previous kernel by \(N_k\), and it remains onto
by the explicit preimages (TC14). Thus every exactness assertion follows
from the maps themselves.

The sum (TC18) kills \(N_k\) by its absolute-convergence reindexing. It
induces a \(C_k\)-equivariant map out of (TC24b), whose exact kernel is
\[
(\ker P/N_k)\oplus\mathbb C_\tau.
\tag{TC24d}
\] Membership in that kernel means exactly \(Pf=0\), independent of
\(c\), and changing \(f\) by \(N_k\) changes neither condition. The
external line has therefore survived the algebraic quotient by rational
scalings; it is the subsequent periodization observation that places the
entire line in its kernel. These are two different, fully specified
operations.

\subsection{4. Two specified completion procedures and their exact
difference}\label{two-specified-completion-procedures-and-their-exact-difference}

The preceding results require no Hilbert-space completion. The relation
to a completion is also exact once its seminorm is specified. On the
original domain \(\mathcal S(A)_0\), let \(\|f\|_\delta\) be Connes's
seminorm, with \(\delta>1\), from Section III, equation (7): \[
\|f\|_\delta^2=
\int_{C_k}|Pf(x)|^2(1+\log^2|x|)^{\delta/2}|x|\,d^*x.
\tag{TC25}
\] Here \(d^*x\) is exactly the original multiplicative Haar measure on
\(C_k\), and the original source supplies the finiteness of this
seminorm on its prescribed domain. Write
\(N_\delta=\{f:\|f\|_\delta=0\}\) and \(H_\delta\) for the completion of
\(\mathcal S(A)_0/N_\delta\). This definition does not identify an
almost-everywhere vanishing sum with pointwise vanishing without an
additional proof.

On \(\mathcal D_\tau\), the pullback seminorm \[
\|(f,c)\|_{\delta,\mathrm{obs}}=\|f\|_\delta
\tag{TC26}
\] has nullspace exactly \(N_\delta\oplus\mathbb C_\tau\). Projection in
(TC17) induces an isometry of its quotient onto
\(\mathcal S(A)_0/N_\delta\): it is onto; two pairs have the same
quotient class exactly when their first components differ by
\(N_\delta\); and the norms agree by definition. Therefore its
completion is canonically isometric to \(H_\delta\). This is an
assertion about the explicitly pulled-back seminorm (TC26), not an
assertion that adjoining a point automatically leaves an unspecified
Hilbert space unchanged.

For any fixed real number \(\lambda>0\), the equally specified seminorm
\[
\|(f,c)\|_{\delta,\lambda}^2
=\|f\|_\delta^2+\lambda|c|^2
\tag{TC27}
\] has nullspace \(N_\delta\oplus0\). Its quotient and completion are
the orthogonal direct sum \(H_\delta\oplus\mathbb C\), with the second
factor's squared norm scaled by \(\lambda\). To verify completeness, a
Cauchy sequence is Cauchy in each component by (TC27), and the two
component limits give a limit with the sum norm; density follows by
approximating the first component by original test functions and
retaining the second component exactly. The idele action on the extra
factor is trivial. Thus a one-dimensional fixed representation can
instead be retained by an explicit added term; (TC26) and (TC27) give
different results and an exact projection between them.

No additive Haar measure on the semiring \(G(A)\), no distributional
trace on its orbit space, and no equality of completed trace formulas
follows from either choice alone. The exact maps and the alternatives
are already specified in (TC17), (TC21), and (TC26)--(TC27).

\subsection{5. Global stabilizers and the supported local-zero
strata}\label{global-stabilizers-and-the-supported-local-zero-strata}

For a supported adele \(a^\bullet\), define its nonzero and zero
coordinate sets \[
T(a)=\{v:a_v\ne0\},\qquad Z(a)=\{v:a_v=0\}.
\tag{TC28}
\] Its stabilizer in \(J\) is exactly \[
J_{Z(a)}=\{j\in J:j_v=1\text{ for every }v\in T(a)\}.
\tag{TC29}
\] At each local field, the equation \(j_va_v=a_v\) forces \(j_v=1\) if
\(a_v\ne0\), by cancellation in that field, and gives no restriction if
\(a_v=0\). The idele restricted-product condition still applies to the
unconstrained coordinates, since (TC29) is a subgroup of \(J\). This
proves the formula. The new external element has stabilizer all of
\(J\); the supported zero \(e\) independently has the same stabilizer.

Let \(c:J\to C_k\) be the quotient. The stabilizer of the supported
orbit \([a]\) in \(A/k^\times\) is \[
\operatorname{Stab}_{C_k}([a])=c(J_{Z(a)}).
\tag{TC30}
\] Indeed \(c(j)[a]=[a]\) means that \(ja=qa\) for some
\(q\in k^\times\). Equivalently \(q^{-1}j\in J_{Z(a)}\), proving both
directions of (TC30). If \(T(a)\ne\varnothing\), the restriction of
\(c\) to \(J_{Z(a)}\) is injective. Its kernel consists of diagonal
\(q\in k^\times\) which equal 1 at every place in \(T(a)\); at even one
such place, injectivity of \(k\to k_v\) forces \(q=1\) in \(k\). If
\(a=0\), (TC30) gives the full group \(C_k\), as it also does at
\([\tau]\).

In particular an adele with every local coordinate nonzero has trivial
stabilizer in \(C_k\), whether or not its coordinates satisfy the idele
condition. If exactly the coordinate at \(v\) is zero, then (TC29) is
the copy of \(k_v^\times\) inserted at that coordinate and equal to 1
elsewhere, and (TC30) is its injective image in \(C_k\). This proves the
stabilizer formula used at Connes's generic points of the local-zero
hyperplanes. The entire old adele subset, its coordinate-zero
conditions, and these algebraic stabilizers embed equivariantly into
(TC7). Global adjunction adds one further fixed orbit; it does not
replace any of the old coordinate-zero strata.

For an arbitrary nonidentity \(g\in C_k\), the old fixed-point set may
also be described exactly as the union, over representatives \(j\) of
\(g\) and \(q\in k^\times\), of the images of adeles satisfying \[
a_v=0\quad\text{whenever }j_v\ne q.
\tag{TC31}
\] This is the same equation \(j_va_v=qa_v\), now solved at each field.
The fixed-point set in \(X_\tau\) is that old set disjointly unioned
with \(\{[\tau]\}\). No closure or transverse-space calculation for this
new orbit is being inferred from the set description.

\subsection{6. Coordinatewise splitting at finitely many
places}\label{coordinatewise-splitting-at-finitely-many-places}

Let \(F\) be a finite nonempty set of places, \(m=|F|\), and put \[
K_F=\prod_{v\in F}k_v,\quad J_F=\prod_{v\in F}k_v^\times,
\quad Q_F=\prod_{v\in F}G(k_v).
\tag{TC32}
\] No topology on the factors \(G(k_v)\) is assumed here. The scalar
group acts coordinatewise, fixing each external local element.

Every point of \(Q_F\) has a unique support subset \(E\subseteq F\) and
amplitudes \(a=(a_v)_{v\in E}\in\prod_{v\in E}k_v\): its coordinate is
\(a_v^\bullet\) on \(E\), and \(\tau_v\) outside \(E\). Denote it by
\((E,a)\). Therefore the carrier has the disjoint decomposition \[
Q_F=\coprod_{E\subseteq F}\prod_{v\in E}k_v.
\tag{TC33}
\] The empty product is a singleton, namely the tuple of all external
elements. If \(x=(E,a)\) and \(y=(E',b)\), then \[
\operatorname{supp}(x+y)=E\cup E',\qquad
\operatorname{supp}(xy)=E\cap E'.
\tag{TC34}
\] For the sum, a coordinate supported in just one input retains that
input amplitude, and a coordinate supported in both has amplitude
\(a_v+b_v\), still supported even if that sum is zero. For the product,
a missing coordinate in either factor is absorbing; on the intersection
the amplitude is \(a_vb_v\), again supported when zero. These coordinate
checks prove all statements of (TC34), including cancellation cases.

There is a canonical equivariant injective semiring map \[
\eta:G(K_F)\longrightarrow Q_F,
\quad\eta(\tau)=(\tau_v)_{v\in F},\quad
\eta(a^\bullet)=(a_v^\bullet)_{v\in F}.
\tag{TC35}
\] Its image consists of the full-support stratum and the single
empty-support point. Coordinate evaluation verifies addition,
multiplication, and both identities. The image types are disjoint, and
equality within full support identifies every amplitude, proving
injectivity. The action agrees coordinatewise, proving equivariance. For
\(m=1\) this is an isomorphism; for \(m\ge2\) any nonempty proper
support subset gives a point outside its image.

\textbf{Theorem TC2 (support orbits and stabilizers).} A point
\((E,a)\in Q_F\) is classified up to the action of \(J_F\) by the pair
\[
T=\{v\in E:a_v\ne0\}\subseteq E\subseteq F.
\tag{TC36}
\] Its stabilizer is \[
\operatorname{Stab}_{J_F}(E,a)
=\{j\in J_F:j_v=1\text{ for }v\in T\}
\cong\prod_{v\in F\setminus T}k_v^\times.
\tag{TC37}
\] There are exactly \(3^m\) orbits in \(Q_F\), exactly \(2^m\) fixed
points, and exactly \(2^{m-|T|}\) different orbits with a specified
nonzero locus \(T\). By contrast \(G(K_F)\) has \(2^m+1\) orbits and two
fixed points.

\textbf{Proof.} Multiplication by a nonzero local scalar preserves each
of the three local statuses: external, supported zero, and supported
nonzero. It therefore preserves \(E\) and \(T\). Conversely if two
points have the same pair, choose \(j_v=b_v/a_v\) on \(T\) and \(j_v=1\)
elsewhere; this takes one point to the other. Thus (TC36) classifies
orbits. A scalar fixes a nonzero supported coordinate only when it is 1,
while it fixes both a supported zero and an external coordinate without
restriction. This proves (TC37).

Each place has independently three possible statuses, so there are
\(3^m\) pairs of status patterns, hence that many orbits. A point is
fixed by every scalar if and only if \(T\) is empty. For each
\(E\subseteq F\), there is exactly one such point \(e_E\), with
supported zeros on \(E\) and external elements outside it. A nonzero
supported amplitude is not fixed by all local scalars, since a local
field has a unit other than 1 and multiplying that amplitude by such a
scalar changes it. Therefore the fixed points are exactly the \(2^m\)
points \(e_E\). With \(T\) prescribed, every coordinate in
\(F\setminus T\) can independently be supported zero or external, giving
\(2^{m-|T|}\) distinct orbits with the same stabilizer (TC37).

In the full-support stratum of (TC35), only supported zero or supported
nonzero can occur at a place, giving \(2^m\) orbits indexed by \(T\).
The extra global external point is one further orbit. The only fixed
point in full support is its supported zero, and the external point is
independently fixed, proving the last assertions. \(\square\)

The arithmetic coordinate map \(Q_F\to K_F\) sends a missing coordinate
to local zero and leaves every supported amplitude unchanged. For a
fixed amplitude tuple with nonzero locus \(T\), its fibre consists
exactly of the \(2^{m-|T|}\) choices of supported zero or external label
at the remaining coordinates. Thus (TC37)'s identical stabilizers do not
identify these points or their orbits: the arithmetic quotient is the
explicit map making the identifications.

\subsection{7. A finite-coordinate function-space
comparison}\label{a-finite-coordinate-function-space-comparison}

The finite support decomposition also permits an exact algebraic
function-space construction, with no unchosen topology on the split
carriers. For \(E\subseteq F\), put \(K_E=\prod_{v\in E}k_v\), and let
\(\mathcal S(K_E)\) be its usual local-product Bruhat--Schwartz space.
For \(E=\varnothing\), set \(K_E=\{*\}\) and
\(\mathcal S(K_E)=\mathbb C\). Define \[
\mathcal F_F=\bigoplus_{E\subseteq F}\mathcal S(K_E).
\tag{TC38}
\] There are finitely many summands. An element specifies a test
function separately on each support stratum of (TC33). The action of
\(j\in J_F\) on the \(E\)-summand is \(f_E(a)\mapsto f_E(j_E^{-1}a)\);
on the empty summand it is trivial.

Fix additive Haar measure on every nonempty \(K_E\). Its two endpoints
are evaluation at the supported zero of that stratum and integration.
For \(E\ne\varnothing\) their map is onto \(\mathbb C^2\): choose a
nonnegative compactly supported test function equal to 1 at zero,
translate it to have value zero at zero without changing its positive
integral, and repeat the two combinations in (TC14). A translation with
the required disjointness exists in every nonempty local product by
translating outside a sufficiently small compact support in one factor.
In the empty summand, evaluation and integration of its single scalar
would be the same functional, so retain one endpoint, not two.

Combining these maps gives a surjective endpoint map with target
dimension \[
2(2^m-1)+1=2^{m+1}-1.
\tag{TC39}
\] Its kernel is the direct sum of the two-endpoint kernels on all
nonempty strata, with zero on the empty stratum. The evaluation
character is trivial for each stratum. The integral character on a
nonempty stratum is \[
\chi_E(j)=\prod_{v\in E}|j_v|_v,
\tag{TC40}
\] by the same variable change as (TC15). Thus its endpoint
representation consists of \(2^m\) trivial evaluation lines and
\(2^m-1\) integral lines with the specified characters. This assertion
counts direct summands; coinciding characters do not merge their
separate endpoint coordinates.

Restriction to the image of (TC35) is the projection \[
\mathcal F_F\longrightarrow\mathcal S(K_F)\oplus\mathbb C_\tau
\tag{TC41}
\] onto the full-support and empty-support summands. It is onto and
equivariant, with kernel exactly the sum over all nonempty proper
\(E\subset F\). A section sets those other functions to zero and is also
equivariant. Hence coordinatewise splitting adds explicitly defined
strata and test-function representations beyond the one-line global
extension; the precise forgetting map is (TC41).

\subsection{8. The proved relation to the original
construction}\label{the-proved-relation-to-the-original-construction}

The new external point is a second global fixed orbit alongside the
supported adele zero. Its function coordinate is a trivial
one-dimensional representation. The exact endpoint quotient (TC13) has
three coordinates with characters \((1,|j|,1)\), whereas Connes's
original source uses the two old coordinates. Periodization along idele
orbits forgets the new coordinate by the exact kernel (TC21), and a
convergent counting orbital sum at that new fixed point imposes its
independent vanishing by (TC24). The two completion procedures
(TC26)--(TC27) show exactly when that line is removed and when it is
retained.

The global supported local-zero loci retain their original stabilizers
through the equivariant inclusion, as proved in (TC29)--(TC31).
Coordinatewise splitting is a different, larger construction:
(TC33)--(TC37) calculate its additional support labels, fixed points,
orbits, and arithmetic fibres, while (TC38)--(TC41) calculate a
corresponding finite-stratum function-space map. No identification of a
new trace distribution with Connes's trace distribution, and no
invariance of a topology or Hilbert completion without a specified
comparison, has been assumed.
