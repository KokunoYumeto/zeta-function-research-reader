Date: 2026-09-12. Scope: the historical identification of all ordinary Split-Zero semiring branches with frame points. Read carrier: output/split_zero_rh_tandem_2026-09-12/tex/split_zero_carriers.tex. No cumulative-paper edits.

## Exact classification

For commutative unital rings \(R,A\) and nontrivial bounded distributive lattices \(L,M\), retain the original carrier
\[
G_L(R)=\{(0,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}\cup\{(r,1_L):r\in R\}.
\]
Addition and multiplication are \((r,\lambda)+(s,\mu)=(r+s,\lambda\vee\mu)\) and \((r,\lambda)(s,\mu)=(rs,\lambda\wedge\mu)\). Write \(z_\lambda=(0,\lambda)\), \(\tau_L=z_{0_L}\), and \(e_L=z_{1_L}\). All homomorphisms preserve zero and unit.

The stronger exact classification is
\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{\rm Semi}(G_L(R),G_M(A))
\cong
\operatorname{Hom}_{\rm Ring}(R,A)
\times\operatorname{Hom}_{\rm DLat}(L,M).
\tag{1}
\]
The pair \((h,p)\) gives \(F(r,\lambda)=(h(r),p(\lambda))\). This lands in the carrier because a source element of non-top support has amplitude zero, whereas a nonzero amplitude can occur only at top support and \(p(1_L)=1_M\). Coordinate operations prove the homomorphism laws.

Conversely, \(z_\lambda+z_\lambda=z_\lambda\) implies that the amplitude \(a\) of \(F(z_\lambda)\) satisfies \(a+a=a\), hence \(a=0\). Thus \(F(z_\lambda)=z_{p(\lambda)}\) for a unique \(p\), and the zero-fibre operations imply preservation of finite joins, finite meets, and bottom. To prove preservation of top, use the original equality
\[
e_L=(1_R,1_L)+(-1_R,1_L).
\]
Its image has top support, because the first summand is the target unit. Its amplitude is zero by additive idempotence. Hence \(F(e_L)=e_M\), so \(p(1_L)=1_M\). Now \((r,1_L)+e_L=(r,1_L)\) implies \(F(r,1_L)\) has top support. Write it as \((h(r),1_M)\). The operations of the supported copy give a unital ring map \(h:R\to A\), and the formulas on the supported copy and zero fibre recover every value of \(F\), consistently at \(e_L\). This proves (1). It is compatible with composition because coordinate maps compose separately.

Consequently branches \(G_L(R)\to G(R)=G_{\mathbb B}(R)\) fixing every supported coefficient correspond exactly to bounded distributive lattice maps \(p:L\to\mathbb B\), where \(\mathbb B=\{0,1\}\). Their filters \(U=p^{-1}(1)\) are the proper prime filters, and conversely the indicator of a proper prime filter preserves the bounds, meets, and joins. The branch sends \(z_\lambda\) to \(e\) for \(\lambda\in U\), and to \(\tau\) otherwise.

## The completeness requirement

If \(L\) is a frame, the zero-amplitude fibre is the complete ordered set \(Z_L=\{z_\lambda:\lambda\in L\}\), ordered exactly as \(L\). A branch preserves arbitrary joins on this fibre precisely when \(p\) is a frame map. In filter form, this says
\[
\bigvee_\alpha\lambda_\alpha\in U
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\lambda_\alpha\in U\text{ for some }\alpha.
\tag{2}
\]
The reverse implication follows from upward closure. Thus the exact additional requirement is complete primeness.

The word continuous must mean arbitrary-join preservation on this fibre, or equivalently Scott continuity there. For the latter equivalence, a branch already preserves finite joins and bottom. For every \(S\subseteq L\), the joins \(d_F=\bigvee_{\lambda\in F}\lambda\), indexed by finite subsets \(F\subseteq S\) including the empty subset, form a directed family with supremum \(\bigvee S\). Directed-supremum preservation therefore gives
\[
p(\bigvee S)=\bigvee_Fp(d_F)=\bigvee_{\lambda\in S}p(\lambda).
\]
The converse follows because directed joins are joins.

## Ideal completion, section, and congruence

Let \(\operatorname{Idl}(L)\) consist of lower subsets containing zero and closed under finite joins. Meets are intersections. A join of ideals contains exactly the elements below finite joins of members of their union; the empty join is \(\{0\}\).

This is a frame. If \(x\in I\cap\bigvee_\alpha J_\alpha\), choose \(a_1,\dots,a_n\) from the union of the \(J_\alpha\) with \(x\le\bigvee_j a_j\). Then
\[
x=\bigvee_j(x\wedge a_j),
\]
where every summand belongs to some \(I\cap J_{\alpha_j}\). This proves \(I\cap\bigvee_\alpha J_\alpha\subseteq\bigvee_\alpha(I\cap J_\alpha)\); the reverse inclusion follows by containment.

Define
\[
q:\operatorname{Idl}(L)\to L,\quad q(I)=\bigvee I,
\qquad
\eta:L\to\operatorname{Idl}(L),\quad\eta(\lambda)=\downarrow\lambda.
\tag{3}
\]
Taking the ideal generated by a union does not change its supremum in \(L\), so \(q\) preserves all joins. The frame law in \(L\) gives
\[
q(I)\wedge q(J)=\bigvee_{i\in I,j\in J}(i\wedge j)
=\bigvee(I\cap J)=q(I\cap J).
\]
For the middle equality, each \(i\wedge j\) lies in the intersection, and every \(k\) in the intersection equals \(k\wedge k\). The bounds are preserved. Thus \(q\) is a frame map, surjective because \(q\eta=\mathrm{id}\).

The map \(\eta\) preserves both bounds and finite joins and meets: the ideal generated by \(\downarrow\lambda\cup\downarrow\mu\) equals \(\downarrow(\lambda\vee\mu)\), while intersections of principal ideals are principal ideals of meets. It preserves arbitrary meets for the same reason. It need not preserve arbitrary joins. The exact adjunction is
\[
q(I)\le\lambda\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad I\subseteq\downarrow\lambda.
\tag{4}
\]
Hence \(q\dashv\eta\), \(q\eta=\mathrm{id}\), and
\[
j=\eta q,\qquad j(I)=\downarrow\bigvee I
\tag{5}
\]
is an inflationary, idempotent, finite-meet-preserving nucleus. Its fixed elements are the principal ideals; their joins as a frame are \(j(\bigvee_\alpha I_\alpha)\), rather than necessarily the unmodified ideal joins.

Equation (1) supplies the exact semiring comparison
\[
Q:G_{\operatorname{Idl}(L)}(R)\twoheadrightarrow G_L(R),
\quad (r,I)\mapsto(r,q(I)),
\]
\[
E:G_L(R)\hookrightarrow G_{\operatorname{Idl}(L)}(R),
\quad(r,\lambda)\mapsto(r,\downarrow\lambda),
\qquad QE=\mathrm{id}.
\tag{6}
\]
Both preserve amplitude, the supported copy of \(R\), \(\tau\), and \(e\). The section \(E\) is a semiring section; \(\eta\) is generally not a frame section.

The kernel congruence is exactly
\[
(r,I)\sim(s,J)\iff r=s\text{ and }\bigvee I=\bigvee J.
\tag{7}
\]
Every element mentioned must belong to the restricted carrier. Nonzero amplitudes force \(I=J=L\), so nontrivial identifications occur only at zero amplitude. The pairs \(z_I\sim z_{\eta q(I)}\) generate the congruence, since every same-supremum pair identifies with the same principal ideal. Equivalently its generators are
\[
z_{\bigvee_{\operatorname{Idl}(L),\alpha}\downarrow\lambda_\alpha}
\sim z_{\downarrow(\bigvee_{L,\alpha}\lambda_\alpha)}.
\tag{8}
\]
Indeed \(I=\bigvee_{\lambda\in I}\downarrow\lambda\).

The semiring quotient has the unrestricted target universal property. If a semiring map \(H\) on the source of \(Q\) identifies (7), define \(\overline H(y)=H(Ey)\). It is a semiring map because \(E\) is, and \(\overline H Q=H\) because \(EQx\sim x\). Surjectivity of \(Q\) proves uniqueness.

## Two distinct lifts and exact descent

Every bounded lattice map \(p:L\to\mathbb B\) has the frame extension
\[
\widehat p:\operatorname{Idl}(L)\to\mathbb B,
\qquad\widehat p(I)=\bigvee_{\lambda\in I}p(\lambda).
\tag{9}
\]
It equals one precisely when \(I\) meets \(U=p^{-1}(1)\). If a generated ideal join meets \(U\), some member of \(U\) is below a finite join from the union. Upward closure and finite primeness imply that one member of that finite join lies in \(U\). This proves arbitrary-join preservation. If both \(I\) and \(J\) meet \(U\), choose \(i\in I\cap U\), \(j\in J\cap U\); their meet belongs to \(I\cap J\cap U\), proving preservation of finite meets. The converse implication follows by containment, and the bounds are preserved.

The identity \(\widehat p\eta=p\) follows since \(\downarrow\lambda\) meets \(U\) exactly when \(\lambda\in U\). Every ideal is the join of its principal ideals, so every frame map on \(\operatorname{Idl}(L)\) is determined by its restriction along \(\eta\). Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{\rm Frm}(\operatorname{Idl}(L),\mathbb B)
\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\rm DLat}(L,\mathbb B).
\tag{10}
\]

There is also the descending ordinary lift \(p q\). The two lifts agree on principal ideals and satisfy
\[
\widehat p(I)\le p(q(I)).
\tag{11}
\]
This inequality follows from \(\lambda\le q(I)\) for every \(\lambda\in I\). Equality for every ideal is equivalent to preservation of all joins by \(p\). One implication follows from (9). For the other, apply equality to the ideal generated by any family \(S\): its supremum is \(\bigvee S\), while (9) evaluates it as \(\bigvee_{\lambda\in S}p(\lambda)\).

Even a factorization of \(\widehat p\) as a function through \(q\) forces its factor to be \(p\), because restricting to principal ideals recovers \(p\). Therefore \(\widehat p\) descends through \(q\) if and only if \(p\) is completely join preserving, equivalently its filter is completely prime. This is exactly the requirement that (9) identify all kernel pairs (7) on their support coordinates. Thus the descending lift and join-preserving lift agree precisely for frame points; the existence of the semiring section does not make them agree for every ordinary branch.

## Boolean atoms and the atomless measure example

A complete Boolean algebra \(B\) is a frame. Put \(c=\bigvee_i(a\wedge b_i)\). For every \(i\), \(a\wedge b_i\le c\) implies \(b_i\le\neg a\vee c\); taking their join and meeting with \(a\) gives \(a\wedge\bigvee_i b_i\le c\). The reverse inequality is immediate.

Frame maps \(p:B\to\mathbb B\) correspond exactly to atoms. To prove this, set \(K=p^{-1}(0)\), \(k=\bigvee K\), and \(a=\neg k\). Join preservation gives \(p(k)=0\), so \(p(a)=1\) and \(a\ne0\). If \(p(b)=0\), then \(b\le k\), so \(a\wedge b=0\). If \(p(b)=1\), then \(p(\neg b)=0\), hence \(\neg b\le k\), so \(a\le b\). These cases prove \(p(b)=1\iff a\le b\). For \(0<c\le a\), the first case is impossible, so \(p(c)=1\) and \(a\le c\), giving \(c=a\). Thus \(a\) is an atom.

Conversely, for an atom \(a\), the map \(p_a(b)=1\iff a\le b\) preserves both bounds and finite meets. If \(a\le\bigvee_i b_i\), then \(a=\bigvee_i(a\wedge b_i)\). Each summand is zero or \(a\); they cannot all be zero. Hence \(a\le b_i\) for some \(i\). This proves arbitrary-join preservation. The two constructions are inverse because the filter of a point contains its recovered atom as least element.

An ordinary Boolean point corresponds instead to an ultrafilter. A prime filter is an ultrafilter because \(b\vee\neg b=1\) puts exactly one of \(b,\neg b\) in it. Conversely, if \(U\) is a maximal proper filter and \(b\notin U\), the filter generated by \(U\cup\{b\}\) is improper. Thus some \(u\in U\) satisfies \(u\wedge b=0\), giving \(\neg b\in U\). This property proves finite primeness: if neither \(b\) nor \(c\) lies in \(U\), then \(\neg(b\vee c)\in U\). Zorn's lemma gives such a maximal proper filter in every nontrivial Boolean algebra, since \(\{1\}\) is proper and a union of a chain of proper filters is proper. This is a choice-dependent existence proof, not a canonical branch selection.

For a concrete complete atomless example take the Lebesgue measurable subsets of \([0,1]\), modulo null symmetric difference. The Boolean operations descend, since changing an input by a null set changes the output only within the union of the input differences. The order is \([A]\le[D]\iff\mu(A\setminus D)=0\).

For any family \(([A_i])\), let \(s\) be the supremum of measures of finite unions of chosen representatives, including the empty union. Choose finite unions \(C_n\) of measure greater than \(s-1/n\), and set \(C=\bigcup_n C_n\). Replace \(C_n\) by the first \(n\) unions combined to obtain an increasing sequence. Continuity from below gives \(\mu(C)=s\). Each finite union with any \(A_i\) adjoined has measure at most \(s\), so \(\mu(C\cup A_i)\le s\) and \(\mu(A_i\setminus C)=0\). If \([D]\) is another upper bound, every selected \(A_i\setminus D\) is null, and their countable union \(C\setminus D\) is null. Hence \([C]\) is the least upper bound. Its universal property establishes independence of representative and selection choices. Complements give arbitrary infima.

For every \(\mu(A)>0\), the function \(t\mapsto\mu(A\cap[0,t])\) is \(1\)-Lipschitz on \([0,1]\), with endpoint values \(0,\mu(A)\). The intermediate value theorem gives a subset of measure \(\mu(A)/2\). Thus there are no atoms, while the top has measure one and differs from bottom. This complete Boolean algebra has ordinary branches by Zorn and no frame branches by the atom theorem.

There is an explicit countable failure and an explicit congruence pair for every ordinary branch. For its ultrafilter \(U\), choose \(a_0=1\), split each \(a_n\in U\) into equal-measure disjoint halves, and choose the unique half \(a_{n+1}\in U\). Then
\[
a_{n+1}\le a_n,\quad a_n\in U,\quad\mu(a_n)=2^{-n},\quad\bigwedge_n a_n=0.
\]
The infimum is zero because its measure is at most every \(2^{-n}\). Put \(b_n=\neg a_n\). Then \(b_n\uparrow1\), but every \(b_n\notin U\). The ideal
\[
I=\bigcup_n\downarrow b_n
\]
has \(q(I)=1=q(B)\) and avoids \(U\). Therefore
\[
\widehat p_U(I)=0,\qquad\widehat p_U(B)=1,\qquad p_U(q(I))=1.
\tag{12}
\]
The pair \(z_I\sim z_B\) belongs to (7), but the join-preserving ideal lift does not identify it. This proves the exact descent obstruction. On the original zero fibre all \(z_{b_n}\) map to \(\tau\), while their supremum \(z_1=e_B\) maps to \(e\), proving failure even of countable continuity.

## Additional audit: the operator-algebraic continuation

The added Section 5a, O1–O2, was read in the same report at SHA256 6B25F43BB383CB4EEF8B552D10D9D80BAFCC74F555BFEBFCFD808C86E0A099D8. The statements and proofs pass the audit. They identify every bounded-lattice branch of the probability measure algebra with a unique unital complex-linear star homomorphism on \(L^\infty(\Omega,\mu;\mathbb C)\), then identify its normal members exactly with the atoms. The factor
\[
\chi_a(f)=\mu(a)^{-1}\int_a f\,d\mu
\]
is retained and proved. No KMS property is implied by this classification alone.

The contractive simple-function extension in O1 is valid: the Boolean branch selects exactly one nonnull cell of each finite partition, refinements give independence and both algebra operations, and the selected coefficient has modulus bounded by the essential-supremum norm. Uniform density of simple functions therefore gives the unique extension. Conversely positivity of a star homomorphism follows by applying it to \(g^*g\), and the inequality \(\|f\|_\infty^2 1-f^*f\ge0\) proves its contractivity. These facts establish the inverse correspondence on the full algebra, rather than only on its projections.

The net statement in O2 is valid in the specified finite-measure space. Here is the complete representative-level version of its supremum construction. For a norm-bounded increasing net \(f_i\ge0\), put \(b=\sup_i\int f_i\,d\mu\). Choose indices whose integrals approach \(b\), then recursively choose a dominating sequence of indices \(j_n\) using directedness. The classes \(f_{j_n}\) increase. Choose representatives and remove the countable union of exceptional null sets on which their successive inequalities or their common norm bound fail; define them to be zero on this union. They are now pointwise increasing and uniformly bounded. Their measurable pointwise supremum \(g\) satisfies \(\int g=b\).

For each fixed original index \(i\), the element \(f_i\vee f_{j_n}\) lies below some net member by directedness. Its integral is therefore at most \(b\). Monotone convergence gives \(\int(g\vee f_i)\le b=\int g\). Since their difference is nonnegative, \(f_i\le g\) almost everywhere. Every upper bound of the original net is an upper bound of the selected sequence, hence of its pointwise supremum. Thus \(g=\sup_i f_i\) in \(L^\infty\). No common null set for the whole uncountable net is needed.

For an increasing net of finite-join projections, the selected sequence consists of projections. Its pointwise supremum is the indicator of the countable union of their selected supports. The least-upper-bound proof shows that this class equals the arbitrary essential join in the measure algebra. Therefore preservation of these \(L^\infty\) suprema is precisely arbitrary-join preservation of the Boolean branch, justifying O2's normality-to-frame implication.

There is no hidden sequential-versus-net discrepancy in this finite-measure setting. Every arbitrary join of measure-algebra elements is already the join of a countable subfamily, by the finite-union/integral construction above. Accordingly a Boolean branch preserving countable joins preserves every join. More generally, any positive functional on this \(L^\infty\) that preserves bounded increasing sequential suprema also preserves bounded increasing net suprema: the selected sequence \(f_{j_n}\) has the same supremum \(g\), and
\[
\varphi(g)=\sup_n\varphi(f_{j_n})
\le\sup_i\varphi(f_i)\le\varphi(g).
\]
This conclusion uses the actual finiteness and faithful measure in the construction; it is not asserted for arbitrary von Neumann algebras without that argument.
