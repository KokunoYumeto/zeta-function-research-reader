\section{The exact two-Stieltjes-space representation of the theta packet}
\label{sec:theta-stieltjes-pair}

We retain the actual function \(g(s)=2\xi(s)\), a nonempty finite
packet of its nontrivial zeros with their complete orders, and the
original monic packet polynomial \(h\). The packet is closed under
conjugation and under \(\rho\mapsto1-\overline\rho\), hence also under
\(\rho\mapsto1-\rho\). No zero in this packet is real: the argument
with the alternating eta series in Section~\ref{sec:historical-packet-ghost} applies to
the whole real interval \(0<s<1\). In particular \(g(1/2)\ne0\).
Throughout this section all polynomial degrees, powers of \(1/2\),
and local nilpotent orders are retained.

\subsection{The quadratic algebra and both reflection branches}

Put
\[
 z=s-\tfrac12,\qquad x=-z^2,\qquad d=\deg h=2D.
 \tag{SP.1}
\]
The even degree follows by partitioning the packet into reflection
pairs \(\{\rho,1-\rho\}\); each pair has the same order at its two
members and neither member is \(1/2\). For each such pair choose one
member, write \(\alpha_\rho=\rho-1/2\), and put
\[
 r_\rho=-\alpha_\rho^2,\qquad
 H(x)=\prod_{\{\rho,1-\rho\}}(x-r_\rho)^{m_\rho}.
 \tag{SP.2}
\]
The product is independent of the chosen member. Distinct reflection
pairs have distinct \(r_\rho\), because equal squares imply equal or
opposite \(\alpha_\rho\). Thus \(H\) is monic of degree \(D\),
\(H(0)\ne0\), and every original order occurs unchanged in \(H\).
Multiplication of the two factors in each pair proves
\[
 h(s)=(-1)^D H\bigl(-(s-\tfrac12)^2\bigr).
 \tag{SP.3}
\]
Conjugation permutes the factors of \(H\), so \(H\) has real
coefficients. Define \(A_H=\mathbb C[x]/(H)\), let \(X\) denote
multiplication by \(x\) in its basis \(1,x,\ldots,x^{D-1}\), and
let \(c_H\) be the column of its unit.

\begin{theorem}[A full algebra isomorphism with its original coordinates]
There is a unital complex algebra isomorphism
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal T:\mathbb C[s]/(h)&\longrightarrow
       \mathbb C[x,z]/(H(x),z^2+x)=A_H\oplus zA_H,\\
 [s]&\longmapsto\tfrac12+z.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{SP.4}
\]
The inverse sends \(x\) to \(-(s-1/2)^2\) and \(z\) to \(s-1/2\).
The multiplication in the displayed direct sum is
\[
 (a,b)(c,e)=(ac-xbe,\,ae+bc).
 \tag{SP.5}
\]
Here the direct sum describes the underlying module, with the
specified multiplication. In the ordered target basis
\(1,x,\ldots,x^{D-1},z,zx,\ldots,zx^{D-1}\), the matrix \(T\)
of \(\mathcal T\) has column \(n\), \(0\le n<2D\), equal to the
coefficients of
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 a_n(x)&=\sum_{2j\le n}\binom n{2j}
                       2^{-(n-2j)}(-x)^j,\\
 b_n(x)&=\sum_{2j+1\le n}\binom n{2j+1}
                       2^{-(n-2j-1)}(-x)^j.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{SP.6}
\]
Its determinant and the original multiplication matrix are exactly
\[
 \det T=(-1)^{D(D-1)/2},\qquad
 T A T^{-1}=\tfrac12 I_{2D}
                    +\begin{pmatrix}0&-X\\I_D&0\end{pmatrix},
 \quad A=M_s.
 \tag{SP.7}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The substitutions in (SP.4) and its inverse send the two defining
relations into zero by (SP.3) and \(z^2=-x\). They compose to the
identity on the generators and therefore on both algebras. Reduction
first by the monic polynomial \(z^2+x\) and then by the monic
polynomial \(H(x)\) gives a unique expression \(a+zb\) with
\(\deg a,\deg b<D\). Multiplication and the relation \(z^2=-x\)
give (SP.5). Expanding \((1/2+z)^n\) and separating even and odd
powers proves (SP.6). In the interlaced basis
\(1,z,x,zx,\ldots,x^{D-1},zx^{D-1}\), this change of basis is
triangular with diagonal entry \((-1)^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\).
Their product is \((-1)^{D(D-1)}=1\). Moving all the odd basis
members after the even members has \(D(D-1)/2\) inversions, proving
the stated determinant. The columns of the inverse are explicitly
the original polynomials \((-(s-1/2)^2)^j\) and
\((s-1/2)(-(s-1/2)^2)^j\). Finally multiplication of \(a+zb\)
by \(1/2+z\) gives \(a/2-xb+z(a+b/2)\), proving (SP.7).
\end{proof}

Here is the complete local meaning of the two branches, including
all derivatives. In the factor belonging to \(r=-\alpha^2\), of
order \(m\), write \(\eta=x-r\) and
\[
 B_r=\mathbb C[\eta]/(\eta^m),\qquad
 Z_r=\alpha\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\binom{1/2}{j}
                         \left(-\frac{\eta}{\alpha^2}\right)^j.
 \tag{SP.8}
\]
The formal binomial identity gives \(Z_r^2=\alpha^2-\eta=-x\)
in \(B_r\). Its constant term is \(\alpha\ne0\), so \(Z_r\)
is invertible. There are inverse algebra maps
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 B_r[z]/(z^2+x)&\longrightarrow B_r\times B_r,
       &a+zb&\longmapsto(a+Z_rb,\ a-Z_rb),\\
 (u,v)&\longmapsto \frac{u+v}{2}
                     +z\frac{u-v}{2Z_r}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{SP.9}
\]
The two idempotents are \((1\pm z/Z_r)/2\). Each component has
length \(m\), and corresponds respectively to the original zero
\(s=1/2+\alpha\) or \(s=1/2-\alpha\). To check the identification
on the full local algebras, in the first original factor put
\(w=s-(1/2+\alpha)\). The map is
\(\eta=-2\alpha w-w^2\), with inverse \(w=Z_r(\eta)-\alpha\).
Its linear coefficient \(-2\alpha\) is nonzero, and the two
compositions are identities modulo the respective \(m\)-th powers.
For the second factor replace \(\alpha\) by \(-\alpha\). This
proves the full local isomorphisms without replacing a local algebra
by evaluation at its centre. Taking their products gives (SP.4).

The entire functional equation \(g(1-s)=g(s)\) also has an exact
form in this variable. There is an entire function
\[
 \Phi(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
       \frac{(-1)^n g^{(2n)}(1/2)}{(2n)!}x^n,
 \qquad g(s)=\Phi\bigl(-(s-\tfrac12)^2\bigr).
 \tag{SP.10}
\]
Indeed the Taylor series of the entire even function \(g(1/2+z)\)
contains only even powers, and converges for every \(z\); its
even-power series therefore has infinite radius in \(x=-z^2\).
By the local isomorphisms above, \(\Phi\) vanishes to exactly order
\(m_\rho\) at each \(r_\rho\). Consequently
\[
 V(x)=(-1)^D\frac{\Phi(x)}{H(x)}
 \quad\hbox{is entire},\qquad
 v_h(s):=\frac{g(s)}{h(s)}=V(x),
 \quad \upsilon=j_hv_h,\quad \mathcal T(\upsilon)=(V\bmod H,0).
 \tag{SP.11}
\]
The element \(V\bmod H\) is a unit in \(A_H\), because the removed
orders were complete. We write \(U_V\) for its multiplication
matrix, with all its Taylor coefficients modulo \(H\) retained.

\subsection{The original measures and a Hilbert space isomorphism}

The two original measures are
\[
 d\mu(t)=|g(\tfrac12+it)|^2\frac{dt}{2\pi},\qquad
 d\nu_h(t)=|v_h(\tfrac12+it)|^2\frac{dt}{2\pi}.
 \tag{SP.12}
\]
Both densities are even. They have all polynomial moments, by the
actual theta decay already established for the kernel construction.
They are positive except at a discrete set, hence give a positive
norm to every nonzero polynomial. Their pushforwards under
\(t\mapsto t^2\) are, on \(x>0\), exactly
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 d\lambda_0(x)&=\frac{|\Phi(x)|^2}{2\pi\sqrt x}\,dx,\\
 d\lambda_h(x)&=\frac{|V(x)|^2}{2\pi\sqrt x}\,dx,
 &d\lambda_0(x)&=|H(x)|^2d\lambda_h(x).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{SP.13}
\]
For example the two half-lines contribute
\(2\,dt=dx/\sqrt x\); this proves the constants in (SP.13).
The formulas use the entire quotients at removable zeros.

\begin{theorem}[Both Hilbert components with an explicit inverse]
For either pair \((\nu,\lambda)=(\nu_h,\lambda_h)\) or
\((\mu,\lambda_0)\), the map
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal U:L^2(\lambda)\oplus L^2(x\lambda)&\longrightarrow L^2(\nu),\\
 (a,b)&\longmapsto\bigl[t\mapsto a(t^2)+itb(t^2)\bigr]
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{SP.14}
\]
is unitary. Its inverse, defined for almost every \(x>0\), is
\[
 a(x)=\frac{f(\sqrt x)+f(-\sqrt x)}2,\qquad
 b(x)=\frac{f(\sqrt x)-f(-\sqrt x)}{2i\sqrt x}.
 \tag{SP.15}
\]
Multiplication by the original coordinate \(s=1/2+it\) is carried
to \((a,b)\mapsto(a/2-xb,a+b/2)\). Its exact domain is
\[
 a\in L^2(\lambda)\cap L^2(x\lambda),\qquad
 b\in L^2(x\lambda)\cap L^2(x^2\lambda).
 \tag{SP.16}
\]
Reflection \(f(t)\mapsto f(-t)\) becomes \((a,b)\mapsto(a,-b)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The two parity components are orthogonal. More explicitly their
sum of squared absolute values at \(t\) and \(-t\) is
\(2|a(t^2)|^2+2t^2|b(t^2)|^2\); integrating proves
\(\|\mathcal U(a,b)\|_\nu^2=\|a\|_\lambda^2+\|b\|_{x\lambda}^2\).
The same identity applied to the even and odd parts of an arbitrary
\(f\in L^2(\nu)\) proves that (SP.15) belongs to the stated direct
sum and is its inverse. There is no atom at \(t=0\), so no extra
value is required there. Multiplication gives
\((1/2+it)(a+itb)=a/2-xb+it(a+b/2)\). On the original space its
domain is \(f\in L^2(\nu)\) with \(tf\in L^2(\nu)\), since
\(|1/2+it|^2=1/4+t^2\). Applying the same parity norm identity to
\(tf\) gives exactly (SP.16), including both conditions on each
component. The reflection formula follows by substitution.
\end{proof}

Multiplication by the original \(h\) is also preserved: from
\(L^2(\mu)\) to \(L^2(\nu_h)\) it is an isometry, and in (SP.14)
it is \((a,b)\mapsto((-1)^DH a,(-1)^DH b)\). It is onto as an
\(L^2\) map, since division by \(h\) off its finitely many real-line
zeros has precisely the inverse norm identity; those points have
measure zero. Its action on polynomial degree, however, remains
the actual multiplication by the monic polynomial of degree \(2D\).
Thus no finite polynomial subspace is identified with one of a
different degree without this explicit multiplication map.

\subsection{The two monic families and their original norms}

Let \(P_n\) be the monic orthogonal polynomial of degree \(n\)
for \(\lambda_h\), and let \(R_n\) be the monic polynomial for
\(x\lambda_h\). Set
\[
 p_n=\int|P_n|^2d\lambda_h,\qquad
 u_n=\int x|R_n|^2d\lambda_h.
 \tag{SP.17}
\]
All these numbers are strictly positive; the zeroth ones retain the
two masses. Orthogonal projection of \(x^n\) onto lower powers
in the positive moment matrix proves existence and uniqueness.
Since that matrix is real, all coefficients are real.

\begin{theorem}[Monicity, the Christoffel transform, and exact norm ratios]
The original vertical-line monic polynomials are
\[
 q_{2n}(s)=(-1)^nP_n(x),\qquad
 q_{2n+1}(s)=(-1)^nzR_n(x),\qquad
 \kappa_{2n}=p_n,\quad\kappa_{2n+1}=u_n.
 \tag{SP.18}
\]
For every \(n\ge0\), \(P_n(0)\ne0\), and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 c_n&=\frac{P_{n+1}(0)}{P_n(0)}<0,\\
 R_n(x)&=\frac{P_{n+1}(x)-c_nP_n(x)}{x},
 &u_n&=-c_np_n.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{SP.19}
\]
Consequently the original recurrence coefficients obey
\[
 a_{2n+1}=-c_n\quad(n\ge0),\qquad
 a_{2n}=\frac{p_n}{-c_{n-1}p_{n-1}}\quad(n\ge1).
 \tag{SP.20}
\]
The same statements with \(\lambda_0\), \(P_n^0,R_n^0,p_n^0,u_n^0\)
give the original theta norms \(\mathfrak h_j\) exactly:
\[
 \mathfrak h_{2n}=p_n^0,\quad
 \mathfrak h_{2n+1}=u_n^0=-c_n^0p_n^0,\quad
 \frac{\mathfrak h_{2n+1}}{\mathfrak h_{2n}}=-c_n^0,\quad
 \frac{\mathfrak h_{2n+2}}{\mathfrak h_{2n+1}}
       =\frac{p_{n+1}^0}{-c_n^0p_n^0}.
 \tag{SP.21}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since \(x=-z^2\), the leading coefficient of each polynomial in
(SP.18) in the original variable \(s\) is one. Lower-degree even
polynomials correspond to lower-degree polynomials in \(x\), and
the odd ones correspond to \(z\) times lower-degree polynomials
in \(x\). Their mixed inner products vanish by parity. Equation
(SP.14) therefore proves all the required orthogonality and norm
identities, and uniqueness identifies the original \(q_j\).

If \(n>0\) and \(P_n(0)=0\), write \(P_n=xQ\), with \(Q\) real,
monic and of degree \(n-1\). Orthogonality to \(Q\) would give
\(0=\int x|Q|^2d\lambda_h>0\), a contradiction. For \(n=0\),
\(P_0(0)=1\). The numerator in (SP.19) vanishes at zero; its quotient
by \(x\) is monic of degree \(n\). For each polynomial \(L\) of
degree below \(n\), its inner product with \(L\) for \(x\lambda_h\)
is \(\int(P_{n+1}-c_nP_n)\overline L\,d\lambda_h=0\). This proves
the asserted polynomial formula. Since \(R_n-P_n\) has lower degree,
\(\int R_nP_n\,d\lambda_h=p_n\), while
\(\int R_nP_{n+1}\,d\lambda_h=0\). Multiplying its numerator by
\(R_n\) and integrating proves \(u_n=-c_np_n\), and positivity
proves \(c_n<0\). Taking successive quotients of (SP.18) gives
(SP.20). The proof uses only positivity and the actual measure
identity, so its application to \(\lambda_0\) proves (SP.21).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Both full jet kernels and the exact weight operator}

Fix an original source degree \(N\ge2D-1\), and put
\(n_e=\lfloor N/2\rfloor\), \(n_o=\lfloor(N-1)/2\rfloor\).
Use the actual residue map \(p\mapsto\upsilon j_hp\) from degree
at most \(N\), with its norm from \(\nu_h\), onto the original
\(E_h=\mathbb C[s]/h\). Write \(G_N^{\rm src}\) for its minimum
metric in the original monomial coordinates. Thus at
\(N=d+m\) it is precisely the earlier \(G_m\), including the
fixed-section endpoint \(m=-1\). Define the full columns
\[
 f_n=U_VP_n(X)c_H,\qquad e_n=U_VR_n(X)c_H,
 \quad
 K_e=\sum_{n=0}^{n_e}\frac{f_nf_n^*}{p_n},\qquad
 K_o=\sum_{n=0}^{n_o}\frac{e_ne_n^*}{u_n}.
 \tag{SP.22}
\]
No column is replaced by scalar evaluation: \(P_n(X)\),
\(R_n(X)\), and \(U_V\) act on the whole length-\(D\) algebra.
Since \(n_e,n_o\ge D-1\), the first \(D\) monic polynomials in
each family give a basis modulo \(H\). The unit \(U_V\) is
invertible, so \(K_e,K_o>0\). Set \(G_e=K_e^{-1}\), \(G_o=K_o^{-1}\).

\begin{theorem}[The complete metric and a positive half-line comparison]
In the coordinates (SP.4), the actual minimum metric is
\[
 T^{-*}G_N^{\rm src}T^{-1}
       =\begin{pmatrix}G_e&0\\0&G_o\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \det G_N^{\rm src}=\det G_e\det G_o.
 \tag{SP.23}
\]
For \(W_N=A^*G_N^{\rm src}+G_N^{\rm src}A-G_N^{\rm src}\),
its full transported form and original relative allowance are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 T^{-*}W_NT^{-1}
   &=\begin{pmatrix}0&G_o-G_eX\\G_o-X^*G_e&0\end{pmatrix},\\
 \epsilon_{N-d}
   &=\left\|G_e^{-1/2}(G_o-G_eX)G_o^{-1/2}\right\|_{\rm op}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{SP.24}
\]
All zero eigenvalues and both signs of the relative form are
retained: they are the positive and negative singular values of
the displayed \(D\)-by-\(D\) matrix, with the remaining zero
eigenvalues of total multiplicity twice its nullity.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For completeness, if a surjective linear map from a coefficient
space with positive Gram matrix \(M\) to a target has matrix \(J\),
its minimum metric is \((JM^{-1}J^*)^{-1}\). Indeed the vector
\(M^{-1}J^*(JM^{-1}J^*)^{-1}y\) has image \(y\); it is orthogonal
in \(M\) to \(\ker J\), since its inner product with \(w\) in that
kernel is \(y^*(JM^{-1}J^*)^{-1}Jw=0\). All other preimages differ
by such a \(w\), so Pythagoras proves the formula. The polynomial
basis (SP.18) has diagonal source Gram matrix \(\kappa_j\).
Under \(T\), its residue columns are \(((-1)^nf_n,0)\) or
\((0,(-1)^ne_n)\). Thus the inverse minimum metric is exactly
\(\operatorname{diag}(K_e,K_o)\), proving the first identity.
The second follows from \(|\det T|=1\), proved in (SP.7).

Substitute (SP.7) and (SP.23) into the definition of \(W_N\).
The diagonal \(1/2\) terms cancel its subtracted metric; multiplying
the remaining two block matrices gives the first formula in
(SP.24). Taking the congruence by \(\operatorname{diag}(G_e^{-1/2},
G_o^{-1/2})\) gives a Hermitian matrix \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}
0&B\\B^*&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). If \(Bv=\sigma u\) and
\(B^*u=\sigma v\), then \((u,v)\) and \((u,-v)\) have eigenvalues
\(\sigma\) and \(-\sigma\). Singular value decomposition supplies
all these vectors; the two kernels supply the remaining vectors.
Its operator norm is consequently \(\|B\|\). An invertible
coordinate change preserves the quotient \(|y^*Wy|/(y^*Gy)\);
this proves the original relative allowance, including its zeros.
\end{proof}

The positive comparison in (SP.24) can also be written entirely
inside one copy of \(A_H\). Define
\[
 R=G_e^{-1/2}G_oG_e^{-1/2}>0,\qquad
 L=G_e^{1/2}XG_e^{-1/2}.
 \tag{SP.25}
\]
Then
\[
 \epsilon_{N-d}^2
   =\left\|(R-L)R^{-1}(R-L)^*\right\|_{\rm op}.
 \tag{SP.26}
\]
To verify this without changing a metric implicitly, observe that
the matrix \(B\) in (SP.24) is \((R-L)G_e^{1/2}G_o^{-1/2}\).
The product of the last factor with its adjoint is \(R^{-1}\),
so \(BB^*=(R-L)R^{-1}(R-L)^*\). This proves (SP.26).
In particular \(\epsilon_{N-d}=0\) holds precisely when
\(G_o=G_eX\). In that case \(X\) is similar to the positive
Hermitian matrix \(R\), hence is diagonalizable with positive
real spectrum. The minimal polynomial of multiplication by \(x\)
on \(A_H\) is \(H\): a polynomial killing this operator kills
the unit, and is therefore divisible by \(H\). Diagonalizability
then implies that \(H\) is squarefree. Thus a repeated original
zero forces a strictly positive finite allowance for every \(N\)
in this theorem. The complete operator in (SP.26) describes this
effect of the retained nilpotents; no bound on its limiting size
has been assumed.

Both finite updates are explicit. When \(N\) is even, passing
to \(N+1\) leaves \(K_e\) fixed and adds
\(e_{n_o+1}e_{n_o+1}^*/u_{n_o+1}\) to \(K_o\); when \(N\) is
odd it leaves \(K_o\) fixed and adds
\(f_{n_e+1}f_{n_e+1}^*/p_{n_e+1}\) to \(K_e\). In either case,
writing \(K\) for the changing block, \(v\) for the full added
column and \(k\) for its positive norm, the determinant ratio is
\[
 \frac{\det G_{N+1}^{\rm src}}{\det G_N^{\rm src}}
       =\left(1+\frac{v^*K^{-1}v}{k}\right)^{-1}.
 \tag{SP.27}
\]
Indeed \(K+vv^*/k=K^{1/2}(I+ww^*)K^{1/2}\) with
\(w=K^{-1/2}v/\sqrt k\); the middle matrix is identity on
\(w^\perp\) and has eigenvalue \(1+\|w\|^2\) on its span.
This argument also proves (SP.27) when \(v=0\), with ratio one.

\subsection{The original tesserine coordinates and support lift}

For an original zero \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\), let
\(\delta=\beta-1/2\). The coordinate introduced here is exactly
\[
 r_\rho=\gamma^2-\delta^2-2i\delta\gamma.
 \tag{SP.28}
\]
Retain the group-algebra element and its original characters
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 Q(\rho)=\rho\,1+(1-\rho)\sigma
             +\overline\rho\,\kappa+(1-\overline\rho)\sigma\kappa
                    \quad\text{in }\mathbb C[K_4],\\
 c_{++}=2,\quad c_{+-}=0,\quad
 c_{-+}=2(2\beta-1)=4\delta,\quad c_{--}=4i\gamma.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{SP.29}
\]
Direct multiplication, with all constants unchanged, gives
\[
 r_\rho-\overline{r_\rho}
       =-4i\delta\gamma=-\frac{c_{-+}c_{--}}4.
 \tag{SP.30}
\]
Every actual \(\gamma\) here is nonzero. It follows that
\(r_\rho\) is real if and only if \(\beta=1/2\); in that event
\(r_\rho=\gamma^2>0\). Thus the selected actual packet lies on
the critical line exactly when the roots of its complete \(H\)
are positive real numbers. Their multiplicities are still exactly
the original ones. On an eigenvector of \(A\) at \(\rho\), the
relative Rayleigh quotient of \(W_N\) is \(2\beta-1\), because
\(u^*A^*Gu+u^*GAu-u^*Gu=(2\beta-1)u^*Gu\). Hence
\[
 |c_{-+}|/2=2|\delta|\le\epsilon_{N-d}.
 \tag{SP.31}
\]
This is the one-factor spectral bound; the tensor and full-orbit
multiplicity bounds proved earlier remain in their original scope.

Finally each algebra isomorphism (SP.4) and (SP.9) induces its
exact split-zero isomorphism: send \(\tau\) to \(\tau\) and a
supported amplitude \(a\) to the supported image of \(a\).
The semiring laws follow directly from the algebra homomorphism
laws and absorption by \(\tau\); the inverse is the same lift
of the displayed inverse. In particular the target of the lift
of (SP.9) is \(G(B_r\times B_r)\), whose common external support
is retained. The map to \(G(B_r)\times G(B_r)\) sends \(\tau\)
to \((\tau,\tau)\) and a supported pair \((u,v)\) to its two
supported coordinates. It is an injective semiring homomorphism,
since both original amplitudes and the external point are recovered;
its image consists exactly of the two coordinates both absent or
both supported. The two mixed-support fibres remain explicit in
the product target. Character evaluation
\(G(\mathbb C[K_4])\to G(\mathbb C)\) likewise sends the supported
\(Q(\rho)\) to supported \(c_{-+}\). At a critical zero this image
is the supported zero \(e\), and the common external \(\tau\)
remains its separate absent fibre throughout these exact maps.

\subsection{The complete rank-one half-line boundary and its derivative cost}

The two positive kernels in (SP.22) have a further exact compatibility
identity. Their finite discrepancy retains the full local algebra, even
though its matrix rank is at most one. Use the kernels at the specified
original degree \(N\), and keep every column \(f_n,e_n\) defined in
(SP.22). For the two possible degree parities one has
\[
 \boxed{
 K_e-XK_o=
 \begin{cases}
       f_n e_n^*/p_n,&N=2n,\\[2pt]
       -f_{n+1}e_n^*/u_n,&N=2n+1.
 \end{cases}}
 \tag{SP.32}
\]
Here the matrices \(K_e,K_o\) in the first row have upper indices
\(n,n-1\), and those in the second row have upper indices \(n,n\).
In particular these are different finite kernels at the two parities.

\begin{proof}
The full residue recurrence and its conjugate give the one-factor
inverse-metric boundary identity
\[
 A\mathcal C_N+\mathcal C_NA^*-\mathcal C_N
   =\frac{z_{N+1}(a_{N+1}z_N)^*
                +(a_{N+1}z_N)z_{N+1}^*}{\kappa_{N+1}},
 \qquad
 \mathcal C_N=(G_N^{\rm src})^{-1},\quad
 z_j=U_\upsilon[q_j]_h.
\]
Indeed substitute \(\mathcal C_N=\sum_{j=0}^Nz_jz_j^*/\kappa_j\)
and \(Az_j=z_{j+1}+b_jz_j-a_jz_{j-1}\). The diagonal cancels
because \(b_j+\overline b_j=1\); each interior adjacent term
cancels because \(a_{j+1}/\kappa_{j+1}=1/\kappa_j\).
Only the displayed last pair remains. Transform this identity by
\(T\) on the left and \(T^*\) on the right. By (SP.7) and
(SP.23), its upper right block is \(K_e-XK_o\).

If \(N=2n\), the two transformed columns are
\(Tz_N=((-1)^nf_n,0)\) and
\(Tz_{N+1}=(0,(-1)^ne_n)\). Their signs cancel, and
\(a_{2n+1}/\kappa_{2n+1}=1/p_n\), giving the first row.
If \(N=2n+1\), they are
\(Tz_N=(0,(-1)^ne_n)\) and
\(Tz_{N+1}=((-1)^{n+1}f_{n+1},0)\). Their product has
the minus sign, and
\(a_{2n+2}/\kappa_{2n+2}=1/u_n\), giving the second row.
No column has been assumed nonzero. This proves (SP.32) on
the whole length-\(D\) algebra at both degree parities.
\end{proof}

For the following scalar contractions use the same current metrics
\(G_e,G_o\). Equation (SP.32), multiplied on the left by \(G_e\)
and on the right by \(G_o\), gives the complete outer-product
formula for \(G_o-G_eX\). Its exact norm is
\[
 \boxed{
 \epsilon_{N-d}^{\,2}=
 \begin{cases}
  (f_n^*G_ef_n)(e_n^*G_oe_n)/p_n^2,&N=2n,\\[2pt]
  (f_{n+1}^*G_ef_{n+1})(e_n^*G_oe_n)/u_n^2,&N=2n+1.
 \end{cases}}
 \tag{SP.33}
\]
To prove this, the matrix in (SP.24) becomes the outer product
of \(G_e^{1/2}f\) and \(G_o^{1/2}e\), divided by the
displayed positive norm, with the displayed parity sign. For
vectors \(a,b\), the norm of \(ab^*\) is \(\|a\|\|b\|\):
Cauchy--Schwarz gives the upper bound, and the vector
\(b/\|b\|\) attains it when \(b\ne0\). If either vector
is zero the equality remains true. This proves (SP.33).
Thus the full relative weight has at most the two nonzero eigenvalues
\(\epsilon_{N-d},-\epsilon_{N-d}\), and all its other eigenvalues
are zero. The full packet and its nilpotents still enter each of
the four quadratic contractions in (SP.33).

Let \(\chi_N\) denote the trace cost of the original one-factor
derivative map at source degree \(N\), as in (QD.7), and put
\(\pi_N=\det G_{N+1}^{\rm src}/\det G_N^{\rm src}\). Define
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
 &\lambda_N&\chi_N\\\hline
 N=2n&\displaystyle\frac{e_n^*G_oe_n}{u_n}
 &\displaystyle\frac{u_n+e_n^*G_oe_n}{p_n^2}
                              (f_n^*G_ef_n)\\[7pt]
 N=2n+1&\displaystyle\frac{f_{n+1}^*G_ef_{n+1}}{p_{n+1}}
 &\displaystyle\frac{p_{n+1}+f_{n+1}^*G_ef_{n+1}}{u_n^2}
                              (e_n^*G_oe_n).
 \end{array}
 \tag{SP.34}
\]
These formulas compute the actual cost, rather than choosing one.
In the original one-column graph frame the next-layer Gram is
\(H_{\rm lay}=\kappa_{N+1}+z_{N+1}^*G_N^{\rm src}z_{N+1}\),
and the derivative row has coefficient
\(a_{N+1}z_N^*G_N^{\rm src}/\kappa_{N+1}\).
Taking its squared Hilbert--Schmidt norm against the original
metric gives
\(\chi_N=H_{\rm lay}(z_N^*G_N^{\rm src}z_N)/\kappa_N^2\).
Inserting the two transformed columns just used proves (SP.34),
including every original norm and sign. The determinant update
(SP.27) now gives, without any division by a zero column,
\[
 \pi_N=(1+\lambda_N)^{-1},\qquad
 \epsilon_{N-d}^2=(1-\pi_N)\chi_N.
 \tag{SP.35}
\]
Substitution from the two rows of (SP.34) verifies the second
identity against (SP.33). If the new column vanishes then
\(\lambda_N=0,\pi_N=1\) while the derivative cost may persist;
if the predecessor column vanishes then \(\chi_N=0\) while
the new column may still contract the metric. These are the two
original zero-frontier cases, carried through the full half-line
kernel formulas.
