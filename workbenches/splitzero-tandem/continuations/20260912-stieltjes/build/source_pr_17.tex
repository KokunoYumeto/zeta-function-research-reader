\subsubsection{2. An actual one-parameter pushforward and its
resolution}\label{pr_17:an-actual-one-parameter-pushforward-and-its-resolution}

Define

\[\iota_k:\mathbb C[S]\to E_h^{\otimes k},\qquad S\mapsto[s_1+\cdots+s_k].\]

Restriction of modules through this map is the exact quasi-coherent pushforward along the finite
scheme morphism. Its adjunction maps are
\texttt{f\ -\textgreater{}\ (l\ -\textgreater{}\ f(1\ tensor\ l))} and
\texttt{a\ tensor\ l\ -\textgreater{}\ a\ f(l)}. Applying G gives \nolinkurl{p\ G(iota)=iota\ p}, with
e sent to e and tau to tau; the morphism is over the same absolute pointed base. No finite field is
substituted.

For the actual finite image E, with sum operator A, there is the free resolution

\[0\to\mathbb C[S]\otimes E\xrightarrow{SI-A}\mathbb C[S]\otimes E\xrightarrow{\mathrm{ev}_A}E_A\to0.\]

Its augmentation sends \nolinkurl{P\ tensor\ v} to \nolinkurl{P(A)v}. Injectivity follows from the highest
polynomial coefficient. Exactness follows from

\[S^jv-A^jv=(SI-A)\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}S^{j-1-i}A^iv.\]

Dualizing with \texttt{C{[}S{]}\ dS} gives \texttt{SI-A\^{}vee} in degrees 0,1, where
\texttt{A\^{}vee(lambda)=lambda\ compose\ A} is the algebraic transpose, not a Hermitian adjoint.
Thus

\[\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathbb C[S]}(E_A,\mathbb C[S]dS)\cong E^\vee,\]

with \texttt{{[}sum\ S\^{}j\ lambda\_j\ dS{]}\ -\textgreater{}\ sum\ (A\^{}vee)\^{}j\ lambda\_j} and
inverse the constant class. With \texttt{C{[}b{]}\^{}i=C\^{}(i+b)}, the exact shifted statement is

\[R\operatorname{Hom}(E_A[-k],\mathbb C[S]dS[1])\simeq E^\vee[k].\]

The right adjoint is the specified module functor \texttt{RHom\_(C{[}S{]})(E\_h\^{}tensor\ k,-)}.
This is not an assertion about pushforward on all sheaves of modules. See Stacks Tags 0AVV and 0AWZ.

The same duality has the concrete pairing

\[\langle v,[P(S)dS]\rangle=\sum_{\lambda\in\operatorname{Spec}A}\operatorname{Res}_{S=\lambda}P(S)((SI-A)^{-1}v)dS.\]

Expansion at infinity gives \texttt{sum\ lambda\_j(A\^{}j\ v)}, proving the formula and perfectness.
On a length-l block,

\[(SI-A)^{-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{l-1}\frac{N^j}{(S-\lambda)^{j+1}}.\]

All poles are retained before taking the trace. The further observation is

\[\operatorname{Tr}(\psi(A))=\sum_\lambda\operatorname{Res}_{S=\lambda}\psi(S)\frac{\chi_A'(S)}{\chi_A(S)}dS.\]

Here \nolinkurl{chi_A=det(SI-A)} is the finite characteristic polynomial, not a replacement for
\nolinkurl{g=2xi}. With \texttt{psi(S)=a\^{}S} this is the finite trace of the same diagonal scaling
correspondence; its cochain sign is \texttt{(-1)\^{}k}.

\subsubsection{3. Exact symmetric-square relation
algebra}\label{pr_17:exact-symmetric-square-relation-algebra}

Retain the coordinate morphisms

\[S=s_1+s_2,\quad r=s_1-s_2,\quad\Delta=r^2,\qquad s_1=(S+r)/2,\quad s_2=(S-r)/2.\]

Swap fixes S and negates r. Even/odd polynomial expansion gives the filtered algebra isomorphism
\texttt{Phi:C{[}S,Delta{]}\ -\textgreater{}\ C{[}s\_1,s\_2{]}\^{}swap}, with \nolinkurl{deg(S)=1},
\nolinkurl{deg(Delta)=2}.

Define H0,H1 without localizing away from r=0:

\[h((S+r)/2)=H_0(S,r^2)+rH_1(S,r^2),\quad h((S-r)/2)=H_0(S,r^2)-rH_1(S,r^2).\]

Then

\[\boxed{(E_h\otimes E_h)^{S_2}\cong\mathbb C[S,\Delta]/(H_0,\Delta H_1).}\]

Proof: the original ideal in \texttt{C{[}S,r{]}} is \nolinkurl{(H0,r\ H1)}. Averaging an expression
\nolinkurl{H0\ a+r\ H1\ b} under \texttt{r\ -\textgreater{}\ -r} gives exactly
\nolinkurl{H0\ a_even+Delta\ H1\ b_odd}. Conversely both generators lie in the original ideal.
Reynolds averaging is exact over C, so the invariant quotient has precisely this ideal, including
all nilpotents.

The unscaled orbit sums of \texttt{s\_1\^{}i\ s\_2\^{}j},
\texttt{0\textless{}=i\textless{}=j\textless{}d}, give dimension \nolinkurl{d(d+1)/2}. The associated
graded Hilbert series for the original degree filtration is

\[\sum_{0\le i\le j<d}z^{i+j}=\frac{(1-z^d)(1-z^{d+1})}{(1-z)(1-z^2)}.\]

No grading of a nonhomogeneous h is asserted.

The original primitive map is explicit:

\[H_0A_0+\Delta H_1A_1=h(s_1)\frac{A_0+rA_1}{2}+h(s_2)\frac{A_0-rA_1}{2}.\]

Its two cochain primitives use \nolinkurl{phi_*\ tensor\ F_h} and \nolinkurl{F_h\ tensor\ phi_*},
respectively. The second primitive has a minus sign, matched by the second tensor-differential sign.
For a relation of degree M, ordered division followed by swap averaging gives
\texttt{deg\ A0\textless{}=M-d}, \texttt{deg\ A1\textless{}=M-d-1}.

The unit \nolinkurl{upsilon_h\ tensor\ upsilon_h} descends to the invariant algebra and remains in
its full jet map. All finite relation quotients and the transitions \texttt{M\ -\textgreater{}\ M+1}
commute with Phi. A class killed by the next relation maps to the receiving e-zero, never to
external absence.

\subsubsection{4. Push the actual norm to a matrix weight, not a scalar
observation}\label{pr_17:push-the-actual-norm-to-a-matrix-weight-not-a-scalar-observation}

Set

\[w_h(t)=\left|\frac{g(1/2+it)}{h(1/2+it)}\right|^2\frac1{2\pi}.\]

The exact real coordinate map is

\[u=t_1+t_2,\quad v=t_1-t_2,\quad dt_1dt_2=\tfrac12du\,dv.\]

It gives \nolinkurl{S=1+iu}, \texttt{Delta=-v\^{}2}. For

\[P(S,\Delta)=\sum_{a=0}^{\lfloor M/2\rfloor}\Delta^a f_a(S),\qquad\deg f_a\le M-2a,\]

define

\[\boxed{\mathsf W_{ac}(u)=\frac{(-1)^{a+c}}2\int_{\mathbb R}v^{2(a+c)}w_h((u+v)/2)w_h((u-v)/2)dv.}\]

Then the original arithmetic norm is exactly

\[\boxed{\|T_h^{(2)}\Phi P\|^2=\int_{\mathbb R}f(1+iu)^*\mathsf W(u)f(1+iu)du.}\]

Proof: substitute the coordinate map and expand the finite polynomial. Exponential moments justify
Fubini. Each finite matrix W(u) is strictly positive: the integrand is the squared modulus of a
nonzero polynomial in -v\^{}2 against a density positive almost everywhere. The entire nonzero g/h
has only discrete zeros on these real lines. The source exponential bound also gives convergence for
each fixed u.

Its scalar entry is \nolinkurl{(w_h*w_h)(u)}. Its other entries retain the relative-coordinate
moments; they are not replaced by that scalar entry.

For general k retain \nolinkurl{S=sum\ s_i}, \nolinkurl{x_i=s_i} for i\textless k and
\nolinkurl{s_k=S-sum\ x_i}. This is a polynomial isomorphism and the real Jacobian is one. Writing
\texttt{P=sum\ x\^{}alpha\ f\_alpha(S)} gives

\[\mathsf W_{\alpha\beta}(u)=\int_{\mathbb R^{k-1}}\overline{\prod_{i<k}(1/2+it_i)^{\alpha_i}}\prod_{i<k}(1/2+it_i)^{\beta_i}\prod_{i<k}w_h(t_i)w_h(u-\sum_{i<k}t_i)d^{k-1}t.\]

The outer coordinate remains \nolinkurl{S=k/2+iu}, and
\texttt{deg\ f\_alpha\textless{}=M-\textbar{}alpha\textbar{}}. This supplies an all-k isometry
retaining the entire filtered source. Constants in exponential-moment bounds depend on h,k and the
degrees; no uniform tensor bound is inferred.

\subsubsection{6. Full nilpotent fibres for every tensor
degree}\label{pr_17:full-nilpotent-fibres-for-every-tensor-degree}

For an ordered tuple of roots with orders m\_i, the local algebra is

\[L=\mathbb C[z_1,\ldots,z_k]/(z_i^{m_i}),\quad A=\rho_\Sigma I+N,\quad N=M_{\sum z_i},\quad D_0=\sum_i(m_i-1).\]

Let \texttt{c\_r={[}t\^{}r{]}\ product\_i(1+t+...+t\^{}(m\_i-1))}, \nolinkurl{c_-1=0}. Then the
complete module pushforward is

\[\boxed{\pi_*L\cong\bigoplus_{r=0}^{\lfloor D_0/2\rfloor}\left(\mathbb C[S]/(S-\rho_\Sigma)^{D_0-2r+1}\right)^{c_r-c_{r-1}}.}\]

Proof and maps: on \texttt{z\_i\^{}j} define \texttt{F\_i=j(m\_i-j)z\_i\^{}(j-1)} and
\texttt{H\_i=(2j-(m\_i-1))z\_i\^{}j}. Direct calculation gives \texttt{{[}N,F{]}=H},
\texttt{{[}H,N{]}=2N}, \texttt{{[}H,F{]}=-2F}. The explicit auxiliary diagonal Gram

\[\|z^\alpha\|_0^2=\prod_i\frac{\alpha_i!(m_i-1)!}{(m_i-1-\alpha_i)!}\]

makes \nolinkurl{F=N*}. A degree-r primitive p with Fp=0 has ladder length \nolinkurl{w+1},
\nolinkurl{w=D0-2r}, since

\[FN^jp=j(w-j+1)N^{j-1}p,\quad\|N^jp\|_0^2=\frac{j!w!}{(w-j)!}\|p\|_0^2.\]

Orthogonal invariant complements construct all ladders; degree counts give \nolinkurl{c_r-c_(r-1)}.
The actual module map is \texttt{{[}P(S){]}-\textgreater{}P(rho\_Sigma+N)p} on each chosen
primitive. This auxiliary Gram is related to the arithmetic one by the explicit operator
\texttt{G\_arithmetic\^{}(-1)G\_0}; it is used for the algebraic decomposition only. All
cross-ladder entries \nolinkurl{U*G_arithmetic\ U} remain in control estimates.

For an occupation list n\_rho with sum k, the symmetric component has graded dimensions given by

\[\prod_\rho\prod_{j=1}^{n_\rho}\frac{1-t^{m_\rho+j-1}}{1-t^j}.\]

These are polynomial orbit-count identities, not evaluations of a rational function at roots of
unity. Apply the same primitive construction to invariants, with \nolinkurl{D0=sum\ n_rho(m_rho-1)}
and \nolinkurl{rho_Sigma=sum\ n_rho\ rho}. Central occupation idempotents are retained even when
different occupations have the same spectral sum.

For an order-three local input taken twice, the symmetric fibre has ladders

\[1,X,X^2,X^3,X^4,\qquad p=z_1^2-z_1z_2+z_2^2,\quad X=z_1+z_2,\quad Xp=0.\]

It is \texttt{C{[}S{]}/(S-2rho)\^{}5\ direct-sum\ C{[}S{]}/(S-2rho)}, dimension six. This is a local
algebra calculation, not an assertion that zeta has a triple zero. The full arithmetic unit commutes
with A and carries the ladder coordinates to the original jets.

Each occupation trace is its full dimension times \texttt{a\^{}(sum\ n\_rho\ rho)}, with dimension
\nolinkurl{product\ binomial(n_rho+m_rho-1,n_rho)}. The cochain trace retains \texttt{(-1)\^{}k}.
Quotienting a top ladder vector, when explicitly requested as a module quotient, makes it the
supported zero; the full pushforward itself keeps it.
