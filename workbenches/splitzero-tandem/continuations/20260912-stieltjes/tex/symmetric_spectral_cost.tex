\section{The signed symmetric summand and its complete spectral cost}
\label{sec:symmetric-spectral-cost}

This section proves the cochain, coordinate, and spectral comparisons
for the symmetric summand in the arithmetic frontier delivery.  The
original packet is \(E=\mathbb C[t]/h\), of dimension \(d\ge1\),
and its operator is \(A=M_t\).  The tensor degree is \(k\ge1\).
All orders in \(h=\prod_\rho(t-\rho)^{m_\rho}\) remain present.
The original joint representative, metric, and layer maps of
(CT.12)--(CT.16) are denoted by \(R_M,G_M,Y_M,C_M\), with
\(M\ge k(d-1)\).  The source's symmetric construction is used
through the maps proved below; its orbit sums keep their literal
factorials.

\subsection{The chain projector and its completed and finite targets}

The completion in this subsection is specifically the completed
projective tensor product \(\widehat\otimes_\pi\) of the
strong-Schwartz locally convex spaces.  The global-retraction source,
equations (16), (19), and (24), constructs continuous maps
\[
 \Lambda:\mathscr B\longrightarrow V,\quad
 \Lambda\Theta=1,\quad
 s:Q\longrightarrow\mathscr B,\quad
 s[F]=(1-\Theta\Lambda)F,\quad qs=1.                 \tag{SS.1}
\]
Its specified cutoffs and moment projection occur in
\(\Lambda=P_V(1-A_\alpha B_\beta)^{-1}Y_{\alpha,\beta}\).
The convergence and continuity of this formula are proved in that
source; no Hilbert-space replacement of these topologies is used here.
Put \(C=[V\xrightarrow\Theta\mathscr B]\) in degrees
\(0,1\), \(P=sq\) as a cochain map with degree-zero part zero,
and \(H^1=\Lambda\).  Then in the two degrees respectively,
\(\Lambda\Theta=1\) and \(\Theta\Lambda=1-sq\) prove
\[
 dH+Hd=1-P.                                           \tag{SS.2}
\]

For homogeneous inputs define the chain permutation by
\[
 T_\pi(x_1\otimes\cdots\otimes x_k)
 =(-1)^{\sum_{i<j,\,\pi(i)>\pi(j)}|x_i||x_j|}
 x_{\pi^{-1}(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\pi^{-1}(k)}.
                                                               \tag{SS.3}
\]
Here the degrees in the exponent belong to the original inputs.
An adjacent interchange contributes \((-1)^{ab}\) for degrees
\(a,b\).  Interchanging twice contributes \((-1)^{2ab}=1\);
disjoint interchanges commute; and the two three-interchange braid
words both contribute \((-1)^{ab+ac+bc}\).  Thus (SS.3)
is a representation of \(S_k\).  On two adjacent factors,
\(d(x\otimes y)=dx\otimes y+(-1)^a x\otimes dy\).
After interchange the coefficients of \(dy\otimes x\) and
\(y\otimes dx\) are respectively \((-1)^{ab}\) and
\((-1)^{ab+b}\), on either side of the cochain equation.
This proves \(dT_\pi=T_\pi d\), first for generators and then
for every permutation.  Each map is continuous and therefore extends
to \(C^{\widehat\otimes_\pi k}\).

The signed average and its complementary map are
\[
 \mathsf S_k=\frac1{k!}\sum_\pi\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)T_\pi,
 \qquad 1-\mathsf S_k.                                \tag{SS.4}
\]
For each \(\gamma\), exactly \(k!\) pairs \((\pi,\sigma)\)
satisfy \(\pi\sigma=\gamma\), and their signed coefficient
is \(\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma)/(k!)^2\).  Hence
\(\mathsf S_k^2=\mathsf S_k\).  In top degree every factor
has degree one, so \(T_\pi=\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)P_\pi\)
and \(\mathsf S_k\) is the ordinary symmetric projection there.
The unsigned chain average has top part
\(k!^{-1}\sum_\pi\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)P_\pi\);
this exact equation specifies its alternating target.

To verify the completed cohomology directly, extend the finite sum
\[
 H_k=\sum_{j=1}^k
 P^{\widehat\otimes_\pi(j-1)}\widehat\otimes_\pi H
 \widehat\otimes_\pi1^{\widehat\otimes_\pi(k-j)}.       \tag{SS.5}
\]
The degree \(-1\) factor contributes the Koszul sign
\((-1)^{\sum_{i<j}|x_i|}\).  Expanding \(dH_k+H_kd\),
the terms in which the differential and homotopy occupy different
slots cancel in pairs.  The term in slot \(j\) is
\(P^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes(1-P)\otimes1^{\otimes(k-j)}\)
by (SS.2).  Summing telescopes to \(1-P^{\otimes k}\).
Continuity extends this identity from the dense algebraic tensor
product.  Replace \(H_k\) by the explicit equivariant homotopy
\[
 \overline H_k=\frac1{k!}\sum_\pi T_\pi H_kT_\pi^{-1}.
                                                               \tag{SS.6}
\]
The operator \(P^{\otimes k}\) commutes with every permutation,
so (SS.6) has the same homotopy identity and commutes with
\(\mathsf S_k\).  The maps \(q^{\widehat\otimes_\pi k}\)
and \(s^{\widehat\otimes_\pi k}\), restricted to the images
of (SS.4), now give continuous inverse cohomology maps.  Thus
\[
 H^r(\operatorname{im}\mathsf S_k)=
 \begin{cases}(Q^{\widehat\otimes_\pi k})^{S_k},&r=k,\\
 0,&r\ne k.\end{cases}                               \tag{SS.7}
\]
The complementary cohomology is the image of
\(1-\frac1{k!}\sum_\pi P_\pi\) on the same completed
\(Q\)-tensor.  The full cochain isomorphism and its inverse are
\(x\mapsto(\mathsf S_kx,(1-\mathsf S_k)x)\) and
\((u,v)\mapsto u+v\).  Equations (SS.5)--(SS.7) prove the
completion statement using the supplied continuous contraction;
they require no exactness assertion for arbitrary completed tensor
products.

Here is the finite-rank cochain map that supports the finite traces.
The full packet jet \(J_h:\mathscr B\to E\) annihilates
\(\Theta V\), and descends to a continuous
\(j_h:Q\to E\).  The original packet inclusion
\(\sigma_h:E\to Q\) satisfies \(j_h\sigma_h=1\), as
\(J_hR_M=1\) and \(qR_M=\sigma_h\) in one variable.
Set \(\iota_h=s\sigma_h:E\to\mathscr B\) and define
\[
 \Pi_h^0=0,\qquad \Pi_h^1=\iota_hJ_h,
 \qquad J_h\iota_h=1.                                \tag{SS.8}
\]
This is a continuous finite-rank cochain projector:
\(\Pi_h^1\Theta=0\) and \((\Pi_h^1)^2=\Pi_h^1\).
It commutes with permutations after taking identical tensor powers.
Consequently \(\Pi_h^{\otimes k}\mathsf S_k\) and
\(\Pi_h^{\otimes k}(1-\mathsf S_k)\) are the finite
cochain projectors with ranks \(\binom{k+d-1}{d-1}\) and
\(d^k-\binom{k+d-1}{d-1}\).  The projector
\(\mathsf S_k\) on the full infinite complex itself has not
been assigned either finite rank.  The finite inclusion and retraction
on cohomology are precisely \(\sigma_h^{\otimes k}\) and
\(j_h^{\otimes k}\), restricted to the respective permutation
summands.  Their composite on the packet is the identity.

For \(U(a)=e^{(\log a)A}\), \(a>0\), the arithmetic action
obeys \(J_hU_{\mathscr B}(a)=U(a)J_h\).  Thus
\(J_hU_{\mathscr B}(a)\iota_h=U(a)\).  If
\(L:F\to X\), \(R:X\to F\), and \(F\) is finite
dimensional, the trace of the finite-rank map \(LR\) is
\(\operatorname{Tr}_F(RL)\): expanding \(LRx\) in a
basis of \(F\) expresses both as the sum of the same coefficient
functionals evaluated on the same image vectors.  Apply this identity
to (SS.8) and its tensor powers.  Expanding a permutation trace in a
tensor basis contracts one matrix index around each cycle, proving
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(U(a)^{\otimes k}|\operatorname{Sym}^k E)
 =\frac1{k!}\sum_{\pi\in S_k}
       \prod_{c\in\operatorname{Cycles}(\pi)}
                    \operatorname{Tr}(U(a)^{|c|}).       \tag{SS.9}
\]
This proof uses all entries of \(U(a)\) and applies to its
nonsemisimple action.  The cochain supertrace carries \((-1)^k\).
The complementary finite supertrace is exactly \((-1)^k\)
times \((\operatorname{Tr}U(a))^k\) minus the trace in
(SS.9).  No infinite trace is assigned by these equations.

At a fixed permutation-invariant support fibre, every displayed
linear map lifts by \((\lambda,x)\mapsto(\lambda,Tx)\).
For arbitrary masks the support of (SS.4) is the specified orbit join
\(c(\lambda)=\bigvee_\pi\pi\lambda\), and the amplitude
maps include the original transports into that fibre.  The two
projection maps then decompose the transported vector in the fibre
over \(c(\lambda)\); their sum is that transport.  Their sum
has not been declared the identity on a different original fibre.
The upstream kernel is exactly the kernel of its specified transport
\(V_\lambda\to V_{c(\lambda)}\).  At invariant masks the
transport is the identity and the decomposition above applies.
Every vanishing projected amplitude remains the supported zero in
its target fibre; external absence is sent to external absence.

\subsection{Orbit coordinates, their Gram, and all local nilpotents}

Let \(e_1,\ldots,e_d\) denote the original power basis with
indices shifted by one.  For \(n\in\mathbb N^d\),
\(|n|=k\), let \(b_n\) be the sum of the distinct basis
tensors having occupancy \(n\).  Define
\[
 N=\binom{k+d-1}{d-1},\quad
 I:\mathbb C^N\longrightarrow E^{\otimes k},\quad
 I e_n=b_n,\quad D_o=I^*I
      =\operatorname{diag}_{|n|=k}\frac{k!}{\prod_i n_i!},
 \quad L=D_o^{-1}I^*.                                  \tag{SS.10}
\]
Distinct tensor basis vectors are orthonormal for this coordinate
calculation; hence \(D_o\) records exactly their orbit sizes.
It is separate from the arithmetic Gram.  One has \(LI=1\)
and \(IL=P_{\rm sym}=k!^{-1}\sum P_\pi\), since either
map replaces every coefficient on an orbit by its average.
Here \(\operatorname{Sym}^kE\) denotes this invariant image.
Its comparison with the symmetric tensor quotient is the quotient
map restricted to that image: it sends
\(b_n\) to \((k!/\prod_i n_i!)e_1^{n_1}\cdots e_d^{n_d}\).
The inverse sends this quotient monomial to
\((\prod_i n_i!/k!)b_n\).  These formulas prove the
isomorphism and retain the exact diagonal factors between the two
presentations.

Permutation invariance of the arithmetic constraints and measure
makes the unique constrained minimum satisfy
\(R_MP_\pi=P_\pi R_M\); applying the inverse permutation
to a feasible minimizer gives a feasible vector of the same norm,
and uniqueness identifies it.  Thus \(G_M\) and \(G_M^{-1}\)
commute with \(P_{\rm sym}\).  Retain the matrices
\[
 G_M^s=I^*G_MI,\quad W_M^s=I^*W_MI,\quad
 A^s=LA^{(k)}I,\quad A^{(k)}I=IA^s,
 \qquad (G_M^s)^{-1}=L G_M^{-1}L^*.                    \tag{SS.11}
\]
The last equality includes both \(D_o^{-1}\) factors in \(L\).
Indeed its product with \(I^*G_MI\) is
\[
 L G_M^{-1}(L^*I^*)G_MI
 =L G_M^{-1}P_{\rm sym}G_MI=LI=1.
\]
This proves the inverse formula without exchanging
arbitrary compression and inversion.

In these unscaled coordinates, if \(Ae_j=\sum_i A_{ij}e_i\),
the full generator is
\[
 A^{(k)} b_n=\Bigl(\sum_i n_i A_{ii}\Bigr)b_n
  +\sum_{\substack{i\ne j\\n_j>0}}
       (n_i+1)A_{ij}\,b_{n+e_i-e_j}.                  \tag{SS.12}
\]
For a specified target tensor of occupancy \(n+e_i-e_j\),
each of its \(n_i+1\) entries equal to \(e_i\) can have
come from an \(e_j\) in a source tensor, and these are exactly
the contributing preimages.  The diagonal term counts its \(n_i\)
unchanged positions.  This proves every coefficient in (SS.12).
Using the original quotient's complete CRT isomorphism, set
\(E_\rho=\mathbb C[x_\rho]/x_\rho^{m_\rho}\),
\(t=\rho+x_\rho\).  The explicit CRT map is reduction modulo
each \((t-\rho)^{m_\rho}\); its inverse is the unique degree
\(<d\) polynomial with the prescribed full jets.  Applying its
tensor power and restriction through \(I,L\) is the exact change
from the original orbit coordinates to the following direct sum:
\[
 \operatorname{Sym}^k E\simeq
 \bigoplus_{\substack{\ell_\rho\ge0\\\sum_\rho\ell_\rho=k}}
       \bigotimes_\rho\operatorname{Sym}^{\ell_\rho}E_\rho,
 \qquad
 w_\ell=\prod_\rho\binom{\ell_\rho+m_\rho-1}{m_\rho-1}.
                                                               \tag{SS.13}
\]
The isomorphism groups the unscaled tensor orbit sums by the number
of factors in each CRT summand; these grouped basis vectors exhaust
the full basis, which proves the direct sum and its dimensions.

On the summand \(\ell\), the generator is
\(\lambda_\ell+N_\ell\), where
\(\lambda_\ell=\sum_\rho\ell_\rho\rho\) and
\(N_\ell\) is the sum of the multiplications by the
\(x_\rho\) in their original slots.  Put
\(h_\ell=\sum_\rho\ell_\rho(m_\rho-1)\).
Every application raises the total local exponent by one, so
\(N_\ell^{h_\ell+1}=0\).  On the tensor of local constants,
\[
 N_\ell^{h_\ell}(\bigotimes_\rho1^{\otimes\ell_\rho})
 =\frac{h_\ell!}
        {\prod_\rho((m_\rho-1)!)^{\ell_\rho}}
       \bigotimes_\rho
               (x_\rho^{m_\rho-1})^{\otimes\ell_\rho}\ne0.
                                                               \tag{SS.14}
\]
The coefficient counts the ordered choices of increments in each
slot, each of which must be incremented exactly \(m_\rho-1\)
times.  Therefore the nilpotent index is exactly \(h_\ell+1\).
Even when different \(\ell\) have the same
\(\lambda_\ell\), their direct summands in (SS.13) are
retained separately.  Traces may kill individual nilpotent diagonal
contributions, while the complete operators (SS.12)--(SS.14) retain
them.

\subsection{Complete symmetric spectral sums and their exact departure}

List \(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_d\) with every algebraic
multiplicity and put
\[
 \delta_i=\operatorname{Re}\rho_i-\tfrac12,\quad
 \sigma_1=\sum_i\delta_i,\quad \sigma_2=\sum_i\delta_i^2,
 \quad a_k=\binom{k+d-1}{d},\quad
 b_k=\binom{k+d-1}{d+1}.                               \tag{SS.15}
\]
Binomial coefficients with lower index exceeding the upper index
are zero.  Thus these formulas include \(k=1\) and \(d=1\).
Triangularize \(A\).  The induced ordered orbit basis is also
triangular under (SS.12), with diagonal
\(\lambda_n=\sum_i n_i\rho_i\), once for each
\(n\in\mathbb N^d\), \(|n|=k\).  For example, order
the monomials compatibly with the triangular basis's index weight;
each off-diagonal substitution in (SS.12) decreases that weight.
Consequently
\[
 \det(z-A^s)=\prod_{|n|=k}(z-\lambda_n),\quad
 \sum_{k\ge0}q^k\operatorname{Tr}(U(a)^{\otimes k}|\operatorname{Sym}^kE)
      =\prod_{i=1}^d(1-q a^{\rho_i})^{-1}.              \tag{SS.16}
\]
The second identity is an identity of formal power series: expand
each geometric series and select its occupancy.  On grouping repeated
roots it is \(\prod_\rho(1-q a^\rho)^{-m_\rho}\), and
the coefficient for grouped occupancy \(\ell\) is precisely
\(w_\ell\) from (SS.13).  This gives the exact multiplicity
dictionary between the cycle trace (SS.9) and the full local jets.

Define the symmetric absolute spectral cost and metric departure by
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal D_k^s&=\sum_{|n|=k}\left|\sum_i n_i\delta_i\right|
       =\sum_{|\ell|=k}w_\ell
                      \left|\sum_\rho\ell_\rho
                              (\operatorname{Re}\rho-\tfrac12)\right|,\\
 \mathfrak d_M^s&=
 \operatorname{Tr}((G_M^s)^{-1}(A^s)^*G_M^s A^s)
                         -\sum_{|n|=k}|\lambda_n|^2,\\
 S_M^s&=(G_M^s)^{-1/2}W_M^s(G_M^s)^{-1/2},\qquad
 \epsilon_M^s=\|S_M^s\|_{\rm op}.
 \end{split}                                                   \tag{SS.17}
\]
The square root is used only as the displayed isometry for computing
the original Hermitian pencil; it changes none of the orbit vectors,
source functions, arithmetic constants, or entries of \(G_M^s\).

\begin{theorem}[Exact occupancy moments and symmetric departure]
The full unweighted occupancies satisfy
\[
 \begin{split}
 \sum_{|n|=k}\sum_i n_i\delta_i&=a_k\sigma_1,\\
 \sum_{|n|=k}\left(\sum_i n_i\delta_i\right)^2
     &=(a_k+b_k)\sigma_2+b_k\sigma_1^2.
 \end{split}                                                  \tag{SS.18}
\]
In the original arithmetic orbit Gram,
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \mathfrak d_M^s\ge0,\qquad
 \operatorname{Tr}S_M^s=2a_k\sigma_1,\\
 \operatorname{Tr}((S_M^s)^2)
     =4\{(a_k+b_k)\sigma_2+b_k\sigma_1^2\}
                                           +2\mathfrak d_M^s.
 \end{gathered}                                                \tag{SS.19}
\]
In particular, if \(\bar\delta=\sigma_1/d\), the exact
mean-square spread of the occupancy offsets is
\[
 \sum_{|n|=k}\left(\sum_i n_i\delta_i-k\bar\delta\right)^2
 =\frac{Nk(k+d)}{d(d+1)}
                        \left(\sigma_2-\frac{\sigma_1^2}{d}\right).
                                                               \tag{SS.20}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The product \(\prod_i(1-q_i)^{-1}\) enumerates every occupancy.
Apply \(q_i\partial_{q_i}\) and then set all \(q_i=q\).
Its coefficient of \(q^k\) gives
\(\sum n_i=[q^k]q(1-q)^{-d-1}=a_k\).
For two distinct indices, applying the two derivatives gives
\(\sum n_i n_j=[q^k]q^2(1-q)^{-d-2}=b_k\).
Applying \(q_i^2\partial_{q_i}^2\) gives
\(\sum n_i(n_i-1)=2b_k\).  These coefficient identities follow
by multiplying geometric series, or by induction from
\((1-q)^{-r}=\sum_{m\ge0}\binom{m+r-1}{r-1}q^m\).
Therefore \(\sum n_i^2=a_k+2b_k\).  Expanding the square,
the cross terms have coefficient \(b_k\); collecting the diagonal
terms gives (SS.18).  The identities
\(a_k=Nk/d\) and \(a_k+b_k=Nk(k+d)/(d(d+1))\)
follow directly from the factorials, including the stated edge cases.
Expanding the centered square proves (SS.20).

Let \(T=(G_M^s)^{1/2}A^s(G_M^s)^{-1/2}\).  A unitary
upper triangularization exists by induction: choose a unit
eigenvector as the first basis vector, complete it to an orthonormal
basis, and repeat on the remaining diagonal block.  If its strictly
upper triangular part is \(N_T\), its Hilbert--Schmidt squared
norm is exactly \(\mathfrak d_M^s\); the diagonal entries are
the complete \(\lambda_n\).  This proves nonnegativity while
retaining every nonzero off-diagonal entry.  Since
\(S_M^s=T+T^*-kI\), its diagonal is
\(2\sum_i n_i\delta_i\); each strictly upper triangular
entry and its adjoint contribute twice their squared absolute value
to the squared trace.  Thus
\(\operatorname{Tr}((S_M^s)^2)=4\sum_n(n\cdot\delta)^2
+2\mathfrak d_M^s\).  Equation (SS.18) proves (SS.19).
\end{proof}

For comparison with (CT.20)--(CT.21), the ordered tensor has an
occupancy \(n\) exactly \(k!/\prod_i n_i!\) times.  The
permutation complement therefore has that multiplicity minus one.
Its characteristic polynomial, trace, and absolute spectral cost are
obtained with these nonnegative integer multiplicities.  In particular
\[
 \mathcal D_k=\mathcal D_k^s+
       \sum_{|n|=k}\left(\frac{k!}{\prod_i n_i!}-1\right)
                                  |n\cdot\delta|.       \tag{SS.21}
\]
This equality retains every contribution omitted by the symmetric
inclusion.  Moreover the permutation projection is orthogonal also
for \(G_M\), because it commutes with that Gram.  Its image and
kernel are invariant under \(A^{(k)}\).  Thus the full metric
operator is the orthogonal direct sum of its symmetric and
complementary restrictions; the squared Hilbert--Schmidt norms and
the eigenvalue-square sums add separately.  The full departure in
(CT.20) is exactly \(\mathfrak d_M^s\) plus the corresponding
complementary departure, each nonnegative.  This proves the full
operator comparison as well as the scalar identity (SS.21).

\subsection{The cost in the original symmetric relation layer}

Restrict the original source maps without changing their Hilbert
targets:
\[
 Y_M^s=Y_MI,\quad C_M^s=C_MI,\quad
 \Delta_M^s=G_M^s-G_{M+1}^s=(Y_M^s)^*Y_M^s,
 \quad W_M^s=-((Y_M^s)^*C_M^s+(C_M^s)^*Y_M^s).          \tag{SS.22}
\]
Their images lie in the invariant original relation layer, whose
dimension is
\(r_{\rm lay}=p_{\le k}(M+1)\).  Indeed an unscaled symmetric
polynomial basis in exact degree \(M+1\) is indexed by its
partitions into at most \(k\) parts.  The invariant full-jet map
is onto at every \(M\ge k(d-1)\): average a complete tensor
remainder to lift each invariant jet.  The difference of the two
kernel dimensions is therefore the number of the new degree
\(M+1\) basis vectors.  The injective polynomial realization and
orthogonal layer quotient transport this dimension to the actual
source layer, proving the assertion with its original relation ideal.
Put
\[
 r_M^s=\operatorname{rank}Y_M^s,\quad
 \tau_M^s=\operatorname{Tr}((G_M^s)^{-1}\Delta_M^s),\quad
 \chi_M^s=\operatorname{Tr}((G_M^s)^{-1}(C_M^s)^*C_M^s).
                                                               \tag{SS.23}
\]
Then the precise relative source costs are
\[
 \begin{split}
 n_\pm(W_M^s)&\le
       \min\{r_M^s,\operatorname{rank}C_M^s,r_{\rm lay}\},\\
 \mathcal D_k^s&\le\tfrac12\|S_M^s\|_1
              \le\sqrt{\tau_M^s\chi_M^s},\qquad
 \mathcal D_k^s\le r_M^s\epsilon_M^s.
 \end{split}                                                  \tag{SS.24}
\]
To prove the inertia bound, the Hermitian form in (SS.22) vanishes
on \(\ker Y_M^s\).  Any strictly positive or strictly negative
subspace of dimension greater than \(\operatorname{rank}Y_M^s\)
intersects that kernel nontrivially, a contradiction.  Apply the same
argument to \(C_M^s\).  In the triangular basis used in (SS.19),
the absolute diagonal sum of \(S_M^s\) is
\(2\mathcal D_k^s\).  Its spectral expansion gives
\(|S_{ii}|\le\sum_j|\lambda_j||U_{ij}|^2\); summing over
\(i\) bounds this sum by the trace norm.  Finally, for any two
finite-domain Hilbert-space maps \(B,C\), their singular-vector
decomposition and entrywise Cauchy--Schwarz prove
\(\|B^*C\|_1\le\|B\|_{\rm HS}\|C\|_{\rm HS}\).
Apply it to \(B=Y_M^s(G_M^s)^{-1/2}\) and
\(C=C_M^s(G_M^s)^{-1/2}\) in (SS.22).  The squared norms
are \(\tau_M^s,\chi_M^s\).  The inertia bound gives at most
\(2r_M^s\) nonzero eigenvalues, proving the last assertion.

If the packet is stable, with full orders, under
\(\rho\mapsto1-\overline\rho\), its original antiunitary
reflection commutes with permutations.  Restricting (CT.16a) to
the invariant space pairs every nonzero eigenvalue of the Hermitian
pencil with its negative.  Thus \(\sigma_1=0\), and with
\(p_M^s=n_+(W_M^s)=n_-(W_M^s)\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 p_M^s&\le\min\{r_M^s,\operatorname{rank}C_M^s,
                   r_{\rm lay},\lfloor N/2\rfloor\},\\
 \mathcal D_k^s&\le p_M^s\epsilon_M^s,\\
 p_M^s(\epsilon_M^s)^2
       &\ge\frac{2Nk(k+d)}{d(d+1)}\sigma_2
                                           +\mathfrak d_M^s.
 \end{split}                                                   \tag{SS.25}
\]
There are \(2p_M^s\) nonzero eigenvalues, each of absolute value
at most \(\epsilon_M^s\).  Bounding their absolute and squared
sums and using (SS.19) proves both inequalities.  They involve no
division by \(p_M^s\); when it vanishes, (SS.19) forces every
displayed nonnegative term to vanish.

Let \(\delta_* =\max_i|\delta_i|\).  If it is positive,
\(|n\cdot\delta|\le k\delta_*\) yields the additional
explicit lower cost
\[
 \mathcal D_k^s\ge
  \frac{(a_k+b_k)\sigma_2+b_k\sigma_1^2}{k\delta_*}.
                                                               \tag{SS.26}
\]
Indeed square each occupancy offset, bound it by
\(k\delta_*|n\cdot\delta|\), and sum using (SS.18).
For a reflected packet this becomes
\(\mathcal D_k^s\ge N(k+d)\sigma_2/(d(d+1)\delta_*)\).
Each repeated eigenvector \(v^{\otimes k}\) is nonzero and
invariant, and its exact original Rayleigh quotient is
\(2k\delta_i\).  Hence \(\epsilon_M^s\ge2k\delta_*\).
Equations (SS.25)--(SS.26) additionally measure the cumulative cost
of every occupancy, with all multiplicities and original layer ranks.
If any \(m_\rho>1\), (SS.14) gives a nonzero nilpotent on
the all-\(\rho\) summand.  An operator with that Jordan block
cannot be unitarily diagonal, so the sum of squares defining
\(\mathfrak d_M^s\) is strictly positive for every positive
\(G_M^s\).  This retains a further finite-metric contribution
even when all \(\delta_i=0\).

Finally the density proved in (JD.8) passes to the invariant source
by the bounded permutation projection.  The canonical representatives
are the restrictions \(R_MI\); hence \(G_M^s\downarrow0\)
and \(\det G_M^s\to0\), while the full jets remain the identity
at every finite \(M\).  With
\(\pi_M^s=\det G_{M+1}^s/\det G_M^s\), the positive
eigenvalues \(t_j\) of
\((G_M^s)^{-1/2}\Delta_M^s(G_M^s)^{-1/2}\) lie in
\((0,1)\), their number is \(r_M^s\), and
\(\tau_M^s=\sum t_j\), \(\pi_M^s=\prod(1-t_j)\).
The arithmetic mean--geometric mean inequality therefore gives
\[
 (\mathcal D_k^s)^2\le\tau_M^s\chi_M^s
 \le r_M^s\bigl(1-(\pi_M^s)^{1/r_M^s}\bigr)\chi_M^s
                  \quad(r_M^s>0).                    \tag{SS.27}
\]
For \(r_M^s=0\), (SS.22) gives \(\Delta_M^s=W_M^s=0\),
without using an exponent \(1/r_M^s\).  Since
\(-\log(1-t)-t=\int_0^t u/(1-u)\,du\), the same finite
source sequence also obeys, for integers \(k(d-1)\le a<b\) and
\(\mathcal D_k^s>0\),
\[
 (\mathcal D_k^s)^2\sum_{M=a}^{b-1}(\chi_M^s)^{-1}
 \le\sum_{M=a}^{b-1}\tau_M^s
 \le\log\frac{\det G_a^s}{\det G_b^s}.                 \tag{SS.28}
\]
Positivity of every denominator follows from (SS.24).
Telescoping proves the last inequality.  Moreover
\(\sum_{M=a}^\infty\tau_M^s=\infty\): otherwise the tail
has \(t_j\le\tau_M^s\le1/2\), and
\(-\log(1-t_j)\le2t_j\) would keep the telescoped logarithm
bounded, contrary to \(\det G_M^s\to0\).  These identities
transfer the entire determinant and relative-energy calculation to
the source's smaller invariant family through its exact inclusion;
they supply no assumed arithmetic upper estimate.
