## 1. Original coordinates and polynomial convention

Let the original monic polynomial have even degree (2D), (D\geq1), and retain exactly the convention

\[
h(s)=(-1)^D H\bigl(-(s-\tfrac12)^2\bigr),\qquad \deg H=D.
\]

Thus no change to the leading coefficient of (h) is made. Put

\[
S=s_1+s_2,\quad T=S-1,\quad r=s_1-s_2,\quad
\Delta=r^2,\quad x=-T^2.
\]

The polynomial expansion is

\[
h\bigl((S+r)/2\bigr)
=A_0(-T^2,r^2)+Tr A_1(-T^2,r^2).
\]

Both (A_0,A_1\in\mathbb C[x,\Delta]) exist uniquely because the even powers of (T+r) separate into terms even in both (T,r), and terms odd in both. The other evaluation changes the sign of the second summand. In particular,

\[
A_0(0,\Delta)=(-1)^D H(-\Delta/4).
\]

Its leading term is (4^{-D}\Delta^D), so it is nonzero even when (H(0)=0). The relevant fact below is (x\nmid A_0\); a nonzero constant term is unnecessary.

The original invariant-quotient proof from PR17 gives the symmetric square as

\[
B=(\mathbb C[s_1,s_2]/(h(s_1),h(s_2)))^{S_2}
\cong
\mathbb C[T,\Delta]/(A_0(-T^2,\Delta),T\Delta A_1(-T^2,\Delta)).
\]

Indeed the original ordered ideal in \(\mathbb C[T,r]\) is generated by (A_0) and (Tr A_1). Taking its (r\mapsto-r) invariants gives (A_0\) and (T\Delta A_1), without dividing by (r). This retains the entire diagonal fibre (r=0).

## 2. Exact parity ideals

Write (R=\mathbb C[x,\Delta]). The direct vector-space decomposition

\[
\mathbb C[T,\Delta]=R\oplus TR,\qquad T^2=-x,
\]

is exact. Decomposing coefficients of the two defining relations into their (T)-even and (T)-odd parts gives respectively

\[
I_e=(A_0,x\Delta A_1),\qquad I_o=(A_0,\Delta A_1).
\]

Consequently

\[
B_e=R/I_e,\qquad B_o=R/I_o,\qquad
B_e\oplus B_o\longrightarrow B,\quad ([f],[g])\longmapsto[f+Tg]
\]

is a vector-space isomorphism onto the two eigenspaces of (T\mapsto-T). Here (B_o) is the coefficient module of the odd part; its actual realization is (TB_o). The multiplication on the pair of coefficient modules is

\[
(e,o)(e',o')=
\bigl(ee'-\alpha(oo'),\ \beta(e)o'+o\beta(e')\bigr),
\]

with the maps proved next. This records the exact ring reconstruction, not only a vector-space dimension count.

## 3. The maps, both exact sequences, and all zero fibres

Define

\[
\alpha:B_o\to B_e,\quad[f]\mapsto[xf],\qquad
\beta:B_e\to B_o,\quad[f]\mapsto[f].
\]

These are (R)-linear maps. The first is well defined because (xI_o\subseteq I_e); the second is the surjection induced by (I_e\subseteq I_o).

To prove injectivity of \(\alpha\), suppose

\[
xf=A_0a+x\Delta A_1b.
\]

Then (x\mid A_0a). Since (R/(x)=\mathbb C[\Delta]) is an integral domain and (A_0(0,\Delta)\ne0), reduction modulo (x) implies (a(0,\Delta)=0). Thus (a=xc), and cancellation in the polynomial ring gives

\[
f=A_0c+\Delta A_1b\in I_o.
\]

This proof does not cancel an element in a quotient algebra and imposes no reducedness assumption. The cokernel is exactly

\[
\operatorname{coker}\alpha
=B_e/xB_e
=R/(A_0,x)
\cong\mathbb C[\Delta]/((-1)^DH(-\Delta/4)).
\]

Its dimension is (D). The scalar factor is retained in the displayed defining polynomial; it generates the same ideal as its nonzero scalar multiples.

For the dimensions of the two parity spaces, let (E=\mathbb C[z]/((-1)^D H(-z^2))), with centered coordinate (z=s-\tfrac12). Its exact original monomial basis is (1,z,\ldots,z^{2D-1}); its even and odd subspaces each have dimension (D). The swap invariants of (E\otimes E) have even part

\[
\operatorname{Sym}^2(E_{\rm even})\oplus
\operatorname{Sym}^2(E_{\rm odd}),
\]

and odd part consisting of the unscaled sums (v\otimes w+w\otimes v), for (v\in E_{\rm even}), (w\in E_{\rm odd}). Therefore

\[
\dim B_e=D(D+1),\qquad \dim B_o=D^2.
\]

In particular \(\dim\ker\beta=D\). There is a complete canonical cyclic description, including every nilpotent at a zero root:

\[
\mathbb C[\Delta]/(A_0(0,\Delta))
\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\ker\beta,
\qquad[p]\longmapsto[p\Delta A_1].
\]

To prove it, the kernel ideal (I_o/I_e) is generated in (B_e) by (\Delta A_1), and (x\Delta A_1=0). Thus every kernel element is (p(\Delta)\Delta A_1). The proposed map is well defined: (A_0(0,\Delta)-A_0(x,\Delta)) is divisible by (x), so its product with (\Delta A_1) vanishes in (B_e), while (A_0(x,\Delta)) itself vanishes. The map is surjective between two spaces of dimension (D), hence is an isomorphism.

This is an isomorphism of \(R\)-modules. To record the inherited multiplication of the kernel ideal as well, write

\[
c(\Delta)=(\Delta A_1)(0,\Delta)
=\frac{(-1)^{D+1}\Delta}{2}H'(-\Delta/4).
\]

The derivative formula follows by differentiating the original expression for \(h((S+r)/2)\) with respect to \(T\) at \(T=0\): its derivative is \((-1)^{D+1}rH'(-\Delta/4)/2=rA_1(0,\Delta)\). Under the displayed module isomorphism, the product inherited from the ideal is exactly

\[
[p]\star[q]=[pqc(\Delta)].
\]

Indeed \(\Delta A_1\) is annihilated by \(x\), so multiplication of two represented kernel elements replaces one factor \(\Delta A_1(x,\Delta)\) by its value at \(x=0\). Thus the module isomorphism also gives an algebra isomorphism when the domain is equipped with this explicitly displayed product. It is generally not an algebra isomorphism for ordinary quotient multiplication. For the valid calibration \(D=1,H(t)=t\), the ideal is the one-dimensional square-zero ideal \((\Delta)\subset\mathbb C[\Delta]/(\Delta^2)\).

An independent direct colon-ideal proof confirms the kernel description without using the dimension count. The polynomials \(A_0\) and \(\Delta A_1\) are coprime. First \(\Delta\nmid A_0\), since \(A_0(x,0)=(-1)^D H(x/4)\ne0\). A nonconstant common divisor of \(A_0,A_1\) would, under the injective substitution \(x=-T^2,\Delta=r^2\), divide both \(h(1/2+(T+r)/2)\) and \(h(1/2+(T-r)/2)\). In the independent coordinates \(T+r,T-r\), these nonzero polynomials depend on separate coordinates and are coprime, including with repeated roots. Therefore

\[
(A_0,x\Delta A_1):(\Delta A_1)=(A_0,x),
\]

which proves the same canonical module isomorphism directly. This independent proof and the multiplication formula were checked by the delegated zero-fibre audit.

The resulting two exact sequences are

\[
0\to B_o\xrightarrow{\alpha}B_e
\longrightarrow\mathbb C[\Delta]/(A_0(0,\Delta))\to0,
\]

\[
0\to\mathbb C[\Delta]/(A_0(0,\Delta))
\xrightarrow{[p]\mapsto[p\Delta A_1]}B_e
\xrightarrow{\beta}B_o\to0.
\]

They should not be replaced by an assertion that \(\alpha\) is an isomorphism. Their two composites are

\[
\beta\alpha=M_x\text{ on }B_o,
\qquad \alpha\beta=M_x\text{ on }B_e.
\]

After localizing at (x), both maps become isomorphisms. Before localization the displayed (D)-dimensional fibre is retained. In particular,

\[
\ker(M_x:B_e\to B_e)=\ker\beta.
\]

Even if (H(0)=0), the proof still applies. Any later analytic exclusion of a central root must come from the specific packet or weight, rather than being imported into these algebraic statements. Likewise no argument here assumes (H) squarefree or that its roots are real.

## 4. The original sum operator and Hermitian weight form

Multiplication by (S=1+T) in the coefficient coordinates is exactly

\[
S=I+J,\qquad J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\alpha\\\beta&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The minus sign is forced by the original equation (T^2=-x). From injectivity of \(\alpha\) and surjectivity of \(\beta\),

\[
\ker J=\ker\beta\oplus0,
\quad\operatorname{coker}J=\operatorname{coker}\alpha,
\quad\dim\ker J=\dim\operatorname{coker}J=D,
\quad\operatorname{rank}J=2D^2.
\]

The geometric zero-sum dimension is determined here. These statements do not discard the higher Jordan structure of the zero-sum fibre.

For the exact parity metric (G=\operatorname{diag}(G_e,G_o)), the two-factor weight form has center (2\cdot1\):

\[
W=S^*G+GS-2G
=\begin{pmatrix}
0&\beta^*G_o-G_e\alpha\\
G_o\beta-\alpha^*G_e&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The upper right block maps the odd coefficient space to the antidual of the even coefficient space. The lower left block is its Hermitian adjoint. This formula would not hold with subtraction of only (G), since the original sum coordinate has real center (1).

## 5. Gaussian matrix weight and parity pushforward

This calibration uses exactly the declared measure (e^{-t^2/2}\,dt), with no probability normalization and no claim to be the arithmetic density. Retain

\[
u=t_1+t_2,\quad v=t_1-t_2,\quad
dt_1dt_2=\tfrac12\,du\,dv.
\]

For (k=a+c), the PR17 relative matrix entry is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf W_{ac}(u)
&=\frac{(-1)^k}{2}e^{-u^2/4}
\int_{\mathbb R}v^{2k}e^{-v^2/4}\,dv\\
&=\sqrt\pi e^{-u^2/4}
(-1)^k2^k(2k-1)!!.
\end{aligned}
\]

The moment identity follows by integration by parts:

\[
I_k=\int v^{2k}e^{-v^2/4}dv=2(2k-1)I_{k-1},
\qquad I_0=2\sqrt\pi.
\]

We use \((-1)!!=1\), so this includes (k=0). In the original unscaled basis (1,\Delta,\Delta^2), the constant matrix is exactly

\[
C=\begin{pmatrix}1&-2&12\\-2&12&-120\\12&-120&1680\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The signs record \(\Delta=-v^2\); replacing them would change the original coordinates. Integrating its scalar entry in (u) gives (2\pi), the original two-variable mass.

For an even function of (u), its pushforward under (x=u^2) is

\[
\int_{\mathbb R}F(u^2)du
=\int_0^\infty F(x)\frac{dx}{\sqrt x}.
\]

Each branch contributes (dx/(2\sqrt x)), and both branches are retained. The exact matrix Stieltjes weight is therefore

\[
\sqrt\pi e^{-x/4}C\,\frac{dx}{\sqrt x}.
\]

Since (T=iu), a polynomial has parity form (f_e(x,\Delta)+iu f_o(x,\Delta)). When the original matrix weight is even in (u), integration kills the cross term, while the odd-odd term contributes the factor \(|iu|^2=x\). Hence the even and odd coefficient metrics use the paired weights

\[
\sqrt\pi e^{-x/4}C\,\frac{dx}{\sqrt x},\qquad
x\sqrt\pi e^{-x/4}C\,\frac{dx}{\sqrt x},
\]

respectively. For Gaussian calibration their scalar moments are, with \(n\geq0\),

\[
\sqrt\pi\int_0^\infty x^{n-1/2}e^{-x/4}dx
=2\pi\frac{(2n)!}{n!}.
\]

The odd metric uses the same formula with (n\) replaced by (n+1). This is an exact way to verify every finite original-coordinate Gram without numerical integration. All powers of (2\), \(\pi\), and the two pushforward branches remain visible.
