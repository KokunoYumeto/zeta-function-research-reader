\subsubsection{1. Source, lower source, and literal masses}\label{endpoint-restriction_fixture--source-lower-source-and-literal-masses}

For positive integer \nolinkurl{lambda} define

\[

\qquad c_\lambda=2^{1-2\lambda}\Gamma(2\lambda).
\]

The measures used here are

\[

\quad v=r_1*r_1=\frac13r_2,
\quad d\nu=\frac{54}{35}v(u)\,du.
\tag{F.1}
\]

Here is the measure identity with its constants. Euler's gamma integral gives \texttt{Gamma(z+1)=z\ Gamma(z)} by integration by parts when \texttt{Re\ z\textgreater{}0}. Multiplying this equality by its conjugate proves the first equality in (F.1). The change from two positive gamma-integral variables to their sum and ratio, with Jacobian equal to the sum, gives

\[
\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)
=\Gamma(a+b)\int_0^1x^{a-1}(1-x)^{b-1}\,dx,
\quad \Re a,\Re b>0.
\]

Set \nolinkurl{a=lambda+iu/2}, \nolinkurl{b=lambda-iu/2}, and then \texttt{x=e\^{}t/(1+e\^{}t)}. This gives

\[
|\Gamma(\lambda+iu/2)|^2
=\Gamma(2\lambda)\int_\mathbb R
       (2\cosh(t/2))^{-2\lambda}e^{iut/2}\,dt.
\]

After \nolinkurl{t=2y}, Fourier inversion, with convention \texttt{hat\ f(t)=integral\ f(u)e\^{}\{-itu\}du}, proves

\[

=2\Gamma(2\lambda)(2\cosh t)^{-2\lambda}
=c_\lambda(\cosh t)^{-2\lambda}.
\tag{F.2}

All inversions and moment differentiations used here are legitimate. The integrand \texttt{(2\ cosh\ y)\^{}(-2lambda)} and every derivative decay exponentially on the real axis. For each \texttt{0\textless{}a\textless{}pi/2}, it is holomorphic on the closed strip \texttt{\textbar{}Im\ y\textbar{}\textless{}=a}; shifting the Fourier integral up or down inside that strip bounds \nolinkurl{r_lambda(u)} by a constant times \texttt{exp(-a\textbar{}u\textbar{})}. The vertical sides of a rectangular contour tend to zero because the integrand decays exponentially in \texttt{\textbar{}Re\ y\textbar{}}, uniformly in that strip. Thus every polynomial moment exists, the Fourier transform extends to imaginary arguments near zero, and its moment-generating function is

\[
\int e^{tu}r_\lambda(u)\,du=c_\lambda(\cos t)^{-2\lambda},
\qquad |t|<\pi/2.
