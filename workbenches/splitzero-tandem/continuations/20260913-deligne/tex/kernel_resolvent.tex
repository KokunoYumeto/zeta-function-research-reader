\section{The full jet kernel, finite resolvents, and the actual analytic tail}
\label{sec:kernel-resolvent}

Retain the arithmetic measure, coordinates and maps of
Section~\ref{sec:tau-boundary-kernel}:
\[
 s=\tfrac12+it,\qquad d\nu_Z(t)=|g(s)/h(s)|^2\frac{dt}{2\pi},
 \quad E=\mathbb C[s]/(h),\quad A=M_s,\quad c=j_h1.
\]
In particular, every selected zero has its complete multiplicity in
\(h\), and \(c\) is the coordinate column of the unit in the retained
monomial basis of \(E\).  Write \(q_k\) for the monic orthogonal
polynomials of \(\nu_Z\), \(\kappa_k=\|q_k\|_{\nu_Z}^2\), and
\(a_k=\kappa_k/\kappa_{k-1}>0\) for \(k\geq1\).  The exact recurrence
is
\[
 sq_k=q_{k+1}+b_kq_k-a_kq_{k-1},\qquad
 b_k+\overline b_k=1,                                    \tag{KR.1}
\]
where the last term is omitted at \(k=0\).

\subsection{A finite compression in the original Mellin coordinate}

Fix \(n\geq d\), so \(N=n-1\) is the degree bound in the preceding
kernel construction.  On the coefficient space with basis
\(q_0,\ldots,q_{n-1}\), put
\[
 H_n=\operatorname{diag}(\kappa_0,\ldots,\kappa_{n-1}),
 \quad (J_n)_{k,k}=b_k,\quad
 (J_n)_{k+1,k}=1,\quad (J_n)_{k,k+1}=-a_{k+1}.             \tag{KR.2}
\]
All unspecified entries are zero, and indices start at zero.
Multiplication of the displayed matrices proves
\[
 J_n^*H_n+H_nJ_n=H_n.                                    \tag{KR.3}
\]
The isometry from this coefficient space to its polynomial image is
\(u\mapsto\sum_kq_ku_k\).  For a standard coefficient Hermitian form,
the corresponding isometry is
\[
 \mathcal I_n u=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{q_k}{\sqrt{\kappa_k}}u_k,
 \qquad B_n=H_n^{1/2}J_nH_n^{-1/2}.                       \tag{KR.4}
\]
This explicitly stated auxiliary coordinate map leaves \(A\), the
packet coordinates, \(q_k\), and \(\kappa_k\) unchanged.
The matrix \(B_n\) has lower off-diagonal entry \(\sqrt{a_{k+1}}\),
upper off-diagonal entry \(-\sqrt{a_{k+1}}\), and satisfies
\(B_n^*+B_n=I\).

\begin{lemma}[Nodes and endpoint resolvents]
The characteristic polynomial of \(J_n\), and hence of \(B_n\), is
\(q_n(z)\).  Its roots \(\lambda_{n,1},\ldots,\lambda_{n,n}\)
are distinct and lie on \(\operatorname{Re}z=1/2\).  There are positive
numbers \(\omega_{n,j}\) and \(\sigma_{n,j}\) such that
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 m_n(z)&=\kappa_0e_0^*(zI-B_n)^{-1}e_0
          =\sum_{j=1}^n\frac{\omega_{n,j}}{z-\lambda_{n,j}},\\
 \sum_j\omega_{n,j}&=\kappa_0,\\
 F_n(z)&=e_{n-1}^*(zI-B_n)^{-1}e_{n-1}
          =\frac{q_{n-1}(z)}{q_n(z)}
          =\sum_{j=1}^n\frac{\sigma_{n,j}}{z-\lambda_{n,j}},\\
 \sum_j\sigma_{n,j}&=1.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{KR.5}
\]
Here \(m_n\) and \(F_n\) are respectively the initial and terminal
observations of the same finite compression.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expanding the tridiagonal determinant along its last row gives
\(D_{k+1}(z)=(z-b_k)D_k(z)+a_kD_{k-1}(z)\), with
\(D_0=1,D_1=z-b_0\).  This is (KR.1), proving \(D_n=q_n\).
The matrix \((B_n-\tfrac12I)/i\) is Hermitian.  If an eigenvector
has first coordinate zero, its first row equation forces its second
coordinate to be zero; recurrence through the rows forces every
coordinate to vanish.  Thus each eigenspace has dimension at most
one.  Hermitian diagonalization gives a complete eigenbasis, hence
all eigenvalues are simple.  The same argument beginning at the last
row shows every eigenvector has nonzero last coordinate.
If \(P_j\) are its rank-one orthogonal eigenprojections, set
\(\omega_{n,j}=\kappa_0e_0^*P_je_0>0\) and
\(\sigma_{n,j}=e_{n-1}^*P_je_{n-1}>0\).
Their sums and the two spectral expansions follow from
\(\sum_jP_j=I\).  The last diagonal cofactor of \(zI-B_n\) is
\(q_{n-1}(z)\), proving the ratio in (KR.5).
\end{proof}

Define \(\delta(z)=|\operatorname{Re}z-1/2|\).
For \(\delta(z)>0\), (KR.3)--(KR.4) give
\(\|(zI-B_n)^{-1}\|\leq\delta(z)^{-1}\): for a vector \(u\),
the real part of \(u^*(zI-B_n)u\) is
\((\operatorname{Re}z-1/2)\|u\|^2\), and Cauchy--Schwarz
then gives the lower singular-value bound.  In particular
\(|F_n(z)|\leq\delta(z)^{-1}\), and
\[
 \operatorname{Re}F_n(z)
  =(\operatorname{Re}z-\tfrac12)
          \sum_j\frac{\sigma_{n,j}}{|z-\lambda_{n,j}|^2}. \tag{KR.6}
\]

\subsection{Exact quadrature and the full confluent kernel}

\begin{theorem}[A polynomial formula valid at every packet centre]
Put
\[
 L_{n,j}(z)=\frac{q_n(z)}{(z-\lambda_{n,j})q_n'(\lambda_{n,j})}.
                                                                    \tag{KR.7}
\]
This denotes the polynomial obtained by cancelling the displayed
linear factor.  For every polynomial \(P\) of degree at most
\(2n-1\),
\[
 \int P(s)\,d\nu_Z=\sum_j\omega_{n,j}P(\lambda_{n,j}).     \tag{KR.8}
\]
Consequently the complete arithmetic kernel is
\[
 \boxed{K_{n-1}
   =\sum_{j=1}^n\frac{L_{n,j}(A)cc^*L_{n,j}(A)^*}
                           {\omega_{n,j}}.}             \tag{KR.9}
\]
Formula (KR.9) includes all Taylor derivatives at all selected
zeros, even when a selected critical zero equals a quadrature node.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For degree below \(n\), the vector \(P(J_n)e_0\) records exactly
the coefficients of \(P\) in the monic orthogonal basis: in the
successive multiplications no term of degree \(n\) occurs.
Its initial coefficient is \(\kappa_0^{-1}\int P\,d\nu_Z\).
Thus the spectral expansion in (KR.5) proves (KR.8) in that degree
range.  For a general \(P\) in the asserted range, polynomial
division gives \(P=q_nU+R\), where \(\deg U\leq n-1\) and
\(\deg R<n\).  On the line, the conjugate of \(U(s)\) is again a
polynomial in \(s\), namely \(\overline{U(1-\overline s)}\).
Orthogonality therefore gives \(\int q_nU\,d\nu_Z=0\).
The same multiple vanishes at all the nodes, proving (KR.8).

The polynomials \(L_{n,j}\) form the interpolation basis of
\(\mathcal P_{n-1}\).  Apply (KR.8) to
\(\overline{L_{n,j}(s)}L_{n,k}(s)\), interpreted as a polynomial
on the line; its degree is at most \(2n-2\).  Their Gram matrix is
\(\operatorname{diag}(\omega_{n,j})\).  In this basis the residue
map has column \(j_hL_{n,j}=L_{n,j}(A)c\).  The definition
\(K=C H^{-1}C^*\) now proves (KR.9).  This is a basis change in
the polynomial source only.  Polynomial evaluation at \(A\)
retains its nilpotent powers, so the formula gives the complete
jets without any distinct-centre or simple-zero assumption.
\end{proof}

To identify the quadrature endpoints exactly, one has
\[
 \sigma_{n,j}
   =\frac{\omega_{n,j}|q_{n-1}(\lambda_{n,j})|^2}
           {\kappa_{n-1}}
   =\frac{\kappa_{n-1}}
           {\omega_{n,j}|q_n'(\lambda_{n,j})|^2}.          \tag{KR.10}
\]
Indeed an eigenvector of \(B_n\) at \(\lambda\) has coordinates
proportional to
\(\overline{q_k(\lambda)}/\sqrt{\kappa_k}\): substitution into
the recurrence proves this assertion, including the endpoint row.
The squared ratio of its last and first coordinates proves the
first equality.  The preceding polynomial kernel formula gives
\(\mathcal K_{n-1}(\lambda_{n,j},\lambda_{n,j})=1/\omega_{n,j}\).
In the original Christoffel--Darboux polynomial identity, set
\(w=\lambda_{n,j}\), divide its common polynomial factor
\(z-\lambda_{n,j}\), then evaluate at that point.  This gives
\[
 \frac1{\omega_{n,j}}
  =\frac{q_n'(\lambda_{n,j})
           \overline{q_{n-1}(\lambda_{n,j})}}{\kappa_{n-1}}.
\]
Its squared modulus gives the second equality in (KR.10).

\begin{theorem}[The exact resolvent metric for the packet]
Suppose, for this formula, that no selected centre equals one of
the specified \(n\) quadrature nodes.  Define maps on the original
space \(E\) by
\[
 Q_n=q_n(A),\qquad X_{n,j}=(A-\lambda_{n,j}I)^{-1}c,
 \quad S_n=\sum_j\sigma_{n,j}X_{n,j}X_{n,j}^*,
 \quad r_n=F_n(A)c.                                      \tag{KR.11}
\]
Then \(Q_n\) is invertible, \(S_n>0\), and
\[
 \boxed{K_{n-1}=\kappa_{n-1}^{-1}Q_nS_nQ_n^*,\qquad
 AS_n+S_nA^*-S_n=cr_n^*+r_nc^*.}                         \tag{KR.12}
\]
In particular, the original relative-control quantity at
\(m=n-1-d\) is exactly
\[
 \epsilon_m=
 \sup_{y\ne0}\frac{|y^*(cr_n^*+r_nc^*)y|}{y^*S_ny}.       \tag{KR.13}
\]
The identity is valid whenever that index belongs to the original
source sequence; \(m=-1\) is its already specified fixed-section
extension.  Every off-line packet obeys the nonintersection
condition for every \(n\).  At an intersecting critical centre,
(KR.9) gives the exact polynomial continuation, with no deletion
of that centre or its jets.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The finite algebra \(E\) has an inverse for a polynomial precisely
when the polynomial is nonzero at every centre: the Euclidean
algorithm gives an inverse modulo \(h\), or, equivalently, the
finite Taylor inverse at each centre gives the inverse under the
complete Chinese-remainder map.  This proves the asserted
invertibilities.  Substitution of (KR.7) and (KR.10) into (KR.9)
proves the first identity in (KR.12).  It proves \(S_n>0\), since
the residue map is onto at \(n\geq d\), hence \(K_{n-1}>0\).
The identity
\(AX_{n,j}=\lambda_{n,j}X_{n,j}+c\), together with
\(\lambda_{n,j}+\overline\lambda_{n,j}=1\), proves
\[
 AS_n+S_nA^*-S_n
 =c\left(\sum_j\sigma_{n,j}X_{n,j}\right)^*
  +\left(\sum_j\sigma_{n,j}X_{n,j}\right)c^*.
\]
The partial fractions in (KR.5), evaluated in the finite algebra,
identify the sum with \(F_n(A)c\).
Because \(Q_n\) commutes with \(A\), both kernel and its weight
form are congruent, with the same factor \(\kappa_{n-1}^{-1}\),
to \(S_n\) and its displayed weight form.  The invertible change
\(y=Q_n^*x\) proves equality of their Rayleigh suprema.  The
previous exact map from \(K_{n-1}\) to \(G_m\) then proves
(KR.13).  All of these maps are stated in the original quotient
coordinates; the Taylor expansion of each rational factor includes
every power through \(m_\rho-1\).
\end{proof}

The scalar form of (KR.13), with every inverse taken on the same
retained packet coordinates, is
\[
 \alpha_n=r_n^*S_n^{-1}r_n,\qquad
 \beta_n=c^*S_n^{-1}c,\qquad
 \gamma_n=c^*S_n^{-1}r_n,
\]
\[
 \boxed{\epsilon_m=|\operatorname{Re}\gamma_n|
      +\sqrt{\alpha_n\beta_n-(\operatorname{Im}\gamma_n)^2}.}
                                                               \tag{KR.13a}
\]
Indeed the nonzero eigenvalues of
\(S_n^{-1/2}(cr_n^*+r_nc^*)S_n^{-1/2}\) coincide, with
multiplicities, with the nonzero eigenvalues of
\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\gamma_n&\beta_n\\
\alpha_n&\overline\gamma_n\end{smallmatrix}\right)\): factor
the former through its two displayed vectors and interchange the
two rectangular factors.  Its characteristic polynomial gives
\(\operatorname{Re}\gamma_n\pm
\sqrt{\alpha_n\beta_n-(\operatorname{Im}\gamma_n)^2}\).
Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(|\gamma_n|^2\leq\alpha_n\beta_n\),
so their greatest absolute value is (KR.13a), including zero or
one-dimensional rank.  The full congruence (KR.12) specifies
exactly how the original \(q_n\), \(\kappa_{n-1}\) and local
unit enter the metric whose relative form is evaluated here.

\subsection{The infinite multiplication operator and its genuine tail}

Let \(\mathscr L=L^2(\nu_Z)\), and let \(M\) be multiplication by
\(s\), with domain \(\{f:sf\in\mathscr L\}\).  It is a closed
operator and \(M^*=I-M\).  These statements follow directly from
the multiplication formula; its resolvent off the line is
multiplication by \((z-s)^{-1}\), of norm at most
\(\delta(z)^{-1}\).  The auxiliary vectors
\(\psi_k=q_k/\sqrt{\kappa_k}\) form a complete orthonormal
basis.  Completeness follows from the positive exponential moment
of the actual arithmetic measure by the Fourier-annihilator proof
already given for the theta measures.

Let \(P_n\) project onto \(\psi_0,\ldots,\psi_{n-1}\), and put
\(Q_n^{\rm tail}=I-P_n\); this projection is distinct from the
polynomial map \(Q_n=q_n(A)\) in (KR.11).  Define
\[
 M_n^{\rm tail}=Q_n^{\rm tail}M Q_n^{\rm tail}
     \quad\hbox{on }Q_n^{\rm tail}\operatorname{Dom}M,
 \qquad
 \zeta_n(z)=\langle\psi_n,(z-M_n^{\rm tail})^{-1}\psi_n\rangle.
                                                               \tag{KR.14}
\]
This tail resolvent exists at every off-line \(z\), with norm at
most \(\delta(z)^{-1}\).  Here is the domain justification.
Every polynomial belongs to \(\operatorname{Dom}M\), so
\(P_n\) preserves that domain and
\(Q_n^{\rm tail}\operatorname{Dom}M
=\operatorname{Dom}M\cap Q_n^{\rm tail}\mathscr L\).
The operator \((M-\tfrac12I)/i\) is self-adjoint multiplication
by \(t\).  Its off-diagonal blocks for \(P_n\oplus Q_n^{\rm tail}\)
extend to a bounded, finite-rank self-adjoint operator, by (KR.1).
Subtracting this operator preserves self-adjointness: for a
self-adjoint \(T\) and bounded self-adjoint \(C\), the definition
of the adjoint gives \((T-C)^*=T^*-C\) on exactly
\(\operatorname{Dom}T^*\).  The resulting diagonal operator
reduces the two projections.  Its tail block is therefore
self-adjoint on the displayed tail domain.  Multiplication by
\(i\) and addition of \(1/2\) gives the claimed tail resolvent.

\begin{theorem}[Exact finite-section, terminal, and tail comparison]
For \(\delta(z)>0\), let
\[
 m(z)=\int\frac{d\nu_Z(t)}{z-s},\qquad
 H_n^{\rm obs}(z)=\frac1{\kappa_n}
                   \int\frac{|q_n(s)|^2}{z-s}\,d\nu_Z(t).
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{H_n^{\rm obs}(z)
       =\frac{\zeta_n(z)}{1+a_nF_n(z)\zeta_n(z)},}         \tag{KR.15}
\]
\[
 \boxed{m(z)-m_n(z)
   =\frac{(-1)^n\kappa_n\zeta_n(z)}
       {q_n(z)^2[1+a_nF_n(z)\zeta_n(z)]}
   =\frac{(-1)^n}{q_n(z)^2}
                   \int\frac{|q_n(s)|^2}{z-s}\,d\nu_Z(t).} \tag{KR.16}
\]
Every denominator in these formulas is nonzero off the original
Mellin line.  The terminal finite observations additionally obey
the exact Schur recurrence
\[
 F_1(z)=\frac1{z-b_0},\qquad
 F_{n+1}(z)=\frac1{z-b_n+a_nF_n(z)}.                      \tag{KR.17}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Under \(\mathcal I_n\oplus I\), the block operator \(M\) is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 B_n&-\sqrt{a_n}\,e_{n-1}\langle\psi_n,\cdot\rangle\\
 \sqrt{a_n}\,\psi_ne_{n-1}^*&M_n^{\rm tail}
 \end{pmatrix}.                                         \tag{KR.18}
\]
The signs follow directly from (KR.1); both coupling maps are
bounded on the displayed summands.  Eliminate the tail component
in \(z-M\).  The finite Schur complement is
\(zI-B_n+a_n\zeta_n(z)e_{n-1}e_{n-1}^*\).
For \(\operatorname{Re}z>1/2\), the real part of its quadratic
form is at least \(\delta(z)\|u\|^2\), because
\(\operatorname{Re}\zeta_n(z)>0\); on the other side all signs
reverse.  The sign of this scalar real part follows either from
the self-adjoint tail resolvent or from the resolvent identity
\(\operatorname{Re}\langle v,(z-M_n^{\rm tail})^{-1}v\rangle
=(\operatorname{Re}z-1/2)\|(z-M_n^{\rm tail})^{-1}v\|^2\).
Thus the Schur complement is invertible and
\(1+a_n\zeta_nF_n\ne0\).
Direct multiplication verifies the rank-one inverse formula
\[
 R_n-\frac{a_n\zeta_n}{1+a_n\zeta_nF_n}
                   R_ne_{n-1}e_{n-1}^*R_n,
 \qquad R_n=(zI-B_n)^{-1}.                               \tag{KR.19}
\]
The analogous bottom Schur calculation, paired against
\(\psi_n\), gives (KR.15), since
\(\langle\psi_n,(z-M)^{-1}\psi_n\rangle=H_n^{\rm obs}(z)\).

The two endpoint cofactors of \(zI-B_n\) give
\[
 e_0^*R_ne_{n-1}
   =\frac{(-1)^{n-1}\sqrt{\kappa_{n-1}/\kappa_0}}{q_n(z)},
 \qquad
 e_{n-1}^*R_ne_0
   =\frac{\sqrt{\kappa_{n-1}/\kappa_0}}{q_n(z)}.           \tag{KR.20}
\]
These cofactors are the products of the successive upper and
lower entries, respectively; the formula also covers \(n=1\).
Pair (KR.19) against \(\sqrt{\kappa_0}e_0\), the coefficient
vector of the unchanged function \(1\).  Substitute (KR.20)
and \(a_n\kappa_{n-1}=\kappa_n\).  This proves the first
equality in (KR.16); (KR.15) proves the second.
Finally (KR.17) is (KR.1) divided by \(q_{n+1}\), equivalently
the last-coordinate finite Schur complement.
\end{proof}

There is also a direct polynomial check on every factor in
(KR.16).  The polynomial
\[
 p_n(z)=\int\frac{q_n(z)-q_n(s)}{z-s}\,d\nu_Z(t)
\]
has degree below \(n\).  At a quadrature node its ratio residue
is \(p_n(\lambda_{n,j})/q_n'(\lambda_{n,j})
=\int L_{n,j}\,d\nu_Z=\omega_{n,j}\), so \(m_n=p_n/q_n\).
Orthogonality to the polynomial
\((q_n(z)-q_n(s))/(z-s)\) gives
\[
 m-m_n=\frac1{q_n(z)^2}
                       \int\frac{q_n(s)^2}{z-s}\,d\nu_Z.
\]
The polynomial
\((-1)^n\overline{q_n(1-\overline s)}\) is monic and has
the same orthogonality conditions as \(q_n\); uniqueness makes
it equal to \(q_n\).  On the line this says
\(q_n(s)^2=(-1)^n|q_n(s)|^2\), giving (KR.16) again.
In particular the explicit error inequality is
\[
 |m(z)-m_n(z)|\leq
       \frac{\kappa_n}{\delta(z)|q_n(z)|^2}.              \tag{KR.21}
\]

The initial and terminal observations have now been related by
the actual block maps (KR.18)--(KR.20), the tail (KR.14), and the
exact remainder (KR.16).  The relative form uses the terminal
observation through (KR.11)--(KR.13).  An estimate for the initial
observation can be propagated through these equalities while
retaining the factors \(q_n\), \(\kappa_n\), \(a_n\), and the
tail denominator.  None of those arithmetic quantities is
discarded in passing from one observation to the other.
