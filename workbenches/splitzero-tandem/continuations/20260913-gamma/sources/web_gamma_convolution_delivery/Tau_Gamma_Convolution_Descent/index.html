1. Scope and the inspected collaboration state

This is a mathematical continuation of Tau Toda Volume Control and Tau Exterior Trace Amplification. It retains their arithmetic source, quotient, scaling action, full packet multiplicities, and canonical minimum-norm metric. The new calculation links those objects to the gamma reference that is already in the repository. The gamma orthogonal polynomials and their general convolution identities are classical. The contribution here is the explicit arithmetic coefficient transform, its source and quotient maps, its quantitative bounds, and the resulting exact substitution into the existing Toda/exterior estimate.

The GitHub read was pinned to main fa4be32f87a9a90f3c02a07f7790dea95a1e7b0f. The separate formalization was read at PR #22 head 811210d24b80813a08972ca23f919db015383137. At that inspected revision the PR explicitly said strict verification was still in progress. No successful certificate for that revision is inferred here. Its invariant-filtration trace budget is retained as the other session’s result, not presented as a new deduction of this note.

The main-branch gamma source read was workbenches/splitzero-tandem/continuations/20260912-stieltjes/tex/theta_gamma_reference.tex, equations TG.2–TG.14. It already constructs the exact one-factor gamma measure, its constants, its monic polynomials and its arithmetic multiplier. We extend that specified reference through the sum pushforward at every tensor degree.

There is no new RH proof or arithmetic asymptotic assertion here. Theorems below have written proofs. The checker supplies finite exact regression evidence, not analytic interval certification and not Lean execution. No inherited file or other-session branch is modified.

3. Carry the repository’s gamma reference through every tensor power

For a fixed real λ>0\lambda>0 define exactly

rλ(t)=|Γ(λ+it/2)|22π,dσλ(t)=rλ(t)dt,cλ=21−2λΓ(2λ). r_\lambda(t)=\frac{|\Gamma(\lambda+it/2)|^2}{2\pi},\qquad d\sigma_\lambda(t)=r_\lambda(t)dt,\qquad c_\lambda=2^{1-2\lambda}\Gamma(2\lambda). \tag{5} (5)

The repository proves, by the beta integral and Fourier inversion,

∫eiytdσλ(t)=cλ(coshy)−2λ. \int e^{iyt}d\sigma_\lambda(t)=c_\lambda(\cosh y)^{-2\lambda}. \tag{6} (6)

Its factor 22 comes from t=2ut=2u in the beta integral. Thus cλc_\lambda, rather than 11, is the mass. Fourier uniqueness for finite measures gives the all-tensor identity

rλ,k:=rλ*k=κλ,krkλ,κλ,k=cλkckλ=2k−1Γ(2λ)kΓ(2kλ). \boxed{ r_{\lambda,k}:=r_\lambda^{*k} =\kappa_{\lambda,k}r_{k\lambda},\qquad \kappa_{\lambda,k}=\frac{c_\lambda^k}{c_{k\lambda}} =2^{k-1}\frac{\Gamma(2\lambda)^k}{\Gamma(2k\lambda)}. } \tag{7} (7)

In particular the mass of the sum measure is cλkc_\lambda^k. For λ=1/4\lambda=1/4 this reads

r1/4,k(u)=2k−1πk/2Γ(k/2)|Γ(k/4+iu/2)|22π,∫r1/4,k=(2π)k/2. \boxed{ r_{1/4,k}(u)= \frac{2^{k-1}\pi^{k/2}}{\Gamma(k/2)} \frac{|\Gamma(k/4+iu/2)|^2}{2\pi},\qquad \int r_{1/4,k}=(2\pi)^{k/2}. } \tag{8} (8)

The literal coordinate map is (t1,…,tk)↦u=∑iti(t_1,\ldots,t_k)\mapsto u=\sum_i t_i; uu is not replaced by an average or a rescaled variable. The original arithmetic scaling coordinate is still S=k/2+iuS=k/2+iu.

For real |θ|<π/2|\theta|<\pi/2, analytic continuation of (6) gives

Zλ,kΓ(θ):=∫eθurλ,k(u)du=cλk(cosθ)−2kλ. Z_{\lambda,k}^{\Gamma}(\theta) :=\int e^{\theta u}r_{\lambda,k}(u)du =c_\lambda^k(\cos\theta)^{-2k\lambda}. \tag{9} (9)

The exponential integrability follows either from the original beta-contour estimate or the gamma vertical-strip bound. This justifies all finite differentiated moment integrals used below.

4. The sum projection and its complete polynomial complement

Define actual Hilbert spaces

ℋk=L2(ℝk,∏idσλ(ti)),ℋΣ=L2(ℝ,rλ,k(u)du). \mathcal H_k=L^2(\mathbb R^k,\prod_i d\sigma_\lambda(t_i)),\qquad \mathcal H_\Sigma=L^2(\mathbb R,r_{\lambda,k}(u)du).

Their isometric inclusion is

UΣ:ℋΣ→ℋk,(UΣf)(𝐭)=f(t1+⋯+tk). U_\Sigma:\mathcal H_\Sigma\to\mathcal H_k,\qquad (U_\Sigma f)(\mathbf t)=f(t_1+\cdots+t_k). \tag{10} (10)

For k≥2k\ge2, its adjoint has the explicit disintegration formula

(CΣF)(u)=1rλ,k(u)∫ℝk−1F(t1,…,tk−1,u−∑i<kti)∏i<krλ(ti)rλ(u−∑i<kti)dk−1t. (C_\Sigma F)(u)=\frac{1}{r_{\lambda,k}(u)} \int_{\mathbb R^{k-1}} F(t_1,\ldots,t_{k-1},u-\sum_{i<k}t_i) \prod_{i<k}r_\lambda(t_i)\,r_\lambda(u-\sum_{i<k}t_i)\,d^{k-1}t. \tag{11} (11)

Its denominator is the actual fibre mass. Fubini proves CΣUΣ=1C_\Sigma U_\Sigma=1 and CΣ=UΣ*C_\Sigma=U_\Sigma^*. For k=1k=1 both maps are the identity. Put PΣ=UΣCΣP_\Sigma=U_\Sigma C_\Sigma and retain

F↦(CΣF,(1−PΣ)F),(f,n)↦UΣf+n. F\longmapsto(C_\Sigma F,(1-P_\Sigma)F),\qquad (f,n)\longmapsto U_\Sigma f+n. \tag{12} (12)

These are inverse Hilbert isometries onto ℋΣ⊕kerCΣ\mathcal H_\Sigma\oplus\ker C_\Sigma. The second component is not discarded. At each original support label ℓ\ell, their reconstructed maps are (ℓ,F)↦(ℓ,CΣF)(\ell,F)\mapsto(\ell,C_\Sigma F) and (ℓ,F)↦(ℓ,(1−PΣ)F)(\ell,F)\mapsto(\ell,(1-P_\Sigma)F). A nonzero normal vector has first image (ℓ,0)(\ell,0), while its second image retains that vector. Both maps send external absence to external absence; their paired inverse is still (12). Norm and Gram observations have their actual quadratic types, not the type of a linear quotient: Gram:Homℂ(E,ℋ)→Herm(E)\operatorname{Gram}:\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb C}(E,\mathcal H) \to\operatorname{Herm}(E), R↦R*RR\mapsto R^*R.

4.1 Exact action on every product-polynomial degree

Let bn(λ)(t)b_n^{(\lambda)}(t) be the repository’s monic gamma polynomial, with

∑n≥0bn(λ)(t)n!zn=(1−iz)−λ+it/2(1+iz)−λ−it/2. \sum_{n\ge0}\frac{b_n^{(\lambda)}(t)}{n!}z^n =(1-iz)^{-\lambda+it/2}(1+iz)^{-\lambda-it/2}.

Set α=2λ\alpha=2\lambda. Its complete norm and recurrence are

∥bn(λ)∥2=cλn!(α)n,bn+1(λ)=tbn(λ)−n(n+α−1)bn−1(λ). \|b_n^{(\lambda)}\|^2=c_\lambda n!(\alpha)_n,\qquad b_{n+1}^{(\lambda)}=t b_n^{(\lambda)}-n(n+\alpha-1)b_{n-1}^{(\lambda)}. \tag{13} (13)

The polynomials are not divided by their norms. Multiplying the kk generating functions proves the literal addition formula

bn(kλ)(∑iti)=∑n1+⋯+nk=nn!∏ini!∏ibni(λ)(ti). b_n^{(k\lambda)}(\sum_i t_i) =\sum_{n_1+\cdots+n_k=n}\frac{n!}{\prod_i n_i!} \prod_i b_{n_i}^{(\lambda)}(t_i). \tag{14} (14)

For a multi-index 𝐧\mathbf n, put n=∑inin=\sum_i n_i. The exact sum projection is

CΣ(∏ibni(λ)(ti))=∏i(α)ni(kα)nbn(kλ)(u). \boxed{ C_\Sigma\left(\prod_i b_{n_i}^{(\lambda)}(t_i)\right) =\frac{\prod_i(\alpha)_{n_i}}{(k\alpha)_n}\,b_n^{(k\lambda)}(u). } \tag{15} (15)

Proof. Pair the product against (14), in the actual product measure. Orthogonality makes the answer zero unless the sum degree is nn, and at degree nn the answer is cλkn!∏i(α)nic_\lambda^k n!\prod_i(\alpha)_{n_i}. The squared norm of the sum polynomial is cλkn!(kα)nc_\lambda^k n!(k\alpha)_n. Their quotient gives (15).

These tests identify the whole conditional projection, not merely its finite polynomial moments: the gamma sum measure has exponential moments, so its polynomials are dense in L2L^2. To verify that last statement, an L2L^2 vector orthogonal to all polynomials defines a finite measure whose Fourier transform is analytic on a strip by Cauchy–Schwarz and exponential integrability. All derivatives at zero vanish; analytic uniqueness and uniqueness of Fourier transforms give the zero vector.

The retained normal polynomial is

n𝐧=∏ibni(λ)(ti)−∏i(α)ni(kα)nbn(kλ)(∑iti). n_{\mathbf n}=\prod_i b_{n_i}^{(\lambda)}(t_i) -\frac{\prod_i(\alpha)_{n_i}}{(k\alpha)_n} b_n^{(k\lambda)}(\sum_i t_i).

Its exact squared norm is

∥n𝐧∥2=cλk[∏ini!(α)ni−n!∏i(α)ni2(kα)n]. \boxed{ \|n_{\mathbf n}\|^2 =c_\lambda^k\left[ \prod_i n_i!(\alpha)_{n_i} -\frac{n!\prod_i(\alpha)_{n_i}^2}{(k\alpha)_n} \right]. } \tag{16} (16)

Thus every degree, every factorial, and the relative component have an explicit map and norm. This is the classical Meixner–Pollaczek addition mechanism, applied with the source’s exact scale and masses; it is not a claim to have discovered a new general family of orthogonal polynomials.

5. Insert the complete arithmetic multiplier, not a substituted gamma measure

Define the complex amplitude and its squared modulus by

Ah,λ(t)=vh(1/2+it)Γ(λ+it/2),Bh,λ(t)=|Ah,λ(t)|2,wh=Bh,λrλ. A_{h,\lambda}(t) =\frac{v_h(1/2+it)}{\Gamma(\lambda+it/2)},\qquad B_{h,\lambda}(t)=|A_{h,\lambda}(t)|^2, \qquad w_h=B_{h,\lambda}r_\lambda. \tag{17} (17)

The entire quotient g/hg/h supplies values at the selected zeros. No pole is inserted into the multiplier at those locations. The full complex amplitude, not only its modulus, remains in the source comparison

P↦∏iΓ(λ+iti/2)2πAh,λ(ti)P(k/2+i∑iti). P\longmapsto \prod_i\frac{\Gamma(\lambda+it_i/2)}{\sqrt{2\pi}} A_{h,\lambda}(t_i)\,P(k/2+i\sum_i t_i). \tag{18} (18)

This is the original Mellin image of 𝒱h,kP\mathcal V_{h,k}P.

The scalar multiplier on the sum line is

Bh,λ;k=CΣ(Bh,λ⊗k). B_{h,\lambda;k}=C_\Sigma(B_{h,\lambda}^{\otimes k}).

The exact pushforward identity is

mh,k(u)=wh*k(u)=rλ,k(u)Bh,λ;k(u). \boxed{ m_{h,k}(u)=w_h^{*k}(u)=r_{\lambda,k}(u)B_{h,\lambda;k}(u). } \tag{19} (19)

This is a norm observation of the original source; it does not project the original finite arithmetic module or change its trace. The maps in (3), including the full unit in η\eta, remain attached to every polynomial. The full field Bh,λ⊗kB_{h,\lambda}^{\otimes k} is recoverable from (12), so its relative complement is retained too.

5.1 A bounded multiplier for every full quartet

For λ=1/4\lambda=1/4, the original formula is

Ah,1/4(t)=−π−1/4(t2+1/4)e−itlogπ/2ζ(1/2+it)h(1/2+it). A_{h,1/4}(t)= \frac{-\pi^{-1/4}(t^2+1/4)e^{-it\log\pi/2}\zeta(1/2+it)}{h(1/2+it)}. \tag{20} (20)

The minus sign and phase are still in (18). The elementary continuation formula

ζ(s)=ss−1−s∫1∞{x}x−s−1dx \zeta(s)=\frac{s}{s-1}-s\int_1^\infty\{x\}x^{-s-1}dx

gives, on this line,

|ζ(1/2+it)|≤1+2t2+1/4≤2(1+|t|). |\zeta(1/2+it)|\le1+2\sqrt{t^2+1/4}\le2(1+|t|).

Let d=degh≥3d=\deg h\ge3 and take Th=max(1,2maxρ|ρ−1/2|)T_h=\max(1,2\max_\rho|\rho-1/2|). For |t|≥Th|t|\ge T_h, |h(1/2+it)|≥(|t|/2)d|h(1/2+it)|\ge(|t|/2)^d, and direct substitution gives

Bh,1/4(t)≤2522dπ|t|6−2d. B_{h,1/4}(t)\le\frac{25\,2^{2d}}{\sqrt\pi}|t|^{6-2d}.

Consequently the finite, specified constant

Ch=max{sup|t|≤Th|Ah,1/4(t)|2,2522dπTh6−2d} C_h=\max\left\{ \sup_{|t|\le T_h}|A_{h,1/4}(t)|^2, \frac{25\,2^{2d}}{\sqrt\pi}T_h^{6-2d} \right\}

satisfies 0≤Bh,1/4≤Ch0\le B_{h,1/4}\le C_h. The compact supremum uses the analytic quotient, not the expression with a removable denominator left untreated. A full nonreal quartet has d=4m≥4d=4m\ge4, so this applies to the entire packet used in the exterior amplification, with constants allowed to depend on that fixed packet.

The positive integral in (11) now proves

0<Bh,1/4;k(u)≤Chk(k≥2),0≤mh,k(u)≤Chkr1/4,k(u). \boxed{ 0<B_{h,1/4;k}(u)\le C_h^k\quad(k\ge2),\qquad 0\le m_{h,k}(u)\le C_h^k r_{1/4,k}(u). } \tag{21} (21)

Strict positivity for k≥2k\ge2 follows because the one-factor multiplier only vanishes at a discrete set; its excluded hyperplanes have zero fibre measure. For k=1k=1 retain its actual isolated zeros.

For a chosen reference below, ChC_h always denotes an explicitly specified upper bound for that reference’s Bh,λB_{h,\lambda}. An explicit alternate reference is available without changing the arithmetic source. For integer L≥0L\ge0,

Ah,1/4+L(t)=Ah,1/4(t)∏j=0L−1(j+1/4+it/2). A_{h,1/4+L}(t) =\frac{A_{h,1/4}(t)}{\prod_{j=0}^{L-1}(j+1/4+it/2)}. \tag{22} (22)

Its product is a known nonvanishing gamma-shift factor on the integration line. Taking L≥max(0,3−d)L\ge\max(0,3-d) makes its squared modulus bounded by the same compact/tail argument. B1,13/4(t)≤1024/π. \boxed{ B_{1,13/4}(t)\le1024/\sqrt\pi. } \tag{23} (23)

This is an analytic estimate for a nonzero seed. Its finite spectral quotient is still zero, as required by the merged correction.

6. Exact arithmetic coefficients at every tensor degree

Use a reference for which B=Bh,λB=B_{h,\lambda} is bounded, as constructed above. Define its actual coefficients

ch,j=∫bj(λ)(t)wh(t)dtcλj!(α)j,𝒜h(z)=∑j≥0(α)jch,jzj. c_{h,j}=\frac{\int b_j^{(\lambda)}(t)w_h(t)dt} {c_\lambda j!(\alpha)_j},\qquad \mathcal A_h(z)=\sum_{j\ge0}(\alpha)_j c_{h,j}z^j. \tag{24} (24)

The series is used coefficientwise; no convergence radius for this formal series is stipulated. Each coefficient is an actual one-factor arithmetic integral. Define

dh,k,n=[zn]𝒜h(z)k. d_{h,k,n}=[z^n]\mathcal A_h(z)^k.

Then the complete L2(rλ,kdu)L^2(r_{\lambda,k}du) expansion is

Bh,λ;k(u)=∑n≥0dh,k,n(kα)nbn(kλ)(u). \boxed{ B_{h,\lambda;k}(u) =\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{d_{h,k,n}}{(k\alpha)_n} b_n^{(k\lambda)}(u). } \tag{25} (25)

Proof. Pair B⊗kB^{\otimes k} with the finite addition formula (14). Each one-factor pairing is cλj!(α)jch,jc_\lambda j!(\alpha)_j c_{h,j}. The multinomial coefficient cancels the displayed factorials and gives

∫Bh,λ;kbn(kλ)rλ,k=cλkn![zn]𝒜h(z)k. \int B_{h,\lambda;k} b_n^{(k\lambda)}r_{\lambda,k} =c_\lambda^k n! [z^n]\mathcal A_h(z)^k.

Divide by the exact norm cλkn!(kα)nc_\lambda^k n!(k\alpha)_n. Completeness gives (25). No exchange of an infinite product of formal series with an integral was needed: every coefficient used a finite polynomial identity.

At degree zero,

ch,0=μh/cλ,dh,k,0=(μh/cλ)k, c_{h,0}=\mu_h/c_\lambda,\qquad d_{h,k,0}=(\mu_h/c_\lambda)^k,

so (25) recovers exactly ∫mh,k=μhk\int m_{h,k}=\mu_h^k.

The retained relative residual is

𝒩h,k=B⊗k−UΣBh,λ;k. \mathcal N_{h,k}=B^{\otimes k}-U_\Sigma B_{h,\lambda;k}.

Its whole norm is calculated by Pythagoras:

∥𝒩h,k∥2=(∫B(t)2dσλ(t))k−cλk∑n≥0n!(kα)n|dh,k,n|2. \boxed{ \|\mathcal N_{h,k}\|^2 =\left(\int B(t)^2d\sigma_\lambda(t)\right)^k -c_\lambda^k\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{n!}{(k\alpha)_n}|d_{h,k,n}|^2. } \tag{26} (26)

The difference is nonnegative because it is the norm of the explicitly retained vector. It is not set to zero by calling the sum map a pushforward.

6.1 Finite exactness and approximation errors

Let BL=∑j=0Lch,jbj(λ)B_L=\sum_{j=0}^L c_{h,j}b_j^{(\lambda)} and Bk,L=CΣ(BL⊗k)B_{k,L}=C_\Sigma(B_L^{\otimes k}). Projection contraction and the tensor telescoping identity give

∥Bh,λ;k−Bk,L∥≤k∥B∥k−1∥B−BL∥. \boxed{ \|B_{h,\lambda;k}-B_{k,L}\| \le k\|B\|^{k-1}\|B-B_L\|. } \tag{27} (27)

These norms have the respective original reference measures. The proof uses ∥BL∥≤∥B∥\|B_L\|\le\|B\| and expands the tensor difference into kk terms. Both sides retain the full mass factors.

More strongly, the moments of Bk,Lrλ,kB_{k,L}r_{\lambda,k} against every polynomial of degree at most LL are exactly those of mh,km_{h,k}, since coefficient n≤Ln\le L in (25) uses only ch,jc_{h,j} with j≤nj\le n.

It follows that the entire degree-NN source Gram, and its degree-NN relation Gram, are determined exactly by ch,0,…,ch,2Nc_{h,0},\ldots,c_{h,2N}. The relation integrands are also degree at most 2N2N. Their tensor dependence is the explicit finite coefficient power in (24), not a fresh kk-dimensional integral.

A truncated Bk,LB_{k,L} need not be nonnegative pointwise. Its relationship to the true positive metric is the exact finite-moment equality just proved when L≥2NL\ge2N, or the error estimate (27) and Cauchy–Schwarz for the specified polynomial tests when L<2NL<2N. Positivity is not inferred from a plotted truncated density.

The multiplier bound gives more than a change of coordinates. For a,b>0a,b>0 with a+b<π/2a+b<\pi/2, every integer r≥0r\ge0 and T≥0T\ge0,
