\section{The original frontier energy, determinant growth, and the entire border fibre}
\label{sec:frontier-energy-dissipation}

The symmetric-frontier delivery of 12 September computes coordinates on
the actual next relation layer.  We retain these coordinates and prove
their exact relation to the accumulated source energy of
Section~\ref{sec:joint-density-dissipation}.  The calculation also
identifies the entire zero fibre of the delivery's smaller border
operator, including its nilpotent extension.

\subsection{Original moment coordinates and the relation graph}

Keep a nonempty full-order packet \(h\), its degree \(d\),
\(g=2\xi\), \(E=\mathbb C[s]/h\),
\(\upsilon=j_h(g/h)\), and \(A=M_s\).  The positive measure
remains
\[
 d\nu_h(t)=|g(1/2+it)/h(1/2+it)|^2\frac{dt}{2\pi}.
\]
Let \(p_n\) be the monic orthogonal polynomial obtained from
\(1,s,\ldots,s^n\) without dividing by its norm, and let
\(\omega_n=\|p_n\|_{\nu_h}^2>0\).
The original generator has the exact recurrence
\[
 sp_n=p_{n+1}+b_np_n-a_np_{n-1},\qquad
 a_n=\omega_n/\omega_{n-1}\ (n\ge1),\quad a_0=0,
 \quad b_n+\overline b_n=1.                              \tag{FE.1}
\]
Here \(p_{-1}=0\).  To verify the coefficients, multiplication
by \(s\) has adjoint multiplication by \(1-s\) on the
original line.  Its pairing against degrees below \(n-1\)
vanishes, the leading coefficient is one, and the pairing against
\(p_{n-1}\) is \(-\omega_n\).  Its diagonal is
\(\int s|p_n|^2d\nu_h/\omega_n\), which proves the last
identity and retains its possibly nonzero imaginary part.

Fix \(k\ge1\) and \(M\ge k(d-1)\), and use
\(A_k=\sum_jA_j\) on the unchanged \(E_k=E^{\otimes k}\).
For \(\alpha\in\mathbb N^k\), put
\[
 p_\alpha=\prod_jp_{\alpha_j}(s_j),\quad
 \omega_\alpha=\prod_j\omega_{\alpha_j},\quad
 z_\alpha=\upsilon^{\otimes k}[p_\alpha]\in E_k.
\]
All remainder coordinates, including every nilpotent jet and the
entire local arithmetic unit, are retained in \(z_\alpha\).
The original source map \(\mathcal T^{(k)}\) is the map
in (CT.11)--(CT.13).  Its orthogonal polynomial images have norms
\(\omega_\alpha\).  The constrained minimum gives exactly
\[
 \mathcal C_M=G_M^{-1}=
     \sum_{|\alpha|\le M}\frac{z_\alpha z_\alpha^*}{\omega_\alpha},
 \qquad
 R_Mu=\sum_{|\alpha|\le M}
       \mathcal T^{(k)}p_\alpha\,
                  \omega_\alpha^{-1}z_\alpha^*G_Mu.
 \tag{FE.2}
\]
This is (CT.5) in the explicitly declared orthogonal source basis;
no moment mass or polynomial norm has been changed.

Order the indices \(\beta\) of total degree \(M+1\), and put
\[
 r=\binom{M+k}{k-1},\quad
 \Omega=\operatorname{diag}(\omega_\beta),\quad
 F=[z_\beta]:\mathbb C^r\to E_k,
 \quad T_+=[\mathcal T^{(k)}p_\beta]:\mathbb C^r\to\mathcal H_k.
\]
The two source maps have the same column ordering.  Since the image
of \(T_+\) is orthogonal to the entire degree-\(M\) image,
\[
 b=T_+-R_MF:\mathbb C^r\longrightarrow\mathcal E_M,
 \quad H=b^*b=\Omega+F^*G_MF,\quad b^*R_M=-F^*G_M.
 \tag{FE.3}
\]
Here \(\mathcal E_M=\mathcal B_{M+1}\cap\mathcal B_M^\perp\)
is the original relation layer.  The complete jets of \(b\)
vanish; both of its terms are orthogonal to \(\mathcal B_M\).
Taking the highest orthogonal degree of \(bu\) recovers
\(T_+u\), proving injectivity.  For any boundary of degree
\(M+1\), subtract the image under \(b\) of its highest
coefficient.  The difference lies in \(\mathcal B_M\).
This gives the inverse on the orthogonal layer and proves surjectivity.
It also gives an original primitive: divide the numerator of each
column of \(b\) in the fixed order by
\(h(s_1),\ldots,h(s_k)\).  The primitive in factor \(j\)
has coefficient \((-1)^{j-1}\); its tensor differential contributes
the same sign, giving the original positive boundary.

Define the second frontier matrix by
\[
 E_+=[w_\beta],\qquad
 w_\beta=\sum_{j:\beta_j>0}a_{\beta_j}z_{\beta-e_j}.
 \tag{FE.4}
\]
The notation \(E_+\) denotes a matrix \(\mathbb C^r\to E_k\).
From (FE.2), the highest coefficient of
\(D^{(k)}R_M-R_MA_k\) is
\(\Omega^{-1}E_+^*G_M\).  Projection onto the graph (FE.3)
therefore proves, with the original types and signs,
\[
 Y_M=-bH^{-1}F^*G_M,\qquad
 C_M=b\Omega^{-1}E_+^*G_M,\qquad
 \Delta_M=Y_M^*Y_M=G_MF H^{-1}F^*G_M.                   \tag{FE.5}
\]
The notation \(C_M\) is the original derivative-layer map in
(CT.15), not the inverse interpolation matrix \(\mathcal C_M\).

Applying (FE.1) to (FE.2) cancels every internal neighbouring pair,
because \(a_{\alpha_j+1}=\omega_{\alpha+e_j}/\omega_\alpha\).
The diagonals give exactly \(k\mathcal C_M\).  The remaining
pairs have top degree \(M+1\), proving
\[
 \mathcal Z_M=A_k\mathcal C_M+\mathcal C_MA_k^*-k\mathcal C_M
     =F\Omega^{-1}E_+^*+E_+\Omega^{-1}F^*,\qquad
 W_M=G_M\mathcal Z_MG_M.                                \tag{FE.6}
\]
Alternatively (FE.5) inserted into
\(W_M=-(Y_M^*C_M+C_M^*Y_M)\) gives exactly (FE.6).
Thus both the polynomial cancellation and its actual boundary
primitive yield the same original form.

\subsection{Exact relative loss and the source derivative energy}

For the next formulas only, use the displayed finite isometries
from the specified metric coordinate spaces and define
\[
 U=G_M^{1/2}F\Omega^{-1/2},\qquad
 V=G_M^{1/2}E_+\Omega^{-1/2},\qquad
 P=UU^*,\qquad K_U=U^*U.
 \tag{FE.7}
\]
The original \(F,E_+,\Omega,G_M\) are still given by
(FE.1)--(FE.5), and can be recovered by the inverse coordinate
maps in (FE.7).  In particular no polynomial is assigned norm one
and no arithmetic coefficient is suppressed.

\begin{theorem}[Frontier energy and every relative Gram eigenvalue]
In these explicitly related coordinates,
\[
 \begin{split}
 S_M&=G_M^{-1/2}W_MG_M^{-1/2}=UV^*+VU^*,\\
 T_M&=G_M^{-1/2}\Delta_MG_M^{-1/2}
       =U(I+K_U)^{-1}U^*=P(I+P)^{-1},\\
 G_M^{-1/2}G_{M+1}G_M^{-1/2}&=(I+P)^{-1}.
 \end{split}                                                   \tag{FE.8}
\]
If \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_q>0\) are all positive
eigenvalues of \(P\), including multiplicity, then
\[
 \begin{split}
 q&=\operatorname{rank}F=\operatorname{rank}Y_M
                       =\operatorname{rank}\Delta_M,\\
 \tau_M&=\operatorname{Tr}(G_M^{-1}\Delta_M)
                =\sum_{i=1}^q\frac{\lambda_i}{1+\lambda_i},\qquad
 \pi_M=\frac{\det G_{M+1}}{\det G_M}
                =\prod_{i=1}^q(1+\lambda_i)^{-1},\\
 \chi_M&=\operatorname{Tr}(G_M^{-1}C_M^*C_M)
                =\|V\|_{\rm HS}^2+\|VU^*\|_{\rm HS}^2.
 \end{split}                                                   \tag{FE.9}
\]
The empty product equals one if \(q=0\).  In addition
\(\operatorname{rank}C_M=\operatorname{rank}E_+\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Equation (FE.6) proves the first line of (FE.8).  The identity
\(H=\Omega^{1/2}(I+U^*U)\Omega^{1/2}\) substituted into
(FE.5) proves the second line's first expression.  The equality
\((I+UU^*)U=U(I+U^*U)\), multiplied by the indicated
invertible factors, identifies it with \(P(I+P)^{-1}\).
Subtract it from \(I\) to get the third line.  The positive
matrices \(I+K_U\) and \(I+P\) are invertible directly from
their quadratic forms.

The graph map \(b\) is injective, and \(H,\Omega,G_M\)
are invertible, so (FE.5) proves both asserted ranks.  Equivalently,
the formula for \(T_M\) identifies its positive eigenvalues
with \(\lambda_i/(1+\lambda_i)\), retaining zeros on
\(\ker P\).  The trace and determinant formulas now follow
from the finite spectral decomposition in these same coordinates.
Finally
\[
 C_MG_M^{-1/2}=b\Omega^{-1/2}V^*,\qquad
 (b\Omega^{-1/2})^*(b\Omega^{-1/2})=I+U^*U.
\]
Its squared Hilbert--Schmidt norm is
\(\operatorname{Tr}(V(I+U^*U)V^*)\), which expands to
the last line of (FE.9).  This is the energy of the original
source-layer map for its inherited \(L^2(d^kx)\) inner product.
\end{proof}

The exact remainder in the accumulated determinant calculation is
therefore also a frontier formula:
\[
 -\log\pi_M-\tau_M
   =\sum_{i=1}^q\left(\log(1+\lambda_i)
                       -\frac{\lambda_i}{1+\lambda_i}\right)
   =\sum_{i=1}^q\int_0^{\lambda_i}\frac{x}{(1+x)^2}\,dx.
 \tag{FE.10}
\]
Differentiate each displayed scalar expression and evaluate at zero
to prove the equality, including every eigenvalue and its sign.
Thus (JD.14) uses the exact original derivative cost
\(\|V\|_{\rm HS}^2+\|VU^*\|_{\rm HS}^2\), and
(JD.16) is precisely the pair of identities
\[
 \sum_{M\ge a}\|Y_M\|_{\rm HS}^2=\operatorname{Tr}G_a,
 \qquad
 \sum_{M\ge a}\sum_i\frac{\lambda_{M,i}}{1+\lambda_{M,i}}
                  =\infty.                             \tag{FE.11}
\]
Both use the already proved actual boundary density; the second
retains its full inverse-interpolation cost.

\subsection{The entire small border operator, including its zero extension}

Let \(n=d^k\), and define
\[
 B=[U,V]:\mathbb C^{2r}\to\mathbb C^n,\quad
 K=B^*B,\quad J_0=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_r\\I_r&0\end{pmatrix},
 \quad A_0=J_0K,\quad H_0=K^{1/2}J_0K^{1/2}.
 \tag{FE.12}
\]
The symbol \(A_0\) here is a finite border operator, not the
arithmetic generator \(A\) or \(A_k\).  This distinction is
accompanied by the exact maps below.  The source's unscaled border
operator is
\[
 \mathsf J[F,E_+]^*G_M[F,E_+],\qquad
 \mathsf J=\begin{pmatrix}0&\Omega^{-1}\\\Omega^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
With \(D_\Omega=\operatorname{diag}(\Omega^{1/2},\Omega^{1/2})\),
it is exactly \(D_\Omega^{-1}A_0D_\Omega\): substitution of
(FE.7) gives
\[
 [F,E_+]^*G_M[F,E_+]=D_\Omega K D_\Omega.
\]
This states the full coordinate similarity, including zero.

Put \(N=\ker K=\ker B\) and \(\mathcal R=\operatorname{ran}K\).
They are orthogonal complements since \(K\) is Hermitian, and
\(K^{1/2}\) restricts to an invertible positive map on
\(\mathcal R\).  There is an exact sequence of spaces with
their specified operators:
\[
 0\longrightarrow(N,0)\longrightarrow(\mathbb C^{2r},A_0)
  \xrightarrow{\ K^{1/2}\ }(\mathcal R,H_0|_{\mathcal R})
  \longrightarrow0.                                    \tag{FE.13}
\]
Indeed \(K^{1/2}A_0=H_0K^{1/2}\), its kernel is \(N\),
and its image is \(\mathcal R\).  On \(N\), \(A_0=0\).
The map
\[
 Q|_{\mathcal R}=B(K^{1/2}|_{\mathcal R})^{-1},\qquad
 Q|_N=0                                                 \tag{FE.14}
\]
is a partial isometry onto \(\operatorname{ran}B\): for
\(x,y\in\mathcal R\), substitution of \(B^*B=K\)
gives \(\langle Qx,Qy\rangle=\langle x,y\rangle\).
It satisfies \(B=QK^{1/2}\) and
\[
 S_M=BJ_0B^*=QH_0Q^*,\qquad
 S_M|_{(\operatorname{ran}B)^\perp}=0.                   \tag{FE.15}
\]
Thus the restriction of \(H_0\) to \(\mathcal R\)
and that of \(S_M\) to \(\operatorname{ran}B\) are
unitarily intertwined by an explicitly proved map; both remaining
orthogonal complements and their zero actions are specified.

\begin{theorem}[Exact zero fibre of the border comparison]
Let \(Z_0=\ker(H_0|_{\mathcal R})\) and \(q_0=\dim Z_0\).
The complete generalized zero eigenspace of \(A_0\) is
\[
 \mathcal N_0=(K^{1/2})^{-1}(Z_0),\qquad A_0^2|_{\mathcal N_0}=0.
 \tag{FE.16}
\]
Define \(L=(K^{1/2}|_{\mathcal R})^{-1}:\mathcal R\to\mathcal R\).
For its restriction to \(z\in Z_0\), set
\[
 Lz=(K^{1/2}|_{\mathcal R})^{-1}z,\qquad
 N_0z=J_0K^{1/2}z\in N.
 \tag{FE.17}
\]
The map \(N_0:Z_0\to N\) is injective, and the exact vector
space decomposition \(\mathcal N_0=N\oplus LZ_0\) gives
\[
 A_0(n+Lz)=N_0z.
 \tag{FE.18}
\]
Consequently its zero Jordan data consist of exactly \(q_0\)
blocks of size two and \(\dim N-q_0\) blocks of size one.
Its nonzero Jordan blocks have size one and coincide, with full
multiplicities, with the nonzero eigenvalues of \(S_M\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If \(A_0^m x=0\), (FE.13) gives
\(H_0^mK^{1/2}x=0\).  Hermitian spectral decomposition of
\(H_0|_{\mathcal R}\) identifies its generalized zero
space with \(Z_0\), proving that \(x\) belongs to the
preimage in (FE.16).  Conversely, if \(K^{1/2}x\in Z_0\),
then \(K^{1/2}A_0x=0\), so \(A_0x\in N\) and
\(A_0^2x=0\).  This proves (FE.16) exactly.

For \(z\in Z_0\), one has
\(K^{1/2}J_0K^{1/2}z=H_0z=0\), proving \(N_0z\in N\).
Since \(J_0\) is invertible and \(K^{1/2}\) is injective
on \(\mathcal R\), \(N_0\) is injective.  Equation
\(K^{1/2}Lz=z\) gives the direct decomposition, and
\(A_0Lz=J_0K Lz=J_0K^{1/2}z\) proves (FE.18).
Choose a basis of \(Z_0\), use its injective image as part of
a basis of \(N\), and extend it in \(N\).  Each pair
\(Lz,N_0z\) is a size-two chain, and the remaining basis
vectors of \(N\) are size-one chains.  This also proves
\(q_0\le\dim N\) without a rank assumption.

For a nonzero eigenvalue \(\lambda\), take an eigenvector
\(z\in\mathcal R\) of \(H_0\).  If \(x=Lz\), then
\(n=A_0x-\lambda x\in N\), so
\(x+n/\lambda\) is an eigenvector of \(A_0\) lifting
\(z\).  The lift is unique because \(A_0\) vanishes on
\(N\) and \(\lambda\ne0\).  This supplies every
eigenvector of the Hermitian quotient and leaves no nontrivial
Jordan chain at a nonzero eigenvalue: applying (FE.13) to such
a chain would give one for the Hermitian quotient, and a residual
chain contained in \(N\) is impossible at nonzero \(\lambda\).
Finally (FE.15) identifies those eigenvalues with the actual
relative form.  All claims include their algebraic multiplicities.
\end{proof}

In particular,
\[
 \dim\ker S_M=n-\operatorname{rank}B+q_0,\quad
 \dim\ker A_0=2r-\operatorname{rank}B,
 \quad \operatorname{mult}_0(A_0)=2r-\operatorname{rank}B+q_0.
 \tag{FE.19}
\]
The first equality is (FE.15), the second uses the invertibility
of \(J_0\), and the third counts (FE.18).  Thus replacing the
source's border calculation by a Hermitian one has an exact
kernel, quotient, complement and zero extension, all explicitly
retained here.
At any fixed retained support \(\lambda\), each displayed
linear map has its Split-Zero lift
\((\lambda,x)\mapsto(\lambda,Tx)\), with external
\(\tau\mapsto\tau\).  A size-two chain in (FE.18) is
therefore the exact supported chain
\[
 (\lambda,Lz)\longmapsto(\lambda,N_0z)
                      \longmapsto(\lambda,0).
 \tag{FE.19a}
\]
The last value is the represented zero in that fibre.  For
\(z\ne0\), injectivity of \(N_0\) proves that the
intermediate value has nonzero amplitude; neither map sends
it to external absence.

\subsection{A quantified link between the frontier bound and determinant cost}

Let \(\lambda_M=\|UU^*\|_{\rm op}\), and retain the original
positive recurrence quantity
\[
 \Gamma_M=\max_{|\alpha|=M}
   \sum_{j=1}^k\{a_{\alpha_j+1}+(k-1)a_{\alpha_j}\}>0.
 \tag{FE.20}
\]
The strict inequality follows from \(a_{\alpha_j+1}>0\).
For clarity, its upper estimate uses an exact matrix comparison.
Let \(Z=[z_\alpha]_{|\alpha|=M}\),
\(\Omega_M=\operatorname{diag}(\omega_\alpha)\), and let
\(L_{\alpha\beta}=a_{\beta_j}\) for
\(\alpha=\beta-e_j\), summed over those \(j\).
Then \(E_+=ZL\), and
\(\Omega_ML\Omega^{-1}L^*\) has nonnegative entries.
Its row sum at \(\alpha\) is the displayed sum in (FE.20):
\(\omega_\alpha a_{\alpha_j+1}/\omega_{\alpha+e_j}=1\)
for each outgoing edge, and the other predecessors contribute
\(\sum_{l\ne j}a_{\alpha_l}\).  For a matrix with
nonnegative entries, an eigenvector component of largest absolute
value bounds the eigenvalue's absolute value by its largest row
sum.  The matrix is similar to the positive Hermitian matrix
\(\Omega_M^{1/2}L\Omega^{-1}L^*\Omega_M^{1/2}\), so
\[
 E_+\Omega^{-1}E_+^*\preceq
       \Gamma_M Z\Omega_M^{-1}Z^*
       \preceq\Gamma_M\mathcal C_M.
\]
It follows that \(VV^*\preceq\Gamma_MI\).
For any vector \(x\), Cauchy--Schwarz applied to (FE.8)
gives \(|x^*S_Mx|\le2\|U^*x\|\|V^*x\|\).
Thus
\[
 \epsilon_M\le2\sqrt{\Gamma_M\lambda_M}.               \tag{FE.21}
\]
Every actual eigenline survives in the full tensor family.  If
\(\delta_* =\max_{\rho\in Z}|\operatorname{Re}\rho-1/2|\),
its repeated tensor gives
\(\epsilon_M\ge2k\delta_*\).  Combining this with
(FE.9) and (FE.21) proves, at every finite degree,
\[
 \lambda_M\ge\frac{k^2\delta_*^2}{\Gamma_M},\qquad
 \frac{\det G_M}{\det G_{M+1}}\ge
       1+\frac{k^2\delta_*^2}{\Gamma_M}.
 \tag{FE.22}
\]
Indeed the determinant inverse is \(\prod_i(1+\lambda_i)\),
which is at least \(1+\lambda_M\).  For integers
\(k(d-1)\le a<b\), multiplication, followed by the increasing logarithm,
gives the explicit source constraint
\[
 \boxed{\log\frac{\det G_a}{\det G_b}\ge
    \sum_{M=a}^{b-1}\log\left(1+
                  \frac{k^2\delta_*^2}{\Gamma_M}\right).}
 \tag{FE.23}
\]
When \(\delta_*=0\), this remains a valid inequality with
zero right side and no division by a zero spectral quantity.
It is an exact necessary growth relation for the constructed
arithmetic matrices, not an assumed upper estimate on that growth.

The original derivative energy supplies a second exact growth bound
without the recurrence maximum.  By (FE.8),
\(\|Y_MG_M^{-1/2}\|_{\rm op}^2
=\lambda_M/(1+\lambda_M)\).  By (FE.9),
\(\|C_MG_M^{-1/2}\|_{\rm op}^2\le\chi_M\).
The original cross-pairing formula, the operator triangle inequality,
and submultiplicativity therefore give
\[
 \epsilon_M^2\le4\frac{\lambda_M}{1+\lambda_M}\chi_M.
 \tag{FE.24}
\]
If \(\delta_*>0\), combine it with the retained eigenline
\(\epsilon_M\ge2k\delta_*\).  The finite number
\(\lambda_M/(1+\lambda_M)\) is strictly below one, so
\[
 \chi_M>k^2\delta_*^2,\qquad
 \lambda_M\ge
       \frac{k^2\delta_*^2}{\chi_M-k^2\delta_*^2},\qquad
 \log\frac{\det G_M}{\det G_{M+1}}
       \ge-\log\left(1-\frac{k^2\delta_*^2}{\chi_M}\right).
 \tag{FE.25}
\]
Every denominator and logarithm in (FE.25) has just been proved
valid.  The middle inequality follows by rearranging (FE.24);
the last uses \(\det G_M/\det G_{M+1}\ge1+\lambda_M\).
Summing it over any \(k(d-1)\le a<b\) gives the corresponding
finite accumulated determinant inequality.  For \(\delta_*=0\),
(FE.24) remains valid, and (FE.25) is left un-divided; no
\(0/0\) value is assigned to an original zero-energy layer.


For the actual conjugation-and-reflection packets, the further exact
original-layer calculation in Section~\ref{sec:quartet-dissipation-continuation}
proves \(\epsilon_M^2\le[\lambda_M/(1+\lambda_M)]\zeta_M
\le[\lambda_M/(1+\lambda_M)]\chi_M\). Here \(\zeta_M\) is the
operator-norm square of the same derivative map whose trace cost is
\(\chi_M\). Its proof uses the two actual reflection parities in
that layer. For \(k=1\), it proves the exact equality
\(\epsilon_M^2=(1-\pi_M)\chi_M\), retaining both zero frontier
cases. Thus (FE.24)--(FE.25) retain their general hypotheses, while
(QD.8)--(QD.15) give the stronger arithmetic conclusions and all
their finite accumulated determinant bounds.
