\section{The original fibre and the auxiliary translation}
The September 13 exponential comparison starts with the actual arithmetic
polynomial and sum operator
\[
 \chi(S)=S^q+\sum_{b=0}^{q-1}c_bS^b,\quad q>0,\qquad
 E=\mathbb C[S]/(\chi),\qquad A=M_S,
\]
and the specified primitive
\[
 \Phi(S)=\frac{S^{q+1}}{q+1}
       +\sum_{b=0}^{q-1}\frac{c_bS^{b+1}}{b+1}.
\]
Its cohomology is the cokernel of
\(D_{u,t}=u\partial_S+\chi(S)-t\), with the omitted differential symbol
understood as the coefficient identification with one-forms. All formulas
below retain this original object at \(a=0\). The parameter \(a\in\mathbb C\)
defines an auxiliary comparison family; a translated polynomial is not
asserted to be a new zeta zero packet.

Set
\begin{equation}\tag{DT.1}
 \chi_a(S)=\chi(S-a),\qquad
 \Phi_a(S)=\Phi(S-a)-\Phi(-a),\qquad
 T_aP(X)=P(X+a).
\end{equation}
Thus \(\Phi_a(0)=0\), and no additive constant in the potential is lost.
Use the same substitution on coefficients of one-forms, with \(dS=dX\).

\begin{proposition}[The coefficient and connection maps]
The map \(T_a\) is an isomorphism of the two-term polynomial complexes
for \(\chi_a\) and \(\chi\), over \(\mathbb C[u,t]\). It induces an
isomorphism of their free rank-\(q\) cohomology modules. On the special
fibre \((u,t)=(0,0)\),
\begin{equation}\tag{DT.2}
 T_a:E_a=\mathbb C[S]/(\chi_a)\xrightarrow{\sim}E,
 \qquad T_aA_aT_a^{-1}=A+aI.
\end{equation}
Every primary multiplicity and nilpotent Jordan block is retained.
For \(u\ne0\), the descended parameter connections satisfy
\begin{equation}\tag{DT.3}
 T_a\nabla_t^a T_a^{-1}=\nabla_t-\frac a u I,
 \qquad \nabla_t=\partial_t-\frac{A(t)}u,
\end{equation}
where \(A(t)\) is the original companion matrix for \(\chi-t\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Substitution gives, for every polynomial \(P\),
\[
 T_a\bigl(uP'+(\chi_a-t)P\bigr)
   =u\partial_X(T_aP)+(\chi(X)-t)T_aP.
\]
The inverse is substitution by \(-a\). A polynomial of degree \(n\)
with leading coefficient \(b\ne0\) is sent by either differential to
a polynomial of degree \(n+q\) with the same leading coefficient.
Successive subtraction of such images therefore gives a unique
representative of degree less than \(q\), over the coefficient ring.
This proves the stated freeness and the base-change identification.
On the special fibre, \(T_a(SP)=(X+a)T_aP\), which proves DT.2.
In particular, \((A_a-(\lambda+a)I)^m\) is conjugate to
\((A-\lambda I)^m\) for every \(m\), proving the multiplicity statement
without discarding the nilpotent part. On arbitrary polynomial
representatives, the connection is \(\partial_t-S/u\). Since \(T_a\)
is independent of \(t\), its conjugate is
\(\partial_t-(X+a)/u\). The commutator with the differential vanishes,
so this equality descends and yields DT.3.
\end{proof}

In the fixed bases \(1,S,\ldots,S^{q-1}\) and
\(1,X,\ldots,X^{q-1}\), the matrix of \(T_a\) is
\begin{equation}\tag{DT.4}
 (C_a)_{rb}=\begin{cases}\binom br a^{b-r},&0\le r\le b<q,\\
                  0,&r>b,\end{cases}
 \qquad C_a^{-1}=C_{-a},\qquad \det C_a=1.
\end{equation}
The inverse and composition rules follow by composing the literal
substitutions. In particular,
\(C_aA_a(t)C_a^{-1}=A(t)+aI\).

\section{Periods and the retained exponential factor}
Fix \(u\ne0\), put \(d=q+1\), and use the original rays
\[
 \theta_j=\frac{\arg u+\pi}{d}+\frac{2\pi j}{d},\qquad
 \Gamma_j=-\ell_0+\ell_j,\qquad 1\le j\le q,
\]
oriented as in the exponential comparison. Let \(\Pi(u,t)\) denote its
period matrix, whose \((j,b)\) entry is
\(\int_{\Gamma_j}e^{(\Phi(X)-tX)/u}X^b\,dX\).
Let \(\Pi_a\) use the identical labelled ray prescription for \(\Phi_a\)
in the \(S\)-plane.

\begin{proposition}[Exact period translation]
Define
\begin{equation}\tag{DT.5}
 h_a(t)=-\Phi(-a)-ta,\qquad f_a(u,t)=\exp\bigl(h_a(t)/u\bigr).
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\tag{DT.6}
 \Pi_a(u,t)=f_a(u,t)\Pi(u,t)C_a.
\end{equation}
The map \(f_aT_a\) intertwines the two parameter connections in DT.3.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The substitution \(S=X+a\) gives the exact identity
\[
 \Phi_a(S)-tS=\Phi(X)-tX+h_a(t).
\]
It changes the contour to \(\Gamma_j-a\). This translated contour
may be moved to \(\Gamma_j\) without changing the integral. Indeed,
truncate the two rays at radius \(R\), join their translated endpoints
by segments of length \(|a|\), and apply Cauchy's theorem to the
resulting closed path. The entire integrand has no finite singularity.
On each joining segment its leading exponent is
\(-R^d/(d|u|)+O(R^{d-1})\), uniformly along that segment; a polynomial
factor grows at most polynomially. Its integral tends to zero.
The two portions at the finite starting point have opposite orientation
and cancel. Absolute convergence on the original and translated rays
follows from the same estimate. Finally, \((X+a)^b\) is the column
expansion DT.4. This proves DT.6 including the scalar and all phases.

Since \(\partial_tf_a=-(a/u)f_a\), for every section \(v\) one has
\[
 \nabla_t(f_aT_av)
  =f_a\left(\nabla_tT_av-\frac a uT_av\right)
  =f_aT_a\nabla_t^av.
\]
The scalar \(f_a\) is nonzero on \(u\ne0\); no extension of this
exponential gauge to \(u=0\) is used. DT.2 is the separately proved
coefficient specialization at that fibre.
\end{proof}

\section{The original positive forms and their control operators}
The period matrices used below are invertible on the stated domain
\(u\ne0\). Here is a complete verification needed for the determinant
ratios. At the monomial potential, the substitution
\(x=r^d/(d|u|)\) in the absolutely convergent ray integral gives
\[
 \Pi^{\rm mon}_{j,b}
 =\frac{(d|u|)^{(b+1)/d}}d\,
   \Gamma\!\left(\frac{b+1}{d}\right)e^{i(b+1)\theta_0}
   (\zeta^{j(b+1)}-1),\quad \zeta=e^{2\pi i/d},
\]
where \(\Gamma(s)=\int_0^\infty x^{s-1}e^{-x}\,dx>0\) for
\(s>0\). The column factors are all nonzero. A dependence among
the remaining columns would give a polynomial
\(\sum_{l=1}^q b_l(Z^l-1)\) of degree at most \(q\), vanishing
at all \(q+1\) roots of unity, including \(1\). This polynomial
is zero, so every \(b_l=0\).

Join the monomial coefficients to the given \((c,t)\) by a straight
path with real parameter \(s\in[0,1]\), keeping \(u\) fixed.
On this compact coefficient path the leading negative ray term
dominates all lower terms uniformly: for sufficiently large \(r\)
the absolute value of any fixed coefficient derivative of the
integrand is at most \(Cr^L\exp[-r^d/(2d|u|)]\).
This is integrable by the same gamma substitution; hence
differentiation under every contour integral is valid. A derivative
in \(c_b\) multiplies the integrand by
\(S^{b+1}/((b+1)u)\), and one in \(t\) multiplies it by \(-S/u\).
The unique reduction proved above, together with the vanishing of
\(\int_{\Gamma_j}e^{(\Phi-tS)/u}D_{u,t}P\,dS\), thus gives
\(\Pi'(s)=\Pi(s)B(s)\) with a continuous matrix \(B(s)\).
That integral vanishes because it is \(u\) times an exact derivative;
both infinite endpoint values are zero and the two finite endpoint
values cancel with the specified contour signs. The determinant
identity
\[
 \frac d{ds}\det\Pi
 =\Tr\bigl(\operatorname{adj}(\Pi)\Pi B\bigr)
 =(\det\Pi)\Tr B
\]
holds also at singular matrices. Therefore
\(\det\Pi(s)=\det\Pi(0)\exp(\int_0^s\Tr B(v)\,dv)\ne0\).
This proves invertibility without removing repeated-root parameters.
Equation DT.6 proves it for the translated matrix as well.

Let \(G_N\) be any one of the original canonical arithmetic Gram
matrices, and retain its index \(N\), cochain degree \(k\), and control
form \(W_N=A^*G_N+G_NA-kG_N\). The exact pullback along DT.2 is
\begin{equation}\tag{DT.7}
 G_N^{[a]}=C_a^*G_NC_a.
\end{equation}
This is a transported specified form. No claim identifies it with a
newly computed canonical theta form for a translated polynomial.

\begin{proposition}[Control and determinant transport]
For the transported form and the original value of \(k\),
\begin{align}
 W_N^{[a]}&=A_a^*G_N^{[a]}+G_N^{[a]}A_a-kG_N^{[a]}
       =C_a^*\bigl(W_N+(a+\bar a)G_N\bigr)C_a,\tag{DT.8}\\
 \Tr\bigl((G_N^{[a]})^{-1}W_N^{[a]}\bigr)
       &=\Tr(G_N^{-1}W_N)+2q\operatorname{Re}a.\tag{DT.9}
\end{align}
Write \(H_\Pi=\Pi^*\Pi\) and
\(B_N^{\mathrm{per}}=G_N^{-1}H_\Pi\). Then
\begin{equation}\tag{DT.10}
 B_N^{\mathrm{per},[a]}
  :=(G_N^{[a]})^{-1}\Pi_a^*\Pi_a
   =|f_a|^2 C_a^{-1}B_N^{\mathrm{per}}C_a.
\end{equation}
Consequently, for the same \((u,t)\) and the same original indices,
\begin{equation}\tag{DT.11}
 \frac{\det B_j^{\mathrm{per},[a]}}{\det B_i^{\mathrm{per},[a]}}
   =\frac{\det B_j^{\mathrm{per}}}{\det B_i^{\mathrm{per}}}
   =\frac{\det G_i}{\det G_j}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Insert \(C_aA_aC_a^{-1}=A+aI\) into the first definition of DT.8.
Taking the inverse Gram and using invariance of trace under similarity
gives DT.9. Equation DT.6 gives
\(\Pi_a^*\Pi_a=|f_a|^2C_a^*H_\Pi C_a\), proving DT.10.
Its determinant contains the factor \(|f_a|^{2q}\); this same factor
occurs at both indices and cancels in their ratio. The remaining period
Gram also cancels, giving DT.11. Thus the original endpoint determinant
cost is retained by this exact comparison.
\end{proof}

\section{The finite-field comparison with its scalar character}
Let \(R_0\subset\mathbb C\) be the finitely generated coefficient ring
of the original exponential construction, and adjoin \(a\). For a
specified map \(R_0[a]\to\mathbb F_Q\) of characteristic \(p>d\),
and specified \(u_0\in\mathbb F_Q^\times\), \(t_0\in\mathbb F_Q\),
reduce every displayed coefficient through that map. Put
\[
 f(X)=\frac{\Phi(X)-t_0X}{u_0},\qquad
 \gamma_a=\frac{-\Phi(-a)-t_0a}{u_0}\in\mathbb F_Q.
\]
Use the Artin--Schreier rank-one sheaf convention in which the
geometric-Frobenius trace at \(x\in\mathbb F_{Q^n}\) is
\(\psi(\Tr_{\mathbb F_{Q^n}/\mathbb F_p}f(x))\), for a fixed
nontrivial additive character \(\psi\) valued in
\(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\), \(\ell\ne p\).

\begin{proposition}[Translation and the constant character line]
For the isomorphism \(\sigma_a:X\mapsto S=X+a\),
\begin{equation}\tag{DT.12}
 \sigma_a^*\mathcal L_\psi((\Phi_a-t_0S)/u_0)
   \simeq\mathcal L_\psi(f)\otimes\mathcal L_\psi(\gamma_a).
\end{equation}
Hence compactly supported cohomology in every degree is identified
with that of \(\mathcal L_\psi(f)\), tensored with the displayed
constant character line. Each geometric-Frobenius eigenvalue is
multiplied by
\begin{equation}\tag{DT.13}
 \zeta_a=\psi(\Tr_{\mathbb F_Q/\mathbb F_p}\gamma_a).
\end{equation}
This is a root of unity, so it preserves each complex absolute value
and hence the purity weights of this finite-field realization.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The potential identity in the period proof holds after the specified
coefficient reduction. The tensor identity of Artin--Schreier character
sheaves can be obtained directly from their covers: if
\(y_1^p-y_1=f\) and \(y_2^p-y_2=\gamma_a\), then
\((y_1+y_2)^p-(y_1+y_2)=f+\gamma_a\). The addition map identifies
the contracted product of the two \(\mathbb F_p\)-torsors with the
third torsor. On the character lines, the addition action becomes the
product of their characters, which gives DT.12. Pullback by the
base-defined isomorphism \(\sigma_a\) preserves compact supports
and commutes with Frobenius. Tensoring with a line pulled back from
the base tensors the compact-support complex with that line: this
can be checked on a cochain resolution before taking cohomology.
Our stated trace convention makes its Frobenius scalar DT.13.
The additive group of \(\mathbb F_p\) has exponent \(p\), so
\(\zeta_a^p=1\), proving the absolute-value assertion.
\end{proof}

These formulas compose with the exponential-family application of
Deligne, \emph{La conjecture de Weil II}, \S\S3.7.2--3.7.4, to the
degree-\(d\) polynomial with nonzero leading coefficient. The source
for that application, including the boundary chart and its hypotheses,
is retained in the exponential comparison note; the present proof
establishes the additional translation maps themselves.

\section{Specialization and split support}
The polynomial chain isomorphism \(T_a\) is defined before either
complex or finite-field realization. Its coefficient map, the
specialization \(u=t=0\), and the inclusion of the original fibre
\(a=0\) induce maps under the same split-zero functor \(G\).
For a ring map \(\varphi:B\to B'\), this functor acts by
\[
 G(\varphi)(\tau)=\tau,\qquad
 G(\varphi)(b^\bullet)=\varphi(b)^\bullet.
\]
Therefore a supported coefficient specialized to zero maps to
\(e=0^\bullet\), and an absent coefficient maps to \(\tau\).
The square with the amplitude maps \(p_B,p_{B'}\) commutes, since
both composites take \(\tau\) to zero and \(b^\bullet\) to
\(\varphi(b)\). On each already specified receiving module fibre,
the coefficient chain relation specializes to the relation
\(\chi P=0\) without changing its label. The functorial map thus
retains both the supported kernel and external absence.

The translation formulas specify exactly how the two realizations
respond to the same coefficient operation: DT.2 shifts the arithmetic
sum operator, DT.9 changes its metric trace by \(2q\operatorname{Re}a\),
DT.10 retains the analytic exponential scalar, and DT.13 supplies the
finite-field character scalar. These are complete comparison maps.
They do not identify a finite-field Frobenius with \(A\), and they
leave the original theta metric estimate as the same determinant
quantity in DT.11. The original arithmetic construction remains
the specified fibre \(a=0\) throughout.
