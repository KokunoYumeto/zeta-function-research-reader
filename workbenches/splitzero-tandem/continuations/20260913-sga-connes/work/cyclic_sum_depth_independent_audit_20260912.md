## 1. Original local ring and exact monomial survival

Retain \(h(s)=\prod_{\rho\in Z}(s-\rho)^{m_\rho}\), with every original complete order \(m_\rho\ge1\), \(d=\deg h\), \(k\ge1\), \(r\ge1\), and

\[
 P_k=\mathbb C[s_1,\ldots,s_k],\qquad
 I_k=(h(s_1),\ldots,h(s_k)),\qquad B_{k,r}=P_k/I_k^r,
 \qquad S=\sum_i s_i.
\]

For a retained ordered root tuple \(\boldsymbol\rho=(\rho_{i_1},\ldots,\rho_{i_k})\), write the exact translated coordinates \(z_j=s_j-\rho_{i_j}\), \(m_j=m_{\rho_{i_j}}\), and

\[
 h(\rho_{i_j}+z_j)=u_j(z_j)z_j^{m_j},\qquad u_j(0)\ne0.
\]

In the local algebra every \(u_j\) is invertible. Thus the original local ideal is exactly \(J=(z_1^{m_1},\ldots,z_k^{m_k})\), and the original power is exactly \(J^r\). This is multiplication of ideal generators by the displayed units; it leaves \(S=\sum_j\rho_{i_j}+\sum_jz_j\) unchanged.

The ideal \(J^r\) is generated by \(z_1^{m_1a_1}\cdots z_k^{m_ka_k}\) with \(\sum_j a_j=r\). A monomial \(z^\alpha\) belongs to it exactly when one can choose integers \(0\le a_j\le\lfloor\alpha_j/m_j\rfloor\) with sum \(r\). Such a choice exists exactly when \(\sum_j\lfloor\alpha_j/m_j\rfloor\ge r\): necessity is immediate, and sufficiency follows by assigning the required \(r\) blocks successively up to each available capacity. Since this is a monomial ideal, the complementary monomials form a linearly independent quotient basis. Therefore

\[
 z^\alpha\ne0\pmod{J^r}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sum_j\left\lfloor\frac{\alpha_j}{m_j}\right\rfloor\le r-1.
\]

There is no additional relation between surviving monomials. Write uniquely \(\alpha_j=m_ja_j+b_j\), \(0\le b_j<m_j\). The full local dimension is consequently

\[
 \left(\prod_jm_j\right)
 \#\{\mathbf a\in\mathbb N^k:\sum_j a_j\le r-1\}
 =\left(\prod_jm_j\right)\binom{k+r-1}{k}.
\]

This also makes the localization precise: each \(z_j^{m_jr}\) is zero, so every polynomial with nonzero constant term has its finite geometric-series inverse. The localized quotient equals the displayed finite monomial quotient.

The original global algebra is the product of these ordered local algebras. Indeed \(h(s_j)^r\in I_k^r\) makes the quotient finite-dimensional with exactly these maximal ideals; distinct point ideals have comaximal powers, giving their full Chinese-remainder factors. Summing the local dimensions gives
\(\dim B_{k,r}=d^k\binom{k+r-1}{k}\).

## 2. Exact nilpotence and collided sums

Put \(M_{\boldsymbol\rho}=\max_jm_j\) and
\(D_{\boldsymbol\rho,r}=\sum_j(m_j-1)+(r-1)M_{\boldsymbol\rho}\).
For a surviving exponent vector,

\[

 \le\sum_j(m_j-1)+M_{\boldsymbol\rho}(r-1).
\]

Equality is attained by taking every \(b_j=m_j-1\) and assigning all \(r-1\) blocks to one index where \(m_j=M_{\boldsymbol\rho}\). Thus \(D_{\boldsymbol\rho,r}\) is exactly the largest surviving degree.

Let \(X=S-\lambda_{\boldsymbol\rho}=\sum_jz_j\), where \(\lambda_{\boldsymbol\rho}=\sum_j\rho_{i_j}\). Every term of \(X^{D_{\boldsymbol\rho,r}+1}\) belongs to \(J^r\). Hence the exact nilpotence index is

\[
 L_{\boldsymbol\rho,r}
 =1+\sum_j(m_j-1)+(r-1)\max_jm_j.
\]

In particular the powers \(1,X,\ldots,X^{L_{\boldsymbol\rho,r}-1}\) are independent: a linear relation with least nonzero power \(q\) would imply \(X^q\) times a unit is zero, contradicting the exact index.

\[

These are precisely the exponents of the minimal polynomial. The same polynomial is the minimal polynomial of multiplication by \(S\), because \(f(M_S)1=f(S)\), and vanishing of the latter is equivalent to vanishing of the multiplication operator. The symmetric group acts simultaneously on the variables and on the conormal indices. An invariant tuple \((a_i)\in E^k\) is determined by \(a_1\), and \(a_1\) is fixed by the stabilizer \(S_{k-1}\) of the first index. Conversely, for such a first component choose any permutation \(\sigma_i\) taking \(1\) to \(i\) and set \(a_i=\sigma_i a_1\). Different choices differ by that stabilizer, so the formula is independent of the choices and produces an invariant tuple. These inverse maps prove

\[
 N^{S_k}\cong A_h\otimes(A_h^{\otimes(k-1)})^{S_{k-1}}.
\]

Take any basis of \(A_h\) of cardinality \(d\). Unscaled sums over permutation orbits of its tensor basis form a basis of \((A_h^{\otimes n})^{S_n}\); the orbits are indexed by \(d\)-component occupation vectors summing to \(n\). Their number is \(\binom{d+n-1}{n}\). For \(n=0\), the tensor power and its invariant space are \(\mathbb C\). This proves

\[
 \dim N^{S_k}=d\binom{d+k-2}{k-1},\qquad
 \dim E^{S_k}=\binom{d+k-1}{k}.
\]

Under the first-component identification, the sum conormal map is exactly

\[
 a_1\longmapsto
 \frac1k\sum_{i=1}^k\sigma_i(c_1a_1)
 =\frac1{k!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_k}\sigma(c_1a_1).
\]

The equality follows because \(c_1a_1\) is fixed by \(S_{k-1}\), so each coset contribution repeats \((k-1)!\) times. This preserves both the original \(1/k\) contraction and the literal Reynolds factor.

The cokernel of the full conormal row is
\(T^{\otimes k}\), \(T=\mathbb C[s]/(h,h')\), \(\dim T=b\), as in CW. Taking invariants is exact: for an invariant target in an image, average any preimage over \(S_k\), and equivariance preserves its image. Therefore the invariant conormal cokernel is \((T^{\otimes k})^{S_k}\), of dimension \(\binom{b+k-1}{k}\) when \(b>0\), and zero when \(b=0\). Subtraction proves exactly

\[
 \operatorname{rank}\partial_N^{S_k}
 =\binom{d+k-1}{k}-\binom{b+k-1}{k},
\]

\[
 \dim\ker\partial_N^{S_k}
 =d\binom{d+k-2}{k-1}
  -\binom{d+k-1}{k}+\binom{b+k-1}{k}.
\]

The source's convention that the final binomial is zero for \(b=0\) is correct. In the declared fixture \(h=s^2,k=2\), these give domain dimension \(4\), target dimension \(3\), cokernel dimension \(1\), rank \(2\), and kernel dimension \(2\).

The same first-component map explicitly verifies the invariant all-jet kernel if needed: it is
\(\operatorname{Ann}_{A_h}(h')\otimes(A_h^{\otimes(k-1)})^{S_{k-1}}\),
of dimension \(b\binom{d+k-2}{k-1}\). Thus the all-jet and sum-jet calculations restrict through the same simultaneous group action and exact averaging map, without suppressing relative conormal coordinates.

### D. Arithmetic unit, product Jacobian and zero endpoints
