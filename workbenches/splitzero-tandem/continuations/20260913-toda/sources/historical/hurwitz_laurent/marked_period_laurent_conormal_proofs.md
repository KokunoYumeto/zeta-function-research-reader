**Theorem 25 (prolate-compatible non-selfadjoint radial deformation).**  
Let

$$
A=A^*
$$

be an operator on $\mathbb C^{N+1}$ in a Fourier-indexed basis. Define

$$
D_\nu
=
\operatorname{diag}
(1,e^\nu,\ldots,e^{N\nu})
$$

and

$$
A_\nu
=
D_\nu A D_\nu^{-1}.
$$

Then $A_\nu$ is similar to $A$, so its spectrum is unchanged. Its selfadjointness defect is

$$
\boxed{
A_\nu-A_\nu^*
=
D_\nu^{-1}
[D_\nu^2,A]
D_\nu^{-1}.
}
$$

For $\nu\neq0$, if $A_{jk}\neq0$ for some $j\neq k$, then $A_\nu$ is not selfadjoint. Replacing $\nu$ by $i\theta$ gives a unitary conjugation and preserves selfadjointness.

**Proof.**  
For real $\nu$,

$$
D_\nu^*=D_\nu,
$$

and therefore

$$
A_\nu^*
=
D_\nu^{-1}AD_\nu.
$$

Hence

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_\nu-A_\nu^*
&=
D_\nu AD_\nu^{-1}
-
D_\nu^{-1}AD_\nu\\
&=
D_\nu^{-1}
\left(
D_\nu^2A-AD_\nu^2
\right)
D_\nu^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The $(j,k)$-entry of the commutator contains

$$
\left(
e^{2j\nu}-e^{2k\nu}
\right)A_{jk}.
$$

For $j\neq k$, $\nu\neq0$, and $A_{jk}\neq0$, this is nonzero.

*Reflections.*  
The common object is not the integer $1$ viewed three times. It is the Laurent algebra of a marked rank-one period lattice. Terzo’s relative exponential relation is the image of the Laurent augmentation $U-1$. The Connes-Consani circle $C_p$ supplies the same lattice through its oriented fundamental group and Frobenius monodromy. A finite character packet gives a positive corank-one moment matrix, and the relation polynomial is exactly its kernel line. The logarithmic modulus of the character is simultaneously the Toeplitz defect, the root-radius defect, the normalized Ford-horocycle coordinate, and the conormal coordinate of the adelic lamination. The tangential part changes phase while remaining unitary; the radial part preserves positivity and corank but breaks Toeplitzness, Laurent $*$-stability, Ford tangency, and selfadjointness of the corresponding positive-diagonal similarity. The Schanuel-RH problem is thereby reduced to a single nontrivial naturality theorem rather than an analogy between relation count, corank, and codimension.
