\section{Which completion retains the arithmetic jets?}
\label{sec:jet-topology}
This section extends the Hilbert/jet calculation of the September 12
orthogonal-boundary note. All changes of topology are specified maps
on the same arithmetic functions. The theta relation space, the operator
\(D=-x\partial_x\), and \(g=2\xi\) are retained.

\subsection{Logarithmic coordinates and the original measure}
Let \(\mathscr B,V,\Theta\) have the definitions in
Section~\ref{sec:arithmetic-input}. Put \(\mathscr H=L^2(\mathbb R_+,dx)\).
The map
\[
 \mathcal LF(y)=e^{y/2}F(e^y),\qquad
 \mathcal L^{-1}f(x)=x^{-1/2}f(\log x)
\]
is unitary from \(\mathscr H\) to \(L^2(\mathbb R,dy)\), since
\(dx=e^y\,dy\). Direct differentiation gives
\[
 \mathcal L D\mathcal L^{-1}=\tfrac12-\partial_y,\qquad
 \mathcal MF(\rho)=\int_{\mathbb R}(\mathcal LF)(y)e^{(\rho-1/2)y}\,dy .
 \tag{JT1}
\]
On \(\mathscr H\), \(D\) denotes the closed maximal distributional
operator which \(\mathcal L\) identifies with
\(1/2-\partial_y\) on the Sobolev domain \(H^1(\mathbb R)\).
For an integer \(r\geq0\), let \(\mathscr H^{(r)}\) be the domain of
the first \(r\) powers of \(D\) in \(\mathscr H\), with norm
\(\|F\|_r^2=\sum_{k=0}^r\|D^kF\|_{\mathscr H}^2\).
The Mellin-line transform makes this the norm with density
\(w_r(t)=\sum_{k=0}^r|1/2+it|^{2k}\) relative to \(dt/(2\pi)\).

\begin{theorem}[Finite differential graph norms and the complete jet graph]
\label{thm:jet-graph}
Let \(Z\) be a nonempty finite packet of actual nontrivial zeros with full
multiplicities, \(E_Z=\mathbb C[t]/h_Z\), and \(J_Z:\mathscr B\to E_Z\)
the complete Mellin-jet map. For every finite \(r\),
\[
 \overline{\operatorname{span}\{D^jf_0:j\geq0\}}^{\,\mathscr H^{(r)}}
       =\mathscr H^{(r)},\quad f_0=\Theta\phi_*,
 \tag{JT2}
\]
\[
 \overline{\{(F,J_ZF):F\in\mathscr B\}}^{\,\mathscr H^{(r)}\oplus E_Z}
       =\mathscr H^{(r)}\oplus E_Z .
 \tag{JT3}
\]
Thus \(J_Z\) is not closable in this norm. The completed theta-boundary
graph is \(\mathscr H^{(r)}\oplus0\); its quotient in (JT3) is \(E_Z\)
through \([(F,u)]\mapsto u\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each \(0<c<\pi/4\), the actual source gives
\(|g(1/2+it)|\leq C_c e^{-c|t|}\).
Indeed \(e^{z/2}f_0(e^z)\) is holomorphic in
\(|\operatorname{Im}z|<\pi/4\): its Gaussian series and derivatives
converge uniformly on compact subsets since
\(\operatorname{Re}(e^{2z})>0\). On smaller horizontal strips it decays
faster than every exponential at the positive end.
Poisson gives \(Jf_0=f_0\), so analytic continuation gives
\(e^{z/2}f_0(e^z)=e^{-z/2}f_0(e^{-z})\); this controls the negative end.
Shift the Fourier contour to height \(c\) or \(-c\).
The vertical integrals vanish by that decay, proving the bound.

The measure \(d\mu_r=w_r(t)|g(1/2+it)|^2dt/(2\pi)\) has a positive
exponential moment. If \(u\in L^2(\mu_r)\) is orthogonal to every
polynomial, \(d\nu=\overline u\,d\mu_r\) is a finite complex measure
with a smaller positive exponential moment, by Cauchy--Schwarz.
Its Fourier transform is analytic in a strip, and every derivative
at zero vanishes. Analytic continuation makes it zero there.
Fourier uniqueness gives \(\nu=0\), hence \(u=0\).
For this uniqueness step, convolve with Gaussian approximate
identities: each convolution is in \(L^2\), since
\(\|\nu*\gamma_\epsilon\|_2\leq
\|\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\|\gamma_\epsilon\|_2\);
its transform is zero, so Plancherel makes it zero, and
weak convergence then gives \(\nu=0\).
Polynomials are therefore dense in \(L^2(\mu_r)\).
Multiplication by \(g(1/2+it)\) is an isometry onto
\(L^2(w_r\,dt/(2\pi))\): divide almost everywhere, since this nonzero
analytic function vanishes on a discrete, measure-zero set.
The Mellin transform of \(D^jf_0\) is
\((1/2+it)^jg(1/2+it)\), proving (JT2).

Every theta polynomial has zero \(J_Z\)-jet. Thus the closure in (JT3)
contains \((h,0)\) for every \(h\). The actual packet section
\(R_{\mathrm{ref}}:E_Z\to\mathscr B\) satisfies \(J_ZR_{\mathrm{ref}}=1\).
Approximate \(R_{\mathrm{ref}}u\) in \(\mathscr H^{(r)}\) by theta polynomials
\(p_n(D)f_0\). Then
\((R_{\mathrm{ref}}u-p_n(D)f_0,u)\to(0,u)\).
This proves (JT3). For \(u\neq0\) it prevents closability.
The boundary and quotient statements follow from the coordinate
projections.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Differential on the completed graph]
The continuous map
\[
 (D,A):\mathscr H^{(r+1)}\oplus E_Z\longrightarrow
           \mathscr H^{(r)}\oplus E_Z,\quad(F,u)\mapsto(DF,Au)
 \tag{JT4}
\]
extends the original \((F,J_ZF)\mapsto(DF,J_ZDF)\).
On the quotient by the boundary closure it is exactly \(A=M_t\).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The norm definitions bound \(D:\mathscr H^{(r+1)}\to\mathscr H^{(r)}\);
the jet identity \(J_ZD=AJ_Z\) follows by retaining the complete
Taylor polynomial of \(s\mathcal MF(s)\). Apply (JT3).
\end{proof}
Its split observation records \((e_{\mathscr H},u^\bullet)\) when the
Hilbert component tends to its supported zero and the jet survives.
The projections compare this point to \(e_{\mathscr H}\) and to
\(u^\bullet\); they do not identify it with \((\tau,\tau)\).

\subsection{Exponential weights and exact jet norms}
For \(a\geq0\), retain the original functions and introduce
\[
 \mathscr H_a=L^2(\mathbb R_+,e^{2a|\log x|}\,dx),\qquad
 \|F\|_a^2=\int_{\mathbb R}|\mathcal LF(y)|^2e^{2a|y|}\,dy.
 \tag{JT5}
\]
The identity inclusions \(\mathscr B\hookrightarrow\mathscr H_a
\hookrightarrow\mathscr H_0\) are continuous. Source seminorms
with powers exceeding \(a+1/2\) control both ends. Smooth functions
compactly supported in log coordinates prove density.

\begin{theorem}[Exact threshold, including the boundary]
\label{thm:weighted-jet}
Put \(\ell_{\rho,k}(F)=(\mathcal MF)^{(k)}(\rho)/k!\) and
\(b=\operatorname{Re}\rho-1/2\), with \(k\geq0\).
The functional on \(\mathscr B\) extends continuously to \(\mathscr H_a\)
if and only if \(|b|<a\). Its exact squared norm is
\[
 \|\ell_{\rho,k}\|^2
 =\frac{(2k)!}{(k!)^2}
 \left(\frac1{[2(a-b)]^{2k+1}}+\frac1{[2(a+b)]^{2k+1}}\right).
 \tag{JT6}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Inner products are conjugate-linear in their first argument.
The Riesz vector in log coordinates must be
\[
 r_{\rho,k}(y)=\frac{y^k}{k!}
      e^{(\overline\rho-1/2)y}e^{-2a|y|}.
\]
Its norm squared is \((k!)^{-2}\int y^{2k}e^{2by-2a|y|}\,dy\).
The two half-lines converge exactly when \(|b|<a\);
their gamma integrals give (JT6).
If \(|b|\geq a\), testing any putative Riesz representative against
compactly supported smooth functions forces it to be this same
non-square-integrable density, a contradiction.
The equality case is included: one end has no exponential decay.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Complete confluent Cauchy Gram]
For distinct centres \(\rho_i\) with
\(|\operatorname{Re}\rho_i-1/2|<a\) and orders \(0\leq k<m_i\),
let \(J_a:\mathscr H_a\to\mathbb C^d\) be their Taylor-jet map.
In the original Taylor coordinates its Gram \(C_a=J_aJ_a^*\) is
\[
 (C_a)_{(i,k),(j,l)}
 =\frac{(k+l)!}{k!\,l!}
 \left(\frac1{(2a-z_{ij})^{k+l+1}}+
       \frac{(-1)^{k+l}}{(2a+z_{ij})^{k+l+1}}\right),
 \quad z_{ij}=\rho_i+\overline\rho_j-1 .
 \tag{JT7}
\]
It is positive definite. The exact right inverse
\(S_a=J_a^*C_a^{-1}\) satisfies
\[
 J_aS_a=1,\quad \|S_au\|_a^2=u^*C_a^{-1}u,\quad
 \|F\|_a^2=\|(1-S_aJ_a)F\|_a^2+(J_aF)^*C_a^{-1}(J_aF).
 \tag{JT8}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The entry is \(\langle r_{\rho_i,k},r_{\rho_j,l}\rangle_a\);
integrating \(y^{k+l}e^{z_{ij}y-2a|y|}/(k!l!)\)
on both half-lines proves (JT7) with its stated sign.
The vectors are independent. After removing their common nonzero
factor, a dependence is an exponential polynomial
\(\sum_iP_i(y)e^{\overline\rho_i y}=0\).
For each \(i\), apply
\(\prod_{j\neq i}(\partial_y-\overline\rho_j)^{m_j}\).
On the \(i\)-th polynomial factor every nonzero constant plus
derivative is invertible on the finite-degree polynomial space;
hence \(P_i=0\). Thus \(C_a>0\).
Now \(S_aJ_a\) is the orthogonal projection onto
\(\operatorname{range}J_a^*=(\ker J_a)^\perp\).
Multiplying the specified matrices proves all of (JT8).
\end{proof}

For an actual packet the unchanged theta subspace lies in \(\ker J_a\).
Consequently there is an onto continuous quotient map
\[
 \mathscr H_a/\overline{\Theta V}^{\,\mathscr H_a}
   \longrightarrow\mathbb C^d,\quad [F]\mapsto J_aF,
 \qquad\ker=(\ker J_a)/\overline{\Theta V}^{\,\mathscr H_a}.
 \tag{JT9}
\]
Its surjectivity follows from (JT8). Define the retained coordinate map
\[
 T:E_Z\longrightarrow\mathbb C^d,\qquad
 T[p]=\bigl(p^{(k)}(\rho_i)/k!\bigr)_{i,k}.
\]
Then \(J_a|_{\mathscr B}=TJ_Z\), and the exact representative difference is
\(R_{\mathrm{ref}}T^{-1}-S_a:\mathbb C^d\to\ker J_a\).
Here \(R_{\mathrm{ref}}\) is the original source map; \(S_a\) is a Hilbert map, with
no claim it lies in \(\mathscr B\).
In \(E_Z\) coordinates the observation Gram is
\(C_E=T^{-1}C_a(T^{-1})^*\) and the right inverse is \(S_aT\).

\subsection{The energy term carried by the weighted topology}
\begin{theorem}[Weighted boundary identity on the actual representatives]
\label{thm:weighted-energy}
On \(\mathscr B\), with \((\Sigma H)(x)=\operatorname{sgn}(\log x)H(x)\),
\[
 \langle DF,H\rangle_a+\langle F,DH\rangle_a-\langle F,H\rangle_a
             =2a\langle F,\Sigma H\rangle_a .
 \tag{JT10}
\]
For an actual packet representative \(R:E_Z\to\mathscr B\) with
\(DR-RA=B=\Theta k\), put \(G_a=R^*R\), \(T_a=R^*\Sigma R\).
Adjoints use exactly \(\mathscr H_a\). Then
\[
 A^*G_a+G_aA-G_a=2aT_a-(B^*R+R^*B),\qquad -G_a\leq T_a\leq G_a.
 \tag{JT11}
\]
For \(Au=\rho u\neq0\),
\[
 2\operatorname{Re}\rho-1
 =2a\frac{u^*T_au}{u^*G_au}
        -2\operatorname{Re}\frac{\langle Ru,Bu\rangle_a}{\|Ru\|_a^2}.
 \tag{JT12}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In log coordinates integrate
\(-(\overline f h)'e^{2a|y|}\) on the two half-lines.
Source decay kills the terms at infinity; those at zero cancel
because the weight is continuous there. The weak derivative of the
weight is \(2a\operatorname{sgn}(y)e^{2a|y|}\).
This proves (JT10) with its positive sign.
Substitute \(DR=RA+B\) and move both boundary terms to obtain (JT11).
Multiplication by sign is a self-adjoint contraction, proving the
form bounds. Evaluation on \(u\) proves (JT12).
\end{proof}
At \(a=0\), (JT11) is the source boundary-control formula.
At \(a>0\), the topology making a jet continuous has its explicitly
retained energy term \(2aT_a\). Off-line jets cannot remain continuous
as \(a\downarrow0\). For line-centred jets, all \(a>0\) work but
(JT6) diverges as \(a\downarrow0\). These are exact comparisons on the
same Mellin map and differential, without a weight theorem assumed.

\subsection{The tesserine coordinate in the actual weight form}
Retain \(c_{-+}=4(\beta-1/2)\), \(c_{--}=4i\gamma\) for
\(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\). For \(p>1\) and \(|\psi|=1\),
\[
 z=\psi\exp\!\left(\frac{\log p}{4}(c_{-+}+c_{--})\right),\qquad
 \frac{u^*(A^*G+GA-G)u}{u^*Gu}=\frac{c_{-+}}2,\qquad
 |b|<a\ \Longleftrightarrow\ |c_{-+}|<4a .
 \tag{JT13}
\]
The first equality is exactly \(z=\psi p^{\rho-1/2}\);
the second is evaluation on \(Au=\rho u\).
Thus the original sector, local holonomy radius, arithmetic weight form
and jet-continuity strip have specified comparison maps.
The scalar evaluations do not replace the original matrix or its
support-labelled source.
