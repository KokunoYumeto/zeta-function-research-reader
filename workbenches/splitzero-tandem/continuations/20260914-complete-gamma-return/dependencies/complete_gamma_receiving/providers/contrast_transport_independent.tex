\section{Exact projector contrast under the original bulk density transport}

Keep the original coordinate $S=c+iy$ and a monic polynomial
$f(S)=\sum_{a=0}^{l}f_aS^a$ of degree $l\geq1$, with $f_l=1$.
For integers $r\geq l$, put
\[
 \mathcal V=\mathbb C[S]_{\leq r+l-1},\qquad
 P=\mathbb C[S]_{\leq r-1},\qquad F=fP,
 \qquad n=\dim\mathcal V=r+l.
 \tag{CTI1}
\]
The convention $\mathbb C[S]_{\leq-1}=\{0\}$ is retained.
Use the increasing original monomial coefficient frames everywhere.
The two inclusion matrices $E,T_f:\mathbb C^r\to\mathbb C^n$ are
\[
 E_{ij}=\begin{cases}1&i=j,\\0&i\ne j,\end{cases}
 \qquad (T_f)_{ij}=f_{i-j},
 \quad 0\leq i<n,\quad0\leq j<r,
 \tag{CTI2}
\]
where $f_a=0$ outside $0\leq a\leq l$. Thus $E$ is the
literal coefficient inclusion and $T_f$ is multiplication by the
entire original $f$, including every lower coefficient.
Both maps are injective, the latter by cancellation in $\mathbb C[S]$.
Degree additivity for a nonzero product gives exactly
\[
 P\cap F=f\mathbb C[S]_{\leq r-l-1}=:K,\qquad
 \dim K=r-l,\qquad P+F=\mathcal V.
 \tag{CTI3}
\]
Indeed $fQ\in P$ is equivalent to $\deg Q\leq r-l-1$.
The sum then has dimension $r+r-(r-l)=r+l$ and is already
contained in $\mathcal V$. At $r=l$, $K=\{0\}$.

\subsection{The full spectrum in a specified original positive form}

Let $M$ be the coefficient Gram of a positive Hermitian form on
$\mathcal V$. Define
\[
 G_P=E^*ME,\quad G_F=T_f^*MT_f,\qquad
 \Pi_P=E G_P^{-1}E^*M,\quad
 \Pi_F=T_f G_F^{-1}T_f^*M,\quad D=\Pi_F-\Pi_P.
 \tag{CTI4}
\]
Injectivity of the two inclusions makes both Grams positive definite.
For either inclusion $T$, direct multiplication gives
\[
 (T(T^*MT)^{-1}T^*M)^2=T(T^*MT)^{-1}T^*M,
 \qquad \Pi_T^*M=M\Pi_T,
 \tag{CTI5}
\]
and $T^*M(x-\Pi_Tx)=0$. These are the actual orthogonal
projections onto their displayed polynomial subspaces. Cyclicity of
trace, including the rectangular factors, gives
$\operatorname{Tr}\Pi_P=\operatorname{Tr}\Pi_F=r$ and
$\operatorname{Tr}D=0$.

The exact kernel of $D$ is $K$. To prove the converse inclusion,
suppose $Dx=0$ and write $z=\Pi_Px=\Pi_Fx$. Then $z\in K$,
while $x-z$ is orthogonal to both $P$ and $F$. By CTI3 it is
orthogonal to all of $\mathcal V$, so it is zero. Therefore
\[
 \ker D=K,\qquad \operatorname{rank}D=2l.
 \tag{CTI6}
\]
This conclusion holds for every positive original form $M$; it does
not require an asymptotic statement about its entries.

For every $x\in\mathcal V$,
\[
 \langle x,Dx\rangle_M
   =\|\Pi_Fx\|_M^2-\|\Pi_Px\|_M^2,
 \qquad -\|x\|_M^2\leq\langle x,Dx\rangle_M\leq\|x\|_M^2.
 \tag{CTI7}
\]
For nonzero $x\in P\cap K^{\perp_M}$, the same expression is
$-\|(I-\Pi_F)x\|_M^2<0$: equality would imply $x\in P\cap F=K$.
For nonzero $x\in F\cap K^{\perp_M}$ it is
$\|(I-\Pi_P)x\|_M^2>0$. Both of these subspaces have dimension $l$.

For explicit spectral transport, form the positive square root
$W=M^{1/2}$ in these same coefficient coordinates. Diagonalizing
the positive Hermitian matrix $M$ and taking its positive scalar
square roots constructs $W$, $W^*W=M$, and both inverse laws.
The matrix $WDW^{-1}$ is Hermitian by CTI5. An orthonormal
eigenbasis exists by induction: an eigenvector has a real eigenvalue
from its Hermitian quadratic form, and its orthogonal complement is
invariant. This construction gives an $M$-orthonormal eigenbasis
for the original $D$.

The strictly negative $l$-dimensional subspace just constructed
forces at least $l$ negative eigenvalues: otherwise it intersects
the nonnegative spectral subspace nontrivially, contradicting
strict negativity. The positive argument is identical. Together
with CTI6 and CTI7 this proves the exact inertia and bounds
\[
 \#\{d_i>0\}=l,\quad \#\{d_i<0\}=l,
 \quad\#\{d_i=0\}=r-l,
 \qquad -1\leq d_i\leq1.
 \tag{CTI8}
\]
Write $|D|_M=W^{-1}|WDW^{-1}|W$, where the absolute value of
the Hermitian matrix is constructed by its absolute eigenvalues.
This specifies the metric meaning of the trace norm. Put
\[
 t_M=\sum_{d_i>0}d_i=-\sum_{d_i<0}d_i
     =\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}|D|_M\leq l,
 \qquad \operatorname{Tr}|D|_M\leq2l,
 \quad 0\leq\operatorname{Tr}D^2\leq2l.
 \tag{CTI9}
\]
The equality of positive and negative sums is the already proved
zero trace. No unweighted singular-value norm of the original
possibly non-Hermitian coefficient matrix is substituted here.

\subsection{A density derivative tested against that exact contrast}

Take a compact union $B$ of nondegenerate intervals of the real
$y$ line and a finite measure $d\mu_0=w_0(y)\,dy$ with
$w_0>0$ almost everywhere on $B$. Let $\psi$ be a bounded real
measurable function on $B$, and construct, for real $s$,
\[
 d\mu_s=e^{s\psi}\,d\mu_0,\quad
 M(s)_{ij}=\int_B\overline{(c+iy)^i}(c+iy)^j\,d\mu_s,
 \quad 0\leq i,j<n.
 \tag{CTI10}
\]
This is a construction on the fixed original polynomial space.
Every entry is finite: the monomials are bounded on the compact
set and $e^{s\psi}$ is bounded there for each fixed $s$.
Every Gram is positive definite: a nonzero polynomial has only
finitely many zeros on the line, so its squared modulus has
strictly positive integral against this positive density.
Finite mass alone, without this support property, would not
justify positivity; the specified interval density proves it.

Differentiation under each integral is valid. On every bounded
$s$ interval the absolute derivative is bounded by a fixed constant
times $w_0(y)$, since both $\psi$ and the monomials are bounded.
Thus, with the original derivative form denoted $M'(s)$,
\[
 M'(s)_{ij}=\int_B\overline{(c+iy)^i}(c+iy)^j\psi(y)\,d\mu_s,
 \qquad A(s)=M(s)^{-1}M'(s),\qquad A(s)^*M(s)=M(s)A(s).
 \tag{CTI11}
\]
Let $a=\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_B\psi$,
$b=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_B\psi$ with respect to $\mu_0$.
These essential bounds are unchanged by $\mu_s$ because its
density relative to $\mu_0$ is everywhere positive. For every
coefficient vector $x$ the integral gives
\[
 a\,x^*M(s)x\leq x^*M'(s)x\leq b\,x^*M(s)x,
 \qquad aI\leq_{M(s)}A(s)\leq_{M(s)}bI.
 \tag{CTI12}
\]
Equivalently, in the actual $L^2(B,\mu_s)$ realization, $A(s)$
is the compression of multiplication by $\psi$ to $\mathcal V$.
For this exact identification, the orthogonal coefficient projection
of a function $g$ has coefficient column $M(s)^{-1}v$, where
$v_i=\int_B\overline{(c+iy)^i}g(y)\,d\mu_s$. Apply it to
$g(y)=\psi(y)\sum_jx_j(c+iy)^j$ to obtain $A(s)x$.

Let $D(s)$ be CTI4 for this same $M(s)$. In its constructed
$M(s)$-orthonormal eigenbasis, put
$\alpha_i=\langle v_i,A(s)v_i\rangle_{M(s)}\in[a,b]$.
Cyclicity of trace and diagonalization of $D(s)$ give
$\operatorname{Tr}(D(s)A(s))=\sum_i d_i\alpha_i$.
The positive terms are at most $bt_{M(s)}$, and the negative
terms are at most $-at_{M(s)}$; interchanging the bounds gives
the lower estimate. Hence the sharp contrast-dependent bound is
\[
 \left|\operatorname{Tr}(D(s)A(s))\right|
 \leq t_{M(s)}(b-a)
 =\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}|D(s)|_{M(s)}\,\operatorname{osc}_B\psi
 \leq l\operatorname{osc}_B\psi.
 \tag{CTI13}
\]
This proof retains the full pairing and requires no commutation
of $D(s)$ with $A(s)$. It bounds an oscillation, so adding any
constant to $\psi$ has zero contribution to this pairing.

Define the full original coefficient determinant ratio
\[
 \mathcal R(s)=
 \frac{\det(T_f^*M(s)T_f)}{\det(E^*M(s)E)}>0.
 \tag{CTI14}
\]
For any differentiable positive matrix $G(s)$, determinant
multilinearity gives
$\det(G+hG'+o(h))=\det G\{1+h\operatorname{Tr}(G^{-1}G')+o(h)\}$.
Indeed in $\det(I+hC+o(h))$ only one diagonal first-order entry
can contribute at order $h$; every nonidentity permutation needs
at least two off-diagonal entries. This proves the logarithmic
derivative formula used below, without differentiating a
moving orthogonal frame. Rectangular trace cyclicity now gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac{d}{ds}\log\mathcal R(s)
 &=\operatorname{Tr}\bigl((T_f^*MT_f)^{-1}T_f^*M'T_f\bigr)
   -\operatorname{Tr}\bigl((E^*ME)^{-1}E^*M'E\bigr)\\
 &=\operatorname{Tr}\bigl((\Pi_F-\Pi_P)M^{-1}M'\bigr)
 =\operatorname{Tr}(D(s)A(s)).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{CTI15}
\]
All forms in this equation are evaluated at the same $s$.
The fundamental theorem of calculus and CTI13 therefore prove
\[
 \left|\log\frac{\mathcal R(1)}{\mathcal R(0)}\right|
 \leq\frac{\operatorname{osc}_B\psi}{2}
       \int_0^1\operatorname{Tr}|D(s)|_{M(s)}\,ds
 \leq l\operatorname{osc}_B\psi.
 \tag{CTI16}
\]
The integrand is continuous: the matrices depend continuously
on $s$, remain positive definite where inverted, and Hermitian
eigenvalue absolute-value sums are continuous. Alternatively
integrating the uniform bound in CTI13 proves the final inequality
directly. Both endpoint ratios contain the complete determinant
of their original $r$-dimensional relation coefficient space.
The factor $l$ comes from the exact contrast, and does not replace
$r$ by an arbitrarily chosen smaller determinant.

\subsection{Instantiation on the original quartet bulk}

Retain the actual source parameters and full polynomial
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 0<\delta<\tfrac12,\quad\gamma>2,\quad k=4l+1,\quad
 e=1+k(m-1),\quad q=e(k+1)^2,\quad c=k/2,\\
 \lambda_{ab}=c+(2a-k)\delta+i(2b-k)\gamma,\qquad
 \chi(S)=\prod_{a,b=0}^k(S-\lambda_{ab})^e,\\
 f(S)=\prod_{j=0}^{l-1}(S-c-(2j+\tfrac12)),\qquad
 d\sigma(y)=\frac{|\Gamma(1/4+iy/2)|^2}{2\pi}\,dy.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{CTI17}
\]
For $0<\epsilon<T<\infty$ use the two original bulk intervals
$B=[-qT,-q\epsilon]\cup[q\epsilon,qT]$ and set
\[
 d\mu_0=|y|^{2q}d\sigma\big|_B,
 \qquad\psi(y)=\log\frac{|\chi(c+iy)|^2}{|y|^{2q}},
 \qquad d\mu_s=|y|^{2q}e^{s\psi(y)}d\sigma\big|_B.
 \tag{CTI18}
\]
Every factor of $\chi$ is nonzero on the line $c+i\mathbb R$:
its root has real displacement $(2a-k)\delta\ne0$ because $k$
is odd. The Gamma factor is finite, continuous, and nonzero on
this compact line segment, since its real part is $1/4$.
Thus the density in CTI18 is finite and positive, and $\psi$ is
a bounded continuous real function on these two intervals.
All positivity and differentiation facts in CTI10--CTI16 apply
to these actual measures. At $s=1$ the measure is exactly
$|\chi(c+iy)|^2d\sigma|_B$; at $s=0$ it is the displayed radial
source with its original mass and coordinate retained.

For the exact original relation inclusion, write
$T_\chi:\mathcal V\to\mathbb C[S]_{\leq q+r+l-1}$ for
multiplication by $\chi$, with its complete coefficient columns.
Equip its target with the positive source
$d\widetilde\mu_s=e^{(s-1)\psi}d\sigma|_B$ and denote its Gram
by $\widetilde M(s)$. Direct integration gives
\[
 T_\chi^*\widetilde M(s)T_\chi=M(s),\qquad
 T_\chi E\mathbb C^r=\chi P,\qquad
 T_\chi T_f\mathbb C^r=\chi fP.
 \tag{CTI19}
\]
This proves the coefficient map is an isometry onto its actual
image. At $s=1$, the target form is the original sigma form
restricted to $B$. The two lifted orthogonal projectors onto
$\chi P$ and $\chi fP$ are obtained from CTI4 by replacing
the inclusions with $T_\chi E,T_\chi T_f$ and the metric with
$\widetilde M(s)$. For either inclusion, matrix multiplication
using CTI19 proves
\[
 \widetilde\Pi_P T_\chi=T_\chi\Pi_P,\qquad
 \widetilde\Pi_F T_\chi=T_\chi\Pi_F,
 \qquad\widetilde D T_\chi=T_\chi D.
 \tag{CTI20}
\]
Both lifted projectors vanish on the orthogonal complement of
$\operatorname{im}T_\chi$. Hence the lifted contrast has exactly
the same nonzero eigenvalues and the same trace absolute value;
only additional zero eigenvalues occur. Equations CTI19--CTI20
are the precise maps returning the contrast estimate to the
original relation spaces, with all coefficients and multiplicities.

In particular, define $\mathcal R_\chi^B$ by the two Grams of
$(f,fS,\ldots,fS^{r-1})$ and $(1,S,\ldots,S^{r-1})$ in
$|\chi|^2d\sigma|_B$, and define $\mathcal R_{\rm rad}^B$
by the same two coefficient lists in $|y|^{2q}d\sigma|_B$.
No determinant factors are canceled by changing their dimensions.
The completed original-density transport is exactly
\[
 \left|\log\mathcal R_\chi^B-
             \log\mathcal R_{\rm rad}^B\right|
 \leq l\left(
  \max_{y\in B}\log\frac{|\chi(c+iy)|^2}{|y|^{2q}}
 -\min_{y\in B}\log\frac{|\chi(c+iy)|^2}{|y|^{2q}}
 \right).
 \tag{CTI21}
\]
The maxima and minima exist by the proved compact continuity.
Any already evaluated bound on this exact density oscillation
can therefore be inserted with coefficient $l$, while the full
original determinant ratio and source maps remain unchanged.
