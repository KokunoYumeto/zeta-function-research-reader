


\section{Original moments and the two sheets of the squared coordinate}

Retain $k=4l+1$, $l\geq1$, $m\geq1$,
$e=1+k(m-1)$, $q=e(k+1)^2$, $c=k/2$, and the original
measure and its mass
\[
 d\sigma(y)=\frac{|\Gamma(1/4+iy/2)|^2}{2\pi}\,dy,
 \qquad c_\sigma=\int_{\mathbb R}d\sigma=\sqrt{2\pi}.
\tag{PHT1}
\]
Use distinct names for the ordinary and even moments:
\[
 m_j=\int_{\mathbb R}y^j\,d\sigma(y),\quad
 \mu_j=m_{2j},\quad
 M_{a,r}=(m_{2a+i+j})_{0\leq i,j<r},\quad
 H_n^{(a)}=\det(\mu_{a+i+j})_{0\leq i,j<n}.
\tag{PHT2}
\]
Here $a,n$ are nonnegative integers, $r\geq1$, and
$H_0^{(a)}=1$. All moments are finite: rotation of Euler's
Gamma integral through any angle $0<\theta<\pi/2$ gives
\[
 \sigma(y)\leq\frac{\Gamma(1/4)^2}{2\pi\sqrt{\cos\theta}}
                         e^{-\theta|y|}.
\]
For the rotation use the argument $\operatorname{sgn}(y)\theta$;
the factor contributed by the power is $e^{-\theta|y|/2}$,
and the absolute remaining Euler integral is
$\Gamma(1/4)(\cos\theta)^{-1/4}$ before squaring.
The small and large circular arcs vanish by the positive
real exponent and by $\cos\theta>0$. This proves the displayed
bound and its constants directly.

The positive-axis and negative-axis maps to $t=y^2$ have
inverse branches $y=\sqrt t$ and $y=-\sqrt t$. Each branch
pushes the original measure to
\[
 d\nu_+(t)=d\nu_-(t)=\frac{\sigma(\sqrt t)}{2\sqrt t}\,dt,
 \quad t>0.
\]
The reversed orientation of the negative branch changes the
integration limits as well as its Jacobian, so its measure is
positive with the same factor $1/2$. Thus the full pushforward is
\[
 d\nu(t)=d\nu_+(t)+d\nu_-(t)
        =\frac{\sigma(\sqrt t)}{\sqrt t}\,dt,
 \quad\int_0^\infty d\nu=c_\sigma,
 \quad\mu_j=\int_0^\infty t^j\,d\nu(t).
\tag{PHT3}
\]
No half-axis contribution has been treated as the whole mass.

Every polynomial has a unique decomposition
$P(y)=P_e(y^2)+yP_o(y^2)$. The exact coefficient map
$(P_e,P_o)\mapsto P_e(y^2)+yP_o(y^2)$ has inverse
\[
 P_e(t)=\frac{P(\sqrt t)+P(-\sqrt t)}2,
 \qquad
 P_o(t)=\frac{P(\sqrt t)-P(-\sqrt t)}{2\sqrt t}.
\tag{PHT4}
\]
Adding the two branch integrals cancels their cross terms and gives
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}|P(y)|^2y^{2a}d\sigma(y)
 =\int_0^\infty|P_e(t)|^2t^a d\nu(t)
  +\int_0^\infty|P_o(t)|^2t^{a+1}d\nu(t).
\tag{PHT5}
\]
These same formulas extend to a unitary map of the corresponding
$L^2$ spaces: the inverse in PHT4 is defined for almost every
$t>0$, and PHT5 proves both its range and norm. The point $t=0$
has zero measure. On the exact multiplication domains this map
transports the original real coordinate operator to
\[
 M_y\longleftrightarrow
 \begin{pmatrix}0&M_t\\I&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 M_y^2\longleftrightarrow
 \begin{pmatrix}M_t&0\\0&M_t\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{PHT6}
\]
Indeed multiplication of $P_e(y^2)+yP_o(y^2)$ by $y$ gives
$(tP_o)(y^2)+yP_e(y^2)$. Thus the two parity blocks retain
their exact coupling under the original coordinate.
Explicitly, in the base Hilbert space
\[
 \mathcal K_a=L^2(t^a d\nu)\oplus L^2(t^{a+1}d\nu),
\]
the first operator has domain
\[
 \{(u,v)\in\mathcal K_a:
       u\in L^2(t^{a+1}d\nu),\quad
       v\in L^2(t^{a+2}d\nu)\}
\]
and acts by $(u,v)\mapsto(tv,u)$. Its squared operator has
domain
\[
 \{(u,v)\in\mathcal K_a:
       u\in L^2(t^{a+2}d\nu),\quad
       v\in L^2(t^{a+3}d\nu)\}
\]
and acts by $(u,v)\mapsto(tu,tv)$. These are exactly the
conditions that the displayed images belong to $\mathcal K_a$.
For the squared domain the intermediate domain conditions
follow from $t\leq1+t^2$. PHT5 then identifies these domains
with $\{P:yP\in L^2(y^{2a}d\sigma)\}$ and
$\{P:y^2P\in L^2(y^{2a}d\sigma)\}$, respectively, including
the membership of $P$ in that base space.

\section{The complete finite parity congruence}

Set $n_e=\lceil r/2\rceil$, $n_o=\lfloor r/2\rfloor$.
Let $\Pi_r$ have columns $e_0,e_2,\ldots,e_{2n_e-2}$ followed
by $e_1,e_3,\ldots,e_{2n_o-1}$ in the original coefficient
frame. Then $\Pi_r^{-1}=\Pi_r^{\mathsf T}$ and
$\det\Pi_r=(-1)^{n_e(n_e-1)/2}$: the $j$-th even index crosses
exactly $j$ smaller odd indices. Evenness of $\sigma$ makes
each odd moment zero. Entrywise substitution therefore proves
\[
 \Pi_r^{\mathsf T}M_{a,r}\Pi_r
 =\begin{pmatrix}
  (\mu_{a+i+j})_{i,j<n_e}&0\\
  0&(\mu_{a+1+i+j})_{i,j<n_o}
 \end{pmatrix}.
\tag{PHT7}
\]
This is a congruence in the original coefficient space. Its
two determinant signs cancel, so with $D_{a,r}=\det M_{a,r}$,
\[
 D_{a,2n}=H_n^{(a)}H_n^{(a+1)},\qquad
 D_{a,2n+1}=H_{n+1}^{(a)}H_n^{(a+1)}.
\tag{PHT8}
\]
Every factor is strictly positive: its quadratic form is
the integral of a nonzero polynomial's squared modulus
against a positive density on $(0,\infty)$.

The original $S$ frame is also retained. The map
$P(S)\mapsto P(c+iy)$ has coefficient matrix
\[
 E_{j,n}=\binom nj c^{n-j}(\sqrt{-1})^j\quad(j\leq n),\qquad
 E^{-1}:Q(y)\mapsto Q((S-c)/i),
 \quad\det E=i^{r(r-1)/2}.
\tag{PHT9}
\]
Entries below the displayed triangular range are zero.
The source Gram in the $S$ frame is exactly $E^*M_{a,r}E$,
and its determinant is $D_{a,r}$ because $|\det E|=1$.
Multiplication by $(S-c)^a$ contributes the exact scalar
$i^a$ under this map; its squared modulus is one. These
identities identify the moments in PHT2 with the actual
monomial Gamma relation subspaces, including their degree.

\section{The two original high degrees reduce to one positive shift}

The stipulated $q=e(k+1)^2$ is even; put $n=q/2$. Define
\[
 Z_a=H_n^{(a)}H_{n+1}^{(a)}(H_n^{(a+1)})^2,
 \qquad A_n^{(a)}=H_n^{(a+1)}/H_n^{(a)}>0.
\tag{PHT10}
\]
At the two required ranks $r=q,q+1$, PHT8 proves the exact
combination
\[
 \frac{D_{q+l,q}}{D_{q,q}}
 \frac{D_{q+l,q+1}}{D_{q,q+1}}
 =\frac{Z_{q+l}}{Z_q},\qquad
 \frac{Z_{a+1}}{Z_a}
 =A_n^{(a)}A_{n+1}^{(a)}(A_n^{(a+1)})^2.
\tag{PHT11}
\]
Hence its logarithm is exactly the finite sum
\[
 \log\frac{Z_{q+l}}{Z_q}
 =\sum_{j=0}^{l-1}
  \{\log A_n^{(q+j)}+\log A_{n+1}^{(q+j)}
                              +2\log A_n^{(q+j+1)}\}.
\tag{PHT12}
\]
This is a contiguous-shift expression for the original two
high determinants, with their unchanged ranks. The original
mass factor in each $H_n^{(a)}$ is $c_\sigma^n$, so $Z_a$
contains $c_\sigma^{4n+1}=c_\sigma^{2q+1}$. It cancels only
after the numerator and denominator in PHT11 are formed.

\section{Original Toda time and the squared-coordinate time}

For $u\geq0$, define the actual pushed-forward family
\[
 d\nu_{a,u}(t)=t^a e^{-ut}d\nu(t),\qquad
 H_n^{(a)}(u)=\det\left(\int_0^\infty
                    t^{a+i+j}e^{-ut}d\nu(t)\right)_{i,j<n}.
\tag{PHT13}
\]
This is precisely the original $y$-source multiplied by
$e^{-u y^2}$, pushed forward through its two branches.
All moment derivatives at $u=0$ exist from the right by
dominated convergence using the original higher moments.
For $\operatorname{Re}u>0$ they are holomorphic, by the same
domination on compact subsets. The real family with $u<0$
does not have finite mass: Gamma reflection and the Euler
bound give $\sigma(y)\geq C e^{-\pi|y|}$ with
$C=\pi/\Gamma(3/4)^2>0$, and
$e^{|u|t-\pi\sqrt t}$ grows exponentially for sufficiently
large $t$. This proves the actual time domain rather than
assigning an unjustified two-sided strip to the quadratic flow.
The reflection formula here follows on $0<\operatorname{Re}z<1$
by changing $x=tv$ in the Euler double integral, obtaining
$\Gamma(z)\Gamma(1-z)=\int_0^\infty t^{z-1}/(1+t)\,dt$.
A keyhole contour with argument in $(0,2\pi)$ has residue
$e^{i\pi(z-1)}$ at $-1$ and straight-segment factor
$1-e^{2\pi iz}$; its vanishing circular arcs give the value
$\pi/\sin(\pi z)$. At $z=1/4+iy/2$, take squared moduli
and use $|\Gamma(3/4-iy/2)|\leq\Gamma(3/4)$ from Euler's
integral. Since $|\sin(\pi/4+i\pi y/2)|^2
=\cosh(\pi y)/2$, this proves the stated lower bound.

Let $p_j^{(a,u)}$ be the monic orthogonal polynomials in this
measure, with norms
\[h_j=H_{j+1}^{(a)}(u)/H_j^{(a)}(u).\]
Their coefficients are obtained by inverting the positive
finite moment Gram, so the moment differentiability just
proved also justifies each coefficient derivative below.
Expansion of $tp_j$ and orthogonality give
\[
 tp_j=p_{j+1}+b_jp_j+\alpha_jp_{j-1},\qquad
 \alpha_j=h_j/h_{j-1}>0\ (j\geq1),\quad\alpha_0=0.
\tag{PHT14}
\]
All other lower coefficients vanish because
$\langle tp_j,p_i\rangle=\langle p_j,tp_i\rangle=0$
for $i<j-1$; comparison against $p_{j-1}$ gives $\alpha_j$.
Differentiating orthogonality under the negative tilt proves
$\partial_u p_j=\alpha_jp_{j-1}$, since its coefficient
against every $p_i$ is that of $tp_j$ for $i<j$.
Also $\partial_u h_j=-b_jh_j$. Differentiating
$b_j=h_j^{-1}\int t p_j^2d\nu_{a,u}$ and using PHT14 gives
\[
 \partial_u b_j=\alpha_j-\alpha_{j+1},\qquad
 \partial_u\alpha_j=\alpha_j(b_{j-1}-b_j).
\tag{PHT15}
\]
In detail the derivative of the numerator of $b_j$ is
$2\alpha_jh_j-\|tp_j\|^2
=h_j(\alpha_j-\alpha_{j+1}-b_j^2)$, and differentiating
$h_j^{-1}$ supplies the missing $b_j^2$.
Since $H_n=\prod_{j<n}h_j$, telescoping PHT15 proves
\[
 (\log H_n^{(a)}(u))''
  =\frac{H_{n+1}^{(a)}(u)H_{n-1}^{(a)}(u)}{H_n^{(a)}(u)^2}>0
  \quad(n\geq1),
\tag{PHT16}
\]
or equivalently
$H_nH_n''-(H_n')^2=H_{n+1}H_{n-1}$.
At $u=0$ these derivatives are the proved right derivatives.
Thus the coefficient and sign of the Hankel--Toda equation
are exactly those of the existing original-source Toda calculation.

There is an explicit map to that calculation's original
linear tilt as well. For real $\theta$, on $L^2(y^{2a}d\sigma)$, multiplication
by $e^{\theta y/2}$ is the isometry from the source weighted
by $e^{\theta y}$ to the original source, with inverse
given by the negative exponential. Under PHT4--5 the same
map is the following exact operator matrix on the two branches:
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
  \cosh(\theta\sqrt t/2)&\sqrt t\sinh(\theta\sqrt t/2)\\
  t^{-1/2}\sinh(\theta\sqrt t/2)&\cosh(\theta\sqrt t/2)
 \end{pmatrix}.
\tag{PHT17}
\]
Substituting $y=\sqrt t$ and $y=-\sqrt t$ proves each entry
and its inverse at $-\theta$. To specify the weighted source
and its full cross Gram, write this matrix as $B_{\theta/2}(t)$.
The two branch integrals give the pulled-back form
\[
 \int_0^\infty
 \begin{pmatrix}\overline u&\overline v\end{pmatrix}
 G_\theta(t)\binom uv\,t^a d\nu(t),\qquad
 G_\theta(t)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  \cosh(\theta\sqrt t)&\sqrt t\sinh(\theta\sqrt t)\\
  \sqrt t\sinh(\theta\sqrt t)&t\cosh(\theta\sqrt t)
 \end{pmatrix}.
\tag{PHT17a}
\]
For real $\theta$, direct matrix multiplication, using
$\cosh^2 x+\sinh^2 x=\cosh(2x)$ and
$2\sinh x\cosh x=\sinh(2x)$, proves
\[
 B_{\theta/2}(t)^*\operatorname{diag}(1,t)B_{\theta/2}(t)
       =G_\theta(t),\qquad
 B_{-\theta/2}(t)B_{\theta/2}(t)=I.
\]
Thus the space of measurable pairs with finite PHT17a norm
is mapped isometrically and onto $\mathcal K_a$ by
$B_{\theta/2}$, with inverse $B_{-\theta/2}$; the equality of
forms proves the domain and range even when these matrices
are unbounded on the base space. If viewed as an operator
on $\mathcal K_a$ itself, its domain consists precisely of
the pairs in $\mathcal K_a$ whose PHT17a integral is finite.
The branch map in PHT4 identifies that source with the full
original space $L^2(e^{\theta y}y^{2a}d\sigma)$.
The quadratic tilt in PHT13
instead gives the diagonal multiplication $e^{-ut/2}$ in
both components. Both are the specified functions of the
operator in PHT6. This proves the relation between the two
times on the original spaces and keeps the parity mixing of
the original linear time.

For real $|\theta|<\pi/2$ the tilted $y$-moments and their
derivatives converge: in the earlier Gamma bound choose
$|\theta|<\theta_0<\pi/2$. The same orthogonal-polynomial
derivative calculation, with real variable $y$ and positive
tilt $e^{\theta y}$, applies to the exactly defined matrix
$M_{a,r}(\theta)_{ij}=\int_{\mathbb R}y^{2a+i+j}
e^{\theta y}d\sigma(y)$ and proves
\[
 (\log\det M_{a,r}(\theta))''\big|_{\theta=0}
   =h_r^{y,a}/h_{r-1}^{y,a}.
\tag{PHT18}
\]
Here the superscript $y,a$ denotes the full original real-line
monic norms. Parity and PHT5 identify them exactly as
$h_{2j}^{y,a}=H_{j+1}^{(a)}/H_j^{(a)}$ and
$h_{2j+1}^{y,a}=H_{j+1}^{(a+1)}/H_j^{(a+1)}$.
Consequently the two original Toda curvatures have the explicit
positive expressions
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \frac{h_{2n}^{y,a}}{h_{2n-1}^{y,a}}
 &=\frac{H_{n+1}^{(a)}H_{n-1}^{(a+1)}}
        {H_n^{(a)}H_n^{(a+1)}},\\
 \frac{h_{2n+1}^{y,a}}{h_{2n}^{y,a}}
 &=\frac{H_{n+1}^{(a+1)}H_n^{(a)}}
        {H_n^{(a+1)}H_{n+1}^{(a)}}.
\end{aligned}\tag{PHT19}
\]
These are exact specializations of the original norm-ratio
Toda controls at the ranks needed by the programme. The
contiguous identities and their positive computation follow
in the attached complete derivation.

\section{The complete universal Gamma center is a positive rational product}

Use exactly the all-index moments $m_j$ in PHT2. The universal
center identified in the low-endpoint transport calculation is
the following finite quantity:
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \mathscr D_{l,N}=\prod_{a=0}^N\prod_{j=0}^{2l-1}(a+j+1/2),\\
 P_k=\log\mathscr D_{l,q-1}+\log\mathscr D_{l,q}
             -\log\mathscr D_{l,2q-1}-\log\mathscr D_{l,2q},\\
 W_k=P_k-\log\frac{m_{2q+2l}}{m_{2q}}
     +\log\frac{D_{q+l,q}}{D_{q,q}}
     +\log\frac{D_{q+l,q+1}}{D_{q,q+1}}.
\end{gathered}\tag{PHT20}
\]
Here $\mathscr D_{l,N}$ denotes exactly the source product
called $D_{l,N}$ in GCR and LET, while $D_{a,r}$ retains
its moment-determinant definition from PHT8. Define
\[
 G_{q,l}=
 \left(\prod_{a=q}^{2q-1}\prod_{j=0}^{2l-1}(a+j+1/2)\right)
 \left(\prod_{a=q+1}^{2q}\prod_{j=0}^{2l-1}(a+j+1/2)\right)>0.
\tag{PHT21}
\]
Cancellation of the explicitly overlapping monic products
gives $e^{P_k}=G_{q,l}^{-1}$. The low moment ratio retains
its precise index conversion:
\[
 \frac{m_{2q+2l}}{m_{2q}}
 =\frac{\mu_{q+l}}{\mu_q}
 =\frac{H_1^{(q+l)}}{H_1^{(q)}}
 =\prod_{s=0}^{l-1}c_0^{(q+s)}.
\tag{PHT22}
\]
The last equality is HCT13 at degree zero and then finite
telescoping. Combining PHT11, HCT37 and PHT22 proves
\[
\begin{gathered}
 W_k=\log\mathcal W_{q,l},\\
 \boxed{\mathcal W_{q,l}=
 \frac1{G_{q,l}}\prod_{s=0}^{l-1}
 \frac{\displaystyle
   \left(\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}c_j^{(q+s)}\right)
   \left(\prod_{j=0}^{n}c_j^{(q+s)}\right)
   \left(\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}c_j^{(q+s+1)}\right)^2}
 {c_0^{(q+s)}},\qquad n=q/2.}
\end{gathered}\tag{PHT23}
\]
Every factor is positive and rational: the initial $c,d$
values are the exact rational Gamma values HCT21, and
HCT22--23 use only positive sums, products and quotients.
The initial norms still contain $c_\sigma$, as HCT19--20
record. Their common powers cancel in PHT11 and PHT22
only after their full source determinants are constructed.

Thus PHT23 is a finite evaluation procedure for the entire
universal center in PHT20, requiring neither a new moment
quadrature nor a difference of nearly equal Gram determinants.
One sufficient finite initial range is $0\leq j\leq n+q+l$:
HCT22--23 then compute all needed coefficients through shift
$q+l$ with the proved decreasing-index range in HCT24.
This statement specifies an exact finite procedure. It does
not assert a bit-complexity estimate or an asymptotic value
along the original coupled sequence $q=e(k+1)^2$, $l=(k-1)/4$.
The positive contiguous identities HCT25--39 provide the
exact equations for that next calculation, with all ranks
and original source factors retained.
