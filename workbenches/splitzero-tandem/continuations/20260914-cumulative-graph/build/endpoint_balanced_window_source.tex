\subsection{Balanced arithmetic norm windows and the strengthened endpoint threshold}\label{endpoint-balanced_window--balanced-arithmetic-norm-windows-and-the-strengthened-endpoint-threshold}

This is a written analytic extension of the supplied arithmetic endpoint note, not a Lean certificate or a numerical enclosure. The fixed packet, original source, Taylor unit, measure and total mass are unchanged. It is intended for the next integration cut, not the already frozen E publication.

\subsubsection{Original measure and constants}\label{endpoint-balanced_window--original-measure-and-constants}

Fix a monic finite divisor h of g=2xi, removing its selected zeros with their full orders, and put d=deg h. On the original line S=k/2+iu, use

\begin{verbatim}
w_h(t)=|(g/h)(1/2+it)|^2/(2pi), m_(h,k)=w_h^{*k},
omega_(h,k,j)=inf_(P monic, degree j) integral |P(k/2+iu)|^2 m_(h,k)(u) du.
\end{verbatim}

The mass is mu\_h\^{}k, not one. The original note proves, for a=pi/2 and B=42+2d,

\begin{verbatim}
m_(h,k)(u) >= c_h vartheta_h^(k-3)
   exp[-a(|u|+k-3)] (1+|u|+k-3)^(-B),  k>=3,
\end{verbatim}

where c\_h=b\_h c\_h\^{}(1)\textgreater0, b\_h=min\_(\textbar v\textbar\textless=1)(w\_h*w\_h)(v)\textgreater0, and vartheta\_h=integral\_{[}-1,1{]} w\_h\textgreater0. Fix 0\textless b\textless a and retain both actual finite exponential moments M\_+=integral exp(bt)w\_h(t)dt and M\_-=integral exp(-bt)w\_h(t)dt. Define X=max(1,M\_+,M\_-) and K=max(1,vartheta\_h\^{}(-1)). None of these definitions normalizes the source.

\subsubsection{Exact preceding estimate}\label{endpoint-balanced_window--exact-preceding-estimate}

The monic Lebesgue minimum, allowing any leading coefficient of modulus one, is

\begin{verbatim}
ell_n(L) = [2 L^(2n+1)/(2n+1)] [2^n (n!)^2/(2n)!]^2.
\end{verbatim}

This includes the original leading phase i\^{}n under S=k/2+iu. For integers n\textgreater=0, r\textgreater=1, k\textgreater=3 and any L\textgreater0, the trial-monomial exponential upper bound and interval Legendre lower bound give

\begin{verbatim}
A_(h,k,n,r) := (omega_(h,k,n+r)/omega_(h,k,n))^(1/(2r))
  <= [(2(n+r))! b^(-2(n+r)) (M_+^k+M_-^k)
       exp[a(L+k-3)] (1+L+k-3)^B
       / (c_h vartheta_h^(k-3) ell_n(L))]^(1/(2r)).       (BW1)
\end{verbatim}

For the balanced-window theorem take integers n\textgreater=k\textgreater=3 and n/2\textless=r\textless=n, and set L=n.~Then

\begin{verbatim}
A_(h,k,n,r) <= Cbal_h n,                              (BW2)
Cbal_h = 256 exp(pi) max(1,3/c_h) X K
            max(1,b^(-3)) 6^(B/3).                  (BW3)
\end{verbatim}

\paragraph{Proof of the uniform constant}\label{endpoint-balanced_window--proof-of-the-uniform-constant}

The central-binomial bound gives ell\_n(n)\textgreater=2 n\^{}(2n+1)/{[}(2n+1)4\^{}n{]}. Also (2(n+r))!\textless=(4n)\^{}(2(n+r)) and M\_+\^{}k+M\_-\^{}k\textless=2X\^{}k. Combining these before taking roots cancels the factor two. The factorial, Legendre and c\_h factors after the 2r-th root are at most

\begin{verbatim}
n * 4 * 8^(n/r) * [(2n+1)/(c_h n)]^(1/(2r))
  <= 256 n max(1,3/c_h).
\end{verbatim}

Here n/r\textless=2, (2n+1)/n\textless=3, and 0\textless1/(2r)\textless=1. The remaining factors obey

\begin{verbatim}
X^(k/(2r)) <= X,
vartheta_h^(-(k-3)/(2r)) <= K,
b^(-(n+r)/r) <= max(1,b^(-3)),
exp[a(n+k-3)/(2r)] <= exp(2a)=exp(pi).
\end{verbatim}

Finally 1+n+k-3\textless=2n and 2r\textgreater=n.~The function log(2x)/x decreases for x\textgreater=3, since its derivative is {[}1-log(2x){]}/x\^{}2\textless0. Hence

\begin{verbatim}
(1+n+k-3)^(B/(2r)) <= (2n)^(B/n) <= 6^(B/3).
\end{verbatim}

Multiplication proves BW2-BW3. This proof works for the original arithmetic density without an evenness hypothesis. The constants depend on fixed h and b only, never n,k,r.

\subsubsection{Composition with the exact consecutive-window product}\label{endpoint-balanced_window--composition-with-the-exact-consecutive-window-product}

This paragraph assumes the separately reviewed exact-window theorem for the original canonical quotient family, including its nonnegative contractions and zero-contraction cases. Write V\_N=det G\_N\textgreater0 and Lambda\_N=omega\_N/V\_N. Its simple product corollary is

\begin{verbatim}
min_(n<=N<n+r) epsilon_N
   <= (Lambda_(n+r)/Lambda_n)^(1/(2r))
   = A_(h,k,n,r) (V_n/V_(n+r))^(1/(2r)).              (BW4)
\end{verbatim}

Do not infer BW4 from BW1 alone. It uses the original rank-two Toda identities and the retained source/quotient maps. If every allowance on the window has the exterior lower bound L\_(h,k), BW2-BW4 imply

\begin{verbatim}
log(V_n/V_(n+r)) >= 2r log[L_(h,k)/(Cbal_h n)].        (BW5)
\end{verbatim}

This holds even if the right side is negative; canonical volume monotonicity independently gives a nonnegative left side.

For the packet consisting exactly of the hypothetical off-line quartet 1/2 +/- delta +/- i gamma, with fixed delta,gamma\textgreater0 and common multiplicity m\textgreater=1, retain

\begin{verbatim}
q=q_k=[1+k(m-1)](k+1)^2,
L_(h,k)=2delta[1+k(m-1)](k+1)floor((k+1)^2/4),
q>=k>=3, L_(h,k)/q>=delta k/2.
\end{verbatim}

Take n=q and r=q-1. Since q\textgreater=3, q/2\textless=q-1\textless=q; the degrees q,\ldots,2q-2 are admitted and the norm endpoint is 2q-1, not 2q. Define the central two-volume cost

\begin{verbatim}
C_k=log(V_q/V_(2q-1)).
\end{verbatim}

Then

\begin{verbatim}
C_k >= 2(q-1) log[delta k/(2 Cbal_h)].                 (BW6)
\end{verbatim}

Canonical monotonicity gives V\_(q-1)\textgreater=V\_q and V\_(2q-1)\textgreater=V\_(2q). Consequently each outer cost dominates this same central cost:

\begin{verbatim}
log(V_(q-1)/V_(2q-1)) >= C_k,
log(V_q/V_(2q)) >= C_k.
\end{verbatim}

For the original four-volume budget B\_k=log{[}V\_(q-1)V\_q/(V\_(2q-1)V\_(2q)){]},

\begin{verbatim}
B_k >= 2 C_k >= 4(q-1) log[delta k/(2 Cbal_h)],
liminf_(k->infinity) B_k/(q_k log k) >= 4.            (BW7)
\end{verbatim}

Indeed q\_k\textgreater= (k+1)\^{}2 tends to infinity, so (q\_k-1)/q\_k tends to one; fixed positive delta/(2Cbal\_h) contributes only O\_h(q\_k). The diagonal choice r=q also yields log(V\_q/V\_(2q))\textgreater=2q log{[}delta k/(2Cbal\_h){]}, but the overlapping shorter window proves the stronger four-volume threshold.

The supplied note's older threshold 2 remains correct under its weaker sinh endpoint estimate. BW7 is a strengthened synthesis using BW4, not a correction to that valid implication. No upper estimate on B\_k or C\_k follows. A separately proved limsup B\_k/(q\_k log k)\textless4 would contradict this quartet; no such upper estimate and no RH proof is asserted here.

\subsubsection{Reference and evidence boundary}\label{endpoint-balanced_window--reference-and-evidence-boundary}

The analytic ingredients are the zeta reflection formula (\href{https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.4.E2}{DLMF 25.4.2}), the gamma vertical-strip estimate, uniform for bounded real part (\href{https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.11.E9}{DLMF 5.11.9}), and the Legendre orthogonality/leading coefficient (\href{https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.3}{DLMF 18.3}). The annulus propagation, convolution restriction, BW1-BW3 and BW5-BW7 are written derivations. Finite regression checks support algebraic fixtures only, not the analytic quantifiers or arithmetic zero hypotheses.
