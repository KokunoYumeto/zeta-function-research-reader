\section{Source enclosures and convergent signed projector control}
\label{sec:incoming-source-metric-control}

This chapter incorporates the complete written derivations in the
user-supplied continuation following merged PR29.  The canonical
secant and quotient-variation identities already occur in that PR.
The four-projector pairing is the same pairing as AW and SP, with
the exact coordinate correspondence proved below.  The source
sandwich, restriction-loss expression for its Hilbert--Schmidt

The supplied Lean draft is preserved separately as uncompiled;
the mathematical scope here is the written proof.

\subsection{The unchanged polynomial and arithmetic source}

Fix the actual full packet \(h\), tensor order \(k\geq1\), and
its monic cyclic polynomial \(\chi=\chi_{h,k}\) of degree \(q\geq1\).
Put \(E=\mathbb C[S]/(\chi)\), \(H=\mathcal P_{2q}\), and \(n=2q+1\).
All coordinates are the original ascending monomial coordinates.
The original analytic source and observation are
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 g=2\xi,\qquad \mathcal M F_h=g/h,\qquad
 \mathcal V P=P(D_1+\cdots+D_k)F_h^{\otimes k},\\
 J^{(k)}\mathcal VP=\eta_{h,k}\pi_\chi P,\qquad
 \eta_{h,k}[P]=\upsilon_h^{\otimes k}P(A_h^{(k)})1,\quad
 \upsilon_h=j_h(g/h),\quad A_h^{(k)}=\sum_{i=1}^k A_{h,i}.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{ISM1}
\]
Here \(A_h\) is multiplication by the original coordinate on
\(E_h=\mathbb C[s]/(h)\).  Thus the domain of \(\eta_{h,k}\)
is \(E\), its codomain is \(E_h^{\otimes k}\), and its full
unit is retained.  The minimal polynomial definition of \(\chi\)
on the cyclic vector \(1\) proves that \([P]\mapsto
P(A_h^{(k)})1\) is injective; multiplication by the invertible
\(\upsilon_h^{\otimes k}\) preserves injectivity.

For \(N\in\{q-1,q,2q-1,2q\}\), let
\(I_N:\mathcal P_N\hookrightarrow H\) be coefficient inclusion,
\(B_N:\mathcal P_{N-q}\to H\) multiplication by \(\chi\) followed
by inclusion, and \(C:E\to H\) the degree-less-than-\(q\)
remainder lift in the retained quotient basis.  The relation
space is zero when \(N=q-1\).  Polynomial division by the
monic \(\chi\) proves the exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow\mathcal P_{N-q}
 \xrightarrow{\ \times\chi\ }\mathcal P_N
 \xrightarrow{\ \pi_\chi\ }E\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{ISM2}
\]
Indeed division writes every polynomial uniquely as
\(\chi Q+r\), \(\deg r<q\); if its class is zero, \(r=0\).
This also proves injectivity of the relation map and every
dimension used below.

Let \(M_0,M_1\) be the actual positive definite Hermitian forms
on this same \(H\).  In the arithmetic comparison these are the
moments of \(r_{1/4}^{*k}(y)\,dy\) and \(w_h^{*k}(y)\,dy\) in
\(S=k/2+iy\), with \(w_h(y)=|(g/h)(1/2+iy)|^2/(2\pi)\).
The finite identities below also hold for any two positive
forms on this unchanged presentation.  The canonical section,
its relation coefficient, and its positive quotient Gram are
\[
 \begin{split}
 Z_N(M)&=(B_N^*MB_N)^{-1}B_N^*MC,\\
 \rho_N(M)&=C-B_NZ_N(M):E\longrightarrow H,\\
 G_N(M)&=\rho_N(M)^*M\rho_N(M),\qquad
 V_N(M)=\det G_N(M)>0.
 \end{split}
 \tag{ISM3}
\]
At \(N=q-1\) the first map is the unique map to the zero
space and \(B_NZ_N=0\); no preceding inverse is used.
For every other degree, injectivity of \(B_N\) and positivity
of \(M\) make its relation Gram positive definite.  Direct
substitution gives \(B_N^*M\rho_N=0\) and
\(\pi_\chi\rho_N=1_E\).  Hence \(\rho_N\) is injective,
which proves positivity of \(G_N\).

\subsection{Both scaled secants, with the original boundary terms}

Fix one admitted \(N\), write \(\rho_a=\rho_N(M_a)\), and put
\[
 \Delta=\rho_1-\rho_0=B_N(Z_N(M_0)-Z_N(M_1)).
 \tag{ISM4}
\]
For every coefficient map \(Z:E\to\mathcal P_{N-q}\),
the two cross terms in the Gram expansion vanish by
\(B_N^*M_a\rho_a=0\), giving
\[
 (\rho_a+B_NZ)^*M_a(\rho_a+B_NZ)
   =G_N(M_a)+(B_NZ)^*M_a(B_NZ).
 \tag{ISM5}
\]
Taking \(a=0,B_NZ=\Delta\), and then \(a=1,B_NZ=-\Delta\),
proves
\[
 \rho_1^*M_0\rho_1=G_0+\Delta^*M_0\Delta,\qquad
 \rho_0^*M_1\rho_0=G_1+\Delta^*M_1\Delta.
 \tag{ISM6}
\]
Here \(G_a=G_N(M_a)\).  Subtracting the displayed matrices
without deleting either boundary term yields, for real \(a,b\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 G_1-aG_0
 &=\rho_1^*(M_1-aM_0)\rho_1+a\Delta^*M_0\Delta,\\
 bG_0-G_1
 &=\rho_0^*(bM_0-M_1)\rho_0+\Delta^*M_1\Delta.
 \end{split}
 \tag{ISM7}
\]
In particular both boundary terms have the positive signs
shown.  For \(0<a\leq b\) and
\(aM_0\preceq M_1\preceq bM_0\), congruence of positive
matrices and (ISM7) give
\[
 aG_N(M_0)\preceq G_N(M_1)\preceq bG_N(M_0)
 \quad\hbox{at every one of the four degrees.}
 \tag{ISM8}
\]
Such finite endpoints always exist: they may be taken to be
the smallest and largest eigenvalues of \(M_0^{-1}M_1\),
which is positive self-adjoint in the original \(M_0\) metric.

Conjugating (ISM8) by \(G_N(M_0)^{-1/2}\) places every
eigenvalue of the conjugated positive matrix in \([a,b]\).
The determinant is its product and logarithm is the sum of
the real logarithms, so
\[
 q\log a\leq\log\frac{V_N(M_1)}{V_N(M_0)}
       \leq q\log b.
 \tag{ISM9}
\]
Set
\[
 \mathcal B(M)=
 \log\frac{V_{q-1}(M)V_q(M)}{V_{2q-1}(M)V_{2q}(M)},
 \qquad \mathcal C=\mathcal B(M_1)-\mathcal B(M_0).
 \tag{ISM10}
\]
There are exactly two positive and two negative terms in
\(\mathcal C\).  Applying the upper endpoint in (ISM9) to
the positive terms and the lower endpoint to the negative
ones, and then reversing these choices, proves
\[
 |\mathcal C|\leq2q\log(b/a).
 \tag{ISM11}
\]
Consequently a certified common source enclosure
\((1-\varepsilon)\widehat M\preceq M\preceq
(1+\varepsilon)\widehat M\), \(0\leq\varepsilon<1\), gives
\[
 |\mathcal B(M)-\mathcal B(\widehat M)|
 \leq2q\log\frac{1+\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}.
 \tag{ISM12}
\]
These are the supplied source-error estimates in their
original forms.  At these same use sites the stronger
previously proved AW/SP estimate must also be retained.
For all ordered eigenvalues
\(\beta_1\leq\cdots\leq\beta_{2q+1}\) of
\(D=M_0^{-1}M_1\), define its full existing expression
\[
 \mathcal L_q(D)=\log\frac{\beta_{q+2}}{\beta_q}
       +2\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}
              \log\frac{\beta_{2q+2-j}}{\beta_j}.
\]
The sum is empty at \(q=1\).  AW's two nested-flag
trace estimates, with the exact operator identification
(ISM16)--(ISM19), give
\[
 |\mathcal C|\leq\mathcal L_q(D)
             \leq(2q-1)\log(b/a).
\]
For the last inequality every displayed eigenvalue ratio
is at most \(b/a\), and its total coefficient is
\(1+2(q-1)=2q-1\).  Thus the same source enclosure in
(ISM12) also gives the stronger
\((2q-1)\log((1+\varepsilon)/(1-\varepsilon))\)
bound.

For the actual moment paths of RMT, let \(J_q\) be its
integral of the pointwise minimum of the full-spectrum,
exact trace-norm, overlap, centered Hilbert--Schmidt,
and angle bounds.  Each bounds the identical derivative
(ISM19); the Hilbert--Schmidt member is (ISM26).
Taking their pointwise minimum before integrating gives
the recursively refined source-error statement
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 |\mathcal C|\leq
 \min\{J_q,\mathcal L_q(D),(2q-1)\log(b/a)\},\\
 \mathcal C\in
 [J_L-\epsilon_L,J_L+\epsilon_L]\cap[-J_q,J_q]
 \qquad(L\geq0).
 \end{gathered}
\]
Here \(J_L,\epsilon_L\) are the fully constructed signed
center and residual in (ISM31)--(ISM37).  Their interval
therefore retains its original orientation in this earlier
source-error calculation.  For generic positive coefficient
forms, the full-spectrum, trace-norm, overlap,
Hilbert--Schmidt and signed bounds still apply, but the
moment-specific angle improvement and its strict
moment-positivity proof are used only on the original
moment paths.  The exact primitive and coefficient
support are established in (ISM38)--(ISM43).
Scaling a source by \(c>0\) leaves \(Z_N,\rho_N\) unchanged
and multiplies \(G_N,V_N\) by \(c,c^q\), respectively,
directly by (ISM3).  Thus its four \(q\log c\) terms cancel
in (ISM10); this proves the scalar invariance without
altering any source form.

\subsection{The canonical projector is exactly the original difference}

Use \(x\in[0,1]\) for the comparison path, retaining the
frequency \(y\) and all other deformation parameters.
Write
\[
 M_x=(1-x)M_0+xM_1,\quad \dot M=M_1-M_0,\quad
 X_x=M_x^{-1}\dot M.
 \tag{ISM13}
\]
Since \(M_xX_x=\dot M=\dot M^*\), this is self-adjoint in
the original \(M_x\) inner product.  Define
\[
 \begin{split}
 P_N&=I_N(I_N^*M_xI_N)^{-1}I_N^*M_x,\\
 Q_N&=B_N(B_N^*M_xB_N)^{-1}B_N^*M_x,\qquad Q_{q-1}=0,\\
 \Pi_N&=\rho_NG_N^{-1}\rho_N^*M_x.
 \end{split}
 \tag{ISM14}
\]
Each formula squares to itself and has \(M_x\)-self-adjoint
adjoint law.  The ranges of the first two are
\(\mathcal P_N\) and \(B_N\mathcal P_{N-q}\); the range of
the third is \(\rho_NE\).  By (ISM2)--(ISM3),
\[
 \mathcal P_N=\rho_NE\mathbin{\perp_{M_x}}B_N\mathcal P_{N-q}.
 \tag{ISM15}
\]
To prove the equality, subtract \(\rho_N\pi_\chi p\) from
any \(p\in\mathcal P_N\); (ISM2) puts the result in the
relation range, and (ISM3) proves orthogonality.  Therefore
the original \(P_N,Q_N\) commute with product \(Q_N\), and
their orthogonal range decomposition proves the exact
operator identity
\[
 \Pi_N=P_N-Q_N,\qquad \operatorname{Tr}\Pi_N=q.
 \tag{ISM16}
\]
The inverse of the range parametrization
\(\rho_N:E\to\operatorname{ran}\Pi_N\) is the literal
\(\pi_\chi\) restricted to that range; both composites are
identities by (ISM3).  Thus (ISM16) identifies actual maps
and their domains, not only their traces.

Define
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal A_x&=\Pi_{q-1}+\Pi_q-\Pi_{2q-1}-\Pi_{2q},\\
 U_x&=Q_{2q-1}+Q_{2q}-Q_q,\\
 W_x&=(P_{2q-1}-P_{q-1})+(P_{2q}-P_q).
 \end{split}
 \tag{ISM17}
\]
Substitution of (ISM16), with \(Q_{q-1}=0\), gives
\(\mathcal A_x=U_x-W_x\) and
\(\operatorname{Tr}\mathcal A_x=0\).
These \(U_x,W_x\) are AW's \(U,W\) and SP's
\(\mathcal R,\mathcal P\), respectively.  The source
relation matrix is related to AW's unpadded matrix by
\(B_N=I_NB_N^{\rm AW}\), so (ISM14) reproduces its
actual projections with no sign or metric change.

Differentiating \(\pi_\chi\rho_N=1\) shows that \(\rho_N'\)
has image in the original relation space.  More explicitly,
differentiation of \(B_N^*M_x\rho_N=0\) gives
\[
 \rho_N'=-B_N(B_N^*M_xB_N)^{-1}B_N^*\dot M\rho_N,\qquad
 G_N'=\rho_N^*\dot M\rho_N.
 \tag{ISM18}
\]
The second identity follows because the two cross terms
containing \(\rho_N'\) vanish by the same orthogonality.
All these matrices are smooth on the closed interval,
since their positive Gram inverses exist there.
Jacobi differentiation and cyclicity of finite trace prove
\[
 \frac{d}{dx}\log V_N=\operatorname{Tr}(X_x\Pi_N),\qquad
 \mathcal B'(x)=\operatorname{Tr}(X_x\mathcal A_x),\qquad
 \mathcal C=\int_0^1\operatorname{Tr}(X_x\mathcal A_x)\,dx.
 \tag{ISM19}
\]
For the original row \(v(y)=(1,S,\ldots,S^{2q})\),
\(\Pi_NM_x^{-1}=\rho_NG_N^{-1}\rho_N^*\).  Thus the
signed density in PR29 is exactly
\[
 \kappa_x(y)=v(y)\mathcal A_xM_x^{-1}v(y)^*,\qquad
 \int\kappa_x\,d\mu_x=\operatorname{Tr}\mathcal A_x=0.
 \tag{ISM20}
\]
The first equality keeps the inverse metric that is
required by the original coordinate kernel.  Integrating
against the original signed measure \(\mu_1-\mu_0\)
gives precisely (ISM19).

\subsection{Restriction losses and the full cross trace}

Fix \(i\leq j\) among admitted degrees.  The map
\(\rho_i-\rho_j:E\to\mathcal P_j\) has zero remainder,
so (ISM2) makes it an original degree-\(j\) relation.
Orthogonality of \(\rho_j\) therefore gives the cross-Grams
\[
 \rho_j^*M_x\rho_i=G_j,\qquad
 \rho_i^*M_x\rho_j=G_j,\qquad
 G_i-G_j=(\rho_i-\rho_j)^*M_x(\rho_i-\rho_j).
 \tag{ISM21}
\]
The exact reverse restriction on \(E\) is
\(T_{i,j}=G_i^{-1}G_j\).  It is positive self-adjoint
in \(G_i\), with spectrum in \((0,1]\), by the last
identity.  Cyclic trace and the first two identities give
\[
 \begin{split}
 \operatorname{Tr}(\Pi_i\Pi_j)
 &=\operatorname{Tr}
 (G_i^{-1}(\rho_i^*M_x\rho_j)G_j^{-1}
                  (\rho_j^*M_x\rho_i))\\
 &=\operatorname{Tr}(G_i^{-1}G_j),\\
 \operatorname{Tr}((\Pi_i-\Pi_j)^2)
 &=2\operatorname{Tr}(1-T_{i,j}).
 \end{split}
 \tag{ISM22}
\]
All traces are finite traces of the original coordinate
operators.  In particular their spectra are not declared
to be arithmetic invariant summands.

Set
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 A_0=\Pi_{q-1}-\Pi_{2q-1},\quad
 A_1=\Pi_q-\Pi_{2q},\\
 \ell_0=\operatorname{Tr}(1-T_{q-1,2q-1}),\quad
 \ell_1=\operatorname{Tr}(1-T_{q,2q}),\\
 Z_x=\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal A_x^2)
       =2\ell_0+2\ell_1+2\operatorname{Tr}(A_0A_1).
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{ISM23}
\]
This retains the cross term with its actual sign.
On the full algebra \(\operatorname{End}(H)\), the map
\(L\mapsto M_x^{1/2}LM_x^{-1/2}\) is an algebra
isomorphism with inverse \(K\mapsto M_x^{-1/2}KM_x^{1/2}\).
It sends the \(M_x\)-self-adjoint operators to Hermitian
operators, and cyclicity preserves traces of every
product.  This proves \(Z_x\geq0\) and, from
\(\|A_0+A_1\|_{\rm HS}^2\leq
2\|A_0\|_{\rm HS}^2+2\|A_1\|_{\rm HS}^2\), proves
\[
 0\leq Z_x\leq4(\ell_0+\ell_1).
 \tag{ISM24}
\]
Here and below the Hilbert--Schmidt norm is computed after
this exact metric isomorphism; no new source metric is used.

Put
\[
 c_x=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}X_x}{n},\qquad
 \Sigma_x=\operatorname{Tr}(X_x^2)
              -\frac{(\operatorname{Tr}X_x)^2}{n}.
 \tag{ISM25}
\]
The trace is real by self-adjointness.  Expanding
\(\operatorname{Tr}((X_x-c_xI)^2)\) proves its equality
with \(\Sigma_x\), hence its nonnegativity.
Trace zero of \(\mathcal A_x\), and Cauchy--Schwarz for
the two Hermitian images in the preceding isomorphism, give
\[
 |\mathcal B'(x)|
 \leq S_q(x):=\sqrt{\Sigma_xZ_x}
 \leq2\sqrt{\Sigma_x(\ell_0+\ell_1)}.
 \tag{ISM26}
\]
This bound can be combined pointwise with the existing
full-spectrum, exact trace-norm, overlap, and angle
bounds on the same \(\mathcal B'\).  It does not replace
any smaller available bound.

\subsection{A specified signed approximation and its exact residual}

For an \(M_x\)-self-adjoint, continuous operator \(Y_x\)
define \(E_x=X_x-c_xI-Y_x\).  It is self-adjoint too.
Expanding the trace and applying the same Cauchy--Schwarz
inequality proves the two signed inequalities
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(Y_x\mathcal A_x)
       -\sqrt{\operatorname{Tr}(E_x^2)Z_x}
 \leq\mathcal B'(x)\leq
 \operatorname{Tr}(Y_x\mathcal A_x)
       +\sqrt{\operatorname{Tr}(E_x^2)Z_x}.
 \tag{ISM27}
\]
Every term is real.  Integration is legitimate by
continuity on the compact interval.  We now construct
such a \(Y_x\), with its residual controlled from the
actual two endpoint forms.

Let \(D=M_0^{-1}M_1\), with ordered positive eigenvalues
\(b_1\leq\cdots\leq b_n\), counted with multiplicity.
For this subsection write \(B_x^\circ=(1-x)I+xD\),
to distinguish it from every original relation matrix.
Since \(M_x=M_0B_x^\circ\), direct inversion gives
\[
 X_x=(B_x^\circ)^{-1}(D-I),\qquad
 \lambda_j(x)=1-x+xb_j,\qquad
 \gamma_j(x)=\frac{b_j-1}{1-x+xb_j}.
 \tag{ISM28}
\]
Take an \(M_0\)-orthogonal eigenbasis of \(D\).
It is also \(M_x\)-orthogonal, since on its \(j\)-th
line \(M_x\) multiplies \(M_0\) by \(\lambda_j(x)>0\).
Therefore (ISM28) gives the full spectrum of \(X_x\)
in the original varying metric.

Define the positive scalar and commuting operator
\[
 a_x=\frac{\lambda_1(x)+\lambda_n(x)}2,\qquad
 H_x=I-B_x^\circ/a_x.
 \tag{ISM29}
\]
Their use does not rescale \(M_x\), its moments, or
its quotient Grams.  The eigenvalues of \(H_x\) are
\(\zeta_j(x)=1-\lambda_j(x)/a_x\), and
\[
 \|H_x\|_{M_x}
 =\frac{x(b_n-b_1)}{2(1-x)+x(b_1+b_n)}
 \leq\vartheta:=\frac{b_n-b_1}{b_n+b_1}<1.
 \tag{ISM30}
\]
Indeed the extrema of \(1-\lambda/a_x\) are equal
and opposite at the two endpoints.  The displayed
scalar function of \(x\) has derivative
\(2(b_n-b_1)/(2(1-x)+x(b_1+b_n))^2\geq0\).
This proves the uniform bound, including \(x=0\).

For every integer \(L\geq0\), form the finite matrix
polynomial and its centered version
\[
 \begin{split}
 X_x^{[L]}&=a_x^{-1}
        \sum_{r=0}^L H_x^r(D-I),\\
 Y_x^{[L]}&=X_x^{[L]}
               -\frac{\operatorname{Tr}X_x^{[L]}}n I.
 \end{split}
 \tag{ISM31}
\]
All factors commute with \(D\) and are real functions of
its self-adjoint operator.  They are consequently
self-adjoint in both \(M_0\) and \(M_x\).
The exact finite geometric-series identity
\((I-H_x)\sum_{r=0}^L H_x^r=I-H_x^{L+1}\),
together with \(B_x^\circ=a_x(I-H_x)\), proves
\[
 \begin{split}
 R_x^{[L]}&:=X_x-X_x^{[L]}=H_x^{L+1}X_x,\\
 E_x^{[L]}&:=X_x-c_xI-Y_x^{[L]}
     =R_x^{[L]}-\frac{\operatorname{Tr}R_x^{[L]}}n I.
 \end{split}
 \tag{ISM32}
\]
No infinite-series interchange is used in this identity.
In the original eigenbasis the residual eigenvalues
are \(\delta_j(x)=\zeta_j(x)^{L+1}\gamma_j(x)\).
Thus its exact centered squared size is
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}((E_x^{[L]})^2)
   =\sum_{j=1}^n\delta_j(x)^2
       -\frac{(\sum_{j=1}^n\delta_j(x))^2}{n}.
 \tag{ISM33}
\]
Both sides are nonnegative, and all multiplicities
and signs of the residual eigenvalues are retained.
The signed center is
\(\operatorname{Tr}(Y_x^{[L]}\mathcal A_x)
=\operatorname{Tr}(X_x^{[L]}\mathcal A_x)\)
by \(\operatorname{Tr}\mathcal A_x=0\).
Equations (ISM27), (ISM31)--(ISM33) therefore provide
a finite constructed signed center and its exact error.

There is also a uniform elementary error bound for
this fixed original pair of forms.  Define
\[
 K_D=\sum_{j=1}^n
       \left(\frac{|b_j-1|}{\min\{1,b_j\}}\right)^2.
 \tag{ISM34}
\]
The denominator of \(\gamma_j(x)\) is at least
\(\min\{1,b_j\}\), so (ISM30)--(ISM33) imply
\(\operatorname{Tr}((E_x^{[L]})^2)
\leq\vartheta^{2L+2}K_D\).
The nested relation and source flags in (ISM17)
give both \(U_x,W_x\) eigenvalues
\(0^{[q]},1^{[2]},2^{[q-1]}\): the first relation
line contributes one, its next \(q-1\) increments
contribute two, and the final increment contributes
one; the source increments give the same list.
In particular both have rank \(q+1\), and each
dominates its range projection.  Their ranges in
dimension \(2q+1\) intersect in dimension at least one.
The trace product of the two range projections is
at least that intersection dimension, by summing
squared projected lengths in an orthonormal basis.
For positive operators, increasing a factor cannot
decrease the product trace, since
\(\operatorname{Tr}(T^{1/2}(S'-S)T^{1/2})\geq0\).
It follows that \(\operatorname{Tr}(U_xW_x)\geq1\).
Using their complete spectra, we obtain
\[
 Z_x=8q-4-2\operatorname{Tr}(U_xW_x)\leq8q-6.
 \tag{ISM35}
\]
This proof includes \(q=1\), where the multiplicity
\(q-1\) is zero.

Set
\[
 \begin{split}
 J_L&=\int_0^1
          \operatorname{Tr}(X_x^{[L]}\mathcal A_x)\,dx,\\
 \epsilon_L&=\int_0^1
       \sqrt{\operatorname{Tr}((E_x^{[L]})^2)Z_x}\,dx.
 \end{split}
 \tag{ISM36}
\]
Every integrand is a specified continuous finite
matrix expression on the original source path.
Integrating (ISM27) with the constructed (ISM31)
and the established uniform bound gives
\[
 J_L-\epsilon_L\leq\mathcal C\leq J_L+\epsilon_L,
 \qquad
 0\leq\epsilon_L\leq
       \vartheta^{L+1}\sqrt{K_D(8q-6)}
       \xrightarrow[L\to\infty]{}0.
 \tag{ISM37}
\]
Thus the supplied signed enclosure now has an
explicit convergent residual on every fixed
original finite source.  It retains the arithmetic
correlation in its center.  The evaluation of the
center integral still uses the specified arithmetic
entries and a controlled integration method; this
calculation supplies no uniform estimate in \(k\).
If \(b_n=b_1\), then \(D=b_1I\), \(H_x=0\),
the residual and \(\mathcal C\) vanish exactly,
and every formula above remains valid.

\subsection{The original primitive and the residual at a proper support}

We finally prove the source comparison underlying
all these metric changes.  In the original
one-factor complex \(V\xrightarrow{\Theta}\mathscr B\),
retain \(h(D)F_h=\Theta\phi_*\), with the original
\(D\) action commuting with \(\Theta\).
For \(v\in E\), the polynomial
\((\rho_1-\rho_0)v=\chi Q_v\) has the coefficient
\(Q_v=(Z_N(M_0)-Z_N(M_1))v\) by (ISM4).
In the multivariate polynomial ring put
\[
 f_v(s_1,\ldots,s_k)
  =\chi(s_1+\cdots+s_k)Q_v(s_1+\cdots+s_k).
 \tag{ISM38}
\]
Its image in \(E_h^{\otimes k}\) is zero, because
\(\chi(A_h^{(k)})1=0\).  Successive monic division
in \(s_1,\ldots,s_k\) constructs specific polynomials
\(Q_{v,i}\) as follows.  Let \(f_{v,0}=f_v\) and write
\(f_{v,i-1}=h(s_i)Q_{v,i}+f_{v,i}\), where
\(\deg_{s_i}f_{v,i}<\deg h\).  Earlier remainder
degree bounds are preserved because \(h(s_i)\)
uses only \(s_i\).  The final remainder \(f_{v,k}\)
has degree below \(\deg h\) in every variable and
zero class in the quotient, so it is zero by the
tensor monomial basis.  Telescoping proves
\[
 f_v=\sum_{i=1}^kh(s_i)Q_{v,i}.
 \tag{ISM39}
\]
This constructs a primitive using precisely the
original relations, without replacing them by a
larger observation kernel.

In the tensor complex
\(\mathsf C^\bullet=[V\xrightarrow{\Theta}\mathscr B]^{\otimes k}\)
define
\[
 K_v=\sum_{i=1}^k(-1)^{i-1}Q_{v,i}(D_1,\ldots,D_k)
  \bigl(F_h^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes\phi_*
                   \otimes F_h^{\otimes(k-i)}\bigr)
       \in\mathsf C^{k-1}.
 \tag{ISM40}

The displayed source tensor has exactly \(i-1\)
preceding factors of degree one.  Its differential
therefore has sign \((-1)^{i-1}\); multiplying by
the sign in (ISM40) gives \(+1\).  The source

\(h(D_i)F_h=\Theta\phi_*\) to (ISM39) now proves
\[
 dK_v=\mathcal V((\rho_1-\rho_0)v).
 \tag{ISM41}
\]
Consequently the two canonical sections have the

their common arithmetic observation is exactly
\(\eta_{h,k}v\) by (ISM1).  Composing with the

\(\sigma_h^{\otimes k}\eta_{h,k}:E\hookrightarrow Q^{\otimes k}\).
Here no map was made natural merely by changing

To express the full two-leg source without deleting
its diagonal, retain the labelled Fourier copy
\(\widehat V\), with the original isomorphism
\(\mathcal F:V\to\widehat V\), and the complex
\(\mathsf T^\bullet=
[V\oplus\widehat V\to\mathscr B]\), whose differential
is \((\phi,\widehat\psi)\mapsto
\Theta(\phi-\mathcal F^{-1}\widehat\psi)\).
There are exact cochain maps from the one-leg complex
to this complex with degree-one map identity and
degree-zero maps
\[
 j_+(v)=(v,0),\qquad j_-(v)=(0,-\mathcal Fv).
\]
Indeed either differential equals \(\Theta v\).
The reverse cochain map has degree-one map identity
and degree-zero map
\(p(\phi,\widehat\psi)=\phi-\mathcal F^{-1}\widehat\psi\).
Thus \(pj_+=pj_-=1\), whereas
\((j_+-j_-)(v)=(v,\mathcal Fv)\) is the full
degree-zero diagonal kernel.  These formulas prove
both composites and retain that kernel explicitly.
Tensoring specified choices of these degree-zero

maps, since the displayed differential identities
preserve each original tensor sign.  Apply one such
map to (ISM40) to obtain
\(\widetilde K_v\in(\mathsf T^{\otimes k})^{k-1}\).
Its top differential is still (ISM41).

For precision at a proper declared support

 [d\widetilde K_v]_\lambda\in

 \tag{ISM42}

First specialize to \(k=1\) and the single coefficient
carrier \(\mathscr I=\{i_0\}\), with active face
\(A=\{i_0\}\).  The coordinate projection onto \(i_0\)
and its inverse coordinate insertion identify this
one-coordinate carrier with the original one-factor

\subseteq\mathscr B\).  Suppose the admitted source

\(\Theta V\subseteq\mathscr B_\lambda\).
The precise kernel map in this single coordinate is
\[
 V/W\longrightarrow\mathscr B_\lambda/\Theta W,
 \qquad [v]\longmapsto[\Theta v]_W.
 \tag{ISM43}
\]
It is injective because \(\Theta\) is injective:
\(\Theta v=\Theta w\), \(w\in W\), forces \(v=w\).
Its range is the kernel of the map to the full
quotient \(\mathscr B/\Theta V\), since being in
that kernel means being represented by
\(\Theta v\).  In this case (ISM42) is exactly
the class of the original one-factor primitive
in \(V/W\).

For an arbitrary admitted face \(A\subseteq\mathscr I\),
restore the original coordinate injections

 (V/W)^{\oplus A}\longrightarrow

 \qquad

 \left[\sum_{a\in A}\iota_a\Theta v_a\right].

Replacing \(v_a\) by \(v_a+w_a\), \(w_a\in W\),

injective.  A representative in its codomain maps

\((\mathscr B/\Theta V)^{\oplus\mathscr I}\)

The coordinate identities above then prove both
inverse composites.  This is the exact
\((V/W)^{\oplus A}\) residual, with its full
ambient carrier specified.
