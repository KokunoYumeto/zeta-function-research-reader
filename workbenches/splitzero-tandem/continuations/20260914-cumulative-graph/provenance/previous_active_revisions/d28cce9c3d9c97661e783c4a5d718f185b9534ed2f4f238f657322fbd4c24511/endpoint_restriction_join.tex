\section{Exact arithmetic restriction certificates and projection products}

This article joins the original arithmetic lower-envelope comparison to
the canonical restriction maps. It proves a positive arithmetic return
gap at every finite degree, carries entry errors through the actual
quotient, and identifies all loss traces with observations of two
original source projections. The balanced product gives a complementary
lower estimate on every inverse spectral power in both endpoint windows.
The complete arithmetic envelope and balanced monic-norm proofs are
retained as explicit source dependencies; their constants appear below.
Every finite matrix and restriction consequence used here is proved.

\subsection{The original source, quotient, and canonical lifts}
Retain $g=2\xi$, the monic packet $h$ of selected zeros with their
complete orders, $v_h=g/h$, and
\[
 w_h(t)=\frac{|v_h(1/2+it)|^2}{2\pi},\quad
 \mu_h=\int_\R w_h(t)dt,\quad m_k=w_h^{*k},\quad c=k/2.
 \tag{ERJ.1}
\]
The original pairing, conjugate-linear in its first entry, is
$\langle P,Q\rangle=\int\overline{P(c+iu)}Q(c+iu)m_k(u)du$.
Its constant norm is $\mu_h^k$. The arithmetic construction supplies
positivity almost everywhere and all polynomial moments. A nonzero
polynomial has finitely many zeros on this line, so its norm is positive.
Let $S=s_1+\cdots+s_k$, let the monic generator of
$(h(s_1),\ldots,h(s_k))\cap\C[S]$ be $\chi$, and put
$E=\C[S]/(\chi)$, $q=\deg\chi\ge1$, $A=M_S$.
The ordered quotient coordinates are $1,S,\ldots,S^{q-1}$.
For $N\ge q-1$ write $\mathcal P_N=\C[S]_{\le N}$,
$\mathcal D_N=\chi\mathcal P_{N-q}$, with negative degree spaces zero.
Monic division proves the exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal P_{N-q}\xrightarrow{\times\chi}
\mathcal P_N\xrightarrow{J_N}E\longrightarrow0.\tag{ERJ.2}
\]
In monomial coordinates let $H_N$ be the source Gram. In the original
monic orthogonal basis $p_0,\ldots,p_N$, put
$O_N=\diag(\omega_0,\ldots,\omega_N)$ and
$B_N=[b_0,\ldots,b_N]$, $b_n=[p_n]_\chi$. All $p_n$ stay monic;
$\omega_0=\mu_h^k$. The monic triangular coefficient change has
determinant one and gives the equal constructions
\[
\begin{gathered}
 K_N=J_NH_N^{-1}J_N^*=B_NO_N^{-1}B_N^*,\quad
 G_N=K_N^{-1},\quad V_N=\det G_N,\\
 R_N=O_N^{-1}B_N^*G_N:(E,G_N)\longrightarrow(\mathcal P_N,O_N),
 \qquad K_N=\sum_{n=0}^N\frac{b_nb_n^*}{\omega_n}.
\end{gathered}\tag{ERJ.3}
\]
Here and below a formula with $O_N$ uses monic source coordinates;
its monomial version conjugates by that explicit coefficient change.
Surjectivity of $B_N$ proves $K_N>0$. Multiplication gives
$B_NR_N=I$ and $R_N^*O_NR_N=G_N$. For $z\in\ker B_N$,
$z^*O_NR_N=z^*B_N^*G_N=0$. Thus every lift $R_Nx+z$ has
squared norm $x^*G_Nx+z^*O_Nz$. This proves the unique minimum property,
$\operatorname{im}R_N=\mathcal D_N^\perp$, and, for $i\le j$,
$G_j\preceq G_i$ and $K_i\preceq K_j$.

The actual analytic representative is
$\mathcal V P=P(D_1+\cdots+D_k)F_h^{\otimes k}$, where
$D=-x\partial_x$, $\mathcal MF_h=v_h$, and
$h(D)F_h=\Theta\phi_0$. Its norm is the pairing in (ERJ.1).
Its full-jet map is $J^{(k)}\mathcal V=\eta J_N$, where
$\eta[P]=[v_h]_h^{\otimes k}P(A_k)1$. Each local factor of
$[v_h]_h$ has nonzero constant coefficient, so its inverse is the
finite geometric expansion in its nilpotent part. The kernel of
$P\mapsto P(A_k)1$ is exactly $(\chi)$ by its definition.
These two facts prove that $\eta$ is injective, retaining every local
derivative and arithmetic unit.

\subsection{Return, action, and the entire original relation correction}
Let $L_{i,j}:\mathcal P_i\hookrightarrow\mathcal P_j$ pad monic
coefficients, and let $P_{i,j}$ be the orthogonal projection retaining
their first $i+1$ entries. Then $B_jL_{i,j}=B_i$ and
$L_{i,j}^*O_jL_{i,j}=O_i$. The original identity transport
$I_{i,j}:(E,G_i)\to(E,G_j)$, $x\mapsto x$, is a contraction and
intertwines $A$. Its metric adjoint is
\[
 T_{i,j}=K_iG_j:(E,G_j)\longrightarrow(E,G_i),\qquad
 L_{i,j}R_iT_{i,j}=P_{i,j}R_j,\qquad
 T_{i,j}T_{j,l}=T_{i,l}.\tag{ERJ.4}
\]
Indeed $x^*G_jy=x^*G_iT_{i,j}y$. The first $i+1$ coordinates of
$R_j$ are $O_i^{-1}B_i^*G_j$, exactly those of $R_iT_{i,j}$.
The composition identity is $K_iG_jK_jG_l=K_iG_l$.
For endomorphism types put $U_{i,j}=T_{i,j}I_{i,j}$ on $(E,G_i)$
and $\widehat U_{i,j}=I_{i,j}T_{i,j}$ on $(E,G_j)$.
Their coordinate matrices are $K_iG_j$, and the original invertible
identity transport satisfies $I_{i,j}U_{i,j}=\widehat U_{i,j}I_{i,j}$.
An endomorphism power or eigenvalue of $T$ below means these
specified similar endomorphisms, with all metrics retained.
As an underlying endomorphism of $E$, $T=T_{i,j}$ is self-adjoint
and positive in both displayed metrics: $G_iT=G_j$ and
$G_jT=G_jK_iG_j$. It obeys $0<T\preceq I$ in each metric.
Its eigenvalues $g_a$ lie in $(0,1]$. The squared singular values
of $I_{i,j}$, of its adjoint in the reverse Hilbert types, and of the
restricted canonical lift in (ERJ.4) are these same $g_a$.
Consequently
\[
 H_{i,j}^{\rm loss}=I-T_{i,j},\quad
 \mathcal L_{i,j}:=\log(V_i/V_j)
 =-\log\det T_{i,j}=-\sum_{a=1}^q\log g_a.\tag{ERJ.5}
\]

Write $W_N=A^*G_N+G_NA-kG_N$,
$\mathsf H_N=K_NW_N$, and
$\mathsf V_N=AK_N+K_NA^*-kK_N$. Direct expansion proves
\[
 AT-TA=\mathsf H_iT-T\mathsf H_j
       =\mathsf V_iG_j-K_iW_j.\tag{ERJ.6}
\]
The two $K_iA^*G_j$ terms and the two $kK_iG_j$ terms cancel.
If $\epsilon_N=\|\mathsf H_N\|_{G_N}$, contraction of the adjoint
gives $\|AT-TA\|_{(E,G_j)\to(E,G_i)}\le\epsilon_i+\epsilon_j$.
Thus the original action accompanies the return through its exact
commutator, including on any chosen return eigenspace.
The representative change is the entire map
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \Delta_{i,j}=L_{i,j}R_i-R_j
 =L_{i,j}R_iH_{i,j}^{\rm loss}-(I-P_{i,j})R_j,\\
 B_j\Delta_{i,j}=0,\qquad
 \operatorname{im}\Delta_{i,j}\subseteq\mathcal D_j\cap\mathcal D_i^\perp,
 \qquad \Delta_{i,j}^*O_j\Delta_{i,j}=G_i-G_j.
\end{gathered}\tag{ERJ.7}
\]
Both terms in the difference have remainder $H_{i,j}^{\rm loss}$.
Orthogonality to $\mathcal D_i$ follows for both complete lifts.
Since $R_j$ is orthogonal to $\mathcal D_j$, the equality
$L_{i,j}R_i=R_j+\Delta_{i,j}$ is an orthogonal sum and proves the Gram.

For the explicit theta primitive keep
$C_+=[V\xrightarrow{\Theta}\mathscr B]$ in degrees zero and one,
with $F_h$ of degree one and $\phi_0$ of degree zero. The original
compatible chain action $D$ commutes with this differential.
Successive monic division gives
$\chi(\sum s_a)=\sum_a h(s_a)Q_a(\mathbf s)$ in the tensor ideal.
For any relation $\chi\kappa$ the cochain
\[
 \sum_{a=1}^k(-1)^{a-1}Q_a(\mathbf D)\kappa(\textstyle\sum_bD_b)
 \bigl(F_h^{\otimes(a-1)}\otimes\phi_0
                    \otimes F_h^{\otimes(k-a)}\bigr)\tag{ERJ.8}
\]
has differential $\mathcal V(\chi\kappa)$: applying the tensor
differential gives a second factor $(-1)^{a-1}$ and
$h(D_a)F_h=\Theta\phi_0$; all other terms have zero differential.
Apply this fixed linear division primitive to every column of (ERJ.7).
On an active split fibre the maps are $x^\bullet\mapsto x^\bullet$
and $x^\bullet\mapsto(Tx)^\bullet$, with $\tau\mapsto\tau$.
They are $G(\C)$-linear by the original fibre operations and linearity
of the displayed maps. The relation image in the internal quotient is
the supported zero $(\ell_j,0)$; the external $\tau$ is retained.
The reverse maps compose by (ERJ.4), while their action comparison
is (ERJ.6). No relation, support label, or primitive is deleted.

\subsection{The exact source projection and principal-angle morphism}
Use the common original Hilbert space $\mathcal P_j$ and its isometries
$\ell_i=L_{i,j}R_i:(E,G_i)\to\mathcal P_j$ and
$\ell_j=R_j:(E,G_j)\to\mathcal P_j$. Let $\Pi_i,\Pi_j$ be the
orthogonal endomorphism projections onto these two images. Because
$R_j\perp\mathcal D_j\supseteq\mathcal D_i$, its lower-degree
projection lies in $\ell_iE$. Also $\ell_ix-\ell_jx\in\mathcal D_j$.
Hence
\[
 \Pi_i\ell_j=P_{i,j}\ell_j=\ell_iT,\quad
 \Pi_j\ell_i=\ell_j,\quad
 (\Pi_i\Pi_j)|_{\ell_iE}=\ell_iT\ell_i^{-1},\quad
 (\Pi_j\Pi_i)|_{\ell_jE}=\ell_jT\ell_j^{-1}.\tag{ERJ.9}
\]
These are similarities on the indicated full images. The range of
$\Pi_i\Pi_j$ is $\ell_iE$ because its restriction there is invertible.
Its kernel is $\ker\Pi_j$: the reverse inclusion follows from
invertibility of $\Pi_i:\ell_jE\to\ell_iE$. This gives its direct
sum into that image and kernel, with exactly $q$ nonzero eigenvalues
$g_a$ and $j+1-q$ zero eigenvalues. Reversing the pair proves the
corresponding assertion for $\Pi_j\Pi_i$.

Choose a $G_i$-orthonormal eigenbasis $x_a$ of $T$. Put
$u_a=\ell_ix_a$ and $v_a=\ell_jx_a/\sqrt{g_a}$.
Since $G_j=G_iT$, both families are orthonormal and
$\langle u_a,v_b\rangle=\delta_{ab}\sqrt{g_a}$ by (ERJ.9).
If $g_a=1$ then $u_a=v_a$. Otherwise
$w_a=(v_a-\sqrt{g_a}u_a)/\sqrt{1-g_a}$ makes mutually
orthonormal pairs $(u_a,w_a)$. On each such plane,
\[
 \Pi_i=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \Pi_j=\begin{pmatrix}g_a&\sqrt{g_a(1-g_a)}\\
                    \sqrt{g_a(1-g_a)}&1-g_a\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{ERJ.10}
\]
Their difference has trace zero and square $(1-g_a)I_2$.
Both vanish on the common orthogonal complement, and the shared
directions with $g_a=1$ contribute zero. Therefore, for every real
$s>0$ and in particular each integer $m\ge1$,
\[
 \Tr_{\mathcal P_j}|\Pi_i-\Pi_j|^{2s}
       =2\sum_a(1-g_a)^s,
 \qquad
 \Tr_{\mathcal P_j}|\Pi_i-\Pi_j|^{2m}
       =2\Tr_E\bigl((H_{i,j}^{\rm loss})^m\bigr).\tag{ERJ.11}
\]
Functional powers here use the proved positive spectral decomposition.
The restricted map $\mathcal R=\Pi_i|_{\ell_jE}$ is the bijection
$\ell_iT_{i,j}\ell_j^{-1}:\ell_jE\to\ell_iE$.
For every $s>0$ the exact inverse singular-value-power observation is
\[
 \sum_a\sigma_a(\mathcal R^{-1})^{2s}
 =\sum_a\sigma_a(T_{i,j}^{-1})^{2s}
 =\sum_a\sigma_a(I_{i,j}^{-1})^{2s}
 =\Tr U_{i,j}^{-s}=\sum_a g_a^{-s}.\tag{ERJ.11a}
\]
This follows from $I_{i,j}^{\dagger}I_{i,j}=U_{i,j}$ and the
equal singular values of a map and its adjoint; inversion reciprocates
the singular values. These are Schatten observations of the original
typed maps, including when $0<2s<1$, where no norm triangle inequality
is asserted. Only the bijection between the canonical images is
inverted; the full projection products retain their ambient zeros.
For a return eigenvector $x$ of eigenvalue $g$ the original lower
portion satisfies
\[
 \frac{\|P_{i,j}R_jx\|_{O_j}^2}{\|R_jx\|_{O_j}^2}=g,
 \qquad
 \frac{\|(I-P_{i,j})R_jx\|_{O_j}^2}{\|R_jx\|_{O_j}^2}=1-g.
 \tag{ERJ.12}
\]
Indeed its first squared norm is $x^*G_jK_iG_jx=gx^*G_jx$;
orthogonal decomposition proves the second. Its old quotient coordinate
is $gx$. Applying the invertible $T^{-1}$ recovers $x$; the complete
representative difference is exactly (ERJ.7).

\subsection{The arithmetic remainder bound at every finite degree}
For the actual quartet $1/2\pm\delta\pm i\gamma$ of common order
$m\ge1$, $\delta,\gamma>0$, retain
\[
 \ell=1+k(m-1),\quad q=\ell(k+1)^2,\quad
 \chi(S)=\prod_{a,b=0}^k
 \bigl(S-c-(2a-k)\delta-i(2b-k)\gamma\bigr)^\ell.
 \tag{ERJ.13}
\]
The top nonzero power of the sum of $k$ local length-$m$ nilpotents
is $k(m-1)$, with coefficient $(k(m-1))!/((m-1)!)^k$ on the product
of their top powers; its next power vanishes by total degree. Each
displayed grid sum occurs and the distinct pairs have distinct real
and imaginary parts. This proves the complete order and degree in
(ERJ.13) by taking the least common multiple of local annihilators.

The complete arithmetic-envelope source proves, for $k\ge3$,
\[
 m_k(u)\ge c_h\vartheta_h^{k-3}
 e^{-\alpha(k-3+|u|)}(k-2+|u|)^{-B},\quad
 \alpha=\pi/2,\quad B=42+2\deg h,\quad
 \vartheta_h=\int_{-1}^1w_h>0.\tag{ERJ.14}
\]
Here $c_h>0$ is its original local-mass convolution constant.
Fix $0<b<\pi/2$, $M_h(\pm b)=\int e^{\pm bt}w_h(t)dt$ and
$M_k=M_h(b)^k+M_h(-b)^k$. Positivity and convolution give
$\int e^{b|u|}m_k\le M_k$, and hence
$\int |u|^{2d}m_k\le M_k(2d)!b^{-2d}$.
Set $R_0=\sqrt{\delta^2+\gamma^2}$, $D=\max(1,2/b,2R_0)$,
$T=Dq$, $r_0=kR_0/T\le1/2$, and retain
\[
\begin{gathered}
 a_k(T)=c_h\vartheta_h^{k-3}e^{-\alpha(k-3+T)}(k-2+T)^{-B},\\
 U_k(T)=\mu_h^k+M_k\frac{(2q-2)!}{(bT)^{2q-2}},\quad
 A_N=\sqrt{(2N+1)/2}\,\frac{4^{N+1}-1}{3},\\
 B_q(r_0)=\left(\frac{1+r_0}{1-r_0}\right)^q,\qquad
 C_N=\frac{U_k(T)A_N^2B_q(r_0)^2}{T a_k(T)}.
\end{gathered}\tag{ERJ.15}
\]
The coordinate map $\Psi_TP(x)=P(c+iTx)$ has inverse
$f\mapsto f((S-c)/(iT))$ and target measure $T m_k(Tx)dx$,
whose mass remains $\mu_h^k$. Put
$\chi_T=(iT)^{-q}\chi(c+iTx)$. Then
$\Psi_T(\chi Q)=(iT)^q\chi_T\Psi_TQ$, so remainder commutes
with this map. Every original raw derivative of order $d$ is multiplied
by $(iT)^d$. The quotient coefficient determinant is
$(iT)^{q(q-1)/2}$, and its transported form determinant is
$T^{-q(q-1)}V_N$; this identical factor cancels in a volume ratio.

List all $q$ roots $z_a$ of $\chi_T$ with repetition. Their moduli
are at most $r_0$. Write
$E(t)=\prod_a(1-z_at)$ and $E(t)^{-1}=\sum_{l\ge0}h_lt^l$.
For \emph{every} integer $s\ge0$, multiplication of these formal
inverse series through order $s$ proves that
$Q_s(x)=\sum_{l=0}^sh_lx^{s-l}$ is the quotient of $x^{q+s}$
by $\chi_T$, and that $x^{q+s}-\chi_TQ_s$ has degree below $q$.
Convolution gives $\|\chi_T\|_1\le(1+r_0)^q$ and
$|h_l|\le\binom{q+l-1}{l}r_0^l$, where $\|\cdot\|_1$
is the coefficient absolute sum. The sole coefficient of
$\chi_TQ_s$ at degree at least $q$ is its leading coefficient one.
Its remaining coefficients are the negative remainder. Therefore
\[
 \|\operatorname{rem}_{\chi_T}x^{q+s}\|_1
 \le(1+r_0)^q\sum_{l=0}^s\binom{q+l-1}{l}r_0^l-1
 \le B_q(r_0).\tag{ERJ.16}
\]
The last bound sums the $q$ geometric series. At degrees below $q$
the remainder has norm one. Linearity proves
$\|\operatorname{rem}_{\chi_T}f\|_1\le B_q(r_0)\|f\|_1$
for every polynomial $f$, without a degree restriction on $s$.

Rodrigues' formula for Legendre polynomials gives orthogonality,
squared norm $2/(2n+1)$, and coefficient norm at most
$\binom{2n}{n}\le4^n$. For the norm, integrate by parts $n$ times;
the endpoint terms vanish because $(x^2-1)^n$ has order $n$ there,
and the remaining beta integral gives $2/(2n+1)$. For the coefficient
bound use
$P_n(x)=2^{-n}\sum_{a=0}^n\binom na^2(x-1)^{n-a}(x+1)^a$,
then sum the coefficient absolute values. Cauchy--Schwarz on each
orthogonal coefficient and summation of $4^n$ yield
$\|f\|_1\le A_N\|f\|_{L^2[-1,1]}$ for $\deg f\le N$.
For $\deg v\le q-1$, each monomial at $u/T$ has modulus at most
$\max(1,|u/T|^{q-1})$, so
$\int|v(u/T)|^2m_k\le U_k(T)\|v\|_1^2$.
Finally (ERJ.14) restricted to $[-T,T]$ gives
$\|P\|^2\ge T a_k(T)\|\Psi_TP\|_{L^2[-1,1]}^2$.
Composing these actual maps proves, for every $N\ge q-1$,
\[
 \|\operatorname{rem}_\chi P\|^2\le C_N\|P\|^2,
 \quad G_{q-1}\preceq C_NG_N,
 \quad C_j^{-1}K_j\preceq K_i\preceq K_j
 \quad(q-1\le i\le j).\tag{ERJ.17}
\]
Apply the first inequality to the least lift $R_Nx$ to obtain the
second. Then use $G_j\preceq G_i\preceq G_{q-1}$ and inversion
of positive forms. Taking $i=j$ proves $C_j\ge1$.
Thus the two original endpoint pairs $(q-1,2q-1)$ and $(q,2q)$
have the explicit positive gaps $C_{2q-1}^{-1}$ and $C_{2q}^{-1}$.

\subsection{Finite comparison matrices and the complete trace error}
All following comparisons use the original quotient coordinates.
For positive source brackets $H_N^-\preceq H_N\preceq H_N^+$,
inversion and congruence give
\[
 L_N=J_N(H_N^+)^{-1}J_N^*\preceq K_N\preceq
 U_N=J_N(H_N^-)^{-1}J_N^*.\tag{ERJ.18}
\]
Their positivity follows from surjectivity of $J_N$.
One actual upper comparator uses the density
$\nu_k(u)=c_h\vartheta_h^{k-3}e^{-\alpha(k-3+|u|)}
(k-2+|u|)^{-B}$ from (ERJ.14). Its even moment of degree $d$ is
\[
 \int u^d\nu_k(u)du=
 \frac{2c_h\vartheta_h^{k-3}e^{-\alpha(k-3)}d!}{(B-1)!}
 \int_0^\infty\frac{t^{B-1}e^{-(k-2)t}}{(\alpha+t)^{d+1}}dt;
 \qquad \int u^d\nu_k(u)du=0\ (d\text{ odd}).\tag{ERJ.19}
\]
Insert $(k-2+u)^{-B}=(B-1)!^{-1}\int_0^\infty
t^{B-1}e^{-(k-2+u)t}dt$ on the positive half-line; Tonelli and
$\int_0^\infty u^de^{-yu}du=d!y^{-d-1}$ prove the formula.
Thus $H_N^\nu\preceq H_N$ is an explicit strictly positive source.
It supplies $U_j$ using comparison moments through $2j$, at most
$4q$ at the second endpoint; actual low-degree source data for $i$
stop at $2i\le2q$. These cutoffs keep the actual and comparison
integrals attached to their stated maps.

Given $0<L_i\preceq K_i\preceq K_j\preceq U_j$, set
$\tau=\Tr(L_i^{-1}U_j)$. The Hermitian matrix
$L_i^{-1/2}U_jL_i^{-1/2}$ has all $q$ eigenvalues at least one.
Each is at most $\tau-q+1$ because the remaining $q-1$ contribute
at least $q-1$. It follows that
\[
 U_j\preceq(\tau-q+1)L_i,\qquad
 g:=\frac1{\tau-q+1}>0,\qquad K_i\succeq gK_j.
 \tag{ERJ.20}
\]
This coefficient is optimal from the trace and the lower bound one:
$\diag(\tau-q+1,1,\ldots,1)$ attains it. An upper rational
$\tau_+\ge\tau$ gives the certified rational gap
$(\tau_+-q+1)^{-1}$.

Now take rational Hermitian brackets for both $K_i,K_j$ as in
(ERJ.18), and define the exact rational quantities
\[
 H_*=I-L_iU_j^{-1},\quad t_m^*=\Tr H_*^m,\quad
 d=\Tr(U_iL_j^{-1})-\Tr(L_iU_j^{-1}),\quad r=1-g.
 \tag{ERJ.21}
\]
Here $g$ is certified for the reference pair $(L_i,U_j)$ itself.
For increasing generalized eigenvalues the exact minimum-maximum
formula is
\[
 \lambda_a(F,G)=\min_{\dim W=a}\ \max_{0\ne x\in W}
                 \frac{x^*Fx}{x^*Gx}.\tag{ERJ.22}
\]
For $G=I$, the span of the first $a$ eigenvectors attains
$\lambda_a$; every $a$-dimensional $W$ intersects the span of
eigenvectors $a,\ldots,q$, whose dimension is $q-a+1$, and hence
its maximum is at least $\lambda_a$. The invertible substitution
$y=G^{1/2}x$ proves the general case with subspace dimensions preserved.
The pointwise Rayleigh quotients for $(L_i,U_j)$, $(K_i,K_j)$,
and $(U_i,L_j)$ are ordered. Their extrema are therefore ordered.
If $\alpha_a=\lambda_a(L_i,U_j)$ and
$\beta_a=\lambda_a(K_i,K_j)$, this proves
\[
 g\le\alpha_a\le\beta_a\le1,
 \qquad0\le\sum_a(\beta_a-\alpha_a)\le d.\tag{ERJ.23}
\]
The sum identity uses similarity of $FG^{-1}$ to its positive
Hermitian representative. The kernels $F,G$ are forms on the dual
quotient coefficient space: $G^{-1}F$ there and $FG^{-1}$ on the
quotient are similar through the invertible map $G$.
These $\beta_a$ are the actual $g_a$
of the return. For $r>0$, integration of the derivative of $x^m$
on $[0,r]$ gives the complete moment enclosure
\[
 a_m:=\max(0,t_m^*-mr^{m-1}d)
 \le s_m:=\Tr\bigl((H_{i,j}^{\rm loss})^m\bigr)
 \le t_m^*\le qr^m.\tag{ERJ.24}
\]
Indeed apply $0\le u^m-v^m\le mr^{m-1}(u-v)$ to
$u=1-\alpha_a$, $v=1-\beta_a$ and sum (ERJ.23).
If $r=0$, all $\alpha_a=\beta_a=1$ and both loss operators vanish;
the exact certificate is zero.

Integrating the geometric series on $[0,x]$ proves
$-\log(1-x)=\sum_{m\ge1}x^m/m$ for $0\le x<1$.
All terms are nonnegative. Its tail is bounded by
$x^{p+1}/((p+1)(1-x))$. Thus for every integer $p\ge1$,
\[
 \boxed{\sum_{m=1}^p\frac{a_m}{m}\le\mathcal L_{i,j}\le
 \sum_{m=1}^p\frac{t_m^*}{m}+
                   \frac{t_{p+1}^*}{(p+1)g}.}\tag{ERJ.25}
\]
The width of the two stated endpoints is at most
\[
 \frac{d(1-r^p)}{g}+\frac{qr^{p+1}}{(p+1)g}.\tag{ERJ.26}
\]
The first term sums $r^{m-1}d$ using (ERJ.24); the second uses
$t_{p+1}^*\le qr^{p+1}$. This accounts for the finite matrix
error and series tail separately with their original dimension.

The sharper coefficient
\[
 c_p(r)=\sum_{l\ge0}\frac{r^l}{p+1+l}
 =\frac{-\log g-\sum_{m=1}^pr^m/m}{r^{p+1}}
 \le\frac1{(p+1)g}\tag{ERJ.27}
\]
can replace the last coefficient in (ERJ.25): the tail at $x\le r$
is at most $c_p(r)x^{p+1}$. To certify its logarithm, write exactly
$x=2^e y$ for rational $x>0$, integer $e$, and $1\le y<2$.
