\subsubsection{Every invariant constituent has a nonzero top coefficient}

Let $I:F\hookrightarrow E$ be an actual injective linear map whose
image is $A$-invariant, with $AI=IA_F$, and put $p=\dim F$.
For the moment suppose $0<p<q$.  The image $I(F)$ is an ideal of
$E$: invariance under $A$ implies invariance under every polynomial
in $A$, and every element of $E$ is such a polynomial applied to $e_0$.
Its inverse image in $\mathbb C[S]$ is an ideal containing $(\chi)$.
It is generated by a unique monic divisor $g$ of $\chi$; writing
$\chi=gh$ gives $\deg h=p$ and $\deg g=q-p$.  The exact map is
\[
 \mathbb C[S]/(h)\longrightarrow I(F),\quad [a]_h\longmapsto[ga]_\chi.
 \tag{SC9}
\]
It is onto by the definition of $(g)$, and is injective because
$gh\mid ga$ in the polynomial domain implies $h\mid a$.
Consequently $[g],[Sg],\ldots,[S^{p-1}g]$ is an ordered basis of
$I(F)$.  Its last member is monic of degree $q-1$, while every earlier
member has smaller degree.  Hence the restricted functional
$L=\ell I:F\to\mathbb C$ is onto.  Also $e_0\notin I(F)$, since an
ideal containing the unit is the whole algebra.  We have proved
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \mathcal R I=e_0L,\quad \operatorname{rank}(\mathcal R I)=1,
 \quad\ker(\mathcal R I)=\ker L,\quad\dim\ker L=p-1,\\
 \mathbb C e_0\cap I(F)=\{0\}\quad\text{inside }E.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{SC10}
\]
These arguments never replace a repeated factor by its square-free
part.  In the basis (SC9), $L$ is exactly the last coordinate; in the
original chosen basis of $F$ it remains the literal row $\ell I$.

\subsubsection{Curvature is an exact rank-one expression}

Write $\partial_t=\tfrac12(\partial_x-i\partial_y)$ and
$\partial_{\bar t}=\tfrac12(\partial_x+i\partial_y)$ for $t=x+iy$.
Since $Y$ is holomorphic,
$\partial_tH=Y^*Y'$,
$\partial_{\bar t}H=Y'^*Y$ and
$\partial_{\bar t}\partial_tH=Y'^*Y'$.
Differentiating $H^{-1}H=I_p$ gives
$\partial_{\bar t}H^{-1}=-H^{-1}Y'^*YH^{-1}$.
The determinant derivative and cyclic invariance of trace therefore give
\[
\begin{split}
 \partial_{\bar t}\partial_t\log\det H
 &=\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(H^{-1}Y'^*Y'
   -H^{-1}Y'^*YH^{-1}Y^*Y'\bigr)\\
 &=\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(H^{-1}Y'^*N Y'\bigr).
\end{split}\tag{SC15}
\]
There is no logarithm-branch choice: $\det H>0$.
Insert the full normal map (SC12), use $N^*=N=N^2$, and retain
the rank-one factors to obtain
\[
 \boxed{\partial_{\bar t}\partial_t\log\det H(t)
   =|t|^2 c_F(t),\qquad
 c_F(t)=\frac{\|z(t)\|_{I_q}^2}{|u|^2}
                   L H(t)^{-1}L^*>0.}
 \tag{SC16}
\]
Strict positivity follows because $z(t)\ne0$, $L\ne0$ and
$H(t)^{-1}>0$.  Thus this curvature vanishes exactly at $t=0$
and vanishes there to precisely quadratic order; it is strictly
positive at every $t\ne0$.  For the dual determinant line with metric
$(\det H)^{-1}$ the convention
$c_1=-(i/(2\pi))\partial\bar\partial\log h$ gives
\[
 c_1=\frac i{2\pi}|t|^2c_F(t)\,dt\wedge d\bar t.
 \tag{SC17}
\]
In these coordinates $i\,dt\wedge d\bar t=2\,dx\wedge dy$;
this records the orientation and factor explicitly.

For completeness the exact formula supplies a convergent Taylor
statement.  Entries of $\Pi$ have convergent complex power series;
entries of $H$, $P$, $N$ and $c_F$ are real analytic because
$\det H$ is everywhere positive.  On any closed disk
$|t|\leq\varepsilon$, continuity of the first real derivatives gives
a finite bound $M_\varepsilon=\sup\|\nabla c_F\|$.
Integrating the derivative along the real segment $s\mapsto st$ proves
$|c_F(t)-c_F(0)|\leq M_\varepsilon|t|$.  Consequently
\[
 \left|\partial_{\bar t}\partial_t\log\det H(t)
                  -|t|^2c_F(0)\right|
 \leq M_\varepsilon|t|^3.
 \tag{SC18}
\]
This estimate follows from the actual analytic functions in (SC11),
and (SC16) supplies the stronger identity at all parameters.

One may check the projector's first derivatives directly:
$\partial_tP=NY'H^{-1}Y^*$ and
$\partial_{\bar t}P=YH^{-1}Y'^*N$.
They vanish at zero by (SC12), explaining the retained stationary
tangent space at that parameter without deleting its acceleration.

For clarity, that resolution is free over
$\mathscr B=\mathscr E\otimes_T\mathscr E$ with alternating maps
$X-Y$ and $\mathcal C_H=(H(X)-H(Y))/(X-Y)$, followed by
the multiplication augmentation.  Over
$(T[Y]/H(Y))[X]$ both factors are monic and therefore
non-zero-divisors; their product is $H(X)$ modulo $H(Y)$.
Cancellation proves each alternating kernel equals the preceding
image.  Tensoring by the diagonal sets $X=Y$ and gives the maps
$0,\chi',0,\chi',\ldots$, proving the stated groups.

Fix $t_0\in\mathbb C$, put $\kappa=T/(t-t_0)$ and
$E_{t_0}=\mathbb C[S]/(\chi-t_0)$.  Tensoring (SC27) gives the
complete kernel-and-cokernel sequence
\[
\begin{split}
0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_1^T(M,\kappa)
 &\longrightarrow E_{t_0}\xrightarrow{\chi'} E_{t_0}\\
 &\longrightarrow M\otimes_T\kappa\longrightarrow0.
\end{split}\tag{SC28}
\]
It computes the actual specialized diagonal groups:
\[
 \begin{split}
 HH_{2a+1}(E_{t_0}/\mathbb C)&=M\otimes_T\kappa
                              =E_{t_0}/(\chi'),\\
 HH_{2a+2}(E_{t_0}/\mathbb C)&=\operatorname{Tor}_1^T(M,\kappa)
                              =\operatorname{ann}_{E_{t_0}}(\chi').
 \end{split}\qquad a\geq0.
 \tag{SC29}
\]
Every arrow is induced by the same polynomial multiplication,
so these are equivariant maps for the original $S$-action.

There is also a representative-level description of the Tor map.
Resolve $\kappa$ by $[T\xrightarrow{t-t_0}T]$ to identify
$\operatorname{Tor}_1^T(M,\kappa)$ with the kernel of $t-t_0$ on
$M$.  If $[v]_{\chi'}$ belongs to this kernel, write
\[
  (\chi-t_0)v=\chi'w.
  \qquad [v]_{\chi'}\longmapsto[w]_{\chi-t_0}.
 \tag{SC30}
\]
Replacing $v$ by $v+\chi'a$ replaces $w$ by $w+(\chi-t_0)a$,
so the map is well-defined.  Its image is annihilated by $\chi'$.
If its image is zero, $w=(\chi-t_0)a$ and cancellation of the
nonzero polynomial $\chi-t_0$ gives $v=\chi'a$, proving
injectivity.  Conversely an annihilated class $[w]_{\chi-t_0}$
satisfies $\chi'w=(\chi-t_0)v$ for a polynomial $v$, giving its
preimage.  These operations commute with multiplication by $S$.

At a root $\lambda$ of multiplicity $m$ in $\chi-t_0$, its local
algebra is $\mathbb C[x]/(x^m)$, $x=S-\lambda$, and
$\chi'=m x^{m-1}a(x)$ with the original invertible local factor
$a$ (this equality is in the local algebra: before reduction,
the derivative of $x^ma(x)$ also contains the term $x^ma'(x)$).
The odd group has local module
$\mathbb C[x]/(x^{m-1})$ and the even group has local module
$(x)/(x^m)$; both have dimension $m-1$, with their full induced
$S=\lambda+x$ action.  The map
$[b]_{x^{m-1}}\mapsto[xb]_{x^m}$ is an explicit module isomorphism
between these local modules, using the stated local coordinate.
For $m=1$ both groups vanish.
Thus the zero even group of the family and the nonzero even group
of a collision fibre are related by the exact Tor term (SC28),
not by omitting specialization data.  The coefficient maps in
the residue construction commute with base change; this calculation
retains the additional derived term required when their kernels
are specialized.  Lifting every arrow at its original Split-Zero
support keeps its receiving supported zero and external absence
distinct as in (SC14).

\subsubsection{The exact neighboring-minor identity and its theta factors}

The ideal basis in (SC9) supplies three actual polynomial inclusions.
Write their increasing-power coefficient matrices as
\[
 J_g=[g,Sg,\ldots,S^{p-1}g],\quad
 J_-=[g,Sg,\ldots,S^{p-2}g],\quad
 J_+=[J_g,e_0].
 \tag{SC31}
\]
For $p=1$, $J_-$ has zero columns.  All three are injective on their
stated coefficient spaces; the first two assertions follow from
their distinct monic leading degrees, and the last from
$e_0\notin I(F)$.  Let
$C=(I^*I)^{-1}I^*J_g$.  Since the columns of $J_g$ lie in
$\operatorname{im}I$, this satisfies $IC=J_g$; it is invertible
because both matrices are bases of that same subspace.  The exact
frame equations are
\[
 H_g(t):=(\Pi J_g)^*\Pi J_g=C^*H(t)C,
 \quad LC=(0,\ldots,0,1),\quad
 h_g(t)=|\det C|^2\det H(t),
 \tag{SC32}
\]
where $h_J(t)=\det((\Pi J)^*\Pi J)$ for each of the three
inclusions.  The scalar $|\det C|^2$ has been retained; it is
independent of $t$, so it has zero logarithmic derivatives.

The Gram of $\Pi J_+$ has block form
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 H_g&(\Pi J_g)^*\Pi e_0\\
 (\Pi e_0)^*\Pi J_g&\|\Pi e_0\|^2
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{SC33}
\]
Eliminate its off-diagonal blocks using triangular matrices of
determinant one.  Its lower-right Schur complement is the squared
norm of the orthogonal projection of $\Pi e_0$ away from
$\operatorname{im}\Pi J_g$, which is $\|z(t)\|^2$ by (SC11).
Thus $h_+=h_g\|z\|^2$.  The last diagonal entry of $H_g^{-1}$,
computed by the adjugate formula, is its upper-left principal minor
divided by $h_g$, namely $h_-/h_g$.  By (SC32) that entry is
exactly $L H^{-1}L^*$.  Formula (SC16) becomes
\[
 \boxed{\partial_{\bar t}\partial_t\log h_g(t)
  =\frac{|t|^2}{|u|^2}\frac{h_+(t)h_-(t)}{h_g(t)^2}.}
 \tag{SC34}
\]
This identity retains all three original polynomial maps.  It is
the precise neighboring-minor equation joining the period
calculation to the source-volume and Toda calculations; it does
not identify differently ordered or differently weighted families.

For each same inclusion $J=J_-,J_g,J_+$, define its original theta
volume and its positive comparison determinant by
\[
 v_{N,J}=\det(J^*G_NJ),\qquad
 b_{N,J}(t)=\det\bigl((J^*G_NJ)^{-1}J^*\Pi(t)^*\Pi(t)J\bigr).
 \tag{SC35}
\]
Injectivity and positivity show that both numbers are positive.
For the empty inclusion both are $1$.  Taking the determinant of
the literal product gives $h_J=v_{N,J}b_{N,J}$, and therefore
\[
 c_F(t)=\frac1{|u|^2}
   \frac{v_{N,J_+}v_{N,J_-}}{v_{N,J_g}^{2}}
   \frac{b_{N,J_+}(t)b_{N,J_-}(t)}{b_{N,J_g}(t)^{2}}.
 \tag{SC36}
\]
Every original theta mass and coefficient inclusion remains in
the $v$ factors; every period comparison remains in the $b$
factors.  This proves the metric dictionary required to use
(SC34) within that original source programme.

For $p=q-1$, the divisor $g$ is monic linear.  The square matrix
$J_+=[g,Sg,\ldots,S^{q-2}g,e_0]$ has determinant
$(-1)^{q-1}$: expand along its last column, and the remaining
minor on rows $1,\ldots,q-1$ is upper triangular with every
diagonal entry $1$.  Thus, with that sign retained,
\[
 \det(\Pi J_+)=(-1)^{q-1}\det\Pi,
 \qquad
 h_+(t)=(2\pi|u|)^q
  \exp\!\left(2\operatorname{Re}
        \frac{\operatorname{Tr}\Phi_t(A+t\mathcal R)}u\right).
 \tag{SC37}
\]
The top minor in (SC34) is therefore the explicitly evaluated full
determinant in this codimension-one case.  The remaining two
minors and their exact theta factors are still those in
(SC31)--(SC36).
