\subsection{The cyclic exponent with its retained coefficient map}
\label{sec:btp-cyclic-update}

The characteristic-zero nilpotent exponent in the cyclic-sum calculation
has the following full coefficient form.  This gives the original map
before specialization, its saturation, its precise cokernel, and the
specialized map.  In particular the characteristic-zero injection and
the specialized injection are no longer treated as the same assertion.

Let \(\rho\) be the retained primary centre, let \(m\geq1\) be its
complete order, let \(k\geq1\), and put \(K=k(m-1)\).  Retain a
coefficient subring \(A\subset\mathbb C\) containing \(\rho\), its already specified
unital map \(\alpha:A\to k_0\) to a finite field of characteristic
\(p>m\), and every coefficient of the original full unit and its
inverse.  The finite-field phase used below requires the stronger
\(p>m+1\).  Since the image of \(\alpha\) is a finite subring of a
field, multiplication by any nonzero element of that image is an
injective self-map of a finite set, hence a surjective map.  The image
is therefore a field and \(\mathfrak q=\ker\alpha\) is maximal.
Put \(\mathscr S=A_{\mathfrak q}\).  The extension
\(\widetilde\alpha:\mathscr S\to k_0\) sends \(a/b\) to
\(\alpha(a)/\alpha(b)\); cross multiplication proves independence
of the chosen fraction.  The ring \(\mathscr S\) is a characteristic-zero
domain, \(p\) is a nonzero nonunit, and every integer prime to \(p\)
is a unit.  These statements use this actual coefficient map; they
make no discrete-valuation or principal-ideal assumption on \(\mathscr S\).

The coefficient datum and the phase datum have separate explicit domains.
If \(A_{\rm coef}\) carries the original centre and the full unit and
inverse, the coefficient maps below are defined over
\(A_{\rm coef}\).  Adjoining the original phase requires the map
\(A_{\rm coef}\to A_{\rm coef}[1/(m+1)]\); a source presentation
which also retains \(1/(m+1)!\) uses that larger, explicitly given
localization.  A coefficient map extends to either localization
exactly when its inverted elements have nonzero images: the extension
must send each inverse to the inverse of its image, and that formula
proves existence and uniqueness in the nonzero case.  Thus the
factorial calculation with \(p>m\) does not itself construct a
phase specialization.  For \(m=1,p=2\), its coefficient map, when
given on \(A_{\rm coef}\), sends the one-dimensional cyclic image
by multiplication by the retained unit \(\overline a_0^{\,k}\),
which is nonzero; \(K=0\) and the nilpotent has index one.
This map has no extension to \(A_{\rm coef}[1/2]\), since the
identity \(2(1/2)=1\) would acquire images \(0=1\).
Every finite-field phase statement below uses its actual stronger
datum \(p>m+1\), with all other retained denominator exclusions.

The original objects and the two explicit coordinate substitutions are
\[
\begin{split}
 E_{\mathscr S}&=\mathscr S[s_1,\ldots,s_k]/((s_i-\rho)^m)_{i=1}^k
       \simeq \mathscr S[y_1,\ldots,y_k]/(y_i^m)_{i=1}^k,
       \qquad y_i=s_i-\rho,\\
 J&=\sum_i y_i,\qquad M=\sum_i s_i=k\rho+J,\\
 \mathcal C_{\mathscr S}^{\rm orig}
   &=\mathscr S[Z]/((Z-k\rho)^{K+1})
       \simeq\mathcal C_{\mathscr S}=\mathscr S[T]/(T^{K+1}),
       \qquad T=Z-k\rho .
\end{split}
\tag{BPC.1}\label{eq:bpc1}
\]
The inverse substitutions are \(s_i=y_i+\rho\) and
\(Z=T+k\rho\); both respect the displayed ideals and their
composites fix every generator.  Successive division by the monic
relations \(y_i^m\) proves that the ordered monomials
\(y^{\mathbf b}\), \(0\leq b_i<m\), are an \(\mathscr S\)-basis.

Write \(h=(s-\rho)^m\), \(g=2\xi\), and retain
\[
\begin{aligned}
 v(y)&=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}a_jy^j,
 &a_j&=\frac{(g/h)^{(j)}(\rho)}{j!}
       =\frac{g^{(m+j)}(\rho)}{(m+j)!},\\
 w(y)&=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}b_jy^j,
 &b_0&=a_0^{-1},\qquad
 b_r=-a_0^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^r a_jb_{r-j},\\
 U&=\prod_i v(y_i),&U^{-1}&=\prod_i w(y_i).
\end{aligned}
\tag{BPC.2}\label{eq:bpc2}
\]
All these coefficients belong to the specified \(A\).  Expanding
\(g=(s-\rho)^m(g/h)\) proves the derivative identity.  The
recurrence makes the constant coefficient of \(vw\) equal to one
and all its positive coefficients of degree below \(m\) zero;
thus it proves the stated inverse.  Multiplication by \(U\) is
an actual \(\mathscr S\)-linear automorphism of \(E_{\mathscr S}\), commuting with
\(J\) and \(M\).  The same proof applies to the full unit induced
by a retained quartet packet on this primary factor, using that
unit's original coefficients throughout.

The unchanged cyclic ring map and the actual unit-weighted module map are
\[
 f:\mathcal C_{\mathscr S}\longrightarrow E_{\mathscr S},\quad T^r\longmapsto J^r,
 \qquad
 f_U:\mathcal C_{\mathscr S}\longrightarrow E_{\mathscr S},\quad T^r\longmapsto UJ^r,
 \qquad f_U^{\rm orig}(Z^j)=UM^j .
\tag{BPC.3}\label{eq:bpc3}
\]
All surviving degrees in \(E_{\mathscr S}\) are at most \(K\), so
\(J^{K+1}=0\) and these maps are well defined.  The weighted
map is a map of modules for \(T\mapsto J\), or for
\(Z\mapsto M\) in the original coordinates:
\(f_U(Tc)=Jf_U(c)\) and \(f_U^{\rm orig}(Zc)=Mf_U^{\rm orig}(c)\).
Its value at one is the actual \(U\).  These formulas also give
its type when \(U\ne1\); they do not require it to be a unital
ring homomorphism.

For \(0\leq r\leq K\) set
\[
 V_r=\sum_{\substack{0\leq b_i<m\\\sum_i b_i=r}}
                  \frac{y^{\mathbf b}}{\prod_i b_i!},
 \qquad W_r=UV_r,\qquad V_{K+1}=W_{K+1}=0.
\tag{BPC.4}\label{eq:bpc4}
\]
Every denominator here is a unit because \(b_i<m<p\).
For each \(r\leq K\), a tuple of this degree exists: fill the
slots successively up to capacity \(m-1\).  Select that tuple
\(\mathbf b^*(r)\) and put
\(\lambda_r(x)=\mathbf b^*(r)!\,[y^{\mathbf b^*(r)}]x\).
Then \(\lambda_r(V_t)=\delta_{rt}\).  The coefficient projection
\(\pi(x)=\sum_r\lambda_r(x)V_r\) deletes every selected pivot
coordinate from \(x-\pi(x)\).  Consequently, with \(F\) the
span of all unselected monomials, the maps just given prove
\[
 E_{\mathscr S}=\Lambda\oplus F,\quad
 \Lambda=\bigoplus_{r=0}^K \mathscr S V_r,\qquad
 E_{\mathscr S}=\Lambda_U\oplus UF,\quad
 \Lambda_U=U\Lambda=\bigoplus_{r=0}^K \mathscr S W_r,
 \quad\pi_U=U\pi U^{-1}.
\tag{BPC.5}\label{eq:bpc5}
\]
The sum defining each \(V_r\) is retained in full; its selected
pivot only supplies this explicit splitting.

The multinomial expansion and a direct coefficient calculation give
\[
 J^r=r!V_r,\qquad JV_r=(r+1)V_{r+1},\qquad
 f_U(T^r)=r!W_r,\qquad JW_r=(r+1)W_{r+1}.
\tag{BPC.6}\label{eq:bpc6}
\]
For the first formula the coefficient of \(y^{\mathbf b}\) is
the number \(r!/\mathbf b!\) of its ordered assignments among
the \(r\) factors.  Terms exceeding a primary order vanish in
the defining quotient.  For the second formula the coefficient
at degree \(r+1\) is
\(\sum_{i:b_i>0}b_i/\mathbf b!=(r+1)/\mathbf b!\).
The last two formulas follow by multiplying by the retained
\(U\).  At degree \(K\) there is exactly one tuple, so
\[
 J^K=\frac{K!}{((m-1)!)^k}\prod_i y_i^{m-1}\ne0,
 \qquad J^{K+1}=0,
 \qquad
 f_U(T^K)=\frac{K!}{((m-1)!)^k}a_0^k\prod_i y_i^{m-1}.
\tag{BPC.7}\label{eq:bpc7}
\]
Its coefficient is nonzero in the characteristic-zero domain
\(\mathscr S\).  Every positive Taylor term of \(U\) kills the top
monomial, which proves the last equality with its full original
scalar.  Thus the old characteristic-zero exponent is exactly
\(K+1=1+k(m-1)\), and \(f_U\) is injective: its columns are
the independent \(W_r\) multiplied by the nonzero \(r!\).
This includes \(m=1\): then \(K=0\), the zeroth power is the
identity and the zero nilpotent has index one.

In the original coordinates the same map has matrix
\[
 [f_U^{\rm orig}]_{(s^{\mathbf b}),(Z^j)}
   =P_k^{-1}[U]_{(y^{\mathbf b})}\mathsf V\Delta B,
 \qquad\Delta=\operatorname{diag}(0!,1!,\ldots,K!),
\tag{BPC.8}\label{eq:bpc8}
\]
where \(P_k=P^{\otimes k}\),
\(P_{ab}=\binom ba\rho^{b-a}\) for \(a\leq b\) and zero
otherwise, \(B_{rj}=\binom jr(k\rho)^{j-r}\) for \(r\leq j\)
and zero otherwise, and
\(\mathsf V_{\mathbf b r}=1/\mathbf b!\) for
\(|\mathbf b|=r\), zero otherwise.  The inverses of \(P\)
and \(B\) replace \(\rho\) and \(k\rho\) by their negatives,
as follows by the inverse substitutions in (BPC.1).
The \(j\)-th column on the right is the original polynomial
\(U\sum_r\binom jr(k\rho)^{j-r}r!V_r=UM^j\), proving
the formula and retaining every original coordinate.

Write \(L_U=f_U(\mathcal C_{\mathscr S})\) and
\(d_r=v_p(r!)=\sum_{a\geq1}\lfloor r/p^a\rfloor\).
Counting, for every integer from one to \(r\), each of its
factors of \(p\) proves the sum formula.  The complementary
factor \(\varepsilon_r=r!/p^{d_r}\) is prime to \(p\), so is
a unit in \(\mathscr S\).  We obtain the actual inclusion and quotient
\[
 L_U=\bigoplus_{r=0}^K r!\mathscr S W_r\subset\Lambda_U,
 \qquad
 C_U:=\Lambda_U/L_U\simeq\bigoplus_{r=0}^K \mathscr S/(p^{d_r}),
 \qquad
 [\sum_r c_rW_r]\longmapsto(c_r\bmod p^{d_r})_r.
\tag{BPC.9}\label{eq:bpc9}
\]
The kernel and surjectivity follow coordinate by coordinate from
the displayed basis; the convention \(\mathscr S/(p^0)=0\) is used.
The extension of these quotient coordinates to \(E_{\mathscr S}\) is
\(x\mapsto\lambda_r(U^{-1}x)\bmod p^{d_r}\), and its kernel
also retains the complementary summand \(UF\).  In particular
\(E_{\mathscr S}/L_U\simeq UF\oplus C_U\).
Over \(Q=\operatorname{Frac}\mathscr S\) every \(r!\) is invertible.
The splitting (BPC.5) therefore proves the precise saturation
\[
 (L_U\otimes_{\mathscr S}Q)\cap E_{\mathscr S}=\Lambda_U
        \quad\hbox{inside }E_{\mathscr S}\otimes_{\mathscr S}Q.
\tag{BPC.10}\label{eq:bpc10}
\]
Indeed an integral element in the rational span of \(\Lambda_U\)
has zero \(UF\)-component, since that free module injects into
its scalar extension.  This proves both inclusions without
dividing an original vector by its factorial in \(E_{\mathscr S}\).

Now take either \(B=\mathscr S/p\mathscr S\) or the specified finite-field target
\(B=k_0\), using the actual coefficient map, and put a bar on
each base change.  The splitting and its inverse in (BPC.5)
base change because all their coefficients lie in \(\mathscr S\).
Thus every \(\overline W_r\) remains a distinct basis vector
of a direct summand.  The exact specialized map is
\[
 \overline f_U(\overline T^r)=
 \begin{cases}\overline{r!}\,\overline W_r,&r<p,\\
 0,&r\geq p,
 \end{cases}
 \quad
 \ker\overline f_U=(\overline T^p)
       =\bigoplus_{r=p}^K B\overline T^r,
 \quad
 \operatorname{im}\overline f_U
       =\bigoplus_{r=0}^{\min(K,p-1)}B\overline W_r.
\tag{BPC.11}\label{eq:bpc11}
\]
The direct sum is empty when \(K<p\).  The factorial is a
unit for \(r<p\), and zero for \(r\geq p\); the independent
basis proves every claim.  The unchanged abstract source
\(B[T]/T^{K+1}\) consequently has nilpotency index \(K+1\),
whereas multiplication by \(\overline J\) on its actual
image, on \(\overline\Lambda_U\), and on \(\overline E_{\mathscr S}\)
has index
\[
                         \min(p,K+1).
\tag{BPC.12}\label{eq:bpc12}
\]
For the upper bound, in characteristic \(p\) the intermediate
binomial coefficients vanish, so
\(\overline J^p=\sum_i\overline y_i^{\,p}=0\), while
\(\overline J^{K+1}=0\) still holds.  For the lower bound put
\(d=\min(p-1,K)\).  Then
\(\overline J^d\overline W_0=\overline{d!}\,\overline W_d\ne0\).
This vector is in the image, which proves all three lower
bounds.  All additional chains of the containing lattice are
retained by
\[
 e_{j,t}=\left(\prod_{a=1}^t\overline{jp+a}\right)
           \overline W_{jp+t},
 \quad 0\leq jp\leq K,\quad
 0\leq t\leq\min(p-1,K-jp).
\tag{BPC.13}\label{eq:bpc13}
\]
Each coefficient here is a unit.  Equation (BPC.6) makes
\(\overline Je_{j,t}=e_{j,t+1}\) until the last position;
the next coefficient is zero modulo \(p\), or the next
degree is \(K+1\).  These are all the reduced lattice chains.

The coefficient loss has the exact homological map
\[
\begin{gathered}
0\longrightarrow\mathcal C_{\mathscr S}\xrightarrow{f_U}\Lambda_U
          \longrightarrow C_U\longrightarrow0,\\
0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_1^{\mathscr S}(C_U,B)
 \xrightarrow{\partial}\overline{\mathcal C}_{\mathscr S}
 \xrightarrow{\overline f_U}\overline\Lambda_U
 \longrightarrow C_U\otimes_{\mathscr S}B\longrightarrow0,\\
\operatorname{Tor}_1^{\mathscr S}(C_U,B)=\bigoplus_{r=p}^K B\epsilon_r,
\qquad \partial\epsilon_r=\overline T^r,
\qquad C_U\otimes_{\mathscr S}B=\bigoplus_{r=p}^K B[\overline W_r].
\end{gathered}
\tag{BPC.14}\label{eq:bpc14}
\]
The first line is an explicit free resolution whose matrix is
\(\Delta\) in (BPC.8).  Tensoring that complex with \(B\)
gives the diagonal matrix \(\overline\Delta\).  Its kernel
and cokernel are exactly (BPC.11), proving the last two lines
and the sign of \(\partial\), which is the inclusion into
the degree-one free module for differential \(+f_U\).
When \(K\geq p\), multiplication by \(\overline T\) on
this kernel has index \(K-p+1\): its \((K-p)\)-th power
sends \(\overline T^p\) to the nonzero \(\overline T^K\).
The original source, actual image, containing lattice, and
connecting image are thus all present with their proved maps.

\subsection{The same cyclic map carries the tensor boundary projector}
\label{sec:btp-tensor-boundary-update}

Put \(N=m+1\) and retain the primitive fixed at the original
point \(s=0\):
\[
 \Phi(s)=\frac{(s-\rho)^N-(-\rho)^N}{N},\qquad
 c=-\frac{(-\rho)^N}{N},\qquad
 \sum_i\Phi(s_i)=\sum_i\frac{y_i^N}{N}+kc.
\tag{BTP.1}\label{eq:btp1}
\]
The ordered tensor complex has the commuting differentials
\(u\partial_{s_i}+h(s_i)\) and the original form
\(ds_1\wedge\cdots\wedge ds_k\).  The one-factor monic
remainder decomposition, tensored with its explicit contracting
homotopy, concentrates its cohomology in degree \(k\), with
the free basis \(s^{\mathbf b}ds_1\wedge\cdots\wedge ds_k\),
\(0\leq b_i<m\).  No permutation quotient is taken.

For \(\zeta=e^{2\pi i/N}\) and the fixed branch of
\(u^{1/N}\), keep
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \beta_b&=(b+1)/N,&
 d_b&=N^{\beta_b-1}e^{\pi i\beta_b}\Gamma(\beta_b),\\
 W_{jb}&=\zeta^{j(b+1)}-1,&
 D(u)&=\operatorname{diag}(d_bu^{\beta_b}),\\
 \Pi_k(u)&=e^{kc/u}W^{\otimes k}D(u)^{\otimes k}P_k .
\end{aligned}
\tag{BTP.2}\label{eq:btp2}
\]
Here \(1\leq j\leq m\), \(0\leq b<m\), and the rows are
the ordered products of the original relative contours
\(\Gamma_j=-\ell_0+\ell_j\), whose outward rays have angles
\((\arg u+\pi)/N+2\pi j/N\).  The one-variable substitution
\(t=r^N/(N|u|)\) gives precisely \(d_bu^{\beta_b}\) on
the zeroth ray and its factor \(\zeta^{j(b+1)}\) on the
\(j\)-th ray.  Their oriented difference gives \(W_{jb}\).
Translation to \(y=s-\rho\) is the original one-factor
contour equality: the remote connector integral tends to
zero by the bound
\(\exp(-R^N/(N|u|)+O(R^{N-1}))\) times a polynomial.
It leaves the factor \(e^{c/u}\) intact.  Absolute
integrability permits Fubini on the ordered product contours,
and the binomial substitution gives \(P_k\), proving (BTP.2).
The polynomial \(\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}a_j(z^j-1)\) cannot vanish
at all \(N\)-th roots unless every coefficient is zero;
this proves \(W\) invertible.  Also \(d_b\ne0\), so every
\(\Pi_k(u)\) is invertible for \(u\ne0\).

In \(y\)-coordinates let
\[
 E_r y^{\mathbf b}
       =\mathbf1_{\{\,|\mathbf b|+k\equiv r\pmod N\}}y^{\mathbf b},
 \quad Q_r=P_k^{-1}E_rP_k,
 \quad \widehat Q_r=U_kQ_rU_k^{-1},
 \quad U_k=[U]_{(s^{\mathbf b})}.
\tag{BTP.3}\label{eq:btp3}
\]
These diagonal zero-one masks exist over the exact retained
coefficient ring.  The period monodromy and its projectors are
\[
 M_k=W^{\otimes k}
    \operatorname{diag}(\zeta^{|\mathbf b|+k})
                (W^{\otimes k})^{-1},
 \qquad
 \mathcal P_r=\frac1N\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\zeta^{-rj}M_k^j,
 \qquad
 \mathcal P_r\Pi_k=\Pi_kQ_r.
\tag{BTP.4}\label{eq:btp4}
\]
One positive circuit multiplies the \(\mathbf b\)-th
diagonal entry in (BTP.2) by \(\zeta^{|\mathbf b|+k}\);
its scalar exponential is single-valued.  This proves the
monodromy formula.  The finite geometric sum is one at
matching character and zero otherwise, proving the
projectors and their intertwining identity.  The horizontal
coefficient convention has the inverse monodromy, with the
same invariant index set.

The source-period map is exactly \(\Pi_kU_k^{-1}\).
Indeed the original source has Mellin transform
\(f(s_1,\ldots,s_k)\prod_i(g/h)(s_i)\), whose full jet
is \(U_k[f]_s\); integration by parts for
\(D_i=-x_i\partial_{x_i}\) gives multiplication by \(s_i\)
with vanishing endpoint terms in the original source space.
It follows that
\[
 \mathcal P_r\Pi_kU_k^{-1}
       =\Pi_kU_k^{-1}\widehat Q_r.
\tag{BTP.5}\label{eq:btp5}
\]
This is the exact source comparison, including the entire
unit and its inverse.  It does not replace the source norm
by the auxiliary period coordinate norm.

The invariant dimension is
\[
 d_{0,k}=\frac{m^k+m(-1)^k}{m+1}.
\tag{BTP.6}\label{eq:btp6}
\]
To prove it, filter the ordered tuples by their total
character:
\(N^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(\sum_{a=1}^{N-1}\zeta^{ja})^k\).
The inner sum is \(m\) for \(j=0\) and \(-1\) otherwise,
giving the formula.  Thus the one-factor vanishing remains
true at \(k=1\); tensor invariants appear for every
\(m\geq2,k\geq2\), and for \(m=1\) precisely when
\(k\) is even.  These are the ordinary local-system
invariants at the original puncture.

The original arithmetic matrix is
\(A_k=k\rho I+R_k\), where \(R_k=P_k^{-1}[J]P_k\).
Each surviving multiplication by \(y_i\) increases the
boundary character by one.  Therefore, on every monomial,
\[
\begin{gathered}
 Q_rR_k^j=R_k^jQ_{r-j},\qquad
 [Q_0,A_k]=R_k(Q_{-1}-Q_0),\\
 Q_0A_kQ_0=k\rho Q_0,\qquad
 (I-Q_0)A_kQ_0=R_kQ_0.
\end{gathered}
\tag{BTP.7}\label{eq:btp7}
\]
Terms that exceed a primary order are zero on both sides.
Conjugation by the commuting \(U_k\) gives the identical
formulas on the actual source invariant plane.  In
particular the scalar compression retains the explicit
outgoing map.

For \(\operatorname{im}Q_0\ne0\), set
\(d_{\min}=N\lceil k/N\rceil-k\) and
\(q_* =\lfloor(K-d_{\min})/N\rfloor\).  Put
\(R_0=Q_0R_k^NQ_0|_{\operatorname{im}Q_0}\).  Then the
complete dilation on this plane is
\[
 Q_0a^{A_k}Q_0|_{\operatorname{im}Q_0}
   =a^{k\rho}\sum_{j=0}^{q_*}
       \frac{(\log a)^{jN}}{(jN)!}R_0^j,
 \qquad R_0^{q_*}\ne0,\quad R_0^{q_*+1}=0.
\tag{BTP.8}\label{eq:btp8}
\]
Expand the original finite exponential through degree
\(K\).  Equation (BTP.7) removes exactly the powers
whose degree is not divisible by \(N\).  Every invariant
input has degree at least \(d_{\min}\), proving the upper
cutoff.  At that minimum degree, distribute any increment
\(jN\leq K-d_{\min}\) among the remaining slot capacities.
Its multinomial coefficient is positive and distinct
monomials cannot cancel, proving the stated nonzero powers.
The source compression is the conjugate of (BTP.8) by the
full \(U_k\).

The original terminal monomial \(\tau_k=\prod_i y_i^{m-1}\)
satisfies
\[
 A_k\tau_k=k\rho\tau_k,\qquad
 U_k\tau_k=a_0^k\tau_k,\qquad
 Q_0\tau_k=\widehat Q_0\tau_k
   =\mathbf1_{\{N\mid k\}}\tau_k .
\tag{BTP.9}\label{eq:btp9}
\]
Multiplication by every \(y_i\) kills this top monomial,
proving the first two formulas.  Its character is
\(km\equiv-k\pmod N\), proving the third, including
the unit conjugation.  Its exact period is
\[
 \Pi_k\tau_k=e^{kc/u}
   \left(N^{-1/N}e^{\pi im/N}\Gamma(m/N)\right)^k
        u^{km/N}(\zeta^{-j}-1)_{j=1}^{m}{}^{\otimes k}.
\tag{BTP.10}\label{eq:btp10}
\]
This is its column in (BTP.2); all its factors are
nonzero.  The corresponding actual corrected-section
class has observation \(a_0^{-k}\Pi_k\tau_k\).
For the original amplified quartet vector the retained
coefficient is
\(C_{h_0,k}=K!((m-1)!)^{-k}((g/h_0)(\rho))^k\), so
its observation is exactly
\(C_{h_0,k}a_0^{-k}\Pi_k\tau_k\).  Multiplication by
\((g/h_0)\) on the terminal monomial has this scalar
because all its positive Taylor terms vanish; this is
also (BPC.7) for that retained unit.  Therefore, on the
infinite subsequence \(N\mid k\), the same original
terminal arithmetic eigenclass lies in the boundary
invariant plane.  Its original dilation still satisfies
\[
 \log\left(\frac{|a^{k\rho}|^2}{a^k}\right)
        =2k(\operatorname{Re}\rho-1/2)\log a.
\tag{BTP.11}\label{eq:btp11}
\]
Taking the real part of \(k\rho\log a\) proves the
identity.  No invariant-plane projection has altered
this arithmetic scalar.

\subsection{Finite-field boundary and the actual coefficient-specialization restriction}
\label{sec:btp-finite-field-update}

Use the already specified finite-field map with
\(p>N\), and write \(k_0=\mathbb F_{Q_0}\),
\(L=\mathbb F_{Q_0^d}\),
\(d=\operatorname{ord}_{(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^\times}(Q_0)\).
Thus \(L\) contains \(\mu_N\).  Retain
\(\psi=\psi_0\circ\operatorname{Tr}_{L/k_0}\),
geometric Frobenius, the original compact-support
one-factor sheaf, and every one-factor Gauss constant.
The already proved SPF decomposition, distributed over
the ordered tensor factors on the common \(u\)-line,
gives the exact isomorphism
\[
 \mathcal F_L^{\otimes k}
   \simeq \mathcal L_\psi(kc/u)\otimes
   \bigoplus_{\substack{\boldsymbol\chi=(\chi_1,\ldots,\chi_k)\\
                       \chi_i^N=1,\ \chi_i\ne1}}
       \mathcal A_{A_{\boldsymbol\chi}}
               \otimes\mathcal K_{\prod_i\chi_i}(u),
 \quad
 A_{\boldsymbol\chi}
    =\prod_i\left(-\chi_i(N)
          \sum_{z\in L^\times}\chi_i(z)\psi(z)\right).
\tag{BTF.1}\label{eq:btf1}
\]
The torsor tensor laws add the Artin--Schreier phases
and multiply the Kummer characters.  Distribution of
tensor over direct sum retains every ordered tuple,
even if different tuples have equal product characters.
These facts prove the displayed map from the established
one-factor map.  The compact-support Kunneth map places
it in degree \(k\) on the original ordered product
source, since each one-factor complex is concentrated
in degree one.  No factor permutation changes its sign.

For \(c\ne0\), choose the original additive character
\(\psi_0(x)=\vartheta(\operatorname{Tr}_{k_0/\mathbb F_p}(bx))\).
The cover \(u=v^N\), \(z^p-z=bc/v^N\) has deck
group \(\mu_N\times\mathbb F_p\).  Its degree is
\(Np\): the second right side has pole order \(N\)
prime to \(p\), whereas a pole of \(f^p-f\) has order
divisible by \(p\), so it is a nontrivial degree-\(p\)
extension.  The displayed commuting deck maps
\((v,z)\mapsto(\zeta v,z+a)\) then exhaust that degree.
On the tuple line the original action is
\[
 (\zeta,a)\longmapsto
   \lambda_{\prod_i\chi_i}(\zeta)^{-1}\vartheta(-ka),
\tag{BTF.2}\label{eq:btf2}
\]
where \(\lambda_\chi(x^{(Q-1)/N})=\chi(x)\) and
\(Q=Q_0^d\).  These are the original SPF inverse
deck-action conventions, tensored without changing
them.  Averaging over the complete finite inertia
image in the characteristic-zero \(\ell\)-adic
coefficient field proves
\[
 P_{I_0,k}=
 \begin{cases}P^{\rm tame}_{0,k},&kc=0,\\
 0,&kc\ne0,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 \dim V_k^{I_0}=
 \begin{cases}\dfrac{m^k+m(-1)^k}{m+1},&kc=0,\\
 0,&kc\ne0.
 \end{cases}
\tag{BTF.3}\label{eq:btf3}
\]
The tame average is one exactly when \(\prod_i\chi_i=1\).
The wild average \(p^{-1}\sum_a\vartheta(-ka)\) is one
for \(p\mid k\) and zero otherwise.  For \(c=0\) the
original additive torsor is trivial, so only the tame
average is present.  Since \(kc=0\) is equivalent to
\(c=0\) or \(p\mid k\), these computations prove
the full case distinction.  The tuple count is the
same geometric sum proved in (BTP.6).

For \(kc\ne0\), the Artin--Schreier factor of
\(z^p-z=bkc/u\) has a single break one: \(z^{-1}\)
is a uniformizer and its difference under
\(z\mapsto z+a\), \(a\ne0\), has valuation two.
Every tuple line has that same wild factor, while
Kummer twists are tame.  Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Swan}_0(\mathcal F_L^{\otimes k})
   =\begin{cases}0,&kc=0,\\m^k,&kc\ne0,\end{cases}
 \qquad N_{\rm mon,k}=0
   \ \text{after }u=v^N,\ z_k^p-z_k=bkc/v^N.
\tag{BTF.4}\label{eq:btf4}
\]
The cover trivializes every geometric character, so
its monodromy is the identity and its logarithm is
zero.  This leaves the original-inertia invariant
space in (BTF.3) unchanged as a separately specified
stalk over the original base.

The exact boundary Frobenius for \(kc=0\) over \(L\)
is the direct sum of the scalars \(A_{\boldsymbol\chi}\)
with \(\prod_i\chi_i=1\).  Over \(k_0\), geometric
Frobenius sends \(\boldsymbol\chi\) to
\(\boldsymbol\chi^{Q_0}\): conjugating the SPF deck
projector by Frobenius replaces its deck argument
by \(\zeta^{Q_0^{-1}}\), and reindexing yields that
character power.  For an orbit \(\mathcal O\) of
length \(e\), each \(\chi_i\) descends uniquely by
norm to \(\kappa_{i,e}\) over \(L_e=\mathbb F_{Q_0^e}\).
Indeed its order divides \(Q_0^e-1\), so it kills
the norm kernel; norm surjectivity proves uniqueness.
Retaining the original denominator \(N\), the
one-factor SPF calculation over this orbit field gives
\[
 \Lambda_{\mathcal O}
   =\prod_i\left(-\kappa_{i,e}(N)
       G_{L_e}(\kappa_{i,e},\psi_0\circ\operatorname{Tr}_{L_e/k_0})\right),
 \qquad
 \det(1-tF_0\mid V_k^{I_0})
       =\prod_{\mathcal O}(1-\Lambda_{\mathcal O}t^e).
\tag{BTF.5}\label{eq:btf5}
\]
For the descent map when \(L_e\) does not contain
all \(N\)-th roots, factor the source power map as
\(y\mapsto x=y^{N/r_i}\mapsto z=x^{r_i}=y^N\),
where \(r_i=\operatorname{ord}\chi_i\).  Functions
constant on the \(\mu_{N/r_i}\)-orbits are exactly
functions on the intermediate root set; the same
map is the identity at the zero fibre.  This
identifies the descended \(\kappa_{i,e}\)-summand
of the original sheaf, whose phase is still
\(z/(Nu)+c/u\).  Thus the displayed scalar is the
actual \(F_0^e\)-action.  A basis
\(v,F_0v,\ldots,F_0^{e-1}v\) of distinct tuple
lines gives the factor \(1-\Lambda_{\mathcal O}t^e\).
The unscaled Gauss absolute-square calculation
\(|G_{L_e}|^2=Q_0^e\) therefore gives
\(|\Lambda_{\mathcal O}|=Q_0^{ek/2}\) and weight
\(k\) for every nonzero boundary orbit block.

To specify the comparison of characteristic types,
put \(B_0=\mathbb Z[\zeta_N,1/N]\) and
\[
 e_j=\frac1N\sum_{a=0}^{N-1}\zeta_N^{-ja}\gamma^a
          \in B_0[C_N],\quad
 M_{B_0}=B_0[y]dy/y^mB_0[y]dy,
 \quad e_j\longmapsto y^{j-1}dy\quad(1\leq j<N).
\tag{BTF.6}\label{eq:btf6}
\]
Give \(\gamma\) the action
\(\gamma(y^bdy)=\zeta_N^{b+1}y^bdy\).  The finite
geometric sum proves that the \(e_j\) are complete
orthogonal idempotents, and the displayed basis
map is an isomorphism
\(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{N-1}B_0e_j\simeq M_{B_0}\).
The actual maps \(B_0\to L\) and
\(B_0\to\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\) send
\(\zeta_N\) to the respective chosen primitive
roots.  They give, respectively, the coefficient
monomial-form projector and the actual SPF Gauss-line
projector: the latter is
\(N^{-1}\sum_a\zeta_\ell^{-ja}D_\zeta^{-a}
 =N^{-1}\sum_a\zeta_\ell^{ja}D_\zeta^a\).
If \(H_j\) is that complete Gauss line, retain its
Frobenius and reconstruct its isotypic component by
\((ae_j)\otimes h\mapsto ah\) from
\((B_0e_j\otimes\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell)
 \otimes H_j^{\rm triv}\), where only the deck
action on the second factor is made trivial.
The scalar map is bijective on each summand and
preserves both deck action and the retained
splitting-field Frobenius, proving the comparison.
Ordered tensor products of these maps retain every
tuple.  This is the common integral representation
and its two proved base changes; it introduces no
field map from characteristic \(p\) to
\(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\).

The terminal form has deck-pullback character
\(\zeta^{m}=\zeta^{-1}\) in each factor; let its
primitive Kummer character be \(\chi_{\rm ter}\).
Its ordered sheaf line is consequently
\[
 \mathcal L_\psi(kc/u)\otimes
 \mathcal A_{[-\chi_{\rm ter}(N)G_L(\chi_{\rm ter},\psi)]^k}
            \otimes\mathcal K_{\chi_{\rm ter}^k}(u).
\tag{BTF.7}\label{eq:btf7}
\]
It has invariants exactly for \(N\mid k\) and
\(kc=0\); this means \(N\mid k\) if \(c=0\),
and \(pN\mid k\) if \(c\ne0\).  Its original
base orbit has length \(d\), because its character
is primitive, with polynomial
\(1-[-\chi_{\rm ter}(N)G_L(\chi_{\rm ter},\psi)]^kt^d\).
These claims follow from (BTF.2)--(BTF.5) without
assigning a finite-field Frobenius to an original
complex arithmetic scalar.

On a coefficient stage admitting the specified map,
the original amplified coefficient instead has the
literal coefficient image
\[
 \overline C_{h_0,k}
   =\overline{K!}\,
      (\overline{(m-1)!})^{-k}\overline u_0^{k},
 \quad u_0=(g/h_0)(\rho),\qquad
 \overline C_{h_0,k}\ne0
   \ \Longleftrightarrow\ K<p\text{ and }\overline u_0\ne0.
\tag{BTF.8}\label{eq:btf8}
\]
The denominator is a unit since \(m-1<p\), while
\(K!\) vanishes exactly for \(K\geq p\).  Retaining
the inverse of the original full unit ensures
\(\overline u_0\ne0\).  The target is the coefficient
algebra \(k_0[y_1,\ldots,y_k]/(y_i^m)\), with
coefficient map obtained by applying \(\alpha\)
to every coefficient in its unchanged monomial
basis.  Its terminal basis vector remains nonzero;
the original multiple vanishes precisely as in
(BTF.8).  This is the map (BPC.11).  It does not
multiply a characteristic-\(p\) scalar into the

For \(m\geq2\), the wild-cancellation orders
\(p\mid k\) have \(K\geq p\), so the specialized

The sheaf invariant lines calculated above
remain as specified by (BTF.7).  For \(m=1\),

Indeed complex conjugation gives
\(\overline G=\chi(-1)G\), while \(G\overline G=Q\),
which proves the algebraic equality in its

with \(c\ne0\), the exact invariant eigenvalue

Finally, the local coefficient map does not
silently extend through new CRT denominators.
In the original off-critical quartet branch
\(\rho=1/2+\delta+i\gamma\), \(\delta>0\),
the complete degree-\(k\) split corner has
centres \(\lambda_0=k\rho\) and
\(\lambda_1=(k-p)\rho+p(1-\overline\rho)\)
whenever \(k\geq p\).  Retain its actual
terminal CRT polynomial \(e(Z)=\sum_r e_rZ^r\)
with \(e(\lambda_0)=1\), \(e(\lambda_1)=0\).
In its own coefficient ring set
\[
 \mathscr D=\sum_{r\geq1}e_r
       \sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\lambda_0^{r-1-j}\lambda_1^j,
 \qquad
 1=p(\rho+\overline\rho-1)\mathscr D,
 \qquad p^{-1}=(\rho+\overline\rho-1)\mathscr D.
\tag{BTF.9}\label{eq:btf9}
\]
The divided difference telescopes term by term:
\((\lambda_0-\lambda_1)\mathscr D
=e(\lambda_0)-e(\lambda_1)=1\).
Since \(\lambda_0-\lambda_1
=p(\rho+\overline\rho-1)\), this proves all
the formulas.  Hence that full stage inverts
\(p\) and has no unital characteristic-\(p\)
specialization.  Replacing \(p\) by any
rational prime \(q\leq k\) proves that the
degree-\(k\) split corner inverts every such
prime.  The directed coefficient union of
all these retained stages therefore contains
\(\mathbb Q\): each reciprocal prime occurs
at its corresponding stage.  The explicit
local maps (BPC.3), (BPC.8), (BPC.11) and
(BPC.14) continue to relate the local
characteristic-zero source, its lattice and
its valid coefficient specialization.  The
absence of an extension of that specialization
is not the zero image of a vector under a
nonexistent map.

 \widehat Q_cf_U=f_UD_c,\qquad

For \(k=p\ell\), \(m\geq2\), its surviving

\begin{gathered}
 \overline J\,\overline W_{p-1}=0,

Equation (BPC.6) and the primitive pivot prove
