\section{Exact arithmetic quotient transport and its unavoidable window defect}
\label{sec:qt-transport}

This calculation uses the literal four windows of (AT13) and (AW5),
the source isometry constructed by (AW9)--(AW10), and the arithmetic
observation of (AT2a). The principal-angle calculations (EW.25)--(EW.35c)
remain available; the new calculation concerns the arithmetic observation
of a repaired source map and the exact operator left in its intertwining
identity.

\subsection{The retained source, forms, and arithmetic maps}

Fix an actual full packet polynomial \(h\) of \(g=2\xi\), an integer
\(k\ge1\), and the original cyclic annihilator \(\chi=\chi_{h,k}\),
monic of degree \(q\ge1\). Retain
\[
 E=\mathbb C[S]/(\chi),\qquad E_h=\mathbb C[s]/(h),\qquad
 H=\mathbb C[S]_{\le2q},\qquad S(u)=k/2+iu.
 \tag{QT.1}\label{eq:qt-objects}
\]
The increasing-power remainder coordinates of \(E\) are fixed.
The actual arithmetic injection, including its full Taylor unit, is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \eta:E&\longrightarrow E_h^{\otimes k},&
 \eta[P]&=\upsilon_h^{\otimes k}P(A_h^{(k)})1,\\
 \upsilon_h&=j_h(g/h),&
 Q_{\rm ar}&=\mathscr B/\Theta V,\\
 \mathcal I&=\sigma_h^{\otimes k}\eta:
                  E\hookrightarrow Q_{\rm ar}^{\otimes k},&
 \mathcal O&=\mathcal I J:H\longrightarrow Q_{\rm ar}^{\otimes k}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{QT.2}\label{eq:qt-observation}
\]
Here \(A_h^{(k)}\) is the original tensor-sum multiplication operator,
\(J\) is literal remainder modulo \(\chi\), \(j_h\) takes the full
Taylor remainder modulo \(h\), and \(\sigma_h:E_h\hookrightarrow Q_{\rm ar}\)
is the original section. Both injections in (QT.2) are the source
constructions of (AT2)--(AT2a), not new choices. In particular no
nilpotent Taylor coefficient of \(h\), \(\chi\), or \(\upsilon_h\)
is discarded. The target is the top cohomology of the original tensor
pushforward over \(\mathbf F_{1,\tau}=\{\tau,1\}\), with one-factor
complex \([V\oplus V\longrightarrow\mathscr B]\) and differential
\((\phi,\psi)\mapsto\Theta(\phi-\widehat\psi)\).

Use precisely the two densities
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 m_1&=w_h^{*k},&
 w_h(u)&=\frac{|(g/h)(1/2+iu)|^2}{2\pi},\\
 m_0(u)&=\frac{(\sqrt{2\pi})^k}{c_{k/4}}
                  \frac{|\Gamma(k/4+iu/2)|^2}{2\pi},&
 c_a&=2^{1-2a}\Gamma(2a),\\
 m_t&=(1-t)m_0+tm_1,\quad 0\le t\le1,&
 v(u)&=(1,S(u),\ldots,S(u)^{2q}),\\
 M(t)&=\int_{\mathbb R}v(u)^*v(u)m_t(u)\,du.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{QT.3}\label{eq:qt-metric}
\]
These densities have the positive almost-everywhere property and all
finite polynomial moments proved in the supplied AT sources. Their
masses are retained:
\(\int m_t=(1-t)(2\pi)^{k/2}+t\mu_h^k\), where
\(\mu_h=\int w_h\).
All orthogonality, operator adjoints, singular values, and
Hilbert--Schmidt norms below use the positive form
\(\langle x,y\rangle_t=x^*M(t)y\).
For operators on \(H\), write \(Z^\sharp=M^{-1}Z^*M\) and
\(\|Z\|_{\mathrm{HS},t}^2=\operatorname{Tr}(Z^\sharp Z)\).
For maps between its orthogonal subspaces, the same notation uses their
restricted forms. A star on a coefficient matrix means its ordinary
conjugate transpose.

At a fixed \(t\), put
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 F=\chi\mathbb C[S]_{\le q}=\ker J,\qquad K=F^\perp,\qquad
 L=\mathbb C[S]_{\le q-1},\\
 \mathsf Q=P_F,\quad \Pi=I-\mathsf Q=P_K,\quad P=P_L,\\
 s:E\longrightarrow L\subset H,\quad s[P]=\text{the degree }<q
       \text{ remainder},\\
 R=R_{2q}(t)=\Pi s,\qquad
 G=R^*MR,\qquad G_\ell=s^*Ms.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{QT.4}\label{eq:qt-splitting}
\]
The symbol \(P_X\) denotes the orthogonal projection onto the specified
subspace \(X\). Monic division gives the literal algebraic direct sum
\(H=L\oplus F\); it does not declare these two subspaces orthogonal.
The minimum-section formula (AT6) proves \(JR=I_E\) and
\(\Pi=RJ\). Thus \(R:(E,G)\to K\) is an isometry with inverse
\(J|_K\). The low-degree Gram is \(G_\ell=G_{q-1}(t)\).

Retain \(U,W,T\) exactly as in (AW5)--(AW10). Those source formulas prove
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 T^\sharp T=TT^\sharp=I,\qquad UT=TW,\qquad
 TL=K,\quad T(L^\perp)=F,\\
 U|_K=0,\qquad I_F\preceq U_F:=U|_F\preceq2I_F.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{QT.5}\label{eq:qt-inputT}
\]
The eigenvalues of \(U_F\) are \(1,1\) and \(q-1\) copies of \(2\).
For \(q=1\), they are just \(1,1\).
In the orthogonal decomposition \(H=K\oplus F\), define its fully typed
blocks by
\[
 T=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 a:K\to K,\ b:F\to K,\ c:K\to F,\ d:F\to F.
 \tag{QT.6}\label{eq:qt-blocks}
\]
In particular \(\Pi T=TP\).

\subsection{An invertible repair preserving every arithmetic coefficient}

Both square diagonal blocks in (QT.6) are invertible. Indeed, if
\(ax=0\) for \(x\in K\), then \(Tx\in F=T(L^\perp)\), so
\(x\in K\cap L^\perp=(F+L)^\perp=0\).
If \(dy=0\) for \(y\in F\), then \(Ty\in K=TL\), so
\(y\in F\cap L=0\). These are injective endomorphisms of finite
spaces, hence bijective.

Define
\[
 X=\mathsf Q T+\Pi
   =\begin{pmatrix}I_K&0\\c&d\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 X^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}I_K&0\\-d^{-1}c&d^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{QT.7}\label{eq:qt-repair}
\]
Multiplying these displayed block matrices gives both inverse identities.
Since \(J\mathsf Q=0\) and \(J\Pi=J\), they obey
\[
 JX=J,\qquad JX^{-1}=J,\qquad
 \mathcal OX=\mathcal O,\qquad \mathcal OX^{-1}=\mathcal O.
 \tag{QT.8}\label{eq:qt-preserve}
\]
These are equalities in the original target, before changing its
presentation. For every original coefficient operator \(B\) on that
target for which the composition is defined,
\(B\mathcal OX=B\mathcal O\). This statement does not require \(B\)
to preserve the cyclic subspace \(\mathcal I(E)\). On that cyclic
subspace the induced map of \(X\) is exactly \(I_E\); it therefore
commutes with every multiplication \(f(A_\chi)\), where \(A_\chi\)
is multiplication by \(S\) on \(E\) and \(f\in\mathbb C[S]\).
The complete arithmetic unit in (QT.2) remains in both sides.
At any nonempty original support label \(A\), the lift is
\((A,x)\mapsto(A,Xx)\), and \(\tau\mapsto\tau\). Consequently the
two supported arithmetic coefficients coincide at that label,
including supported zero; no supported zero is replaced by \(\tau\).

Moreover, \(X\) is the unique linear map nearest to \(T\), in the
original Hilbert--Schmidt norm, among all maps \(Z\) satisfying
\(\mathcal OZ=\mathcal O\). Indeed injectivity of \(\mathcal I\) gives
\(JZ=J\), equivalently \(\Pi Z=\Pi\). Such a \(Z\) has top block
\((I_K,0)\), whereas its lower block is unconstrained. Orthogonality
of the two output summands gives the exact identity
\[
 \|Z-T\|_{\mathrm{HS},t}^2
 =\|I_K-a\|_{\mathrm{HS},t}^2+\|b\|_{\mathrm{HS},t}^2
    +\|\mathsf Q(Z-T)\|_{\mathrm{HS},t}^2.
 \tag{QT.9}\label{eq:qt-nearest}
\]
Its last summand vanishes precisely for \(Z=X\). Unitarity gives
\(\|a\|_{\mathrm{HS},t}^2+\|b\|_{\mathrm{HS},t}^2=q\),
and hence
\[
 \|X-T\|_{\mathrm{HS},t}^2
 =2q-2\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}_K a.
 \tag{QT.10}\label{eq:qt-cost}
\]
Every map and inverse constructed here is smooth in \(t\): the source
Grams, their positive inverses, and the fixed-phase Gram--Schmidt
construction of \(T\) are smooth on the compact parameter interval.

\subsection{The exact quotient contraction and its original metric}

For comparison, the unique nearest linear map to \(T\) that merely
preserves \(F\) is
\[
 Y=T-\Pi T\mathsf Q
  =\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\c&d\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{QT.11}\label{eq:qt-descent}
\]
The same orthogonal-block minimization proves this assertion:
preserving \(F\) forces precisely the upper-right block to be zero.
It is invertible since \(a,d\) are. Its quotient action is the
explicit invertible map
\(\mathfrak a=JTR:E\to E\).

Here its entire original-metric contraction can be calculated.
Put \(\mathfrak w=JTs:E\to E\).
Because \(Ts(E)=K\), \(Ts=R\mathfrak w\); the isometry of \(T\)
therefore gives \(\mathfrak w^*G\mathfrak w=G_\ell\).
Also \(s^*MR=G\), since \(s-R\) is relation-valued and orthogonal
to \(R(E)\). The projection formula onto \(L=s(E)\) is
\(P=sG_\ell^{-1}s^*M\), so \(PR=sG_\ell^{-1}G\).
Using \(\Pi T=TP\) yields all of the following exact identities:
\[
 \mathfrak a=\mathfrak wG_\ell^{-1}G,\qquad
 \mathfrak a^*G\mathfrak a=GG_\ell^{-1}G,\qquad
 \mathfrak a^{\sharp_G}\mathfrak a=G_\ell^{-1}G,
 \quad \mathfrak a^{\sharp_G}=G^{-1}\mathfrak a^*G.
 \tag{QT.12}\label{eq:qt-quotientmetric}
\]
No basis diagonalization or deletion of multiplicities entered these
identities. In particular
\[
 \begin{split}
 \ell(t)&:=\|b\|_{\mathrm{HS},t}^2
       =\|c\|_{\mathrm{HS},t}^2
       =q-\operatorname{Tr}(G_\ell^{-1}G),\\
 |\det X|^2&=|\det d|^2
       =\det(a^\sharp a)
       =\frac{\det G}{\det G_\ell}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{QT.13}\label{eq:qt-leakagedet}
\]
For the first line, the diagonal blocks of \(TT^\sharp=I\) and
\(T^\sharp T=I\) respectively give
\(aa^\sharp+bb^\sharp=I_K\) and
\(a^\sharp a+c^\sharp c=I_K\). Take traces and then use (QT.12).
For the second line, triangularity of \(X\) gives \(\det X=\det d\).
The identities
\(dd^\sharp=I_F-cc^\sharp\) and
\(a^\sharp a=I_K-c^\sharp c\) show that their determinants coincide:
the nonzero eigenvalues of \(cc^\sharp\) and \(c^\sharp c\),
with their multiplicities, coincide, because for any eigenvalue
\(\lambda>0\), \(c\) and \(\lambda^{-1}c^\sharp\) are inverse
maps between the corresponding eigenspaces. The extra dimension
of \(F\) contributes the eigenvalue one after subtracting from its
identity. Finally take determinants in (QT.12).

The repair's source-energy change is also exact, for every \(z\in H\):
\[
 \|Xz\|_t^2-\|z\|_t^2
       =\|\Pi z\|_t^2-\|\Pi Tz\|_t^2.
 \tag{QT.14}\label{eq:qt-energy}
\]
Indeed \(Xz=\mathsf QTz+\Pi z\) has orthogonal summands, whereas
the isometry gives
\(\|z\|_t^2=\|\mathsf QTz\|_t^2+\|\Pi Tz\|_t^2\).
Thus its nonunitarity is a calculated source energy, while its
arithmetic class is unchanged by (QT.8).

\subsection{A positive original-moment obstruction and a sharp repair minimum}

Write \(\chi(S)=\sum_{j=0}^q\alpha_jS^j\), and put
\[
 \chi^\dagger(S)=\sum_{j=0}^q\overline{\alpha_j}(k-S)^j,\quad
 v=\chi\chi^\dagger\in F,\qquad
 \beta(t)=
 \frac{\left(\int_{\mathbb R}|\chi(S(u))|^2m_t(u)\,du\right)^2}
 {\left(\int_{\mathbb R}m_t(u)\,du\right)
  \left(\int_{\mathbb R}|\chi(S(u))|^4m_t(u)\,du\right)}.
 \tag{QT.15}\label{eq:qt-momentfloor}
\]
These are the literal reflected polynomial and relation of (CR7).
All displayed integrals are finite. Both denominator factors and
the numerator are strictly positive, because a nonzero polynomial
has finitely many zeros on the line and \(m_t>0\) almost everywhere.
On that line \(k-S=\overline S\), so
\(\langle1,v\rangle_t=\int|\chi|^2m_t>0\).
As \(1\in L\), orthogonal projection followed by Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \|Pv\|_t^2\ge
       \frac{|\langle1,v\rangle_t|^2}{\|1\|_t^2}.
 \]
Since \(v\in F\) and \(bv=\Pi Tv=TPv\), isometry of \(T\) implies
\(\|bv\|_t=\|Pv\|_t\).
The operator norm of \(b\) is at most its Hilbert--Schmidt norm.
Dividing the preceding inequality by
\(\|v\|_t^2=\int|\chi|^4m_t\) proves the explicit floor
\[
 0<\beta(t)\le\ell(t).
 \tag{QT.16}\label{eq:qt-positive}
\]
It also proves \(L\ne K\), directly on the original density.

The two retained windows have a nonzero intertwining defect after
any arithmetic-class-preserving repair. More precisely,
\[
 UX-XW=-\Pi W,\qquad
 \min_{\mathcal OZ=\mathcal O}\|UZ-ZW\|_{\mathrm{HS},t}^2
       =\|\Pi W\|_{\mathrm{HS},t}^2
       =:\nu(t)^2.
 \tag{QT.17}\label{eq:qt-minimum}
\]
To prove the first equality, \(U\mathsf Q=U\), \(U\Pi=0\), and
\(UT=TW\) give \(UX=TW\).
Since \(\Pi TW=\Pi UT=0\), one also has
\(XW=\mathsf QTW+\Pi W=TW+\Pi W\).
For any competing \(Z\), (QT.8)'s argument gives \(\Pi Z=\Pi\).
Then
\(\Pi(UZ-ZW)=-\Pi W\), since \(\Pi U=0\).
Orthogonality of output components proves its squared norm is at
least \(\|\Pi W\|_{\mathrm{HS},t}^2\), and \(X\) attains it.

This minimum has its complete arithmetic quotient expression:
\[
 \begin{split}
 \nu(t)^2
 &=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(G^{-1}R^*MW^2R\right)
   =\operatorname{Tr}_K(c^\sharp U_F^2c),\\
 0<\beta(t)&\le\ell(t)\le\nu(t)^2\le4\ell(t).
 \end{split}
 \tag{QT.18}\label{eq:qt-minimumenergy}
\]
For the first trace, use \(\Pi=RG^{-1}R^*M\), which is the
orthogonal projection formula from (QT.4), and
\(\|\Pi W\|_{\mathrm{HS},t}^2=\operatorname{Tr}(\Pi W^2)\).
For the second, \(W=T^\sharp UT\), hence
the \(K,K\) block of \(W^2=T^\sharp U^2T\) is \(c^\sharp U_F^2c\).
Finally (QT.5) gives
\(I_F\preceq U_F^2\preceq4I_F\); taking traces after the congruence
by \(c\) and using (QT.13), (QT.16) proves every inequality.

In fact there is no invertible \(F\)-preserving operator \(Z\) with
\(UZ=ZW\), even if its induced quotient map is allowed to change.
An invertible such \(Z\) has \(Z^{-1}F=F\).
The purported intertwining and \(\operatorname{im}U=F\) would give
\(\operatorname{im}W=Z^{-1}F=F\).
But (AW7)--(AW10) give \(\operatorname{im}W=L^\perp\).
Thus \(F=L^\perp\) and \(K=L\), contradicting the original positive
pairing just proved. This obstruction does not stop the construction:
(QT.7), (QT.17), and the next subsection give an invertible exact
quotient repair, its attained optimal defect, and its full effect on
the signed arithmetic calculation.

\subsection{Square-zero conjugation error and the signed arithmetic bound}

The repaired intertwining has an especially explicit conjugation form:
\[
 XWX^{-1}=U+\mathcal N,\qquad
 \mathcal N=\Pi WX^{-1}
   =\begin{pmatrix}0&c^\sharp U_F\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \mathcal N^2=0,\qquad
 \|\mathcal N\|_{\mathrm{HS},t}^2=\nu(t)^2.
 \tag{QT.19}\label{eq:qt-nilpotent}
\]
Indeed \(W=T^\sharp UT\) gives its complete two-by-two blocks
\[
 W=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  c^\sharp U_Fc&c^\sharp U_Fd\\
  d^\sharp U_Fc&d^\sharp U_Fd
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \]
Multiply its top row by \(X^{-1}\) in (QT.7): the first entry is
\(c^\sharp U_Fc-c^\sharp U_Fdd^{-1}c=0\), and the second is
\(c^\sharp U_Fdd^{-1}=c^\sharp U_F\).
This proves the displayed matrix, its square-zero property, and its
norm equality by (QT.18). Its range is in \(K\) and it vanishes on
\(K\); its nonzero arithmetic output on suitable relation vectors
has not been suppressed.

Write
\[
 \mathcal C(t)=M(t)^{-1}(M(1)-M(0)),\qquad
 \mathcal E(t)=\operatorname{Re}
       \operatorname{Tr}\!\left(WX^{-1}[\mathcal C,X]\right),
 \quad [\mathcal C,X]=\mathcal CX-X\mathcal C.
 \tag{QT.20}\label{eq:qt-correctedintegrand}
\]
The original four-window value is
\(\mathcal B(t)=\log(V_{q-1}(t)V_q(t)/(V_{2q-1}(t)V_{2q}(t)))\),
where \(V_j=\det G_j\) uses the unchanged minimum quotient Grams.
The exact signed formula (AT15) now becomes
\[
 \begin{split}
 \dot{\mathcal B}(t)
 &=\mathcal E(t)-\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}
                                      (\mathcal N\mathcal C),\\
 \Delta\mathcal B
 &=\int_0^1\mathcal E(t)\,dt
       -\int_0^1\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}
                                      (\mathcal N(t)\mathcal C(t))\,dt.
 \end{split}
 \tag{QT.21}\label{eq:qt-signedexact}
\]
For the first identity, substitute
\(U=XWX^{-1}-\mathcal N\) from (QT.19) into
\(\dot{\mathcal B}=\operatorname{Tr}((U-W)\mathcal C)\).
Cyclicity of the finite trace transforms its first two terms into
\(\operatorname{Tr}(WX^{-1}[\mathcal C,X])\).
The left-hand side is real, so taking real parts gives exactly (QT.21).
Integrating its smooth functions proves the second identity.
This repaired commutator preserves \(\mathcal O\) exactly; the
explicit square-zero contribution is the remaining correction.

It is controlled by a particular part of the original arithmetic
source energy, rather than by its whole spectral spread. Set
\[
 e(t)^2
 :=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(G^{-1}\dot R^*M\dot R\right)
 =-\frac12\operatorname{Tr}(G^{-1}\ddot G)\ge0.
 \tag{QT.22}\label{eq:qt-speed}
\]
Here all dots differentiate the same source interpolation (QT.3).
For completeness the AT variation identity gives, with
\(B:\mathbb C[S]_{\le q}\to H\) multiplication by \(\chi\),
\[
 \dot R=-B(B^*MB)^{-1}B^*(M(1)-M(0))R
       =-\mathsf Q\mathcal C R.
 \]
Substituting this formula into \(\dot R^*M\dot R\) cancels the
middle factor \(B^*MB\), giving
\[
 \dot R^*M\dot R
 =R^*(M(1)-M(0))B(B^*MB)^{-1}B^*(M(1)-M(0))R.
 \]
The fully proved second variation (AT7) is minus twice this matrix,
which proves (QT.22).
Moreover, under the isometry \(R:(E,G)\to K\),
\(\mathsf Q\mathcal C|_K\) has coefficient map
\(\mathsf Q\mathcal C R=-\dot R\). Thus
\[
 \|\mathsf Q\mathcal C\Pi\|_{\mathrm{HS},t}=e(t).
 \tag{QT.23}\label{eq:qt-crossspeed}
\]
Only this off-diagonal metric-variation block contributes to
\(\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal N\mathcal C)\), since (QT.19) has a
single upper-right block. In any orthonormal bases of \(K,F\),
the trace is the sum of entrywise products of those two blocks.
Cauchy--Schwarz on that finite list, then (QT.19), (QT.23), proves
the new quantitative transport estimate
\[
 \boxed{\;
 |\dot{\mathcal B}(t)-\mathcal E(t)|
       \le\nu(t)e(t)\le2\sqrt{\ell(t)}\,e(t).
 \;}
 \tag{QT.24}\label{eq:qt-localbound}
\]
In particular the error vanishes exactly when
\(\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal N\mathcal C)=0\),
and the sufficient condition \(e(t)=0\)
does not require the whole relative source operator to be scalar.

Every quantity is continuous on the compact interval. Define the
fully specified original-source quantities
\[
 \mathfrak E=\int_0^1\mathcal E(t)\,dt,\qquad
 \mathfrak F=\int_0^1\nu(t)e(t)\,dt.
 \]
Integration, followed by Cauchy--Schwarz for real functions of \(t\),
gives
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 |\Delta\mathcal B-\mathfrak E|\le\mathfrak F
 \le
 \left(\int_0^1\nu(t)^2\,dt\right)^{1/2}
 \left(-\frac12\int_0^1\operatorname{Tr}(G^{-1}\ddot G)\,dt\right)^{1/2}.
 \quad}
 \tag{QT.25}\label{eq:qt-globalbound}
\]
This is an interval centered at the actual class-preserving
commutator, with an explicitly calculated relation-speed error.
It supplements, rather than assumes a sign for, the original
signed correction.

To combine with the fully retained AW spectrum, let
\(b_1\le\cdots\le b_{2q+1}\) be the positive eigenvalues of the actual
relative source operator \(M(0)^{-1}M(1)\), and put
\[
 \mathfrak L_q=
 \log\frac{b_{q+2}}{b_q}
       +2\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}\log\frac{b_{2q+2-j}}{b_j}.
 \]
The sum is empty when \(q=1\).
The original proof (AW14) and (QT.25) give simultaneously
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \max\{-\mathfrak L_q,\mathfrak E-\mathfrak F\}
 \le\Delta\mathcal B\le
 \min\{\mathfrak L_q,\mathfrak E+\mathfrak F\}.
 \quad}
 \tag{QT.26}\label{eq:qt-intersection}
\]
This follows by intersecting two intervals both proved for the same
unchanged signed value; the second interval keeps the measured
quotient-transport defect and the original source-variation energy.
None of these expressions treats the arithmetic density or its full
Taylor unit as a freely selectable coefficient.

If \(M(1)=c_0M(0)\) with \(c_0>0\), the source mass retains that
literal factor, \(\mathcal C(t)\) is scalar, and \(R\) is constant.
Thus \(\mathcal E=e=\mathfrak F=0\), and (QT.21) gives
\(\Delta\mathcal B=0\) exactly. The strictly positive obstruction
\(\nu(t)^2\) to a class-preserving window intertwiner still exists;
its contraction with this scalar metric variation is zero.
This illustrates why the nonzero source obstruction alone is not
a proof of an RH contradiction. Equations (QT.17)--(QT.26) compute
its exact contribution on the retained arithmetic programme.

\subsection{The literal finite relation complex and its computed homotopy}

The repair also acts on the original finite cochain complex, not just
on its quotient. Put the two terms in degrees \(-1,0\):
\[
 \mathsf C^{-1}=\mathbb C[S]_{\le q},\qquad
 \mathsf C^0=H,\qquad
 \partial=B=\times\chi,\qquad H_F=B^*MB.
 \]
These are exactly the \(N=2q\) terms of (AT3), before taking
cohomology. Multiplication by the literal monic \(\chi\) is injective,
so \(H_F\) is positive definite. Define the three actual coefficient
maps
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 X^{-1}_{\rm deg}:=H_F^{-1}B^*MTB:
                     \mathsf C^{-1}\longrightarrow\mathsf C^{-1},
 \qquad X^0_{\rm deg}:=X:\mathsf C^0\longrightarrow\mathsf C^0,\\
 K_X:=H_F^{-1}B^*M(T-I):
                     \mathsf C^0\longrightarrow\mathsf C^{-1}.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{QT.27}\label{eq:qt-chainmaps}
\]
The superscript \(-1\) in \(X^{-1}_{\rm deg}\) labels cochain degree;
it does not denote the inverse operator. The latter exists, as the
calculation below also proves. Substituting the original projection
\(\mathsf Q=BH_F^{-1}B^*M\) gives every chain and homotopy identity:
\[
 X^0_{\rm deg}B=BX^{-1}_{\rm deg},\qquad
 X^0_{\rm deg}-I_H=BK_X,\qquad
 X^{-1}_{\rm deg}-I_{\mathsf C^{-1}}=K_XB.
 \tag{QT.28}\label{eq:qt-chainhomotopy}
\]
For the first equality, \(\Pi B=0\), hence
\(XB=\mathsf QTB=BH_F^{-1}B^*MTB\).
For the second,
\(X-I=\mathsf QT+\Pi-I=\mathsf Q(T-I)=BK_X\).
For the third use \(H_F=B^*MB\) directly in (QT.27).
These three substitutions prove that the displayed two-term map is
a cochain map and that \(K_X\) is a cochain homotopy from its identity
to that map, with the displayed signs and degrees. Moreover
\(X(F)=F\) by (QT.7), and \(X|_F=d\) is invertible. Since
\(B:\mathsf C^{-1}\to F\) is an isomorphism, the first equality
identifies \(X^{-1}_{\rm deg}=B^{-1}(X|_F)B\), proving its
invertibility without using an inverse on the infinite source.

In particular, for every polynomial vector \(z\in H\), the change
of its original source representative is the literal relation
\(\chi K_Xz\), with its full coefficients given by (QT.27).
Composing the original source comparison \(\mathcal V\) of (AT2)
gives
\(\mathcal V Xz-\mathcal V z=\mathcal V(\chi K_Xz)\).
The comparison identifies the latter's arithmetic class with
\(\mathcal I J(\chi K_Xz)=0\), using the same full unit
\(\upsilon_h^{\otimes k}\) of (QT.2). Thus the computed finite
homotopy and the equality in the original arithmetic target agree
under the already constructed source map; no new choice of
cohomology section is made here.
Every map in (QT.27)--(QT.28) lifts at a nonempty original support
label by retaining that label and its displayed coefficient map,
with \(\tau\mapsto\tau\). Both sides of the homotopy equations
therefore agree at each such supported fibre, including its
supported zero. This is the exact morphism connecting the repaired
source calculation to the relation complex used by the programme.
