- Printed p.178, Corollary (1.8.12): a lisse iota-mixed sheaf on a connected finite-type F_q-scheme, iota-pure at one point, is pointwise iota-pure. Normality is not a hypothesis of this corollary; mixedness is.

## Direct verification for the intake family

Here q denotes the packet rank, Q the finite-field cardinality, and d=q+1. Assume q>0, retain the actual coefficient ring R_0 generated by the c_a and 1/d!, and fix a specified homomorphism R_0 -> F_Q. The check does not assert an unspecified reduction of complex coefficients or a field map C -> F_Q.

For residue characteristic p>d, u_0 in F_Q^*, and arbitrary t_0 in F_Q, put

    f(S) = (Phi(S) - t_0 S) / u_0.

Every denominator in Phi is invertible. Its leading coefficient is 1/(d u_0), so deg(f)=d and p does not divide d. The leading homogeneous form is S^d/(d u_0). On P^0 its zero scheme is empty, hence smooth. No hypothesis about distinct critical points, square-free chi, or simple roots is needed. Repeated roots of chi therefore do not obstruct this application.

Choose ell != p and a nontrivial additive character psi:F_p -> Qbar_ell^*. The cover Y^p-Y=f(S) gives the rank-one eigensheaf L_psi(f). Substituting n=1 and d=q+1 into the verified theorem gives precisely intake (48): only H_c^1 survives; its dimension is q; its Frobenius eigenvalues are pure of weight 1, hence have complex absolute value Q^(1/2). This is the finite-field geometric cohomology operator; no identification with A=M_S is supplied.

For fixed specialized coefficients, (u,t) in G_m x A^1 defines a morphism into Deligne's good parameter open by taking the coefficients of (Phi-tS)/u. Pulling back the universal compact-support family yields a lisse rank-q family. This base is connected; the application concerns the open u != 0 throughout. Constant rank alone would not establish purity propagation.

The intake's local infinity formula is also algebraically consistent: b(0,u,t)=1/(du) is a unit; on b v^d=1 the derivative in v is d b v^(d-1), with inverse v/d. Thus the cover is finite etale there, and x_infinity=w v gives x_infinity^(-d)=w^(-d)b. Its relative derivative at w=0 is the unit v. This verifies the displayed local normalization without asserting anything at u=0.
