\section{The actual single-primary marked family and its complete boundary monodromy}
\label{sec:sp-primary}

This calculation specializes the original marked polynomial family to a
single selected zero with its complete order. The parameter \(t\) is
fixed at its original value zero. The variable \(s\), its ascending
monomial basis, the primitive with value zero at \(s=0\), and the
original theta source are retained. A translation below is a displayed
coordinate isomorphism on that same family; its constant term and
contour transport are part of the calculation.

\subsection{The actual packet and the exact Pascal map}

Let \(\rho\) be a selected actual nontrivial zero of \(g=2\xi\), of
complete order \(m\ge1\). Put
\[
 n=m+1,\qquad h(s)=(s-\rho)^m,\qquad
 E_h=\mathbb C[s]/((s-\rho)^m),\qquad
 A_s=M_s:E_h\to E_h.
 \tag{SP.1}\label{eq:sp-packet}
\]
The original polynomial primitive is exactly
\[
 \Phi_h(s)=\frac{(s-\rho)^n-(-\rho)^n}{n}
       =\frac{y^n}{n}+c,\qquad
 y=s-\rho,\qquad c=-\frac{(-\rho)^n}{n}.
 \tag{SP.2}\label{eq:sp-phase}
\]
Differentiation gives \(\Phi_h'=h\), and substitution \(s=0\)
gives \(\Phi_h(0)=0\). Thus its constant has not been reassigned.
Since a nontrivial zero \(\rho\) is nonzero, this complex number
\(c\) is nonzero; later coefficient specializations may send it
to zero and are treated separately.

Write \(e_s=(1,s,\ldots,s^{m-1})\) and
\(e_y=(1,y,\ldots,y^{m-1})\) as ordered rows. The coefficient map
from the former basis to the latter is the explicit Pascal matrix
\[
 e_s=e_yP,\qquad
 P_{ab}=
 \begin{cases}\binom ba\rho^{\,b-a},&0\le a\le b<m,\\0,&a>b,\end{cases}
 \quad
 (P^{-1})_{ab}=
 \begin{cases}\binom ba(-\rho)^{\,b-a},&a\le b,\\0,&a>b.\end{cases}
 \tag{SP.3}\label{eq:sp-pascal}
\]
The binomial theorem proves the first identity. Substituting
\(s=y+\rho\) and \(y=s-\rho\) in succession gives the identity on
every monomial, proving the inverse formula. Both matrices have
diagonal entries one and determinant one. If \(x_s\) is an original
coefficient column, its new coordinates are exactly \(x_y=Px_s\).

The polynomial-exponential complex at \(t=0\) is
\[
 \mathbb C[u,s]\xrightarrow{\,L_u\,}
 \mathbb C[u,s]\,ds,\qquad
 L_u=u\partial_s+(s-\rho)^m .
 \tag{SP.4}\label{eq:sp-complex}
\]
Under the same translation \(ds=dy\) and \(L_u=u\partial_y+y^m\).
For a nonzero polynomial \(Q\), the degree of \(L_uQ\) in \(s\)
is exactly \(\deg Q+m\), with the same leading coefficient.
Successive subtraction of its leading monomials therefore gives
a unique remainder of degree below \(m\). The complex is injective
in degree zero and its degree-one cohomology is the free module
\[
 \mathcal H=\bigoplus_{b=0}^{m-1}\mathbb C[u]\,[s^bds],
 \qquad
 \mathcal H/u\mathcal H=E_h\,ds .
 \tag{SP.5}\label{eq:sp-lattice}
\]
The unique-remainder proof also verifies the equality after
specialization: at \(u=0\) the differential is multiplication
by the original \(h\), retaining its complete primary order.

\subsection{The two boundary coefficients in the unchanged basis}

The BC connection is defined by monic division of
\(\Phi_h(s)s^b\) by \(h(s)\). Here that division is explicit:
\[
 \Phi_h(s)s^b
   =h(s)\,\frac{(s-\rho)s^b}{n}+cs^b,\qquad
 q_b(s)=\frac{(s-\rho)s^b}{n},\qquad r_b(s)=cs^b .
 \tag{SP.6}\label{eq:sp-division}
\]
For \(0\le b<m\), its quotient derivative is already a remainder:
\[
 q_b'(s)=\frac{(b+1)s^b-b\rho s^{b-1}}{n},
 \tag{SP.7}\label{eq:sp-Bcolumn}
\]
where the second term is zero for \(b=0\).
Subtracting \(L_uq_b\) in (SP.6) proves
\([\Phi_hs^b]=[cs^b-uq_b']\) in (SP.5).
Thus the exact coefficient matrices and connection are
\[
 C_s=cI_m,\qquad
 (B_s)_{bb}=\frac{b+1}{n},\qquad
 (B_s)_{b-1,b}=-\frac{b\rho}{n}\ (1\le b<m),\qquad
 \nabla_u=\partial_u-\frac{cI_m}{u^2}+\frac{B_s}{u},
 \tag{SP.8}\label{eq:sp-connection}
\]
and all other entries of \(B_s\) are zero.
The induced degree-one formula comes from the original chain
connection
\(\nabla_u^1=\partial_u-\Phi_h/u^2\) and
\(\nabla_u^0=\partial_u-\Phi_h/u^2+1/u\).
Indeed
\([L_u,\partial_u]=-\partial_s\) and
\([L_u,-\Phi_h/u^2]=-h/u\), so
\(\nabla_u^1L_u=L_u\nabla_u^0\).
This proves the connection on the stated complex, with the
degree-zero correction and the marked scalar retained.

In the \(y\)-coordinates, the same division of
\((y^n/n+c)y^a\) gives the quotient \(y^{a+1}/n\).
Consequently
\[
 PB_sP^{-1}=\operatorname{diag}(\beta_0,\ldots,\beta_{m-1}),
 \qquad \beta_a=\frac{a+1}{n},\qquad
 PC_sP^{-1}=cI_m,\qquad \operatorname{Tr}B_s=\frac m2.
 \tag{SP.9}\label{eq:sp-diagonal}
\]
This also proves the matrix conjugation directly, without replacing
the original matrix by one having the same eigenvalues.

\subsection{The original ordered contours and their exact translation}

Fix a branch \(\log u=\log|u|+i\arg u\) on a simply connected
sector of \(\mathbb C^\times\). Put
\[
 \vartheta_j=\frac{\arg u+\pi}{n}+\frac{2\pi j}{n},\qquad
 \ell_j=\{r e^{i\vartheta_j}:0\le r<\infty\},\qquad
 \Gamma_j=-\ell_0+\ell_j\quad(1\le j\le m),
 \tag{SP.10}\label{eq:sp-contours}
\]
with each \(\ell_j\) oriented toward infinity.
These are the original ordered contours in the \(s\)-plane.
Their periods, in the original monomial columns, are
\[
 (\Pi_s(u))_{jb}
   =\int_{\Gamma_j} e^{\Phi_h(s)/u}s^b\,ds ,
       \qquad 1\le j\le m,\quad0\le b<m.
 \tag{SP.11}\label{eq:sp-perioddef}
\]

We prove the translation needed to evaluate these exact contours.
For \(a\in[0,1]\), translate the broken contour by \(a\rho\).
Its two ends remain in the same ordered decay sectors, while
its junction moves from \(0\) to \(a\rho\).
Truncate its two rays at radius \(R\).
Cauchy's theorem applies to the quadrilateral swept by each
finite ray because the integrand is entire in \(s\).
The two junction paths have opposite orientations in
\(-\ell_0+\ell_j\) and cancel. Each remaining connector has
length at most \(|\rho|\) and lies at
\(s=R e^{i\vartheta_l}+a\rho\), \(l=0,j\).
Uniformly for \(a\in[0,1]\),
\[
 \operatorname{Re}\frac{\Phi_h(s)}u
 =-\frac{R^n}{n|u|}+O(R^{n-1})+O(1).
 \tag{SP.12}\label{eq:sp-decay}
\]
This follows by expanding
\((R e^{i\vartheta_l}+(a-1)\rho)^n\), whose leading term
divided by \(nu\) has the displayed negative real value.
The polynomial factor is \(O((1+R)^b)\); the connector
integrals therefore tend to zero as \(R\to\infty\).
The same estimate proves absolute convergence of all rays.
It follows that each period over its original \(\Gamma_j\)
equals the period over \(\rho+\Gamma_j\).
This is the exact sector-preserving contour deformation,
with the original orientation and junction cancellation.

On the latter contour set \(s=\rho+y\).
The binomial expansion is exactly (SP.3), while (SP.2)
retains the factor \(e^{c/u}\).
Let
\[
 \zeta=e^{2\pi i/n},\qquad
 W_{ja}=\zeta^{j(a+1)}-1,\qquad
 d_a=n^{\beta_a-1}e^{\pi i\beta_a}\Gamma(\beta_a),\qquad
 D(u)=\operatorname{diag}(d_a u^{\beta_a})_{a=0}^{m-1}.
 \tag{SP.13}\label{eq:sp-gamma}
\]
On the \(j\)-th \(y\)-ray, the substitution
\(v=r^n/(n|u|)\), with its specified positive real radial
variable, gives
\[
 \int_{\ell_j} e^{y^n/(nu)}y^a\,dy
 =n^{\beta_a-1}e^{\pi i\beta_a}
       \Gamma(\beta_a)u^{\beta_a}\zeta^{j(a+1)} .
 \]
Subtracting the \(j=0\) ray proves the complete ordered period
matrix in the original columns:
\[
 \boxed{\quad \Pi_s(u)=e^{c/u}W D(u)P .\quad}
 \tag{SP.14}\label{eq:sp-periodmatrix}
\]
Every factor in this equality has been specified, including the
original primitive constant, the gamma constants, all powers of
\(\rho\), and the ordered contour phase.

For completeness, \(W\) is invertible. If its columns have a
linear relation with coefficients \(a_1,\ldots,a_m\), then
\(\sum_{r=1}^m a_r(z^r-1)\) vanishes at every nontrivial
\(n\)-th root of unity. It also vanishes at \(1\).
Its degree is at most \(m=n-1\); division by the degree-\(n\)
polynomial \(z^n-1\), or the elementary bound on the number of
roots of a nonzero polynomial, makes it the zero polynomial.
All its coefficients \(a_r\) are zero.
The gamma factors in (SP.13) are positive-real gamma values
times nonzero constants, since \(0<\beta_a<1\).
Thus (SP.14) is invertible for every \(u\ne0\).
Its determinant is exactly
\[
 \det\Pi_s(u)
 =K_m u^{m/2}e^{mc/u},\qquad
 K_m=n^{-m/2}e^{\pi im/2}
       \left(\prod_{a=0}^{m-1}\Gamma(\beta_a)\right)\det W\ne0.
 \tag{SP.15}\label{eq:sp-det}
\]
Here \(\sum_a\beta_a=m/2\) and \(\det P=1\) prove every
factor. This is the single-primary value of the original BC
determinant, with \(\operatorname{Tr}C_s=mc\).

\subsection{Complete monodromy, its finite cover, and the boundary invariants}

Under one positive circuit of \(u=0\), the ordered decay rays
move \(\ell_j\) to \(\ell_{j+1}\), with indices modulo \(n\).
Thus their transported relative cycles satisfy
\[
 \Gamma_j\longmapsto
 \begin{cases}
  \Gamma_{j+1}-\Gamma_1,&1\le j<m,\\
  -\Gamma_1,&j=m.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{SP.16}\label{eq:sp-cyclemonodromy}
\]
The equality follows by writing each as
\(-\ell_1+\ell_{j+1}\) and subtracting the defining
\(-\ell_0+\ell_1\). It remains valid for \(m=1\), when
the second case is the single map \(\Gamma_1\mapsto-\Gamma_1\).
Let \(M_{\rm cyc}\) be the matrix whose rows record (SP.16).
The constant exponential is single-valued on \(\mathbb C^\times\).
Equation (SP.14) therefore gives
\[
 \Pi_s^{\rm cont}
 =M_{\rm cyc}\Pi_s,\qquad
 M_{\rm cyc}
 =W\operatorname{diag}(\zeta,\zeta^2,\ldots,\zeta^m)W^{-1},
 \quad M_{\rm cyc}^n=I,\quad
 \ker(M_{\rm cyc}-I)=0 .
 \tag{SP.17}\label{eq:sp-monodromy}
\]
For example the row difference in (SP.16), applied to the
column \(W_{\bullet a}\), is
\(\zeta^{a+1}(\zeta^{j(a+1)}-1)\), proving the stated
diagonalization directly. None of \(\zeta,\ldots,\zeta^m\)
equals one.

The signs for horizontal coefficient solutions can also be
specified without an implicit duality convention.
The connection (SP.8) acts on coefficient columns.
Its horizontal equation is
\(x_s'=(cI/u^2-B_s/u)x_s\), whose fundamental matrix is
\[
 H_s(u)=P^{-1}e^{-c/u}
           \operatorname{diag}(u^{-\beta_a})_{a=0}^{m-1}.
 \tag{SP.18}\label{eq:sp-horizontal}
\]
Differentiate it and use (SP.9) to verify the equation.
Its coefficient monodromy is conjugate to
\(\operatorname{diag}(\zeta^{-1},\ldots,\zeta^{-m})\).
This is the inverse convention to the period-cycle action
in (SP.17). Its eigenvalue set is the same set of all
nontrivial \(n\)-th roots, so it too has zero invariants.

On the finite ramified cover \(u=v^n\), the pulled-back
connection in the \(y\)-basis is exactly
\[
 \partial_v-\frac{nc}{v^{n+1}}I_m+
        \frac1v\operatorname{diag}(1,2,\ldots,m).
 \tag{SP.19}\label{eq:sp-coverconnection}
\]
The meromorphic basis change \(x_y=\operatorname{diag}
(v^{-1},\ldots,v^{-m})z\) cancels precisely the integer
diagonal term and leaves
\(\partial_v-nc\,v^{-n-1}I_m\).
Its horizontal factor remains \(e^{-c/v^n}\).
It is not a meromorphic gauge removing that irregular factor
when \(c\ne0\). Analytic continuation of the displayed
fundamental matrix around \(v=0\) is, nevertheless, the
identity. Thus the actual finite-cover unipotent monodromy
logarithm is exactly
\[
 N_{\rm boundary}=0 .
 \tag{SP.20}\label{eq:sp-Nboundary}
\]
There is no uncomputed sector transition in this one-critical-value
calculation: (SP.18) is an exact diagonal fundamental solution
on every branch, and after (SP.19) all columns have the same
explicit scalar exponential and integer powers. On two
overlapping sectors with the same branch they are identical.
This proves the absence of additional Stokes factors for this
specified family; it is not a claim about a packet with
several distinct critical values.

Let \(j:\Delta^\times\hookrightarrow\Delta\) be a small complex
disc punctured at zero, and let \(\mathcal V_{\rm an}\) denote
either the period local system or its horizontal dual, with the
convention just specified. Its stalk at zero under ordinary
\(j_*\) is the invariant subspace: a local section is a vector
unchanged by a circuit of the puncture. Equation (SP.17) or
(SP.18) proves
\[
 (j_*\mathcal V_{\rm an})_0=0,\qquad
 (j_!\mathcal V_{\rm an})_0=0.
 \tag{SP.21}\label{eq:sp-analyticboundary}
\]
Moreover the circle cochain complex is
\([\mathbb C^m\xrightarrow{M-I}\mathbb C^m]\);
cutting the circle at one point and identifying its endpoints
by \(M\) gives exactly this cellular differential.
Since \(M-I\) is invertible, both its kernel and cokernel
are zero. Thus the derived local invariant and coinvariant
groups also vanish. This statement concerns the local system
specified above and does not replace the marked algebraic
fibre \(\mathcal H/u\mathcal H\) in (SP.5).

\subsection{The same full marked fibre retains the arithmetic nilpotent}

In the \(y\)-basis, let \(J_m\) be the matrix of multiplication
by \(y\) on \(\mathbb C[y]/y^m\); explicitly
\((J_m)_{a+1,a}=1\) for \(0\le a<m-1\), with all other
entries zero. Then
\[
 A_s=P^{-1}(\rho I_m+J_m)P,\qquad
 N_{\rm ar}:=A_s-\rho I_m=P^{-1}J_mP,\qquad
 J_m^m=0,\quad J_m^{m-1}\ne0 .
 \tag{SP.22}\label{eq:sp-arithmetic}
\]
For \(m=1\), the last statement means \(J_1^0=I_1\ne0\)
and \(J_1=0\); its nilpotency index is one.
These identities follow by applying multiplication to each
of the ordered monomials; no monodromy identification is used.
They reconstruct the full original companion multiplication
matrix, since the polynomial identity \((s-\rho)^m=0\)
determines its last column and (SP.3) its complete change
of coordinates.

The relation between this arithmetic action and the boundary
connection is also explicit. From
\(\beta_{a+1}-\beta_a=1/n\),
\[
 [B_s,A_s]=\frac1nN_{\rm ar},\qquad
 [\nabla_u,A_s]=\frac{N_{\rm ar}}{nu}.
 \tag{SP.23}\label{eq:sp-commutator}
\]
The first identity is verified on every \(y^a\), including
the last one where \(J_my^{m-1}=0\), and then conjugated
by \(P\). The scalar \(C_s=cI\) commutes with \(A_s\),
and \(A_s\) is constant in \(u\), proving the second.
Thus for \(m>1\) this marked arithmetic action is not a
horizontal endomorphism of the displayed boundary connection.
The exact nonhorizontal term has been computed, rather
than discarding the action.

It can be transported through the actual period isomorphism.
Set \(D_0=\operatorname{diag}(d_a)\), with the constants
in (SP.13), and
\[
 K_0=WD_0J_mD_0^{-1}W^{-1}.
 \]
The scalar exponential cancels by its actual inverse in
conjugation, and (SP.14) gives
\[
 \Pi_s A_s\Pi_s^{-1}
    =\rho I_m+u^{1/n}K_0,\qquad
 K_0^m=0,\quad K_0^{m-1}\ne0,\qquad
 M_{\rm cyc}K_0M_{\rm cyc}^{-1}=\zeta K_0 .
 \tag{SP.24}\label{eq:sp-periodaction}
\]
Indeed the \((a+1,a)\) entry of
\(\operatorname{diag}(u^{\beta})J_m
\operatorname{diag}(u^{-\beta})\) is \(u^{1/n}\).
The same calculation with \(\zeta^{a+1}\) proves the last
identity. Differentiating (SP.24) retains the exact
\(1/(nu)\) commutator in (SP.23).
This is the concrete connection between the nonzero
arithmetic nilpotent and the finite boundary monodromy,
on the same rank-\(m\) space at each \(u\ne0\).

\subsection{The complete Taylor unit and the original theta source}

The entire quotient and its full Taylor coefficients are
\[
 v_h(s)=\frac{g(s)}{(s-\rho)^m},\qquad
 a_j=\frac{v_h^{(j)}(\rho)}{j!}
       =\frac{g^{(m+j)}(\rho)}{(m+j)!}\quad(0\le j<m),
 \qquad a_0\ne0 .
 \tag{SP.25}\label{eq:sp-unitcoeff}
\]
The equality follows by dividing the convergent Taylor
series of \(g\), whose first nonzero term is of order \(m\).
In the unchanged marked fibre its multiplication matrix is
\[
 U_s=P^{-1}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}a_jJ_m^j\right)P,
 \qquad
 U_s^{-1}=P^{-1}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}b_jJ_m^j\right)P,
 \quad
 b_0=a_0^{-1},\quad
 b_r=-a_0^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^r a_jb_{r-j}.
 \tag{SP.26}\label{eq:sp-unitmatrix}
\]
Multiplying the two finite series proves the inverse:
the constant coefficient is one and every coefficient of
\(J_m^r\), \(1\le r<m\), is zero by the displayed recursion.
Terms of degree at least \(m\) vanish by \(J_m^m=0\).
All coefficients \(a_j\), including their derivative constants,
remain in the same matrix.
When \(u\ne0\), the occurrence of this same \(U_s\) on the
displayed remainder lattice means its constant coefficient
extension in that specified basis. It is not an assertion of
an unrecorded multiplication operation on twisted polynomial
cohomology. The exact multiplication defect is calculated below.

Retain the original function spaces \(V,\mathscr B\), Euler
operator \(D=-x\partial_x\), Fourier convention, theta map
\(\Theta\phi(x)=2\sum_{r\ge1}\phi(rx)\), and seed
\[
 \phi_*=(4\pi^2x^4-6\pi x^2)e^{-\pi x^2},\qquad
 \mathcal M\Theta\phi_*=g,\qquad
 \mathcal MF(s)=\int_0^\infty F(x)x^s\,\frac{dx}{x}.
 \]
Specifically \(V\) is the even Schwartz subspace with
\(\phi(0)=\int\phi=0\), and \(\mathscr B\) consists of smooth
functions on \(\mathbb R_{>0}\) with finite seminorms
\(\sup_{x>0}x^b|D^rF(x)|\) for every integer \(b\) and
every nonnegative integer \(r\).
The original iterated Euler inverse gives \(F_h\in\mathscr B\)
with \(\mathcal MF_h=v_h\). The actual polynomial maps are
\[
 \alpha_h(P)=P(D)\phi_*,\qquad
 \mathcal T_h(P)=P(D)F_h,\qquad
 \Theta\alpha_h(P)=\mathcal T_h(hP),\qquad
 J_h\mathcal T_h(P)=U_s[P]_s .
 \tag{SP.27}\label{eq:sp-source}
\]
Here \(J_h=j_h\mathcal M\) and \([P]_s\) is the original
remainder coordinate column. These are the inherited exact
Euler/theta identities in original marked equations (18)--(19)
and BC's source square. In particular
\([\mathbb C[s]\xrightarrow{h}\mathbb C[s]ds]\)
maps to \([V\xrightarrow{\Theta}\mathscr B]\) by
\((\alpha_h,\mathcal T_h)\).
Its degree-one injection is \(\sigma_hU_s:E_h\hookrightarrow
Q_{\rm ar}=\mathscr B/\Theta V\), where
\(\sigma_h\) is the original corrected section.

The coordinate change is realized on these exact source vectors:
\[
 \mathcal T_h(y^b)=(D-\rho)^bF_h,\qquad
 \bigl(\mathcal T_h(s^b)\bigr)_{b=0}^{m-1}
 =\bigl(\mathcal T_h(y^a)\bigr)_{a=0}^{m-1}P.
 \tag{SP.28}\label{eq:sp-sourcetranslation}
\]
The binomial theorem for the commuting scalar \(\rho\) and
operator \(D\) proves both identities.
Thus the Pascal map, full nilpotent and Taylor unit have
explicit source representatives on the same theta complex.
At general \(u\), the original source deformation is still
\(\Theta+u\mathcal T_h\partial_s\alpha_h^{-1}\) on
\(\alpha_h(\mathbb C[s])\); its square with (SP.4) commutes.
There is no assertion that the unit-valued coefficient
map turns this deformed differential into the undeformed
theta map.

Under the period map, (SP.26) has the exact image
\[
 \Pi_s U_s\Pi_s^{-1}
 =\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}a_j u^{j/n}K_0^j,\qquad
 [\nabla_u,U_s]
 =\frac1{nu}P^{-1}
       \left(\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}j a_jJ_m^j\right)P.
 \tag{SP.29}\label{eq:sp-unittransport}
\]
The first follows by taking powers in (SP.24); the second
uses \([B_y,J_m^j]=(j/n)J_m^j\), proved by induction from
(SP.23). These formulas specify exactly how every Taylor
coefficient is transported and why its arithmetic
multiplication is not silently identified with a constant
flat monodromy operator.

More explicitly put
\(v_{\rm pol}(s)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}a_j(s-\rho)^j\), the exact
original Taylor remainder of \(v_h\). For every polynomial
\(Q(s)\), the Leibniz rule in the original differential gives
\[
 v_{\rm pol}L_uQ
 =L_u(v_{\rm pol}Q)-u v_{\rm pol}'Q.
 \tag{SP.30}\label{eq:sp-unitdifferential}
\]
Indeed the \(h v_{\rm pol}Q\) terms agree and the derivative
of \(v_{\rm pol}Q\) contributes exactly the additional
\(u v_{\rm pol}'Q\). At the marked fibre \(u=0\) this is
ordinary multiplication of the full class
\(\upsilon_h\in E_h^\times\), with inverse (SP.26).
Away from that fibre it records precisely the derivative
term needed to compare multiplication on representatives
with the specified coefficient extension \(U_s\).
For a remainder polynomial \(p\) of degree less than \(m\),
divide \(v_{\rm pol}p=hq+r\) monically. The degree of \(q\)
is at most \(m-2\), with \(q=0\) when the product has
degree less than \(m\). Consequently \(q'\) already has
degree below \(m\), and
\[
 [v_{\rm pol}p]_{\mathcal H_u}
       =[r-uq']_{\mathcal H_u}.
 \tag{SP.31}\label{eq:sp-unitremainder}
\]
Subtracting \(L_uq=hq+uq'\) proves the identity with its
specified correction. The constant matrix \(U_s\) gives
the coefficient \(r\); (SP.31) computes exactly its
difference from multiplying these representatives and
then taking the differential remainder.
No term involving \(v_h'\) or its Taylor coefficients has
been deleted.

\subsection{The finite-cover lattice and the original source metric}

The marked lattice also has an explicit image on the cover
\(u=v^n\). After retaining and factoring its common scalar
\(e^{c/v^n}\) in the period expression, its columns are
\[
 e^{-c/v^n}\Pi_s(v^n)
 =WD_0\operatorname{diag}(v,v^2,\ldots,v^m)P .
 \tag{SP.32}\label{eq:sp-coverlattice}
\]
This is an equality on the punctured disc that also defines
a holomorphic matrix on the whole \(v\)-disc.
The constant invertible matrices \(WD_0,P\) identify its
image lattice with
\(\bigoplus_{a=0}^{m-1}v^{a+1}\mathbb C\{v\}\).
Its cokernel in the displayed ambient holomorphic free
module is consequently
\[
 \bigoplus_{a=0}^{m-1}\mathbb C\{v\}/(v^{a+1}),\qquad
 \text{length}=\sum_{a=0}^{m-1}(a+1)=\frac{m(m+1)}2 .
 \tag{SP.33}\label{eq:sp-latticequotient}
\]
Both assertions follow by multiplying by those constant
inverses and computing the image and quotient in each
one-dimensional coordinate. The image lattice itself
has a rank-\(m\) quotient by multiplication by \(v\),
as does the pulled-back marked lattice.
Its inclusion into the ambient constant lattice induces
zero modulo \(v\), since every column contains a positive
power of \(v\). Thus the vanishing boundary invariants
and the nonzero marked fibre have their exact lattice
map and cokernel recorded; neither is used to erase the
other's coefficients. The scalar exponential factored
in (SP.32) remains an irregular factor and is not being
declared a meromorphic gauge.

In the original source norm, retain the positive matrix
\[
 (G_\theta)_{ab}
 =\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
   \overline{(1/2+it)}^{\,a}(1/2+it)^b
   |v_h(1/2+it)|^2\,dt,\qquad 0\le a,b<m .
 \tag{SP.34}\label{eq:sp-sourcegram}
\]
Mellin--Plancherel identifies this with the Gram of
\(\mathcal T_h(s^b)\) in the actual \(L^2(dx)\) source.
The source moments are finite by the inherited arithmetic
source estimates. A zero polynomial-vector norm would
make its entire Mellin transform \(Pv_h\) zero; since
\(v_h\ne0\), this forces \(P=0\).
Hence \(G_\theta\) is positive definite, with its original
mass and full multiplier retained.
The actual comparison endomorphism is
\[
 T_{\rm per}(u)=G_\theta^{-1}\Pi_s(u)^*\Pi_s(u)
 =e^{2\operatorname{Re}(c/u)}
     G_\theta^{-1}P^*D(u)^*W^*WD(u)P .
 \tag{SP.35}\label{eq:sp-metriccomparison}
\]
Its positivity and self-adjointness are in \(G_\theta\):
\(x^*G_\theta T_{\rm per}x=\|\Pi_sx\|^2>0\) for \(x\ne0\).
The ratio of its largest and smallest eigenvalues is unchanged
by its explicit positive scalar \(e^{2\operatorname{Re}(c/u)}\).
Every other factor in (SP.35), including the original
Pascal matrix and \(G_\theta\), remains. In particular the
scalar cancellation alone gives no estimate for that ratio.

Fix \(u\ne0\). For a larger polynomial source \(\mathbb C[s]_{\le N}\),
\(N\ge m-1\), let \(J_{u,N}\) be the unique differential
remainder for (SP.4). It is onto the same \(m\)-dimensional
remainder module and has exact kernel
\[
 \ker J_{u,N}
   =L_u(\mathbb C[s]_{\le N-m}),\qquad
 \operatorname{Per}_N=\Pi_s(u)J_{u,N}.
 \tag{SP.36}\label{eq:sp-higherkernel}
\]
When \(N=m-1\) the relation space is zero.
The degree argument for (SP.5) proves the kernel and
surjectivity. For the period factorization, each polynomial
differential relation has integral
\[
 \int_{\Gamma_j}e^{\Phi_h/u}L_uQ\,ds
 =u\int_{\Gamma_j}d(e^{\Phi_h/u}Q)=0;
 \]
the endpoint values vanish by the same polynomial decay
estimate (SP.12), and the junction endpoints cancel with
the original orientations.
Thus the period Gram at degree \(N\) has rank exactly \(m\),
with kernel (SP.36). Its restriction to the displayed
\(m\)-dimensional remainder frame is positive definite.
It is not a positive definite Gram on an enlarged source
with more columns.

\section{The finite-field sheaf of the single-primary phase}
\label{sec:SPF}

\subsection{Specified coefficient specialization and all constants}

Let the specified single primary polynomial be
\[
 h(s)=(s-\rho)^m,\qquad m\geq1,\qquad n=m+1.
 \tag{SPF.1}\label{eq:SPF1}
\]
Retain the given coefficient ring \(R\), including \(\rho\), \(1/n!\),
and every coefficient of the full Taylor class
\(\upsilon_h=j_h(g/h)\) and its inverse in the retained
\(m\)-dimensional remainder basis.
The finite-field datum for this calculation is the specified ring
homomorphism
\(\alpha:R\longrightarrow k_0=\mathbf F_{Q_0}\), of characteristic
\(p>n\). All coefficients, including the unit and its inverse, are
mapped by this same homomorphism. We continue to write \(\rho\) for
\(\alpha(\rho)\). At \(t=0\), the specified primitive with value zero
at \(s=0\) is exactly
\[
 \Phi(s)=\frac{(s-\rho)^n-(-\rho)^n}{n},\qquad
 \Phi'(s)=h(s),\qquad
 c=-\frac{(-\rho)^n}{n}.
 \tag{SPF.2}\label{eq:SPF2}
\]
In particular, the constant \(c\) is retained throughout. The only
constant-field extension we require is
\[
 k=\mathbf F_Q,\qquad Q=Q_0^d,\qquad
 d=\operatorname{ord}_{(\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)^\times}(Q_0).
 \tag{SPF.3}\label{eq:SPF3}
\]
Thus \(n\mid Q-1\). This is an explicit finite extension because
\(\gcd(Q_0,n)=1\). All original coefficients are mapped by
\(R\xrightarrow{\alpha}k_0\hookrightarrow k\).

Fix \(\ell\ne p\), work over \(\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell\), and fix a
nontrivial additive character \(\psi_0:k_0\to
\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell^\times\). On \(k\) use
\(\psi=\psi_0\circ\operatorname{Tr}_{k/k_0}\).
For a further extension \(k_r=\mathbf F_{Q^r}\), write
\[
 \psi_r=\psi\circ\operatorname{Tr}_{k_r/k},\qquad
 \chi_r=\chi\circ\operatorname{N}_{k_r/k}.
 \tag{SPF.4}\label{eq:SPF4}
\]
Every Frobenius below is geometric Frobenius, the inverse of
\(x\mapsto x^{Q^r}\) at a \(k_r\)-rational point. Define
\(\mathcal L_\psi(f)\) by the Artin--Schreier torsor convention whose
trace there is \(\psi_r(f)\). Define the multiplicative Kummer sheaf
\(\mathcal K_\chi\) by the convention whose trace there is
\(\chi_r(x)\). These conventions fix both inverse characters and
cohomological signs.

Let
\[
 \begin{split}
 \pi_s:\mathbf A^1_s\times\mathbf G_{m,u}&\longrightarrow
                  \mathbf G_{m,u},\\
 \mathcal F_0&=R^1\pi_{s!}\mathcal L_{\psi_0}(\Phi(s)/u)
                    \quad\hbox{over }k_0,\\
 \mathcal F&=(\mathcal F_0)_k
       =R^1\pi_{s!}\mathcal L_\psi(\Phi(s)/u)
                    \quad\hbox{over }k .
 \end{split}
 \tag{SPF.5}\label{eq:SPF5}
\]
The equality is compact-support base change. Our computation concerns
this precise sheaf. It does not replace the specified phase by
an expression involving \(g/h\), and does not remove that unit from
the original coefficient ring.

The source isomorphism
\[
 T:\mathbf A^1_y\times\mathbf G_{m,u}
       \longrightarrow\mathbf A^1_s\times\mathbf G_{m,u},
 \qquad T(y,u)=(y+\rho,u),\qquad
 T^{-1}(s,u)=(s-\rho,u)
 \tag{SPF.6}\label{eq:SPF6}
\]
fixes the coefficient ring and satisfies \(\pi_sT=\pi_y\).
It preserves compact supports because it is an isomorphism.
For any original coefficient function \(v(s)\), the transported
function is exactly \(v(y+\rho)\); for an invertible such function
the inverse is exactly \(v^{-1}(y+\rho)\).
Direct substitution into \eqref{eq:SPF2}, and the additive torsor
tensor-product law, give
\[
 T^*\mathcal L_\psi(\Phi(s)/u)
  =\mathcal L_\psi(y^n/(nu))
      \otimes\pi_y^*\mathcal L_\psi(c/u).
 \tag{SPF.7}\label{eq:SPF7}
\]
The tensor-product law follows by adding two torsor coordinates:
if \(z_i^p-z_i=f_i\), their sum satisfies the equation for \(f_1+f_2\);
the associated one-dimensional character representations multiply.
Projection formula therefore yields
\[
 \mathcal F=
 \mathcal L_\psi(c/u)\otimes
 R^1\pi_{y!}\mathcal L_\psi(y^n/(nu)).
 \tag{SPF.8}\label{eq:SPF8}
\]

\subsection{The actual power-map splitting, including its zero stalk}

Put \(X_n=\{\chi:k^\times\to\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell^\times:
\chi^n=1\}\), so \(\#X_n=n\), and let
\(q:\mathbf A^1_y\to\mathbf A^1_z\) be \(z=y^n\).
Let \(j:\mathbf G_{m,z}\hookrightarrow\mathbf A^1_z\).
Then the following is an isomorphism of sheaves over \(k\), not
merely an equality of trace functions:
\[
 q_*\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell
   \simeq \overline{\mathbf Q}_{\ell,\mathbf A^1_z}
     \oplus
     \bigoplus_{\substack{\chi\in X_n\\\chi\ne1}}
          j_!\mathcal K_\chi.
 \tag{SPF.9}\label{eq:SPF9}
\]
Here is the explicit splitting. The deck group on \(\mathbf G_m\)
is \(\mu_n(k)\). On the stalk with basis \(e_r\), indexed by
\(r^n=z\), set \(D_\zeta e_r=e_{\zeta r}\). For \(\chi\in X_n\)
there is a unique character \(\lambda_\chi:\mu_n(k)\to
\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell^\times\) with
\[
 \lambda_\chi(x^{(Q-1)/n})=\chi(x)\quad(x\in k^\times),\qquad
 e_\chi=\frac1n\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_n(k)}
                   \lambda_\chi(\zeta)D_\zeta.
 \tag{SPF.10}\label{eq:SPF10}
\]
Uniqueness and existence follow because
\(x\mapsto x^{(Q-1)/n}\) surjects onto \(\mu_n(k)\), with kernel
the subgroup of \(n\)th powers, on which \(\chi\) is trivial.
Character orthogonality gives
\(e_\chi e_{\chi'}=\delta_{\chi,\chi'}e_\chi\) and
\(\sum_{\chi\in X_n}e_\chi=1\). Its image is the
\(\lambda_\chi^{-1}\)-eigenline of the operators \(D_\zeta\).
For \(z=x\in k_r^\times\), a chosen root has
\(r^{Q^r}=x^{(Q^r-1)/n}r\); geometric Frobenius consequently acts
on this eigenline by
\(\lambda_\chi(x^{(Q^r-1)/n})=\chi_r(x)\).
It is therefore the stated Kummer summand with the stated descent
convention.

At \(z=0\), the geometric fibre of \(q\) has one point. Its
constant-sheaf stalk has trivial deck action. Thus \(e_\chi\)
has zero image there for \(\chi\ne1\), and the trivial projector
has a one-dimensional stalk. The unit map from the constant sheaf
identifies the latter summand with the constant sheaf on
\(\mathbf A^1_z\). Each other summand is canonically \(j_!\mathcal
K_\chi\): the adjunction map is an isomorphism at every geometric
stalk, on \(\mathbf G_m\) and at zero. Finally, \(q\) is finite,
so its higher direct images on this sheaf vanish; the argument
has neither suppressed the ramified fibre nor added a term at it.

\subsection{Compact-support cohomology and the negative Gauss line}

For a nonzero scalar \(a\), write
\[
 \mathcal E_{\chi,a}=
       \mathcal K_\chi(z)\otimes\mathcal L_\psi(az)
       \quad\hbox{on }\mathbf G_{m,z},\qquad
 H_\chi=H_c^1(\mathbf G_{m,\bar k},\mathcal E_{\chi,1}).
 \tag{SPF.11}\label{eq:SPF11}
\]
For \(\chi\ne1\) one has
\[
 H_c^i(\mathbf G_{m,\bar k},\mathcal E_{\chi,a})=0
       \ (i\ne1),\qquad
 \dim H_\chi=1.
 \tag{SPF.12}\label{eq:SPF12}
\]
We give the complete application of the curve formulas. A lisse
sheaf on the connected, nonproper curve \(\mathbf G_m\) has no
nonzero compactly supported section: a nonzero locally constant
section has support the whole curve. Thus \(H_c^0=0\).
Poincare duality identifies \(H_c^2\) with the Tate-twisted dual
of the global invariants of the dual sheaf. Those invariants
are zero, since the Artin--Schreier factor at infinity has
nontrivial wild inertia and the Kummer factor is tame.
The Swan conductors at zero and infinity are respectively \(0\)
and \(1\). The second value also follows from the direct local
calculation below, with the local parameter \(z^{-1}\) and
nonzero numerator \(a\). The Euler--Poincare formula for this
rank-one sheaf gives
\[
 \chi_c(\mathbf G_m,\mathcal E_{\chi,a})
  =1\cdot\chi_c(\mathbf G_m,\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell)
        -\operatorname{Swan}_0-\operatorname{Swan}_\infty
  =0-0-1=-1.
 \tag{SPF.13}\label{eq:SPF13}
\]
Together with the two vanishing groups and the cohomological
dimension bound for a curve, this proves \eqref{eq:SPF12}.
The formulas used here, including their proofs and geometric
Frobenius convention, are in Katz,
\emph{Gauss Sums, Kloosterman Sums, and Monodromy Groups},
Sections 2.1--2.3, especially 2.3.1--2.3.3.

The trivial summand of \eqref{eq:SPF9} instead contributes
\[
 H_c^i(\mathbf A^1_{\bar k},\mathcal L_\psi(az))=0
                       \quad\hbox{for every }i,\ a\ne0.
 \tag{SPF.14}\label{eq:SPF14}
\]
Indeed \(H_c^0=0\) for the same support reason, \(H_c^2=0\)
by its nontrivial wild inertia at infinity and duality, and
Euler--Poincare gives \(1-1=0\), hence \(H_c^1=0\).
This proves why the final rank is \(n-1\), with no trivial
character contribution.

Set
\[
 G_k(\chi,\psi)=\sum_{z\in k^\times}\chi(z)\psi(z).
 \tag{SPF.15}\label{eq:SPF15}
\]
The trace formula, with \eqref{eq:SPF12}, gives the exact
one-dimensional geometric Frobenius action
\[
 \operatorname{Fr}_Q\big|H_\chi
       =-G_k(\chi,\psi).
 \tag{SPF.16}\label{eq:SPF16}
\]
The minus sign is the cohomological sign in degree one.
There is no Tate twist in this identity.

This computation is an isomorphism of the entire parameter
sheaf. On
\(\mathbf G_{m,z}\times\mathbf G_{m,a}\), use the source
isomorphism
\((z,a)\mapsto(w=az,a)\), whose inverse is
\((w,a)\mapsto(w/a,a)\).
The multiplicative Kummer torsor law gives
\[
 \mathcal K_\chi(w/a)\otimes\mathcal L_\psi(w)
  \simeq
 \operatorname{pr}_w^*
       (\mathcal K_\chi(w)\otimes\mathcal L_\psi(w))
      \otimes\operatorname{pr}_a^*\mathcal K_{\chi^{-1}}(a).
 \tag{SPF.17}\label{eq:SPF17}
\]
This law follows by multiplying or dividing the root coordinates
of Kummer torsors; the associated character representations
multiply or invert accordingly. The isomorphism preserves
compact supports in every fibre, so projection formula and
base change identify the parameter sheaf with
\(\underline{H_\chi}\otimes\mathcal K_{\chi^{-1}}(a)\).
Here \(\underline{H_\chi}\) is the pullback from
\(\operatorname{Spec}k\) of the one-dimensional Galois module
in \eqref{eq:SPF16}; it is geometrically constant.

\subsection{The exact decomposition on the original parameter}

For a nonzero scalar \(\gamma\), denote by \(\mathcal A_\gamma\)
the geometrically constant one-dimensional sheaf with geometric
\(\operatorname{Fr}_Q\)-eigenvalue \(\gamma\). In its use below
\(\gamma\) is the actual eigenvalue in \eqref{eq:SPF16}, or
that eigenvalue times a root of unity, so this notation
denotes an existing continuous Galois representation.
Combining the preceding source maps with
\(a=1/(nu)\) gives
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \mathcal F\simeq
 \mathcal L_\psi(c/u)\otimes
 \bigoplus_{\substack{\chi\in X_n\\\chi\ne1}}
  \mathcal A_{-G_k(\chi,\psi)}
           \otimes\mathcal K_{\chi^{-1}}(1/(nu)).}
 \tag{SPF.18}\label{eq:SPF18}
\]
In particular, \(\mathcal F\) is lisse of rank \(n-1=m\).
The inverse Kummer character here is essential.
Since inversion twice returns \(\chi\) and multiplication by
the constant \(n\) has trace \(\chi(n)\), the same isomorphism
on \(\mathbf G_{m,u}\) is exactly
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \mathcal F\simeq
 \mathcal L_\psi(c/u)\otimes
 \bigoplus_{\substack{\chi\in X_n\\\chi\ne1}}
  \mathcal A_{-\chi(n)G_k(\chi,\psi)}
                 \otimes\mathcal K_\chi(u).}
 \tag{SPF.19}\label{eq:SPF19}
\]
Neither the factor \(n\) nor the additive factor \(c/u\) has
been dropped.

For completeness this determines every finite-extension trace.
Applying the trace formula to the base change of the
one-dimensional group \(H_\chi\) proves directly
\[
 -G_{k_r}(\chi_r,\psi_r)
       =\bigl(-G_k(\chi,\psi)\bigr)^r.
 \tag{SPF.20}\label{eq:SPF20}
\]
Thus the usual lifting sign is derived from this actual group,
without an independent convention for a normalized Gauss sum.
For \(u\in k_r^\times\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 \operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Fr}_{Q^r,u}\mid\mathcal F_{\bar u})
  &=-\sum_{s\in k_r}\psi_r(\Phi(s)/u)\\
  &=-\psi_r(c/u)
       \sum_{\substack{\chi\in X_n\\\chi\ne1}}
           \chi_r(nu)G_{k_r}(\chi_r,\psi_r)\\
  &=\psi_r(c/u)
       \sum_{\substack{\chi\in X_n\\\chi\ne1}}
          \bigl(-\chi(n)G_k(\chi,\psi)\bigr)^r\chi_r(u).
 \end{split}
 \tag{SPF.21}\label{eq:SPF21}
\]
To verify the middle equality also by finite sums, translate
\(s\) to \(y=s-\rho\). The number of \(n\)th roots of
\(z\in k_r^\times\) is \(\sum_{\chi\in X_n}\chi_r(z)\).
There is one root at zero. The trivial-character part is
\(1+\sum_{z\ne0}\psi_r(az)=0\).
For each other character the change of variable \(w=az\)
gives \(\chi_r(a)^{-1}G_{k_r}(\chi_r,\psi_r)\);
here \(a^{-1}=nu\). In the last equality,
\(\chi_r(n)=\chi(n)^r\), since \(n\in k^\times\) and its
extension-field norm is \(n^r\).

\subsection{Inertia at zero, including a zero constant specialization}

Let \(I_0\) denote geometric inertia over
\(\bar k((u))\), let \(P_0\) be its wild subgroup, and let
\(V=\mathcal F_{\bar\eta}\).
Write \(\psi(x)=
\vartheta(\operatorname{Tr}_{k/\mathbf F_p}(b x))\), where
\(\vartheta:\mathbf F_p\to\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell^\times\)
is a fixed nontrivial additive character and \(b\in k^\times\).
Such a unique \(b\) exists by nondegeneracy of the finite-field
trace pairing. Then
\(\mathcal L_\psi(c/u)=\mathcal L_\vartheta(bc/u)\).
Put \(d_0=bc\).

First suppose \(c\ne0\). The Artin--Schreier equation
\[
 z^p-z=d_0/u
 \tag{SPF.22}\label{eq:SPF22}
\]
defines a degree-\(p\) extension of \(\bar k((u))\).
Indeed \(f^p-f=d_0/u\) has no Laurent-series solution:
if \(f\) has a pole of order \(r>0\), then \(f^p-f\)
has pole order \(pr\), whereas the right side has order one;
if \(f\) has no pole, neither does \(f^p-f\).
An Artin--Schreier polynomial of prime degree with no root
is irreducible, since its roots form one translate of
\(\mathbf F_p\) and its Galois group is a subgroup of that
group, hence has order either one or \(p\).

Let \(v_E\) be its integer-valued normalized valuation, and
write \(v_E(u)=e\) for the ramification index. The equation
forces \(v_E(z)<0\), hence \(p\,v_E(z)=-e\).
Thus \(p\mid e\); since \(1\le e\le [E:\bar k((u))]=p\),
one has \(e=p\) and \(v_E(z)=-1\). The extension is
therefore totally ramified and \(z^{-1}\) is a uniformizer.
For \(\sigma_a(z)=z+a\), \(a\in\mathbf F_p^\times\),
\[
 v_E\bigl(\sigma_a(z^{-1})-z^{-1}\bigr)
  =v_E\left(\frac{-a}{z(z+a)}\right)=2.
 \tag{SPF.23}\label{eq:SPF23}
\]
The lower ramification groups are consequently
\(G_0=G_1=\mathbf F_p\), \(G_2=1\); their unique positive
upper break is one. Every nontrivial character of this group
has Swan conductor one. In particular the character
\(\varepsilon_{d_0}\) supplied by
\(\mathcal L_\vartheta(d_0/u)\) is nontrivial on \(P_0\).
All geometrically constant factors of
\eqref{eq:SPF19} are trivial on \(I_0\), and all Kummer
factors are trivial on \(P_0\). Thus
\[
 V|_{P_0}\simeq \varepsilon_{d_0}^{\oplus(n-1)},\qquad
 V^{P_0}=0,\qquad V^{I_0}=0,\qquad
 \operatorname{Swan}_0(\mathcal F)=n-1
                   \quad(c\ne0).
 \tag{SPF.24}\label{eq:SPF24}
\]
The first vanishing follows explicitly by choosing
\(\sigma\in P_0\) with \(\varepsilon_{d_0}(\sigma)\ne1\):
an invariant vector \(v\) would satisfy
\((\varepsilon_{d_0}(\sigma)-1)v=0\), hence \(v=0\).
Swan conductors add under direct sum.

Now suppose \(c=0\). This occurs exactly when \(\rho=0\)
in the specified residue field, since \(n\ne0\) there.
The additive factor is the constant sheaf, and
\[
 V|_{I_0}\simeq
    \bigoplus_{\substack{\chi\in X_n\\\chi\ne1}}
            \mathcal K_\chi|_{I_0},\qquad
 V^{I_0}=0,\qquad
 \operatorname{Swan}_0(\mathcal F)=0
                      \quad(c=0).
 \tag{SPF.25}\label{eq:SPF25}
\]
Each displayed character is nontrivial on tame inertia:
the totally ramified Kummer cover \(u=v^n\) has deck group
\(\mu_n\), and its summand is the nontrivial
\(\lambda_\chi^{-1}\)-character proved in
\eqref{eq:SPF10}. An invariant in a direct sum of these
eigenlines has invariant components in each, hence is zero.
Thus this specialization does not produce an invariant line.

Here is one finite cover that kills all geometric inertia in
either case and keeps track of the additive factor:
\[
 u=v^n,\qquad z^p-z=\frac{bc}{v^n}.
 \tag{SPF.26}\label{eq:SPF26}
\]
Both maps are finite etale on the punctured \(u\)-line or its
pullback: their derivatives there are \(nv^{n-1}\ne0\) and
\(-1\ne0\). The pullback of \(\mathcal K_\chi(u)\) is
\(\mathcal K_{\chi^n}(v)\), hence constant. The pullback of
the Artin--Schreier factor is trivialized by the displayed
torsor itself. For \(c=0\), the first map alone suffices and
the second is a disjoint trivial torsor. For \(c\ne0\), the
second map remains a nontrivial degree-\(p\) extension
after the first: its right side has pole order \(n\), and
\(p\nmid n\), so the preceding pole-order argument applies.
The composite has degree \(np\). Constants
\(\mathcal A_{-\chi(n)G_k}\) can retain arithmetic
Frobenius action; they have no geometric inertia action.

On the resulting finite-index subgroup of \(I_0\), every
monodromy matrix is the identity. Its unipotent logarithm is
therefore exactly
\[
 N=\log(1)=0.
 \tag{SPF.27}\label{eq:SPF27}
\]
Equivalently, the original inertia representation has finite
image and is a direct sum of finite-order characters, so its
unipotent part is already the identity. Formula
\eqref{eq:SPF27} and the vanishing \(V^{I_0}=0\) hold
simultaneously. Invariants refer to the original group
\(I_0\); after the cover its restricted representation is
trivial, so the new inertia invariants are the whole
rank-\(m\) vector space.

\subsection{Purity proved by the exact Gauss calculation}

For every complex embedding of the cyclotomic field of the
character values, and every nontrivial \(\chi\),
\[
 |G_k(\chi,\psi)|^2
   =\sum_{t\in k^\times}\chi(t)
       \sum_{y\in k^\times}\psi((t-1)y)
   =(Q-1)-\sum_{\substack{t\in k^\times\\t\ne1}}\chi(t)
   =Q.
 \tag{SPF.28}\label{eq:SPF28}
\]
The first equality expands the square of the sum, uses the
complex conjugates \(\overline{\chi(y)}=\chi(y)^{-1}\) and
\(\overline{\psi(y)}=\psi(-y)\), and sets \(x=ty\).
The inner sum is \(Q-1\) at \(t=1\) and \(-1\) otherwise,
by nontrivial additive-character orthogonality.
Multiplicative-character orthogonality gives
\(\sum_{t\ne0}\chi(t)=0\), so the sum over \(t\ne1\) is
\(-1\). This proves every equality, with the original
unscaled Gauss sum.

Every local eigenvalue in \eqref{eq:SPF21} is
\[
 \psi_r(c/u)\,
       \bigl(-\chi(n)G_k(\chi,\psi)\bigr)^r\chi_r(u),
 \qquad
 \left|\psi_r(c/u)
       \bigl(-\chi(n)G_k(\chi,\psi)\bigr)^r\chi_r(u)\right|
       =Q^{r/2}.
 \tag{SPF.29}\label{eq:SPF29}
\]
All character factors have absolute value one, and
\eqref{eq:SPF28} gives the last equality. Thus the
specified sheaf is pointwise pure of weight one.
No division by \(Q^{1/2}\), half Tate twist, or change of
the original phase was made. Over \(k_0\) the same purity
follows as well: for a closed point of degree \(e\), pass
to a constant extension in which its Frobenius has a
positive power covered by \eqref{eq:SPF29}. If its original
eigenvalue is \(\beta\) and that power is \(r\), the computed
equality \(|\beta^r|=Q_0^{er/2}\) implies
\(|\beta|=Q_0^{e/2}\). The inertia calculation is geometric
and is unchanged by constant-field extension.

This establishes the exact finite-field decomposition,
purity, invariant-space vanishing, and finite-cover
logarithm of the sheaf in \eqref{eq:SPF5}. The finite-field
parameter and its Frobenius eigenvalues have been
constructed here. No complex off-critical zeta zero or
identification of such a zero with one of these finite-field
Frobenius eigenvalues is constructed by this calculation.
