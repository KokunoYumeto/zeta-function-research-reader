\section{The original Hilbert objects and their maps}
Fix a monic polynomial $\chi\in\mathbb C[S]$ of degree $q\ge1$ and retain
$E=\mathbb C[S]/(\chi)$ with ordered remainder basis $1,S,\ldots,S^{q-1}$.
The original multiplication endomorphism is $A=M_S$. Fix the original monic
orthogonal polynomial columns $p_n$, their literal positive squared norms
$\omega_n$, and $b_n=[p_n]_\chi$. The degree-$N$ source
$\mathcal P_N=(\mathbb C^{N+1},O_N)$ has
$O_N=\operatorname{diag}(\omega_0,\ldots,\omega_N)$ and quotient map
$B_N=[b_0,\ldots,b_N]$. For $N\ge q-1$, its first $q$ columns form an
invertible triangular matrix, so $B_N$ is surjective and
\begin{equation}
 K_N=B_NO_N^{-1}B_N^*,\quad G_N=K_N^{-1},\quad
 R_N=O_N^{-1}B_N^*G_N,\quad V_N=\det G_N>0.\tag{RM.1}
\end{equation}
Here $K_N$ is a positive form on the conjugate dual coefficient space and
$G_N$ is the original quotient inner product, conjugate-linear in its first
argument. Direct multiplication proves $B_NR_N=1$ and
$R_N^*O_NR_N=G_N$. If $u\in\ker B_N$, then
$u^*O_NR_Nx=u^*B_N^*G_Nx=0$. Thus $R_N$ is the actual least-norm lift.

Fix $q-1\le i\le j$, set $r=j-i$, and write
\begin{equation}
 F=[b_{i+1},\ldots,b_j],\quad
 \Omega=\operatorname{diag}(\omega_{i+1},\ldots,\omega_j),\quad
 D=K_j-K_i=F\Omega^{-1}F^*.\tag{RM.2}
\end{equation}
For $r=0$ these maps have their literal zero-dimensional domains.
The identity $I:E_i=(E,G_i)\to E_j=(E,G_j)$ has adjoint
$T=K_iG_j:E_j\to E_i$, since $G_iT=G_j$. Consequently
\begin{equation}
 Q=TI\in\operatorname{End}(E_i),\qquad
 Z=Q^{-1}-1=DG_i=FF^\dagger,\qquad F^\dagger=\Omega^{-1}F^*G_i.
 \tag{RM.3}
\end{equation}
In particular $G_iZ=G_iDG_i\succeq0$, so $Z$ is positive self-adjoint
in the original $G_i$. With its full $q$ eigenvalues $\lambda_a\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
 g_a=(1+\lambda_a)^{-1},\qquad
 \frac{V_i}{V_j}=\det(1+Z)=\prod_{a=1}^q(1+\lambda_a).
 \tag{RM.4}
\end{equation}
All zero $\lambda_a$ contribute the factor one and remain in the dimension.
If $Z=0$, then $V_i=V_j>0$, the ratio equals one, and its logarithm equals
zero. This corrects the incoming note's phrase ``the volume is zero.''

For the real scalar $k$ in the arithmetic action identity, put
$\mathsf H_N=K_NA^*G_N+A-k1$. Regard $\mathsf H_j$ on $E_i$ via the
specified coordinate identity and denote that endomorphism by
$\widehat{\mathsf H}_j$. Calculation in the original coordinates gives
\begin{align}
 \mathsf H_iQ-Q\widehat{\mathsf H}_j
 &=(K_iA^*G_i+A-k1)K_iG_j
   -K_iG_j(K_jA^*G_j+A-k1)\notag\\
 &=AK_iG_j-K_iG_jA=[A,Q].\notag
\end{align}
Multiplying $[A,Q^{-1}]=-Q^{-1}[A,Q]Q^{-1}$ out proves
\begin{equation}
 [A,Z]=\widehat{\mathsf H}_j(1+Z)-(1+Z)\mathsf H_i.\tag{RM.5}
\end{equation}
Thus the cost and action are related even when their eigenspaces differ.

\section{The source correction, all cross-pairings, and exterior area}
The map $\Phi=R_iF:(\mathbb C^r,\Omega)\to\mathcal P_i$ has image Gram
$\Phi^*O_i\Phi=F^*G_iF$. Trace cyclicity and Hermitian symmetry give
\begin{align}
 t_1&=\Tr Z=\sum_{n=i+1}^j\frac{b_n^*G_ib_n}{\omega_n},\tag{RM.6}\\
 t_2&=\Tr Z^2=\sum_{n,m=i+1}^j
 \frac{|b_n^*G_ib_m|^2}{\omega_n\omega_m},\tag{RM.7}\\
 \mathcal A_2&=\frac{t_1^2-t_2}{2}
 =\sum_{i<n<m\le j}\frac{(b_n^*G_ib_n)(b_m^*G_ib_m)
 -|b_n^*G_ib_m|^2}{\omega_n\omega_m}.\tag{RM.8}
\end{align}
The diagonal terms cancel in $t_1^2-t_2$; each unordered pair occurs
twice, proving the last identity with its literal factor $1/2$.
In unscaled exterior bases endowed with determinant Gram matrices,
\begin{equation}
 \|\Phi\|_\HS^2=t_1,\qquad
 \|\bigwedge^2\Phi\|_\HS^2=\mathcal A_2.\tag{RM.9}
\end{equation}
Indeed the source vector $e_n\wedge e_m$ has squared norm
$\omega_n\omega_m$ and its image has exactly the numerator in (RM.8).
The alternating tensor map $u\wedge v\mapsto u\otimes v-v\otimes u$
has image Gram twice the exterior Gram. Applying this map in both source
and target yields the commutative exterior/tensor square and cancels that
same factor in the Hilbert--Schmidt ratio; neither Gram is identified
with the other without the factor.

Let $L:\mathcal P_i\to\mathcal P_j$ pad by zero and let
$N:(\mathbb C^r,\Omega)\to\mathcal P_j$ be the actual new-column
inclusion. Their images are orthogonal and $B_jN=F$, $B_jL=B_i$.
Hence the retained relation map
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak b=N-LR_iF,\quad B_j\mathfrak b=0,\quad
 \mathfrak b^*O_j\mathfrak b=\Omega+F^*G_iF.\tag{RM.10}
\end{equation}
This proves the relation identity and its full norm. Under the original
source realization $\mathcal V$, the equality
$J^{(k)}\mathcal V=\eta\pi_\chi$ makes both components have the same
arithmetic jets. The original internal quotient sends
$(\ell_j,\mathfrak b v)$ to $(\ell_j,0)$, preserving the receiving
label. Its lift sends external absence $\tau$ to $\tau$. The coefficient
amplitude map $p_R:G(R)\to R$ sends both $0_R^\bullet$ and $\tau$ to
$0_R$, and the exact fibre $p_R^{-1}(0_R)=\{0_R^\bullet,\tau\}$
records precisely the information lost by this subsequent observation.
Likewise a dependent admitted wedge remains an admitted source vector
whose observed squared area is $0^\bullet$.

\section{Sharp determinant bound, exact remainder, and all boundary cases}
For $q\ge2$, positivity gives $t_1^2/q\le t_2\le t_1^2$ by
Cauchy--Schwarz and the nonnegative pair products. Define
\begin{equation}
 d=\sqrt{\frac{qt_2-t_1^2}{q-1}},\quad
 a=\frac{t_1+(q-1)d}{q},\quad b=\frac{t_1-d}{q}.\tag{RM.11}
\end{equation}
Thus $0\le d\le t_1$, $0\le b\le a$, and direct expansion proves
$a+(q-1)b=t_1$ and $a^2+(q-1)b^2=t_2$.
For any eigenvalue $x$, the other $q-1$ eigenvalues give
$t_2\ge x^2+(t_1-x)^2/(q-1)$. Solving that quadratic yields $x\le a$.
Consequently $Q\succeq(1+a)^{-1}1$ in $G_i$.

Here is a direct algebraic proof of the entire interpolation remainder.
For $s\ge1$, let
\begin{equation}
 H_s(x)=\frac1{s+b}-\frac{x-b}{(s+b)^2}
       +\frac{(x-b)^2}{(s+a)(s+b)^2}.\tag{RM.12}
\end{equation}
Its value and derivative agree with $1/(s+x)$ at $b$, and its value
agrees at $a$. Multiplication by the common denominator proves, identically,
\begin{equation}
 \frac1{s+x}-H_s(x)
 =\frac{(a-x)(x-b)^2}{(s+x)(s+b)^2(s+a)}.\tag{RM.13}
\end{equation}
Integrate $1/s-1/(s+x)$ over $[1,\infty)$ to obtain $\log(1+x)$.
Integration of $1/s-H_s(x)$ gives its quadratic interpolant
\begin{align}
 P(x)&=\log(1+b)+\frac{x-b}{1+b}+c(x-b)^2,\notag\\
 c&=\frac{\log(1+a)-\log(1+b)-(a-b)/(1+b)}{(a-b)^2}<0\quad(a>b).
 \tag{RM.14}
\end{align}
The coefficient integrals converge: the quadratic coefficient is
$-\int_1^\infty((s+a)(s+b)^2)^{-1}ds$, and the other two are the
displayed logarithm and reciprocal. Therefore, for $0\le x\le a$,
\begin{equation}
 P(x)-\log(1+x)=(a-x)(x-b)^2
 \int_1^\infty\frac{ds}{(s+x)(s+b)^2(s+a)}\ge0.\tag{RM.15}
\end{equation}
The trace of any quadratic depends only on $q,t_1,t_2$. Those values
equal the moments of $a,b,\ldots,b$, so summing (RM.15) proves
\begin{equation}
 \log(V_i/V_j)\le U_2:=\log(1+a)+(q-1)\log(1+b).\tag{RM.16}
\end{equation}
The remainder in (RM.15) is strictly positive away from $x=a,b$. If $m$ eigenvalues
equal $a$, the first trace identity gives
$ma+(q-m)b=a+(q-1)b$, hence $(m-1)(a-b)=0$. Equality for $a>b$
therefore occurs exactly once at $a$ and $q-1$ times at $b$.
That spectrum realizes the prescribed moments, proving sharpness from
these data. For $a=b$, zero variance forces $Z=b1$ and equality already
holds. For $q=1$, the ratio is exactly $1+t_1$. For $q=0$, its empty
determinant is one. The case $i=j$ gives $Z=0$ at every admitted dimension.

With $t_3=\Tr Z^3$, set
\begin{equation}
 C_3=-t_3+(a+2b)t_2-(2ab+b^2)t_1+qab^2
 =\Tr((a1-Z)(Z-b1)^2)\ge0.\tag{RM.17}
\end{equation}
Finite spectral summation of (RM.15) proves the exact loss
\begin{equation}
 U_2-\log\det(1+Z)=\int_1^\infty
 \frac{\Tr((a1-Z)(Z-b1)^2(s1+Z)^{-1})}{(s+b)^2(s+a)}\,ds.
 \tag{RM.18}
\end{equation}
Every factor is a function of the same $G_i$-self-adjoint endomorphism.
Since $(s+x)(s+b)^2(s+a)\le(s+a)^4$, the loss is at least
$C_3\int_1^\infty(s+a)^{-4}ds=C_3/(3(1+a)^3)$.
Also Jensen's scalar inequality, whose strictness follows from
$\frac{d^2}{dx^2}\log(1+x)=-(1+x)^{-2}<0$, gives
$U_2\le q\log(1+t_1/q)$, strictly unless $a=b$.

For $q\ge2$ and $t_1>0$,
$\mathcal A_2=(q-1)b(t_1-qb/2)$ and $0\le b\le t_1/q$ imply
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\mathcal A_2}{(q-1)t_1}\le b
 \le\frac{2\mathcal A_2}{(q-1)t_1},\qquad
 \frac{V_i}{V_j}\le(1+t_1)
 \left(1+\frac{2\mathcal A_2}{(q-1)t_1}\right)^{q-1}.\tag{RM.19}
\end{equation}
When $\mathcal A_2=0<t_1$, all eigenvalue pair products vanish, so exactly
one eigenvalue is positive and the ratio is $1+t_1$.

\section{The comparator join in the original metric}
Let $U_j$ be the actual explicit upper kernel supplied by the arithmetic
comparison construction, expressed in the same quotient coordinates.
That construction proves $U_j\succeq K_j\succeq K_i$ as dual forms.
Define from the available lower-degree arithmetic data and this comparator
\begin{equation}
 D_+=U_j-K_i,\quad Z_+=D_+G_i,\quad
 t_1^+=\Tr Z_+,\quad t_2^+=\Tr Z_+^2.\tag{RM.20}
\end{equation}
Then $G_i(Z_+-Z)=G_i(U_j-K_j)G_i\succeq0$. Thus both operators are
positive self-adjoint in $G_i$ and $Z_+\succeq Z$ there. For completeness,
ordered eigenvalues increase under this order: for an orthonormal eigenbasis
of either operator, the top-$m$ span gives the lower inequality below;
every $m$-dimensional subspace intersects the span of eigenvectors numbered
$m,\ldots,q$, which gives the upper inequality. This proves the max--min characterization
\[
 \lambda_m(X)=\max_{\dim M=m}\ \min_{v\in M\setminus0}
 \frac{\langle v,Xv\rangle_{G_i}}{\langle v,v\rangle_{G_i}}.
\]
Applying the pointwise form inequality inside this characterization yields
$\lambda_m(Z_+)\ge\lambda_m(Z)$ for each of the original $q$ indices.
In particular, for $q\ge2$, with $a_+,b_+$ obtained by (RM.11) from $t_1^+,t_2^+$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{V_i}{V_j}\le\det(1+Z_+)=\det(U_jG_i)
 \le(1+a_+)(1+b_+)^{q-1}.\tag{RM.21}
\end{equation}
No monotonicity of the sharp bound as a function of two separately varied
trace numbers was used; the first inequality follows from the actual form
order, and the second follows from (RM.16) for $Z_+$.
For $q\ge2$, $Q\succeq(1+a_+)^{-1}1$ is a derived finite gap.
For $q=1$, the comparator determinant is $1+t_1^+$ and
$Q\succeq(1+t_1^+)^{-1}1$; for $q=0$, the empty determinant is one.
When $i\le q$, the actual input to $K_i,G_i$ uses moments only through
$2i\le2q$; $U_j$ uses the explicit comparison measure's moments through
$2j\le4q$ in the two endpoint blocks. Formula (RM.21) supplies a certificate
without introducing the unknown actual high-degree moments into its inputs.

There is an exact source map behind this comparison. Interpret $D_+$ as
a positive Hermitian form on the conjugate dual coefficient space $E^\vee$.
Let
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal C_+=E^\vee/\ker D_+,\quad
 \langle[\alpha],[\beta]\rangle_+=\alpha^*D_+\beta,\quad
 J_+[\alpha]=D_+\alpha\in E_i.\tag{RM.22}
\end{equation}
These expressions are independent of representatives. Positivity implies
the only zero-norm classes are the removed radical: diagonalize the positive
Hermitian form in the stated coordinate space to prove
$\alpha^*D_+\alpha=0\Longleftrightarrow D_+\alpha=0$.
This diagonalization proves a fact about that form and does not alter $G_i$.
The adjoint is $J_+^\dagger v=[G_iv]$, hence
$J_+J_+^\dagger=Z_+$ and
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_+=R_iJ_+:\mathcal C_+\to\mathcal P_i,\quad
 \|\Phi_+\|_\HS^2=t_1^+,\quad
 \|\bigwedge^2\Phi_+\|_\HS^2=((t_1^+)^2-t_2^+)/2.\tag{RM.23}
\end{equation}
To prove the second identity in any Gram basis, diagonalize the positive
endomorphism $\Phi_+^\dagger\Phi_+$ on its existing source Hilbert space.
Its exterior eigenvalues are all unordered products. Its nonzero eigenvalues
agree, with multiplicity, with those of $\Phi_+\Phi_+^\dagger$ by the
mutually inverse maps $\Phi_+$ and $\lambda^{-1}\Phi_+^\dagger$ on each
nonzero eigenspace. Finally $R_i$ is an isometry from $E_i$ into
$\mathcal P_i$, which proves (RM.23), retaining the zero eigenspaces.

The original correction factors through this source by an exact contraction.
For $\alpha\in\ker D_+$, (RM.2) and $D\preceq D_+$ imply
$\alpha^*F\Omega^{-1}F^*\alpha=0$, so $F^*\alpha=0$.
Thus $Fv\in\im D_+$ for every $v$. There is a unique class $C v=[u]$
solving $D_+u=Fv$. This defines a linear map
\begin{equation}
 C:(\mathbb C^r,\Omega)\to\mathcal C_+,\qquad
 J_+C=F,\qquad \Phi_+C=\Phi.\tag{RM.24}
\end{equation}
Let $s=u^*D_+u\ge0$. The equality $D_+u=Fv$ and Cauchy--Schwarz in
$\Omega$ give
$s=|u^*Fv|\le (u^*F\Omega^{-1}F^*u)^{1/2}(v^*\Omega v)^{1/2}
\le s^{1/2}(v^*\Omega v)^{1/2}$. Dividing when $s>0$, with the case
$s=0$ immediate, proves $\|Cv\|_+^2\le v^*\Omega v$.
The full kernel is $\ker C=\ker F$ by (RM.24) and uniqueness of the
class. Exterior functoriality gives
$\bigwedge^2\Phi=(\bigwedge^2\Phi_+)(\bigwedge^2 C)$, preserving
every admitted pair. This is the exact morphism from the old source
correction to the new positive-form source. The original relation
$N-L\Phi$ from (RM.10) is retained, with its original supported-zero image.

The cost-action defect of the comparator also has an explicit form.
Put $\Delta=U_j-K_j$. Then
$Z_+=Z+\Delta G_i$, and direct expansion using
$\widehat{\mathsf H}_j=A+K_jA^*G_j-k1$ gives
\begin{align}
 [A,Z_+]&=\widehat{\mathsf H}_j(1+Z_+)
 -(1+Z_+)\mathsf H_i+\mathcal E,\tag{RM.25}\\
 \mathcal E&=[A,\Delta G_i]-\widehat{\mathsf H}_j\Delta G_i
 +\Delta G_i\mathsf H_i\notag\\
 &=\Delta A^*G_i-K_jA^*G_j\Delta G_i.\notag
\end{align}
The second line defines the exact defect; substituting the displayed
control matrices and cancelling equal terms proves the last expression.
It is not discarded or asserted small.

\section{Rank information with every zero direction retained}
Let $W=\im F\subset E_i$ and $\rho=\dim W$. Since
$\langle v,Zv\rangle_{G_i}=\|F^\dagger v\|_\Omega^2$, one has
\begin{equation}
 \ker Z=\ker F^\dagger=W^{\perp_{G_i}},\qquad
 E_i=W\mathbin{\oplus^{\perp_{G_i}}}W^{\perp_{G_i}}.\tag{RM.28}
\end{equation}
To check invariance and positivity on $W$, $Z=FF^\dagger$ has image in
$W$, and the only vector of $W$ in its kernel is zero by (RM.28).
The restriction $Z_W$ is therefore positive definite on its inherited
metric, and the literal inclusion/projection maps give
\begin{equation}
 Z=Z_W\oplus0_{q-\rho},\quad
 Q=(1_W+Z_W)^{-1}\oplus1_{q-\rho},\quad
 \det(1_E+Z)=\det(1_W+Z_W)\,1^{q-\rho}.\tag{RM.29}
\end{equation}
The source kernel is also retained. Put
$\mathcal U=(\ker F)^{\perp_\Omega}$ in the original source.
Its orthogonal projection $P_{\mathcal U}$ satisfies
$F=F|_{\mathcal U}P_{\mathcal U}$. The map
$F|_{\mathcal U}:\mathcal U\to W$ is a bijection: injectivity is the
orthogonal intersection with $\ker F$, and surjectivity follows by
decomposing any source preimage. Thus it identifies the source Hilbert
quotient $(\mathbb C^r,\Omega)/\ker F$ with $W$ by a specified map;
the norms on these objects are related by that map's retained positive
Gram, rather than being asserted equal. Its composition with $R_i$
is precisely the old least-lift correction on the quotient source.
For $v\in\ker F$, the original relation (RM.10) is $Nv$, so the kernel
directions remain actual newly admitted theta relations, with their norm
$v^*\Omega v$ and their receiving supported-zero labels.

Any proved integer upper bound $\rho\le R\le q$ gives a sharper
finite determinant certificate. The $\rho$ positive eigenvalues and
$R-\rho$ expressly retained zeros have the same $t_1,t_2$. For $R\ge2$ set
\begin{equation}
 d_R=\sqrt{\frac{Rt_2-t_1^2}{R-1}},\quad
 a_R=\frac{t_1+(R-1)d_R}{R},\quad

Applying the already proved scalar argument to that $R$-entry list gives

\end{equation}

\section{The entire comparison excess is the source contraction defect}
The contraction in (RM.24) has a concrete adjoint:
\begin{equation}
 C^\dagger[\alpha]=\Omega^{-1}F^*\alpha.\tag{RM.32}
\end{equation}
It is well-defined because $\ker D_+\subset\ker F^*$ was proved above.
For $Cv=[u]$, its defining equation gives
$\langle Cv,[\alpha]\rangle_+=u^*D_+\alpha=v^*F^*\alpha
=\langle v,\Omega^{-1}F^*\alpha\rangle_\Omega$, proving the adjoint
identity. Define $\mathcal L=1_{\mathcal C_+}-CC^\dagger$. Then
\begin{align}
 \langle[\alpha],\mathcal L[\beta]\rangle_+
 &=\alpha^*(D_+-F\Omega^{-1}F^*)\beta
 =\alpha^*(U_j-K_j)\beta,\tag{RM.33}\\
 \mathcal L&\succeq0,\qquad
 J_+\mathcal L J_+^\dagger=(U_j-K_j)G_i=Z_+-Z.\tag{RM.34}
\end{align}
The first equality follows by (RM.32) and the definition of the adjoint;
the second follows by (RM.2) and (RM.20). The right-hand form is
nonnegative by the actual upper-kernel comparison. Multiplying out the
left side of (RM.34) gives $J_+J_+^\dagger-J_+CC^\dagger J_+^\dagger
=Z_+-FF^\dagger$, proving its last equality. Thus the complete excess
of the comparator over the arithmetic cost is retained as a specific

For clarity, the arithmetic upper kernel itself is formed on the original
