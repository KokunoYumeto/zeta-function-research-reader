\subsection{1. Coordinates, mass, and exponential moments}\label{coordinates-mass-and-exponential-moments}

Fix the original integer \texttt{k\ \textgreater{}=\ 1}, period \texttt{L\ \textgreater{}\ 0}, phase \texttt{0\ \textless{}=\ theta\ \textless{}\ 2\ pi}, and the original nonempty full-order packet \texttt{h}. Throughout,

\[
c=k/2,\qquad
u_{n,\theta}=\frac{2\pi n+\theta}{L},\qquad
S_{n,\theta}=c+i u_{n,\theta},\qquad n\in\mathbb Z,
\tag{CQ1}
\]

\[
v_h=g/h,\qquad g=2\xi,\qquad
w_h(t)=\frac{|v_h(1/2+it)|^2}{2\pi},\qquad
m_{h,k}=w_h^{*k},\qquad
\nu_{n,\theta}=\frac{2\pi}{L}m_{h,k}(u_{n,\theta}).
\tag{CQ2}
\]

No mass is divided out. Put

\[
I_\theta=\{n\in\mathbb Z:\nu_{n,\theta}>0\},\qquad
\mathcal H_\theta=\ell^2(I_\theta,\nu_\theta).
\tag{CQ3}
\]

The notation \texttt{ell\^{}2(nu)} on all integers means this same Hilbert space after quotienting the zero seminorm coordinates. A zero-mass coordinate is not an evaluation functional on that Hilbert space.

The predecessor's actual-source input, explicitly recorded immediately before P54, is that for each \texttt{0\ \textless{}\ b\ \textless{}\ pi/2},

\[
f_b(t)=e^{b|t|}w_h(t)\in L^1(\mathbb R)\cap L^\infty(\mathbb R).
\tag{CQ4}
\]

This is an inherited estimate for the specified entire quotient, not an extra assumption about a new density. Its consequence for sampled measures is proved here. In the convolution integral, \texttt{\textbar{}t\_1+...+t\_k\textbar{}\ \textless{}=\ \textbar{}t\_1\textbar{}+...+\textbar{}t\_k\textbar{}}, so

\[
e^{b|u|}m_{h,k}(u)
\le f_b^{*k}(u)
\le C_{b,h,k},\qquad
C_{b,h,k}:=\|f_b\|_\infty\|f_b\|_1^{k-1}.
\tag{CQ5}
\]

The second inequality follows by integrating one bounded factor and the remaining \texttt{k-1} factors in \texttt{L\^{}1}. It includes \texttt{k=1}. For any \texttt{0\ \textless{}=\ a\ \textless{}\ b}, the literal sum satisfies

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{n\in I_\theta}\nu_{n,\theta}e^{a|u_{n,\theta}|}
&\le\frac{2\pi C_{b,h,k}}L
\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}e^{-(b-a)|2\pi n+\theta|/L}\\
&=\frac{2\pi C_{b,h,k}}L
\frac{e^{-(b-a)\theta/L}+e^{-(b-a)(2\pi-\theta)/L}}
{1-e^{-2\pi(b-a)/L}}<\infty.
\end{aligned}
\tag{CQ6}
\]

The two series use \texttt{n\ \textgreater{}=\ 0} and \texttt{n\ \textless{}=\ -1}, respectively. Formula CQ6 also includes \texttt{theta=0} without deleting its zeroth term.

Let \texttt{chi(S)=sum\_\{j=0\}\^{}q\ chi\_j\ S\^{}j} be the literal monic original polynomial, of degree \texttt{q\textgreater{}0}. For real \texttt{u},

\[
|\chi(c+iu)|\le
C_\chi(1+|u|)^q,\qquad
C_\chi=\sum_{j=0}^q|\chi_j|\max(1,c)^j.
\tag{CQ7}
\]

Choose \texttt{0\ \textless{}\ a\_1\ \textless{}\ a\_2\ \textless{}\ b}. The continuous function \texttt{(1+t)\^{}\{2q\}\ exp(-(a\_2-a\_1)t)} is bounded on \texttt{t\ \textgreater{}=\ 0}, since it tends to zero at infinity. Multiplying CQ7 by the exponential and using CQ6 proves

\[
\sum_{n\in I_\theta}
|\chi(S_{n,\theta})|^2\nu_{n,\theta}
e^{a_1|u_{n,\theta}|}<\infty.
\tag{CQ8}
\]

Thus the original discrete measure and its \texttt{\textbar{}chi\textbar{}\^{}2} multiple each have a finite positive exponential moment. All their polynomial moments exist.

\subsection{2. A complete polynomial-density proof on the shifted lattice}\label{a-complete-polynomial-density-proof-on-the-shifted-lattice}

The following argument applies to either measure. Retain the shifted lattice CQ1, a subset \texttt{I} of its indices, and positive masses \texttt{mu\_n} such that

\[
\sum_{n\in I}\mu_ne^{a|u_{n,\theta}|}<\infty
\quad\text{for some }a>0.
\tag{CQ9}
\]

Suppose \texttt{f} in \texttt{ell\^{}2(I,mu)} is orthogonal to all polynomial evaluation vectors. Since \texttt{u=(S-c)/i}, the evaluations of polynomials in \texttt{S} and in the real coordinate \texttt{u} span the same subspace. Conjugating the orthogonality equations if necessary gives

\[
\sum_{n\in I}f_n\mu_nu_{n,\theta}^{j}=0
\quad(j=0,1,2,\ldots).
\tag{CQ10}
\]

Define

\[
F(z)=\sum_{n\in I}f_n\mu_ne^{zu_{n,\theta}},
\qquad |\Re z|<a/2.
\tag{CQ11}
\]

For \texttt{0\ \textless{}=\ t\ \textless{}\ a/2}, Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
\sum_{n\in I}|f_n|\mu_ne^{t|u_{n,\theta}|}
\le\|f\|_{\ell^2(\mu)}
\left(\sum_{n\in I}\mu_ne^{2t|u_{n,\theta}|}\right)^{1/2}<\infty.
\tag{CQ12}
\]

This proves locally uniform absolute convergence on the open strip in CQ11. For every derivative order \texttt{j} and every smaller closed strip, choose \texttt{t\textquotesingle{}} strictly between its width and \texttt{a/2}. The bound

\texttt{sup\_\{x\ \textgreater{}=\ 0\}\ x\^{}j\ exp(-(t\textquotesingle{}-t)x)\ \textless{}\ infinity}

and CQ12 at \texttt{t\textquotesingle{}} prove locally uniform absolute convergence of the derivative series. Hence \texttt{F} is holomorphic and CQ10 says that all its derivatives at zero vanish. Its convergent Taylor series is zero on a disk about zero. Holomorphic uniqueness on the connected strip therefore gives \texttt{F(z)=0} on that entire strip.

There is no need to appeal to an unproved general Fourier-measure uniqueness statement here. CQ12 at \texttt{t=0} allows termwise integration, and for each \texttt{m\ in\ I},

\[
\begin{aligned}
0&=\frac1L\int_0^L F(it)e^{-it u_{m,\theta}}\,dt\\
&=\sum_{n\in I}f_n\mu_n
\frac1L\int_0^L e^{2\pi i(n-m)t/L}\,dt
=f_m\mu_m.
\end{aligned}
\tag{CQ13}
\]

Because \texttt{mu\_m\textgreater{}0}, every coordinate \texttt{f\_m} vanishes. The orthogonal complement of the polynomial span is zero, so that span is dense. CQ6 proves density in \texttt{H\_theta}; CQ8 proves density in the Hilbert space for the positive atoms of \texttt{\textbar{}chi\textbar{}\^{}2\ nu\_theta}.

\subsection{3. The closed relation space, exact map, metric, and kernel}\label{the-closed-relation-space-exact-map-metric-and-kernel}

Define

\[
Z_\theta=\{n\in I_\theta:\chi(S_{n,\theta})=0\},\qquad
p_\theta(S)=\prod_{n\in Z_\theta}(S-S_{n,\theta}),
\tag{CQ14}
\]

where an empty product is \texttt{1}. The sample points are distinct, so \texttt{Z\_theta} has at most \texttt{q} members and \texttt{p\_theta} is a squarefree divisor of \texttt{chi}.

Let \texttt{R\_theta} be the closure in \texttt{H\_theta} of the evaluation vectors of \texttt{chi\ C{[}S{]}}. Every such vector vanishes at all indices in \texttt{Z\_theta}. Each of those coordinates is continuous, since

\[
|f_n|\le\nu_{n,\theta}^{-1/2}\|f\|_{\mathcal H_\theta}
\qquad(n\in Z_\theta).
\tag{CQ15}
\]

It follows that \texttt{R\_theta} is contained in the subspace of vectors vanishing on \texttt{Z\_theta}. For the reverse inclusion, let \texttt{f} vanish there and set

\[
g_n=\frac{f_n}{\chi(S_{n,\theta})}
\quad(n\in I_\theta\setminus Z_\theta).
\tag{CQ16}
\]

This vector is square-integrable for the weighted masses

\texttt{mu\_n\ =\ \textbar{}chi(S\_\{n,theta\})\textbar{}\^{}2\ nu\_\{n,theta\}},

because its squared norm is exactly \texttt{sum\_\{I\_theta\textbackslash{}Z\_theta\}\ \textbar{}f\_n\textbar{}\^{}2\ nu\_\{n,theta\}}. By Section 2 there are polynomials \texttt{P\_j} tending to \texttt{g} in that weighted norm. Multiplication by the literal \texttt{chi} gives

\[
\|\chi P_j-f\|_{\mathcal H_\theta}^2
=\sum_{n\in I_\theta\setminus Z_\theta}
|P_j(S_{n,\theta})-g_n|^2
|\chi(S_{n,\theta})|^2\nu_{n,\theta}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{CQ17}
\]

At indices in \texttt{Z\_theta}, both terms on the left vanish. Consequently

\[
\mathcal R_\theta
=\{f\in\mathcal H_\theta:f_n=0\text{ for all }n\in Z_\theta\}.
\tag{CQ18}
\]

Restriction to \texttt{Z\_theta} therefore induces the exact isometric isomorphism

\[
\mathcal H_\theta/\mathcal R_\theta
\xrightarrow{\sim}
\left(\mathbb C^{Z_\theta},
\ \|(v_n)\|^2=\sum_{n\in Z_\theta}\nu_{n,\theta}|v_n|^2\right).
\tag{CQ19}
\]

The quotient norm is exactly the displayed norm: the coordinate projection onto \texttt{Z\_theta} is orthogonal, and the complementary projection has range \texttt{R\_theta}.

Since adding \texttt{chi\ Q} to a polynomial changes its evaluation vector by an element of \texttt{R\_theta}, there is a well-defined linear comparison

\[
T_\theta:E=\mathbb C[S]/(\chi)
\longrightarrow\mathcal H_\theta/\mathcal R_\theta,
\qquad
[P]\longmapsto(P(S_{n,\theta}))_{n\in Z_\theta}.
\tag{CQ20}
\]

It is surjective. When \texttt{Z\_theta} is nonempty, the literal Lagrange polynomials

\[
L_n(S)=\prod_{m\in Z_\theta\setminus\{n\}}
\frac{S-S_{m,\theta}}{S_{n,\theta}-S_{m,\theta}}
\tag{CQ21}
\]

map to its coordinate basis; when it is empty, the target is zero. A polynomial vanishes at every distinct point indexed by \texttt{Z\_theta} if and only if it is divisible by their squarefree product. Hence the exact kernel is

\[
\ker T_\theta=(p_\theta)/(\chi)\subseteq E.
\tag{CQ22}
\]

This retains the multiplicities in \texttt{chi}. In the primary decomposition of \texttt{E}, each unsampled primary block lies in the kernel; at a sampled root of multiplicity \texttt{m}, the kernel is the maximal ideal \texttt{(S-rho)/(S-rho)\^{}m}, while its scalar value survives. CQ20 is a linear map to a Hilbert quotient; no multiplication of arbitrary square-summable sequences is required.

\subsection{4. Why the original arithmetic source needs no extra positivity hypothesis}\label{why-the-original-arithmetic-source-needs-no-extra-positivity-hypothesis}

For \texttt{k\ \textgreater{}=\ 2}, the actual entire nonzero function \texttt{v\_h} has isolated zeros. Thus \texttt{w\_h(t)\textgreater{}0} off a discrete subset of the real axis. For fixed \texttt{u}, both \texttt{w\_h(t)} and \texttt{w\_h(u-t)} are positive for almost every \texttt{t}; their product is integrable by boundedness and integrability from CQ4. Its integral is strictly positive. Thus \texttt{m\_\{h,2\}(u)\textgreater{}0} for every real \texttt{u}. Inductively, convolving the everywhere-positive \texttt{m\_\{h,k-1\}} with the almost-everywhere-positive nonzero \texttt{w\_h} gives \texttt{m\_\{h,k\}(u)\textgreater{}0}. CQ5 makes the integrals finite. Therefore \texttt{I\_theta=Z} for \texttt{k\ \textgreater{}=\ 2} and CQ14 is exactly the index set displayed in H64.

For \texttt{k=1}, the original cyclic algebra is \texttt{C{[}S{]}/(h)}, with generator multiplication by \texttt{S}. Its annihilator ideal is exactly \texttt{(h)}: \texttt{h(M\_S)=0}, and any polynomial annihilating \texttt{M\_S} also annihilates \texttt{1}, so is divisible by the unchanged \texttt{h}. Consequently \texttt{(chi\_\{h,1\})=(h)}. If \texttt{chi\_\{h,1\}} denotes the monic minimal polynomial and \texttt{a\_h} is the original leading coefficient of \texttt{h}, then \texttt{chi\_\{h,1\}=h/a\_h}; this identifies the ideal generator and does not replace the original source \texttt{v\_h=g/h}. Equality \texttt{chi\_\{h,1\}=h} is asserted only when the source separately fixes \texttt{a\_h=1}.

Write the unchanged packet as

\[
h(S)=a_h\prod_{\rho\in Z_h}(S-\rho)^{m_\rho},
\qquad a_h\ne0,\qquad m_\rho=\operatorname{ord}_\rho g.
\tag{CQ23}
\]

For a selected root \texttt{rho}, full-order removal gives the exact nonzero value

\[
v_h(\rho)=
\frac{g^{(m_\rho)}(\rho)}{h^{(m_\rho)}(\rho)}=
\frac{g^{(m_\rho)}(\rho)}
{a_hm_\rho!\prod_{\sigma\in Z_h\setminus\{\rho\}}
(\rho-\sigma)^{m_\sigma}}\ne0.
\tag{CQ24}
\]

This follows by factoring \texttt{(S-rho)\^{}\{m\_rho\}} from both \texttt{g} and the unchanged \texttt{h}; the numerator derivative, original leading coefficient \texttt{a\_h}, and every denominator factor are nonzero by their definitions. If a sample satisfies \texttt{S\_\{n,theta\}=rho}, then \texttt{Re\ rho=1/2} and

\[
\nu_{n,\theta}
=\frac{2\pi}L w_h(\Im\rho)
=\frac1L|v_h(\rho)|^2
=\frac{|g^{(m_\rho)}(\rho)|^2}
{L|a_h|^2(m_\rho!)^2\prod_{\sigma\in Z_h\setminus\{\rho\}}|\rho-\sigma|^{2m_\sigma}}>0.
\tag{CQ25}
\]

Thus every root appearing in H64 has positive mass also at \texttt{k=1}. Other zero-mass lattice coordinates are discarded by the Hilbert-space definition CQ3, but none removes a summand from H64. The full-order hypothesis at NOTE.md line 67 and the preserved unit at lines 85--90 already supply the needed fact. A useful exposition repair is to define \texttt{I\_theta} and then state CQ25; it does not change H64's conclusion for this source.

For comparison, outside this arithmetic domain the omission of positive support can change the theorem. With \texttt{nu\_0=0}, \texttt{nu\_n=e\^{}\{-\textbar{}n\textbar{}\}} for \texttt{n\ !=\ 0}, and \texttt{chi(S)=S-S\_\{0,theta\}}, Section 3 gives zero completed quotient, whereas a sum over all sampled polynomial roots would give one copy of \texttt{C}. \subsection{5. The direct-integral target and its exact zero map}\label{the-direct-integral-target-and-its-exact-zero-map}

Retain the fixed polynomial \texttt{chi} and \texttt{c=k/2}. Let

\[
\mathcal F=
\{\theta\in[0,2\pi):
\theta\equiv L\Im\rho\pmod{2\pi}
\text{ for some root }\rho\text{ of }\chi
\text{ with }\Re\rho=c\}.
\tag{CQ26}
\]

Each such root specifies exactly one phase in the chosen half-open interval, and then exactly one integer \texttt{n} with \texttt{rho=S\_\{n,theta\}}. Therefore \texttt{F} is finite, with at most \texttt{q} elements. For \texttt{theta} outside \texttt{F}, the index set \texttt{Z\_theta} is empty, so CQ19 is the zero Hilbert space.

A concrete measurable realization prevents ambiguity about the field of targets. Map CQ19 into the fixed Hilbert space \texttt{ell\^{}2(Z)} by

\[
(v_n)_{n\in Z_\theta}\longmapsto
\left(\mathbf1_{n\in Z_\theta}\sqrt{\nu_{n,\theta}}v_n\right)_{n\in\mathbb Z}.
\tag{CQ27}
\]

This is an isometry onto the range of the diagonal orthogonal projection with diagonal entries \texttt{1\_\{nu\_\{n,theta\}\textgreater{}0\}\ 1\_\{chi(S\_\{n,theta\})=0\}}. Those entries are measurable: the first condition is measurable for the original continuous density, and the second is the zero set of a continuous function of \texttt{theta}. Hence these ranges define a measurable Hilbert field. Their direct integral has norm

\[
\int_0^{2\pi}\sum_{n\in Z_\theta}
\nu_{n,\theta}|v_n(\theta)|^2\,\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}.
\tag{CQ28}
\]

Every section is zero outside the finite set \texttt{F}; its norm in CQ28 is zero. The direct integral is therefore the zero Hilbert space. For each \texttt{{[}P{]}\ in\ E}, the evaluation sections of CQ20 are measurable through CQ27 and supported in \texttt{F}. They consequently define the zero class in the direct integral. The induced map and its exact kernel are

\[
E\longrightarrow
\int_{[0,2\pi)}^\oplus
(\mathcal H_\theta/\mathcal R_\theta)\,\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}
=0,
\qquad \ker=E.
\tag{CQ29}
\]

Individual exceptional fibres can remain nonzero and carry the positive masses in CQ19; a finite set has zero measure for the actual phase measure \texttt{dtheta/(2\ pi)}. CQ29 proves precisely the completed-target claim at NOTE.md line 715. It does not change the source isometry H13 or the finite quotient H24.

On a retained split support label \texttt{ell}, the actual induced map is \texttt{(ell,v)\ -\textgreater{}\ (ell,0)}, while external absence maps to external absence. This is the lift of CQ29 by the stated H4; its coefficient kernel is all of \texttt{E}, exactly as CQ29 records.
