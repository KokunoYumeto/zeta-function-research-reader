\section{Fixed arithmetic source, maps and forms}

Fix the original full actual packet $h$ for $g=2\xi$, an integer
$k\ge1$, and its original cyclic polynomial $\chi=\chi_{h,k}$,
monic of degree $q\ge1$. Keep $E=\C[S]/(\chi)$ in its
increasing-power remainder frame. The full Taylor unit
$\upsilon_h=j_h(g/h)$ occurs in the unchanged injections
\[
 \eta:E\hookrightarrow E_h^{\otimes k},\quad
 \eta[P]=\upsilon_h^{\otimes k}P(A_h^{(k)})1,
 \qquad \sigma_h^{\otimes k}\eta:E\hookrightarrow Q^{\otimes k},
 \tag{AGT1}
\]
where $E_h=\C[s]/(h)$ and $Q=\mathscr B/\Theta V$.
The latter is the top cohomology of the original tensor pushforward
over $\mathbf F_{1,\tau}$. All these maps and the original theta
primitives are supplied, with their complete proofs, in the retained
AT and AW dependency packages.

The common polynomial source is $H=\cP_{2q}$, with coordinate
$S=k/2+iu$ on the original integration line. Its original measures
are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 m_1(u)&=w_h^{*k}(u),&
 w_h(u)&=\frac{|(g/h)(1/2+iu)|^2}{2\pi},\\
 m_0(u)&=\frac{(\sqrt{2\pi})^k}{c_{k/4}}
       \frac{|\Gamma(k/4+iu/2)|^2}{2\pi},&
 c_a&=2^{1-2a}\Gamma(2a).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{AGT2}
\]
Their full masses $(2\pi)^{k/2}$ and $\mu_h^k$ remain present.
Set $m_t=(1-t)m_0+tm_1$, $0\le t\le1$. In the original coefficient
frame write $M(t)$ for its Gram on $H$, and
$\langle x,y\rangle_t=x^*M(t)y$.
The complete source-density proofs give all finite moments and
positivity almost everywhere. Therefore this form is positive on
every nonzero polynomial, for every $t$ in the closed interval.

For each $q-1\le N\le2q$, use the original exact sequence
$0\to\cP_{N-q}\xrightarrow{\times\chi}\cP_N
\xrightarrow{J_N}E\to0$, with $\cP_{-1}=0$.
Let $R_N(t):E\hookrightarrow\cP_N\subset H$ be its unique
minimum-norm section, and retain
\[
 G_N(t)=R_N(t)^*M(t)R_N(t),\qquad V_N(t)=\det G_N(t).
 \tag{AGT3}
\]
The inclusions into $H$ are part of this formula.
Write $P_N,Q_N$ for the orthogonal projections in $H$ onto
$\cP_N$ and $\chi\cP_{N-q}$ respectively, and
$Z_N=P_N-Q_N$. Then $Z_N$ projects onto $R_NE$ and has rank $q$.

The existing complete determinant-variation proof gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal B(t)&=\log\frac{V_{q-1}(t)V_q(t)}
                              {V_{2q-1}(t)V_{2q}(t)},\\
 C(t)&=M(t)^{-1}(M(1)-M(0)),\\
 \dot{\mathcal B}(t)
 &=\Tr\bigl((Z_{q-1}-Z_{2q}+Z_q-Z_{2q-1})C(t)\bigr).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{AGT4}
\]
This groups the same four signed terms into two pairs; all four
original cutoff indices and quotient Grams remain fixed.

\section{Relation energy, Gram ratios and the full principal angles}

\begin{theorem}
For $q-1\le i\le j\le2q$, define the actual difference of
minimum sections $D_{ij}=R_i-R_j:E\to H$. Then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 D_{ij}E&\subset\chi\cP_{j-q},& R_j&=Z_jR_i,\\
 G_i-G_j&=D_{ij}^*M D_{ij},&
 K_{ij}&=G_i^{-1/2}G_jG_i^{-1/2},\\
 I-K_{ij}&=G_i^{-1/2}D_{ij}^*M D_{ij}G_i^{-1/2},&
 0&\prec K_{ij}\preceq I.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{AGT5}
\]
The eigenvalues of $K_{ij}$, including multiplicities, are
$\cos^2\theta_{ij,a}$ for the full principal-angle list between
$R_iE$ and $R_jE$, with $0\le\theta_{ij,a}<\pi/2$ and
$1\le a\le q$. Consequently
\[
 \log(V_i/V_j)=\sum_{a=1}^q-\log\cos^2\theta_{ij,a}.
 \tag{AGT6}
\]
The nonzero eigenvalues of $Z_i-Z_j$ are exactly the pairs
$\sin\theta_{ij,a},-\sin\theta_{ij,a}$ for the nonzero angles;
the remaining eigenvalues are zero.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Both sections have the same remainder, so their difference lies
in the displayed relation space. The vector $R_jx$ is orthogonal
to that entire space. Also $R_ix\in\cP_j$. Thus the orthogonal
splitting of $R_ix$ into its relation and minimum-lift components
is $D_{ij}x+R_jx$, proving $R_j=Z_jR_i$ and the Gram identity.
Positivity of $G_j$ and this nonnegative difference prove the
last inequalities in (AGT5). Each square root is the unique
positive Hermitian square root on the displayed original
coefficient space; the order of its products is retained.

For the principal-angle calculation put
$U_i=R_iG_i^{-1/2}$ and $U_j=R_jG_j^{-1/2}$.
They are isometries from the displayed Euclidean coefficient
space to the two original source subspaces: $U_i^*MU_i=I$
and $U_j^*MU_j=I$. Their full cross matrix is
\[
 U_j^*MU_i=G_j^{-1/2}R_j^*MR_iG_i^{-1/2}
          =G_j^{1/2}G_i^{-1/2}.
 \tag{AGT7}
\]
The equality uses $R_j^*MD_{ij}=0$. Its squared singular values
are therefore the eigenvalues of $K_{ij}$, with no commutation
of the two Grams presumed. These singular values are positive
and at most one, defining the angles just stated. Determinants
of the displayed positive matrices give (AGT6), keeping all
original determinant factors.

Here is also a direct proof of the projection-difference spectrum.
Choose singular-vector bases $e_a$ in $R_iE$ and $f_a$ in $R_jE$
for (AGT7), so their cross inner products are
$\langle f_a,e_b\rangle_t=c_a\delta_{ab}$, with
$c_a=\cos\theta_{ij,a}>0$. For $c_a=1$, the equality of norms
gives $e_a=f_a$. For $c_a<1$, put
$s_a=\sqrt{1-c_a^2}$ and $r_a=(f_a-c_ae_a)/s_a$.
Substitution in these inner products proves that all resulting
pairs $(e_a,r_a)$ are orthonormal, orthogonal to the other pairs
and to the common vectors. On each pair the exact matrix is
\[
 \left.(Z_i-Z_j)\right|_{\operatorname{span}(e_a,r_a)}
 =\begin{pmatrix}s_a^2&-s_ac_a\\-s_ac_a&-s_a^2\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{AGT8}
\]
It has trace zero and determinant $-s_a^2$, hence eigenvalues
$s_a,-s_a$. The difference vanishes on the common vectors
and on the orthogonal complement of the two subspaces' sum.
This proves the full spectrum, including all zero-angle cases.
\end{proof}

The difference $D_{ij}$ in (AGT5) is an original polynomial
relation. The original source map $\mathcal V$ therefore gives
its literal theta primitive, while its full-jet and arithmetic
cohomology observations are zero. Precisely,
$J_jD_{ij}=0$, $\eta J_jD_{ij}=0$ and
$\sigma_h^{\otimes k}\eta J_jD_{ij}=0$; nevertheless its
source energy is exactly $G_i-G_j$ in (AGT5).
At each nonempty original support label $A$, the corresponding
linear maps lift as $(A,x)\mapsto(A,fx)$, with $\tau\mapsto\tau$.
The boundary then has a supported-zero class at that same
label, and (AGT5) retains its entire preimage energy.

\section{The actual four-volume has at most \texorpdfstring{$2q-1$}{2q-1} nonzero angles}

Apply the theorem to the two pairs $(q-1,2q)$ and $(q,2q-1)$.
For the second pair both $q$-dimensional minimum-section
subspaces lie in
\[
 \cP_{2q-1}\cap(\chi\cP_0)^\perp,
 \qquad\dim\bigl(\cP_{2q-1}\cap(\chi\cP_0)^\perp\bigr)=2q-1.
 \tag{AGT9}
\]
Indeed both are orthogonal to $\chi\cP_0$, a one-dimensional
subspace of $\cP_{2q-1}$. Their intersection thus has dimension
at least one, by the dimension formula for a sum of subspaces.
Each common unit vector gives singular value one in the
principal-angle calculation, so this pair has at least one
exact zero angle. When $q=1$ its two cutoff indices coincide,
and its single angle is exactly zero.

Retain both full lists of $q$ angles. Their combined logarithmic
cost from (AGT6) is exactly $\mathcal B(t)$. Remove one of the
proved zero contributions only in the following scalar list,
and write the remaining $m=2q-1$ entries as
\[
 x_\ell(t)=-\log\cos^2\theta_\ell(t)\ge0,
 \quad\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}x_\ell(t)=\mathcal B(t),
 \quad\sin\theta_\ell(t)=\sqrt{1-e^{-x_\ell(t)}}.
 \tag{AGT10}
\]
Other zero angles remain in the list. The zero-angle eigenspaces
and the full source maps were not removed from their original
spaces. No differentiability of a chosen eigenvector or ordered
angle branch will be needed.

Let $c_{\min}(t),c_{\max}(t)$ be the extreme eigenvalues of
$C(t)$ for $M(t)$. A self-adjoint projection difference with
the spectrum in (AGT8) is a sum of its positive and negative
rank-one eigenprojections with coefficients $s_a,-s_a$.
Each eigenprojection's trace against $C(t)$ is the Rayleigh
quotient of $C(t)$ at its unit vector and lies between these
two extremes. Subtracting each pair gives an absolute value
at most $s_a(c_{\max}-c_{\min})$. Apply this to both terms
of (AGT4), retaining their signs, to obtain
\[
 |\dot{\mathcal B}(t)|
 \le\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\sqrt{1-e^{-x_\ell(t)}}\right)
                       (c_{\max}(t)-c_{\min}(t)).
 \tag{AGT11}
\]
This first estimate retains all principal angles through the
actual source relation energies in (AGT5).

\section{The moment identity forces strict positivity}

Write the original polynomial as $\chi(S)=\sum_{j=0}^qa_jS^j$,
$a_q=1$. The antilinear algebra involution on $\C[S]$ defined by
\[
 \chi^{\#_k}(S)=\sum_{j=0}^q\overline{a_j}(k-S)^j
 \tag{AGT12}
\]
satisfies $\chi^{\#_k}(S)=\overline{\chi(S)}$ on the original
line, because $\overline S=k-S$. Its degree is $q$ and its
leading coefficient is $(-1)^q$. Applying it twice restores
each coefficient and the variable since $k$ is real.

Each term in (AGT10) is nonnegative, so $\mathcal B(t)\ge0$.
If it were zero, all angles of the first pair would be zero,
hence $R_{q-1}E=R_{2q}E$. The first space is $\cP_{q-1}$;
the second is $(\chi\cP_q)^\perp$ in $H$.
It would follow that $1$ is orthogonal to $\chi\cP_q$.
But $\chi^{\#_k}\in\cP_q$, and therefore this orthogonality
would imply
\[
 0=\langle1,\chi\chi^{\#_k}\rangle_t
   =\int_{\mathbb R}|\chi(k/2+iu)|^2m_t(u)\,du>0.
 \tag{AGT13}
\]
The last strict inequality is positivity of the original form
on the nonzero polynomial $\chi$. This contradiction proves
$\mathcal B(t)>0$ at every $t\in[0,1]$. Thus the strict
positivity follows from an exact identity in the actual
moment form; an arbitrary positive coefficient form has not
been substituted for that form.

\section{A nonlinear endpoint estimate in every degree}

\begin{theorem}
Put $m=2q-1$, let $b_{\min},b_{\max}$ be the extreme
eigenvalues of the original relative Gram
$M(0)^{-1}M(1)$, and set $\kappa=b_{\max}/b_{\min}$.
Then the original two endpoint four-volumes satisfy
\[
 \left|2\operatorname{arcosh}\!\left(e^{\mathcal B(1)/(2m)}\right)
       -2\operatorname{arcosh}\!\left(e^{\mathcal B(0)/(2m)}\right)
 \right|\le\log\kappa.
 \tag{AGT14}
\]
In particular this holds for every actual full quartet
quotient, with its unchanged value of $q$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The function $f(x)=\sqrt{1-e^{-x}}$ is continuous for $x\ge0$,
with $f(0)=0$. For $x>0$ its two derivatives give
\[
 f'(x)=\frac{e^{-x}}{2\sqrt{1-e^{-x}}},\qquad
 f''(x)=-\frac{e^{-x}}{2\sqrt{1-e^{-x}}}
        -\frac{e^{-2x}}{4(1-e^{-x})^{3/2}}<0.
 \tag{AGT15}
\]
It is consequently concave on the whole closed half-line,
by continuity at zero. More explicitly, its decreasing
derivative shows that its tangent at any positive $a$ is
above its graph on $(0,\infty)$, and continuity extends
this inequality to zero. Choose
$a=\mathcal B(t)/m>0$, proved admissible in (AGT13), and
sum those tangent inequalities at the entries in (AGT10).
Their linear terms cancel exactly, giving
\[
 \sum_{\ell=1}^m f(x_\ell(t))
 \le m\sqrt{1-e^{-\mathcal B(t)/m}},\qquad
 |\dot{\mathcal B}(t)|
 \le m\sqrt{1-e^{-\mathcal B(t)/m}}
                 (c_{\max}(t)-c_{\min}(t)).
 \tag{AGT16}
\]
This argument allows every remaining zero angle without
differentiating it.

The positive finite Grams and their sections are smooth
through the whole compact interpolation interval. Therefore
$\mathcal B$ is smooth and, by (AGT13), has a positive
minimum there. The real function
$\Phi_m(B)=2\operatorname{arcosh}(e^{B/(2m)})$, using the
nonnegative real inverse of the hyperbolic cosine, satisfies
\[
 \Phi_m'(B)=\frac{1}{m\sqrt{1-e^{-B/m}}},\qquad B>0.
 \tag{AGT17}
\]
One obtains this by differentiating
$\cosh(\Phi_m(B)/2)=e^{B/(2m)}$. Multiply (AGT16) by
this positive derivative.

To integrate the remaining spectral width, set
$D=M(0)^{-1}M(1)$. It is positive and self-adjoint for the
original $M(0)$ metric, and
$C(t)=((1-t)I+tD)^{-1}(D-I)$. Each relative eigenvalue
$b>0$ gives $c_b(t)=(b-1)/(1-t+tb)$.
Its derivative with respect to $b$ is $(1-t+tb)^{-2}>0$,
so the same extreme eigenvalues give the extremes for all
$t$. Direct integration of the logarithmic derivative yields
\[
 \int_0^1(c_{\max}(t)-c_{\min}(t))\,dt
 =\int_0^1\frac{d}{dt}
      \log\frac{1-t+tb_{\max}}{1-t+tb_{\min}}\,dt
 =\log\kappa.
 \tag{AGT18}
\]
Integrating $|(\Phi_m\circ\mathcal B)'|\le c_{\max}-c_{\min}$
proves (AGT14), including repeated relative eigenvalues.
\end{proof}

An explicit interval for the original arithmetic endpoint is
obtained without an implicit inversion. Put
$A_0=\operatorname{arcosh}(e^{\mathcal B(0)/(2m)})$
and $L=\tfrac12\log\kappa$. Since $2m\log\cosh A$ is
increasing on $A\ge0$, (AGT14) is equivalent to
\[
 2m\log\cosh(\max\{0,A_0-L\})
 \le\mathcal B(1)\le2m\log\cosh(A_0+L).
 \tag{AGT19}
\]
The lower endpoint can be zero although the actual
$\mathcal B(1)$ was proved strictly positive.
Also $\Phi_m'(B)>1/m$ for finite positive $B$, so (AGT14)
implies $|\mathcal B(1)-\mathcal B(0)|\le m\log\kappa$,
with strict inequality in this last comparison whenever
the two four-volumes differ. The full-relative-spectrum
bound in AW13--AW14 remains available as a second, separately
proved bound; none of its eigenvalues is discarded by
the exact identities (AGT5)--(AGT11).

When $q=1$, $m=1$ and the second pair is identical.
Equations (AGT14)--(AGT19) recover AW19--AW23 with exactly
the original constant and mass factors. If the endpoint
forms differ by a positive scalar only, then $\kappa=1$;
every quotient determinant has the same literal scalar to
the power $q$, and the four-volume is unchanged, as the
new inequality also proves.
