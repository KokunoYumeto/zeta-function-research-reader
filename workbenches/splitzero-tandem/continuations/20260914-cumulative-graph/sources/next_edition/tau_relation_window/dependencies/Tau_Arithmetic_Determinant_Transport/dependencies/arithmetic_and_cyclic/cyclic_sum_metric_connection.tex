\section{The cyclic sum source inside the full connection}
\label{sec:cyclic-sum-metric-connection}

Keep the original nonempty dagger-stable packet \(h\), its full orders,
\(g=2\xi\), \(v_h=g/h\), and \(k\ge2\). Use exactly (NN.1),
\(S=k/2+iu\), and \(m_k(u)=w_h^{*k}(u)>0\), with
\(w_h=|a_h|^2\) and \(a_h(t)=v_h(1/2+it)/\sqrt{2\pi}\).
The complete cyclic-sum source note is retained in the source appendices.
Its annihilator is
\[
 \chi(S)=\prod_\lambda(S-\lambda)^{\ell_\lambda},\quad
 \ell_\lambda=\max_{\sum_i\rho_i=\lambda}
               \left(1+\sum_i(m_{\rho_i}-1)\right),\quad
 C_\chi=\mathbb C[S]/(\chi).
 \tag{CM.1}
\]
Keep the full algebra \(E=E_h^{\otimes k}\), the original unit
\(U_k=\upsilon_h^{\otimes k}\), the injective algebra map
\(\alpha:C_\chi\to E^{S_k}\), and the separate module injection
\(\eta=M_{U_k}\alpha\). Their exact construction, retraction,
complement, nilpotent orders and trace comparison are proved in the
cyclic conormal section and the complete delivered source. In particular
the original analytic source is the injective map
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \mathcal V_k(P)=P(D_1+\cdots+D_k)F_h^{\otimes k},\\
 \mathscr U_k\mathcal V_k(P)=\Psi c_P,\qquad
 c_P(u)=P(k/2+iu),\\
 J^{(k)}\mathcal V_k(P)=\eta[P]_\chi.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{CM.2}
\]
Here \(\mathscr U_k\) is the original Mellin--Fourier isometry and
the change to the original sum and relative coordinates. Its norm is
\(\int |c_P|^2m_k\,du\), with its original mass \(\mu_h^k\).

\subsection{Complete polynomial moments and the original finite metric}

Put \(\mu_j=\int t^j|a_h|^2dt\),
\(\nu_j=\int t^j|a_h'|^2dt\), and
\(\theta_j=\int t^j\overline{a_h}a_h'dt\).
The fixed arithmetic phase makes \(\overline{a_h}a_h'=w_h'/2\);
integration by parts with the original decay gives
\[
 \theta_0=0,\qquad \theta_j=-\frac j2\mu_{j-1}\quad(j\ge1).
 \tag{CM.3}
\]
Define the formal series \(\mathcal M(z)=\sum\mu_jz^j/j!\) and
\(\mathcal N(z)=\sum\nu_jz^j/j!\). The entire finite moment arrays are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 M_{j,k}&=\int u^jm_k(u)du=j![z^j]\mathcal M(z)^k,\\
 B_{j,k}&=\int u^j\|\partial_u\Psi(u,\cdot)\|^2du\\
 &=j![z^j]\left(\frac1k\mathcal N(z)\mathcal M(z)^{k-1}
                 +\frac{k-1}{4k}z^2\mathcal M(z)^k\right).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{CM.4}
\]
For completeness, the multinomial expansion of
\((t_1+\cdots+t_k)^j\) and Fubini give the first identity. The
derivative is \(k^{-1}\sum_i a_h'(t_i)\prod_{r\ne i}a_h(t_r)\).
There are \(k\) diagonal terms, each carrying \(k^{-2}\), and
\(k(k-1)\) distinct-index terms with that same factor. Their formal
series are respectively \(\mathcal N\mathcal M^{k-1}/k\) and
\((k-1)\Theta(z)^2\mathcal M^{k-2}/k\), where
\(\Theta(z)=-z\mathcal M(z)/2\) by (CM.3). This proves the second
identity with every cross term and the original \(k^{-2}\) retained.

In the literal source coordinates \(1,S,\ldots,S^N\), write
\(c_j(u)=(k/2+iu)^j\). Its original amplitude and derivative Gram
matrices are, entry by entry,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 H^0_{ij}&=\int\overline{c_i}c_j m_k\,du,\\
 H^\partial_{ij}&=\int\overline{c_i}c_j\|\Psi'\|^2du
                  +\int\overline{c_i'}c_j'm_k\,du\\
 &\quad+\frac12\int\overline{c_i}c_j'm_k'\,du
                  +\frac12\int\overline{c_i'}c_jm_k'\,du,
 \qquad c_j'=ij(k/2+iu)^{j-1}\quad(j\ge1),\quad c_0'=0.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{CM.5}
\]
Each integrand coefficient is obtained by multiplying the displayed
original polynomials. Integrating its \(u^r\) coefficient uses
\(M_{r,k}\), \(B_{r,k}\), or
\(\int u^rm_k'=-rM_{r-1,k}\), with zero for \(r=0\).
This is an explicit finite matrix construction from the original
one-factor arrays; it keeps the two separate cross terms.
Both forms are positive definite. The amplitude map is injective by
\(\Psi\ne0\) almost everywhere. A zero derivative would give an
\(L^2(du;\mathscr H)\) function constant in \(u\), hence zero,
and the same argument then gives the zero polynomial.

Let \(p_j\) be the original monic orthogonal polynomials for \(m_k\),
with full norms
\(\omega_j=\int|p_j(k/2+iu)|^2m_k(u)\,du\), and
\(b_j=[p_j]_\chi\) in the fixed
remainder coordinates. For \(N\ge\deg\chi-1\), put
\[
 K_N=\sum_{j=0}^N b_jb_j^*/\omega_j,\quad G_N=K_N^{-1},\quad
 T_Nu=\sum_{j=0}^N\operatorname{coeff}(p_j)
                             \frac{b_j^*G_Nu}{\omega_j}.
 \tag{CM.6}
\]
Thus \(T_N\) is the actual coefficient matrix of the canonical
cyclic representative, and its two complete forms are
\[
 \mathcal R_N=\mathcal V_k T_N,\qquad
 \mathcal R_N^*\mathcal R_N=T_N^*H^0T_N=G_N,\qquad
 (\partial_u\mathscr U_k\mathcal R_N)^*
          (\partial_u\mathscr U_k\mathcal R_N)=T_N^*H^\partial T_N.
 \tag{CM.7}
\]
The first equality on forms follows by multiplying (CM.6) in the
orthogonal polynomial coordinates; the second is the definition of the
original derivative Gram (CM.5). The latter has not been replaced by
the constant-column Fisher estimate.

\subsection{A full real-exponential family of the same information identity}

The original-amplitude density theorem proves
\(|a_h^{(j)}(t)|\le C_{h,j,b}e^{-b|t|}\) for every \(b<\pi/4\).
It follows, by choosing \(2b>|\theta|\), that for each real
\(|\theta|<\pi/2\) the following are finite actual integrals:
\[
 M(\theta)=\int e^{\theta t}|a_h(t)|^2dt>0,\qquad
 N(\theta)=\int e^{\theta t}|a_h'(t)|^2dt.
 \tag{CM.8}
\]
The same estimates with a slightly larger real exponential on compact
subsets prove differentiation under the integral for every polynomial
power. Hence \(M,N\) are holomorphic for \(|\Re z|<\pi/2\), and
their Taylor coefficients at zero are the original arrays in (CM.4).
Using Fubini with this actual exponential, rather than only formal
coefficients, gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int e^{\theta u}m_k(u)du&=M(\theta)^k,\\
 B_k(\theta):=\int e^{\theta u}\|\Psi'(u)\|^2du
   &=\frac1k N(\theta)M(\theta)^{k-1}
               +\frac{k-1}{4k}\theta^2M(\theta)^k.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{CM.9}
\]
In this calculation
\(\int e^{\theta t}\overline{a_h}a_h'
=-\theta M(\theta)/2\). The diagonal and distinct-index product
terms are therefore exactly those used in (CM.4).

The exact Hilbert-space multiplier is
\[
 L^2(e^{\theta t}dt)\xrightarrow{f\mapsto e^{\theta t/2}f}L^2(dt),
 \quad f\longmapsto e^{\theta t/2}f,
 \quad\text{inverse }q\longmapsto e^{-\theta t/2}q.
 \tag{CM.10}
\]
It is unitary by direct integration. For the original amplitude set
\(a_\theta=e^{\theta t/2}a_h\). Its mass and derivative energy are
\[
 \|a_\theta\|^2=M(\theta),\qquad
 \|a_\theta'\|^2=N(\theta)-\frac{\theta^2}{4}M(\theta)>0.
 \tag{CM.11}
\]
Expansion of \(a_\theta'\), followed by the cross integral above,
proves the equality. If its derivative norm were zero, the nonzero
\(L^2\) function \(a_\theta\) would be constant, which is impossible;
this proves strict positivity.
The product amplitude is exactly \(\Psi_\theta=e^{\theta u/2}\Psi\),
its density is \(m_{k,\theta}=e^{\theta u}m_k\), and its relative
line projection is the same \(\Pi_0\), since the multiplier is a
nonzero scalar on each fibre. For
\(n_k=(I-\Pi_0)\Psi'\), its normal component is consequently
\(n_{k,\theta}=e^{\theta u/2}n_k\).
Applying the complete product-rule proof of (FC.4) to this specific
amplitude, including its mass, proves
\[
 \boxed{
 \int e^{\theta u}\frac{(m_k'+\theta m_k)^2}{m_k}\,du
  +4\int e^{\theta u}\|n_k(u)\|^2du
  =\frac4k M(\theta)^{k-1}
             \left(N(\theta)-\frac{\theta^2}{4}M(\theta)\right).}
 \tag{CM.12}
\]
All square quotients are taken away from the already stated fibre
zero sets; \(m_k>0\) itself everywhere. The multiplier keeps the same
zeros of the original one-factor amplitude. Its normal component is
nonzero on the same fibres as that in (FC.21)--(FC.27), so the second
integral is strictly positive. No mass has been assigned the value one.
The arithmetic maps (CM.1)--(CM.2) still use the original \(U_k\);
(CM.10) specifies the exact weighted Hilbert comparison used solely
to evaluate the displayed original exponential integrals.

\subsection{All cyclic normal energy is retained under frame enlargement}

The cyclic source uses the single relative coefficient \(1\) at every
scalar degree \(N\). Its line projection is \(\Pi_0\). The complete
relative frame of total degree \(m\) has projection \(\Pi_m\).
For any fixed cyclic polynomial \(P\), \(m\ge\deg P\), the actual
derivative amplitude has the three mutually orthogonal components
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 d(P)&=\partial_u(\Psi c_P)=t_0(P)+b_m(P)+r_m(P),\\
 t_0(P)&=\Pi_0d(P)
       =\Psi\left(ic_{P'}+\frac{m_k'}{2m_k}c_P\right),\\
 b_m(P)&=(\Pi_m-\Pi_0)d(P),\qquad
 r_m(P)=(I-\Pi_m)d(P),\\
 n_kc_P&=b_m(P)+r_m(P).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{CM.13}
\]
Here \(c_{P'}(u)=P'(k/2+iu)\). The derivative of the cyclic
coefficient is \(ic_{P'}\); multiplication by \(c_P\) commutes with
the fibre projections. Hence projecting its derivative gives precisely
the first two formulas, and subtracting proves the last one.
The nesting \(\Pi_0\le\Pi_m\) proves orthogonality of all three
components. With the original global norms this gives
\[
 \|n_kc_P\|^2=\|b_m(P)\|^2+\|r_m(P)\|^2,\qquad
 \|d(P)\|^2=\|t_0(P)\|^2+\|b_m(P)\|^2+\|r_m(P)\|^2.
 \tag{CM.14}
\]
The proved density limit (NN.4) now yields the exact convergence
\[
 r_m(P)\to0,\qquad b_m(P)\to n_kc_P\quad\text{in }\mathscr K.
 \tag{CM.15}
\]
Thus the complete fixed cyclic normal vector is the limit of the added
relative tangential vectors, with its entire positive norm retained by
(CM.14). This is the explicit map between the constant relative frame
of the new cyclic construction and the enlarging full relative frames.
It makes no substitution of one frame for another. For a fixed finite
collection of original cyclic polynomials, the corresponding restricted
Gram matrices converge as well: each entry is a pairing of finitely
many convergent \(\mathscr K\) vectors. No statement for a sequence
of changing canonical polynomials is inferred from this fixed-source
convergence.

\subsection{The canonical metric difference has its actual conormal lift}

For \(N\ge\deg\chi-1\), let \(R^{\rm full}_N\) be the original
full-source minimum on the whole arithmetic quotient \(E\), and let
\(\mathcal R_N\) be (CM.7). This full minimum exists at this degree:
\(\deg\chi-1\ge k(d-1)\). Here is a direct proof with the original
monic polynomial retained. For every \(0\le q\le k(d-1)\), choose
\(0\le\alpha_i<d\) with \(\sum_i\alpha_i=q\). The monomial
\(\mathbf s^\alpha\) in \(S^q\) has coefficient
\(q!/\prod_i\alpha_i!\ne0\). It is already a standard remainder
monomial. Every term with some exponent at least \(d\), when reduced
by the original monic \(h(s_i)\), strictly lowers total degree.
Thus the remainder of \(S^q\) has total degree exactly \(q\), with
that same nonzero coefficient. Remainders of lower powers have strictly
lower degree. A nonzero polynomial in \(S\) of degree at most
\(k(d-1)\) therefore cannot annihilate \(S\) in the full algebra.
Since \(\chi\) is its exact annihilator, the asserted degree inequality
follows. Every monic multivariate remainder now belongs to the original
full source at degree \(N\), so the full jet map is onto. Set
\[
 \Delta_N=\mathcal R_N-R^{\rm full}_N\eta,
 \qquad E_N=\Delta_N^*\Delta_N\succeq0.
 \tag{CM.16}
\]
Both representatives have the same full original jets. Their difference
is therefore an actual polynomial-source boundary. The full minimum
is orthogonal to that boundary, proving the complete Gram and control
identities
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 G_N&=\eta^*G_N^{\rm full}\eta+E_N,\\
 W_N-\eta^*W_N^{\rm full}\eta
     &=A_\chi^*E_N+E_NA_\chi-kE_N,\qquad A_\chi=M_S.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{CM.17}
\]
The second equality follows by substituting the first and using
\(A_k\eta=\eta A_\chi\). For an actual eigenvector
\(A_\chi x=\lambda x\), its exact contribution is
\[
 x^*(W_N-\eta^*W_N^{\rm full}\eta)x
       =(2\Re\lambda-k)\|\Delta_Nx\|^2.
 \tag{CM.18}
\]
This is the full effect of the positive Gram difference on that
original eigenline, including either sign of \(2\Re\lambda-k\).

Write \(\Delta_Nx=\mathcal T_h^{(k)}Q_x\) in the original
polynomial coordinates. Since its full jet is zero and \(U_k\) is a
unit, \(Q_x\in I=(h(s_1),\ldots,h(s_k))\). Fixed-order monic
division supplies \(Q_x=\sum_i h(s_i)Q_{i,x}\), linearly in \(x\).
Denote the fixed conormal map of this ideal by
\(\partial_I:I/I^2\to E\),
\([Q]\mapsto[k^{-1}\sum_i\partial_{s_i}Q]_I\).
It is independent of the source cutoff \(N\).
The retained conormal map and its derivative observation are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \kappa_N:C_\chi&\longrightarrow I/I^2,\quad
                    x\longmapsto[Q_x]_{I^2},\\
 \partial_I\kappa_N(x)&=\frac1k\sum_i h'(s_i)[Q_{i,x}]_I,\\
 J^{(k)}\mathscr L_k\Delta_Nx
       &=U_k\partial_I\kappa_N(x),\qquad
       \mathscr L_k=\frac1k\sum_i\log x_i.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{CM.19}
\]
The first expression is independent of the displayed division because
it is the actual class of the uniquely specified polynomial \(Q_x\).
The derivative of \(I^2\) lies in \(I\); its product rule gives the
second expression and proves independence of every conormal coordinate
choice. For the last equality differentiate the full \(UQ_x\): the
term \((\partial_S^{\rm rel}U)Q_x\) lies in \(I\), whereas the
other term is the stated \(U_k\) times the complete conormal row.
Mellin differentiation supplies exactly \(\mathscr L_k\), as in
(FC.34). Thus the actual canonical-minimum difference has an explicit
map into the original conormal layer and then to its full arithmetic
derivative, with every selected order and unit retained.

The original quotient sends the supported class of \(Q_x\) to its
supported zero. The conormal lift in (CM.19) is retained before that
quotient, so its possibly nonzero differentiated observation is obtained
from the stated original relation, not from differentiating the scalar
supported zero. For \(Q_x\in I^2\) the conormal class and this
derivative vanish; for general \(Q_x\in I\) their full kernels are
related exactly by
\(\ker\kappa_N=\{x:Q_x\in I^2\}\) and
\(\ker(J^{(k)}\mathscr L_k\Delta_N)
=\kappa_N^{-1}(\ker\partial_I)\), since \(U_k\) is invertible.
The original conormal kernel is computed in the conormal section. The
external \(\tau\) maps to \(\tau\) along every split lift.
