\section{The full mixed phase boundary on the original arithmetic source}
\label{sec:mbg-source}

Retain the actual quartet packet, its full order and its original primitive
from QCV and QPS:
Throughout, \(m,k\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\).
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \rho=\tfrac12+\delta+i\gamma,\quad
 0<\delta<\tfrac12,\quad\gamma>2,\\
 h(s)=\prod_{r\in\{\rho,\bar\rho,1-\rho,1-\bar\rho\}}(s-r)^m,
 \quad d=4m,\quad \Phi'=h,\quad\Phi(0)=0.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{MBG.1}\label{eq:mbg-packet}
\]
The condition that this is an actual full divisor of \(g=2\xi\) is the
counterfactual packet under investigation. Every map below is calculated
on that packet; none of its zeros is asserted to have been found.
Write \(\Phi(s)=A+F(s-\tfrac12)\) as in QCV, with the retained real
constant \(A>0\), and let
\[
 q_\Phi(t)=\operatorname{Im}F(it),\qquad
 \Phi(\tfrac12+it)=A+iq_\Phi(t),\qquad
 q_\Phi'(t)=
 \left[((t-\gamma)^2+\delta^2)((t+\gamma)^2+\delta^2)\right]^m>0.
 \tag{MBG.2}\label{eq:mbg-line}
\]
Indeed \(dF(it)/dt=i h(\tfrac12+it)\); substituting \(it\) into the
quartet polynomial gives the displayed real expression. Since \(F\) is
odd and real, \(q_\Phi\) is real and odd, of degree \(d+1\) with leading
coefficient \(1/(d+1)\). This proves all claims in (MBG.2) without a
change of the primitive constant.

\subsection{One finite matrix for the whole boundary, including mixed pairings}

Use \(E_h=\mathbb C[s]/(h)\) in its original ordered monomial basis,
\(C=\Phi(A_h)\), \(v_h=g/h\), and the entire original Taylor unit
\(\upsilon_h=j_h(v_h)\). For a coefficient vector \(w\), set
\[
 R_h(w)=\operatorname{rem}_h(\upsilon_h^{-1}w),\qquad
 (\mathsf S w)(t)=v_h(\tfrac12+it)R_h(w;\tfrac12+it).
 \tag{MBG.3}\label{eq:mbg-S}
\]
Thus \(\mathsf S\) is the Mellin transform of the original theta section
\(s_h\), with no altered coefficient, unit, or measure. Its Hilbert
target is \(\mathscr H=L^2(\mathbb R,dt/(2\pi))\). The tensor target
is \(\mathscr H^{\otimes k}=L^2(\mathbb R^k,\prod_i dt_i/(2\pi))\).
All adjoints below use these measures and the displayed coefficient
frames; inner products are conjugate linear in their first entry.
Let \(\mathsf Q\) be multiplication by \(q_\Phi(t)\), with domain
\(\{f:q_\Phi f\in\mathscr H\}\), and let
\(\mathsf T=A I+i\mathsf Q\). All columns of \(\mathsf S\) are in
the domains of every power used here, by the original polynomial
Mellin decay estimates. Define the complete matrices
\[
 G=\mathsf S^*\mathsf S,\qquad
 J=\mathsf S^*\mathsf Q\mathsf S,\qquad
 Z=\mathsf S^*\mathsf Q^2\mathsf S,\qquad
 H=\mathsf S^*\mathsf T\mathsf S=A G+iJ.
 \tag{MBG.4}\label{eq:mbg-moments}
\]
For example, putting \(r_a(s)=R_h(s^a;s)\), their entries are
\[
 \begin{split}
 G_{ab}&=\frac1{2\pi}\int
   |v_h(\tfrac12+it)|^2\overline{r_a(\tfrac12+it)}r_b(\tfrac12+it)\,dt,\\
 J_{ab}&=\frac1{2\pi}\int q_\Phi(t)
   |v_h(\tfrac12+it)|^2\overline{r_a(\tfrac12+it)}r_b(\tfrac12+it)\,dt,\\
 Z_{ab}&=\frac1{2\pi}\int q_\Phi(t)^2
   |v_h(\tfrac12+it)|^2\overline{r_a(\tfrac12+it)}r_b(\tfrac12+it)\,dt,
 \qquad 0\le a,b<d .
 \end{split}
 \tag{MBG.5}\label{eq:mbg-all-entries}
\]
These matrices retain pairings between different full root blocks.
In particular no CRT idempotents have been declared orthogonal.
The injectivity of \(\mathsf S\), equivalently \(j_h(v_hR_h(w))=w\),
proves \(G>0\).

Here are explicit coordinate maps for that original positive norm:
\[
 \mathsf F=\mathsf S G^{-1/2},\quad \mathsf F^*\mathsf F=I_d,\quad
 J_0=G^{-1/2}JG^{-1/2},\quad X=A I_d+iJ_0,\quad
 C_0=G^{1/2}CG^{-1/2},\quad L=X-C_0.
 \tag{MBG.6}\label{eq:mbg-coordinates}
\]
The map \(w\mapsto G^{1/2}w\) has exactly the original norm
\((w^*Gw)^{1/2}\); these coordinates do not redefine that norm.
The orthogonal projection onto \(\mathsf S E_h\) is
\(\mathsf P=\mathsf F\mathsf F^*\). Define
\[
 \mathsf R=(I-\mathsf P)\mathsf T\mathsf F,\qquad
 K=\mathsf R^*\mathsf R
  =G^{-1/2}(Z-JG^{-1}J)G^{-1/2}.
 \tag{MBG.7}\label{eq:mbg-covariance}
\]
For the last equality, \(\mathsf T\mathsf F=\mathsf F X+\mathsf R\)
and \(\mathsf F^*\mathsf R=0\). The \(A\)-part lies entirely in
\(\mathsf P\mathscr H\), while the orthogonal part is
\(i(I-\mathsf P)\mathsf Q\mathsf F\). Expanding its squared norm
gives exactly (MBG.7).

In fact \(K>0\), not merely \(K\ge0\). If \(\mathsf R x=0\), then
\(\mathsf Q\mathsf F x\in\mathsf S E_h\). For \(x\ne0\), the
polynomial \(p=R_h(G^{-1/2}x)\) is nonzero and has degree less than
\(d\). Cancelling \(v_h\) on an open interval where it is nonzero
would say that \(q_\Phi(t)p(\tfrac12+it)\) equals a polynomial in
\(\tfrac12+it\) of degree less than \(d\). Replacing \(q_\Phi(t)\)
by \((\Phi(\tfrac12+it)-A)/i\) makes this an identity of polynomials
in \(s\), by infinitely many points in that interval. Its left
degree is \(d+1+\deg p\), a contradiction. Hence \(\mathsf R\) is
injective and \(K>0\). Set
\[
 \varkappa_-=\lambda_{\min}(K)>0,\qquad
 \varkappa_+=\lambda_{\max}(K),\qquad \ell=\|L\|_2 .
 \tag{MBG.8}\label{eq:mbg-finite-constants}
\]
They are the eigenvalues and norm of the explicit finite matrices
(MBG.4)--(MBG.7), not constants assigned by a purity hypothesis.

For any \(d\)-dimensional endomorphism \(Y\), write
\(Y^{[k]}=\sum_{i=1}^k I^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes Y\otimes
I^{\otimes(k-i)}\). On the original full tensor space let
\(\mathsf S_k=\mathsf S^{\otimes k}\), \(G_k=G^{\otimes k}\),
\(\mathsf T_k=\sum_i\mathsf T_i\), \(C_k=C^{[k]}\), and
\[
 \mathsf B_k=\mathsf T_k\mathsf S_k-\mathsf S_k C_k .
 \tag{MBG.9}\label{eq:mbg-boundary}
\]
This is exactly the Mellin image of QPS.15, including its original
theta primitive and tensor differential signs. The entire boundary
Gram, for arbitrary mixtures of all \(4^k\) full blocks, is
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 G_k^{-1/2}\mathsf B_k^*\mathsf B_kG_k^{-1/2}
       =(L^{[k]})^*L^{[k]}+K^{[k]} .\quad}
 \tag{MBG.10}\label{eq:mbg-total-gram}
\]
To prove it, put \(\mathsf F_k=\mathsf F^{\otimes k}\) and
\[
 \mathsf R_{i,k}
   =\mathsf F^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes\mathsf R
                                  \otimes\mathsf F^{\otimes(k-i)}.
\]
The tensor product rule and (MBG.6)--(MBG.7) give
\(\mathsf B_kG_k^{-1/2}
=\mathsf F_kL^{[k]}+\sum_i\mathsf R_{i,k}\).
Here \(\mathsf F_k^*\mathsf R_{i,k}=0\). For \(i\ne j\),
\(\mathsf R_{i,k}^*\mathsf R_{j,k}=0\), because at position \(i\)
one tensor factor is \(\mathsf R^*\mathsf F=0\). For \(i=j\) that
product is the \(i\)-th copy of \(K\). Expanding the full squared
operator norm proves (MBG.10), with no omitted cross term:
all remaining cross terms are in \((L^{[k]})^*L^{[k]}\).
Consequently
\[
 k\varkappa_-G_k\ \preceq\ \mathsf B_k^*\mathsf B_k\
 \preceq (k^2\ell^2+k\varkappa_+)G_k .
 \tag{MBG.11}\label{eq:mbg-boundary-bounds}
\]
The upper bound uses \(\|L^{[k]}\|\le k\ell\); the lower bound uses
the positive summands \(K_i\ge\varkappa_-I\).
This proves full mixed-boundary injectivity quantitatively.

The actual graph map and its induced norm are
\[
 \mathsf{Gr}_k(w)=(\mathsf S_k w,\mathsf B_k w)
       \in\mathscr H^{\otimes k}\oplus\mathscr H^{\otimes k},\qquad
 G_k^{\rm gr}=G_k+\mathsf B_k^*\mathsf B_k .
 \tag{MBG.12}\label{eq:mbg-graph}
\]
Projection onto the first summand followed by the inverse of
\(\mathsf S_k:E_h^{\otimes k}\to\operatorname{im}\mathsf S_k\)
is a specified inverse on this graph. Both maps are bounded on their
finite dimensional domains. Its relative condition number satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{cond}\!\left(G_k^{-1/2}G_k^{\rm gr}G_k^{-1/2}\right)
 \le
 \frac{1+k^2\ell^2+k\varkappa_+}{1+k\varkappa_-}.
 \tag{MBG.13}\label{eq:mbg-graph-condition}
\]
Thus this exact graph/source comparison has at most linear condition
growth in \(k\), with the displayed original finite constants retained.
For an actual injective cyclic inclusion \(\eta:E_{\rm cyc}\to
E_h^{\otimes k}\), congruence by \(\eta\) in (MBG.11) proves the same
two-sided constants for
\(\eta^*G_k^{\rm gr}\eta\) relative to \(\eta^*G_k\eta\).
This is a proof about this graph and that actual inclusion. The original
period metric and the four polynomial source windows are calculated
separately below; they are not identified with this graph metric.

\subsection{The arithmetic and phase boundary on the identical terminal class}

For each original root \(r\) put
\[
 z_r=e_r(s-r)^{m-1},\qquad
 a_r=\frac{m!}{g^{(m)}(r)},\qquad
 f_r=\mathcal M s_h(z_r)=a_r\frac{g(s)}{s-r}.
 \tag{MBG.14}\label{eq:mbg-terminal}
\]
The last equality follows in the full original remainder frame.
Writing \(H_r=h/(s-r)^m\), the degree-\((d-1)\) representative of
\(z_r\) is \(H_r(s)(s-r)^{m-1}/H_r(r)\): its full jet at \(r\)
is \(y^{m-1}\), and all other full jets vanish. Multiplication by
\(\upsilon_h^{-1}\) multiplies this terminal jet by \(v_h(r)^{-1}\).
Since \(v_h(r)H_r(r)=g^{(m)}(r)/m!\), multiplying its representative
by \(v_h(s)\) proves (MBG.14). No Taylor coefficient in the surrounding
module was discarded in obtaining this terminal identity.

For \(r=\rho\) retain \(c=\Phi(\rho)=A+U+iV\), with \(U,V>0\).
On the original source define the two literal defects
\[
 b_{\rm ar}=(D-\rho)s_h(z_\rho),\qquad
 b_{\rm ph}=(\Phi(D)-c)s_h(z_\rho).
 \tag{MBG.15}\label{eq:mbg-two-defects}
\]
Their Mellin transforms are respectively \(a_\rho g(s)\) and
\(a_\rho Q_\rho(s)g(s)\), where the complete polynomial is
\[
 Q_\rho(s)=\frac{\Phi(s)-\Phi(\rho)}{s-\rho},\qquad
 b_{\rm ph}=Q_\rho(D)b_{\rm ar}.
 \tag{MBG.16}\label{eq:mbg-divided-difference}
\]
Polynomial division proves that quotient is a polynomial of degree
\(d\). The equality follows by factoring \(\Phi(D)-\Phi(\rho)\)
on the stated common polynomial domain, and preserves the original
scalar \(a_\rho\).

The exact scalar cross-pairings can be evaluated as integrals with the
unchanged source mass. Put \(N_\rho=\|s_h(z_\rho)\|^2>0\) and use the
probability measure only as notation for these ratios:
\[
 d\nu_\rho(t)=
 \frac{|a_\rho|^2|g(\tfrac12+it)|^2}
 {2\pi N_\rho[\delta^2+(t-\gamma)^2]}\,dt,\qquad
 \begin{array}{ll}
 m_t=\int t\,d\nu_\rho,&m_q=\int q_\Phi(t)\,d\nu_\rho,\\
 v_t=\int(t-m_t)^2\,d\nu_\rho,&
 v_q=\int(q_\Phi-m_q)^2\,d\nu_\rho,\\
 c_{tq}=\int(t-m_t)(q_\Phi-m_q)\,d\nu_\rho .
 \end{array}
 \tag{MBG.17}\label{eq:mbg-terminal-moments}
\]
Every norm formula below retains \(N_\rho\); introducing \(\nu_\rho\)
does not rescale a source vector. All moments exist by the same decay.
The density is positive on an open interval and almost everywhere,
since nonzero entire \(g\) has isolated zeros. In particular
\[
 v_t>0,\qquad v_q>0,\qquad c_{tq}>0,\qquad
 v_tv_q-c_{tq}^2>0.
 \tag{MBG.18}\label{eq:mbg-strict-covariance}
\]
For \(c_{tq}\), integrate
\((t-u)(q_\Phi(t)-q_\Phi(u))/2\) against
\(d\nu_\rho(t)d\nu_\rho(u)\). Expansion gives \(c_{tq}\);
strict monotonicity in (MBG.2) makes the integrand positive off the
diagonal, a set of positive measure. Equality in Cauchy--Schwarz for
the last inequality would make \(q_\Phi(t)-m_q\) a scalar multiple of
\(t-m_t\) almost everywhere. Continuity on an interval and polynomial
identity would then make \(q_\Phi\) affine, contrary to degree \(d+1\).
This proves all the strict signs.
The exact two means are
\[
 \mu_{\rm ar}=-\delta+i(m_t-\gamma),\qquad
 \mu_{\rm ph}=-U+i(m_q-V).
 \tag{MBG.19}\label{eq:mbg-means}
\]
These are \(\langle f,b\rangle/N_\rho\) in the original inner-product
convention. In particular \(\mu_{\rm ph}\ne0\).

For \(f=s_h(z_\rho)\), define \(\mathcal B_{{\rm ar},k}\) and
\(\mathcal B_{{\rm ph},k}\) by summing over the \(k\) ordered tensor
positions with respectively \(b_{\rm ar}\) and \(b_{\rm ph}\) in that
position and \(f\) in every other position. The entire joint Gram is
\[
 \frac1{N_\rho^k}
 \begin{pmatrix}
  \langle\mathcal B_{{\rm ar},k},\mathcal B_{{\rm ar},k}\rangle&
  \langle\mathcal B_{{\rm ar},k},\mathcal B_{{\rm ph},k}\rangle\\
  \langle\mathcal B_{{\rm ph},k},\mathcal B_{{\rm ar},k}\rangle&
  \langle\mathcal B_{{\rm ph},k},\mathcal B_{{\rm ph},k}\rangle
 \end{pmatrix}
 =
 k^2
 \begin{pmatrix}
  |\mu_{\rm ar}|^2&\bar\mu_{\rm ar}\mu_{\rm ph}\\
  \bar\mu_{\rm ph}\mu_{\rm ar}&|\mu_{\rm ph}|^2
 \end{pmatrix}
 +k\begin{pmatrix}v_t&c_{tq}\\c_{tq}&v_q\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{MBG.20}\label{eq:mbg-joint-tensor}
\]
In a diagonal tensor pairing the centered multipliers are
\(i(t-m_t)\) and \(i(q_\Phi-m_q)\), giving the displayed real
covariance. In two different positions the measure factors, giving
the product of means. There are \(k\) diagonal and \(k(k-1)\)
ordered off-diagonal pairs. This proves (MBG.20) with every term.
For the same nonzero class there is consequently the exact comparison
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \beta=\mu_{\rm ar}/\mu_{\rm ph},\qquad
 \mathcal E_k=\mathcal B_{{\rm ar},k}
                         -\beta\mathcal B_{{\rm ph},k},\\
 \langle f^{\otimes k},\mathcal E_k\rangle=0,\qquad
 \frac{\|\mathcal E_k\|^2}{N_\rho^k}
   =k\left(v_t+|\beta|^2v_q-2c_{tq}\operatorname{Re}\beta\right).
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{MBG.21}\label{eq:mbg-square-root-error}
\]
The coefficient in parentheses is strictly positive by (MBG.18).
The centered means cancel by the stated value of \(\beta\), and
expansion of (MBG.20) proves the norm equality. This is a calculated
square-root error between the two actual tensor boundaries; their
nonzero mean terms are still of order \(k\). In particular it does
not discard the original discrepancy:
\[
 \operatorname{Re}\frac{\langle f^{\otimes k},
                      \mathcal B_{{\rm ar},k}\rangle}{N_\rho^k}
     =-k\delta,\qquad
 \operatorname{Re}\frac{\langle f^{\otimes k},
                      \mathcal B_{{\rm ph},k}\rangle}{N_\rho^k}
     =-kU.
 \tag{MBG.22}\label{eq:mbg-mean-retained}
\]
Multiplication by the original nonzero coefficient
\(c_{h,k}=[k(m-1)]!((m-1)!)^{-k}((g/h)(\rho))^k\) multiplies
each vector by that coefficient and every pairing and squared norm
by \(|c_{h,k}|^2\). Thus (MBG.20)--(MBG.22) are exact on the
specified amplified arithmetic class of MAI, with its coefficient
retained on both sides.

\subsection{The exact attachment to the four original polynomial windows}

The preceding finite jet graph has a concrete map to the original
polynomial source, including its relations. Retain
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \chi=\chi_{h,k},\quad q=\deg\chi=[1+k(m-1)](k+1)^2,\quad
 \mathcal P_N=\mathbb C[S]_{\le N},\\
 \Sigma=s_1+\cdots+s_k,\quad
 \mathfrak h=(h(s_1),\ldots,h(s_k)).
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{MBG.23}\label{eq:mbg-window-data}
\]
All windows below have \(N\ge q-1\), with
\(\mathcal P_{-1}=0\). Their maps have the precise domains
\[
 B_N:\mathcal P_{N-q}\longrightarrow\mathcal P_N,\quad Q\longmapsto\chi Q,
 \qquad
 J_N:\mathcal P_N\longrightarrow E_{\rm cyc}=\mathbb C[S]/(\chi).
 \tag{MBG.23a}\label{eq:mbg-window-domains}
\]
The map \(s_N:E_{\rm cyc}\to\mathcal P_N\) selects the unique
representative of degree less than \(q\). Monic division proves
\(\ker J_N=\operatorname{im}B_N\), injectivity of \(B_N\), and
\(J_Ns_N=I\), including the stated empty relation space.
The degree formula applies to the full quartet and its original
cyclic vector; all \((k+1)^2\) distinct arithmetic centres have
nilpotence length \(1+k(m-1)\). Distinctness follows from
\(\delta,\gamma>0\); at every tuple the highest nilpotent coefficient
\([k(m-1)]!/((m-1)!)^k\) is nonzero. Thus no factor in this
annihilator has been silently removed.
Let \(\mathfrak r\) denote successive monic remainders in each
variable modulo \(h(s_i)\); the remainder operations commute.
For \(P\in\mathcal P_N\) put
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal V_NP&=\Bigl(\prod_i v_h(s_i)\Bigr)P(\Sigma),\\
 \eta[P]&=\upsilon_h^{\otimes k}P(A_h^{[k]})1,\qquad
 J_NP=[P]\pmod\chi,\\
 \mathsf D_NP&=
  \Bigl(\prod_i v_h(s_i)\Bigr)
  \left[
   \left(\sum_i\Phi(s_i)\right)P(\Sigma)
       -\mathfrak r\!\left(\left(\sum_i\Phi(s_i)\right)P(\Sigma)\right)
  \right].
 \end{split}
 \tag{MBG.24}\label{eq:mbg-polynomial-source}
\]
These are Mellin representatives on the original product line, with
the same product measure as before. In particular
\(\|\mathcal V_NP\|^2\) is exactly the arithmetic convolution norm
with density \(w_h^{*k}\) used in AT. The full unit cancels only in
the proved composite of maps
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathsf S_k\eta J_NP&=\Bigl(\prod_i v_h(s_i)\Bigr)\mathfrak r(P(\Sigma)),\\
 \mathsf S_k C_k\eta J_NP&=
  \Bigl(\prod_i v_h(s_i)\Bigr)
          \mathfrak r\!\left(\left(\sum_i\Phi(s_i)\right)P(\Sigma)\right),\\
 \mathsf D_N&=\mathsf T_k\mathcal V_N-\mathsf S_k C_k\eta J_N .
 \end{split}
 \tag{MBG.25}\label{eq:mbg-strict-window-map}
\]
For example \(R_h^{\otimes k}\upsilon_h^{\otimes k}\) is the identity
on full tensor classes, whose unique polynomial representative is
\(\mathfrak r\); this proves the first line. Multiplication by the
original phase gives the second. These equalities prove all types
and the last identity.

The literal difference
\(\mathcal V_N-\mathsf S_k\eta J_N\) is a theta relation with an
explicit polynomial primitive. Its contribution to the full graph map
and all cross-pairings is also exact. Put
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \mathfrak I_N=\eta J_N,\qquad
 \Delta_N=\mathcal V_N-\mathsf S_k\mathfrak I_N,\qquad
 \mathsf E_N=(\Delta_N,\mathsf T_k\Delta_N),\\
 (\mathcal V_N,\mathsf D_N)
      =\mathsf{Gr}_k\mathfrak I_N+\mathsf E_N .
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{MBG.25a}\label{eq:mbg-graph-relation-composite}
\]
The second component follows by subtracting (MBG.9) composed with
\(\mathfrak I_N\) from (MBG.25); the first is the definition of
\(\Delta_N\). Therefore, in the unchanged coefficient frames,
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 Y_N=\mathsf S_k^*\Delta_N+
                   \mathsf B_k^*\mathsf T_k\Delta_N,\qquad
 Z_N^{\rm rel}=\Delta_N^*\Delta_N+
                  (\mathsf T_k\Delta_N)^*\mathsf T_k\Delta_N,\\
 \widehat M_N=\mathfrak I_N^*G_k^{\rm gr}\mathfrak I_N
     +\mathfrak I_N^*Y_N+Y_N^*\mathfrak I_N+Z_N^{\rm rel}.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{MBG.25b}\label{eq:mbg-graph-relation-cross}
\]
This is the expansion of the squared norm of the preceding exact
sum of maps. In particular neither cross term is zero by a declared
splitting. Each is an actual finite polynomial integral in the same
original product measure; (MBG.28)--(MBG.29) below give the complete
entry formula for their sum. On a relation \(\chi Q\) the first
summand of (MBG.25a) vanishes, while its second is retained.

To construct the promised primitive on any polynomial \(F\),
set \(F_0=F\) and divide successively
\[
 F_{i-1}=h(s_i)Q_i+F_i,\qquad \deg_{s_i}F_i<d
       \quad(1\le i\le k).
 \tag{MBG.26}\label{eq:mbg-divisions}
\]
Earlier reduced variables remain reduced by this division, so
\(F_k=\mathfrak rF\) and \(F-\mathfrak rF=\sum_i h(s_i)Q_i\).
After multiplication by \(\prod_jv_h(s_j)\), the \(i\)-th term is
\(g(s_i)\prod_{j\ne i}v_h(s_j)Q_i\). Each monomial of \(Q_i\)
is the Mellin transform of a tensor with \(D_i\)-polynomial applied
to the original seed \(\phi_*\) in degree zero at position \(i\)
and the original Euler-inverse theta sources in degree one elsewhere.
Give that cochain sign \((-1)^{i-1}\). Its tensor differential
contributes the second sign \((-1)^{i-1}\), and hence exactly the
displayed term. Summing proves the primitive for \(F=P(\Sigma)\)
and for the phase polynomial in (MBG.24). This retains the actual
relation source before passing to cohomology.
In particular on the full original relation space one has
\[
 \mathsf D_N(\chi Q)=\mathsf T_k\mathcal V_N(\chi Q),\qquad
 \|\mathsf D_N(\chi Q)\|^2\ge k^2A^2
                                  \|\mathcal V_N(\chi Q)\|^2.
 \tag{MBG.27}\label{eq:mbg-relation-control}
\]
Here \(\mathfrak r(\chi(\Sigma)Q(\Sigma))=0\), since
\(\chi(A_h^{[k]})1=0\), and multiplication by the phase preserves
that zero class. On the product line the multiplier of
\(\mathsf T_k\) has real part \(kA\), proving the inequality.
Thus the kernel directions in the original four windows retain
their source and their controlled nonzero phase energy.

All entries of the resulting graph Gram are explicit finite sums of
original one-variable moments. For \(0\le j\le N\), expand
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P_j(s_1,\ldots,s_k)&=
  \left(\sum_i\Phi(s_i)\right)\Sigma^j
       -\mathfrak r\!\left(\left(\sum_i\Phi(s_i)\right)\Sigma^j\right)
 \\&=\sum_{\alpha}d_{\alpha j}s_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots s_k^{\alpha_k},\\
 M_{ab}^{(h)}&=\frac1{2\pi}\int
    \bar s^{\,a}s^b|v_h(s)|^2\,dt,\quad s=\tfrac12+it .
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{MBG.28}\label{eq:mbg-coefficients}
\]
Every coefficient \(d_{\alpha j}\) is specified by the finite monic
division (MBG.26), and each exponent satisfies
\(\alpha_i\le N+d+1\). Absolute integrability and the finite sum
give the complete formula
\[
 (D_N)_{ij}:=\langle\mathsf D_NS^i,\mathsf D_NS^j\rangle
   =\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\bar d_{\alpha i}d_{\beta j}
                          \prod_{\ell=1}^k M_{\alpha_\ell\beta_\ell}^{(h)},
 \qquad \widehat M_N=M_N+D_N .
 \tag{MBG.29}\label{eq:mbg-finite-window-gram}
\]
Here \(M_N\) is precisely the arithmetic source Gram of AT, and
\(\widehat M_N\) is the norm of
\(P\mapsto(\mathcal V_NP,\mathsf D_NP)\). The source
\(\mathcal V_N\) is injective: a nonzero polynomial in \(\Sigma\)
cannot vanish on every product-line point. Thus both Grams are
positive definite, including the relation-source restrictions.
The boundary Gram \(D_N\) is itself positive definite. For a
nonzero \(P\) of degree \(n\), the polynomial
\((\sum_i\Phi(s_i))P(\Sigma)\) has \(s_1\)-degree \(n+d+1\)
with nonzero leading coefficient. Its complete remainder has
\(s_1\)-degree less than \(d\). Their difference is therefore a
nonzero polynomial. After multiplication by the nonzero entire
product \(\prod_i v_h(s_i)\), it cannot vanish on a product of
open intervals of the Mellin line: fixing all but one variable
successively would force every polynomial coefficient to vanish.
Its squared integral is consequently strictly positive.

Retain multiplication \(B_N:Q\mapsto\chi Q\), the literal remainder

\subsection{Original mixed support and the existing analytic period comparison}

Every displayed linear map lifts at an original outer label \((W,S)\)

separately to each coordinate:
\[
 (W,S;B,(x_i)_{i\in B})
 \longmapsto (W,S;B,(f(x_i))_{i\in B}),\qquad

 \tag{MBG.34}\label{eq:mbg-faces}
\]

commutes with \(f\), because \(f(0)=0\). At the empty mask the
unique zero tuple remains a present labelled vertex. Thus none of
these maps identifies it with \(\tau_{\rm out}\), nor removes
proper-source kernels. These are the existing MCF/CFA module maps,
not claims that Gram matrices or linear sections are ring maps.
Here is the precise coefficient domain of this analytic attachment.
At an original arithmetic realization \(\alpha:R\to\mathbb C\),
regard its complex source as an \(R\)-module through \(\alpha\).

\(f(\alpha(a)x)=\alpha(a)f(x)\). For an original coefficient arrow

 \mu_X:R'\otimes_R X\longrightarrow X,\quad
       b\otimes x\longmapsto\alpha'(b)x,\qquad
 \mu_Y(1\otimes f)=f\mu_X .
 \tag{MBG.34a}\label{eq:mbg-scalar-realization}
\]
Balancing follows from \(\alpha'\lambda=\alpha\); evaluating a pure
