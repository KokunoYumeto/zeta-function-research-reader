\section{The actual sectorial comparison of the original full packet}
\label{GraphV2:sec:spg-sectorial}

Retain the original nonempty packet
\(h(s)=\prod_{\rho\in Z}(s-\rho)^{m_\rho}\), its degree \(d\),
the primitive \(\Phi'=h\), \(\Phi(0)=0\), and the ordered roots
and monomial columns of FPB. Put \(D=d+1\). For each root retain
\(N_\rho=m_\rho+1\), \(c_\rho=\Phi(\rho)\), the specified
phase-root branch \(b_\rho\), the coordinate \(w=yA_\rho(y)\),
and its inverse \(y=\psi_\rho(w)\). Thus
\[
 \Phi(\rho+\psi_\rho(w))=c_\rho+w^{N_\rho}/N_\rho,
 \qquad h(\rho+\psi_\rho(w))\psi_\rho'(w)=w^{m_\rho}.
 \tag{SPG.1}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-phase}
\]
The matrices \(C,B\) are the original polynomial-division matrices,
so \(G=-C/u^2+B/u\). The formal comparison of FPB is
\(T\in\operatorname{GL}_d(\mathbb{C}[[u]])\), with
\(T'+D_{\rm loc}T=TG\) and its explicitly retained constant
determinant \(\det T=\det T_0\ne0\).
Here \(D_{\rm loc}\) is the diagonal matrix with entry
\(-c_\rho/u^2+\beta_{\rho,a}/u\), where
\(\beta_{\rho,a}=(a+1)/N_\rho\), \(0\le a<m_\rho\).

\subsection{A specified algebraic Borel germ for every original column}

Fix a row \((\rho,a)\), abbreviate \(N=N_\rho\), \(m=m_\rho\),
\(\beta=(a+1)/N\), and keep the original column index \(0\le b<d\).
Define the holomorphic germ
\[
 H_b(w)=(\rho+\psi_\rho(w))^b\psi_\rho'(w)
       =\sum_{n\ge0}H_{b,n}w^n.
 \tag{SPG.2}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-H}
\]
The phase coordinate is holomorphic on a neighbourhood of zero: the
chosen binomial root of the nonzero polynomial germ is holomorphic on
a sufficiently small disc, and the holomorphic inverse theorem applies
because its derivative at zero is \(b_\rho\ne0\). FPB's exact
remainder gives, with \((\beta)_q=\prod_{j=0}^{q-1}(\beta+j)\),
\[
 (T_q)_{(\rho,a),b}=(-N)^q(\beta)_q H_{b,a+qN}.
 \tag{SPG.3}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-coefficients}
\]
Consequently the following transform is an actual convergent algebraic
germ, whose coefficients have all been specified:
\[
 \mathcal B_{\rho,a;b}(\xi)
  :=\sum_{q\ge0}\frac{(T_q)_{(\rho,a),b}}{(\beta)_q}\xi^q
   =\sum_{q\ge0}H_{b,a+qN}(-N\xi)^q.
 \tag{SPG.4}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-Borel}
\]
Indeed, for \(\zeta=e^{2\pi i/N}\), \(z^N=-N\xi\), it is
the exact root-of-unity filter
\[
 \mathcal B_{\rho,a;b}(\xi)
  =\frac{z^{-a}}N\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\zeta^{-aj}H_b(\zeta^jz).
 \tag{SPG.5}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-filter}
\]
Expand the right side in powers of \(z\); the sum is one on
exponents congruent to \(a\) modulo \(N\), and zero on the other
exponents. It therefore is single valued and holomorphic at \(\xi=0\),
regardless of the temporary choice of the root \(z\).
All its expressions are algebraic because \(\rho+\psi_\rho(w)\)
satisfies the original polynomial equation in (SPG.1), and
\(\psi_\rho'=w^m/h(\rho+\psi_\rho)\).

For a disc on which \(|H_b(w)|\le M\) on \(|w|=r\), the Cauchy
coefficient estimate and \(0<\beta<1\) give
\[
 |(T_q)_{(\rho,a),b}|
   \le M r^{-a}(N/r^N)^q(\beta)_q
   \le M r^{-a}(N/r^N)^q q!.
 \tag{SPG.6}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-Gevrey}
\]
This is a bound for the original formal coefficients, with their
factor \((-N)^q(\beta)_q\) retained in the defining formula.

Write the row of germs \(\mathcal B_{\rho,a;b}\) as
\(\mathcal B_{\rho,a}\). Set \(A_\rho^{\rm c}=C-c_\rho I_d\),
an abbreviation for this specified matrix, not a new arithmetic
operator. Comparing coefficients of the original equation
\(T'+D_{\rm loc}T=TG\) gives
\[
 T_{0,\rho a}A_\rho^{\rm c}=0,\qquad
 T_{q,\rho a}A_\rho^{\rm c}
  +T_{q-1,\rho a}\bigl((q-1+\beta)I_d-B\bigr)=0\quad(q\ge1).
 \tag{SPG.7}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-row-recurrence}
\]
Here each \(T_{q,\rho a}\) is a row with all original columns.
Divide by \((\beta)_{q-1}\), sum the convergent germs, and
differentiate term by term. The result is the exact identity
\[
 \frac{d}{d\xi}\left\{
   \xi^{\beta-1}\mathcal B_{\rho,a}(\xi)
                     (A_\rho^{\rm c}+\xi I_d)\right\}
       =\xi^{\beta-1}\mathcal B_{\rho,a}(\xi)B.
 \tag{SPG.8}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-Borel-ODE}
\]
For a direct coefficient check, the left coefficient of
\(\xi^{q+\beta-2}\) for \(q\ge1\) is
\((q+\beta-1)\{\mathcal B_q A_\rho^{\rm c}
                   +\mathcal B_{q-1}\}\), and the corresponding
right coefficient is \(\mathcal B_{q-1}B\).
Equation (SPG.7) proves equality. The lowest coefficient vanishes
because \(\mathcal B_0 A_\rho^{\rm c}=0\). A branch of
\(\xi^{\beta-1}\) is used consistently on both sides.

\subsection{Continuation along the original critical-value rays}

Retain the finite set of actual differences
\[
 \mathcal A=\{c_\rho-c_\sigma:\rho,\sigma\in Z\}.
 \tag{SPG.9}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-actions}
\]
An admissible direction \(\theta\in\mathbb{R}\) means that no
nonzero member of \(\mathcal A\) lies on
\(L_\theta=\{t e^{i\theta}:t>0\}\). Zero differences remain in
the data and do not have an argument. No claim that distinct roots
have distinct critical values is required here.

For \(\xi=t e^{i\theta}\), use the retained lift of the argument
and set
\[
 z(\xi)=\exp\left(\frac{\log N+\log\xi+i\pi}{N}\right),
 \qquad s_{\rho,j}(\xi)=\rho+\psi_\rho(\zeta^jz(\xi)),
 \quad 0\le j<N,
 \tag{SPG.10}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-lifts}
\]
initially for small positive \(t\). Continue these branches by the
same original polynomial equation
\[
 \Phi(s_{\rho,j}(\xi))=c_\rho-\xi.
 \tag{SPG.11}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-inverse-polynomial}
\]
They continue uniquely along the entire open ray. To prove this, a
multiple root of (SPG.11) would satisfy \(h(s)=0\), hence
\(s=\sigma\in Z\) and \(\xi=c_\rho-c_\sigma\). Such a point
does not occur on an admissible open ray. The implicit function
theorem therefore continues each simple root locally. No root can
escape to infinity above a compact part of the ray, because the
usual elementary polynomial root bound follows from the triangle
inequality for its fixed leading coefficient and bounded remaining
coefficients. Cover a compact segment by finitely many such local
neighbourhoods. This proves the stated continuation. It also proves
continuation on a sufficiently thin open wedge about the ray, using
the original germ near zero. Equation (SPG.5) then continues
\(\mathcal B\) on that same wedge, and (SPG.8) remains true by
analytic continuation.

There is an explicit bound at infinity. All roots of (SPG.11) have
\(|s|\) bounded above and below by positive multiples of
\(|\xi|^{1/D}\) for sufficiently large \(|\xi|\). One way to
see both inequalities is to substitute \(s=\xi^{1/D}v\): the
equation tends to \(v^D/D=-1\), whose \(D\) roots are distinct
and nonzero. The implicit function theorem at each of those roots,
in the parameter \(\xi^{-1/D}\), supplies all \(D\) branches
for large \(|\xi|\). Since \(h(s)=s^d+O(s^{d-1})\),
\(|h(s)|\) is at least a positive multiple of \(|\xi|^{d/D}\).
Using (SPG.1) in (SPG.5) proves, for each original column,
\[
 |\mathcal B_{\rho,a;b}(\xi)|
       \le C_{\rho,a,b,\theta}
                   |\xi|^{(b+1)/D-\beta}
       \quad (|\xi|\hbox{ sufficiently large on the wedge}).
 \tag{SPG.12}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-infinity}
\]
In detail the factor \(z^{-a}\psi'(\zeta^jz)\) has order
\(|\xi|^{(m-a)/N-d/D}\), and \(s^b\) contributes
\(|\xi|^{b/D}\). Since \((m-a)/N=1-\beta\), their sum is
the exponent in (SPG.12). The bound is uniform on smaller closed
wedges. This retains the derivative factor responsible for the
decay exponent; it is not a bound for an unweighted inverse root.

\subsection{The exact sectorial gauge, not only its asymptotic series}

On the logarithmic sector
\(\mathfrak S_\theta=\{u\ne0:|\arg u-\theta|<\pi/2\}\),
with these arguments as real lifts, define the complete row
\[
 T_{\theta,\rho a}(u)
  =\frac{u^{-\beta}}{\Gamma(\beta)}
       \int_{L_\theta} e^{-\xi/u}\xi^{\beta-1}
                         \mathcal B_{\rho,a}(\xi)\,d\xi.
 \tag{SPG.13}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-Laplace}
\]
The branch \(\log\xi=\log t+i\theta\) and the branch of
\(u^{-\beta}\) from \(\mathfrak S_\theta\) are part of the
definition. Near zero, the integrand is bounded by a multiple of
\(|\xi|^{\beta-1}\), which is integrable because \(\beta>0\).
At infinity, (SPG.12) bounds the nonexponential part by a power of
\(|\xi|\), while
\(\operatorname{Re}(e^{i\theta}/u)>0\). The integral and its
\(u\)-derivatives converge uniformly on compact subsets of the
sector. Thus (SPG.13) is holomorphic there.

It has precisely the original asymptotic expansion
\[
 T_{\theta}(u)\sim\sum_{q\ge0}u^qT_q=T(u)
    \quad\hbox{on every smaller closed subsector of }
             \mathfrak S_\theta.
 \tag{SPG.14}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-asymptotic}
\]
Here is a direct proof of the required all-orders statement. Subtract
the Taylor polynomial of \(\mathcal B\) through order \(q-1\)
on a disc of radius \(R\) inside its holomorphic germ. Its remainder
is bounded by \(C_q|\xi|^q\) on a smaller disc. On a closed
subsector the exponential satisfies
\(|e^{-\xi/u}|\le e^{-c|\xi|/|u|}\) for a fixed \(c>0\).
The integral of the small-disc remainder, multiplied by
\(|u|^{-\beta}\), is therefore \(O(|u|^q)\). On the ray
outside that disc the algebraic bound, and the finitely many
polynomial terms subtracted, make the remaining integral
\(O(|u|^{-M_q}e^{-cR/(2|u|)})\), which is \(O(|u|^q)\)
as well. Integrating a Taylor monomial gives exactly
\[
 \frac{u^{-\beta}}{\Gamma(\beta)}
     \int_{L_\theta}e^{-\xi/u}\xi^{q+\beta-1}\,d\xi
           =(\beta)_q u^q.
\]
For \(u\) on the central ray this is the positive real gamma
integral after \(\xi=ut\); elsewhere it follows by holomorphy
on the same connected sector. This proves every coefficient and
the estimate (SPG.14).

The integral satisfies the exact original gauge equation
\[
 T_\theta'+D_{\rm loc}T_\theta=T_\theta G.
 \tag{SPG.15}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-exact-gauge}
\]
To prove this rather than deduce it from an asymptotic expansion,
put \(F(\xi)=\xi^{\beta-1}\mathcal B_{\rho,a}(\xi)
(A_\rho^{\rm c}+\xi I_d)\). At zero, (SPG.7) gives
\(F(\xi)=O(|\xi|^\beta)\), so its endpoint is zero. At infinity
it has algebraic growth by (SPG.12), so
\(e^{-\xi/u}F(\xi)\) tends to zero. Integrate (SPG.8) by
parts on a truncated ray and take its two endpoints to their limits.
The result is
\[
 u\,\mathcal L_\beta(\mathcal B_{\rho,a})B
       =\mathcal L_\beta\bigl(
                  \mathcal B_{\rho,a}(A_\rho^{\rm c}+\xi I_d)
                              \bigr),
 \tag{SPG.16}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-integration-parts}
\]
where \(\mathcal L_\beta\) denotes the integral in (SPG.13).
Differentiating that integral directly also gives
\(u^2T_{\theta,\rho a}'+\beta uT_{\theta,\rho a}
       =\mathcal L_\beta(\xi\mathcal B_{\rho,a})\).
Substitution in (SPG.16) is exactly the row of (SPG.15).

The determinant is the original constant:
\[
 \det T_\theta(u)=\det T_0\ne0
                 \quad(u\in\mathfrak S_\theta).
 \tag{SPG.17}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-determinant}
\]
The multilinear derivative of a determinant and (SPG.15) give
\((\det T_\theta)'=(\operatorname{Tr}G-
\operatorname{Tr}D_{\rm loc})\det T_\theta=0\), without assuming
invertibility in deriving this equality. Along the central ray,
(SPG.14) gives its limiting value \(\det T_0\). Since the sector
is connected, the determinant has that value throughout. In
particular the constructed analytic gauge is invertible everywhere
in its stated sector.

\subsection{Evaluation as actual lifted contours, with all gamma factors}

Retain exactly the homogeneous constants of FPB:
\[
 d_{\rho,a}=N_\rho^{\beta_{\rho,a}-1}
           e^{\pi i\beta_{\rho,a}}\Gamma(\beta_{\rho,a}),
 \qquad (W_\rho)_{la}=\zeta_\rho^{l(a+1)}-1.
 \tag{SPG.18}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-gamma}
\]
Set \(W_{\rm loc}=\bigoplus_\rho W_\rho\) and define
\[
 P_\theta(u)=W_{\rm loc}
    \operatorname{diag}_{\rho,a}
       \bigl(e^{c_\rho/u}d_{\rho,a}u^{\beta_{\rho,a}}\bigr)
                      T_\theta(u).
 \tag{SPG.19}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-period-matrix}
\]
It is an actual invertible analytic matrix, and (SPG.15) proves
\(P_\theta'=P_\theta G\).

Let \(\gamma_{\rho,j,\theta}\) be the oriented path
\(s_{\rho,j}(t e^{i\theta})\), \(0<t<\infty\), with its
initial endpoint \(\rho\). All its periods converge. Near the
endpoint, \(s-\rho=O(t^{1/N})\) and
\(ds/dt=O(t^{1/N-1})\), which is integrable. At infinity,
\(\Phi(s)=c_\rho-t e^{i\theta}\) gives exponential decay for
\(u\in\mathfrak S_\theta\), and (SPG.11) bounds all remaining
polynomial and derivative factors.

Before multiplication by \(W_{\rm loc}\), row \((\rho,a)\)
of (SPG.19) is exactly
\[
 \frac1N\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\zeta^{-j(a+1)}
       \int_{\gamma_{\rho,j,\theta}}e^{\Phi(s)/u}s^b\,ds
                 \quad\hbox{in column }b.
 \tag{SPG.20}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-Fourier-contour}
\]
The factors in this identity can be checked without an unspecified
phase convention. Insert (SPG.5) into (SPG.13), multiply by
\(e^{c_\rho/u}d_{\rho,a}u^\beta\), and substitute
\(\xi=-z^N/N\) on its specified branch. Then
\[
 N^{\beta-1}e^{\pi i\beta}
   \xi^{\beta-1}\frac{z^{-a}}N\,d\xi
          =\frac1N\,dz.
\]
Indeed \(\log\xi=N\log z-\log N-i\pi\) and
\(d\xi=-z^{N-1}dz\); the two minus signs cancel and the power
of \(z\) is zero because \(N\beta=a+1\).
Finally \(ds=\zeta^j\psi_\rho'(\zeta^jz)dz\), giving the
extra \(\zeta^{-j}\) and hence the coefficient in (SPG.20).
The exponential becomes exactly \(e^{\Phi(s)/u}\). This proves
the identity with the original Jacobian, gamma constants, primitive
constant, and branch orientation.

Multiplying the Fourier rows by the original \(W_\rho\) gives
the especially concrete exact formula
\[
 (P_\theta)_{(\rho,l),b}
    =\int_{\gamma_{\rho,l,\theta}-\gamma_{\rho,0,\theta}}
             e^{\Phi(s)/u}s^b\,ds,
       \qquad 1\le l\le m_\rho.
 \tag{SPG.21}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-actual-local-cycles}
\]
For verification, the coefficient of \(\gamma_{\rho,j,\theta}\)
after this multiplication is
\(N^{-1}\sum_{a=0}^{m-1}
(\zeta^{l(a+1)}-1)\zeta^{-j(a+1)}
=\delta_{jl}-\delta_{j0}\).
Thus the formal local ray labels have become actual global
rapid-decay paths through a proved analytic integral, rather than
through an assumed convergence of \(T(u)\).

\subsection{The exact integer comparison to the original global rays}

Let \(\Pi_h\) be the original ordered global ray matrix of GRM,
whose rows are \(\Gamma_n=-\ell_0+\ell_n\), \(1\le n\le d\),
and whose ray arguments are
\((\arg u+\pi+2\pi n)/D\). Each lifted path has a unique
label \(n_{\rho,j}(\theta)\in\{0,\ldots,d\}\) determined by
\[
 \frac{s_{\rho,j}(t e^{i\theta})}{(Dt)^{1/D}}
    \longrightarrow
        \exp\left(\frac{i(\theta+\pi+2\pi n_{\rho,j}(\theta))}{D}\right)
                    \quad(t\longrightarrow\infty).
 \tag{SPG.22}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-end-label}
\]
The large-parameter implicit-function argument used for (SPG.12)
proves existence and uniqueness of this limit, with precisely these
labels on the retained lift of \(\theta\).

Define the actual integer matrix, with the original row orders,
\[
 (E_\theta)_{(\rho,l),n}
    =\delta_{n_{\rho,l}(\theta),n}
                    -\delta_{n_{\rho,0}(\theta),n},
 \qquad 1\le n\le d.
 \tag{SPG.23}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-incidence}
\]
Its entries are determined by continuation of the original polynomial
inverse along the explicitly given rays; there is no free choice of
an incidence pattern. The exact contour comparison is
\[
                    P_\theta=E_\theta\Pi_h.
 \tag{SPG.24}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-global-comparison}
\]
To prove it, attach the same finite path from \(0\) to \(\rho\)
to both paths in (SPG.21); its integral cancels. A path from \(0\)
to infinity whose endpoint is in the labelled decaying sector has
the same integral as the corresponding original ray. For a complete
justification, truncate the paths at large radius and join their
endpoints inside that decaying sector. Cauchy's theorem applies
because the integrand is entire in \(s\). Along the joining arc
the leading term of the real phase is at most a negative constant
times the radius to the power \(D\), while the lower terms are
\(O(R^{D-1})\), uniformly on any closed subsector in the stated
\(u\)-domain. Its polynomial factor and the arc length cannot
overcome this decay. The joining integral tends to zero. For the
initial comparison one may use the ray at argument
\((\theta+\pi+2\pi n)/D\); it and the original ray defined by
\(\arg u\) lie in the same strictly decaying sector because
\(|\arg u-\theta|<\pi/2\). The same arc argument relates them.
Taking the difference of the two endpoint labels gives precisely
(SPG.23)--(SPG.24).

This matrix is unimodular:
\[
                    E_\theta\in\operatorname{GL}_d(\mathbb Z).
 \tag{SPG.25}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-unimodular}
\]
Indeed the nonzero determinants of \(P_\theta\) and \(\Pi_h\)
prove that \(E_\theta\) has full rank. Regard each row as an
oriented edge joining its two endpoint labels among \(d+1\)
vertices, with column zero deleted. A loop or a disconnected
component would give a zero row or a nonzero vector in the kernel:
in the latter case take the indicator of a component not containing
vertex zero. Thus the graph is connected. It has exactly \(d\)
edges on \(d+1\) vertices, so it is a tree. One proof is to grow
a connected set from a vertex, needing an edge for each added
vertex; a cycle would allow removal of an edge while preserving
connectedness, contradicting that minimum count. A leaf other than
vertex zero has exactly one nonzero entry, equal to \(1\) or
\(-1\), in its column. Expanding the determinant in that column
and removing the leaf proves \(\det E_\theta=\pm1\) by induction.
This also proves that all the actual lifted cycles in (SPG.21) form
a full integral basis in the original global ray lattice.

All constants in this comparison can be checked through determinants:
\[
 \det E_\theta
   =\frac{\det T_0\,
         \prod_\rho\bigl(\det W_\rho
                         \prod_{a=0}^{m_\rho-1}d_{\rho,a}\bigr)}{K_d},
 \tag{SPG.26}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-det-constants}
\]
where \(K_d\) is the unchanged global gamma and contour constant
in GRM.5. The factors \(u^{d/2}\) and
\(\exp(\sum_\rho m_\rho c_\rho/u)\) cancel between the two
actual determinants, because both primitives and both bases have
already been fixed. Formula (SPG.25) proves that the displayed
retained expression is the actual orientation sign; it is not a
normalization imposed on the period matrices.

\subsection{Actual transitions and their exact critical-value support}

Within an interval of admissible directions containing no argument
of a nonzero action, the algebraic branches in (SPG.10) continue
continuously with the direction. Their distinct endpoint labels cannot
change there. Hence \(E_\theta\) is constant in that interval.
Equivalently one can rotate the Laplace contour inside the action-free
wedge; its large arc vanishes by the same exponential estimate and
its small arc vanishes by \(\beta>0\). The two analytic gauges
then agree on their common domain.

For two sufficiently close directions on opposite sides of an action
direction \(\theta_0\), let the superscripts \(+\) and \(-\)
denote increasing and decreasing arguments on the same logarithmic
cover. Their actual transition matrix is
\[
 \mathfrak S_{\theta_0}=E_{\theta_0+}E_{\theta_0-}^{-1},
 \qquad P_{\theta_0+}=\mathfrak S_{\theta_0}P_{\theta_0-},
 \qquad \mathfrak S_{\theta_0}\in\operatorname{GL}_d(\mathbb Z).
 \tag{SPG.27}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-Stokes}
\]
Here the matrices are the actual endpoint-incidence matrices, and
(SPG.24) proves the equality. No matrix is inferred merely from a
characteristic polynomial. The full data needed to evaluate every
entry are the original inverse-polynomial paths and their endpoint
labels in (SPG.10)--(SPG.23).

There is exact control on which original critical blocks can mix:
\[
 \begin{cases}
  (\mathfrak S_{\theta_0})_{\rho\sigma}=0,
       &\rho\ne\sigma,\quad
             c_\rho-c_\sigma\notin e^{i\theta_0}\mathbb{R}_{>0},\\
  (\mathfrak S_{\theta_0})_{\rho\sigma}=\delta_{\rho\sigma}I,
       &c_\rho=c_\sigma.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{SPG.28}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-critical-support}
\]
In the second line the rectangular off-diagonal block is zero and
the square diagonal block is the identity of its original size.
To prove the assertion without assuming a Stokes theorem, conjugate
by the fixed block matrix \(W_{\rm loc}\), and write
\(\mathfrak S^\chi=W_{\rm loc}^{-1}\mathfrak S W_{\rm loc}\).
If \(\mathcal D_0(u)=\operatorname{diag}_{\rho,a}
(e^{c_\rho/u}d_{\rho,a}u^{\beta_{\rho,a}})\), then
\[
 \mathfrak S^\chi-I
   =\mathcal D_0(u)
       \bigl(T_{\theta_0+}T_{\theta_0-}^{-1}-I\bigr)
            \mathcal D_0(u)^{-1}.
 \tag{SPG.29}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-flat-transition}
\]
The middle matrix is smaller than every fixed power of \(|u|\)
on every smaller closed subsector of the overlap, because both
gauges have the same full asymptotic expansion and their inverses
tend to \(T_0^{-1}\). If its corresponding constant transition
entry were nonzero, (SPG.29) would therefore require
\(u^{\beta_\sigma-\beta_\rho}
 e^{(c_\sigma-c_\rho)/u}\) to be smaller than every power on
the whole overlap. Equal constants make that impossible. For unequal
constants it requires
\(\operatorname{Re}((c_\sigma-c_\rho)e^{-i\varphi})<0\)
throughout the open interval of overlap arguments \(\varphi\).
The matrices \(E_{\theta_0\pm}\) do not depend on how close the
two directions are taken within their adjacent intervals. Letting
them approach \(\theta_0\), that interval exhausts
\((\theta_0-\pi/2,\theta_0+\pi/2)\). The only nonzero complex
number with the required strict negative real part on this whole
interval is a negative real multiple of \(e^{i\theta_0}\).
Thus \(c_\rho-c_\sigma\in e^{i\theta_0}\mathbb{R}_{>0}\).
This proves the entrywise assertion in the character basis and hence
the stated block assertion, since \(W_{\rm loc}\) acts within
each original root block. It follows in particular that the transition
is unipotent after ordering the critical-value blocks along the
specified action direction. Equal-value blocks remain intact.

\subsection{The exact original source and ordered tensors}

Keep the complete original unit matrix \(U_h=M_{[g/h]}\) in the
original monomial basis. On the actual theta-jet coordinate
\(q=U_hp\), the analytic comparison is
\[
 P_\theta^\theta=P_\theta U_h^{-1}
       =E_\theta\Pi_h U_h^{-1},
 \qquad
 P_{\theta_0+}^\theta
       =\mathfrak S_{\theta_0}P_{\theta_0-}^\theta.
 \tag{SPG.30}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-theta}
\]
The two uses of the letter \(\theta\) in \(P_\theta^\theta\)
mean respectively the ray direction (subscript) and the inherited
theta source (superscript). The matrices \(U_h\) and \(U_h^{-1}\)
retain all Taylor coefficients and their original nonzero constants.
Every equality follows by multiplying the proved analytic equality
on the right by this same original inverse unit. The arithmetic
operator on the sectorial period coordinates is exactly
\(P_\theta A_h P_\theta^{-1}\), or
\(P_\theta^\theta A_h(P_\theta^\theta)^{-1}\) on theta jets;
the two are equal because \(A_h\) commutes with its full unit.
No intertwining with Frobenius is inserted here.

For the original source Gram \(G_\theta\) of FPB.47, the exact
sectorial period form is
\[
 P_\theta^*P_\theta
      =\Pi_h^* E_\theta^*E_\theta\Pi_h,
 \qquad
 (G_\theta)^{-1}P_\theta^*P_\theta
      =(G_\theta)^{-1}\Pi_h^*E_\theta^*E_\theta\Pi_h.
 \tag{SPG.31}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-metric}
\]
This is equality in the unchanged original coefficient coordinates.
On theta-jet coordinates the source form is
\(U_h^{-*}G_\theta U_h^{-1}\), and the period form is
\(U_h^{-*}\Pi_h^*E_\theta^*E_\theta\Pi_hU_h^{-1}\).
These follow by substituting \(p=U_h^{-1}q\) in the quadratic
forms. In particular the generally nonidentity integer Gram
\(E_\theta^*E_\theta\) has not been omitted from the comparison.

All the constructions tensor in the original factor order and common
parameter \(u\):
\[
 T_{\theta,k}=T_\theta^{\otimes k},\quad
 P_{\theta,k}=P_\theta^{\otimes k},\quad
 E_{\theta,k}=E_\theta^{\otimes k},\quad
 P_{\theta,k}=E_{\theta,k}\Pi_h^{\otimes k},\quad
 \mathfrak S_{\theta_0,k}=\mathfrak S_{\theta_0}^{\otimes k}.
 \tag{SPG.32}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-tensor}
\]
The connection is the original tensor sum because differentiating
the Kronecker product differentiates one retained factor at a time.
The form is \(ds_1\wedge\cdots\wedge ds_k\), with no
permutation and hence no extra sign. In the local block every
ordered tuple has exponential constant \(\sum_i c_{\rho_i}\)
and fractional exponent \(\sum_i\beta_{\rho_i,a_i}\). The
complete theta factor is \(U_h^{\otimes k}\), and the original
source Gram is \(G_\theta^{\otimes k}\). Every tuple remains,
including all tuples sharing a critical sum. Formula (SPG.32) is an
exact amplified analytic comparison, not a replacement by the
single-primary polynomial or by formal character counts.

\subsection{The signed path of every actual transition entry}

For the two actual adjacent trees of (SPG.23), keep the vertex labels
\(0,\ldots,d\), the original row orders, and all edge orientations.
The row indexed by a new edge \(f\) of \(E_+\) is the difference
of the vertex at its original head \(h_+(f)\) and its original tail
\(t_+(f)\). The subscript \(+\) here denotes that tree; it does
not change the given orientation. Root the old tree \(E_-\) at
vertex zero. For an old edge \(e\), let \(p_-(e),c_-(e)\) be
the parent and child vertices, and let \(\epsilon_e\) equal \(1\)
if its original orientation points from parent to child and \(-1\)
otherwise. Let \(\mathcal P_-(v)\) be the unique old-tree path
from zero to \(v\), with \(\mathcal P_-(0)=\varnothing\).
Then every entry of the original transition in (SPG.27) is exactly
\[
 (\mathfrak S_{\theta_0})_{f,e}
   =\epsilon_e\left(
       \mathbf1_{e\in\mathcal P_-(h_+(f))}
        -\mathbf1_{e\in\mathcal P_-(t_+(f))}\right).
 \tag{SPG.33}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-signed-path}
\]
Indeed the old inverse incidence matrix is
\((E_-^{-1})_{v,e}=\epsilon_e\mathbf1_{e\in\mathcal P_-(v)}\):
the sum of original edge differences along a root path telescopes
to its endpoint value, with value zero fixed at the root. Subtract
the two endpoint rows of that matrix to obtain (SPG.33). The
intersection of the two root paths is their common initial part;
those terms cancel exactly as integers. Their symmetric difference
is the unique path from \(t_+(f)\) to \(h_+(f)\). Its coefficient
is \(1\) when traversing an old edge in its original orientation
and \(-1\) when traversing it against that orientation. Thus
\[
 (\mathfrak S_{\theta_0})_{f,e}\in\{-1,0,1\},\qquad
 \sum_e |(\mathfrak S_{\theta_0})_{f,e}|^2
   =\operatorname{dist}_{\mathcal T_-}(t_+(f),h_+(f)).
 \tag{SPG.34}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-path-count}
\]
All distances and signs concern the actual endpoint trees of the
original polynomial. Combining (SPG.33) with (SPG.28) proves that
an old edge can occur on this path only in its permitted
critical-value block, except for the identity entry. In particular
no unspecified integer coefficient remains in the transition formula.

Let
\(L_{+-}=\sum_f\operatorname{dist}_{\mathcal T_-}
(t_+(f),h_+(f))\), and define \(L_{-+}\) by exchanging the two
trees. The exact Frobenius norm, the operator bounds, and the two
directions of the original period comparison are
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \|\mathfrak S_{\theta_0}\|_{\rm F}^2=L_{+-},\qquad
 \|\mathfrak S_{\theta_0}^{-1}\|_{\rm F}^2=L_{-+},\qquad
 \|\mathfrak S_{\theta_0}\|_2\le\sqrt{L_{+-}}\le d,
 \quad \|\mathfrak S_{\theta_0}^{-1}\|_2\le\sqrt{L_{-+}}\le d,\\
 L_{-+}^{-1}\|P_-p\|_2^2
     \le\|P_+p\|_2^2\le L_{+-}\|P_-p\|_2^2.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{SPG.35}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-path-norms}
\]
The operator norm of a matrix is bounded by its Frobenius norm,
as follows by applying Cauchy--Schwarz to each row and summing.
Every path has at most \(d\) edges and there are \(d\) rows,
giving the displayed upper bounds. Apply the same estimate to the
inverse and use \(P_+=\mathfrak S P_-\) to prove both period
inequalities. These bounds retain the actual Euclidean period norms.

\subsection{The mixed split-support comparison and its complete fibres}

Apply the oriented-tree calculation TSI.1--12 to this actual
\(E_\theta\) and its original root zero. For precision, its
coefficient semiring is the original
\(\mathbb G=G(\mathbb Z)=\{\tau\}\sqcup\mathbb Z^\bullet\).
Here \(\tau\) is the absent-support zero, \(0^\bullet\) is the
supported zero, and supported addition and multiplication retain
the integer operations. The amplitude together with the support
is injective. No finite residue field replaces this integer target.
Lift each nonzero entry of \(E=E_\theta\) and \(F=E^{-1}\)
to its supported integer, and lift a zero entry to \(\tau\),
obtaining the exact semiring matrices \(\widehat E,\widehat F\).
Their products are
\[
 \mathsf P=\widehat E\widehat F,\qquad
 \mathsf Q=\widehat F\widehat E,
 \tag{SPG.36}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-split-products}
\]
with \(1^\bullet\) on the diagonal, \(0^\bullet\) at strict
ancestor positions, and \(\tau\) elsewhere. On the edge module
\(\mathsf P\) uses edge ancestry; on the nonroot vertex module
\(\mathsf Q\) uses vertex ancestry. The proof is exact cancellation
of two supported opposite integers at an ancestor, one supported
unit on the diagonal, and no contribution off those positions.
The absence of a contribution and the cancellation of contributions
are different elements of \(\mathbb G\).

Both products preserve every amplitude and close a support mask under
descendants. Write this action generically as \(H\), on its stated
edge or nonroot vertex module, and write
\(\operatorname{Desc}(A)\) for the descendants of a mask \(A\),
including its own elements. Then
\[
 \operatorname{amp}(Hx)=\operatorname{amp}(x),\qquad
 \operatorname{supp}(Hx)=\operatorname{Desc}(\operatorname{supp}x),
 \qquad H^2=H.
 \tag{SPG.37}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-mask-closure}
\]
The entire fibre of this map, rather than only its image, can be
constructed. Let \(z\) have descendant-closed mask \(B\), and
let \(a_i=\operatorname{amp}(z_i)\), using \(a_i=0\) outside
\(B\). Denote by \(\operatorname{Min}(B)\) the vertices or edges
in \(B\) with no strict ancestor in \(B\), and put
\(\operatorname{NZ}(a)=\{i:a_i\ne0\}\). The exact parametrization
is
\[
 \begin{split}
 H^{-1}(z)&=\{x_A:
       \operatorname{Min}(B)\cup\operatorname{NZ}(a)
                                  \subseteq A\subseteq B\},\\
 (x_A)_i&=
   \begin{cases}a_i^\bullet,&i\in A,\\ \tau,&i\notin A,\end{cases}
 \qquad
 |H^{-1}(z)|=2^{\,|B\setminus
       (\operatorname{Min}(B)\cup\operatorname{NZ}(a))|}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{SPG.38}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-full-fibre}
\]
For necessity, (SPG.37) requires \(A\subseteq B\), and every
minimal element of \(B\) must itself be in \(A\): no other
element of \(B\) can generate it by descendant closure.
Every nonzero amplitude must also have been supported at the input.
For sufficiency, a finite descendant-closed set is precisely the
descendant closure of its minimal elements. Thus the displayed
inclusions give \(\operatorname{Desc}(A)=B\), and the definition
of \(x_A\) gives all the required amplitudes. The injective
amplitude/support observation proves \(Hx_A=z\) and proves that
different masks give different fibre elements. This proves the
parametrization and the count. It includes \(B=\varnothing\),
whose fibre is the single all-\(\tau\) vector.
For a target whose mask is not descendant closed, (SPG.37) proves
that its fibre is empty. Thus both image and nonimage targets have
their complete fibres specified.

There are exact mutually inverse semimodule maps
\[
 \widehat E:\operatorname{im}\mathsf Q
                     \xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{im}\mathsf P,
 \qquad
 \widehat F:\operatorname{im}\mathsf P
                     \xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{im}\mathsf Q.
 \tag{SPG.39}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-retract-isomorphism}
\]
For example, \(x=\mathsf Qx\) implies
\(\mathsf P\widehat Ex=\widehat E\mathsf Qx=\widehat Ex\)
and \(\widehat F\widehat Ex=\mathsf Qx=x\); the other direction
is the same associativity identity. The image masks are exactly the
descendant-closed masks. Formula (SPG.38) records all the fibres of
the maps into those images, so no equality on the images discards
the distinct missing-support inputs that preceded closure.

There is a second support comparison for the actual Stokes matrix.
Retain \(\widehat E_+\widehat F_-\) as its specified two-stage
split composite, and distinguish it from the entrywise minimal lift
\(\widehat{\mathfrak S}\). For a new edge \(f\) and old edge
\(e\), the complete value of that composite is
\[
 (\widehat E_+\widehat F_-)_{f,e}=
 \begin{cases}
  (\mathfrak S_{f,e})^\bullet,
        &e\in\mathcal P_-(h_+(f))\mathbin{\triangle}
                                      \mathcal P_-(t_+(f)),\\
  0^\bullet,
        &e\in\mathcal P_-(h_+(f))\cap\mathcal P_-(t_+(f)),\\
  \tau,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{SPG.40}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-transition-supported-cancellation}
\]
The first case has exactly one nonzero product. The second has two
opposite supported products. The last has no product. This proves
each entry, retaining the common initial path which cancelled in
the integer formula (SPG.33). In particular the root-path stages
and their support masks remain explicit even where the final integer
amplitude is zero. Neither this comparison nor (SPG.39) asserts a
ring homomorphism: these are semimodule maps with the specified
split coefficients, and ordinary linear maps under the amplitude
observation.

On an original independent coefficient face, apply these maps to its
coefficient module while retaining that face's original label. On a
separate original source-leg mask, retain the identity on the label.
The scalar map \(\mathbb Z\to R\) to any original coefficient
ring gives \(G(\mathbb Z)\to G(R)\), so the same displayed
matrix actions are defined on each such coefficient module. This
map sends supported zero to supported zero and \(\tau\) to
\(\tau\); it commutes with all the displayed sums and products.
Any proper-source kernel carried as a separate labelled summand is
retained under this coefficient action. No quotient of the original
theta complex or removal of an original source leg occurs in the
support comparison.

\subsection{Every original source metric factor under the transition}

In the actual rooted tree define
\(\ell_v=|\mathcal P(v)|\),
\(s_\theta=\sum_{v=1}^d\ell_v\), and
\(t_\theta=2d-\deg(0)\). The exact matrix
\(L_\theta=E_\theta^*E_\theta\) is the original Dirichlet
tree Laplacian, including root-edge contributions in its diagonal.
Its inverse has entries
\[
 (L_\theta^{-1})_{vw}=|\mathcal P(v)\cap\mathcal P(w)|,
 \qquad
 \frac1{s_\theta}I\ \le L_\theta\le\ t_\theta I,
 \qquad
 s_\theta\le\frac{d(d+1)}2.
 \tag{SPG.41}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-exact-tree-metric}
\]
The inverse formula follows from \(F=E^{-1}\) and its signed
path entries: each edge in the intersection contributes its squared
orientation sign, hence one. The trace of this inverse is
\(s_\theta\); the trace of \(L_\theta\) is
\(t_\theta\). Both are positive definite, so their largest
eigenvalues are at most their traces. The bound on \(s_\theta\)
counts equal ancestor pairs and at most one ordered ancestor pair
for each unordered pair of distinct vertices. These arguments prove
all the inequalities with the actual tree fixed.

For every original coefficient column \(p\), with all its jets,
the source and both period norms therefore obey exactly
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \|\mathcal T_h(p)\|_{L^2(dx)}^2=p^*G_\theta p,\qquad
 \|P_\theta p\|_2^2=p^*\Pi_h^*L_\theta\Pi_h p,\\
 \frac1{s_\theta}\|\Pi_h p\|_2^2
      \le\|P_\theta p\|_2^2
      \le t_\theta\|\Pi_h p\|_2^2.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{SPG.42}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-period-source-norms}
\]
Here \(p\) denotes its unchanged degree-below-\(d\) polynomial
representative in \(\mathcal T_h(p)\); thus the first form is
precisely the original section norm, not a new quotient norm.
The middle identity is the actual period equality, and the two
bounds are substitutions into (SPG.41).

There is also an exact metric on the sectorial period coordinates
which carries the same original source norm. Put
\[
 H_\theta^{\rm src}(u)
       =E_\theta^{-*}\Pi_h(u)^{-*}G_\theta
                                   \Pi_h(u)^{-1}E_\theta^{-1}.
 \tag{SPG.43}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-source-period-metric}
\]
It is positive definite since it is the pullback of the positive
original \(G_\theta\) by an invertible map. On theta-jet
coordinates \(q=U_hp\), both the source form and the period
matrix contain the same retained \(U_h^{-1}\). Explicitly,
\[
 \begin{split}
 H_\theta^{\rm src}
   &=(P_\theta U_h^{-1})^{-*}
       (U_h^{-*}G_\theta U_h^{-1})
                           (P_\theta U_h^{-1})^{-1},\\
 (P_\theta p)^*H_\theta^{\rm src}(P_\theta p)
   &=(P_\theta U_h^{-1}q)^*H_\theta^{\rm src}
                         (P_\theta U_h^{-1}q)
     =p^*G_\theta p=q^*U_h^{-*}G_\theta U_h^{-1}q.
 \end{split}
 \tag{SPG.44}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-source-isometry}
\]
These identities result by multiplying the displayed matrices; the
inverse-unit factors cancel only through this exact coordinate
square, after being retained on both sides. Under the actual
transition,
\[
 \mathfrak S_{\theta_0}^*H_+^{\rm src}
                         \mathfrak S_{\theta_0}=H_-^{\rm src}.
 \tag{SPG.45}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-transition-source-metric}
\]
Indeed \(P_+=\mathfrak S P_-\), and substitution into
\(H_\pm^{\rm src}=P_\pm^{-*}G_\theta P_\pm^{-1}\) proves the
identity. This is an isometry between the two explicitly transported
source forms. The Euclidean norms in (SPG.35) and (SPG.42) remain
their original Euclidean norms and are not identified with these
source forms.

For the ordered \(k\)-fold product all these form identities are
Kronecker products of the displayed matrices. In particular
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 s_\theta^{-k}\|\Pi_h^{\otimes k}p\|_2^2
   \le\|P_\theta^{\otimes k}p\|_2^2
   \le t_\theta^k\|\Pi_h^{\otimes k}p\|_2^2,\\
 (\mathfrak S^{\otimes k})^*(H_+^{\rm src})^{\otimes k}
         \mathfrak S^{\otimes k}=(H_-^{\rm src})^{\otimes k},
 \qquad
 \|\mathfrak S^{\otimes k}\|_2=\|\mathfrak S\|_2^k.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{SPG.46}\label{GraphV2:eq:spg-tensor-metric}
\]
For the inequalities, diagonalize the positive matrices in
(SPG.41); each eigenvalue of a Kronecker power is a product of
the original eigenvalues, proving exactly the stated bounds.
The norm equality follows by diagonalizing \(\mathfrak S^*
\mathfrak S\) in the same way. The constants therefore have
their proved multiplicative growth under this particular tensor
operation. No uniform-in-\(k\) error bound or arithmetic purity
is substituted for that calculation.
