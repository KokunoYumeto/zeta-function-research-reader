\section{The calculated phase operator on the original full jets and theta source}
\label{GraphV2:sec:qcv-phase-source-attachment}

Use precisely \(h,\Phi_h,r_j,m,A,U,V\) from (QCV.1)--(QCV.27).
Here \(h\) is an actual full arithmetic quartet divisor of \(g=2\xi\).
Put \(E_h=\mathbb{C}[s]/(h)\), \(A_h=M_s\), and
\[
 E_{h,k}=E_h^{\otimes k},\qquad
 C_h=M_{[\Phi_h(s)]},\qquad
 C_{h,k}=\sum_{i=1}^k
 I^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes C_h\otimes I^{\otimes(k-i)}.
 \tag{QPS.1}
\]
Thus \(C_h=\Phi_h(A_h)\) is an exact polynomial of the original
arithmetic operator. No new comparison is postulated.

\subsection{The complete jet map and its inverse}

The original monomial basis of \(E_h\) is
\(1,s,\ldots,s^{4m-1}\). The full jet map has entries
\[
 \mathcal J_h:E_h\longrightarrow
       \bigoplus_{j=1}^4\mathbb{C}[y]/(y^m),\qquad
 (\mathcal J_h p)_j=p(r_j+y)\bmod y^m,\qquad
 (\mathcal J_h)_{(j,a),b}=\binom ba r_j^{b-a},
 \tag{QPS.2}
\]
where the entry is zero if \(a>b\), and \(0\leq a<m\),
\(0\leq b<4m\). This includes every Taylor coefficient with its
binomial factor.

Here is an explicit inverse, including all full-order idempotents.
Write
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 H_j(s)=h(s)/(s-r_j)^m,\quad
 v_{j,\ell}=H_j^{(\ell)}(r_j)/\ell!\quad(0\leq\ell<m),\\
 w_{j,0}=v_{j,0}^{-1},\quad
 w_{j,\ell}=-v_{j,0}^{-1}
             \sum_{a=1}^{\ell}v_{j,a}w_{j,\ell-a}.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{QPS.3}
\]
The nonzero value \(v_{j,0}=b_j\) is calculated in (QCV.9).
Define \(W_j(y)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}w_{j,\ell}y^\ell\), and let
\(r_h\) denote the unique remainder of degree less than \(4m\)
upon division by the original monic \(h\). Then
\[
 e_j=r_h\bigl(H_j(s)W_j(s-r_j)\bigr),\qquad
 \mathcal J_h^{-1}(p_1,\ldots,p_4)
     =r_h\left(\sum_{j=1}^4 e_j(s)p_j(s-r_j)\right).
 \tag{QPS.4}
\]
Each \(p_j\) in this formula is its unique representative of degree
less than \(m\). Using any other lift gives the same class: the
difference is a multiple of \(y^m\), and multiplication by \(e_j\)
has zero full jets both at \(r_j\) and at all other roots.
The recurrence (QPS.3) says
\(H_j(r_j+y)W_j(y)\equiv1\bmod y^m\). At another root \(r_\ell\)
the polynomial \(H_j\) is divisible by \((s-r_\ell)^m\).
Consequently \(\mathcal J_h(e_j)\) has value \(1\) at the \(j\)-th
full block and \(0\) at the others. This proves that (QPS.4) is
a right inverse. If all full jets of a polynomial \(p\) vanish,
each \((s-r_j)^m\) divides \(p\); distinct factors are coprime,
so their product \(h\) divides \(p\). This proves injectivity and
hence the inverse assertion. In particular
\[
 e_je_\ell=\mathbf1_{j=\ell}e_j,\qquad \sum_{j=1}^4e_j=1
 \quad\hbox{in }E_h.
 \tag{QPS.5}
\]

\subsection{The phase operator is invertible on every original tensor block}

Equation (QCV.9), reduced modulo \(y^m\), gives the exact operator
identities
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal J_h C_h\mathcal J_h^{-1}
    &=\bigoplus_{j=1}^4\Phi_h(r_j)I_m,\qquad
 C_h=\sum_{j=1}^4\Phi_h(r_j)M_{e_j},\\
 \mathcal J_h A_h\mathcal J_h^{-1}
    &=\bigoplus_{j=1}^4(r_jI_m+M_y).
 \end{split}
 \tag{QPS.6}
\]
The disappearance of higher powers in \(\Phi_h(r_j+y)\bmod y^m\)
changes neither the local vector space nor its vectors: the phase
acts as its nonzero scalar on all \(m\) jets. In particular the full
nilpotent action \(M_y\) in the second line remains in the same
object.

Put \(\mathcal J_{h,k}=\mathcal J_h^{\otimes k}\) in the unchanged
tensor order, and \(e_{\mathbf j}=\bigotimes_i e_{j_i}\).
For \(\mathbf j=(j_1,\ldots,j_k)\), set
\(c_{\mathbf j}=\sum_i\Phi_h(r_{j_i})\), calculated in
(QCV.21)--(QCV.23). By (QCV.27) every \(c_{\mathbf j}\ne0\).
It follows, with an explicit inverse, that
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 C_{h,k}=\sum_{\mathbf j}c_{\mathbf j}M_{e_{\mathbf j}},\qquad
 C_{h,k}^{-1}
    =M_{p_{h,k}},\qquad
 p_{h,k}=\sum_{\mathbf j}c_{\mathbf j}^{-1}e_{\mathbf j},\\
 C_{h,k}C_{h,k}^{-1}
    =C_{h,k}^{-1}C_{h,k}=I_{E_{h,k}}.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{QPS.7}
\]
All four-to-the-\(k\) full idempotents are retained, including those
sharing a critical value. The last equalities follow by multiplying
the orthogonal idempotents in (QPS.5). Multiplication by a unit is
a module automorphism here; it is not being called a unital ring
automorphism.

For clarity the arithmetic scalar and the phase scalar on the same
tuple are related by a completely specified map. At the grid point
\((a,b)\) of (QCV.22), the arithmetic tensor centre and its phase
centre are respectively
\[
 \lambda_{k,a,b}=\frac{k}{2}+(2a-k)\delta+i(2b-k)\gamma,\qquad
 c_{k,a,b}=kA+(2a-k)U+i(2b-k)V.
 \tag{QPS.8}
\]
The real affine map
\[
 x+iy\longmapsto kA+\frac{U}{\delta}(x-k/2)
                           +i\frac{V}{\gamma}y
 \tag{QPS.9}
\]
maps the first finite grid bijectively to the second. Its inverse is
\(X+iY\mapsto k/2+(\delta/U)(X-kA)+i(\gamma/V)Y\).
Both inverses exist by (QCV.10). Equation (QPS.9) is a map of
the scalar grids. The full operator map retaining nilpotents is
the polynomial construction (QPS.1) and its exact full-jet action
(QPS.6), not an extension of (QPS.9) asserted without proof.

\subsection{The original full Taylor unit and the literal theta boundary}

Retain the original source notation and all its scalars:
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \phi_*=(4\pi^2x^4-6\pi x^2)e^{-\pi x^2},\quad
 D=-x\partial_x,\quad \Theta\phi=2\sum_{n\geq1}\phi(nx),\\
 v_h=g/h,\quad \upsilon_h=j_h(v_h)\in E_h^\times,\quad
 \varepsilon_h=\upsilon_h^{-1},\quad
 R_h(w)=r_h(\varepsilon_hw),\\
 s_h(w)=S_h\Theta\bigl(R_h(w)(D)\phi_*\bigr),\qquad
 \mathcal M s_h(w)=v_hR_h(w),\qquad j_h\mathcal M s_h(w)=w.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{QPS.10}
\]
These are precisely the original maps of (HCA.5)--(HCA.6);
\(S_h\) is the programme's Euler inverse on the zero-full-jet
range. At \(r_j\) every Taylor coefficient
\[
 (v_h)_{j,\ell}=\frac{1}{\ell!}v_h^{(\ell)}(r_j)
 \quad(0\leq\ell<m)
 \tag{QPS.11}
\]
remains in the multiplication operator \(\upsilon_h\). Its constant
coefficient is nonzero because \(h\) uses the full order of \(g\).
The reciprocal coefficients are given by the same exact finite
convolution recurrence as (QPS.3), with \(v_{j,\ell}\) replaced
by \((v_h)_{j,\ell}\). Thus no scalar-only replacement of this unit
has entered (QPS.10).

The numerator in the following expression has zero class in \(E_h\),
so the displayed quotient is an actual polynomial:
\[
 K_{\Phi,h}(w;s)=
   \frac{\Phi_h(s)R_h(w;s)-R_h(C_hw;s)}{h(s)}
   \in\mathbb{C}[s].
 \tag{QPS.12}
\]
Indeed modulo \(h\) its numerator is
\([\Phi_h]\varepsilon_hw-\varepsilon_h[\Phi_h]w=0\).
Mellin transformation of the original theta source gives the exact
function identity
\[
 \Phi_h(D)s_h(w)-s_h(C_hw)
      =\Theta\bigl(K_{\Phi,h}(w;D)\phi_*\bigr).
 \tag{QPS.13}
\]
Both sides have Mellin transform \(g(s)K_{\Phi,h}(w;s)\):
use (QPS.10) on the left and
\(\mathcal M\Theta(P(D)\phi_*)=gP\) on the right.
The original Mellin injectivity on the stated source spaces proves
the equality. All polynomials in \(D\) preserve those spaces.

For a pure tensor \(w=w_1\otimes\cdots\otimes w_k\), let
\(\mathcal S_k=s_h^{\otimes k}\), and form the degree-\((k-1)\)
cochain
\[
 \mathcal H_k(w)=
 \sum_{i=1}^k(-1)^{i-1}
 s_h(w_1)\otimes\cdots\otimes
 K_{\Phi,h}(w_i;D)\phi_*\otimes\cdots\otimes s_h(w_k).
 \tag{QPS.14}
\]
Each \(s_h(w_j)\) lies in degree one of the original complex
\([V\xrightarrow{\Theta}\mathscr B]\); the displayed middle factor
lies in \(V\), in degree zero. Consequently the tensor differential
on its \(i\)-th factor has sign \((-1)^{i-1}\), and all other
differentials in that summand vanish. Applying (QPS.13) gives
\[
 \left(\sum_{i=1}^k\Phi_h(D_i)\right)\mathcal S_k(w)
           -\mathcal S_k(C_{h,k}w)
       =d\,\mathcal H_k(w).
 \tag{QPS.15}
\]
Linearity proves this equality for every tensor, not only pure ones.
This supplies the literal homotopy connecting the calculated phase
operator to the original theta source, with its full Taylor unit
and the tensor signs. Proper-source kernels and independently
supported coefficients are not quotiented out in this calculation:
the identity takes place in the original full complex.

\subsection{The original norm retains its complete comparison factor}

No orthogonality of the full jet idempotents is assumed. The original
marked theta Gram matrix in the monomial basis is exactly
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 (G_h)_{a,b}=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}
 \overline{v_h(\tfrac12+it)R_h(s^a;\tfrac12+it)}
 v_h(\tfrac12+it)R_h(s^b;\tfrac12+it)\,dt,\\
 0\leq a,b<4m,\qquad G_{h,k}=G_h^{\otimes k}.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{QPS.16}
\]
This is the original Mellin-line norm and its ordered Hilbert tensor
norm. The integral converges by the original source estimates.
It is positive definite: a vector of zero norm has the entire
Mellin representative \(v_hR_h(w)\) zero on the line, hence
identically zero, hence \(w=j_h(v_hR_h(w))=0\) by (QPS.10).
The full factors \(v_h\) and \(R_h\) remain in every entry.
The norm of the inverse in this original metric is exactly
\[
 \|C_{h,k}^{-1}\|_{G_{h,k}}
 =\left\|
 G_{h,k}^{1/2}\mathcal J_{h,k}^{-1}
 \left(\bigoplus_{\mathbf j}c_{\mathbf j}^{-1}I_{m^k}\right)
 \mathcal J_{h,k}G_{h,k}^{-1/2}
 \right\|_2.
 \tag{QPS.17}
\]
To verify it, the map from a vector \(v\) with norm
\((v^*G_{h,k}v)^{1/2}\) to \(G_{h,k}^{1/2}v\) preserves that norm
exactly. Conjugating the operator by this map computes its norm
in Euclidean coordinates. Substitution of (QPS.7) and (QPS.2)
then gives the displayed equality. These are explicit coordinate
maps for the original norm, not a change to its definition.

Submultiplicativity and (QCV.27) give the useful bound with its
entire metric factor:
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \kappa_{h,k}
  =\|G_{h,k}^{1/2}\mathcal J_{h,k}^{-1}\|_2
       \|\mathcal J_{h,k}G_{h,k}^{-1/2}\|_2,\\
 \|C_{h,k}^{-1}\|_{G_{h,k}}
 <\frac{\kappa_{h,k}}
 {kA[1-(65/289)^m]}.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{QPS.18}
\]
The factor itself satisfies the exact tensor formula
\[
 \kappa_{h,k}=\kappa_{h,1}^{\,k}.
 \tag{QPS.19}
\]
Indeed \(G_{h,k}^{1/2}=(G_h^{1/2})^{\otimes k}\) and likewise for
the inverse square root: the displayed positive definite tensor
matrix squares to \(G_h^{\otimes k}\), so uniqueness of the
positive square root proves the claim. Also
\(\mathcal J_{h,k}=\mathcal J_h^{\otimes k}\). Singular-value
decompositions show \(\|X\otimes Y\|_2=\|X\|_2\|Y\|_2\):
tensor the two unitary changes of basis, after which the singular
values are the pairwise products of the original ones. Applying
this fact to the two factors in (QPS.18) proves (QPS.19).
This proves the precise metric consequence of the critical-value
bound. It does not remove \(\kappa_{h,k}\), assert that it is
uniform in \(k\), or turn the phase bound into an arithmetic
purity bound. The inverse, the full jet comparison and the original
theta boundary connecting them have all been constructed above.

\subsection{Exact phase cross-pairing on that same original source}

Since \(F\) is odd with real coefficients, \(F(it)\) is purely
imaginary for real \(t\). The original constant in (QCV.4) therefore
gives the exact line identity
\[
 \operatorname{Re}\Phi_h(\tfrac12+it)=A
                     \qquad(t\in\mathbb{R}).
 \tag{QPS.20}
\]
Let \(\mathcal T_k=\sum_i\Phi_h(D_i)\), with its domain on the
original \(k\)-variable Mellin source. The representatives
\(\mathcal S_k w\) belong to this domain: their Mellin transforms
are the finite linear combinations of products
\(v_h(s_i)R_h(w_i;s_i)\) from (QPS.10), and the original decay
estimates remain integrable after multiplication by any fixed
polynomial \(\Phi_h(s_i)\).
Mellin transformation of each \(D_i\) is multiplication by \(s_i\).
Using the original product measure \(dt_1\cdots dt_k/(2\pi)^k\)
and (QPS.20) thus proves
\[
 \operatorname{Re}
 \langle\mathcal S_k w,\mathcal T_k\mathcal S_k w\rangle
     =kA\|\mathcal S_k w\|^2.
 \tag{QPS.21}
\]
The pairing is conjugate linear in its first argument.
On a full phase block with \(C_{h,k}w=c_{k,a,b}w\), subtract
the scalar term in (QPS.15) to obtain
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \operatorname{Re}
 \langle\mathcal S_k w,d\mathcal H_k(w)\rangle
       =-(2a-k)U\|\mathcal S_k w\|^2,\\
 \|d\mathcal H_k(w)\|
       \geq |2a-k|U\|\mathcal S_k w\|.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{QPS.22}
\]
The first equality uses
\(\operatorname{Re}c_{k,a,b}=kA+(2a-k)U\). For nonzero \(w\),
the second follows by bounding the absolute value of the real
pairing by
\(\|\mathcal S_k w\|\|d\mathcal H_k(w)\|\) and dividing by the
nonzero source norm; for \(w=0\) it holds directly. In particular,
on the original all-\(\rho\) block \(a=b=k\), the lower bound is
\(kU\|\mathcal S_k w\|\), with the exact strictly positive \(U\)
in (QCV.10). This is the same full-unit theta boundary as (QPS.15).
It is not a scalar replacement for the source norm or its kernel.

There is also an exact norm equality with every remaining term.
Define the real odd polynomial \(q_\Phi(t)=\operatorname{Im}F(it)\);
it has degree \(4m+1\) and leading coefficient \(1/(4m+1)\).
Let
\(\Psi_w(t_1,\ldots,t_k)=
\mathcal M^{\otimes k}(\mathcal S_k w)
(\tfrac12+it_1,\ldots,\tfrac12+it_k)\), including all factors
\(v_h\) and \(R_h\) in (QPS.10). On the same phase block,
Mellin transformation of (QPS.15) gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 \|d\mathcal H_k(w)\|^2
   &=(2a-k)^2U^2\|\mathcal S_k w\|^2\\
   &\quad+\frac1{(2\pi)^k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^k}
    \left[\sum_{i=1}^kq_\Phi(t_i)-(2b-k)V\right]^2
                      |\Psi_w(t_1,\ldots,t_k)|^2\,dt_1\cdots dt_k.
 \end{split}
 \tag{QPS.23}
\]
Indeed its multiplier is exactly
\(-(2a-k)U+i[\sum_iq_\Phi(t_i)-(2b-k)V]\), whose squared
modulus is the sum displayed. For nonzero \(w\), the integral
is strictly positive. Section injectivity gives nonzero norm and
hence a point where the continuous \(\Psi_w\) is nonzero.
It stays nonzero on an open neighborhood. The nonconstant
polynomial \(\sum_iq_\Phi(t_i)-(2b-k)V\) cannot vanish throughout
that neighborhood: fix all but one coordinate in an open box
and use the nonzero leading coefficient in the remaining
coordinate. It is therefore nonzero on a smaller open set there,
where the nonnegative integrand is positive. This proves strict
positivity of its integral, including when \(a=k/2\).

For the exact original terminal class at \(r_j\), put
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 z_j=e_j(s-r_j)^{m-1}\in E_h,\quad
 f_j=s_h(z_j)\ne0,\quad
 c_j=\Phi_h(r_j),\\
 g_j=(\Phi_h(D)-c_j)f_j,\quad
 \mu_j=\frac{\langle f_j,g_j\rangle}{\|f_j\|^2},\quad
 \nu_j=\frac{\|g_j\|^2}{\|f_j\|^2}.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{QPS.24}
\]
The jet of \(z_j\) is \(y^{m-1}\) at its original full block
and zero elsewhere, so nonvanishing and section injectivity
prove \(f_j\ne0\). Write the signs in (QCV.8) as
\(c_j=A+\epsilon_jU+i\eta_jV\), where
\((\epsilon_j,\eta_j)=(+,+),(+,-),(-,-),(-,+)\) in the original
root order. Equation (QPS.21) for \(k=1\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{Re}\mu_j=-\epsilon_jU,\qquad
 \nu_j\geq|\mu_j|^2
 \tag{QPS.25}
\]
by the same inner-product convention and Cauchy--Schwarz.
On the original tensor representative \(f_j^{\otimes k}\)
the literal defect is
\[
 \mathcal B_{j,k}
   =\sum_{i=1}^k
       f_j^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes g_j\otimes f_j^{\otimes(k-i)}.
 \tag{QPS.26}
\]
Its \(k\) diagonal pairings are
\(\|g_j\|^2\|f_j\|^{2k-2}\). Each of the \(k(k-1)\) ordered
off-diagonal pairings is
\(|\langle f_j,g_j\rangle|^2\|f_j\|^{2k-4}\), since the two
distinguished factors contribute conjugate inner products.
Consequently
\[
 \frac{\|\mathcal B_{j,k}\|^2}{\|f_j^{\otimes k}\|^2}
   =k\nu_j+k(k-1)|\mu_j|^2
   =k^2|\mu_j|^2+k(\nu_j-|\mu_j|^2)
   \geq k^2U^2.
 \tag{QPS.27}
\]
Any original nonzero scalar coefficient multiplying the class
\(z_j^{\otimes k}\) multiplies both squared norms by its squared
absolute value; equations (QPS.26)--(QPS.27) remain exact with
that coefficient retained on both sides. No coefficient has
been set to one by altering the original source.

Finally the full phase-boundary map
\(w\mapsto d\mathcal H_k(w)\) is injective even on mixtures of
different phase blocks. If it were zero for a nonzero \(w\), put
\(P=R_h^{\otimes k}(w)\ne0\), a polynomial of degree less than
\(d=4m\) in each variable. Dividing its Mellin equation by the
nonzero entire product \(\prod_i v_h(s_i)\) gives the polynomial
identity
\[
 \left(\sum_{i=1}^k\Phi_h(s_i)\right)P(s_1,\ldots,s_k)
       =R_h^{\otimes k}(C_{h,k}w).
 \tag{QPS.28}
\]
If \(b\) is the degree of \(P\) in \(s_1\), its left side has
degree \(b+d+1\) in \(s_1\): the nonzero leading coefficient
comes from \(\Phi_h(s_1)P\), and the other summands have degree
at most \(b\). The right side has degree less than \(d\) in
\(s_1\), a contradiction. This proves the injectivity using the
same full representatives and their actual theta boundary.

\subsection{The full quartet connection and its exact lattice obstruction}

Put \(d=4m\), \(\mathcal A=\mathbb{C}[u]\), and retain the
original polynomial differential
\(\mathcal L_u=u\partial_s+h(s)\). Its cokernel over \(\mathcal A\)
is free with ordered basis
\(e=(1,s,\ldots,s^{d-1})\). To see this directly, applying
\(\mathcal L_u\) to \(s^n\) gives \(s^{n+d}\) plus terms of
strictly lower \(s\)-degree. Successive reduction therefore
produces a representative of degree less than \(d\). If a nonzero
\(q(s)\in\mathcal A[s]\) has degree \(n\), then
\(\mathcal L_uq\) has degree \(n+d\), with the same leading
coefficient as \(q\); hence it cannot be a nonzero representative
of degree less than \(d\). This proves existence and uniqueness
of the reduction. The ordered \(k\)-variable reductions commute,
giving the free original lattice
\[
 L_{h,k}=\mathcal A\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}E_{h,k},\qquad
 M_{h,k}=L_{h,k}[u^{-1}].
 \tag{QPS.29}
\]
This is the same marked lattice and its localization used in the
programme's localization--Tor calculation.

For each original basis column \(s^b\), perform the exact monic
polynomial division
\[
 \Phi_h(s)s^b=h(s)q_b(s)+r_b(s),\qquad
 \deg r_b<d,\qquad
 C_{a,b}=[s^a]r_b,\quad B_{a,b}=[s^a]q_b',
                 \quad 0\leq a,b<d.
 \tag{QPS.30}
\]
Here \(\deg q_b\leq b+1\leq d\), so \(\deg q_b'<d\).
The matrix \(C\) is exactly \(C_h\) from (QPS.1), with the
original \(\Phi_h(0)=0\), and \(B\) is a specified constant
matrix. On the localized cokernel the original connection
\(\partial_u-\Phi_h(s)/u^2\) acts in this row basis as
\[
 \nabla e=e\left(-\frac{C}{u^2}+\frac{B}{u}\right).
 \tag{QPS.31}
\]
Indeed \([\mathcal L_u q_b]=0\) gives
\([h q_b]=-u[q_b']\). Substituting (QPS.30) in
\([-\Phi_hs^b/u^2]\) proves (QPS.31) with its displayed signs.
The connection descends after localization because
\[
 [\,\partial_u-\Phi_h/u^2,\mathcal L_u\,]
       =u^{-1}\mathcal L_u.
 \tag{QPS.32}
\]
This follows by applying both sides to an arbitrary polynomial:
\([\partial_u,u\partial_s]=\partial_s\) and
\([-\Phi_h/u^2,u\partial_s]=h/u\).
For the ordered tensor lattice and row basis \(e_k\), the same
calculation adds the \(k\) factor connections:
\[
 \nabla e_k=e_k\left(-\frac{C_{h,k}}{u^2}
                         +\frac{B_{h,k}}u\right),\qquad
 B_{h,k}=\sum_{i=1}^k
     I^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes B\otimes I^{\otimes(k-i)}.
 \tag{QPS.33}
\]
No commutation of \(C_{h,k}\) with \(B_{h,k}\) is used.

Let \(\mathcal D_{\mathcal A}\) be the Weyl algebra generated by
\(u,\partial\) with \(\partial u-u\partial=1\), acting on
\(M_{h,k}\) through the displayed connection. For every integer
\(r\geq0\), write \(f_r=u^{-r}e_k\), a row of all original
basis vectors in that layer. The exact recurrence is
\[
 \begin{split}
 u\nabla f_r&=-f_{r+1}C_{h,k}+f_rB_{h,k}-r f_r,\\
 f_{r+1}&=-
   (u\nabla f_r-f_rB_{h,k}+r f_r)C_{h,k}^{-1}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{QPS.34}
\]
The inverse exists by (QPS.7), proved from the actual theta
exclusion and the original critical-value bound. If every entry
of \(f_r\) belongs to \(\mathcal D_{\mathcal A}L_{h,k}\), the
same holds for its derivative, its product by \(u\), and every
constant linear combination of those entries. Thus (QPS.34)
puts every entry of \(f_{r+1}\) in that module. The initial row
\(f_0=e_k\) lies in \(L_{h,k}\); induction proves
\[
 \mathcal D_{\mathcal A}L_{h,k}=M_{h,k}.
 \tag{QPS.35}
\]
This is an induction on entire ordered layers. It makes no
assumption that an arbitrary intermediate submodule is stable
under multiplication by \(B_{h,k}\) or \(C_{h,k}\).

Finally set \(Q_{h,k}=M_{h,k}/L_{h,k}\), as an \(\mathcal A\)-module,
and define
\(\Omega:L_{h,k}\to Q_{h,k}\) by
\(\Omega(v)=[\nabla v]\). It is \(\mathcal A\)-linear: for
\(a\in\mathcal A\), the difference
\(\nabla(av)-a\nabla v=a'v\) is in \(L_{h,k}\).
In column coefficients a vector
\(v=v_0+uv_1+u^2v_2(u)\) has
\[
 \Omega(v)=
  \left[-\frac{C_{h,k}v_0}{u^2}
       +\frac{B_{h,k}v_0-C_{h,k}v_1}{u}\right].
 \tag{QPS.36}
\]
Terms from the coefficient derivative and from \(u^2v_2(u)\)
are in \(L_{h,k}\). Every element of \(Q_{h,k}\) annihilated
by \(u^2\) has a unique representative \(x/u^2+y/u\), with
\(x,y\in E_{h,k}\). Solving the two equations in (QPS.36)
in the unchanged, possibly noncommuting matrix order gives
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 v_0=-C_{h,k}^{-1}x,\qquad
 v_1=-C_{h,k}^{-1}B_{h,k}C_{h,k}^{-1}x-C_{h,k}^{-1}y,\\
 \ker\Omega=u^2L_{h,k},\qquad
 \overline\Omega:L_{h,k}/u^2L_{h,k}
          \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }Q_{h,k}[u^2].
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{QPS.37}
\]
The displayed \(v_0,v_1\) provide the inverse, and substitution
proves it. If \(\Omega(v)=0\), first \(C_{h,k}v_0=0\) and
then \(C_{h,k}v_1=0\); invertibility proves the stated kernel.
The two modules are therefore related by this exact isomorphism.
The quotient \(Q_{h,k}\) is not being declared a connection
module: the original lattice is not preserved by \(\nabla\).
Equations (QPS.34)--(QPS.37) extend the scalar-primary
localization calculation to the full original quartet and every
ordered tensor power through the inverse already constructed on
the original full jets.
