\section{The exact tensor-order rate of the original high moments}
\label{sec:hmr-original-source}

The calculation below sharpens the linear-in-$k$ error of TW15b on
the actual degrees used by BRU5--7. It retains the original amplitude
and every source mass. Its final matrix statement concerns the full
finite polynomial source; the exact quotient and boundary maps are
then written down so that no spectral statement is silently passed
through a quotient.

\subsection{The original packet and the two proved source envelopes}

Keep the complete quartet of BRU1,
\[
 \rho=\tfrac12+\delta+i\gamma,\quad
 0<\delta<\tfrac12,\quad \gamma>2,\qquad
 h(s)=\prod_{r\in\{\rho,\bar\rho,1-\rho,1-\bar\rho\}}(s-r)^m,
 \quad m\geq1.
 \tag{HMR1}
\]
Here $m$ is the complete multiplicity in $g=2\xi$; the calculation
is on that original hypothetical counterfactual vertex. Put
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 v_h=g/h,\quad U=M_{j_hv_h},\quad
 w(y)=\frac{|v_h(1/2+iy)|^2}{2\pi},\quad
 m_k=w^{*k},\\
 \alpha=\pi/2,\quad
 \mu_h=\int_{\mathbb R}w(y)\,dy,\quad
 M_\alpha=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{\alpha y}w(y)\,dy,\quad
 p=8m-\tfrac92,\quad B=42+8m.
 \end{gathered}\tag{HMR2}
\]
The unit $U$ remains the full multiplication operator on
$\mathbb C[s]/(h)$. None of its Taylor coefficients is replaced by
a scalar. Real-entire conjugation gives $w(-y)=w(y)$, and the
nonzero entire $v_h$ has isolated zeros; thus $w>0$ almost
everywhere. The earlier proved original-source estimates TW2,
TW5 and TW7 give, with their original positive constants,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 e^{\alpha|y|}w(y)&\leq C_h(1+|y|)^{-p},&
 0<\mu_h,M_\alpha&<\infty,\\
 m_k(y)&\leq kC_hM_\alpha^{k-1}e^{-\alpha|y|}
                  &&(k\geq1),\\
 m_3(y)&\geq c_he^{-\alpha|y|}(1+|y|)^{-B}.
 \end{aligned}\tag{HMR3}
\]
Here $C_h$ is precisely the tilt constant of BT12, and $c_h$
is precisely the AU10/CJ15 constant retained in TW7 at $k=3$.
The upper convolution estimate in HMR3 follows by dropping only
the factor $(1+|y|/k)^{-p}\leq1$ from TW5. The complete proofs
of those source estimates remain in the cumulative repository;
the following argument strengthens their consequence without
requiring a pointwise lower estimate for the one-factor zeta
numerator.

\subsection{An exact lower convolution estimate with truncated tilted mass}

For every fixed $R>0$ define the literal finite integral
\[
 M_R=\int_{-R}^{R}e^{\alpha t}w(t)\,dt>0,\qquad
 T_R=\frac{2C_h}{p-1}(1+R)^{1-p}.
 \tag{HMR4}
\]
Then
\[
 M_R\leq M_\alpha\leq M_R+T_R,\qquad
 M_R\uparrow M_\alpha\quad(R\longrightarrow\infty).
 \tag{HMR5}
\]
Indeed the omitted positive integrand is bounded by
$C_h(1+|t|)^{-p}$ by HMR3. Integrating on the two omitted
half-lines gives exactly $T_R$. Monotone convergence proves
the limit. Positivity follows from positivity almost everywhere
on the nonempty interval.

For $k\geq3$ and $|y|\geq(k-3)R$, the improved lower bound is
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 m_k(y)\geq c_h M_R^{k-3}e^{-\alpha|y|}
                  (1+|y|+(k-3)R)^{-B}.}
 \tag{HMR6}
\]
For $k=3$ it is exactly HMR3. For $k>3$ first take
$y\geq(k-3)R$ and write the original convolution as
\[
 m_k(y)=\int_{\mathbb R^{k-3}}
       m_3(y-Z)\prod_{j=1}^{k-3}w(t_j)\,dt_1\cdots dt_{k-3},
 \qquad Z=\sum_{j=1}^{k-3}t_j.
\]
Restrict to $[-R,R]^{k-3}$. On this cube
$y-Z\geq0$ and $y-Z\leq y+(k-3)R$. The last line of HMR3
therefore bounds the integrand from below by
\[
 c_he^{-\alpha y}(1+y+(k-3)R)^{-B}
                    \prod_{j=1}^{k-3}e^{\alpha t_j}w(t_j).
\]
Tonelli and the product integral give HMR6 on the positive
half-line. Evenness of the convolution gives its negative
half-line statement. The convolution uses the original
Lebesgue coordinates, with no change in Jacobian or source mass.

\subsection{Finite high-moment bounds and their exact rate}

For integers $k\geq3$, $n\geq1$ and real $R>0$ set
\[
 I_{k,2n}=\int_{\mathbb R}y^{2n}m_k(y)\,dy,\qquad
 J_n=(2n)!\alpha^{-2n},\qquad
 D_{k,n,R}=1+\frac{2n+1}{\alpha}+(k-3)R.
 \tag{HMR7}
\]
This $D_{k,n,R}$ is a displayed positive scalar and is distinct
from the earlier polynomial comparison constant $D_N$ in TW11.
For $k\geq3$ and $2n/\alpha\geq(k-3)R$ one has
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \frac{2c_h}{\alpha e^2(2n)}
       M_R^{k-3}D_{k,n,R}^{-B}
 \ \leq\ \frac{I_{k,2n}}{J_n}\
 \leq\ \frac{2kC_h}{\alpha}M_\alpha^{k-1}.}
 \tag{HMR8}
\]
To prove the upper bound, insert HMR3 on both half-lines:
$\int_0^\infty y^{2n}e^{-\alpha y}\,dy
=(2n)!\alpha^{-2n-1}$. This identity follows by
$2n$ integrations by parts, with vanishing exponential
boundary terms.

For the lower bound restrict both half-lines to
$|y|\in[2n/\alpha,(2n+1)/\alpha]$. Their individual lengths
are $1/\alpha$; throughout them, HMR6 gives
\[
 I_{k,2n}\geq
 \frac{2c_h}{\alpha}M_R^{k-3}
       (2n/\alpha)^{2n}e^{-2n-1}D_{k,n,R}^{-B}.
 \tag{HMR9}
\]
For every integer $a\geq1$, monotonicity of $\log x$ gives
\[
 \log(a!)=\sum_{j=1}^a\log j
 \leq\int_1^a\log x\,dx+\log a
 =a\log a-a+1+\log a.
 \tag{HMR10}
\]
For $a=1$ this is equality. For $a>1$ integrate on each
$[j,j+1]$, $1\leq j<a$, to bound the first $a-1$
summands, and then add the last summand. Consequently
$(2n)^{2n}e^{-2n-1}/(2n)!\geq e^{-2}/(2n)$.
Division of HMR9 by $J_n$ proves the lower bound in HMR8.

Let $n_k$ be any sequence of positive integers satisfying
\[
 \frac{n_k}{k}\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
 \frac{\log n_k}{k}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{HMR11}
\]
The exact original-source rate is
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \lim_{k\to\infty}\frac1k
 \log\frac{I_{k,2n_k}}{(2n_k)!\alpha^{-2n_k}}
 =\log M_\alpha.}
 \tag{HMR12}
\]
Fix $R>0$. The first condition in HMR11 eventually guarantees
the finite-domain inequality required in HMR8.
Moreover $\log D_{k,n_k,R}/k\to0$: eventually $k\leq n_k$,
so $D_{k,n_k,R}$ is bounded above by a fixed positive
multiple of $n_k+1$ and bounded below by one.
Taking logarithms of both sides of HMR8 gives
\[
 \log M_R\leq
 \liminf_k\frac1k\log(I_{k,2n_k}/J_{n_k})
 \leq\limsup_k\frac1k\log(I_{k,2n_k}/J_{n_k})
 \leq\log M_\alpha.
\]
All terms suppressed by these limits are explicitly the
constants, $\log k$, $\log(2n_k)$ and $B\log D_{k,n_k,R}$
already displayed in HMR8, divided by $k$.
Now HMR5 and continuity of the logarithm on positive numbers
let $R$ tend to infinity. This proves HMR12. The order of the
two limits is part of the proof; no growing $R$ is substituted
into a fixed-$R$ estimate.

\subsection{The same rate in the unchanged original sum coordinate}

The source coordinate is still $S=k/2+iy$. Put $c=k/2$ and
define its diagonal power norm
\[
 L_{k,n}=\int_{\mathbb R}|S^n|^2m_k(y)\,dy
        =\int_{\mathbb R}(c^2+y^2)^n m_k(y)\,dy.
 \tag{HMR13}
\]
For $z\geq0$ introduce the exact finite polynomial
\[
 E_n(z)=\sum_{r=0}^n
   \binom nr z^{2r}\frac{(2n-2r)!}{(2n)!}.
 \tag{HMR14}
\]
Binomial expansion in HMR13 and integration of HMR3 give
\[
 I_{k,2n}\leq L_{k,n}
 \leq\frac{2kC_h}{\alpha}M_\alpha^{k-1}J_nE_n(\alpha c).
 \tag{HMR15}
\]
Every term in the binomial expansion is nonnegative, and all
$c^{2r}$ and factorial factors appear exactly in HMR14.

For every $n\geq1$ and $z\geq0$ the stronger finite bound is
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 E_n(z)\leq\exp(z^2/\mathfrak c_n)\leq\exp(z^2/n),
 \qquad \mathfrak c_n=\left(\frac{(2n)!}{n!}\right)^{1/n}>n.}
 \tag{HMR16}
\]
For $1\leq r\leq n$, exact cancellation of the even
factors of $(2n)!/(2n-2r)!$ gives
\[
 \binom nr\frac{(2n-2r)!}{(2n)!}
 =\frac1{r!\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}2(2n-2j-1)}.
\]
The $n$ factors $a_j=2(2n-2j-1)$, $0\leq j<n$,
are positive and decreasing. Hence the mean of the
first $r$ logarithms is at least the mean of all
$n$ logarithms: every omitted term is at most
the last retained term, which is at most the
retained mean. Their full product is
$\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}a_j=(2n)!/n!$, so
$\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}a_j\geq\mathfrak c_n^r$.
Also $(2n)!/n!=\prod_{j=n+1}^{2n}j>n^n$,
which proves $\mathfrak c_n>n$.
Termwise comparison of HMR14 with the exponential
series proves HMR16, including its $r=0$ term
and $z=0$ case.

For every sequence satisfying HMR11 one therefore has
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \lim_{k\to\infty}\frac1k\log(L_{k,n_k}/J_{n_k})
 =\log M_\alpha.}
 \tag{HMR17}
\]
Indeed HMR14 and HMR16 give
$0\leq k^{-1}\log E_{n_k}(\alpha k/2)
\leq\alpha^2k/(4n_k)\to0$.
HMR12 and the two sides of HMR15 give HMR17.

In the original quartet the four degrees are exactly
\[
 \ell_k=1+k(m-1),\quad q_k=(k+1)^2\ell_k,\qquad
 n_k\in\{q_k-1,q_k,2q_k-1,2q_k\}.
 \tag{HMR18}
\]
Each such choice has $n_k\geq k^2$ and $\log n_k/k\to0$,
because $m$ is fixed and $q_k$ is its displayed polynomial
in $k$. Thus both HMR12 and HMR17 apply at each of the
four actual degrees. For the mixed Gram entries one still
uses their exact sums below; these diagonal results do not
assign signs to off-diagonal entries.

\subsection{An unavoidable exponential condition number on the full source}

Use precisely the fixed one-factor measure of TW3:
\[
 d\sigma(y)=\frac{|\Gamma(1/4+iy/2)|^2}{2\pi}\,dy,\qquad
 \sigma(\mathbb R)=\sqrt{2\pi}.
 \tag{HMR19}
\]
Its original constants and inequalities TW4 are
\[
 c_\Gamma e^{-\alpha|y|}(1+|y|)^{-1/2}
 \leq\frac{d\sigma}{dy}
 \leq C_\Gamma e^{-\alpha|y|}(1+|y|)^{-1/2},\qquad
 c_\Gamma=\sqrt2e^{-7/6},\quad
 C_\Gamma=\sqrt{2\sqrt5}e^{2/3}.
 \tag{HMR20}
\]
Write $L^\sigma_{k,n}=\int(c^2+y^2)^n\,d\sigma(y)$.
For $n\geq1$ the finite bounds are
\[
 \frac{\sqrt2c_\Gamma e^{-\alpha}}{2n}
 \leq\frac{L^\sigma_{k,n}}{J_n}
 \leq\frac{2C_\Gamma}{\alpha}E_n(\alpha c).
 \tag{HMR21}
\]
For the lower bound first replace $(c^2+y^2)^n$ by
$y^{2n}$, restrict to $|y|\geq1$, and use
$(1+|y|)^{-1/2}\geq1/(\sqrt2|y|)$ there.
The resulting integral is
$\sqrt2c_\Gamma\alpha^{-2n}\Gamma(2n,\alpha)$.
Integration by parts gives the exact formula
\[
 \Gamma(2n,\alpha)=(2n-1)!e^{-\alpha}
              \sum_{j=0}^{2n-1}\frac{\alpha^j}{j!}
 \geq(2n-1)!e^{-\alpha}.
\]
Dividing by $J_n$ proves the lower bound in HMR21.
The upper bound follows by dropping the factor
$(1+|y|)^{-1/2}$ and using the same binomial integral
as in HMR15. It follows that at the degrees HMR18,
\[
 \lim_{k\to\infty}\frac1k\log(L^\sigma_{k,n_k}/J_{n_k})=0,
 \quad
 \lim_{k\to\infty}\frac1k\log(L_{k,n_k}/L^\sigma_{k,n_k})
 =\log M_\alpha .
 \tag{HMR22}
\]

For every $N\geq0$, both source forms on the very same
space $\mathcal P_N=\operatorname{span}(1,S,\ldots,S^N)$
are positive definite. Let their matrices in this increasing
power frame be $H_{k,N}$ and $H^{\sigma,k}_N$. Define
\[
 D_{k,N}=(H^{\sigma,k}_N)^{-1/2}
              H_{k,N}(H^{\sigma,k}_N)^{-1/2}>0,\qquad
 \kappa_{k,N}=
       \frac{\lambda_{\max}(D_{k,N})}{\lambda_{\min}(D_{k,N})}.
 \tag{HMR23}
\]
This operator $D_{k,N}$ is distinguished by both its indices
from the scalar $D_{k,n,R}$ in HMR7 and the scalar $D_N$ of
TW11. Square roots are the unique positive Hermitian ones.
For $a\ne0$ the quotient of the two original norms is the
Rayleigh quotient of $D_{k,N}$ on
$b=(H^{\sigma,k}_N)^{1/2}a$. Its minimum and maximum
are the smallest and largest eigenvalues, by expanding
$b$ in an orthonormal eigenbasis.

The constant vector and the degree-$N$ power vector give
the two exact quotients
\[
 R_{k,0}=\frac{\mu_h^k}{\sqrt{2\pi}},\qquad
 R_{k,N}=\frac{L_{k,N}}{L^\sigma_{k,N}},\qquad
 \kappa_{k,N}\geq\frac{R_{k,N}}{R_{k,0}}.
 \tag{HMR24}
\]
The first identity follows from the original product mass
$\int w^{*k}=\mu_h^k$. The inequality follows from
$\lambda_{\max}\geq R_{k,N}$ and
$\lambda_{\min}\leq R_{k,0}$, all of them positive.
In particular the literal finite bound, for $k\geq3$,
$R>0$, $N\geq1$ and $2N/\alpha\geq(k-3)R$, is
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \kappa_{k,N}\geq
 \frac{\sqrt{2\pi}c_h}
 {e^2(2N)C_\Gamma E_N(\alpha k/2)D_{k,N,R}^{B}}\,
 \frac{M_R^{k-3}}{\mu_h^k}.}
 \tag{HMR24a}
\]
This follows by dividing the lower bound HMR8,
which also bounds $L_{k,N}/J_N$ from below,
by the upper bound HMR21, and then dividing by
the displayed $R_{k,0}$. Thus every constant
in the finite comparison is retained.
Taking logarithms using HMR22 proves the genuine lower
rate for the full finite source, at each $N_k$ in HMR18:
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log\kappa_{k,N_k}}{k}
 \geq \log(M_\alpha/\mu_h)>0.}
 \tag{HMR25}
\]
For strictness, evenness and absolute integrability give
\[
 M_\alpha-\mu_h
 =\int_{\mathbb R}(\cosh(\alpha y)-1)w(y)\,dy>0.
 \tag{HMR26}
\]
The integrand is nonnegative and is positive almost
everywhere away from $y=0$. It is integrable by HMR3.
This proves every inequality in HMR25 on the original
source. It does not identify the exact condition-number
rate or the spectrum after a quotient.

\subsection{The exact map into the coupled relation and boundary calculation}

The unchanged quotient polynomial and full-unit inclusion are
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \chi(S)=\prod_{a,b=0}^k
 \bigl(S-k/2-(2a-k)\delta-i(2b-k)\gamma\bigr)^{\ell_k},\\
 E=\mathbb C[S]/(\chi),\qquad
 \eta([P])=U^{\otimes k}[P(s_1+\cdots+s_k)].
 \end{gathered}\tag{HMR27}
\]
Let $J_N:\mathcal P_N\twoheadrightarrow E$ be literal
remainder, $N\geq q_k-1$. On the coefficient frames
of these same spaces its kernel is
$\chi\mathcal P_{N-q_k}$; the latter is zero when
$N=q_k-1$. Retain the fixed actual period observation
$\Lambda:E\twoheadrightarrow B$ of BRU3 and
$I:K=\ker\Lambda\hookrightarrow E$.

Every original moment entering the finite calculation
has the explicit expression
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 (H_{k,N})_{ab}
 &=\sum_{r=0}^a\sum_{s=0}^b
 \binom ar\binom bs c^{a+b-r-s}(-i)^r i^s
                       \mathfrak m_{k,r+s},\\
 \mathfrak m_{k,d}
 &=\sum_{d_1+\cdots+d_k=d}
       \frac{d!}{d_1!\cdots d_k!}
          \prod_{j=1}^k\int_{\mathbb R}t^{d_j}w(t)\,dt,\\
 \mathfrak m_{k,0}&=\mu_h^k,\qquad
 \mathfrak m_{k,2n}=I_{k,2n},\qquad
 \mathfrak m_{k,2n+1}=0 .
 \end{aligned}\tag{HMR28}
\]
The first equality expands $\overline S^{\,a}S^b$
with both signs retained; the second expands the
original convolution sum, justified by absolute
moment convergence and Fubini. Evenness gives the
last equality. This is the literal map from the
moments in HMR8 into every finite source entry.

For either positive source matrix $H$, define on
the same coefficient quotient
\[
 G_N(H)=(J_NH^{-1}J_N^*)^{-1},\qquad
 R_N(H)=H^{-1}J_N^*G_N(H).
 \tag{HMR29}
\]
These matrices are defined because $J_N$ is surjective.
Multiplication gives $J_NR_N=1_E$, and for
$z\in\ker J_N$,
$z^*HR_N=z^*J_N^*G_N=0$. Thus the lift is orthogonal
to all relations. Every other lift is $R_Ne+z$,
whose norm squared equals $e^*G_Ne+z^*Hz$.
This proves the minimum property, uniqueness and
$R_N^*HR_N=G_N$, including the zero-relation case.

The fixed boundary and its full kernel then have
the exact induced matrices and inverse decomposition
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 H_N^K=I^*G_NI,\qquad
 Q_N=(\Lambda G_N^{-1}\Lambda^*)^{-1},\qquad
 S_N=G_N^{-1}\Lambda^*Q_N,\\
 \Lambda S_N=1_B,\quad I^*G_NS_N=0,\quad
 G_N^{-1}=I(H_N^K)^{-1}I^*+S_NQ_N^{-1}S_N^* .
 \end{gathered}\tag{HMR30}
\]
Indeed $[I,S_N]$ is a bijection $K\oplus B\to E$:
subtract $S_N\Lambda e$ from $e$ to obtain its
unique kernel component. In this frame $G_N$
is exactly $\operatorname{diag}(H_N^K,Q_N)$;
inverting this congruence proves the last identity.
Empty factors have determinant one and their unique
empty inverse. Hence HMR30 also covers both extreme ranks.

If the smallest and largest eigenvalues in HMR23
are $a,b$, the original forms satisfy
$aH_N^{\sigma,k}\preceq H_{k,N}\preceq bH_N^{\sigma,k}$.
Minimizing on every identical fibre of $J_N$ gives
\[
 aG_N(H_N^{\sigma,k})\preceq G_N(H_{k,N})
                  \preceq bG_N(H_N^{\sigma,k}).
 \tag{HMR31}
\]
Restriction by the same $I$ proves the corresponding
kernel inequality. Minimizing on identical fibres
of $\Lambda$ proves the corresponding boundary
inequality. These are the exact induced comparisons;
HMR25 supplies no reverse lower condition estimate
for either of these two induced forms.

In particular the BRU original relation rows remain
$d_j=\chi p_{j+1-q_k}$, where $p_{j+1-q_k}$ is
monic orthogonal to lower degrees in the actual
measure $|\chi|^2m_k(y)\,dy$. With
$a_j=\|d_j\|^2$ and
$\alpha_j=(\langle d_j,S^b\rangle)_{b<q_k}$,
HMR28--29 gives literally
\[
 G_j=G_{j+1}+\alpha_j^*\alpha_j/a_j.
 \tag{HMR32}
\]
Subtracting the orthogonal projection onto the
new relation line from $R_j$ proves this identity;
it is the same $x=1$ map as BRU5--6.
In the frame of HMR30 at degree $j+1$ put
$b_j=\alpha_jI$, $c_j=\alpha_jS_{j+1}$,
$\beta_j=b_j(H_{j+1}^K)^{-1}b_j^*$ and
$\nu_j=c_jQ_{j+1}^{-1}c_j^*$.
The rank-one determinant identity in this frame is
\[
 \frac{\det G_j}{\det G_{j+1}}
 =1+\frac{\beta_j+\nu_j}{a_j}
 =\left(1+\frac{\beta_j}{a_j}\right)
   \left(1+\frac{\nu_j}{a_j+\beta_j}\right).
 \tag{HMR33}
\]
For completeness, conjugating by the positive square root
of $G_{j+1}$ reduces the first determinant ratio to
$\det(1+v^*v/a_j)=1+vv^*/a_j$.
For $v=0$ this is immediate. For $v\ne0$,
on the kernel of the row $v$ the operator is identity,
and on its remaining line its eigenvalue is the displayed
scalar. HMR30 gives $vv^*=\beta_j+\nu_j$.
The second equality is direct multiplication of positive
scalars. It keeps the entire original kernel contribution.

Define the original signed endpoint scalar by
\[
 F_{\mathrm{ar},k}
 =\log\det G_{q_k-1}+\log\det G_{q_k}
  -\log\det G_{2q_k-1}-\log\det G_{2q_k}.
 \tag{HMR34}
\]
Telescoping HMR33 proves its exact coupled expression
\[
 F_{\mathrm{ar},k}
 =\sum_{j=q_k-1}^{2q_k-1}w_j
    \left[
      \log\left(1+\frac{\beta_j}{a_j}\right)
      +\log\left(1+\frac{\nu_j}{a_j+\beta_j}\right)
    \right],
 \quad w_{q_k-1}=w_{2q_k-1}=1,\quad w_j=2
 \ \text{otherwise}.
 \tag{HMR35}
\]
This is the BRU20 decomposition on the very same source;
no sign of a difference with its Gamma counterpart is
inferred from HMR25. The fixed one-factor $\sigma$ used
in HMR19 is also distinct from the $k$-fold Gamma measure
at the $x=0$ endpoint of BRU4. HMR28--35 applies to
that endpoint by inserting its literal moments, not
by replacing its mass with $\sqrt{2\pi}$.

Multiplying a source form by a positive scalar $t_k$
multiplies each $G_N$ by $t_k$, as is immediate from
HMR29. Its contribution to HMR34 is therefore
$q_k\log(t_k)(1+1-1-1)=0$.
The determinant calculation below now bounds this very signed
endpoint cost through HMR29--30. It retains the original
off-diagonal moments, orthogonal relations, and coupled kernel
and boundary factors in HMR28--35. The scalar cancellation just
proved is used with the exact nonnegative determinant deficits,
so the resulting estimate concerns the actual quotient forms.

\subsection{Determinant mass returned through the original source maps}

For the same fixed quartet and $k\geq3$, retain the constants
$p,B,M_\alpha,c_h,C_h,c_\Gamma,C_\Gamma$ above and put
\[
\begin{gathered}
 M=B/2,\quad \vartheta_h=\int_{-1}^1w(y)\,dy,\qquad
 A_k=\frac{C_h}{c_\Gamma}k^{p+1}M_\alpha^{k-1},\\
 a_k=\frac{c_h\vartheta_h^{k-3}e^{-\alpha(k-3)}(k-2)^{-B}}
                 {C_\Gamma2^{B/2}},\qquad
 b_h=\frac{c_h}{C_\Gamma2^{3B/2}},\\
 \Xi_{k,N}=(N+1)\log A_k
       -\log\frac{\det H_{k,N}}{\det H_N^{\sigma,k}}\geq0 .
\end{gathered}\tag{HMR36}
\]
The $C_h$ here is the same original tilt constant called
$C_h^{\rm tilt}$ in TWA2. The full polynomial upper envelope
gives $0<D_{k,N}\preceq A_kI$ with $D_{k,N}$ precisely HMR23.
Its eigenvalues divided by $A_k$ lie in $(0,1]$, so their
negative logarithms sum to $\Xi_{k,N}$, proving positivity.
Their product is $e^{-\Xi_{k,N}}$ and is no larger than each
individual factor. Consequently the following full-vector
lower comparison supplements HMR31:
\[
 A_ke^{-\Xi_{k,N}}H_N^{\sigma,k}
 \preceq H_{k,N}\preceq A_kH_N^{\sigma,k},\qquad
 A_ke^{-\Xi_{k,N}}G_N^\sigma
 \preceq G_N\preceq A_kG_N^\sigma .
 \tag{HMR37}
\]
Here $G_N^\sigma=G_N(H_N^{\sigma,k})$, and the second
comparison follows by the already proved minimum property
on the same $J_N$ fibres. This lower bound does not replace
an exactly known larger smallest eigenvalue in HMR31.

The complete determinant proof DMR1--20, retained as a full
source body in this edition, gives a finite upper estimate
for HMR36 at every integer $N\geq0$. Its constants are recorded
here to specify the receiving bound completely. For every
real $R\geq1$ use the literal $M_R$ of HMR4 and set
\[
\begin{gathered}
 Y=\max\{2,(k-3)R\},\qquad
 r=\max\left\{1,\left\lceil
 \frac{\log(b_hM_R^{k-3}/a_k)}{\log2}\right\rceil\right\},
 \quad d=2r+M,\quad L=N+d,\quad g_0=\log\sqrt{2\pi},\\
 Z_{L,Y}=\frac{e^2(L+1)(L+2)(2L+1)
                   [2(L+2)]^{Y+1}}{\sqrt{2\pi}},\\
 \mathcal E_{N,r,Y}
 =2r^2\log(N+2r)+M[g_0+2L\log L]+2rg_0
   +4r^2\log\max(1,r)+(2r+M)\log Z_{L,Y},\\
 \mathfrak P_{k,N,R}
 =(N+1)\{\log A_k-\log b_h-(k-3)\log M_R\}
       +\mathcal E_{N,r,Y},\qquad
 0\leq\Xi_{k,N}\leq\mathfrak P_{k,N,R}.
\end{gathered}\tag{HMR38}
\]
In that proof the exact rational source multiplier is
$y^r/[(y+iY)^r(y+i)^M]$, the auxiliary polynomial is
$y^r(y+iY)^r(y+i)^M$, and its complete divided-jet frame
determinant is
$(-iY)^{r^2}(-i)^{rM}(i(Y-1))^{rM}$.
The Gram block inequality for these two polynomial families,
the full Gamma recurrence, the divided-jet kernel estimate,
and this determinant yield
\[
 \log\frac{\det H_{k,N}}{\det H_N^{\sigma,k}}
 \geq(N+1)[\log b_h+(k-3)\log M_R]-\mathcal E_{N,r,Y}.
\]
Subtraction from the definition HMR36 proves exactly HMR38.
The triangular coordinate change from $1,y,\ldots,y^N$ to
$1,S,\ldots,S^N$ has diagonal $1,i,\ldots,i^N$ and determinant
of modulus one; both source forms undergo the same congruence.
Thus this determinant statement uses the unchanged $S=k/2+iy$
coordinate and retains the original masses.

We prove the transport of this determinant bound, including
its kernel and boundary components. For any $H,H_0>0$ in
one original source frame with $H\preceq A H_0$, and any

\[
 D=H_0^{-1/2}HH_0^{-1/2},\quad E_0=D/A,\quad
 L_0=JH_0^{-1/2},\quad Q_0=(L_0L_0^*)^{-1},\quad
 W_0=L_0^*Q_0^{1/2}.
 \tag{HMR39}
\]
Then $W_0^*W_0=I_q$. Choose an isometry $K_0$ onto
$\ker L_0$; the matrix $[K_0,W_0]$ is unitary. In this
frame write
$E_0=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}F&C\\C^*&V\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
The positive Schur complement $T=V-C^*F^{-1}C$ satisfies
$\det E_0=\det F\det T$ and
$(W_0^*E_0^{-1}W_0)^{-1}=T$, as follows by multiplying
the two triangular block eliminations. Both $F$ and $T$
are positive contractions: $F\preceq I$ is restriction,
and $T\preceq V\preceq I$. Consequently
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \det E_0\leq\det T\leq1,\qquad
 G(H)=A Q_0^{1/2}TQ_0^{1/2},\quad G(H_0)=Q_0,\\
 0\leq q\log A-\log\frac{\det G(H)}{\det G(H_0)}
       \leq-\log\det E_0 .
\end{gathered}\tag{HMR40}
\]
The identities for $G$ follow by inserting HMR39 into
$(JH^{-1}J^*)^{-1}$ with that exact matrix order.
When $\ker L_0=0$, its empty determinant is one and
$T=E_0$ in the unitary frame, so the proof still applies.

For any fixed injective source column $Y_0$, its original

       =\det(V_0^*E_0V_0)\geq\det E_0.

Complete $V_0$ to a unitary matrix and use the same block
determinant formula, whose complementary positive Schur

also has determinant at most one. Applying this identity to
