\section{Exact exterior constants for the original arithmetic polynomial}

This calculation retains the original monic polynomial, its translated
coordinate, and every root multiplicity.  Let
\[
 \ell\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0},\quad k=4\ell+1,\quad
 m\in\mathbb Z_{\geq1},\quad e=1+k(m-1),\quad q=e(k+1)^2,
 \quad c\in\mathbb C,\quad \delta,\gamma\in\mathbb R.
 \tag{IBC1}
\]
Put \(d=\delta^2\), \(g=\gamma^2\), \(K=k(k+2)\), and
\[
 n_a=2a-k,\qquad z_{ab}=n_a\delta+i n_b\gamma,\qquad
 \chi(c+x)=\prod_{a,b=0}^{k}(x-z_{ab})^e.
 \tag{IBC2}
\]
In particular the degree is exactly \(q\).  Define the real logarithm
of the following positive quotient on the original coordinate:
\[
 L_\chi(y)=\log\frac{|\chi(c+iy)|^2}{|y|^{2q}},
 \qquad y\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\},\quad \chi(c+iy)\ne0.
 \tag{IBC3}
\]
All assertions about its exterior series assume
\[
 \tau=\frac{k^2(d+g)}{y^2}<1.
 \tag{IBC4}
\]
This inequality makes each factor in (IBC2) nonzero at \(x=iy\).
The case \(d=g=0\) gives \(\chi(c+x)=x^q\) and \(L_\chi(y)=0\);
formulas with a quotient by a power of \(\tau\) below use their displayed
continuous value at \(\tau=0\).

\subsection{The finite grid moments}

Define the actual finite averages
\[
 \mu_{2r}=\frac1{k+1}\sum_{a=0}^{k}n_a^{2r}.
 \tag{IBC5}
\]
Odd moments vanish under the exact permutation \(a\mapsto k-a\).
The even moments needed here are
\[
 \mu_2=\frac K3,\qquad
 \mu_4=\frac{K(3K-4)}{15},\qquad
 \mu_6=\frac{K(3K^2-12K+16)}{21}.
 \tag{IBC6}
\]
Here is a derivation including the constants.  Summing a finite geometric
series gives, first for real \(t\ne0\) and then by continuity at zero,
\[
 \frac1{k+1}\sum_{a=0}^{k}e^{n_at}
 =\frac{\sinh((k+1)t)}{(k+1)\sinh t}.
 \tag{IBC7}
\]
Multiply its Taylor series by \(\sinh t\), and compare the coefficients
of \(t^3,t^5,t^7\).  Because \(\mu_0=1\), the three resulting equations are
\[
 \begin{split}
 3\mu_2+1&=(k+1)^2,\\
 5\mu_4+10\mu_2+1&=(k+1)^4,\\
 7\mu_6+35\mu_4+21\mu_2+1&=(k+1)^6.
 \end{split}
 \tag{IBC8}
\]
Substitute \((k+1)^2=K+1\) and solve successively.  The numerators of
\(\mu_4\) and \(\mu_6\), before division by \(5\) and \(7\), respectively,
are \(K^2-4K/3\) and \(K^3-4K^2+16K/3\).  This proves (IBC6).

\subsection{Convergent exterior series and its first three coefficients}

For every \(y\) satisfying (IBC4) one has the absolutely convergent
identity
\[
 L_\chi(y)=\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{p_{2r}}{y^{2r}},
 \qquad
 p_{2r}=\frac{e(-1)^{r+1}}{r}
             \operatorname{Re}\sum_{a,b=0}^{k}z_{ab}^{\,2r}.
 \tag{IBC9}
\]
To prove it, use the exact factorization
\[
 \frac{|\chi(c+iy)|^2}{|y|^{2q}}
 =\prod_{a,b=0}^{k}|1+i z_{ab}/y|^{2e}.
 \tag{IBC10}
\]
The disk \(|z_{ab}/y|<1\) lies in the domain of the Taylor logarithm of
\(1+i z_{ab}/y\).  Thus the logarithm in (IBC3) equals
\(2e\operatorname{Re}\sum_{a,b}\log(1+i z_{ab}/y)\).
In the series
\(\log(1+w)=\sum_{n\geq1}(-1)^{n+1}w^n/n\), odd powers cancel under
\((a,b)\mapsto(k-a,k-b)\), which sends \(z_{ab}\) to \(-z_{ab}\).
The term \(n=2r\) is precisely (IBC9).
Absolute convergence follows from the finite root set and (IBC4);
all rearrangements made here are therefore valid.

The first two coefficients are
\[
 p_2=\frac{qk(k+2)}3(d-g)
 \tag{IBC11}
\]
and
\[
 \begin{split}
 p_4
 &=q\left(3\mu_2^2dg-\frac{\mu_4}{2}(d^2+g^2)\right)\\
 &=q\left[
    \frac{k^2(k+2)^2}{3}dg
    -\frac{k(k+2)(3k^2+6k-4)}{30}(d^2+g^2)
   \right].
 \end{split}
 \tag{IBC12}
\]
Indeed,
\[
 \begin{split}
 \operatorname{Re}\sum_{a,b}z_{ab}^2
    &=(k+1)^2\mu_2(d-g),\\
 \operatorname{Re}\sum_{a,b}z_{ab}^4
    &=(k+1)^2\{\mu_4(d^2+g^2)-6\mu_2^2dg\}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{IBC13}
\]
These identities follow by expanding each power and using the independent
finite sums over \(a\) and \(b\).  Multiplication by \(e\), and by
\(-e/2\) in the second line, proves (IBC11)--(IBC12).
For use when an additional signed term is useful, the next coefficient is
\[
 \begin{split}
 p_6
  &=\frac q3\{\mu_6(d^3-g^3)
                   -15\mu_4\mu_2d^2g+15\mu_4\mu_2dg^2\}\\
  &=\frac{q(d-g)}3
       \{\mu_6(d^2+dg+g^2)-15\mu_4\mu_2dg\}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{IBC14}
\]
The real terms of
\((u+iv)^6=u^6-15u^4v^2+15u^2v^4-v^6
  +i(6u^5v-20u^3v^3+6uv^5)\)
prove this formula after substituting \(u=n_a\delta\), \(v=n_b\gamma\).

\subsection{Finite remainder estimates preserving the root radii}

For \(s\geq1\) and \(0\leq t<1\), set
\[
 h_s(t)=-\log(1-t)-\sum_{r=1}^{s-1}\frac{t^r}{r}
       =\sum_{r=s}^{\infty}\frac{t^r}{r},\qquad
 F_s(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^j}{s+j}.
 \tag{IBC15}
\]
Then \(h_s(t)=t^sF_s(t)\), \(F_s(0)=1/s\), and \(F_s\) is
nondecreasing because every coefficient of its power series is
nonnegative.  Define the following finite sums on the original roots:
\[
 \begin{split}
 r_{ab}(y)&=\frac{n_a^2d+n_b^2g}{y^2},\\
 {\cal B}_s(y)&=e\sum_{a,b=0}^{k}h_s(r_{ab}(y)),\\
 M_{2s}&=e\sum_{a,b=0}^{k}|z_{ab}|^{2s}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{IBC16}
\]
For each \(s\geq1\), the exact series (IBC9) gives
\[
 \left|L_\chi(y)-\sum_{r=1}^{s-1}\frac{p_{2r}}{y^{2r}}\right|
 \leq {\cal B}_s(y)
 \leq\frac{M_{2s}}{|y|^{2s}}F_s(\tau)
 \leq\frac{M_{2s}}{s|y|^{2s}(1-\tau)}
 \leq\frac{q\tau^s}{s(1-\tau)}.
 \tag{IBC17}
\]
For the first inequality, take the absolute value of each term of the
absolutely convergent tail, use
\(|\operatorname{Re} z_{ab}^{2r}|\leq |z_{ab}|^{2r}\),
and sum \(\sum_{r\geq s}r_{ab}^r/r\).
For the second, use \(r_{ab}\leq\tau\) and the monotonicity of \(F_s\).
For the third, each coefficient \(1/(s+j)\) is at most \(1/s\).
For the last, \(|z_{ab}|^2\leq k^2(d+g)\) and there are exactly
\(e(k+1)^2=q\) roots counted with multiplicity.
This proves each comparison in (IBC17); the first bound is a finite
sum and retains the individual radii instead of replacing them by their
maximum.

In particular the remainder after the quadratic coefficient obeys the
claimed estimate with exactly the stated factor:
\[
 L_\chi(y)=\frac{p_2}{y^2}+E_2(y),\qquad
 |E_2(y)|\leq \frac{q\tau^2}{2(1-\tau)}.
 \tag{IBC18}
\]
Two sharper finite estimates, available from the same calculation, are
\[
 |E_2(y)|\leq {\cal B}_2(y)
       \leq \frac{M_4}{y^4}F_2(\tau)
       \leq q\{-\log(1-\tau)-\tau\},
 \tag{IBC19}
\]
where
\[
 M_4=q\{\mu_4(d^2+g^2)+2\mu_2^2dg\}.
 \tag{IBC20}
\]
To verify the last step of (IBC19), use
\(M_4/y^4\leq q\tau^2\) and \(\tau^2F_2(\tau)=h_2(\tau)\).
Expanding \((n_a^2d+n_b^2g)^2\) proves (IBC20).

Retaining the signed fourth-order term yields
\[
 L_\chi(y)=\frac{p_2}{y^2}+\frac{p_4}{y^4}+E_3(y),
 \quad
 |E_3(y)|\leq{\cal B}_3(y)
 \leq\frac{M_6}{|y|^6}F_3(\tau)
 \leq\frac{M_6}{3|y|^6(1-\tau)},
 \tag{IBC21}
\]
with the exact moment
\[
 M_6=q\{\mu_6(d^3+g^3)+3\mu_4\mu_2dg(d+g)\}.
 \tag{IBC22}
\]
This time expand \((n_a^2d+n_b^2g)^3\), and use (IBC6).
Consequently a signed enclosure for \(E_2\), which can be intersected
with (IBC19), is
\[
 \frac{p_4}{y^4}-{\cal B}_3(y)
 \leq E_2(y)\leq
 \frac{p_4}{y^4}+{\cal B}_3(y).
 \tag{IBC23}
\]
If (IBC14) is also retained, (IBC17) with \(s=4\) gives the finite
error \({\cal B}_4(y)\leq q\tau^4/[4(1-\tau)]\).
In the case \(d=g\), the computed coefficients explicitly read
\[
 p_2=p_6=0,\qquad
 p_4=\frac{2qK(K+2)}{15}\,d^2.
 \tag{IBC24}
\]
This is a consequence of (IBC11)--(IBC14), not the removal of any term
from the original polynomial.

\subsection{Exact second and fourth powers of the Gamma-factor offsets}

Retain the monic polynomial
\[
 f_\ell(S)=\prod_{j=0}^{\ell-1}(S-c-t_j),
 \qquad t_j=2j+\frac12.
 \tag{IBC25}
\]
For \(\ell=0\) it is the constant polynomial \(1\).
Its two needed power sums are
\[
 \begin{split}
 T_2:=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}t_j^2
   &=\frac{\ell(16\ell^2-12\ell-1)}{12}\\
   &=\frac{(k-1)(k^2-5k+3)}{48},
 \end{split}
 \tag{IBC26}
\]
and
\[
 \begin{split}
 T_4:=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}t_j^4
   &=\frac{\ell(768\ell^4-960\ell^3-160\ell^2+360\ell+7)}{240}\\
   &=\frac{(k-1)(3k^4-27k^3+53k^2+53k-75)}{960}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{IBC27}
\]
For completeness put \(S_r(n)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}j^r\).  The exact formulas
\[
 \begin{split}
 S_0(n)&=n,& S_1(n)&=\frac{n(n-1)}2,\\
 S_2(n)&=\frac{n(n-1)(2n-1)}6,&
 S_3(n)&=\frac{n^2(n-1)^2}{4},\\
 S_4(n)&=\frac{n(n-1)(2n-1)(3n^2-3n-1)}{30}
 \end{split}
 \tag{IBC28}
\]
are proved by finite differences: each right side vanishes at \(n=0\),
and its value at \(n+1\) minus its value at \(n\), after expansion, is
\(n^r\) for its respective \(r\).  Expanding the original offsets gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 T_2&=4S_2(\ell)+2S_1(\ell)+\ell/4,\\
 T_4&=16S_4(\ell)+16S_3(\ell)+6S_2(\ell)+S_1(\ell)+\ell/16.
 \end{split}
 \tag{IBC29}
\]
Substitute (IBC28), collect the displayed powers of \(\ell\), and then
substitute \(\ell=(k-1)/4\).  This proves both forms of
(IBC26)--(IBC27), including their zero values for the empty sums.

These constants control the actual Gamma polynomial on the same line
without an additional exterior restriction:
\[
 L_f(y):=\log\frac{|f_\ell(c+iy)|^2}{|y|^{2\ell}}
       =\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\log(1+t_j^2/y^2),
       \qquad y\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}.
 \tag{IBC30}
\]
For all such \(y\),
\[
 0\leq \frac{T_2}{y^2}-L_f(y)\leq\frac{T_4}{2y^4}.
 \tag{IBC31}
\]
After retaining the signed fourth-order coefficient one has the
stronger one-sided statement
\[
 \begin{split}
 L_f(y)&=\frac{T_2}{y^2}-\frac{T_4}{2y^4}+R_f(y),\\
 \sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}
       \frac{t_j^6}{3|y|^6(1+t_j^2/y^2)}
   &\leq R_f(y)\leq
       \sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\frac{t_j^6}{3|y|^6}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{IBC32}
\]
Indeed, for every real \(x\geq0\), elementary integration gives
\[
 x-\log(1+x)=\int_0^x\frac{t}{1+t}\,dt,\qquad
 \log(1+x)-x+\frac{x^2}{2}
       =\int_0^x\frac{t^2}{1+t}\,dt.
 \tag{IBC33}
\]
The first integral lies between \(0\) and \(x^2/2\).
The second lies between \(x^3/[3(1+x)]\) and \(x^3/3\), because
\(1/(1+x)\leq1/(1+t)\leq1\) on the entire integration interval.
Apply these inequalities to each \(x=t_j^2/y^2\) in (IBC30).
This proves (IBC31)--(IBC32) and keeps all offset factors.
