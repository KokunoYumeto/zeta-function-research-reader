\section{Exact rational construction of the joint Gamma certificate}
\label{sec:rational-gamma-certificate}
Fix integers $l\ge1,m\ge1$, and retain
\[
 k=4l+1,\quad c=k/2,\quad e=1+k(m-1),\quad q=(k+1)^2e,
 \quad d=\delta^2\in(0,1/4),\quad g=\gamma^2>4.
\]
The original coefficient is $S$, and $x=S-c$. Translation is the
explicit invertible coefficient map
\[
 S^n=\sum_{j=0}^n\binom njc^{n-j}x^j,\qquad
 x^n=\sum_{j=0}^n\binom nj(-c)^{n-j}S^j.\tag{RGC1}
\]
Both triangular matrices have determinant one. Every polynomial,
remainder and metric below is carried through these matrices; the
original source norm is retained.

\subsection{The complete packet and all polynomial coefficients}
In the translated variable the original packet is exactly
\[
 \chi(c+x)=\prod_{a,b\in\{1,3,\ldots,k\}}
 \bigl[(x^2+a^2d+b^2g)^2-4a^2dx^2\bigr]^e.\tag{RGC2}
\]
Indeed the factor displayed is the product of
$(x-a\delta-ib\gamma)$, $(x-a\delta+ib\gamma)$,
$(x+a\delta-ib\gamma)$ and $(x+a\delta+ib\gamma)$.
The positive odd integers index each quartet exactly once because
$k$ is odd. Its degree is $4((k+1)/2)^2e=q$, with all original
multiplicities present. Consequently every coefficient belongs to
$\mathbb Q[d,g]$.

Use the original monic sigma polynomials, written in the translated
coordinate:
\[
 p_0=1,\quad p_1=x,\quad
 p_{n+1}=xp_n+n(n-\tfrac12)p_{n-1},\qquad
 h_n=n!(\tfrac12)_n,\quad \omega_n^\sigma=c_\sigma h_n,
 \quad c_\sigma=\sqrt{2\pi}.\tag{RGC3}
\]
Thus $p_n\in\mathbb Q[x]$, $h_n\in\mathbb Q_{>0}$.
Let $v_n\in\mathbb Q[d,g]^q$ be the coefficient column of
$\operatorname{rem}_{\chi}p_n$ in $1,x,\ldots,x^{q-1}$.
These entries are polynomial because division by the monic
polynomial RGC2 uses no variable denominator. Let
\[
 w_n=(p_n(2j+\tfrac12))_{j=0}^{l-1}\in\mathbb Q^l.
 \tag{RGC4}
\]
This is evaluation at the original nodes $\alpha_j=c+2j+1/2$,
after the exact translation RGC1.

\subsection{Mass-preserving covariance identities}
For $M\ge q+l-1$ and $N\ge q-1$, define the following rational
matrices, retaining the original source covariance by its explicit
scalar factor:
\begin{align}
 \widehat K_N&=\sum_{n=0}^N v_nv_n^{\mathsf T}/h_n,
 &K_N&=c_\sigma^{-1}\widehat K_N,\\
 \widehat C_M&=\sum_{n=0}^M v_nw_n^{\mathsf T}/h_n,
 &C_M&=c_\sigma^{-1}\widehat C_M,\\
 \widehat A_M&=\sum_{n=0}^M w_nw_n^{\mathsf T}/h_n,
 &A_M&=c_\sigma^{-1}\widehat A_M.\tag{RGC5}
\end{align}
These equalities follow by expanding the actual inverse source
Gram in its complete orthogonal basis $p_0,\ldots,p_M$, whose
norms are exactly $c_\sigma h_n$. All vectors are real for
real $d,g$; transpose is therefore the actual Hermitian adjoint.
In particular
\[
 G_N^\sigma=c_\sigma\widehat K_N^{-1}.\tag{RGC6}
\]
The covariance $\widehat K_N$ is positive definite: its first
$q$ columns are the coefficient vectors of $p_0,\ldots,p_{q-1}$,
a triangular basis with diagonal one. The joint covariance of
$(v_n,w_n)$ is positive definite for $M\ge q+l-1$.
To prove this assertion with the original maps, the remainder map
to $\mathbb R[x]/\chi(c+x)$ and the evaluation map at the $l$
distinct real points $2j+1/2$ are jointly onto by monic division
and interpolation. Every root of RGC2 has nonzero imaginary
part, so those evaluations are coprime to $\chi$. Their joint
kernel in $\mathcal P_M$ is precisely $\chi f\mathcal P_{M-q-l}$.
Applying this onto map to the positive source Gram proves the
assertion and both strict Schur inequalities. No determinant
denominator used below vanishes on the stated parameter domain.

For $M=N+l$ put $U_N=[v_{N+1},\ldots,v_{N+l}]$ and
$D_N=\operatorname{diag}(h_{N+1},\ldots,h_{N+l})$.
The original metric of the first small endomorphism is
$\Omega_N=c_\sigma D_N$, and that of the second is
$A_M=c_\sigma^{-1}\widehat A_M$. Substitution into the original
joint-Schur formulas gives the exact equalities
\begin{align}
 X_N&=D_N^{-1}U_N^{\mathsf T}\widehat K_N^{-1}U_N,\\
 Y_N&=\widehat A_M^{-1}\widehat C_M^{\mathsf T}
            \widehat K_M^{-1}\widehat C_M.\tag{RGC7}
\end{align}
For example the three scalar factors in
$C_M^*G_M^\sigma C_M$ are
$c_\sigma^{-1}c_\sigma c_\sigma^{-1}=c_\sigma^{-1}$;
the inverse of $A_M$ supplies $c_\sigma$.
Thus RGC7 is an equality of the original endomorphisms after
the proved coordinate map, with their original forms still as
specified. Their entries belong to $\mathbb Q(d,g)$.
Self-adjointness is in these positive forms; ordinary Euclidean
symmetry of RGC7 is unnecessary. The actual spectrum of $X_N$
is nonnegative, and that of $Y_N$ lies in $[0,1)$ by the strict
joint Schur inequality already proved.

\subsection{A finite rational enclosure of the exact return}
Use the original endpoints
\[
 (N_0,N_1,N_2,N_3)=(q-1,q,2q-1,2q),\qquad
 (\varepsilon_0,\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2,\varepsilon_3)=(1,1,-1,-1).
\]
Set
\[
 s=2+\sum_{r=0}^3\operatorname{Tr}X_{N_r},\qquad
 Z_N=I-(I+X_N)/s.\tag{RGC8}
\]
All entries and all power traces of these eight endomorphisms
$Z_{N_r},Y_{N_r}$ are rational functions in $d,g$.
Since $\lambda_{\max}(X_N)\le\operatorname{Tr}X_N<s-1$,
the eigenvalues of $Z_N$ also lie in $[0,1)$.
For any such matrix $T$, and integer $p\ge1$, set
\[
 b_p(T)=\sum_{n=1}^p\frac{\operatorname{Tr}T^n}{n},\quad
 a_p(T)=\operatorname{Tr}T^p,\quad
 u_p(T)=b_p(T)+\frac{b_{2p}(T)-b_p(T)}{1-a_p(T)}.\tag{RGC9}
\]
The last expression is used at an order with $a_p(T)<1$.
Here is a direct proof of its bound. If the eigenvalues are
$t_i\in[0,1)$ and $r_p(t)=\sum_{n>p}t^n/n$, then
$r_{2p}(t)\le t^p r_p(t)$. Summing and using nonnegative
summands gives
\[
 \sum_i r_{2p}(t_i)\le a_p(T)\sum_i r_p(t_i).
\]
The identity
$\sum_i r_p(t_i)=b_{2p}(T)-b_p(T)+\sum_i r_{2p}(t_i)$
now proves
\[
 b_p(T)\le-\log\det(I-T)\le u_p(T).\tag{RGC10}
\]
Because $a_p(T)\to0$, a finite admitted order exists. Both
bounds converge to the same logarithm as $p\to\infty$.

For independently chosen admitted orders in each of the eight
blocks, let $L_r$ be the sum of its two lower bounds and $U_r$
the sum of its two upper bounds. The exact four-endpoint identity
then proves
\[
 L_2+L_3-U_0-U_1
 \ \le R_k^0-R_k^\sigma\le\
 U_2+U_3-L_0-L_1.\tag{RGC11}
\]
Every finite bound in RGC11 is a rational function evaluated at
the actual $d,g$. In particular for rational $d,g$ the entire
certificate can be computed in exact rational arithmetic.
The source factor $c_\sigma$, the tensor mass $C_k$, and
$\det M_f$ disappeared only through RGC5--7 and the already
proved signed cancellation of their four original contributions.
No approximate Gamma values or approximate period matrices are
inputs to this Gamma-reference certificate.

This also proves a terminating enclosure procedure on every
fixed closed rational rectangle strictly inside
$(0,1/4)\times(4,\infty)$. Indeed the entries in RGC7--8 are
continuous there and every denominator is nonzero. Strict Schur
positivity and compactness imply a common spectral radius
$\rho<1$ for the eight continuous families. Hence their
$a_p\le l\rho^p$ are uniformly less than one at a sufficiently
large finite order, and the bound widths converge uniformly to
zero. Rational interval arithmetic on subdivided rectangles
certifies these inequalities in finitely many subdivisions:
polynomial interval bounds converge to point values as the
diameters decrease; compactness gives a positive lower bound
for the finitely many denominators and for $1-a_p$.
The same argument bounds each rational endpoint value to any
prescribed positive interval-arithmetic error. Increasing $p$
and subdividing supplies any prescribed positive excess width
over the actual variation of the return on that rectangle.
One must retain this variation for a parameter rectangle; an
interval cannot in general shrink to a single value until the
parameter region shrinks as well.

These claims concern the exact original Gamma comparison on
the full packet parameter domain. Evaluating a rational pair
$(d,g)$ is a calibration of that family, and makes no claim
that its roots are zeros of $2\xi$. The original theta norm,
its arithmetic defect, its full-unit map and its mixed support
are returned by the separate exact receiving equations. The
rational construction supplies their specified Gamma term.

\subsection{Exact signed determinant and a geometric scalar certificate}
There is a faster finite evaluation route at rational $d,g$ that
also retains cancellation before any logarithm is bounded. Form
the positive rational function
\[
 \mathcal D_k(d,g)=\prod_{r=0}^3
 \left\{\det(I+X_{N_r})\det(I-Y_{N_r})\right\}^{\varepsilon_r}.
 \tag{RGC12}
\]
Positivity and RGC7 prove that this lies in
$\mathbb Q(d,g)_{>0}$, and the exact return is
$R_k^0-R_k^\sigma=\log\mathcal D_k(d,g)$ by the four original
signs. In particular no separate endpoint-log error need be added
when this signed product has first been computed exactly.

For any positive rational $r$, there is a unique integer $h$
such that $r=2^h s$, $1\le s<2$. It is obtained by comparing
the positive numerator and denominator of $r$ with integer
powers of two. This is an exact finite comparison of integers.
Put $t=(s-1)/(s+1)\in[0,1/3)$, and for integers $p\ge0$ set
\[
 B_p(t)=2\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\frac{t^{2j+1}}{2j+1},\qquad
 E_p(t)=\frac{2t^{2p+1}}{(2p+1)(1-t^2)}.
 \tag{RGC13}
\]
The sum at $p=0$ is zero. The identity
$\log s=2\int_0^t(1-z^2)^{-1}dz$ and the geometric series give
\[
 B_p(t)\le\log s\le B_p(t)+E_p(t).
 \tag{RGC14}
\]
The proof is termwise integration of the nonnegative series;
every omitted denominator is at least $2p+1$, so its geometric
numerator sum is bounded by $E_p(t)$. The same formula at
$t=1/3$ bounds $\log2$. Define $b=B_p(t)$, $e=E_p(t)$,
$b_2=B_p(1/3)$ and $e_2=E_p(1/3)$. Restoring the exact
integer exponent gives the entirely rational interval
\[
 \begin{cases}
 h b_2+b\ \le\log r\le h(b_2+e_2)+b+e,&h\ge0,\\
 h(b_2+e_2)+b\ \le\log r\le h b_2+b+e,&h<0.
 \end{cases}\tag{RGC15}
\]
Its width is $|h|e_2+e$. Since $0\le t\le1/3$,
\[
 0\le |h|e_2+e
 \le\frac{3(|h|+1)}{4(2p+1)}9^{-p}
 \le\frac{3(|h|+1)}4 9^{-p}.
 \tag{RGC16}
\]
Every quantity in RGC13--16 is rational. Given any positive
rational tolerance $\epsilon$, the least nonnegative integer
$p$ satisfying $9^p\ge3(|h|+1)/(4\epsilon)$ is a finite
stopping choice with width at most $\epsilon$. It can be found
by exact integer multiplication and comparison. Applying this
procedure to $r=\mathcal D_k(d,g)$ proves a geometric rational
enclosure for the complete signed return. Small positive
Schur gaps remain in the exact rational product; they do not
make the scalar expansion parameter approach one. The exponent
$h$ and all rational numerator/denominator sizes are retained
in the construction. This proof asserts no polynomial bit
complexity for forming the original large quotient matrices.

For the full degree-$36$ calibration $k=5,m=1,d=1/16,g=9$,
all four cutoffs $35,36,71,72$ were evaluated in exact rational
arithmetic, including the complete original relation-Gram
dimensions $0,1,36,37$. The independent identity checked at
each endpoint is
\[
 \frac{D_{1,N}}{\mathfrak R_N}
       (1+X_N)(1-Y_N)=\chi(c+1/2)^2,
 \qquad D_{1,N}=\prod_{n=0}^N(n+1/2)(n+3/2).
\]
The fixed multiplication determinant on the right cancels
with the four signs. Exact rational data, the full coefficient
algorithm and the finite logarithm proof are supplied in
\texttt{exact\_rational/}. This calibration confirms the
opposite signs of the incoming return and the GSR functional,
on the full stipulated polynomial degrees; it asserts no
zeta-zero coordinates.
