This calculation supplies the exact coordinate morphisms between the
incoming joint-Schur formula and the existing GCR and GSR formulas.
It then uses the already exactly computable low endpoints to remove
four unnecessary trace approximations from the incoming certificate.
All degrees, source masses, full confluent multiplicities and mixed
covariance factors remain present. No statement below asserts the
existence of a zero of $\xi$ outside the critical line.

\section{Original finite source and its orthogonal polynomials}

Fix integers $l\geq1$, $m\geq1$ and real parameters
$0<\delta<1/2$, $\gamma>2$. Retain
\[
\begin{gathered}
 k=4l+1,\quad c=k/2,\quad e=1+k(m-1),\quad q=e(k+1)^2,\\
 \lambda_{ab}=c+(2a-k)\delta+i(2b-k)\gamma,\qquad
 \chi(S)=\prod_{a,b=0}^k(S-\lambda_{ab})^e,\\
 t_j=2j+\tfrac12,\quad \alpha_j=c+t_j,\quad
 f(S)=\prod_{j=0}^{l-1}(S-\alpha_j),\quad
 E=\C[S]/(\chi),\quad \Pp_N=\C[S]_{\leq N}.
\end{gathered}\tag{JSA1}
\]
Negative-degree polynomial spaces are zero. All quotient coordinates
on $E$ are the original increasing monomials $1,S,\ldots,S^{q-1}$.
Let $J_N:\Pp_N\to E$ be monic remainder. For $N\geq q-1$ it
is onto and has kernel $\chi\Pp_{N-q}$: polynomial division proves
both assertions, including the zero kernel at $N=q-1$.

The two unchanged measures on the original line $S=c+iy$ are
\[
\begin{gathered}
 d\sigma(y)=\frac{|\Gamma(1/4+iy/2)|^2}{2\pi}\,dy,
 \quad c_\sigma=\sqrt{2\pi},\quad c_a=2^{1-2a}\Gamma(2a),\\
 dm_0(y)=\frac{c_\sigma^k}{c_{k/4}}
       \frac{|\Gamma(k/4+iy/2)|^2}{2\pi}\,dy,
 \quad C_k=\frac{c_\sigma^k}{4^l c_{k/4}}
          =\frac{(2\pi)^{k/2}}{\sqrt2\,\Gamma(k/2)}.
\end{gathered}\tag{JSA2}
\]
Gamma recurrence, applied before taking an absolute value, gives
\[
 \frac{\Gamma(l+1/4+iy/2)}{\Gamma(1/4+iy/2)}
  =(i/2)^l\prod_{j=0}^{l-1}(y-it_j).
\]
For real $y$, $f(c+iy)=i^l\prod_j(y+it_j)$, so
\[
 dm_0=C_k|f(c+iy)|^2d\sigma.\tag{JSA3}
\]
The conjugate products have been related only on this real integration
line. Multiplication $T_{f,N}:\Pp_N\to\Pp_{N+l}$ is injective
and satisfies $H_N^0=C_kT_{f,N}^*H_{N+l}^\sigma T_{f,N}$ in
the increasing monomial frames. Here $H$ always denotes the actual
Gram for its stated measure.

For completeness the original mass and polynomial norms can be
calculated directly. For $a>0$, the Euler integrals for the two Gamma
factors, with $u+v=w$ and $r=\log(u/v)$, have Jacobian
$w e^r/(1+e^r)^2$. Integrating $w$ therefore gives
\[
 |\Gamma(a+iy/2)|^2
  =2\Gamma(2a)\int_{\mathbb R}
       e^{iyt}(2\cosh t)^{-2a}\,dt.
\]
Fourier inversion gives the characteristic function
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}e^{isy}
    \frac{|\Gamma(a+iy/2)|^2}{2\pi}\,dy
   =2\Gamma(2a)(2\cosh s)^{-2a}.\tag{JSA4}
\]
These integrals are absolutely convergent; the integrand on the
right before Fourier inversion is smooth with exponentially
decaying derivatives, so the usual Fourier inversion follows also
by an integrable Gaussian regularization and dominated convergence.
At $a=1/4$ this is $c_\sigma(\cosh s)^{-1/2}$.

Define monic $p_n$ by
\[
 \sum_{n\geq0}p_n(S)\frac{z^n}{n!}
   =(1-z^2)^{-1/4}\exp((S-c)\operatorname{arctanh}z).
\tag{JSA5}
\]
For real $z,w$ sufficiently close to zero, JSA4 and
$\cosh(\operatorname{arctanh}z-\operatorname{arctanh}w)
=(1-zw)/\sqrt{(1-z^2)(1-w^2)}$ show that the integral of the
first generating function times the conjugate second generating
function is $c_\sigma(1-zw)^{-1/2}$. Differentiation under the
integral is justified by the exponential Gamma decay and the
bounded parameters. Comparing coefficients proves
\[
 \langle p_n,p_r\rangle_\sigma
   =\boldsymbol1_{n=r}\omega_n,
 \quad\omega_n=c_\sigma n!(1/2)_n,
 \quad p_{n+1}=(S-c)p_n+n(n-1/2)p_{n-1}.
\tag{JSA6}
\]
The recurrence also follows by differentiating JSA5. For general
$a>0$ the generating function
$(1-z^2)^{-a}\exp((S-c)\operatorname{arctanh}z)$ has integrated
product $c_a(1-zw)^{-2a}$ by JSA4. Its degree-$n$ monic norm is
therefore $c_a n!(2a)_n$. Taking $a=k/4$ and multiplying by the
original factor $c_\sigma^k/c_{k/4}$ gives the monic norms for $dm_0$,
\[
 \omega_n^0=c_\sigma^k n!\frac{\Gamma(n+k/2)}{\Gamma(k/2)}.
\]
Every monic triangular change has determinant one. Hence
\[
 \frac{\det H_N^f}{\det H_N^\sigma}
   =D_{l,N}:=\prod_{a=0}^N\prod_{r=0}^{2l-1}(a+r+1/2),
 \quad H_N^f=T_{f,N}^*H_{N+l}^\sigma T_{f,N}.
\tag{JSA7}
\]
Indeed $H_N^0=C_kH_N^f$ and
$\omega_a^0/(C_k\omega_a)=\Gamma(a+2l+1/2)/\Gamma(a+1/2)$,
which proves every displayed factor.

\section{The exact factorial morphism on all primary jets}

Order the pairs $(a,b)$ lexicographically and the derivatives within
each root increasingly. Define
\[
 \mathcal E^{\rm d}_{(\lambda,r),n}
    =\begin{cases}\binom nr\lambda^{n-r}&n\geq r,\\0&n<r,
      \end{cases}
 \quad
 D_\chi=\diag_{\lambda,\,0\leq r<e}(r!),
 \quad\mathcal E^{\rm r}=D_\chi\mathcal E^{\rm d}.
\tag{JSA8}
\]
Here $\mathcal E^{\rm d}:E\to\bigoplus_\lambda\C^e$ is the
full divided-derivative evaluation, and $\mathcal E^{\rm r}$
is the full raw-derivative evaluation. Both are invertible:
vanishing of all derivatives through order $e-1$ at every root
means divisibility by the degree-$q$ polynomial $\chi$, and a
representative of degree less than $q$ must then be zero.
Since their source and target dimensions are $q$, injectivity
proves surjectivity. The exact isomorphism of their targets is
$D_\chi$, with inverse $\diag(1/r!)$; no derivative order is
discarded.

For multiplication by $f$ the two block matrices are
\[
 (F^{\rm d}_\lambda)_{rs}=f^{(r-s)}(\lambda)/(r-s)!,
 \quad
 (F^{\rm r}_\lambda)_{rs}=\binom rs f^{(r-s)}(\lambda)
 \quad(r\geq s),
\tag{JSA9}
\]
and zero above the diagonal or between different roots. The
Taylor coefficient multiplication and the raw Leibniz rule
prove these formulas directly. In particular
\[
 F^{\rm r}=D_\chi F^{\rm d}D_\chi^{-1},\qquad
 M_f=(\mathcal E^{\rm d})^{-1}F^{\rm d}\mathcal E^{\rm d}
    =(\mathcal E^{\rm r})^{-1}F^{\rm r}\mathcal E^{\rm r},
 \qquad \det M_f=\prod_{a,b}f(\lambda_{ab})^e.
\tag{JSA10}
\]
The imaginary parts $(2b-k)\gamma$ are nonzero because $k$ is
odd, whereas all $\alpha_j$ are real. Every diagonal factor in
JSA10 is therefore nonzero. The inverse of $M_f$ is the remainder
of the Bezout inverse of $f$ modulo $\chi$. This same map obeys
$\eta M_f=M_{f(\sum s_i)}\eta$ on the original weighted cyclic
inclusion: evaluate the polynomial in the commuting sum operator
and retain the commuting original factor $\upsilon_h^{\otimes k}$.

For any onto observation $A$ from a source with Gram $H>0$, write
$K=AH^{-1}A^*$. A target-coordinate change $T$ produces
\[
 K'=TKT^*,\quad G'=(K')^{-1}=T^{-*}K^{-1}T^{-1},\quad
 H^{-1}(TA)^*G'T=H^{-1}A^*K^{-1}.
\tag{JSA11}
\]
These identities follow by multiplying the displayed inverses.
Thus the minimum source representative is the same vector after
the exact target-coordinate isomorphism. Applied to JSA8, the
joint covariance transforms by $\diag(D_\chi,I_l)$, and both
its arithmetic Schur complement and its inverse transform by
the corresponding congruences. In particular raw jets introduce
no hidden factorial into the original quotient metric.

\section{Joint covariance and the complete GCR cross factors}

Fix $N\geq q-1$ and $M=N+l$. Define
\[
 Z_M:P\mapsto(P(\alpha_0),\ldots,P(\alpha_{l-1})),\quad
 \mathscr J_M=\binom{J_M}{Z_M},\quad
 \mathscr J_M(H_M^\sigma)^{-1}\mathscr J_M^*
   =\begin{pmatrix}K_M&C_M\\C_M^*&A_M\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{JSA12}
\]
Interpolation modulo the coprime product $\chi f$ proves that
$\mathscr J_M$ is onto and has kernel $\chi f\Pp_{M-q-l}$.
Its covariance is positive definite: a nonzero target covector
pulls back to a nonzero source covector by surjectivity, and
$H_M^{-1}>0$. Completing squares proves positivity of both
\[
 S_M=K_M-C_MA_M^{-1}C_M^*,\qquad
 L_M=A_M-C_M^*K_M^{-1}C_M.
\tag{JSA13}
\]
The source minimum for $Ax=v$ is
$H^{-1}A^*(AH^{-1}A^*)^{-1}v$: it has observation $v$ and is
$H$-orthogonal to $\ker A$, which proves its unique minimum
property by expansion of the squared norm. The map $T_{f,N}$
identifies the source constraint $J_NP=v$ with the joint
constraint $(J_M(fP),Z_M(fP))=(M_fv,0)$. Conversely the latter
constraint implies $f\mid Q$ and $Q=fP$ for a unique
$P\in\Pp_N$; invertibility of $M_f$ then gives $J_NP=v$.
Consequently the exact original quotient and its section are
\[
\begin{gathered}
 G_N^0=C_k M_f^*S_M^{-1}M_f,\\
 R_N^0=T_{f,N}^{-1}(H_M^\sigma)^{-1}\mathscr J_M^*
   \begin{pmatrix}K_M&C_M\\C_M^*&A_M\end{pmatrix}^{-1}
   \binom{M_f}{0}.
\end{gathered}\tag{JSA14}
\]
The inverse $T_{f,N}^{-1}$ is used only on $\ker Z_M=f\Pp_N$.
Direct block multiplication yields
\[
 G_N^0=C_kM_f^*K_M^{-1}M_f
       +C_k(C_M^*K_M^{-1}M_f)^*L_M^{-1}
                    (C_M^*K_M^{-1}M_f).
\tag{JSA15}
\]
In particular the finite update retains the actual $C_M$ on
both sides of the conditional inverse.

To identify every GCR factor, set
\[
\begin{gathered}
 U=[Jp_{N+1},\ldots,Jp_{N+l}],\quad
 \Omega=\diag(\omega_{N+1},\ldots,\omega_{N+l}),\quad
 G_N^\sigma=K_N^{-1},\\
 X=\Omega^{-1}U^*G_N^\sigma U,\quad
 Y=A_M^{-1}C_M^*K_M^{-1}C_M,\quad
 K_M=K_N+U\Omega^{-1}U^*.
\end{gathered}\tag{JSA16}
\]
The last equality is exactly the difference of the two finite
orthogonal-polynomial covariance sums. Put $E_d=\mathcal E^{\rm d}$.
The GCR divided-jet matrices, with their original meanings, are
\[
 \mathcal K_N=E_dK_NE_d^*,\quad
 \mathcal U=E_dU\Omega^{-1/2},\quad
 \mathcal V=E_dC_MA_M^{-1/2},\quad
 \mathcal S=E_dS_ME_d^*.
\tag{JSA17}
\]
Thus GCR's $K+\mathcal U\mathcal U^*-\mathcal V\mathcal V^*$
is literally $\mathcal S$. Its two positive factors satisfy
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal A&=I+\mathcal U^*\mathcal K_N^{-1}\mathcal U
    =\Omega^{1/2}(I+X)\Omega^{-1/2},\\
 \mathcal B&=I-\mathcal V^*(\mathcal K_N+
                        \mathcal U\mathcal U^*)^{-1}\mathcal V
    =A_M^{1/2}(I-Y)A_M^{-1/2},\\
 \mathcal B&=I-\mathcal V^*\mathcal K_N^{-1}\mathcal V
   +\mathcal V^*\mathcal K_N^{-1}\mathcal U\mathcal A^{-1}
                   \mathcal U^*\mathcal K_N^{-1}\mathcal V.
\end{aligned}\tag{JSA18}
\]
The last formula follows by multiplying out the inverse of
$\mathcal K_N+\mathcal U\mathcal U^*$; the preceding equality
proves that it is exactly the incoming mixed conditional factor.
It contains the full cross product of the new-degree columns and
the extra-node columns. Positivity follows from JSA13.
Consequently $X$ is positive self-adjoint in $\Omega$, $Y$ is
positive self-adjoint in $A_M$, and its eigenvalues are strictly
less than one. These are metric similarities, not assertions
that their raw matrices are Hermitian in an unspecified metric.

Write $\mathcal W=(\mathcal U\ \mathcal V)$ and
$\mathcal J=\diag(I_l,-I_l)$. Computing a block determinant
in either order proves $\det(I+AB)=\det(I+BA)$, even for
rectangular $A,B$. Applied here it proves
\[
 \mathfrak d_N:=\frac{\det S_M}{\det K_N}
 =\det(I_{2l}+\mathcal J\mathcal W^*\mathcal K_N^{-1}\mathcal W)
 =\det(I+X)\det(I-Y)>0.
\tag{JSA19}
\]
In particular the incoming notation $d_N=\log\det(I+X)$
is the logarithm of the degree-increase factor alone, whereas
the GCR notation $d_N$ is the entire positive number
$\mathfrak d_N$. Their exact relation is
$\log\mathfrak d_N=d_N-\beta_N$ with
$\beta_N=-\log\det(I-Y)$.

\section{Original relation map and the signed endpoint equality}

Let $B_N$ consist of the original columns
$\chi,\chi S,\ldots,\chi S^{N-q}$. When $N=q-1$ it has no
columns. The minimum sections satisfy
\[
\begin{gathered}
 R_N^0=R_N^\sigma-
 B_N(B_N^*H_N^0B_N)^{-1}B_N^*H_N^0R_N^\sigma,\\
 (R_N^\sigma)^*H_N^0R_N^\sigma-G_N^0
  =(R_N^\sigma-R_N^0)^*H_N^0(R_N^\sigma-R_N^0).
\end{gathered}\tag{JSA20}
\]
Indeed the first vector has the same remainder and is
$H_N^0$-orthogonal to every column of $B_N$, so it is the
unique minimum. Expanding the square and using that
orthogonality proves the second identity. At the empty
relation space both correction expressions are zero.

For $r_N=N+1-q$, retain the full relation determinant
\[
 \mathfrak R_N=
 \frac{\det\Gr_\sigma(f\chi,f\chi S,\ldots,f\chi S^{r_N-1})}
      {\det\Gr_\sigma(\chi,\chi S,\ldots,\chi S^{r_N-1})}.
\tag{JSA21}
\]
Both empty determinants are one. The frame
$(1,\ldots,S^{q-1},\chi,\ldots,\chi S^{N-q})$ has determinant
one in increasing monomial coordinates because $\chi$ is monic.
In this frame elimination of the relation cross block gives
$\det H=\det(B_N^*HB_N)\det G$. The quotient block is $G$
by the proved minimum property. Apply the identity to $H_N^f$
and $H_N^\sigma$ and then use JSA7 and JSA14. This gives
\[
 \log\mathfrak d_N
   =2\log|\det M_f|-\log D_{l,N}+\log\mathfrak R_N.
\tag{JSA22}
\]
Thus the two $l$-dimensional determinants, the GCR $2l$ determinant,
and the GSR determinant on the entire original relation subspace
have exactly the same endpoint value under JSA22.

Set $N_0=q-1,N_1=q,N_2=2q-1,N_3=2q$ and
$(\epsilon_0,\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2,\epsilon_3)=(1,1,-1,-1)$.
Taking determinants in JSA14 first gives
\[
 \log\det G_N^0-\log\det G_N^\sigma
  =q\log C_k+2\log|\det M_f|-\log\mathfrak d_N.
\tag{JSA23}
\]
Only after constructing all four endpoints do the first two
terms cancel. If $\mathfrak T=\sum_r\epsilon_r
(\log\det G_{N_r}^0-\log\det G_{N_r}^\sigma)$, the incoming
return is consequently
\[
 R_k^0-R_k^\sigma=-\mathfrak T
  =\sum_{r=0}^3\epsilon_r\log\mathfrak d_{N_r}
  =\sum_{r=0}^3\epsilon_r
       \{\log\det(I+X_{N_r})+\log\det(I-Y_{N_r})\}.
\tag{JSA24}
\]
This verifies the four signs against the original GCR and GSR
definitions, including the minus sign between this return and
$\mathfrak T$.

There is also an exact morphism to the incoming conditional
evaluation covariance. The covariance of $Z_M$ restricted to
$\ker J_M$ is $L_M$: subtract from $H_M^{-1}$ its minimum-section
part $H_M^{-1}J_M^*K_M^{-1}J_MH_M^{-1}$ and multiply by $Z_M$
and $Z_M^*$. The remaining operator is the inverse metric on
the embedded subspace $\ker J_M$. Identifying that subspace by
multiplication by $\chi$ therefore proves
\[
 L_M=\diag(\chi(\alpha_j))\,
       A_{M-q}^{\chi\sigma}\,
       \diag(\overline{\chi(\alpha_j)}).
\tag{JSA25}
\]
Here $A_n^{\chi\sigma}$ uses the exact Gram of
$|\chi(c+iy)|^2d\sigma$ on $\Pp_n$. The factors $\chi(\alpha_j)$
are nonzero by coprimality. No cross covariance has been removed
in identifying the restricted metric.

\section{A narrower finite certificate from the two exact low endpoints}

At the two low endpoints JSA21 gives, exactly,
\[
 \mathfrak R_{q-1}=1,\qquad
 \mathfrak R_q=\mathfrak r_\chi:={
    \int |f(c+iy)\chi(c+iy)|^2d\sigma(y)\over
    \int |\chi(c+iy)|^2d\sigma(y)}>0.
\tag{JSA26}
\]
These are finite original polynomial moments, whose values can
also be calculated by expanding in the $p_n$ basis and JSA6.
In particular no low-endpoint logarithmic series is needed.
Their exact sum is
\[
 E_{\rm low}:=\log\mathfrak d_{q-1}+\log\mathfrak d_q
  =4\log|\det M_f|-\log D_{l,q-1}-\log D_{l,q}
                        +\log\mathfrak r_\chi.
\tag{JSA27}
\]

For direct comparison with the incoming certificate retain its
same common scalar
$s=2+\sum_{r=0}^3\Tr X_{N_r}$ and put
$Z_N=I-(I+X_N)/s$. Each $Z_N$ is positive self-adjoint in
$\Omega_N$ and has spectrum in $[0,1)$: every eigenvalue of
$X_N$ is nonnegative and no larger than $\Tr X_N\leq s-2$.
Together with JSA18 this proves the same assertion for $Y_N$
in its own metric. For any such matrix $T$ define
\[
 b_p(T)=\sum_{n=1}^p{\Tr T^n\over n},\quad
 a_p(T)=\Tr T^p,\quad
 u_p(T)=b_p(T)+{b_{2p}(T)-b_p(T)\over1-a_p(T)}.
\tag{JSA28}
\]
There is a finite $p$ with $a_p(T)<1$, because if $\rho<1$ is
the largest eigenvalue then $a_p(T)\leq l\rho^p\to0$.
For $0\leq x<1$ put $r_p(x)=\sum_{n>p}x^n/n$. Termwise,
$r_{2p}(x)\leq x^p r_p(x)$. Sum over the eigenvalues; the
largest $x^p$ is at most $a_p(T)$. Writing the total tail as
the sum from $p+1$ to $2p$ plus the tail above $2p$ yields
\[
 b_p(T)\leq-\log\det(I-T)\leq u_p(T),
\tag{JSA29}
\]
by moving $a_p$ times the tail to the left. Both bounds converge
to the exact value as $p\to\infty$ along admitted orders.

For $r=2,3$ choose admitted orders independently on $Z_{N_r}$
and $Y_{N_r}$ and set
\[
 L_r=b_{p_r}(Z_{N_r})+b_{q_r}(Y_{N_r}),\quad
 U_r=u_{p_r}(Z_{N_r})+u_{q_r}(Y_{N_r}).
\]
The complete return has the following finite enclosure:
\[
 \boxed{
 E_{\rm low}-2l\log s+L_2+L_3
 \ \leq R_k^0-R_k^\sigma\leq
 E_{\rm low}-2l\log s+U_2+U_3.}
\tag{JSA30}
\]
To prove it, use
$\log\mathfrak d_N=l\log s+
\log\det(I-Z_N)+\log\det(I-Y_N)$
at the two high endpoints in JSA24 and substitute JSA27 at
the two low endpoints. JSA29 then proves both bounds. This
uses only four trace series, each with its full specified
original metric and mixed factor.

The comparison with the incoming eight-series certificate is
exact. If the incoming low-endpoint bounds are $[L_0,U_0]$
and $[L_1,U_1]$ for the corresponding positive log tails, their
true sum is $2l\log s-E_{\rm low}$. It belongs to
$[L_0+L_1,U_0+U_1]$. Therefore both endpoints in JSA30 are
inside the incoming interval
$[L_2+L_3-U_0-U_1,U_2+U_3-L_0-L_1]$. Its width decreases by
exactly $(U_0-L_0)+(U_1-L_1)$ when the same high-endpoint
orders and exact low moments are used. If numerical moment
values are used, their proved errors must be added to JSA30;
the zero-width statement refers to the exact quantities JSA26.

For a direct combination with the existing relation estimates,
write
\[
 C_{\rm rel}=\log D_{l,q-1}+\log D_{l,q}
       -\log\mathfrak r_\chi-\log D_{l,2q-1}-\log D_{l,2q}.
\]
JSA22 and JSA24 prove the exact equality
\[
 R_k^0-R_k^\sigma=-C_{\rm rel}
                      -\log\mathfrak R_{2q-1}-\log\mathfrak R_{2q}.
\tag{JSA31}
\]
Thus the proved GSR bounds $\ell_N\leq\log\mathfrak R_N\leq u_N$
on these very same relation determinants give the interval
$[-C_{\rm rel}-u_{2q-1}-u_{2q},
  -C_{\rm rel}-\ell_{2q-1}-\ell_{2q}]$.
Intersecting this interval with JSA30 is justified by JSA31,
not by treating the two constructions as independent models.
All surviving original-source error terms still enter the
arithmetic interval with their actual proved original-degree
estimates. A fixed-input convergent certificate alone makes
no tensor-uniform assertion.

\section{The reduced certificate also has exact rational bounds}

The rational construction RGC1--11 is valid for the full original
parameter domain. Its exact factorization is, with $d=\delta^2$
and $g=\gamma^2$,
\[
 \chi(c+x)=\prod_{a,b\in\{1,3,\ldots,k\}}
  \big((x^2+a^2d+b^2g)^2-4a^2dx^2\big)^e.
\tag{JSA32}
\]
Expanding the four original factors with roots
$a\delta+ib\gamma$, $a\delta-ib\gamma$,
$-a\delta+ib\gamma$, $-a\delta-ib\gamma$ gives the displayed
factor. Every quartet is included once, so its degree is
$4((k+1)/2)^2e=q$ and its coefficients lie in $\mathbb Q[d,g]$.
Translation by $c$ is the exact triangular binomial map with
inverse translation by $-c$; both determinants are one.

In these coordinates $p_n(c+x)\in\mathbb Q[x]$, and the
coefficient columns of their remainders modulo JSA32 lie in
$\mathbb Q[d,g]^q$. Their evaluation columns at $x=t_j$ lie
in $\mathbb Q^l$. Write $h_n=\omega_n/c_\sigma=n!(1/2)_n$.
All covariance sums are $c_\sigma^{-1}$ times rational matrices:
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \widehat K_N=\sum_{n=0}^N v_nv_n^{\mathsf T}/h_n,
 \quad\widehat C_M=\sum_{n=0}^M v_nw_n^{\mathsf T}/h_n,
 \quad\widehat A_M=\sum_{n=0}^M w_nw_n^{\mathsf T}/h_n,\\
 K_N=c_\sigma^{-1}\widehat K_N,\quad
 C_M=c_\sigma^{-1}\widehat C_M,\quad
 A_M=c_\sigma^{-1}\widehat A_M.
\end{gathered}\tag{JSA33}
\]
Here the coefficient vectors are real, so transpose is their
actual adjoint. JSA12--13 prove that the inverse denominators
never vanish on $0<d<1/4$, $g>4$. If
$D_N=\diag(h_{N+1},\ldots,h_{N+l})$, substituting the mass
factors of JSA33 and $\Omega_N=c_\sigma D_N$ in JSA16 gives
\[
 X_N=D_N^{-1}U_N^{\mathsf T}\widehat K_N^{-1}U_N,\qquad
 Y_N=\widehat A_M^{-1}\widehat C_M^{\mathsf T}
                    \widehat K_M^{-1}\widehat C_M.
\tag{JSA34}
\]
These are exact equalities of the original endomorphisms and
their retained metrics; in particular their entries and all
power traces lie in $\mathbb Q(d,g)$.

The low-endpoint constant in JSA30 has the exact expression
\[
 E_{\rm low}-2l\log s=\log H_{\rm low},\qquad
 H_{\rm low}=
 \frac{|\det M_f|^4\mathfrak r_\chi}
      {D_{l,q-1}D_{l,q}s^{2l}}\in\mathbb Q(d,g)_{>0}.
\tag{JSA35}
\]
To prove rationality of each factor, $\det M_f$ is the
determinant of polynomial multiplication in the rational
remainder algebra of JSA32, so it is a real polynomial in
$d,g$. It is nonzero by JSA10. The source Gram for real

\section{Exact coefficient map for the incoming mixed-word receiver}

The incoming reduced pair receiver also has an exact dictionary
to the existing MWR receiver. Retain the incoming nodal order

 E_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&-i\gamma\\1&i\gamma\end{pmatrix},\qquad

 z_0=A+i\gamma B,\quad A,B\in\mathbb R.
\]
The actual reflected unit values give

\[
 E_0^{-1}U_+^{\rm nod}E_0=AI+BJ,\qquad
 E_0^{-1}U_-^{\rm nod}E_0=AI-BJ,
 \quad\det(AI\pm BJ)=A^2+\gamma^2B^2>0.
\tag{JSA39}
\]

independent phase for the two legs has been supplied. With the

 T_\pm=e^{F_\pm}M(AI\pm BJ)E_0^{-1},\\

\end{gathered}\tag{JSA40}
