The calculation below retains every root and multiplicity of the
original sum packet, its original coefficient, the Gamma source mass,
and both high relation degrees. It first proves the density expansion,
then transports it through the actual polynomial multiplication map.
Finally it bounds both omitted integration regions relative to the
retained segment, so that the complete relation determinants have a
proved error $o(q)$ in the stated growing family. The resulting
explicit integral determinants remain to be evaluated; no value is
assigned to them by this comparison.

\section{Every original root and its convergent exterior expansion}

Fix $0<\delta<1/2$, $\gamma>2$, integers $m,l\geq1$, and set
\[
\begin{gathered}
 d=\delta^2,\quad g=\gamma^2,\quad k=4l+1,\quad
 c=k/2,\quad e=1+k(m-1),\quad q=e(k+1)^2,\\
 \omega_{ab}=(2a-k)\delta+i(2b-k)\gamma,
 \quad \lambda_{ab}=c+\omega_{ab},\\
 \chi(S)=\prod_{a,b=0}^k(S-\lambda_{ab})^e,
 \quad R_*=k\sqrt{d+g},\quad
 f(S)=\prod_{j=0}^{l-1}(S-c-t_j),\quad t_j=2j+1/2.
\end{gathered}\tag{BRD1}
\]
Both reflections of each root and its conjugate occur with exactly
the same multiplicity. In particular every odd power sum vanishes.
Write $P_j=e\sum_{a,b}\omega_{ab}^j$. Conjugation proves $P_j$
is real, and the replacement $(a,b)\mapsto(k-a,k-b)$ proves
$P_j=0$ for odd $j$. Directly,
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \sum_{a=0}^k(2a-k)=0,\qquad
 \sum_{a=0}^k(2a-k)^2=\frac{(k+1)k(k+2)}3,\\
 P_2=\frac{qk(k+2)(d-g)}3=:p_2<0.
\end{gathered}\tag{BRD2}
\]
The second identity follows by expanding $4\sum a^2-4k\sum a+
(k+1)k^2$ with $\sum a=k(k+1)/2$ and
$\sum a^2=k(k+1)(2k+1)/6$. In the double sum for $P_2$ the
mixed term vanishes by the first identity, while the two remaining
terms give BRD2 with the complete factor $e(k+1)^2=q$.

For real $|y|>R_*$, expand each factor by the absolutely convergent
series $\log(1-z)=-\sum_{n\geq1}z^n/n$ on $|z|<1$ and take
twice its real part. This defines the real logarithm of the squared
modulus irrespective of a phase choice for the product. The odd
power sums vanish before any estimate is made. Thus
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \log\frac{|\chi(c+iy)|^2}{|y|^{2q}}
   =\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}P_{2j}}{j y^{2j}}
   =\frac{p_2}{y^2}+E_\chi(y),\\
 \tau(y)=R_*^2/y^2<1,\qquad
 |E_\chi(y)|\leq
 q\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{\tau(y)^j}{j}
 \leq\frac{q\tau(y)^2}{2(1-\tau(y))}.
\end{gathered}\tag{BRD3}
\]
Indeed $|P_{2j}|\leq qR_*^{2j}$. The exact preceding series is
also bounded by $q[-\log(1-\tau)-\tau]$; its geometric bound in
BRD3 uses $1/j\leq1/2$ only for the omitted powers $j\geq2$.
The full polynomial and its first moment coefficient have not been
replaced by a freely chosen degree-$q$ polynomial.

At $y=qz$ define
\[
 a_k=\frac{p_2}{q^2}
  =\frac{d-g}{3e}\left(1-\frac1{(k+1)^2}\right),\qquad
 \tau_\epsilon=\frac{k^2(d+g)}{q^2\epsilon^2},\qquad
 \Delta_\chi(\epsilon)=
       \frac{q\tau_\epsilon^2}{2(1-\tau_\epsilon)}.
\tag{BRD4}
\]
Whenever $\tau_\epsilon<1$, BRD3 gives, uniformly on
$|z|\geq\epsilon$,
\[
 |\chi(c+iqz)|^2=q^{2q}|z|^{2q}
      \exp\{a_k/z^2+E_\chi(qz)\},
 \qquad |E_\chi(qz)|\leq\Delta_\chi(\epsilon).
\tag{BRD5}
\]
For $m=1$ the logarithm in BRD5 differs from
$(d-g)/(3z^2)$ by at most
\[
 \frac{g-d}{3(k+1)^2\epsilon^2}+\Delta_\chi(\epsilon),
\tag{BRD6}
\]
which tends to zero as $k\to\infty$ for each fixed original
$d,g,\epsilon>0$. For fixed $m\geq2$ its absolute value is
at most
\[
 \frac{g-d}{3e\epsilon^2}+\Delta_\chi(\epsilon),
\tag{BRD7}
\]
which also tends to zero. The ratios themselves therefore converge
uniformly on $|z|\geq\epsilon$ respectively to
$\exp((d-g)/(3z^2))$ and to $1$: an absolute logarithmic error
$b$ gives a multiplicative interval $[e^{-b},e^b]$.
This retains the actual distinction between the two multiplicity
families rather than discarding their factor $e$.

For the exact Gamma comparison polynomial,
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \frac{|f(c+iy)|^2}{|y|^{2l}}
    =\prod_{j=0}^{l-1}(1+t_j^2/y^2),\qquad
 S_2:=\sum_{j<l}t_j^2=\frac{l(16l^2-12l-1)}{12},\\
 0\leq\log\frac{|f(c+iy)|^2}{|y|^{2l}}
        \leq\frac{S_2}{y^2},\qquad
 -\frac{S_4}{2y^4}\leq
 \log\frac{|f(c+iy)|^2}{|y|^{2l}}-\frac{S_2}{y^2}\leq0,\\
 S_4=\sum_{j<l}t_j^4
 =\frac{l(768l^4-960l^3-160l^2+360l+7)}{240}.
\end{gathered}\tag{BRD8}
\]
The finite $S_2$ formula follows by expanding $4j^2+2j+1/4$.
For $S_4$ expand $16j^4+16j^3+6j^2+j+1/16$ and sum.
The needed fourth-power sum is
$\sum_{j<l}j^4=l(l-1)(2l-1)(3l^2-3l-1)/30$;
its value at $l=0$ is zero and its finite difference at $l$
is $l^4$, which proves the formula. The analogous finite
differences prove the lower-power sums. Their substitution gives
the displayed $S_4$ without omitting any offset.
For $u\geq0$, integration of $1/(1+u)$ gives
$u-u^2/2\leq\log(1+u)\leq u$. Apply these inequalities to
each displayed factor to obtain the remainder, with no extra
smallness assumption on $t_j/y$.

\section{The original multiplication and coefficient maps}

Let $r\geq1$ and $N=q+r-1$. Multiplication by the actual monic
$\chi$ defines
\[
 T_{\chi,r}:\C[S]_{\leq r-1}\longrightarrow\C[S]_{\leq N},
 \quad P\longmapsto\chi P,
 \qquad (T_{\chi,r})_{ij}=\chi_{i-j}.
\tag{BRD9}
\]
Here $\chi_h$ is the coefficient of $S^h$ in the actual polynomial,
and is zero outside $0\leq h\leq q$. Cancellation in $\C[S]$
proves injectivity. Its image is precisely the original relation
space $\ker J_N=\chi\C[S]_{\leq r-1}$, by polynomial division.
The original relation forms are
\[
 H_{0,r}=T_{\chi,r}^*H_N^\sigma T_{\chi,r},\qquad
 H_{f,r}=T_{f,N}^*H_{N+l}^\sigma T_{f,N}
             \text{ pulled back by }T_{\chi,r},
\tag{BRD10}
\]
or equivalently the Grams of $\chi S^j$ and $f\chi S^j$,
$0\leq j<r$. The notation in the second expression means
$H_{f,r}=T_{\chi,r}^*T_{f,N}^*H_{N+l}^\sigma
T_{f,N}T_{\chi,r}$. Its measure is the original
\[
 d\sigma(y)=\frac{|\Gamma(1/4+iy/2)|^2}{2\pi}\,dy.
\tag{BRD11}
\]

The coordinate map $\mathcal T_r:P(S)\mapsto P(c+iqz)$ has
matrix and inverse
\[
 (\mathcal T_r)_{ij}=\binom ji c^{j-i}(iq)^i\quad(i\leq j),
 \qquad Q(z)\longmapsto Q((S-c)/(iq)),
 \quad\det\mathcal T_r=(iq)^{r(r-1)/2}.
\tag{BRD12}
\]
The exact intertwining square is
$\mathcal T_{N+1}T_{\chi,r}=T_{\chi(c+iqz),r}\mathcal T_r$.
Both sides send $P$ to the very same polynomial
$\chi(c+iqz)P(c+iqz)$. This proves the equality of source
maps before any integration or determinant comparison.

Fix $0<\epsilon<R<\infty$ with $q\epsilon>R_*$ and partition
the original real line into
\[
 I=\{|y|<q\epsilon\},\quad
 B=\{q\epsilon\leq|y|\leq qR\},\quad
 F=\{|y|>qR\}.
\tag{BRD13}
\]
Let $H_{a,r}^I,H_{a,r}^B,H_{a,r}^F$, for $a=0,f$, denote
the exact restrictions of BRD10 to these regions. Then
\[
 H_{a,r}=H_{a,r}^I+H_{a,r}^B+H_{a,r}^F.\tag{BRD14}
\]
All three forms are positive definite: their scalar weight is
positive almost everywhere on a nonempty interval, and a nonzero
polynomial cannot vanish on that interval. This also proves that
every inverse and determinant below exists.

\section{The two full-degree bulk determinant comparisons}

For $s=0,l$ let $K_{s,r}$ be the moment Gram on the original
segment in the real $z$ coefficient frame:
\[
 (K_{s,r})_{ij}=\int_{\epsilon\leq|z|\leq R}
 z^{i+j}|z|^{2q+2s}\exp(a_k/z^2)\sigma(qz)\,dz,
 \quad 0\leq i,j<r.
\tag{BRD15}
\]
The corresponding forms in the original $S$ frame are
\[
 B_{s,r}=q^{2q+2s+1}\mathcal T_r^*K_{s,r}\mathcal T_r.
\tag{BRD16}
\]
This retains the Jacobian $dy=q\,dz$, the density factor
$q^{2q+2s}$, the exact coefficient map, and the original Gamma
density $\sigma(qz)$. In particular the source has not been
replaced by a probability measure.

Put $\Delta_f(\epsilon)=S_2/(q^2\epsilon^2)$ and
$\Delta_\chi=\Delta_\chi(\epsilon)$. The pointwise inequalities
BRD5 and BRD8 prove, by integrating each actual squared
polynomial, the Loewner inequalities
\[
\begin{gathered}
 e^{-\Delta_\chi}B_{0,r}\preceq H_{0,r}^B
                    \preceq e^{\Delta_\chi}B_{0,r},\\
 e^{-\Delta_\chi}B_{l,r}\preceq H_{f,r}^B
                    \preceq e^{\Delta_\chi+\Delta_f(\epsilon)}B_{l,r}.
\end{gathered}\tag{BRD17}
\]
They have been proved on exactly the stipulated segment $B$.
If $aA\preceq H\preceq bA$ with $A,H>0$, congruence by
$A^{-1/2}$ puts every eigenvalue in $[a,b]$; their product
proves $r\log a\leq\log\det H-\log\det A\leq r\log b$.
Consequently
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \log\frac{\det H_{f,r}^B}{\det H_{0,r}^B}
   =2lr\log q+\log\frac{\det K_{l,r}}{\det K_{0,r}}
                       +b_r,\\
 -2r\Delta_\chi\leq b_r
          \leq2r\Delta_\chi+r\Delta_f(\epsilon).
\end{gathered}\tag{BRD18}
\]
The determinant of $\mathcal T_r$ cancels only in this ratio:
each individual determinant in BRD16 has factor
$q^{r(2q+2s+1)+r(r-1)}$.

For fixed $d,g,m,\epsilon$ and $r=q$ or $q+1$, BRD4 gives
the explicit finite error
\[
 2r\Delta_\chi+r\Delta_f(\epsilon)
 =\frac{r k^4(d+g)^2}{q^3\epsilon^4(1-\tau_\epsilon)}
   +\frac{rS_2}{q^2\epsilon^2}.\tag{BRD19}
\]
Its first term is $O(1)$ for $m=1$ and $O(k^{-2})$ for fixed
$m\geq2$. Its second term is $O(k)$ and $O(1)$, respectively.
Indeed $r/q\leq1+1/q$, $q=e(k+1)^2$,
$S_2\leq4l^3$, and $e=1$ or $e=1+k(m-1)$; substituting these
actual degrees gives each stated bound. In both cases BRD19
is $o(q)$. A bound on a density alone would not establish this:
the full relation dimension $r$ is explicitly present in BRD19.

\section{Exact tail determinants before any estimate}

Define the actual, dimension-$r$ positive matrix
\[
 D_{a,r}=(H_{a,r}^B)^{-1/2}
       (H_{a,r}^I+H_{a,r}^F)(H_{a,r}^B)^{-1/2},
 \qquad t_{a,r}=\log\det(I_r+D_{a,r}),\quad a=0,f.
\tag{BRD20}
\]
Factoring BRD14 by the two square roots of its bulk Gram gives
the exact identities
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \log\det H_{a,r}=\log\det H_{a,r}^B+t_{a,r},\\
 \log\mathfrak R_{q+r-1}
  =2lr\log q+\log\frac{\det K_{l,r}}{\det K_{0,r}}
                         +b_r+t_{f,r}-t_{0,r},
 \qquad\mathfrak R_N=\det H_{f,r}/\det H_{0,r}.
\end{gathered}\tag{BRD21}
\]
The same determinant equals
$\det[I+(H_{a,r}^B)^{-1}(H_{a,r}^I+H_{a,r}^F)]$ by similarity.
No tail is removed by this equality. The following calculation
now proves an actual bound for both of these exact corrections.

\section{An explicit common source estimate for the inner and far regions}

Set once and for all $\epsilon=2^{-10}$ and $R=64$. For the
uniform statements in this section impose the explicit finite
thresholds
\[
 \frac{k\sqrt{d+g}}q\leq\epsilon/2,\qquad
 \frac{2l-3/2}{q}\leq\epsilon.\tag{BRD22}
\]
Both hold eventually for every fixed original $d,g,m$. For
example $k\geq2048\sqrt{d+g}$ suffices: $q\geq k^2$ proves
the first inequality, and $t_{l-1}<k/2$ proves the second
because $\sqrt{d+g}>2$. In particular
$\tau_\epsilon\leq1/4$, so the bulk expansion has a strict
convergence margin even at the displayed threshold. These inequalities
specify which already defined finite sources satisfy the uniform
bounds; they assert no property of a zeta zero. For $r=q$ or
$q+1$, write $Q(z)=P(c+iqz)$, so $\deg Q\leq r-1\leq q$.
The positive subsegment $1\leq z\leq2$ lies inside $B$.

Here are the needed Gamma bounds with their original constants.
Rotate the Euler integral of $\Gamma(a+iy/2)$ through the angle
$\operatorname{sgn}(y)\theta$, where $0<\theta<\pi/2$.
On the principal branch its value is
\[
 e^{i\operatorname{sgn}(y)\theta(a+iy/2)}
 \int_0^\infty t^{a+iy/2-1}
                  e^{-t e^{i\operatorname{sgn}(y)\theta}}dt.
\]
The circular arcs vanish at infinity by positive cosine and
at zero by $a>0$, so Cauchy's theorem proves the contour
identity. Taking absolute values proves
$|\Gamma(a+iy/2)|\leq e^{-\theta|y|/2}
\Gamma(a)(\cos\theta)^{-a}$. At $a=1/4$, $\theta=1$, this gives
\[
 \sigma(y)\leq C_Ue^{-|y|},\quad
 C_U=\frac{\Gamma(1/4)^2}{2\pi\sqrt{\cos1}}.
\tag{BRD23}
\]
Here is the needed reflection identity on its precise domain.
For $0<\operatorname{Re}z<1$, the product of the two absolutely
convergent Euler integrals, with $u=tv$, gives
\[
 \Gamma(z)\Gamma(1-z)=\int_0^\infty\frac{t^{z-1}}{1+t}\,dt.
\]
Integrate $w^{z-1}/(1+w)$ around a keyhole contour about the
positive real axis with argument in $(0,2\pi)$. The large and
small circular contributions are respectively
$O(R^{\operatorname{Re}z-1})$ and
$O(\rho^{\operatorname{Re}z})$, so both vanish. The two straight
segments give $(1-e^{2\pi iz})$ times the displayed real integral.
The only pole is $w=-1$, with residue $e^{i\pi(z-1)}$. The
residue theorem therefore evaluates the real integral as
$2\pi i e^{i\pi(z-1)}/(1-e^{2\pi iz})=\pi/\sin(\pi z)$.
This proves the reflection identity used here, including at
$z=1/4+iy/2$. At that point it gives
$|\Gamma(1/4+iy/2)|^2|\Gamma(3/4-iy/2)|^2
=2\pi^2/\cosh(\pi y)$.
The unrotated Euler integral bounds the second Gamma factor
by $\Gamma(3/4)^2$, whence
\[
 \sigma(y)\geq\frac{C_L}{\cosh(\pi y)}
       \geq C_Le^{-\pi|y|},\quad
 C_L=\frac{\pi}{\Gamma(3/4)^2},\qquad
 \frac{C_U}{C_L}=\frac1{\sqrt{\cos1}}.
\tag{BRD24}
\]
The last equality uses the reflection identity at $1/4$,
$\Gamma(1/4)\Gamma(3/4)=\pi\sqrt2$.

For the polynomial bound define the Legendre polynomials by
$P_j(x)=(2^j j!)^{-1}(d/dx)^j(x^2-1)^j$.
Their leading coefficient is
$a_j=(2j)!/[2^j(j!)^2]$. Every derivative of $(x^2-1)^j$
of order less than $j$ vanishes at both endpoints. Thus
$j$ integrations by parts prove orthogonality against lower
degrees, and for the norm they give explicitly
\[
 \int_{-1}^1P_j(x)^2dx
  =\frac{a_j}{2^j}\int_{-1}^1(1-x^2)^jdx
  =\frac{a_j}{2^j}
       \frac{2^{2j+1}(j!)^2}{(2j+1)!}
  =\frac2{2j+1}.
\]
The middle integral follows by $x=2t-1$ and the beta integral
$\int_0^1t^j(1-t)^jdt=(j!)^2/(2j+1)!$, which also follows
by repeated integration by parts. Consequently
$\sqrt{2j+1}P_j(2z-3)$, $0\leq j<r$, are an orthonormal
basis on $[1,2]$ for the polynomial space in question. For
real $|x|\geq1$, their integral representation
\[
 P_j(x)=\frac1\pi\int_0^\pi
            (x+\sqrt{x^2-1}\cos\theta)^j\,d\theta
\tag{BRD25}
\]
gives $|P_j(x)|\leq(|x|+\sqrt{x^2-1})^j\leq(2|x|)^j$.
For negative $x$ use $P_j(-x)=(-1)^jP_j(x)$.
The representation follows by expanding the integrand: odd
powers of cosine integrate to zero and
$\pi^{-1}\int_0^\pi\cos^{2h}\theta\,d\theta
=\binom{2h}{h}/4^h$, obtained by integration by parts.
The resulting polynomial is the Rodrigues polynomial, as
direct expansion of its derivative verifies. Thus the bound
uses an explicit polynomial identity.
Expansion of $Q$ in this orthonormal basis and Cauchy--Schwarz
therefore proves
\[
 |Q(z)|^2\leq
 \sum_{j=0}^{r-1}(2j+1)|P_j(2z-3)|^2
                      \int_1^2|Q(t)|^2dt.
\tag{BRD26}
\]

Pair every root $\omega$ with $-\omega$, keeping multiplicities.
For real $y$,
$|(-iy-\omega)(-iy+\omega)|\leq y^2+|\omega|^2$,
and for $|y|>R_*$ the reverse triangle inequality gives the
lower bound $y^2-R_*^2$. Consequently BRD22 implies on $1\leq z\leq2$
\[
 |\chi(c+iqz)|^2\geq q^{2q}(3/4)^q,
 \qquad |f(c+iqz)|^2\geq q^{2l}.
\tag{BRD27}
\]
In particular, for $a=0,f$ and $s=0,l$ respectively, the actual
bulk quadratic form is at least
\[
 q^{2q+2s+1}(3/4)^q C_Le^{-2\pi q}
                           \int_1^2|Q(z)|^2dz.
\tag{BRD28}
\]

On $|z|\leq\epsilon$, the upper paired-root estimate and BRD22
give
\[
 |\chi(c+iqz)|^2\leq q^{2q}(2\epsilon^2)^q,
 \quad |f(c+iqz)|^2\leq q^{2l}(2\epsilon^2)^l,
 \quad\sum_{j<r}(2j+1)|P_j(2z-3)|^2\leq r^2 8^{2q}.
\tag{BRD29}
\]
For the last bound $|2z-3|\leq3+2\epsilon$, so BRD25 bounds
each polynomial by $(6+4\epsilon)^j\leq8^j$, and
$\sum_{j<r}(2j+1)=r^2$, $r-1\leq q$. Integrate over a
segment of length $2\epsilon$, apply BRD23 and BRD26, and
divide by BRD28. For both actual forms this proves
\[
 H_{a,r}^I\preceq\kappa_{I,r}H_{a,r}^B,
 \quad\kappa_{I,r}=\frac{2\epsilon r^2}{\sqrt{\cos1}}
             \left(\frac43\,2^{-13}e^{2\pi}\right)^q
 <\frac{2\epsilon r^2}{\sqrt{\cos1}}e^{-2q}.
\tag{BRD30}
\]
For the numerator the intermediate bound contains the additional
factor $(2\epsilon^2)^l\leq1$, which was retained in BRD29
before obtaining the common bound. To justify the last strict
inequality without a decimal estimate, $\log2>2/3$ follows
from the positive series for $2\operatorname{arctanh}(1/3)$,
and $\log(4/3)<1/3$ follows by integrating $1/t<1$ on
$1<t<4/3$. Also
\[
 \frac{22}7-\pi=\int_0^1\frac{x^4(1-x)^4}{1+x^2}\,dx>0.
\]
Polynomial division and $\int_0^1(1+x^2)^{-1}dx=\pi/4$
prove this identity. Thus the logarithm of the parenthesized
base is less than $-26/3+44/7+1/3=-43/21<-2$.

For the far region a sharper use of BRD5 avoids a large
coefficient bound. Define $\Delta_\chi(B)$ by BRD4 with
$\epsilon$ replaced by $B$, and let
\[
 \eta=-a_k+\Delta_\chi(1),\quad
 D_s=2q+2s+2r-2,\quad
 \Delta_{f,0}(B)=0,\quad
 \Delta_{f,l}(B)=S_2/(q^2B^2).
\tag{BRD31}
\]
On $[1,2]$ one may replace $(3/4)^q$ in BRD28 by $e^{-\eta}$,
by BRD5 and $a_k<0$. On $|z|\geq B\geq2$, BRD25--26 give
the polynomial bound
$r^2((4+6/B)|z|)^{2r-2}\int_1^2|Q|^2$.
The exact upper weights are at most
$q^{2q+2s}|z|^{2q+2s}
\exp(\Delta_\chi(B)+\Delta_{f,s}(B))$,
because the negative term $a_k/z^2$ only decreases the bound.
For this parameterized estimate define the exact moving far
region $F_B=\{|y|>qB\}$ and let $H_{a,r}^{F_B}$ be the
original form integrated over that region. The bulk denominator
continues to be the fixed region
$\epsilon\leq|z|\leq64$, containing $[1,2]$.
After BRD23 and $dy=q\,dz$, division by the improved BRD28
therefore gives
\[
 H_{a,r}^{F_B}\preceq\kappa_{F,s,r}H_{a,r}^B,
\tag{BRD32}
\]
where at any $B>D_s/q$, $B\geq2$,
\[
 \kappa_{F,s,r}=
 \frac{2r^2}{\sqrt{\cos1}}
 \frac{\exp(2\pi q+\eta+\Delta_\chi(B)+\Delta_{f,s}(B))
 (4+6/B)^{2r-2}B^{D_s}e^{-qB}}{q-D_s/B}.
\tag{BRD33}
\]
The last integral bound used here is fully explicit:
$\log(t/B)\leq(t-B)/B$ for $t\geq B$ implies
\[
 \int_B^\infty t^{D_s}e^{-qt}dt
       \leq\frac{B^{D_s}e^{-qB}}{q-D_s/B}.
\tag{BRD34}
\]
It is applied to both tails, which accounts for the factor two
in BRD33.

Specialize now to $B=64$, so $F_B=F$ is exactly the far region
of the original partition BRD13. We have $D_s\leq5q$, since $r-1\leq q$ and
$2l\leq q$. Define
\[
 c_F=64-2\pi-5\log64-2\log(131/32)>20.
\tag{BRD35}
\]
Indeed $\pi<4$, $\log2<1$, and $131/32<8$ give respectively
$2\pi<8$, $5\log64<30$, and $2\log(131/32)<6$.
The weaker threshold $q\geq2R_*$, already implied by BRD22,
gives $\tau_1\leq1/4$. Since $k\geq5$,
\[
 -a_k\leq7q/60,\quad \Delta_\chi(1)\leq q/24,
 \quad\Delta_\chi(64)\leq q/24,
 \quad\Delta_{f,l}(64)\leq q/8192.
\tag{BRD36}
\]
For the first inequality use
$-a_k\leq q\tau_1(k+2)/(3k)$ and $(k+2)/k\leq7/5$.
For the last, $S_2\leq4l^3\leq q^3/2$ follows from
$2l\leq q$. Their sum is less than $q/4$. Hence BRD33 gives
the uniform bound, for both $s=0,l$,
\[
 \kappa_{F,s,r}\leq
 \frac{128r^2}{59q\sqrt{\cos1}}e^{-19q}
 \leq\frac{512q}{59\sqrt{\cos1}}e^{-19q}
 =:\kappa_F.
\tag{BRD37}
\]
Every root-size threshold and every numerator degree has thus
been included in the tail estimate.

\section{The complete high relation determinants and signed return}

Combining the actual positive-form inequalities BRD30 and BRD37
with BRD20 proves
\[
 0\leq t_{a,r}\leq r\log(1+\kappa_{I,r}+\kappa_F),
 \qquad a=0,f,\quad r=q,q+1.
\tag{BRD38}
\]
Indeed the eigenvalues of $D_{a,r}$ lie in
$[0,\kappa_{I,r}+\kappa_F]$ by congruence and addition.
The tails are the exact forms in BRD14 and BRD20; BRD38 is a
proved estimate for their determinants, not an assumption that
those forms are absent. In fact the difference of their two
nonnegative logarithms has the sharper bound
\[
 |t_{f,r}-t_{0,r}|\leq r\log(1+\kappa_{I,r}+\kappa_F).
\tag{BRD39}
\]
Both terms belong to that same interval by BRD38.

For all original degrees satisfying BRD22, put
\[
 \mathcal E_r=2r\Delta_\chi(\epsilon)+r\Delta_f(\epsilon)
              +r\log(1+\kappa_{I,r}+\kappa_F).
\tag{BRD40}
\]
Equations BRD18, BRD21 and BRD38 first give the stronger
one-sided statement. If
$U_r=2lr\log q+\log(\det K_{l,r}/\det K_{0,r})$ and

\[
 -2r\Delta_\chi(\epsilon)-L_r
 \leq\log\mathfrak R_{q+r-1}-U_r
 \leq2r\Delta_\chi(\epsilon)+r\Delta_f(\epsilon)+L_r.
\tag{BRD41a}
\]
In particular they prove the full, finite absolute bound
\[
 \boxed{\left|\log\mathfrak R_{q+r-1}
    -2lr\log q-\log\frac{\det K_{l,r}}{\det K_{0,r}}
       \right|\leq\mathcal E_r,
       \quad r=q,q+1.}
\tag{BRD41}
\]
For fixed original $d,g,m$, $\mathcal E_r=o(q)$.
BRD19 proves the bulk part. The tail part is at most
$r(\kappa_{I,r}+\kappa_F)$, which tends to zero exponentially
by BRD30 and BRD37, with $r\leq q+1$.
More precisely the bulk error is $O(k)$ for $m=1$ and
$O(1)$ for each fixed $m\geq2$, while these displayed finite
formulas give the actual constants and thresholds.
