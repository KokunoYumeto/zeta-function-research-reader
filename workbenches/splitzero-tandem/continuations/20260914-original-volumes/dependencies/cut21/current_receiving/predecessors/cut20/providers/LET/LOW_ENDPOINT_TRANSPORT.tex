CTR1--54. It treats the original one-column relation determinant separately,

\section{Objects and the low source maps}
Retain
\[
 k=4l+1,\quad l\ge1,\quad m\ge1,\quad e=1+k(m-1),\quad
 q=e(k+1)^2,\quad c=k/2,\quad 0<\delta<1/2,\quad\gamma>2.
 \tag{LET1}
\]
Set \(d=\delta^2,\ g=\gamma^2\), and define the complete original objects
\[
 \chi(S)=\prod_{a,b=0}^k
 [S-c-(2a-k)\delta-i(2b-k)\gamma]^e,\qquad
 f(S)=\prod_{j=0}^{l-1}(S-c-t_j),\quad t_j=2j+\tfrac12,
 \qquad \sigma(y)=\frac{|\Gamma(1/4+iy/2)|^2}{2\pi}.
 \tag{LET2}
\]
Write \(m_j=\int_{\mathbb R}y^j\sigma(y)\,dy\), with the index denoting the
literal power of \(y\). Thus \(m_0=\sqrt{2\pi}\), odd moments vanish, and
CTR50--53 supply all even moments by their exact rational recurrence with
the full mass factor. The original low relation endpoint is
\[
 \mathfrak r_\chi=
 \frac{\int_{\mathbb R}|f(c+iy)\chi(c+iy)|^2\sigma(y)\,dy}
      {\int_{\mathbb R}|\chi(c+iy)|^2\sigma(y)\,dy}.
 \tag{LET3}
\]
All four scalar integrals used below are finite by the proved Gamma upper
bound and strictly positive because their polynomial factors vanish at
only finitely many real points.

Put \(\epsilon=2^{-10}\), and partition the line into
\(I=\{|y|<q\epsilon\}\), \(B=\{q\epsilon\le |y|\le64q\}\), and
\(F=\{|y|>64q\}\). Assume the explicit finite threshold
\[
 k\ge2048\sqrt{d+g}.
 \tag{LET4}
\]
Then \(q\ge k^2\) gives \(k\sqrt{d+g}/q\le\epsilon/2\).
Also \(t_{l-1}<k/2\) and \(\sqrt{d+g}>2\) give
\(t_{l-1}/q\le\epsilon\). This is the same original-parameter domain
already used for the complete high-endpoint comparison.

On the bulk Hilbert space \(L^2(B,\sigma(y)\,dy)\), the maps from the
one-dimensional coefficient space are
\[
 T_{\rm pow}u(y)=(iy)^q u,\qquad
 T_\chi u(y)=\chi(c+iy)u,\qquad
 (Uh)(y)=\frac{\chi(c+iy)}{(iy)^q}h(y).
 \tag{LET5}
\]
Their exact identities are \(UT_{\rm pow}=T_\chi\) and
\(UM_f=M_fU\), where \(M_f\) is multiplication by \(f(c+iy)\).
Both \(U\) and its inverse are bounded: the bulk is compact, \(y\ne0\)
there, and LET4 places every displacement root strictly inside its inner
radius. The squared modulus of \(U\) is the original density ratio.
Taking scalar Hilbert Grams proves the source comparisons below while
retaining the original arithmetic quotient and its multiplication maps.

\section{A scalar ratio transport retaining the degree \(l\)}
Let \(w\) be a finite positive measure, \(F_0\ge0\) a measurable function
with \(0<\int F_0\,dw<\infty\), and let \(\psi\) be a bounded real
function. Define the two literal ratios
\[
 R_w=\frac{\int F_0\,dw}{\int dw},\qquad
 R_{e^\psi w}=\frac{\int F_0e^\psi\,dw}{\int e^\psi\,dw}.
\]
If \(a\le\psi\le b\) almost everywhere, positivity of the integrals gives
\[
 e^a\le\frac{\int F_0e^\psi\,dw}{\int F_0\,dw}\le e^b,\qquad
 e^a\le\frac{\int e^\psi\,dw}{\int dw}\le e^b.
\]
Division and logarithms therefore prove the exact finite estimate
\[
 \left|\log R_{e^\psi w}-\log R_w\right|\le b-a.
 \tag{LET6}
\]
No restriction on the degree of \(F_0=|f|^2\) enters this scalar proof.

Apply LET6 to
\(dw=1_B(y)|y|^{2q}\sigma(y)\,dy\) and \(F_0(y)=|f(c+iy)|^2\).
For \(y=qz\), the unchanged root product has the expansion
\[
 \psi_\chi(qz):=\log\frac{|\chi(c+iqz)|^2}{|qz|^{2q}}
 =\frac{a_k}{z^2}+E_\chi(qz),\quad
 a_k=\frac{k(k+2)(d-g)}{3q},\quad
 |E_\chi(qz)|\le\delta_\chi,
\]
\[
 \tau_\epsilon=\frac{k^2(d+g)}{q^2\epsilon^2}\le\tfrac14,\qquad
 \delta_\chi=\frac{q\tau_\epsilon^2}{2(1-\tau_\epsilon)}.
 \tag{LET7}
\]
For completeness, write each factor as
\(iqz(1+i\zeta_{ab}/(qz))\). The logarithmic Taylor series converges
absolutely; the permutation \((a,b)\mapsto(k-a,k-b)\) cancels odd powers.
The quadratic sum is
\(e\operatorname{Re}\sum_{a,b}\zeta_{ab}^2=
qk(k+2)(d-g)/3\). The remaining even powers have absolute sum at most
\(q\sum_{j\ge2}\tau_\epsilon^j/j
 \le q\tau_\epsilon^2/[2(1-\tau_\epsilon)]\).
This proves every coefficient and remainder in LET7.

Since \(z^{-2}\in[64^{-2},\epsilon^{-2}]\), define the finite constant
\[
 E_0=|a_k|(\epsilon^{-2}-64^{-2})+2\delta_\chi.
 \tag{LET8}
\]
The exact bulk difference
\[
 d_\chi=
 \log\frac{\int_B|f\chi|^2d\sigma}{\int_B|\chi|^2d\sigma}
 -\log\frac{\int_B|f(c+iy)|^2|y|^{2q}d\sigma}
             {\int_B|y|^{2q}d\sigma}
\]
satisfies \(|d_\chi|\le E_0\) by LET6. All products in the first
ratio are evaluated at \(c+iy\).
The original numerator multiplication also gives
\[
 1\le \frac{|f(c+iy)|^2}{|y|^{2l}}
 =\prod_{j=0}^{l-1}(1+t_j^2/y^2)\le e^{\Delta_f},
 \qquad
 \Delta_f=\frac{S_2}{q^2\epsilon^2},\quad
 S_2=\sum_{j=0}^{l-1}t_j^2
     =\frac{l(16l^2-12l-1)}{12}.
 \tag{LET9}
\]
The upper bound follows by integrating the inequality
\((\log(1+x))'=1/(1+x)\le1\) from zero. Expanding each \(t_j^2\) and
summing \(j,j^2\) gives the stated \(S_2\).
Consequently the logarithmic correction \(d_f\) obtained by replacing
the bulk numerator \(|f(c+iy)|^2|y|^{2q}\) by
\(|y|^{2q+2l}\) satisfies \(0\le d_f\le\Delta_f\), with that sign.

\section{The four scalar tails on the original line}
Consider the four positive integrals with weights
\[
 h_{\chi,0}=|\chi(c+iy)|^2,\quad
 h_{\chi,f}=|f(c+iy)\chi(c+iy)|^2,\quad
 h_{{\rm pow},0}=|y|^{2q},\quad
 h_{{\rm pow},l}=|y|^{2q+2l}.
\]
For each weight let \(H=\int h\,d\sigma=H^I+H^B+H^F\) and
\[
 t_H=\log\left(1+\frac{H^I+H^F}{H^B}\right)\ge0,\qquad
 \log H=\log H^B+t_H.
 \tag{LET10}
\]
Here every \(H^B>0\). We prove a common bound at scalar rank one rather
than applying a high-rank statement outside its specified domain.

The original contour and reflection calculation BRD23--24 proves
\[
 C_Le^{-\pi|y|}\le\sigma(y)\le C_Ue^{-|y|},\qquad
 C_L=\frac{\pi}{\Gamma(3/4)^2},\quad
 C_U=\frac{\Gamma(1/4)^2}{2\pi\sqrt{\cos1}},\quad
 C_U/C_L=(\cos1)^{-1/2}.
 \tag{LET11}
\]
Those proofs retain the Euler contour rotation and keyhole residue;
they are included in the complete source dependencies. Pairing the roots
of \(\chi\) as \(\zeta,-\zeta\) gives
\[
 |\chi(c+iy)|^2\le(y^2+k^2(d+g))^q.
\]
On \(1\le z\le2\), the reverse triangle inequality instead gives
\(|\chi(c+iqz)|^2\ge q^{2q}(3/4)^q\), and
\(|f(c+iqz)|^2\ge q^{2l}\).
On \(|z|\le\epsilon\), LET4 gives the respective upper bounds
\(q^{2q}(2\epsilon^2)^q\) and \(q^{2l}(2\epsilon^2)^l\).
The coefficient polynomial is exactly the constant one. In particular
its scalar value is bounded by \(8^{2q}\int_1^2 1\,dz\), with no
missing coefficient directions. Integrating over the inner segment and
dividing by the positive bulk subsegment gives for both actual weights
\[
 H^I/H^B\le\kappa_{I,1}
 :=\frac{2\epsilon}{\sqrt{\cos1}}
   \left(\frac43\,2^{-13}e^{2\pi}\right)^q.
 \tag{LET12}
\]
Indeed the numerator/denominator ratio before enlarging it is
\(2\epsilon(\cos1)^{-1/2}
 (4/3)^q e^{2\pi q}(2\epsilon^2)^{q+s}\), \(s=0,l\).
Multiplication by \(8^{2q}\ge1\) gives LET12 because
\(2\epsilon^2\,8^2=2^{-13}\) and
\((2\epsilon^2)^s\le1\).
For the reference powers the corresponding stronger constant is
\(2\epsilon(\cos1)^{-1/2}\epsilon^{2s}
 (2^{-14}e^{2\pi})^q\), hence is at most LET12.

For the far estimate retain \(D_s=2q+2s\le5q\).
At \(z\ge64\), integration of
\(\log(z/64)\le(z-64)/64\) gives
\[
 \int_{64}^{\infty}z^{D_s}e^{-qz}dz
 \le\frac{64^{D_s}e^{-64q}}{q-D_s/64},
 \qquad q-D_s/64\ge59q/64.
 \tag{LET13}
\]
For the actual weights, LET7 on the bulk subsegment gives the lower
factor \(e^{-\eta}\), where
\(\eta=-a_k+\delta_\chi(1)\), and
\(\delta_\chi(x)=q\tau_x^2/[2(1-\tau_x)]\).
The far upper weights have the extra factors
\(e^{\delta_\chi(64)+\Delta_{f,s}(64)}\), where
\(\Delta_{f,0}=0\), \(\Delta_{f,l}(x)=S_2/(q^2x^2)\).
Using LET11, both real tails, and the Jacobian \(dy=q\,dz\), their
ratio is at most
\[
 \frac{2}{\sqrt{\cos1}}\,
 \frac{e^{2\pi q+\eta+\delta_\chi(64)+\Delta_{f,s}(64)}
             64^{D_s}e^{-64q}}{q-D_s/64}.
 \tag{LET14}
\]
No polynomial-growth factor is needed for the constant coefficient.
The original domain implies \(\tau_1\le1/4\) and \(k\ge5\).
It gives separately
\[
 -a_k\le7q/60,\quad \delta_\chi(1)\le q/24,\quad
 \delta_\chi(64)\le q/24,\quad
 \Delta_{f,l}(64)\le q/8192.
\]
The last follows from \(S_2\le4l^3\) and \(2l\le q\).
Their sum is less than \(q/4\).
Using \(64-2\pi-5\log64>20\), LET13--14 therefore give
\[
 H^F/H^B\le \frac{128}{59q\sqrt{\cos1}}e^{-19q}
 \le\kappa_F:=\frac{512q}{59\sqrt{\cos1}}e^{-19q}.
 \tag{LET15}
\]
The elementary displayed strict exponent bound follows from
\(\pi<4\) and \(\log2<1\). For the reference powers all three correction
factors in LET14 equal one, so the same inequality follows directly.
Thus the four actual scalar corrections obey
\[
 0\le t_H\le L_1:=\log(1+\kappa_{I,1}+\kappa_F).
 \tag{LET16}
\]
The constant \(\kappa_{I,1}\) decays exponentially: as proved by the
positive series for \(\log2\), \(\log2>2/3\); also
\(\log(4/3)<1/3\) and
\(22/7-\pi=\int_0^1x^4(1-x)^4/(1+x^2)\,dx>0\).
Their substitution gives
\(\log((4/3)2^{-13}e^{2\pi})<-43/21<-2\).

The complete exact low-endpoint transfer is now
\[
 \log\mathfrak r_\chi-\log\frac{m_{2q+2l}}{m_{2q}}
 =d_\chi+d_f+t_{H_{\chi,f}}-t_{H_{\chi,0}}
                 -t_{H_{{\rm pow},l}}+t_{H_{{\rm pow},0}}.
 \tag{LET17}
\]
It follows by writing each of the four full integrals as its own bulk
integral times \(e^{t_H}\). In particular, with
\(a_0=E_0+2L_1\) and \(b_0=a_0+\Delta_f\),
\[
 \boxed{
 -a_0\le\log\mathfrak r_\chi-\log\frac{m_{2q+2l}}{m_{2q}}\le b_0.}
 \tag{LET18}
\]
The exact one-sided numerator correction occurs only in the upper bound.
Since
\[
 |a_k|\le|d-g|/(3e),\qquad
 \delta_\chi\le\frac{2(d+g)^2}{3e^3k^2\epsilon^4},\qquad
 \Delta_f\le\frac{1}{16e^2k\epsilon^2},
\]
the finite errors in LET18 are \(O(1)\) at fixed \(m=1,\delta,\gamma\)
and \(O(k^{-1})\) for each fixed \(m\ge2,\delta,\gamma\).
The last displayed bound uses \(S_2\le k^3/16\) and \(q\ge ek^2\).
Both tail corrections have the already proved exponential decay.

\section{The whole original signed Gamma functional}
Retain the full high reference matrices
\[
 (\mathsf M_{a,r})_{ij}=m_{2a+i+j},\qquad
 \mathcal H_{q,l,r}=\det\mathsf M_{q+l,r}/\det\mathsf M_{q,r},
 \qquad r=q,q+1.
\]
Let
\[
 P_k=\log D_{l,q-1}+\log D_{l,q}
       -\log D_{l,2q-1}-\log D_{l,2q},\qquad
 D_{l,N}=\prod_{a=0}^{N}\prod_{j=0}^{2l-1}(a+j+1/2),
\]
\[
 W_k=P_k-\log\frac{m_{2q+2l}}{m_{2q}}
              +\log\mathcal H_{q,l,q}+\log\mathcal H_{q,l,q+1}.
 \tag{LET19}
\]
All factors of the original density are retained in the moment matrices;
CTR53 proves their exact rational moment calculation with mass factors
displayed before cancellation. The exact old identity is
\(\mathfrak T_k=P_k-\log\mathfrak r_\chi+
\log\mathfrak R_{2q-1}+\log\mathfrak R_{2q}\).

Write the proved high-endpoint constants from CTR45--48 as
\[
 A=2E_{\chi,l}+2(L_q+L_{q+1}),\qquad
 B=A+(2q+1)\Delta_f,\qquad E_{\chi,l}=lE_0.
\]
Their complete signed sum has error in \([-A,B]\).
The minus sign of the low endpoint reverses LET18. Therefore
\[
 \boxed{W_k-A-b_0\le\mathfrak T_k\le W_k+B+a_0,}
 \qquad
 \boxed{-W_k-B-a_0\le R_k^0-R_k^\sigma\le -W_k+A+b_0.}
 \tag{LET20}
\]
This is a direct substitution of exact determinant identities and finite
proved errors. In particular the entire Gamma reference \(W_k\) depends
only on the original integers \(q,l\) and the same fixed Gamma density.
The original \(\delta,\gamma\) remain in its error constants, finite
threshold, original quotient, and all exact relation maps.
Both sides have error \(O(k)=o(q)\); at every fixed \(m\ge2\) the error
is \(O(1)\). No value is assigned here to the remaining explicit \(W_k\).

Finally, preserve the exact earlier arithmetic notation
\(\Delta_\Gamma=\delta_\sigma-\mathfrak T_k\) and
\(B_{\rm ar}=B_0+\Delta_\Gamma\), with
\(-a_-\le\delta_\sigma\le a_+\).
Adding these signed intervals gives the completed receiving formula
\[
 B_0-W_k-B-a_0-a_-\ \le B_{\rm ar}\le\
 B_0-W_k+A+b_0+a_+ .
 \tag{LET21}
\]
The baseline \(B_0\) in LET21 is the existing arithmetic quantity,
whereas the lowercase \(a_0,b_0\) are the low-source errors explicitly
defined above. Subtracting the original \(4q\log(D_h k)\) gives the
corresponding interval for the unchanged HC residual.
