\section{The full polynomial period deformation and its entire-amplitude forcing}
\label{GraphV3:sec:pde-full-deformation}

Retain the original nonempty complete packet $h(s)$, its degree $n\geq1$,
its primitive $\Phi_h(0)=0$, its arithmetic coefficient space
$E_h=\mathbb{C}[s]/(h)$ in the increasing frame $1,s,\ldots,s^{n-1}$,
and the actual entire amplitude $v_h=2\xi/h$. All zero orders in $h$
are complete, so this last quotient is entire and its Taylor unit
$\upsilon_h=j_h(v_h)$ is invertible in $E_h$. Write
\[
 h(s)=s^n+\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}h_rs^r,\qquad
 f_t(s)=\Phi_h(s)-ts,\qquad
 A_h(t)=A_h+tR,\quad R=e_0e_{n-1}^{\mathsf T}.
 \tag{PDE1}
\]
Here $A_h$ is the original arithmetic multiplication by $s$ on $E_h$;
$A_h(t)$ is its displayed companion deformation on the same coefficient
frame. The parameter $t$ does not replace the original marked arithmetic
quotient by a different quotient. The matrix $R$ includes the case
$n=1$, where it is the identity.

Fix $u\ne0$ on a specified branch of $\log u$. Set $d=n+1$ and retain
the actual rays and their orientations
\[
 \theta_j=\frac{\arg u+\pi+2\pi j}{d},\quad
 \ell_j(r)=re^{i\theta_j}\ (r\geq0),\quad
 \Gamma_j=-\ell_0+\ell_j\quad(1\leq j\leq n).
 \tag{PDE2}
\]
For an admitted amplitude $a$, let $\mathcal I_t(a)$ be the column with
entries $\int_{\Gamma_j}e^{f_t/u}a(s)\,ds$. Every amplitude used below is
a polynomial times a derivative of the fixed $v_h$, or a polynomial.
IPM1--6 proves their convergence on these rays with the original theta
bound; the explicit derivative estimate below also proves the required
first-derivative convergence directly. Compact sets of parameters have
a common dominating function with negative leading term
$-r^d/(d|u|)$ and lower polynomial terms. Thus these integrals and their
displayed parameter derivatives are entire in $t$. The fixed rays have
no $t$-dependent endpoint or orientation.

\subsection{The exact determinant on the whole polynomial family}

For this calculation only, allow all lower phase coefficients to vary:
\[
 f_{\mathbf c}(s)=\frac{s^{n+1}}{n+1}+\sum_{j=0}^n c_js^j,
 \quad h_{\mathbf c}=f_{\mathbf c}',\quad
 L_{\mathbf c}=u\partial_s+h_{\mathbf c},\quad
 \Pi(\mathbf c,u)_{j,b}
       =\int_{\Gamma_j}e^{f_{\mathbf c}/u}s^b\,ds.
 \tag{PDE3}
\]
The row index is $1\leq j\leq n$ and the column index $0\leq b<n$.
The original phase $f_t$ is the specified coefficient slice of this
family. No coefficient of that slice will be suppressed.

Ordinary monic division gives, for $0\leq j\leq n$ and $0\leq b<n$,
\[
 s^{b+j}=h_{\mathbf c}q_{b,j}+r_{b,j},\qquad
 \deg r_{b,j}<n,\quad\deg q_{b,j}\leq b+j-n.
 \tag{PDE4}
\]
A negative bound means the zero polynomial. In particular
$\deg q_{b,j}'\leq b-1<n$, so the exact differential reduction is
\[
 s^{b+j}=L_{\mathbf c}q_{b,j}
                    +(r_{b,j}-u q_{b,j}').
 \tag{PDE5}
\]
The leading term of $L_{\mathbf c}P$ has degree $n+\deg P$ and the same
leading coefficient as $P$. Repeated subtraction therefore proves that
the cokernel of $L_{\mathbf c}$ has the unique increasing frame of
degrees less than $n$, for every $\mathbf c$ and $u$. Formula PDE5 is
its literal remainder, not a specialization at $u=0$.

Let $B_j(\mathbf c,u)$ have these remainder columns, and let
$M_{\mathbf c}$ be multiplication by $s$ on
$\mathbb{C}[s]/(h_{\mathbf c})$. Ordinary remainders form
$M_{\mathbf c}^j$. The correction column $-u q_{b,j}'$ has row index
strictly less than $b$. It is consequently strictly triangular and has
zero trace. Thus, retaining its full nonzero matrix,
\[
 B_j=M_{\mathbf c}^{j}-u(\operatorname{coeff}q_{b,j}')_{b=0}^{n-1},
 \qquad \operatorname{Tr}B_j=\operatorname{Tr}M_{\mathbf c}^{j}.
 \tag{PDE6}
\]
The boundary integral of $e^{f_{\mathbf c}/u}q_{b,j}$ vanishes at
the two outer ends and cancels at the common origin. Differentiating
the original integrals and applying PDE5 gives
\[
 \partial_{c_j}\Pi=\frac1u\Pi B_j\quad(0\leq j\leq n).
 \tag{PDE7}
\]

Define the polynomial in the original coefficients
\[
 \mathcal S(\mathbf c)
  =\operatorname{Tr} f_{\mathbf c}(M_{\mathbf c})
  =\frac{\operatorname{Tr}M_{\mathbf c}^{n+1}}{n+1}
                +\sum_{j=0}^n c_j\operatorname{Tr}M_{\mathbf c}^j.
 \tag{PDE8}
\]
Its derivative with respect to $c_j$ is
$\operatorname{Tr}M_{\mathbf c}^j$. Here is a proof including repeated
roots. On the open set where $h_{\mathbf c}$ has distinct roots
$r_1,\ldots,r_n$, they admit local holomorphic enumerations and
$\mathcal S=\sum_i f_{\mathbf c}(r_i)$. Differentiation gives
$\partial_{c_j}\mathcal S=\sum_i r_i^j$, since
$f_{\mathbf c}'(r_i)=0$ makes every root-derivative term exactly zero.
Both sides of the identity are polynomials in $\mathbf c$; the open
set is dense because the derivative $s^n-1$ occurs in this family
and has distinct roots. Equality extends as a polynomial identity to
every coefficient value. Equivalently, the trace at a repeated root
is its full multiplicity times the scalar value, since powers of its
nilpotent part have zero diagonal. No multiplicity is removed.

Put $D=\det\Pi$. Multilinearity of the determinant in its columns
gives $\partial_{c_j}D=u^{-1}(\operatorname{Tr}B_j)D$ without any
prior assumption that $\Pi$ is invertible. PDE6 and PDE8 imply that
every derivative of $D\exp(-\mathcal S/u)$ is zero on the connected
coefficient space. Its value is determined at $\mathbf c=0$.

For completeness, write $\zeta=\exp(2\pi i/d)$ and
$\beta_b=(b+1)/d$. Direct integration on the original two rays gives
\[
 \Pi(0,u)_{j,b}
  =u^{(b+1)/d}d^{\beta_b-1}
          e^{i\pi\beta_b}\Gamma(\beta_b)
                       (\zeta^{j(b+1)}-1).
 \tag{PDE9}
\]
The power of $u$ uses the branch fixed in PDE2. To compute the remaining
determinant let $V_{r,b}=\zeta^{rb}$, $0\leq r,b<d$. Subtract row zero
from each other row and expand at its first column. The resulting
minor is exactly $(\zeta^{j(b+1)}-1)_{1\leq j\leq n,0\leq b<n}$,
with no row or column reversal. The Vandermonde formula gives
\[
 \det V=\prod_{0\leq p<q<d}(\zeta^q-\zeta^p),\qquad
 \zeta^q-\zeta^p
       =2i e^{\pi i(p+q)/d}\sin\frac{\pi(q-p)}d.
 \tag{PDE10}
\]
Every displayed sine is positive. The phase of this product is
$\pi d(d-1)/4+\pi(d-1)^2/2
=\pi(d-1)(3d-2)/4$.
Its first term comes from the $d(d-1)/2$ factors $i$, and its second
from $\sum_{p<q}(p+q)=d(d-1)^2/2$.
Also $V^*V=dI$ by the finite geometric sum, so $|\det V|=d^{d/2}$.
The retained gamma product is
$\prod_{b=0}^{n-1}\Gamma((b+1)/d)=(2\pi)^{n/2}/\sqrt d$,
the multiplication identity used in IPM21, also recorded in DLMF
5.5.7 at \url{https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.5.E7}.
The powers of $d$ from PDE9 sum to $-n/2$ and cancel this product's
$d^{-1/2}$ against $d^{d/2}$. Its additional phase is
$\pi\sum_b\beta_b=\pi n/2$. The total phase is therefore
$3\pi n(n+1)/4$. Consequently the exact determinant is
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \det\Pi(\mathbf c,u)
   =(2\pi)^{n/2}u^{n/2}
       \exp\left(\frac{3\pi i n(n+1)}4
                         +\frac{\mathcal S(\mathbf c)}u\right).}
 \tag{PDE11}
\]
In particular it never vanishes for $u\ne0$, including coefficients
with repeated critical points or repeated critical values. This proves
invertibility rather than assuming it to use a determinant equation.
For $n=1$ the formula is the downward Gaussian for $u>0$ on
$\arg u=0$, continued on the specified branch:
$-i\sqrt{2\pi u}\exp((c_0-c_1^2/2)/u)$.

\subsection{The original full quartet and the exact complex-parameter transport}

For the original full quartet $n=4m$, retain
$\rho=1/2+\delta+i\gamma$, $0<\delta<1/2$, $\gamma>2$, and
$h=\prod_{r\in\{\rho,\bar\rho,1-\rho,1-\bar\rho\}}(s-r)^m$.
Its reflection is $h(1-s)=h(s)$. With the original primitive constant
$A=\Phi_h(1/2)$, differentiation and evaluation at $s=1/2$ prove
\[
 \Phi_h(1-s)=2A-\Phi_h(s),\qquad
 f_t(1-s)=2A-t-f_t(s),\qquad
 \mathcal S(f_t)=n(A-t/2).
 \tag{PDE12}
\]
For the trace assertion, reflection pairs the roots of $h(s)-t$ with
their full multiplicities. Each pair has the displayed sum of phase
values. A root fixed by reflection has $s=1/2$ and phase value
$A-t/2$ itself. Summing over all $n$ roots, or taking the trace of the
corresponding algebra involution, proves the identity even at every
multiple-root parameter. Hence on this original quartet
\[
 \det\Pi_h(t,u)
  =(2\pi)^{n/2}u^{n/2}
       e^{3\pi i n(n+1)/4}\,e^{n(A-t/2)/u}.
 \tag{PDE13}
\]
Neither the original $A$ nor the $t$ term has been discarded.

Direct differentiation in the single parameter $t$ gives the full
matrix equation, not only its determinant:
\[
 \Pi_h'(t)=-\frac1u\Pi_h(t)A_h(t),\qquad
 T(t_1,t_0)=\Pi_h(t_1)\Pi_h(t_0)^{-1},\qquad
 T(t_2,t_1)T(t_1,t_0)=T(t_2,t_0).
 \tag{PDE14}
\]
For the first $n-1$ columns this is differentiation by $-s/u$.
For the last use
$\mathcal I_t(s^n)=t\mathcal I_t(1)-\sum_{r<n}h_r\mathcal I_t(s^r)$,
which is the integral of $L_h1$. This includes the $n=1$ case.
PDE11 proves that every displayed inverse exists. The inverse arrow
is $T(t_0,t_1)$ and all its kernels are zero. This constructs an entire
transport between the actual fixed-ray polynomial period spaces for
all complex $t$. It is not an assertion that a chosen critical-path
incidence matrix stays unchanged when critical points collide.

These periods also have an explicit cochain retraction, including its
homotopy. Let $C_t=[\mathbb{C}[s]\xrightarrow{L_h}\mathbb{C}[s]ds]$
in degrees zero and one and $V=[0\to\mathbb{C}^n]$. Set
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 p^0=0,\quad p^1(Pds)=\mathcal I_t(P),\qquad
 i^0=0,\quad i^1(x)=\operatorname{poly}_{<n}(\Pi_h(t)^{-1}x)ds,\\
 H^1(Pds)=\sigma_{t,u}P,\quad H^0=0,\qquad
 pi=I_V,\qquad I_{C_t}-ip=dH+Hd.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{PDE14a}
\]
Here the finite leading-term division defining $\sigma_{t,u}$ and its
remainder is spelled out in PDE22. The boundary identity gives
$p^1L_h=0$, so $p$ is a cochain map. Uniqueness of that division gives
$\sigma_{t,u}L_h=I$, and period reduction gives
$i^1p^1=r_{t,u}$. Thus the degree-zero homotopy identity is
$\sigma_{t,u}L_h=I$ and the degree-one identity is
$L_h\sigma_{t,u}=I-r_{t,u}$, proving every assertion in PDE14a.
The polynomial complex has zero degree-zero cohomology and its
degree-one period map is an isomorphism. Multiplication by the entire
$v_h$ does not silently preserve this cochain retraction: its exact
differential defect is the derivative term in PDE24 below.

For independently varying parameters $t_1,\ldots,t_k$, the original
ordered tensor transport is $\bigotimes_i T(t_i,t_i^0)$. Its inverse is
the tensor of the stated inverses. Fubini on the proved absolute
integrals identifies it with the original product-ray map, including
the order $ds_1\wedge\cdots\wedge ds_k$. Its whole-target determinant is
\[
 \det\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^k\Pi_h(t_i,u)\right)
             =\prod_{i=1}^k(\det\Pi_h(t_i,u))^{n^{k-1}}.
 \tag{PDE15}
\]
For instance triangularize each finite matrix over $\mathbb{C}$;
the tensor diagonal consists of every ordered product of diagonal
entries, each factor occurring $n^{k-1}$ times. This proves PDE15
without deleting an ordered tensor direction. On the cyclic image
one instead uses the actual restricted Gram written below; the
whole-target determinant is not its determinant.

\subsection{The inhomogeneous equation retains the entire amplitude}

Keep $v=v_h$ fixed as $t$ varies and define, in the original frame,
\[
 \Psi(t)=(\mathcal I_t(vs^b))_{b=0}^{n-1},\quad
 b_v(t)=\mathcal I_t(v'),\quad
 U=M_{\upsilon_h},\quad H(t)=\Pi_h(t)^*\Pi_h(t).
 \tag{PDE16}
\]
The letter $b_v$ denotes the displayed target column, not a boundary
parameter or a coefficient-face label. The actual whole-amplitude
integration by parts is
\[
 \mathcal I_t(vs^n)
    =t\mathcal I_t(v)-\sum_{r<n}h_r\mathcal I_t(vs^r)
                             -u\mathcal I_t(v').
\]
It follows from integrating $u\partial_s(e^{f_t/u}v)$.
The outer endpoints vanish by the original entire-amplitude growth
bound; the two evaluations at the common origin cancel with their
opposite signs. Thus the exact equation is
\[
 \Psi'(t)=-\frac1u\Psi(t)A_h(t)+b_v(t)e_{n-1}^{\mathsf T}.
 \tag{PDE17}
\]
The derivative column is present even though $v$ is a unit in the
finite arithmetic quotient. It is an integral of the entire derivative,
not its finite Taylor replacement.

PDE14 and PDE17 prove by direct product differentiation
\[
 \begin{split}
 C_v(t)&:=\Psi(t)\Pi_h(t)^{-1}\ ,\\
 C_v(t)&=\Psi(0)\Pi_h(0)^{-1}
      +\int_0^t b_v(\tau)e_{n-1}^{\mathsf T}
                                         \Pi_h(\tau)^{-1}\,d\tau,\\
 \Psi(t)&=C_v(t)\Pi_h(t).
 \end{split}
 \tag{PDE18}
\]
All entries of the integrand are entire. Integrating their locally
convergent power series gives a primitive on the complex plane, so
the integral is path independent. This proves the displayed formula
on every path, with its complete initial source matrix. The initial
matrix is the actual whole-amplitude integral already constructed in
IPM9; it is not assumed equal to $U$ or to the identity.

To express the same map in the original coefficient target put
\[
 B_v(t)=\Pi_h(t)^{-1}\Psi(t),\quad E_v(t)=B_v(t)-U.
 \tag{PDE19}
\]
Its exact evolution and the full-unit commutator are
\[
 \begin{split}
 B_v'&=\frac1u[A_h(t),B_v]
                         +\Pi_h(t)^{-1}b_v(t)e_{n-1}^{\mathsf T},\\
 E_v'&=\frac1u[A_h(t),E_v]
          +\frac{t}{u}[R,U]
                         +\Pi_h(t)^{-1}b_v(t)e_{n-1}^{\mathsf T}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{PDE20}
\]
Here $[X,Y]=XY-YX$. Indeed $A_h$ and $U$ are the original commuting
multiplication operators on $E_h$, while $A_h(t)=A_h+tR$.
This proves the sign and source of the additional commutator.
For a direct formula in terms of PDE18,
$B_v(t)=\Pi_h(t)^{-1}C_v(t)\Pi_h(t)$.
The original finite-jet observation is $\Pi_h(t)U$, so its complete
Gram difference from the entire observation is
\[
 \Psi^*\Psi-U^*HU
       =U^*HE_v+E_v^*HU+E_v^*HE_v.
 \tag{PDE21}
\]
This identity keeps both original cross-pairings and the full Taylor
unit. The whole-amplitude map can have a kernel; it is exactly
$\ker B_v(t)$ because $\Pi_h(t)$ is invertible. No claim that this
kernel vanishes follows from PDE11.

\subsection{A finite original-source formula at every polynomial degree}

For each $N\geq0$, let $\mathcal P_N=\mathbb{C}[s]_{\leq N}$ and
$\mathcal P_r=0$ for $r<0$. Original leading-term subtraction constructs
the unique linear maps
\[
 \sigma_{t,u,N}:\mathcal P_N\longrightarrow\mathcal P_{N-n},\quad
 r_{t,u,N}:\mathcal P_N\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}[s]_{<n},\quad
 P=L_h\sigma_{t,u,N}P+r_{t,u,N}P,
 \quad L_h=u\partial_s+h-t.
 \tag{PDE22}
\]
At each step subtract the leading coefficient times
$L_h(s^{\deg P-n})$; its leading degree decreases strictly. This
terminates after finitely many steps. If two such decompositions
existed, $L_h$ of their quotient difference would have degree at least
$n$, unless it were zero, contradicting a nonzero remainder of degree
less than $n$. These operations prove uniqueness and linearity, and
give polynomial matrices in every original $h_r,t,u$.

Let $J_{h,N}=\operatorname{rem}_h$ be the original marked arithmetic
remainder. Applying it to PDE22 yields the exact map identity
\[
 r_{t,u,N}-J_{h,N}
    =J_h(t-u\partial_s)\sigma_{t,u,N}.
 \tag{PDE23}
\]
In particular differential reduction is not silently identified with
the arithmetic jet remainder at nonzero $u$.

Let $\mathcal I_t^{v'}(Q)=\mathcal I_t(v'Q)$ on
$\mathcal P_{N-n}$. Since
$vL_hQ=L_h(vQ)-u v'Q$, the original whole-amplitude observation and
its coefficient target have the finite exact expressions
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal O_N(t)P:=\mathcal I_t(vP)
    &=\Psi(t)r_{t,u,N}P-u\mathcal I_t^{v'}\sigma_{t,u,N}P,\\
 W_N(t):=\Pi_h(t)^{-1}\mathcal O_N(t)
    &=B_v(t)r_{t,u,N}
                  -u\Pi_h(t)^{-1}\mathcal I_t^{v'}\sigma_{t,u,N},\\
 W_N-UJ_{h,N}
    &=E_vr_{t,u,N}+U J_h(t-u\partial_s)\sigma_{t,u,N}
                  -u\Pi_h(t)^{-1}\mathcal I_t^{v'}\sigma_{t,u,N}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{PDE24}
\]
This is a finite formula at every requested degree; its derivative
columns are specified convergent integrals. Its coefficient and
whole-amplitude versions have the same exact kernel because the
receiving matrix $\Pi_h(t)$ is invertible. On an original relation
$P=hQ$ the formula, or direct integration by parts, gives
\[
 \mathcal O_N(t)(hQ)
       =t\mathcal I_t(vQ)-u\mathcal I_t(vQ')-u\mathcal I_t(v'Q).
 \tag{PDE25}
\]
This is exactly the original IPM12 relation image, with its product
derivative expanded. It has not been set to zero. On a truncated
local chain, PDE17 adds $-[e^{f_t/u}v]_{\partial\Gamma}
e_{n-1}^{\mathsf T}$, PDE24 adds
$u[e^{f_t/u}v\sigma_{t,u,N}P]_{\partial\Gamma}$ in its first line,
and PDE25 adds $u[e^{f_t/u}vQ]_{\partial\Gamma}$.
The signs follow from the same derivative identity. Gluing maps add
these endpoint evaluations with the original chain coefficients;
only common oppositely oriented evaluations cancel.

\subsection{Explicit bounds for the new derivative columns}

Use the original positive growth envelope from IPM3,
\[
 \mathcal X(R)=1+2C_\theta R(R+1)\pi^{-(R+1)/2}
                          \Gamma((R+1)/2,\pi),\quad
 C_\theta=(1-e^{-3\pi})^{-1},\quad R\geq1.
 \tag{PDE26}
\]
It is nondecreasing: its incomplete-gamma factor is the integral
$\int_1^\infty e^{-\pi x}x^{(R-1)/2}\,dx$, whose integrand is
nondecreasing in $R$. IPM4 proves
$|v(s)|\leq2^n|s|^{-n}\mathcal X(|s|)$ for
$|s|\geq\max(1,2R_h)$, where $R_h$ is the maximum root modulus.
For $r=|s|\geq\max(2,4R_h)$, the disc of radius $r/2$ about $s$
has $r/2\leq|z|\leq3r/2$. Cauchy's derivative formula and the
maximum bound on that disc prove
\[
 |v'(s)|\leq 2^{2n+1}r^{-n-1}\mathcal X(3r/2).
 \tag{PDE27}
\]
For radii $R_2>R_0>R_1\geq\max(2,4R_h)$ the maximum principle on
$|z|\leq R_2$ and the same derivative formula give, for every $b\geq0$,
\[
 \max_{|s|=R_0}|v'(s)s^b|
      \leq M_b'
      :=\frac{2^nR_0^b R_2^{-n}\mathcal X(R_2)}{R_2-R_0}.
 \tag{PDE28}
\]
The amplitude remains $v'(s)s^b$; $M_b'$ is its displayed upper bound.

Write $f_t=s^{n+1}/(n+1)+\sum_{j=0}^n c_j(t)s^j$ and put
$E_t(r)=\exp[-r^{n+1}/((n+1)|u|)+\sum_{j=0}^n|c_j(t)|r^j/|u|]$.
Integrate the original Taylor polynomial of $v'(s)s^b$ through degree
$L-1$ on the two finite ray segments of radius $R_1$. Its entrywise
error against $\mathcal I_t(v's^b)$ is at most
\[
 \begin{split}
 e_{b,L}'(t)
 &=\frac{2M_b'}{(1-R_1/R_0)R_0^L}
                  \int_0^{R_1}E_t(r)r^L\,dr\\
 &\quad+2^{2n+2}\int_{R_1}^\infty
             E_t(r)r^{b-n-1}\mathcal X(3r/2)\,dr .
 \end{split}
 \tag{PDE29}
\]
The first term is the exact Cauchy geometric-series tail bound,
integrated on both rays; the second is PDE27 on both exterior rays.
Their convergence follows from the negative degree-$n+1$ phase term
and $\log\mathcal X(R)=O(R\log R)$, the explicit estimate of IPM3.
Thus PDE29 supplies finite entry errors for every new column in
PDE18 and PDE24; for a vector of $n$ entries its Euclidean error is
at most $\sqrt n$ times this entry bound. Multiplication by the
explicit finite matrices in PDE24 retains their actual norms and
all their original coefficients. It does not assert a bound uniform
in a separate growing tensor degree.

There is also an explicit inverse bound with the full phase constant
present. For the original full quartet and $|t|\leq T$, set
\[
 \begin{split}
 E_T(r)&=\exp\left[-\frac{r^{n+1}}{(n+1)|u|}
            +\frac{\sum_{j=0}^n|c_j(0)|r^j+Tr}{|u|}\right],\\
 p_T&=\left(n\sum_{b=0}^{n-1}
                      \left(2\int_0^\infty E_T(r)r^b\,dr\right)^2
                \right)^{1/2},\\
 d_T&=(2\pi|u|)^{n/2}
                 \exp\left(\operatorname{Re}\frac{nA}{u}
                                      -\frac{nT}{2|u|}\right).
 \end{split}
 \tag{PDE30}
\]
The Frobenius entry bound proves $\|\Pi_h(t)\|\leq p_T$; PDE13
proves $|\det\Pi_h(t)|\geq d_T$. The product of its singular values
is $|\det\Pi_h(t)|$, and each of the largest $n-1$ is at most
$p_T$. Therefore
\[
 \|\Pi_h(t)^{-1}\|\leq p_T^{n-1}/d_T\quad(|t|\leq T).
 \tag{PDE31}
\]
For example a bound $b_T$ for $\|b_v(t)\|$ is obtained by the same
two-ray formula as PDE29, using the full compact geometric bound
$M_0'/(1-r/R_0)$ instead of its Taylor tail and retaining the same
exterior integral. Taking the maximum coefficients on $|t|\leq T$
uses $E_T$ directly. Integration along the straight segment then gives
\[
 \|C_v(t)-C_v(0)\|
            \leq |t|b_T p_T^{n-1}/d_T.
 \tag{PDE32}
\]
This is a compact-parameter bound on the actual inhomogeneous
transport. The constants depend on the full original packet and
$u$ as displayed. None has been declared harmless as $k$ grows.

\subsection{Return to the original cyclic source and every support face}

Keep the full quartet cyclic polynomial and degree from PGS3, explicitly
\[
 \chi_{h,k}(S)=\prod_{a,b=0}^k
 \left(S-\frac k2-(2a-k)\delta-i(2b-k)\gamma\right)^{1+k(m-1)},
 \quad q=[1+k(m-1)](k+1)^2.
 \tag{PDE33a}
\]
The original cyclic remainder space is $E=\mathbb{C}[S]/(\chi_{h,k})$,
in its increasing monomial frame, and $J_{\chi,N}P=[P]$.
For the original
$\mathcal P_N^S=\mathbb{C}[S]_{\leq N}$ define the literal polynomial
inclusion, before any finite quotient,
\[
 I_{k,N}(S^j)=\sum_{b_1+\cdots+b_k=j}
                       \frac{j!}{\prod_i b_i!}\bigotimes_i s_i^{b_i}.
 \tag{PDE33}
\]
Every $b_i$ is nonnegative and at most $N$. Substitution
$s_2=\cdots=s_k=0$ is a left inverse, so this inclusion has zero
kernel. With $W_N(t_i)$ from PDE24 put
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \boldsymbol\Pi=\bigotimes_i\Pi_h(t_i,u),\qquad
 \mathcal W_N=\left(\bigotimes_i W_N(t_i)\right)I_{k,N},\qquad
 \mathcal O_{k,N}=\boldsymbol\Pi\mathcal W_N,\\
 \mathcal J_N=\left(\bigotimes_iJ_{h,N}\right)I_{k,N}
                  =\iota J_{\chi,N},\qquad
 \mathcal Z_N=U^{\otimes k}\mathcal J_N,\qquad
 \mathcal E_N=\mathcal W_N-\mathcal Z_N.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{PDE34}
\]
The original successive arithmetic division proves the displayed
identity for $\mathcal J_N$, including every primary multiplicity;
$\iota$ is the original cyclic inclusion into $E_h^{\otimes k}$.
Fubini and PDE24 prove the whole-amplitude identity on every actual
column, with all multinomial coefficients retained. On
$\operatorname{im}B_N=\chi\mathcal P_{N-q}^S$, its image is exactly
the IPM15b derivative-and-boundary relation image. Define
\[
 \mathcal R_N=\mathcal O_{k,N}(\operatorname{im}B_N),\quad
 \overline{\mathcal R}_N
    =\mathcal W_N(\operatorname{im}B_N),\quad
 \boldsymbol\Pi:\overline{\mathcal R}_N\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
                                                   \mathcal R_N.
 \tag{PDE35}
\]
Its inverse is the restriction of the full tensor inverse. For
$N\geq q-1$, it induces the exact isomorphism of receiving quotients and their induced

For $0\leq N<q-1$, the same receiving-subspace isomorphism holds,

\subsection{The correctly typed primary coordinate extension on the full quartet}

The example attached to IPM32 needs an explicit coefficient arrow:
its full-quartet space has dimension $q$, whereas the identical-primary
matrix of IPM25 has dimension $q_\rho=1+k(m-1)$. Here is that arrow,
with its inverse and derived fibre. In this subsection $v$ denotes
the original ramification coordinate of IPM25; the entire amplitude
is always written $v_h$.

Put $\kappa=k\rho$ and $\chi=(S-\kappa)^{q_\rho}\chi_\circ$.
The other centres in PDE33a are distinct from $\kappa$ because
$\delta\gamma\ne0$. Let $t_\kappa(S)$ be the Taylor polynomial of
$1/\chi_\circ(S)$ at $\kappa$ through order $q_\rho-1$, and let
$e_\kappa=[\chi_\circ t_\kappa]\in E$. Then its residue on the
$\kappa$ primary is one and its residue on every other primary is
zero. Polynomial divisibility by all the relatively prime full powers
proves $e_\kappa^2=e_\kappa$. Define
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 E_\kappa=\mathbb{C}[X]/(X^{q_\rho}),\quad
 p_\kappa[P]=\sum_{j=0}^{q_\rho-1}\frac{P^{(j)}(\kappa)}{j!}X^j,
 \quad i_\kappa[X^j]=e_\kappa(S-\kappa)^j,\\
 p_\kappa i_\kappa=I,\qquad
 i_\kappa p_\kappa=M_{e_\kappa},\qquad
 E=i_\kappa E_\kappa\oplus\ker p_\kappa.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{PDE37}
\]
The equalities follow by the displayed Taylor jets on each full
primary, so they prove the decomposition in the original coefficient
algebra, without choosing values on only the reduced roots.

Let $D_{k,v}X^j=v^{k+j}X^j$ as in IPM25. On $v\ne0$ set
\[
 U_v^+=(I-i_\kappa p_\kappa)+i_\kappa D_{k,v}p_\kappa,
 \qquad
 U_v^-=(I-i_\kappa p_\kappa)+i_\kappa D_{k,v}^{-1}p_\kappa.
 \tag{PDE38}
\]
PDE37 proves $U_v^+U_v^-=U_v^-U_v^+=I_E$ by multiplication.
They are genuine maps on the full $q$-dimensional space, with zero
kernels on this domain. Their determinants and absolute costs are
\[
 \det U_v^+=v^{kq_\rho+q_\rho(q_\rho-1)/2},\quad
 \log|\det U_v^-|^2
       =-2\left(kq_\rho+\frac{q_\rho(q_\rho-1)}2\right)\log|v|.
 \tag{PDE39}
\]
The complement in PDE37 is acted on by the identity. Including an
original primary scalar $e^F$ in that block multiplies its determinant
by $e^{q_\rho F}$; a scalar on the entire space instead contributes
$e^{qF}$. These are their actual different exponents.
Thus PDE38 supplies a correctly typed example for IPM32 and its
IPM36 cost. This coefficient extension is not identified with the
full-packet analytic normal-coordinate map; that separate map retains
the full phase, Jacobian and packet comparisons of IAL/IPM.

For the ordinary boundary specialization use the regular map $U_v^+$
over $\mathcal O=\mathbb{C}[a][[v]]$. On the primary it has cokernel
\[

                     =kq_\rho+q_\rho(q_\rho-1)/2,
 \tag{PDE40}
\]
