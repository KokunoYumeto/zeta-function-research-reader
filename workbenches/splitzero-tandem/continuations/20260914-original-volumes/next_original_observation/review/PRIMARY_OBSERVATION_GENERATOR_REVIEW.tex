\section{Independent derivation of the original observation generator}
\label{sec:POGR}

Retain the four distinct roots $\mathcal R=\{\rho,\bar\rho,
1-\rho,1-\bar\rho\}$, their common complete multiplicity $m$,
$h(X)=\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal R}(X-\alpha)^m$, and the entire unit
$v_h=g/h$ from BRU1. Let $n=4m$, $E_h=\mathbb C[X]/(h)$,
$A_h=M_X$, and $U=M_{[v_h]}$ on this coefficient algebra. On the
original packet $E=\mathbb C[S]/(\chi)$ let $A=M_S$,
$c=k/2$, and $Y=(A-cI)/i$. The inclusion
$\iota[P]=[P(X_1+\cdots+X_k)]$ and the unchanged maps are
\[
 V=\Pi^{\otimes k}U^{\otimes k}\iota,
 \qquad L=P_kV=\jmath\Lambda,
 \qquad K=\ker\Lambda.
 \tag{POGR1}
\]
Here $\Pi$ acts on the increasing polynomial coefficient frame of
$E_h$, using the original oriented ray integrals, and $P_k$ is the
literal averaging idempotent in BRU3. All coordinate maps in POGR1
remain the original ones. The only source orders are $s\in\{1,k\}$,
with $b=s/2$, $M_s=(2\pi)^{s/2}$ and $h_d=M_s d!(b)_d$.

\subsection{The generating identity with its original scalar and phase}
Let $r_d=p_d(Y)[1]$, where
$p_0=1$, $p_1=y$ and
$p_{d+1}=yp_d-d(b+d-1)p_{d-1}$. Set
$\ell_d=\Lambda r_d$ and $\lambda_d=\ell_d/\sqrt{h_d}$.
As a convergent germ at $z=0$, with the branches fixed there by
$\arctan 0=0$ and $\log(1+0^2)=0$, one has
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal F(z)&=\sum_{d=0}^\infty r_d\frac{z^d}{d!}
   =(1+z^2)^{-b/2}\exp((\arctan z)Y)[1],\\
 \jmath\sum_{d=0}^\infty\sqrt{h_d}\lambda_d\frac{z^d}{d!}
   &=(1+z^2)^{-s/4}P_k
    \left(\Pi U\exp\left(\frac{\arctan z}{i}
                         (A_h-\tfrac12 I)\right)[1]\right)^{\otimes k}.
 \end{split}\tag{POGR2}
\]
To prove convergence and the first identity without assuming a
special-function generating formula, define its displayed analytic
right side for $|z|<1$. Differentiation gives
$(1+z^2)\mathcal F'=(Y-bzI)\mathcal F$ and
$\mathcal F(0)=[1]$. Equating Taylor coefficients gives exactly
$r_0=[1]$, $r_1=Y[1]$, and the displayed recurrence, which uniquely
specify every coefficient. For the second identity, the quotient
inclusion intertwines $Y$ with
$\sum_{a=1}^k(A_h-\tfrac12 I)_a/i$. The summands commute, so the
exponential factors into their ordered tensor product. Apply $V$
and then $P_k$. This proves both the phase $1/i$ and the fact that
the source scalar $(1+z^2)^{-s/4}$ occurs once outside the tensor.
The identity uses the centre $k/2$ for either permitted source order.

In particular no convergence assertion beyond the stated germ is
needed to compute any of the original rows. Every coefficient through
degree $2q$ is a finite derivative of POGR2 at zero. Explicitly,
\[
 r_d=d!\sum_{a=0}^d\frac{Y^a[1]}{a!}
 [z^d]\left((1+z^2)^{-s/4}(\arctan z)^a\right).
 \tag{POGR3}
\]
This identity follows by expanding the finite-order Taylor coefficient
of the matrix exponential: terms of exponential degree greater than
$d$ have a zero of order greater than $d$ at zero.

\subsection{Finite primary polynomial before every period integral}
For $\alpha\in\mathcal R$ set
$H_\alpha(X)=h(X)/(X-\alpha)^m$ and define the polynomial
\[
 \begin{split}
 E_\alpha(X)&=H_\alpha(X)
  \sum_{v=0}^{m-1}\frac{(1/H_\alpha)^{(v)}(\alpha)}{v!}
                  (X-\alpha)^v,\\
 B_{\alpha,j}(X)&=\operatorname{rem}_h
                         \bigl(E_\alpha(X)(X-\alpha)^j\bigr),
                         \qquad 0\leq j<m.
 \end{split}\tag{POGR4}
\]
The inverse in this formula is holomorphic near $\alpha$, since
$H_\alpha(\alpha)\ne0$. Its truncated Taylor series gives
$E_\alpha\equiv1\pmod{(X-\alpha)^m}$. At every other primary root
$\beta$, $H_\alpha$ is divisible by $(X-\beta)^m$, so
$E_\alpha\equiv0$ there. Consequently $B_{\alpha,j}$ has local
class $(X-\alpha)^j$ at $\alpha$ and zero at each other root.
The difference of two polynomials of degree less than $n$ with the
same local classes is divisible by all four relatively prime factors,
and hence by their degree-$n$ product. It is therefore zero. This
also proves directly the uniqueness and completeness of these
$4m$ primary coefficient vectors, with every monic remainder fixed.

For a new complex parameter $\vartheta$ (the original deformation
parameter remains fixed at $t=0$), the unique degree-less-than-$n$
polynomial representative of
$U\exp(\vartheta(A_h-\tfrac12 I)/i)[1]$ is exactly
\[
 T_\vartheta(X)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal R}
 e^{\vartheta(\alpha-1/2)/i}
 \sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\sum_{a=0}^{j}
 \frac{v_h^{(a)}(\alpha)}{a!}
 \frac{(\vartheta/i)^{j-a}}{(j-a)!}\,B_{\alpha,j}(X).
 \tag{POGR5}
\]
Indeed the product rule shows that the coefficient of
$(X-\alpha)^j$ in the local Taylor expansion of
$v_h(X)e^{\vartheta(X-1/2)/i}$ is precisely the coefficient multiplying
$B_{\alpha,j}$. POGR4 and uniqueness now prove POGR5. Thus this
calculation retains $v_h^{(a)}(\alpha)$ for every $0\leq a<m$;
it never assigns a value to the unit or deletes its higher primary jets.

Let $\Gamma_\nu$ and $\Phi_h,u$ be the oriented rays, phase,
and fixed nonzero parameter of BRU3. Define the full period vectors
\[
 \pi_{\alpha,j}=
 \left(\int_{\Gamma_\nu}e^{\Phi_h(X)/u}
                   B_{\alpha,j}(X)\,dX\right)_{\nu=1}^{n}.
 \tag{POGR6}
\]
Each $B_{\alpha,j}$ is the specified finite polynomial. Its integral
is a finite linear combination of the original entries of $\Pi$,
so the inherited convergence of those entries suffices. Therefore
\[
 \Pi U e^{\vartheta(A_h-1/2)/i}[1]
  =\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal R}e^{\vartheta(\alpha-1/2)/i}
    \sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\sum_{a=0}^{j}
     \frac{v_h^{(a)}(\alpha)}{a!}
     \frac{(\vartheta/i)^{j-a}}{(j-a)!}\,\pi_{\alpha,j}.
 \tag{POGR7}
\]
This is a coefficientwise application of the original period matrix.
The integrand is the polynomial $T_\vartheta(X)$ of POGR5. Replacing it
inside the rays by the unreduced entire function
$v_h(X)e^{\vartheta(X-1/2)/i}$ would require an additional integral identity;
POGR4--7 do not make that substitution.

For complete explicitness set
$\boldsymbol\alpha=(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k)$,
$0\leq a_i\leq j_i<m$ and $J=\sum_i(j_i-a_i)$. The right side
of POGR2, after its scalar factor, is the finite sum
\[
 \sum_{\boldsymbol\alpha,\boldsymbol j,\boldsymbol a}
 e^{\vartheta\sum_i(\alpha_i-1/2)/i}
 (\vartheta/i)^J
 \left(\prod_{i=1}^k
       \frac{v_h^{(a_i)}(\alpha_i)}{a_i!(j_i-a_i)!}\right)
 P_k(\pi_{\alpha_1,j_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\pi_{\alpha_k,j_k}),
 \qquad \vartheta=\arctan z.
 \tag{POGR8}
\]
The polynomial degree in $\vartheta$ at each ordered primary tuple is at most
$k(m-1)$, the original nilpotent height. The exponential exponent
uses the actual sum of the primary roots. Expanding the finite tensor
product in POGR7 proves POGR8 with every factorial and orientation
retained. The original averaging matrix acts after this expansion.

\subsection{The exact action defect and a closed coupled recurrence}
Fix the original section $S_*:B\to E$, $\Lambda S_*=I_B$, and the
original kernel inclusion $I:K\hookrightarrow E$. The map
$[I,S_*]:K\oplus B\to E$ is a bijection: its inverse sends
$x$ to $(I^{-1}(x-S_*\Lambda x),\Lambda x)$, with $I^{-1}$ only
applied to the displayed kernel vector. Define four actual blocks
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 A_{KK}=I^{-1}(1-S_*\Lambda)YI,&
 A_{KB}=I^{-1}(1-S_*\Lambda)YS_*,\\
 A_{BK}=\Lambda YI,& A_{BB}=\Lambda YS_*.
 \end{array}\tag{POGR9}
\]
These have types $K\to K$, $B\to K$, $K\to B$, and $B\to B$,
respectively. Their definition is the full conjugation of $Y$ by
$[I,S_*]$; it deletes no block. Put
$k_d=I^{-1}(r_d-S_*\ell_d)$. Applying the two inverse coordinate
maps to the exact recurrence for $r_d$ gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 k_{d+1}&=A_{KK}k_d+A_{KB}\ell_d-d(b+d-1)k_{d-1},\\
 \ell_{d+1}&=A_{BK}k_d+A_{BB}\ell_d-d(b+d-1)\ell_{d-1}.
 \end{split}\tag{POGR10}
\]
At $d=0$ the terms with index $-1$ have coefficient zero. The initial
vectors are the exact kernel and boundary components of $[1]$.
The second line alone has the retained defect $A_{BK}k_d$. It is a
closed recurrence on $B$ for every input precisely when
$A_{BK}=0$, which is equivalent to $YK\subseteq K$:
$\Lambda YI=0$ says exactly that every $YI\kappa$ lies in
$\ker\Lambda$. No such invariance is assumed here. Even when the
defect vanishes on some particular vectors, that alone does not
establish this all-input statement.

In the original source coordinates put
$\kappa_d=k_d/\sqrt{h_d}$ and
$a_d=\sqrt{(d+1)(b+d)}$, with $a_{-1}=0$. Since
$h_{d+1}/h_d=a_d^2$ and $d(b+d-1)=a_{d-1}^2$, POGR10 is
equivalently
\[
 \begin{split}
 a_d\kappa_{d+1}
   &=A_{KK}\kappa_d+A_{KB}\lambda_d-a_{d-1}\kappa_{d-1},\\
 a_d\lambda_{d+1}
   &=A_{BK}\kappa_d+A_{BB}\lambda_d-a_{d-1}\lambda_{d-1}.
 \end{split}\tag{POGR11}
\]
Thus all masses in $\lambda_d$ remain exactly those of GMB1 and
the recurrence retains the complete original kernel feedback.

There is also a literal ambient form of the same defect. Since
$\Pi$ is invertible, define on $(\mathbb C^n)^{\otimes k}$
\[
 \mathcal T=\sum_{a=1}^k
 \left(\Pi\frac{A_h-\tfrac12 I}{i}\Pi^{-1}\right)_a.
 \tag{POGR12}
\]
Multiplication by $v_h$ commutes with $A_h$, and the original cyclic
inclusion intertwines the sum action, so $VY=\mathcal TV$.
Using $P_k^2=P_k$ gives the exact identity
\[
 \begin{split}
 LY&=P_k\mathcal T L+P_k\mathcal T(1-P_k)V,\\
 \jmath A_{BK}&=P_k\mathcal T(1-P_k)VI,\\
 \jmath\ell_{d+1}
 &=P_k\mathcal T\jmath\ell_d
   +P_k\mathcal T(1-P_k)Vr_d
   -d(b+d-1)\jmath\ell_{d-1}.
 \end{split}\tag{POGR13}
\]
The middle equality uses $P_kVI=LI=0$, so $VI=(1-P_k)VI$.
Each identity follows by multiplication of the displayed matrices;
it includes the finite projection and its discarded ambient component.
The total right side of the last identity belongs to $\jmath B$.
Neither summand involving $\mathcal T$ individually is asserted to
preserve that image. Equations POGR9--11 are the fully typed recurrence
inside $K\oplus B$, with all terms defined even when an ambient
compressed action fails to preserve $\jmath B$.
