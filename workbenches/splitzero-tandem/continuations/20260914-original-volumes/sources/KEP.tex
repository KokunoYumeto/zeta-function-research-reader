\subsection{Constructive existence and uniqueness of the two endpoints}
\label{sec:shifted-gamma-endpoint-existence}

We retain the original positive coordinate $x$, the parameter
$0\leq\lambda\leq1/4$, and the exact parameter map $\kappa=2\lambda$.
The derivative of the potential under consideration is
\begin{equation}\tag{KEP1}
 V'_\lambda(x)=\int_\kappa^\infty\frac{du}{x+u^2}-\frac2x,
 \qquad x>0.
\end{equation}
We prove directly that there is exactly one ordered pair $0<a<b$ solving
the two endpoint equations
\begin{equation}\tag{KEP2}
 \int_\kappa^\infty\frac{du}{\sqrt{(a+u^2)(b+u^2)}}
       =\frac2{\sqrt{ab}},\qquad
 \int_\kappa^\infty\left(1-
       \frac{u^2}{\sqrt{(a+u^2)(b+u^2)}}\right)du=4.
\end{equation}
The proof supplies a monotone construction, its exact parameter derivative,
and uniform positive endpoint bounds on the full parameter interval. It
uses no theorem asserting the number of support intervals of an
equilibrium measure.

For positive $a,b$, allowing $a=b$ during the construction, set
\begin{equation}\tag{KEP3}
 R_u=\sqrt{(a+u^2)(b+u^2)},\qquad
 G_\kappa(a,b)=\sqrt{ab}\int_\kappa^\infty\frac{du}{R_u},\qquad
 B_\kappa(a,b)=\int_\kappa^\infty\left(1-\frac{u^2}{R_u}\right)du.
\end{equation}
These definitions are in the original $x$ coordinate: in particular a
diagonal value $a=b=s$ below means the endpoint $x=s$, not $x=\sqrt{s}$.
The integrands are positive. Their convergence at infinity follows from
the explicit inequalities, valid for every $T>0$,
\begin{equation}\tag{KEP4}
 0<\int_T^\infty\frac{du}{R_u}\leq\frac1T,\qquad
 0<\int_T^\infty\left(1-\frac{u^2}{R_u}\right)du
 \leq\frac{a+b}{2T}+\frac{ab}{6T^3}.
\end{equation}
Indeed $R_u\geq u^2$, and the exact rationalization
\[
 1-\frac{u^2}{R_u}
 =\frac{(a+b)u^2+ab}{R_u(R_u+u^2)}
 \leq\frac{a+b}{2u^2}+\frac{ab}{2u^4}
\]
proves the second estimate. At finite $u$, positive $a,b$ keep the
denominators nonzero. Both functions are smooth in $a,b>0$ and $\kappa\geq0$.
To justify this assertion explicitly, on each compact positive set of
$a,b$, every fixed order of $a,b$ derivatives of the integrands is bounded
at finite $u$ and is $O(u^{-2})$ at infinity. A $\kappa$ derivative evaluates
an integrand or one of its derivatives at its finite lower limit. The
same formulas extend smoothly to $\kappa$ in a neighbourhood of zero, by
allowing a short negative lower-limit interval; this is only a local
parameter extension of the integrals, without changing the original
domain $\kappa\geq0$.

Subscripts $a,b$ in the following equations denote partial derivatives
with $\kappa$ fixed. Direct differentiation, including the prefactor
$\sqrt{ab}$ in $G_\kappa$, gives
\begin{align}\tag{KEP5}
 (B_\kappa)_a&=\frac12\int_\kappa^\infty
               \frac{u^2\,du}{(a+u^2)R_u}>0,
 & (B_\kappa)_b&=\frac12\int_\kappa^\infty
               \frac{u^2\,du}{(b+u^2)R_u}>0,\\
 (G_\kappa)_a&=\sqrt{\frac ba}(B_\kappa)_a>0,
 & (G_\kappa)_b&=\sqrt{\frac ab}(B_\kappa)_b>0.\notag
\end{align}
Strict positivity holds also at $\kappa=0$, because the displayed integrands
are strictly positive for every $u>0$. For example
\[
 \partial_a\left(\frac{\sqrt{ab}}{R_u}\right)
 =\frac12\sqrt{\frac ba}\frac{u^2}{(a+u^2)R_u},
\]
which proves the first derivative identity for $G_\kappa$ without dropping
its coefficient.

Fix $\kappa\geq0$. On the diagonal the exact value is
\begin{equation}\tag{KEP6}
 G_\kappa(s,s)=B_\kappa(s,s)
 =s\int_\kappa^\infty\frac{du}{s+u^2}
 =\sqrt{s}\left(\frac\pi2-\arctan\frac \kappa{\sqrt{s}}\right).
\end{equation}
This is strictly increasing with $s>0$ by (KEP5), tends to zero as
$s\downarrow0$, and tends to infinity as $s\to\infty$. The first limit
also follows from its upper bound $\pi\sqrt{s}/2$; the last follows
because the parenthetical expression tends to $\pi/2$. There is therefore
exactly one $s=s(\kappa)>0$ with
\begin{equation}\tag{KEP7}
 G_\kappa(s,s)=2,
 \qquad B_\kappa(s,s)=2.
\end{equation}

For each $0<a<s$, there is exactly one $b=b_\kappa(a)>s$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\tag{KEP8}
 G_\kappa(a,b_\kappa(a))=2.
\end{equation}
To prove the assertion, first $G_\kappa(a,s)<G_\kappa(s,s)=2$ by strict
monotonicity. For fixed $a>0$, the integral representation
\[
 G_\kappa(a,b)=\int_\kappa^\infty
  \frac{\sqrt a}{\sqrt{a+u^2}}
  \frac{\sqrt b}{\sqrt{b+u^2}}\,du
\]
increases as $b\to\infty$ to the divergent integral
$\int_\kappa^\infty\sqrt a/\sqrt{a+u^2}\,du$.
Monotone convergence thus proves $G_\kappa(a,b)\to\infty$.
Continuity and strict monotonicity in $b$ prove (KEP8) and its uniqueness.
The nonzero derivative $(G_\kappa)_b$ proves that $b_\kappa(a)$ is smooth, with
the exact derivative
\begin{equation}\tag{KEP9}
 b_\kappa'(a)=-\frac{(G_\kappa)_a}{(G_\kappa)_b}
        =-\frac ba\frac{(B_\kappa)_a}{(B_\kappa)_b}<0,
 \qquad b=b_\kappa(a).
\end{equation}

We need both end limits of this entire curve. As $a\uparrow s$,
$b_\kappa(a)$ decreases to $s$. Indeed it decreases to a finite limit at
least $s$, since it is bounded above by any one value $b_\kappa(a_0)$ with
$a_0<s$. Continuity of $G_\kappa$ and uniqueness of the solution
$G_\kappa(s,b)=2$ identify that limit as $s$. At the other end,
\begin{equation}\tag{KEP10}
 b_\kappa(a)\longrightarrow\infty\quad\hbox{as }a\downarrow0.
\end{equation}
Otherwise monotonicity would bound $b_\kappa(a)$ by a finite $M$ for all
sufficiently small $a$, whence $2=G_\kappa(a,b_\kappa(a))\leq G_\kappa(a,M)$.
The latter quantity tends to zero: its integrand tends to zero at
every $u>0$, and for $0<a\leq a_0$ it is dominated by the integrable
integrand defining $G_\kappa(a_0,M)$. The single value $u=0$, when present
as an endpoint, has zero measure. Dominated convergence gives the
contradiction.

Now define the second endpoint functional on this curve by
$Q_\kappa(a)=B_\kappa(a,b_\kappa(a))$, for $0<a<s$. Its derivative, with the full
coefficient from (KEP9), is
\begin{equation}\tag{KEP11}
 Q_\kappa'(a)=(B_\kappa)_a+(B_\kappa)_b b_\kappa'(a)
       =\left(1-\frac ba\right)(B_\kappa)_a<0.
\end{equation}
At the diagonal end, continuity and (KEP7) give
$Q_\kappa(a)\to2$ as $a\uparrow s$. At the other end $Q_\kappa(a)\to\infty$.
For the latter assertion, once $\sqrt b>\kappa$ we have the concrete lower
bound
\begin{equation}\tag{KEP12}
 B_\kappa(a,b)\geq\int_\kappa^{\sqrt b}
               \left(1-\frac u{\sqrt b}\right)du
 =\frac{\sqrt b}{2}-\kappa+\frac{\kappa^2}{2\sqrt b}.
\end{equation}
The inequality follows from $R_u\geq u\sqrt b$ for $u\geq0$ and
the nonnegative integrand of $B_\kappa$ outside the indicated interval.
At $u=0$ it follows by continuity. Equation (KEP10) makes the last
expression tend to infinity. The intermediate value theorem and the
strict derivative in (KEP11) now give exactly one
\begin{equation}\tag{KEP13}
 a(\kappa)\in(0,s(\kappa))\quad\hbox{with}\quad Q_\kappa(a(\kappa))=4,
 \qquad b(\kappa)=b_\kappa(a(\kappa))>s(\kappa).
\end{equation}
These are the required endpoints in (KEP2).

This construction covers every possible ordered solution. In fact if
$a<b$ and $G_\kappa(a,b)=2$, then
$G_\kappa(a,a)<2<G_\kappa(b,b)$, so necessarily $a<s(\kappa)<b$.
Uniqueness of (KEP8) then places the pair on the same curve, and the
strict monotonicity in (KEP11) proves that the second equation can
hold only at (KEP13). Thus the endpoint uniqueness is proved directly
from the actual integrals, before any use of global energy uniqueness.

The construction also provides finite monotone brackets. First bracket
the diagonal solution of (KEP7) by positivity and its two end limits.
For a chosen $a<s$, increase $b$ until $G_\kappa(a,b)>2$ and then use
strict monotonicity to locate (KEP8). Take $a=s/2^j$ successively;
the limit $Q_\kappa(a)\to\infty$ ensures that a finite $j$ gives
$Q_\kappa(a)>4$, whereas the diagonal end has value $2$.
These values bracket the unique solution of (KEP13).
The omitted positive tails in evaluating either integral have the
explicit bounds (KEP4), with an additional factor $\sqrt{ab}$ for
$G_\kappa$. This supplies a construction by the displayed original
integrals and monotone root intervals; no numerical root is assumed
in the existence proof.

We next prove parameter regularity and retain its exact derivative.
The endpoint Jacobian at any ordered positive pair is
\begin{equation}\tag{KEP14}
 \mathcal J_\kappa(a,b):=
 \det\begin{pmatrix}(G_\kappa)_a&(G_\kappa)_b\\
                    (B_\kappa)_a&(B_\kappa)_b\end{pmatrix}
 =\frac{b-a}{\sqrt{ab}}(B_\kappa)_a(B_\kappa)_b>0.
\end{equation}
The integral smoothness proved after (KEP4) and this nonzero determinant
give smooth dependence of the locally determined solution on $\kappa$.
Uniqueness of the ordered solution makes all local solutions agree
where their parameter intervals overlap. Hence $a(\kappa),b(\kappa)$ are smooth
for all $\kappa\geq0$, including one-sided derivatives of every order at
zero and a smooth local extension through zero. The lower-limit
derivatives used here are exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{KEP15}
 \partial_\kappa G_\kappa(a,b)=-\frac{\sqrt{ab}}{R_\kappa},\qquad
 \partial_\kappa B_\kappa(a,b)=-\left(1-\frac{\kappa^2}{R_\kappa}\right),
 \qquad R_\kappa=\sqrt{(a+\kappa^2)(b+\kappa^2)}.
\end{equation}
Differentiating both equations at the endpoints gives the original
two-coordinate linear system
\begin{equation}\tag{KEP16}
 \begin{pmatrix}(G_\kappa)_a&(G_\kappa)_b\\
                (B_\kappa)_a&(B_\kappa)_b\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}a'(\kappa)\\b'(\kappa)\end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{ab}/R_\kappa\\1-\kappa^2/R_\kappa\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad (a,b)=(a(\kappa),b(\kappa)).
\end{equation}
Cramer's rule, followed by the exact identities in (KEP5), yields
\begin{align}\tag{KEP17}
 a'(\kappa)&=
 \frac{\sqrt{a/b}(B_\kappa)_b}{\mathcal J_\kappa(a,b)}
       \left(\frac{b+\kappa^2}{R_\kappa}-1\right)>0,\\
 b'(\kappa)&=
 \frac{\sqrt{b/a}(B_\kappa)_a}{\mathcal J_\kappa(a,b)}
       \left(1-\frac{a+\kappa^2}{R_\kappa}\right)>0.\notag
\end{align}
Both strict signs follow from
$a+\kappa^2<R_\kappa<b+\kappa^2$, which holds because $a<b$.
The map back to the stipulated parameter is
\begin{equation}\tag{KEP18}
 a_\lambda=a(2\lambda),\quad b_\lambda=b(2\lambda),\qquad
 \frac{da_\lambda}{d\lambda}=2a'(2\lambda),\quad
 \frac{db_\lambda}{d\lambda}=2b'(2\lambda).
\end{equation}

For every fixed $C_*>0$, continuity and strict endpoint ordering give
the quantitative existence of positive finite constants
\begin{equation}\tag{KEP19}
 a(0)\leq a(\kappa)<b(\kappa)\leq b(C_*)\quad(0\leq \kappa\leq C_*),
 \qquad
 \Delta_{C_*}:=\min_{0\leq \kappa\leq C_*}(b(\kappa)-a(\kappa))>0.
\end{equation}
The first bounds also use the proved monotonicity. The minimum in the
second statement is attained by continuity on the compact interval;
its attaining pair still has $a<b$ by (KEP13), so its value is
strictly positive. The same compactness argument applied to (KEP14)
gives
$\min_{0\leq \kappa\leq C_*}\mathcal J_\kappa(a(\kappa),b(\kappa))>0$.
All fixed orders of endpoint derivatives are bounded on this compact
interval by smoothness and the local extension at zero.
In particular these statements hold uniformly for the entire original
range $C_*=1/2$, equivalently $0\leq\lambda\leq1/4$.

Finally the endpoint at $\kappa=0$ is connected exactly to the original
elliptic equations. Put $u_0=\sqrt{a(0)}$, $v_0=\sqrt{b(0)}$, and
$k_0=\sqrt{1-u_0^2/v_0^2}$. For arbitrary positive $a<b$, substitution
$u=\sqrt a\tan\theta$ proves
\[
 \int_0^\infty\frac{du}{\sqrt{(a+u^2)(b+u^2)}}
 =\frac1{\sqrt b}K\left(\sqrt{1-a/b}\right),
 \quad
 K(k)=\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{d\theta}
                   {\sqrt{1-k^2\sin^2\theta}}.
\]
To check the second integral including its factor, use
$\sqrt{x}=(2/\pi)\int_0^\infty x/(x+u^2)\,du$.
Integrating this positive identity against
$dx/[\pi\sqrt{(b-x)(x-a)}]$ on $[a,b]$ and using
\[
 \frac1\pi\int_a^b
 \frac{dx}{(x+u^2)\sqrt{(b-x)(x-a)}}=\frac1{R_u}
\]
gives
\[
 \frac1\pi\int_a^b
 \frac{\sqrt{x}\,dx}{\sqrt{(b-x)(x-a)}}
 =\frac2\pi B_0(a,b).
\]
The substitution $x=b\cos^2\theta+a\sin^2\theta$ evaluates the
left side as $2\sqrt b\,E(\sqrt{1-a/b})/\pi$, with
$E(k)=\int_0^{\pi/2}\sqrt{1-k^2\sin^2\theta}\,d\theta$.
All exchanges are Tonelli exchanges of positive integrands. The
arcsine integral used above follows itself by this same trigonometric
substitution and then the tangent substitution in
$\int_0^{\pi/2}d\theta/(b\cos^2\theta+a\sin^2\theta+u^2)$.
Therefore the endpoint equations at $\kappa=0$ are precisely
\begin{equation}\tag{KEP20}
 u_0K(k_0)=2,\qquad v_0E(k_0)=4,
 \qquad k_0^2=1-u_0^2/v_0^2.
\end{equation}
Thus the entire parameter family has been constructed from the actual
endpoint system and returns to the previously calculated elliptic
endpoint at zero, with every coordinate map and coefficient explicit.
