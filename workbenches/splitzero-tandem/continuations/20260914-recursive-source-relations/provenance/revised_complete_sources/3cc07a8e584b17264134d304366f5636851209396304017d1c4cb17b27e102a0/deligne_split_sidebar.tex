\section{Deligne machinery inside Split-Zero cohomology}


\subsection{Deligne's image comparison inside the Split-Zero source complexes}
\label{sec:deligne-split-sidebar}

This sidebar integrates the delivered specialization-curvature, exponential-comparison,
and logarithmic-constituent-control notes. Their logarithmic cohomology and
constituent-coupling calculations are reproduced with complete proofs and
explicitly joined to the existing Split-Zero complexes; they are attributed
to those web contributions. The sidebar supplies the maps connecting the compact-support comparison,
its dual, the Split-Zero internal quotient, and the compactified curve of
arithmetic representatives.  It also calculates the logarithmic de Rham
complex of that curve.  In particular, it identifies which cohomology
groups the exceptional directions occupy and the divisor twist in their
support-compatible dual.

The source reference is P.~Deligne, \emph{La conjecture de Weil II},
Publications Math\'ematiques de l'IH\'ES \textbf{52} (1980), 137--252,
\url{https://www.numdam.org/item/PMIHES_1980__52__137_0/}.
The specific weight comparison is in (3.3.4)--(3.3.6), printed page 206;
the preceding geometric argument is in (3.2.8)--(3.2.13).
The source-language S20 records for printed pages 201--207 were read in full,
including the strict inequality in (3.2.10) and the squared eigenvalue in
(3.2.13). The complete S20 typed transcription is retained separately from
this integration.  The symbol $Q_0$ in (DS7)--(DS8) denotes a finite-field
cardinality; later $Q_0$ denotes the explicitly defined quotient Gram,
as in the supplied logarithmic note; $q$ denotes the dimension of the actual arithmetic packet.

\subsubsection{The comparison image, its entire cone, and its dual}

Let $c:C^\bullet\to D^\bullet$ be a cochain map of complexes of vector
spaces over a field $K$.  All differentials raise degree by one.  Write
$Z_C^m=\ker d_C^m$, $B_C^m=\operatorname{im}d_C^{m-1}$, and similarly
for $D$.  Define
\[
 K_c^m=\{x\in Z_C^m:c(x)\in B_D^m\},\qquad
 I_c^m=\operatorname{im}\bigl(H^m(c):H^m(C)\to H^m(D)\bigr).
 \tag{DS1}
\]
There are exact sequences with the displayed maps:
\[
\begin{split}
0&\longrightarrow K_c^m/B_C^m
 \xrightarrow{[x]\mapsto[x]} H^m(C)
 \xrightarrow{[x]\mapsto[cx]} I_c^m\longrightarrow0,\\
0&\longrightarrow I_c^m\longrightarrow H^m(D)
 \longrightarrow H^m(D)/I_c^m\longrightarrow0.
\end{split}\tag{DS2}
\]
Indeed, $B_C^m\subset K_c^m$ because $c$ commutes with the differentials.
The kernel of the second arrow in the first sequence is exactly the
classes represented by $K_c^m$, and every element of $I_c^m$ has the
indicated preimage.  The second sequence is the defining inclusion and
quotient.  Thus both the lost source classes and the extra target classes
are present.

The single complex retaining both sides is
\[
 \operatorname{Cone}(c)^m=D^m\oplus C^{m+1},\qquad
 d(y,x)=(d_Dy+cx,-d_Cx).
 \tag{DS3}
\]
Expansion gives $d^2(y,x)=(d_D^2y+d_Dcx-cd_Cx,d_C^2x)=0$.
The maps $y\mapsto(y,0)$ and $(y,x)\mapsto x$ give the exact sequence
of complexes $0\to D\to\operatorname{Cone}(c)\to C[1]\to0$, where
$C[1]^m=C^{m+1}$ has differential $-d_C$.  To make the relevant
cohomology segment explicit, a cone cocycle obeys
$d_Cx=0$ and $cx=-d_Dy$, so its projection represents a class in
$K_c^{m+1}/B_C^{m+1}$.  Conversely, if $cx=d_Dv$, then $(-v,x)$
is a cone cocycle.  A projected class is zero precisely when subtracting
a cone boundary leaves a representative $(y,0)$ with $d_Dy=0$.
Finally $(y,0)$ is a cone boundary precisely when
$y=d_Du+cv$ with $d_Cv=0$.  This proves the exact segment
\[
 H^m(C)\xrightarrow{H^m(c)} H^m(D)
 \longrightarrow H^m(\operatorname{Cone}(c))
 \longrightarrow H^{m+1}(C)\xrightarrow{H^{m+1}(c)}H^{m+1}(D).
 \tag{DS4}
\]
Every chain action commuting with $c$ acts on (DS1)--(DS4), since it
preserves both cycles and boundaries; on the cone its action is the
direct sum of the two specified actions.

For a finite-dimensional linear map $f:V\to W$, its ordinary linear
transpose $f^\vee:W^\vee\to V^\vee$ gives a perfect pairing
\[
 \operatorname{im}f\ \times\ \operatorname{im}f^\vee\longrightarrow K,
 \qquad (f(v),f^\vee\lambda)\longmapsto\lambda(f(v)).
 \tag{DS5}
\]
If $v$ is changed by $\ker f$, the value is unchanged.  If $\lambda$
is changed by $\ker f^\vee$, its restriction to $\operatorname{im}f$
is unchanged.  A nonzero $f(v)$ is detected by some functional on $W$,
and a nonzero $f^\vee\lambda$ is detected by some $v$, proving
nondegeneracy on both sides.  In particular
\[
 (\operatorname{im}f)^\vee\simeq\operatorname{im}f^\vee,
 \quad \ker f^\vee=(\operatorname{im}f)^\perp,
 \quad \operatorname{im}f^\vee=(\ker f)^\perp.
 \tag{DS6}
\]
For invertible actions $T_V,T_W$ with $fT_V=T_Wf$, the dual actions
are $(T_V^{-1})^\vee,(T_W^{-1})^\vee$; their induced actions on
the two images make the evaluation in (DS5) invariant.  For an additive
action $A$ and a specified scalar $b$, the action $bI-A^\vee$ on the
dual instead satisfies
$\langle Av,\lambda\rangle+\langle v,(bI-A^\vee)\lambda\rangle
=b\langle v,\lambda\rangle$.  These are two explicitly defined
action conventions, with different types of weight parameter.

Deligne's comparison takes
\[
 C=R\Gamma_c(X,\mathcal F),\quad D=R\Gamma(X,\mathcal F),\quad
 c=c_{X,\mathcal F},\quad I_c^m=\operatorname{im}c_m.
 \tag{DS7}
\]
Here $X=X_0\otimes_{\mathbb F_{Q_0}}\overline{\mathbb F}_{Q_0}$,
$X_0$ is smooth of pure dimension $N$ and finite type, and
$\mathcal F_0$ is a smooth $\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$-sheaf,
$\ell\ne\operatorname{char}\mathbb F_{Q_0}$, pure of weight $n$.
In the source theorem the upper weight bound on $H_c^m$ is $n+m$.
The dual of $\mathcal F$ has weight $-n$, and Poincar\'e duality gives
the exact degree and twist
\[
 H^m(X,\mathcal F)^\vee
 \simeq H_c^{2N-m}(X,\mathcal F^\vee)(N).
 \tag{DS8}
\]
The right side has upper weight
$-n+(2N-m)-2N=-n-m$ by the already established compact-support
theorem.  Dualizing gives the lower weight bound $n+m$ on $H^m$.
Since $c_m$ is Frobenius equivariant, choose a basis first adapted to
its kernel in the source and then to its image in the target.
The corresponding action matrices are block triangular.  Every
eigenvalue on $I_c^m$ therefore occurs on both $H_c^m$ and $H^m$,
with its algebraic conjugates.  The two bounds force its weight to
equal $n+m$.  This proves the image step (3.3.6) from the two stated
source bounds and proves exactly why (DS7) retains the cone (DS3).

The tensor comparison has an equally explicit algebraic map.  For
finite-dimensional $f_a:V_a\to W_a$ ($1\leq a\leq r$),
\[
 \operatorname{im}\left(\bigotimes_{a=1}^r f_a\right)
 =\bigotimes_{a=1}^r\operatorname{im}f_a
 \ \subseteq\ \bigotimes_{a=1}^r W_a.
 \tag{DS9}
\]
The tensor map sends a pure tensor to the pure tensor of its images,
so its image is contained in the right side.  Conversely these image
pure tensors span the right side and each has a preimage; inclusivity
into the target follows by extending a basis of each image to a basis
of $W_a$.  Thus tensor powers preserve the same comparison image.
For complexes, the tensor differential is
$d(x\otimes y)=dx\otimes y+(-1)^{\deg x}x\otimes dy$; swapping
the factors has the sign $(-1)^{\deg x\deg y}$.  In characteristic
zero a specified symmetry projection is
$\frac1{r!}\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak S_r}\pi$ with those graded signs.
Its idempotence follows because each group element occurs exactly
$r!$ times in the double sum.  No unspecific tensor eigenvalue is
substituted for the eigenvalue on this actual image.

\subsubsection{The internal Split-Zero lift of the same maps}

For a complex vector space $V$, set $\mathsf S(V)=\{\tau\}\sqcup V^\bullet$.
Supported addition and scalar multiplication are the original operations:
$v^\bullet+w^\bullet=(v+w)^\bullet$ and
$a^\bullet v^\bullet=(av)^\bullet$.  External $\tau$ is the additive
identity and is absorbing for scalar multiplication.  The supported
zero is $e_V=0_V^\bullet$.  Every linear map $f:V\to W$ has the
canonical lift
\[
 \mathsf S(f)(\tau)=\tau,\qquad
 \mathsf S(f)(v^\bullet)=f(v)^\bullet.
 \tag{DS10}
\]
Checking the two supported inputs and inputs containing $\tau$ proves
additivity, the scalar law and composition.  For $B\subseteq V$,
$\mathsf S(V)\to\mathsf S(V/B)$ sends $b^\bullet$ to $e_{V/B}$ for
every $b\in B$, and sends $\tau$ to $\tau$.  Two supported vectors
have the same image exactly when their difference lies in $B$.
This is the coequalizer of inclusion $\mathsf S(B)\to\mathsf S(V)$
and the map $b^\bullet\mapsto e_V$, $\tau\mapsto\tau$: any map
equalizing these has equal values on $v^\bullet$ and $(v+b)^\bullet$,
by additivity, and hence has a unique factorization through the
displayed quotient.

Applying (DS10) to all maps in (DS1)--(DS4) lifts their source kernels,
images, quotients and actions.  A differential squared is the
supported-zero map on represented vectors and still sends $\tau$ to
$\tau$.  The ordinary kernel $\ker H^m(c)$ is carried by the
represented fibre of $\mathsf S(H^m(c))^{-1}(e)$; the preimage of
external absence is only $\{\tau\}$.  These two fibres are disjoint.
Their exact common carrier is
$\mathsf S(\ker H^m(c))=\{\tau\}\sqcup(\ker H^m(c))^\bullet$,
with the inclusion of each of the two fibres.  If
$p_V:\mathsf S(V)\to V$ sends $v^\bullet$ to $v$ and $\tau$ to
$0$, this carrier is precisely $p_V^{-1}(\ker H^m(c))$.
Thus the amplitude projection proves the two inclusions while
retaining the two distinct receiving zeros.  With a join semilattice
of supports, carry out the construction
in each fibre and use the original transition maps; (DS1)--(DS4) commute
with those transitions because their definitions use the commuting
cochain maps.  No support label is discarded.

The coefficient comparison remains
\[
 \begin{array}{ccc}
 G(\mathbb Z)&\xrightarrow{G(\jmath)}&G(\mathbb C)\\
 p_{\mathbb Z}\downarrow&&\downarrow p_{\mathbb C}\\
 \mathbb Z&\xrightarrow{\jmath}&\mathbb C.
 \end{array}\qquad
 p_R(a^\bullet)=a,\quad p_R(\tau)=0.
 \tag{DS11}
\]
It commutes on every supported integer and on $\tau$.  In particular
the linear/coherent calculation below is the amplitude image of a
specified Split-Zero diagram; its supported exceptional kernel remains
available before applying $p$.

\subsubsection{The original source relation produces the curve}

Fix the actual cyclic object $E=\mathbb C[S]/(\chi)$, $q=\deg\chi\geq1$,
and the original action $A=M_S$.  The monic source spaces
$\mathcal P_N$, their positive Gram matrices $O_N$, and their full
remainder maps $B_N:\mathcal P_N\to E$ give
\[
 K_N=B_NO_N^{-1}B_N^*,\qquad G_N=K_N^{-1},\qquad
 R_N=O_N^{-1}B_N^*G_N.
 \tag{DS12}
\]
Take $i\leq j$ with both remainder maps onto, and retain the source
inclusion $L_{ij}$ with
$B_jL_{ij}=B_i$ and $L_{ij}^*O_jL_{ij}=O_i$.  Put
\[
 \Delta=L_{ij}R_i-R_j,
 \quad C=\Delta^*O_j\Delta=G_i-G_j,
 \quad R(z)=R_j+z\Delta.
 \tag{DS13}
\]
The equalities follow directly: $B_NR_N=I$,
$R_j^*O_j\Delta=G_jB_j\Delta=0$, and expansion of
$(L_{ij}R_i)^*O_j(L_{ij}R_i)$ gives $G_j+\Delta^*O_j\Delta$.
For the kernel order, the actual source projection
$P=L_{ij}O_i^{-1}L_{ij}^*O_j$ obeys $P^2=P$ and
$P^*O_j=O_jP$ by the retained source isometry.  Therefore
$K_j-K_i=B_j(I-P)O_j^{-1}(I-P)^*B_j^*\succeq0$;
expanding the right side gives $B_jO_j^{-1}B_j^*-B_iO_i^{-1}B_i^*$.
Therefore
\[
 B_jR(z)=I,\qquad R(z)^*O_jR(z)=G_j+|z|^2C.
 \tag{DS14}
\]
The source is the original theta source with its arithmetic coefficient
unit and every full jet; the new variable $z$ parametrizes only the
comparison and does not replace $S$.

Let $E_0=\ker\Delta$, $E_1=E_0^{\perp_{G_j}}$, $r=\dim E_1$, and
let $P_0,P_1$ be the specified projections.  On
$X=\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb C}$ with coordinates $[u:v]$, finite coordinate
$z=v/u$ and infinity coordinate $w=u/v$, set $D=\{\infty\}$ and
\[
 \mathscr E=(E_0\otimes\mathcal O_X)
 \oplus(E_1\otimes\mathcal O_X(-D)).
 \tag{DS15}
\]
The source morphism is
\[
 \Psi(x_0+x_1\otimes(u,v))
 =R_jx_0+uR_jx_1+v\Delta x_1.
 \tag{DS16}
\]
It is fibrewise injective.  At $u\ne0$, apply $B_j$ and use
$E_0\cap E_1=0$; at $u=0$, apply $B_j$ to force $x_0=0$ and then
use injectivity of $\Delta|_{E_1}$.  Thus the original source Gram
defines a smooth positive metric on this bundle, including infinity.
The observation $\alpha=B_j\Psi$ is identity in the finite frame and
$P_0+wP_1$ in the infinity frame.  Consequently
\[
 0\to\mathscr E\xrightarrow{\alpha}E\otimes\mathcal O_X
 \to i_*E_1\to0,
 \tag{DS17}
\]
where $i:D\hookrightarrow X$.  In fact its image lattice is
$\mathcal M=E_0\mathcal O_X+E\mathcal O_X(-D)$; at infinity it is
$E_0\mathcal O+wE\mathcal O$.

Tensoring the two locally free terms of (DS17) with the residue field
at infinity gives $[E\xrightarrow{P_0}E]$ in degrees $-1,0$.
Its cohomology is $E_1$ in both degrees.  Intrinsically the negative
term is $E_1\otimes\mathcal O(-D)|_D$.  This calculation is the
derived fibre of the skyscraper cokernel, and explicitly retains the
Tor term that ordinary right-exact specialization would omit.
Dualizing (DS17) gives
\[
 0\to E^*\mathcal O_X\to\mathscr E^\vee
 \to i_*\bigl(E_1^*\otimes\mathcal O(D)|_D\bigr)\to0.
 \tag{DS18}
\]
To check its terminal term, resolve $\mathcal O_D$ by
$[\mathcal O(-D)\to\mathcal O]$ and apply $\mathcal Hom(-,\mathcal O)$.
The resulting cokernel is $\mathcal O(D)|_D$.
The two boundary factors pair by evaluation and
$\mathcal O(-D)|_D\otimes\mathcal O(D)|_D\to\mathbb C_D$.

\subsubsection{A calculated compact-support/ordinary de Rham comparison}

Transport the trivial connection through $\alpha$ on $X\setminus D$.
All connection complexes here are complexes of sheaves of
$\mathbb C$-vector spaces: the differentials satisfy the Leibniz
rule and are not asserted to be $\mathcal O_X$-linear.
In the infinity frame it is
\[
 \nabla=d+P_1\frac{dw}{w},\qquad
 \mathscr K=[\mathscr E\xrightarrow{\nabla}
                 \mathscr E\otimes\Omega_X^1(\log D)]
 \quad\hbox{in degrees }0,1.
 \tag{DS19}
\]
Indeed $\alpha^{-1}d\alpha=P_1dw/w$.  Its residue is $P_1$, its
horizontal monodromy is $\exp(-2\pi iP_1)=I$, and the map $\alpha$
is a morphism to
$\mathscr K_{\rm ord}=[E\mathcal O_X\xrightarrow d
E\Omega_X^1(\log D)]$.

Here is the complete local calculation of the extension at infinity.
On analytic power series the $E_0$ differential, with $dw/w$ as the
one-form frame, is
$\sum a_nw^n\mapsto\sum n a_nw^n$.  Its kernel and cokernel are
the constant terms, both $E_0$.  The $E_1$ differential is
\[
 \sum_{n\geq0}b_nw^n\longmapsto
 \sum_{n\geq0}(n+1)b_nw^n.
 \tag{DS20}
\]
It is invertible: its inverse divides the $n$th coefficient by $n+1$,
which preserves every disk of convergence.  Thus the $E_1$ part has
zero derived stalk at infinity.  On a punctured disk, integrating a
Laurent series term by term divides its $w^n$ coefficient by $n$ for
$n\ne0$; the only one-form class remaining is $dw/w$ with integral
$2\pi i$ around a positively oriented $w$-circle.  These statements,
together with the usual local primitive on a disk, identify the
$E_0$ part with $Rj_*\underline{E_0}$ and the $E_1$ part with
$j_!\underline{E_1}$, where $j:X\setminus D\hookrightarrow X$.
The identification for $j_!$ is the extension-by-zero map of flat
sections; its stalk cohomology is an isomorphism on the open disk and
is zero on both sides at infinity by (DS20).  The identification for
$Rj_*$ is the restriction map to the punctured disk and the two
Laurent calculations.  This constructs the comparison on the present
curve rather than assigning compact support by name.

Its entire global cohomology is
\[
 \mathbb H^0(X,\mathscr K)=E_0,\quad
 \mathbb H^1(X,\mathscr K)=0,\quad
 \mathbb H^2(X,\mathscr K)=E_1\otimes\mathbb T,
 \quad\mathbb T=H^1(X,\Omega_X^1).
 \tag{DS21}
\]
For a direct verification, $\Omega_X^1(\log D)=\mathcal O_X(-1)$.
The two terms of $\mathscr K$ are therefore
$E_0\mathcal O\oplus E_1\mathcal O(-1)$ and
$E_0\mathcal O(-1)\oplus E_1\mathcal O(-2)$.
Using the two affine charts, polynomial division of Laurent
polynomials proves
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 H^0(\mathcal O)=\mathbb C,\quad H^1(\mathcal O)=0,
 \quad H^0(\mathcal O(-1))=H^1(\mathcal O(-1))=0,\\
 H^0(\mathcal O(-2))=0,\quad H^1(\mathcal O(-2))=\mathbb C.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{DS22}
\]
More explicitly, after identifying $\mathcal O(-2)$ with
$\Omega_X^1$, a Laurent one-form on the overlap has every term
except $dw/w$ removable by a one-form on one of the charts.
The coefficient of $dw/w$ is not removable and is its stated
one-dimensional quotient. Retain $\mathbb T$ as this quotient line, with
its original generator $[dz/z]=-[dw/w]$.  The double complex for these two terms
has only the entries $E_0$ in total degree zero and $E_1\otimes\mathbb T$ in total
degree two.  There is no differential to or from them, proving
(DS21) without an uncomputed higher differential.

Taking the quotient of the two complexes in (DS19) gives
\[
 \mathscr Q_D=[i_*E_1\xrightarrow{0}i_*E_1\,dw/w]
 \quad\hbox{in degrees }0,1.
 \tag{DS23}
\]
The zero differential follows by reducing $w\partial_w$ modulo $w$.
The termwise short exact sequence
$0\to\mathscr K\to\mathscr K_{\rm ord}\to\mathscr Q_D\to0$
and the fact $\mathbb H^0(\mathscr K_{\rm ord})=E$,
$\mathbb H^{>0}(\mathscr K_{\rm ord})=0$ give
\[
0\to E_0\to E\to E_1\to0,
\qquad
 E_1\,dw/w\xrightarrow{\ \delta\ }E_1\otimes\mathbb T
 \quad\hbox{an isomorphism}.
 \tag{DS24}
\]
Fix the \v Cech convention $s_V-s_U$ for the overlap, with $U$ the
finite chart and $V$ the infinity chart, and the total-complex
differential $\delta+(-1)^p\nabla$ on \v Cech degree $p$.
Lifting a boundary one-form
as $(0,v\,dw/w)$ makes its connecting class $v[dw/w]$; thus
$\delta(v\,dw/w)=v\otimes[dw/w]=-v\otimes[dz/z]$.  In the original finite coordinate
$dw/w=-dz/z$.  Both that sign and the chosen positive orientation of
the $w$-circle are retained.  Under (DS21), the ordinary comparison
image is exactly $E_0$ in degree zero and zero in degrees one and two.
The full $E_1$ occurs in its boundary comparison and in degree two.

\subsubsection{The divisor twist in the support-compatible dual}

The bundle dual in (DS18) and the dual de Rham comparison are joined
by the exact additional map of line bundles
$\mathcal O(-D)\hookrightarrow\mathcal O$.  Define
\[
 \mathscr E^{\mathrm{cd}}=\mathscr E^\vee\otimes\mathcal O(-D),
 \qquad
 \mathscr K^{\mathrm{cd}}=
 [\mathscr E^{\mathrm{cd}}\xrightarrow{\nabla^{\mathrm{cd}}}
  \mathscr E^{\mathrm{cd}}\otimes\Omega_X^1(\log D)].
 \tag{DS25}
\]
The dual connection has residue $-P_1^\vee$.  Multiplication of its
local frame by $w$, the local generator of $\mathcal O(-D)$, adds
$I$ to the residue.  Hence the residue of (DS25) is $P_0^\vee$,
and
\[
 \mathscr E^{\mathrm{cd}}
 =E_0^*\mathcal O(-D)\oplus E_1^*\mathcal O,
 \qquad
 \begin{array}{c|ccc}
 m&0&1&2\\\hline
 \mathbb H^m(\mathscr K^{\mathrm{cd}})&E_1^*&0&E_0^*\otimes\mathbb T.
 \end{array}
 \tag{DS26}
\]
The proof is exactly the power-series calculation (DS20) and the
four line-bundle groups (DS22), now with the two summands exchanged.

Evaluation is a morphism
$\mathscr E\otimes\mathscr E^{\mathrm{cd}}\to\mathcal O(-D)$.
The connections obey the Leibniz identity for evaluation, since the
dual connection cancels the original connection term.  Wedge product
therefore gives a cochain map
\[
 \mathscr K\otimes_{\mathbb C}\mathscr K^{\mathrm{cd}}
 \longrightarrow [\mathcal O(-D)\xrightarrow d\Omega_X^1]
 =:\mathscr K_c(\mathbb C).
 \tag{DS27}
\]
On a degree-zero tensor it is evaluation; on degree-one tensors it
is evaluation times the retained one-form; on two one-forms it is
zero because $\Omega_X^2=0$.  These prescriptions and the tensor
sign in (DS9) verify the cochain identity in every degree.
The degreewise evaluation and wedge maps factor through their
$\mathcal O_X$-balanced tensor products; the displayed complex
tensor product is over $\mathbb C$, so its differential is defined.
Define the trace on $\mathbb H^2(\mathscr K_c(\mathbb C))$ by
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}_D([dz/z])=1,\qquad
 \operatorname{Tr}_D([dw/w])=-1
 =-\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{|w|=\epsilon}^{\mathrm{counterclockwise}}\frac{dw}{w}.
 \tag{DS28}
\]
Holomorphic one-forms on either chart have zero overlap residue, so
this is well-defined on (DS22).  The induced pairings are
\[
 E_0\times E_0^*\to\mathbb C,
 \quad E_1\times E_1^*\to\mathbb C,
 \tag{DS29}
\]
ordinary evaluation after applying the retained trace on $\mathbb T$,
in complementary degrees $0,2$ and $2,0$.
They are perfect, since a nonzero vector is detected by a linear
functional.  Thus (DS25)--(DS29) prove the support-compatible
duality, including the dimension-one degree change and its actual
boundary line factor.  The finite-field analogue of the trace target
is $\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell(-1)$; the characteristic-zero calculation
here has the explicit residue normalization (DS28).

\subsubsection{Retaining the arithmetic action through the exceptional fibre}

Transporting the original $A$ through $\alpha$ gives
\[
 \widehat A_\infty
 =(P_0+w^{-1}P_1)A(P_0+wP_1)
 =\begin{pmatrix}A_{00}&wA_{01}\\w^{-1}A_{10}&A_{11}\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{DS30}
\]
It extends regularly on $\mathscr E$ exactly when $AE_0\subseteq E_0$,
since its sole possible negative power is $w^{-1}P_1AP_0$.
The explicit repair on the same ambient arithmetic target is
\[
 F_A=\sum_{a=0}^{q-1}A^aE_0,\qquad
 \mathcal M_A=F_A\mathcal O_X+E\mathcal O_X(-D).
 \tag{DS31}
\]
Cayley--Hamilton puts $A^qE_0$ in the displayed sum, proving
invariance; every invariant subspace containing $E_0$ contains this
sum.  Similarly every coherent $A$-stable submodule of $E\mathcal O$
containing $\mathcal M$ contains $\sum_{a=0}^{q-1}A^a\mathcal M$,
which is exactly $\mathcal M_A$ in (DS31).  There are exact sequences
\[
\begin{split}
0&\to\mathcal M\to\mathcal M_A\to i_*(F_A/E_0)\to0,\\
0&\to\mathcal M_A\to E\mathcal O_X\to i_*(E/F_A)\to0.
\end{split}\tag{DS32}
\]
The second is $A$ equivariant on every term.  The connection $d$ on
$E\mathcal O$ preserves $\mathcal M_A$ logarithmically, because
$d(wv)=w\,dv+v\,dw$ lies in
$\mathcal M_A\otimes\Omega^1(\log D)$.  It commutes with the
constant original $A$.  Define its two-term complex $\mathscr K_A$
with the same degree convention as (DS19).  Calculation with any
vector-space complement of $F_A$ proves, intrinsically,
\[
 \mathbb H^0(\mathscr K_A)=F_A,\quad
 \mathbb H^1(\mathscr K_A)=0,\quad
 \mathbb H^2(\mathscr K_A)=(E/F_A)\otimes\mathbb T,
 \tag{DS33}
\]
with actions $A|_{F_A}$ and $\overline A$, respectively.  The
identifications are independent of that auxiliary complement: the
degree-zero map is inclusion into $E$ and the degree-two map is the
connecting quotient map in (DS32), as in (DS24).

Its dual lattice has a coordinate-free formula.  Put
$F_A^\perp=\{\lambda\in E^*: \lambda(F_A)=0\}$.  Then
\[
 \mathcal M_A^\vee(-D)=F_A^\perp\mathcal O_X+E^*\mathcal O_X(-D).
 \tag{DS34}
\]
Indeed a meromorphic functional belongs to the left side precisely
when its value on every element of $F_A\mathcal O+wE\mathcal O$
lies in $w\mathcal O$.  This first forces it to be holomorphic,
by testing $wE$, and then forces its constant term to annihilate
$F_A$.  This is exactly the right side.  Fix the additive dual
