## 7. New restriction: unavoidable frontier cost and determinant growth

This section is a new consequence proved directly from the retained finite arithmetic objects above. It does not assume purity, select a new metric, replace the arithmetic measure, discard nilpotents, or assert an unproved asymptotic estimate.

### 7.1 Exact symmetric frontier matrices

Fix the original actual packet \(h\), \(k\ge1\), and \(M\ge k(d-1)\). Let
\[
\mathcal C=\mathcal C_M^{\mathrm{sym}},\quad
G=G_M^{\mathrm{sym}}=\mathcal C^{-1},\quad
A=A_k^{\mathrm{sym}},\quad
\Delta\mathcal C=\mathcal C_{M+1}^{\mathrm{sym}}-\mathcal C_M^{\mathrm{sym}}.
\]
Let \(Q_+\) be the inclusion whose columns are the **unscaled** orbit sums in the degree-\(M+1\) source basis. Define
\[
\Omega_s=Q_+^*\Omega Q_+,\qquad F_s=L_kFQ_+,\qquad E_s=L_kE_+Q_+.
\]
Thus \(\Omega_s\) contains exactly the orbit norms already displayed. The orthogonal projection onto symmetric source coordinates is \(Q_+(Q_+^*Q_+)^{-1}Q_+^*\); since \(\Omega\) is constant within each orbit, it commutes with that projection. Equivariance makes \(L_kF\) and \(L_kE_+\) vanish on its complementary subspace. Therefore the complete identities transfer exactly as
\[
\Delta\mathcal C=F_s\Omega_s^{-1}F_s^*,
\]
\[
\mathcal Z=A\mathcal C+\mathcal CA^*-k\mathcal C
=F_s\Omega_s^{-1}E_s^*+E_s\Omega_s^{-1}F_s^*,
\]
\[
E_s\Omega_s^{-1}E_s^*\preceq\Gamma_{k,M}\mathcal C.
\]
For example, the last statement follows by applying the exact congruence \(L_k(\cdot)L_k^*\) to the full lowering inequality. The left side is the displayed restricted factorization; the right side is \(\Gamma_{k,M}L_k\mathcal C_ML_k^*=\Gamma_{k,M}\mathcal C\).

Every \(a_{n+1}\) is positive, so \(\Gamma_{k,M}>0\). For calculations only, introduce the explicit congruences
\[
X=G^{1/2}F_s\Omega_s^{-1/2},\qquad
Y=G^{1/2}E_s\Omega_s^{-1/2},\qquad
K=XX^*=G^{1/2}\Delta\mathcal C G^{1/2}.
\]
Positive square roots are uniquely defined by diagonalizing the retained positive matrices. These formulas are invertible changes of comparison coordinates with explicitly retained inverses; they do not redefine \(G\), the orbit basis, or any source function. Set
\[
H=G^{1/2}\mathcal ZG^{1/2}=XY^*+YX^*.
\]
The lowering estimate becomes \(YY^*\preceq\Gamma_{k,M}I\), and hence
\[
\|H\|_F^2\le4\Gamma_{k,M}\operatorname{tr}K.
\tag{7.1}
\]
Indeed \(\|XY^*\|_F^2=\operatorname{tr}(X^*X\,Y^*Y)\le\Gamma_{k,M}\operatorname{tr}(X^*X)\), and the triangle inequality gives \(\|XY^*+YX^*\|_F\le2\|XY^*\|_F\).

### 7.2 All spectral positions, including all multiplicities

List the roots of \(h\) as \(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_d\), repeated according to their original orders, and put
\[
\delta_i=\Re\rho_i-\tfrac12.
\]
No root is assumed to lie on the critical line. The eigenvalues of \(A\), with algebraic multiplicity, are
\[
\eta_{\mathbf n}=\sum_{i=1}^dn_i\rho_i,
\qquad \mathbf n\in\mathbb N_0^d,\quad \sum_i n_i=k.
\]
To prove this without removing nilpotents, triangularize the original \(A_h\). Such triangularization follows inductively from an eigenvector and the induced operator on the quotient. The induced orbit monomials in \(\operatorname{Sym}^kE_h\), ordered compatibly with the triangular flag, then make the retained sum operator triangular, with the displayed diagonal entries. The off-diagonal nilpotent terms remain in that operator.

Let \(B=G^{1/2}AG^{-1/2}\), so \(H=B+B^*-kI\). Unitary Schur triangularization preserves the Frobenius norm. If \(T=U^*BU\) is upper triangular, there is the exact identity
\[
\|H\|_F^2=\sum_{|\mathbf n|=k}(2\Re\eta_{\mathbf n}-k)^2
+2\sum_{i<j}|T_{ij}|^2.
\]
Indeed the diagonal entries of \(T+T^*-kI\) are the first displayed terms, and its \((i,j)\), \((j,i)\) entries for \(i<j\) are \(T_{ij}\), \(\bar T_{ij}\). Thus
\[
\|H\|_F^2\ge
4\sum_{|\mathbf n|=k}\left(\sum_i n_i\delta_i\right)^2.
\tag{7.2}
\]
This inequality proves precisely why nilpotent or nonnormal terms cannot invalidate the restriction: they can only add nonnegative off-diagonal contributions to this Frobenius lower bound.

The sum in (7.2) can be evaluated exactly. For \(d\ge2\), extracting the coefficient of \(z^k\) from products of geometric series gives
\[
\sum_{|\mathbf n|=k}n_1=\binom{k+d-1}{d},\quad
\sum_{|\mathbf n|=k}n_1(n_1-1)=2\binom{k+d-1}{d+1},
\]
\[
\sum_{|\mathbf n|=k}n_1n_2=\binom{k+d-1}{d+1}.
\]
The generating functions are respectively \(z(1-z)^{-d-1}\), \(2z^2(1-z)^{-d-2}\), and \(z^2(1-z)^{-d-2}\). Expanding the square and using Pascal's identity gives
\[
\mathcal S_{h,k}:=
\sum_{|\mathbf n|=k}\left(\sum_i n_i\delta_i\right)^2
=\binom{k+d}{d+1}\sum_i\delta_i^2
+\binom{k+d-1}{d+1}\left(\sum_i\delta_i\right)^2.
\tag{7.3}
\]
Binomial coefficients with upper index smaller than the nonnegative lower index are zero. For \(d=1\), direct evaluation gives \(k^2\delta_1^2\); the displayed right side is \([\binom{k+1}{2}+\binom{k}{2}]\delta_1^2=k^2\delta_1^2\), so the same identity holds.

Combining (7.1)–(7.3) proves the arithmetic restriction
\[
\boxed{\Gamma_{k,M}\operatorname{tr}
\left(G_M^{\mathrm{sym}}
(\mathcal C_{M+1}^{\mathrm{sym}}-\mathcal C_M^{\mathrm{sym}})\right)
\ge\mathcal S_{h,k}.}
\tag{7.4}
\]
The trace is unchanged by cyclic permutation, so it equals \(\operatorname{tr}K\). Every matrix in (7.4) is calculated from the original \(|g/h|^2dy/(2\pi)\), full jets, and unscaled orbit factors.

The restriction is invariant under a genuine coordinate change, with its exact morphism retained. For invertible quotient and frontier changes \(S,R\), let
\[
A'=SAS^{-1},\quad \mathcal C'=S\mathcal CS^*,\quad
F_s'=SF_sR,\quad E_s'=SE_sR,\quad\Omega_s'=R^*\Omega_sR.
\]
Then direct cancellation of \(R,R^{-1}\) gives \(\Delta\mathcal C'=S\Delta\mathcal CS^*\), and substitution gives \(\mathcal Z'=S\mathcal ZS^*\). The corresponding metric is \(G'=S^{-*}GS^{-1}\); consequently
\[
\operatorname{tr}(G'\Delta\mathcal C')=\operatorname{tr}(G\Delta\mathcal C),
\qquad
\frac{\det(\mathcal C'+\Delta\mathcal C')}{\det\mathcal C'}
=\frac{\det(\mathcal C+\Delta\mathcal C)}{\det\mathcal C}.
\]
Moreover \(U_S=(\mathcal C')^{-1/2}S\mathcal C^{1/2}\) has \(U_SU_S^*=I\), and \(H'=U_SHU_S^*\). This proves that the comparison-coordinate maps used in the proof preserve the precise trace, determinant ratio, and Frobenius quantity; they do not impose an arithmetic normalization.

### 7.3 Determinant growth at every support step

The exact update gives
\[
\frac{\det\mathcal C_{M+1}^{\mathrm{sym}}}
{\det\mathcal C_M^{\mathrm{sym}}}=
\det(I+K).
\]
If \(\kappa_1,\ldots,\kappa_m\ge0\) are the eigenvalues of \(K\), expansion of \(\prod_j(1+\kappa_j)\) gives \(\det(I+K)\ge1+\sum_j\kappa_j\). Therefore (7.4) proves
\[
\boxed{\frac{\det\mathcal C_{M+1}^{\mathrm{sym}}}
{\det\mathcal C_M^{\mathrm{sym}}}
\ge1+\frac{\mathcal S_{h,k}}{\Gamma_{k,M}}.}
\tag{7.5}
\]
For every pair of finite support degrees \(M_1>M_0\ge k(d-1)\), multiplication of these inequalities proves
\[
\boxed{\frac{\det\mathcal C_{M_1}^{\mathrm{sym}}}
{\det\mathcal C_{M_0}^{\mathrm{sym}}}
\ge\prod_{M=M_0}^{M_1-1}
\left(1+\frac{\mathcal S_{h,k}}{\Gamma_{k,M}}\right).}
\tag{7.6}
\]
There is no assumed convergence or asymptotic estimate in this finite product.

For a reflection-stable packet, the exact pairing \(\rho\mapsto1-\bar\rho\), including full multiplicities, gives \(\sum_i\delta_i=0\). Then
\[
\boxed{\mathcal S_{h,k}=\binom{k+d}{d+1}
\sum_{i=1}^d(\Re\rho_i-\tfrac12)^2.}
\tag{7.7}
\]
Thus any actual off-axis zero in such a packet makes the right side strictly positive, forcing a nonzero relative interpolation increment and the determinant growth (7.5) at **every** admitted total-degree support. This holds inside the smaller symmetric summand that retains the amplification witnesses, not only in the full tensor module.

### 7.4 Exact witness cost and why this does not already prove RH

For an actual eigenvector \(v\) of \(A_h\), let \(u=v^{\otimes k}\) in the literal symmetric coordinates and \(q=u^*Gu>0\). Put
\[
x=\Omega_s^{-1/2}F_s^*Gu,\qquad
y=\Omega_s^{-1/2}E_s^*Gu.
\]
The retained frontier identity, with no absolute-value replacement, gives
\[
\boxed{\Re(x^*y)=k(\Re\rho-\tfrac12)q.}
\tag{7.8}
\]
Define \(\alpha=\|x\|^2/q\), \(\beta=\|y\|^2/q\). Then \(\beta\le\Gamma_{k,M}\), and Cauchy–Schwarz gives
\[
\alpha\beta\ge k^2(\Re\rho-\tfrac12)^2.
\]
With the actual symmetric relative increment
\[
\lambda_{k,M}^{\mathrm{sym}}
=\max_{w\ne0}\frac{w^*\Delta\mathcal Cw}{w^*\mathcal Cw}
=\lambda_{\max}(K),
\]
we have \(\alpha\le\lambda_{k,M}^{\mathrm{sym}}\), by substituting \(w=Gu\). Hence
\[
\boxed{\Gamma_{k,M}\lambda_{k,M}^{\mathrm{sym}}
\ge k^2(\Re\rho-\tfrac12)^2.}
\tag{7.9}
\]
Equation (7.8) also retains the required nonzero signed phase correlation between the two original frontier maps. An off-axis eigenline cannot be hidden solely by small absolute representative norms or by a vanishing signed cross pairing.

The complete actual counterexample restriction from this interval is therefore a persistent signed frontier witness (7.8), unavoidable quadratic tensor cost (7.9), and the stronger aggregate determinant restrictions (7.4)–(7.7), including every spectral multiplicity. No calculation in the original messages or in this audit bounds the actual arithmetic determinant growth above by an incompatible quantity, or bounds \(\Gamma\lambda\) by a subquadratic quantity. Consequently these are proved restrictions on a possible RH counterexample, not a proof that the restrictions cannot be met.
