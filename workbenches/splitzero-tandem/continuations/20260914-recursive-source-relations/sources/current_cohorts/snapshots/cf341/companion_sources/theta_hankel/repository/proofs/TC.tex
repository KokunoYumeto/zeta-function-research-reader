\section{The original integral and its two omitted regions}
Retain
\[
\phi_*(x)=(4\pi^2x^4-6\pi x^2)e^{-\pi x^2},\qquad
f_0(x)=2\sum_{n\ge1}\phi_*(nx),\qquad
\psi(y)=e^{y/2}f_0(e^y).
\tag{TC1}
\]
The original reflection and Mellin calculation give
$\psi(-y)=\psi(y)$ and
$g(1/2+iz)=\int_{\R}\psi(y)e^{izy}\,dy$.
For every even integer $J\ge0$, write $M_J=\int_{\R}y^J\psi(y)\,dy$.
Substitution of (TC1), with its theta factor two and reflection factor
two, gives
\[
M_J=\int_0^\infty I_J(y)\,dy,\qquad
I_J(y)=4y^J\sum_{n\ge1}
\left(4\pi^2n^4e^{9y/2}-6\pi n^2e^{5y/2}\right)
e^{-\pi n^2e^{2y}} .
\tag{TC2}
\]
At $y\ge0$, each summand is nonnegative; it is strictly positive at
$y>0$, because $2\pi n^2e^{2y}-3>0$. This also proves positivity of
every $M_J$ once convergence is established below.

For an integer $N\ge1$ and a real $L>0$, retain exactly the terms
$1\le n<N$ on $0\le y\le L$. Denote their integral by $M_J^{N,L}$.
Thus $N$ is the first omitted integer. Set
\[
r_N=e^{4/N-2\pi N},\qquad r_1=e^{4-2\pi},\qquad
\lambda_J(L)=2\pi e^{2L}-\frac92-\frac{J}{L}.
\tag{TC3}
\]
Both $r_N$ and $r_1$ lie strictly between zero and one.

\begin{theorem}[Two explicit positive tails]
For $\lambda_J(L)>0$ the full original moment satisfies
\[
0\le M_J-M_J^{N,L}\le T_{J,N,L}+Y_{J,L},
\tag{TC4}
\]
where every factor is retained in
\[
T_{J,N,L}
=\frac{16\pi^2L^{J+1}e^{9L/2}N^4e^{-\pi N^2}}{1-r_N},
\qquad
Y_{J,L}
=\frac{16\pi^2L^Je^{9L/2-\pi e^{2L}}}
{(1-r_1)\lambda_J(L)}.
\tag{TC5}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $n=N+\ell$, $\ell\ge0$, use
$(N+\ell)^4\le N^4e^{4\ell/N}$, which follows from
$\log(1+x)\le x$ for $x\ge0$, and
$(N+\ell)^2\ge N^2+2N\ell$. For $y\ge0$ these imply
\[
\sum_{n\ge N}n^4e^{-\pi n^2e^{2y}}
\le
\frac{N^4e^{-\pi N^2e^{2y}}}
{1-e^{4/N-2\pi Ne^{2y}}}
\le\frac{N^4e^{-\pi N^2}}{1-r_N}.
\tag{TC6}
\]
The negative term in each parenthesis of (TC2) can be dropped for
an upper bound. On $[0,L]$ one has $y^J\le L^J$ and
$e^{9y/2}\le e^{9L/2}$. Integrating that bound over an interval
of length $L$ proves the first expression in (TC5).

For the second region, apply the first bound in (TC6) with $N=1$,
retaining its numerator's dependence on $y$ and bounding its
denominator below by $1-r_1$. Consequently
\[
0\le I_J(y)\le
\frac{16\pi^2}{1-r_1}y^Je^{9y/2-\pi e^{2y}}
\qquad(y\ge0).
\tag{TC7}
\]
Write $y=L+v$ with $v\ge0$. The elementary inequalities
$(L+v)^J\le L^Je^{Jv/L}$ and
$e^{2(L+v)}\ge e^{2L}(1+2v)$ yield
\[
y^Je^{9y/2-\pi e^{2y}}
\le L^Je^{9L/2-\pi e^{2L}}e^{-\lambda_J(L)v}.
\tag{TC8}
\]
Its integral for $v\ge0$ is the numerator divided by
$\lambda_J(L)$, proving the second bound.
The two omitted regions are disjoint: the first has $0\le y\le L$
and $n\ge N$; the second has $y>L$ and every $n\ge1$.
Every omitted summand is nonnegative. Tonelli's theorem therefore
justifies all exchanges before convergence is known; the finite
upper bounds then prove convergence and (TC4).
\end{proof}

For every fixed $J$, the bounds tend to zero by first increasing $L$
and then increasing $N$. Indeed (TC8) is dominated by the negative
double exponential as $L\to\infty$, while for fixed $L$ the factor
$N^4e^{-\pi N^2}$ tends to zero and $r_N\to0$.
The finite integrand
\[
y^J e^{y/2}\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}(16x_n(y)^2-24x_n(y))e^{-x_n(y)},
\qquad x_n(y)=\pi n^2e^{2y},
\tag{TC9}
\]
is entire as a function of the original complex coordinate $y$.
Expanding $x_n$ shows that it is exactly the retained integrand
of (TC2); no scalar factor or negative term has been changed.
This entire extension supplies the analyticity condition for
validated complex-ball quadrature on the finite real intervals.

\section{Exact propagation to the original Hankel matrices}
Define, in the original coefficient convention,
\[
a_j=\frac{(-1)^jM_{2j}}{(2j)!},\qquad
F(w)=\sum_{j\ge0}a_jw^j,\qquad
-\frac{F'(w)}{F(w)}=\sum_{n\ge0}b_nw^n.
\tag{TC10}
\]
The original entire-function comparison is
$F(z^2)=g(1/2+iz)$, and $a_0=M_0>0$. Multiplication of the
last identity by $F$ and extraction of the coefficient of $w^n$
prove the complete recurrence
\[
b_n=
\frac{-(n+1)a_{n+1}-\sum_{j=1}^na_jb_{n-j}}{a_0}.
\tag{TC11}
\]
In particular the original mass remains in every division by $a_0$.
The two real symmetric matrices of dimension $d+1$ are
\[
H_d=(b_{r+s})_{0\le r,s\le d},\qquad
H_d^{(1)}=(b_{r+s+1})_{0\le r,s\le d}.
\tag{TC12}
\]

\begin{proposition}[Finite enclosures and signs]
Suppose that closed rational intervals enclose the finite integrals
and upper bounds in (TC4)--(TC5). Adding the interval
$[0,T_{J,N,L}+Y_{J,L}]$ with outward upper endpoints gives rational
enclosures of the full moments. Applying (TC10)--(TC11) by interval
arithmetic, with an enclosure of $a_0$ having positive lower
endpoint, gives enclosures of every required $b_n$.
An interval $LDL^{\mathsf T}$ recursion whose every pivot has
positive lower endpoint proves positive definiteness of the
original matrix in (TC12).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Addition, subtraction, multiplication and division by an interval
excluding zero each contain the exact operation on contained
arguments. This follows for multiplication from the extrema of a
bilinear function on a rectangle; division reduces to multiplication
by the interval reciprocal. Induction in (TC11) proves the assertion.
No independence of the interval arguments is required for enclosure.

For a symmetric matrix $A$, the exact recursion in the unchanged
coordinate order is
\[
\begin{split}
D_j&=A_{jj}-\sum_{k<j}L_{jk}^2D_k,\\
L_{jj}&=1,\qquad
L_{ij}=\frac{A_{ij}-\sum_{k<j}L_{ik}L_{jk}D_k}{D_j}
\quad(i>j),\qquad L_{ij}=0\quad(i<j).
\end{split}
\tag{TC13}
\]
If the interval pivots have positive lower endpoints, induction
shows that every exact $D_j$ is positive and every division is
defined. Formula (TC13) makes $A_{ij}=\sum_kL_{ik}D_kL_{jk}$
for all $i\ge j$; symmetry gives the remaining entries.
Thus $A=LDL^{\mathsf T}$. The triangular $L$ is invertible with
determinant one. For every nonzero real vector $v$,
$v^{\mathsf T}Av=\sum_jD_j((L^{\mathsf T}v)_j)^2>0$.
The same equality for complex vectors uses the conjugate transpose
and squared moduli and proves Hermitian positive definiteness.
\end{proof}

For a specified rational vector $c=(c_0,\ldots,c_d)$ let
$\gamma_n=\sum_{r+s=n}c_rc_s\in\Q$. The exact signed witness is
\[
c^{\mathsf T}H_dc=\sum_{n=0}^{2d}\gamma_nb_n.
\tag{TC14}
\]
If $b_n\in[\ell_n,u_n]$, its upper endpoint is
\[
U(c)=
\sum_{\gamma_n\ge0}\gamma_nu_n
+\sum_{\gamma_n<0}\gamma_n\ell_n.
\tag{TC15}
\]
Every comparison here is rational. A strict inequality $U(c)<0$
is a finite negative certificate. If $\widehat b_n$ have errors
$|b_n-\widehat b_n|\le\epsilon_n$, the exact error in (TC14)
is at most $\sum_n|\gamma_n|\epsilon_n$, by the triangle
inequality. These statements include all mixed matrix entries.

\section{Quantitative rational form of the counterfactual separator}
We record the full argument to show precisely how a negative
counterfactual form becomes a rational certificate. The entire
factorization in the supplied workbench gives the distinct reciprocal
zeros $\beta_j$ of $F$, with positive integer multiplicities $m_j$,
and
\[
\sum_jm_j|\beta_j|<\infty,\qquad
b_n=\sum_jm_j\beta_j^{n+1}.
\tag{TC16}
\]
No reciprocal zero is zero. The multiset is stable under complex
conjugation. The actual map from a zeta zero
$\rho=1/2+\delta+i\gamma$ is
\[
\beta=-\frac{1}{(\rho-1/2)^2}
=\frac{\gamma^2-\delta^2+2i\gamma\delta}
{(\gamma^2+\delta^2)^2}.
\tag{TC17}
\]
The reflected pair $\rho,1-\rho$ corresponds to a single zero of
$F$ with the same multiplicity. Both primary blocks remain in
the original packet before taking its half-trace.

Let
\[
S_*=\sum_jm_j|\beta_j|,\qquad
R_*=\max\{1,\sup_j|\beta_j|\}<\infty,\qquad
\|P\|_{R_*}=\sum_{r=0}^{\deg P}|p_r|R_*^r
\tag{TC18}
\]
for $P(X)=\sum p_rX^r$. This coefficient norm is an explicit
error-control map; it changes neither $P$ nor the original Hankel
form. Define
\[
\mathcal Q(P)=\sum_jm_j\beta_jP(\beta_j)^2
=p^{\mathsf T}H_{\deg P}p.
\tag{TC19}
\]
Absolute convergence follows from
$|P(\beta_j)|\le\|P\|_{R_*}$ and (TC16). The sum is real for
real $P$ because conjugate terms pair, and real reciprocal terms
are real. The equality with the finite matrix follows by expanding
the finite polynomial product and using (TC16).

\begin{lemma}[Coefficient perturbation with the full reciprocal tail]
For real polynomials $P,E$ one has
\[
|\mathcal Q(P+E)-\mathcal Q(P)|
\le S_*\|E\|_{R_*}\bigl(2\|P\|_{R_*}+\|E\|_{R_*}\bigr).
\tag{TC20}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The polynomial identity $(P+E)^2-P^2=2PE+E^2$ holds at every
$\beta_j$ including every multiplicity. Take absolute values
term by term in the absolutely convergent series (TC19), bound
$|P(\beta_j)|$ and $|E(\beta_j)|$ by (TC18), and sum
$m_j|\beta_j|$. This gives (TC20).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Explicit finite degree and rational coefficient accuracy]
Choose any nonreal reciprocal zero $\beta$ and its conjugate of
multiplicity $m$. Put $r=|\beta|/2$. Let $\mathcal A$ be the finite
set of all other distinct reciprocal zeros of modulus at least
$r$, and set
\[
B(X)=\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal A}(X-\alpha),\quad
B_0=\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal A}(r+|\alpha|),\quad
T_*=\sum_{|\beta_j|<r}m_j|\beta_j|.
\tag{TC21}
\]
Choose either $c\in\C$ satisfying $\beta c^2=-1$ and define
\[
C_*=\frac{|c|B_0}{|B(\beta)|}
\left(1+\frac{|\Re\beta|+r}{|\Im\beta|}\right).
\tag{TC22}
\]
If $T_*=0$, take $N_*=0$. Otherwise take the explicit integer
\[
N_*=\max\left\{0,\,
1+\left\lfloor\frac{\log(C_*^2T_*/m)}{\log4}\right\rfloor\right\}.
\tag{TC23}
\]
For any integer $N\ge N_*$ put
\[
w_N=\frac{c}{\beta^NB(\beta)},\qquad
v_N=\frac{\Im w_N}{\Im\beta},\quad
u_N=\Re w_N-(\Re\beta)v_N,\quad
P_N(X)=X^NB(X)(u_N+v_NX).
\tag{TC24}
\]
Then $\mathcal Q(P_N)\le-m$. A rational polynomial
$\widetilde P_N$ of degree at most
$D=N+\#\mathcal A+1$ can be obtained by rounding each of the
$D+1$ original coefficients to a nearest multiple of $1/H$,
with either choice allowed at a tie, for any
positive integer $H$ with
\[
\frac{\sum_{r'=0}^D R_*^{r'}}{2H}
\le
\min\left\{1,\frac{m}{2S_*(2\|P_N\|_{R_*}+1)}\right\}.
\tag{TC25}
\]
Every such rounding satisfies
$\mathcal Q(\widetilde P_N)\le-m/2<0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Absolute summability in (TC16) makes $\mathcal A$ finite.
It is conjugation stable; hence $B$ has real coefficients,
$B(\beta)\ne0$, and $u_N,v_N$ are real. Evaluation of their
formulas gives $u_N+v_N\beta=w_N$ and therefore
$P_N(\beta)=c$ and $P_N(\overline\beta)=\overline c$.
The selected two terms of (TC19) sum to $-2m$ exactly.
All other terms with modulus at least $r$ vanish.

For $|X|\le r$, one has $|B(X)|\le B_0$ and
\[
|u_N+v_NX|
\le |w_N|\left(1+\frac{|\Re\beta|+r}{|\Im\beta|}\right).
\]
Consequently $|P_N(X)|\le C_*2^{-N}$. The absolute sum of
all remaining terms is at most $C_*^2T_*4^{-N}$, so
\[
\mathcal Q(P_N)\le-2m+C_*^2T_*4^{-N}.
\tag{TC26}
\]
The chosen integer (TC23) makes the last summand at most $m$;
if $T_*=0$ it is zero. This proves the first assertion.
The coefficient rounding error $E=\widetilde P_N-P_N$ has
each coefficient at most $1/(2H)$ in absolute value.
Thus $\|E\|_{R_*}\le\sum_{r'=0}^DR_*^{r'}/(2H)$.
The bound in (TC25) and (TC20) make the change in the quadratic
form at most $m/2$, proving the strict negative result.
Every original coefficient slot is included in the error bound.
A nonzero coefficient may round to zero; an original zero
coefficient remains an exact zero.
\end{proof}

All constants in this counterfactual degree bound retain their
dependence on the chosen actual zero and on the full reciprocal
set. It supplies no universal dimension for an off-critical
witness. Once a rational polynomial is specified, however,
(TC14)--(TC15) require only finitely many original moments.
As their enclosing intervals shrink, their propagated intervals
shrink to the exact coefficients: (TC11) is a finite composition
of continuous rational operations with denominator $a_0>0$.
The strict margin in (TC25) therefore eventually yields a strictly
negative rational upper bound in (TC15).
