## 1. Original coordinates, coefficient phases and raw jets

The original variables are `S=c+iu`, `c=k/2`, with integer k, and the polynomial source norm remains `∫|P(c+iu)|²m_k(u)du`. The coordinate map Ψ:P(S)↦P(c+iu) is an invertible complex algebra map, with inverse f(u)↦f((S−c)/i). Its action identity is `Ψ M_S=(c+iM_u)Ψ`. For a degree-q monic χ, the leading coefficient of Ψχ is i^q. Thus `ψ=i^(−q)Ψχ` is monic, and Taylor expansion at the unchanged c gives exactly

`ψ_j = i^(j−q) χ^(j)(c)/j!`.

No reality or dagger-stability hypothesis on ψ is used in these identities. In particular the optimizer applies to complex coefficients with their phases retained separately.

For a root λ of χ, its corresponding u root is ζ=(λ−c)/i. The chain rule gives `(ΨP)^(d)(ζ)=i^dP^(d)(λ)`. With each root listed once in the fixed order and raw derivative orders d=0,…,ell_a−1, E_N is a q by N+1 matrix. Its written entries `j! ζ_a^(j−d)/(j−d)!` are the exact raw derivatives for j≥d and vanish for j<d.

A polynomial has all these jets zero exactly when each corresponding factor `(u−ζ_a)^(ell_a)` divides it. Coprimality at distinct roots makes this equivalent to divisibility by their product ψ. At N=q−1, a nonzero multiple of ψ cannot occur, so Z=E_(q−1) is injective and hence invertible. At N=q the kernel has dimension one and is generated by the full monic coefficient vector `(ψ_0,…,ψ_q)`, including its zero coefficients.

## 2. OW.4: the exact signed cofactor map

For the retained increasing columns, let Δ_j be the determinant after deleting column j from E_q. Append one of the matrix's rows as the last row of a square matrix. Its determinant is zero because that row is repeated. Expansion along the final row has sign `(−1)^(q+j)` at the column indexed j from zero. Removing the common factor `(−1)^q` proves that the vector with entries `(−1)^jΔ_j` is in the kernel of E_q.

The last entry is `(−1)^qΔ_q=(−1)^q det Z`. Since ψ_q=1, the scalar relating that vector to ψ is exactly `(−1)^q det Z`. Therefore

`Δ_j=(−1)^(q−j) ψ_j det Z`.

This proves the sign before taking a modulus and for every j, including a coefficient that vanishes. Such a coefficient forces a zero cofactor; it does not remove the associated source column or any derivative row. For q=1, E_1=[1,ζ], ψ=[−ζ,1], so Δ_0=ζ and Δ_1=1, agreeing with the same formula.

## 3. OW.5: raw confluent determinant and factorials

Start with the ordinary Vandermonde matrix with increasing powers and with distinct nodes ordered cluster by cluster. Its determinant is the product of every later node minus every earlier node. Within each cluster, replace successive evaluation rows by their successive divided-difference rows. The determinant multiplier removes precisely the Vandermonde product inside that cluster, with no row permutation. When the cluster nodes tend to ζ_a, the divided difference of the monomial u^j of order d tends to its d-th raw derivative at ζ_a divided by d!; this follows directly by its finite polynomial Taylor expansion.

The factors between separate clusters tend to `(ζ_(a')−ζ_a)^(ell_a ell_(a'))` for a<a'. Multiplying each order-d limiting row by d! restores the actual raw derivative row. Hence

`det Z = [∏_a∏_(d=0)^(ell_a−1)d!] ∏_(a<a')(ζ_(a')−ζ_a)^(ell_a ell_(a'))`.

The nodes for different clusters are distinct. The expression is never asserted across an unidentified root collision; such a collision is represented from the outset by its one complete cluster length. The proof may equivalently be read as cancellation in a polynomial determinant identity. For one cluster the formula reduces to the product of the diagonal raw-derivative factorials; for q=1 it is the empty product one.

## 4. OW.6–OW.7: literal masses and determinant coefficients

For 0<b<π/2, the stated arithmetic source has finite Laplace integrals at both b and −b. Fubini on the literal k-fold convolution gives `∫e^(±bu)m_k(u)du=M_h(±b)^k`. Thus `M_k=M_h(b)^k+M_h(−b)^k` retains both Laplace values and their full masses; it is not a probability-normalized quantity.

All `β_j=b^(2j)/(2j)!` are positive. Expanding the determinant of `A_q(τ)=E_q diag(τ_jβ_j)E_q*` by Cauchy–Binet gives one term for each q-element subset of its q+1 columns. Its term is the squared modulus of the corresponding minor, times the product of the selected diagonal entries. Substituting the signed cofactor identity therefore gives exactly

`det A_q(τ)=|det Z|² Σ_j |ψ_j|² ∏_(l≠j)β_l τ_l`.

With `B_β=∏_(l=0)^qβ_l`, the coefficient a_j is `B_βη_j`, where

`η_j=|ψ_j|²/β_j=(2j)! |χ^(j)(c)|² / ((j!)² b^(2j))`.

Here the phase i^(j−q) has modulus one, but its exact earlier map remains available. At j=q, χ^(q)=q!, so `η_q=(2q)!b^(−2q)>0`; no factorial q! remains erroneously in its denominator. Zero coefficients remain zero terms in the same q+1-variable determinant.

The auxiliary positive-cone map `τ↦(s,τ/s)` with s=Στ is bijective with inverse `(s,t)↦st`. The determinant is homogeneous of degree q, so it acquires s^q. The cancellation with q log s is exact. This is a coordinate change of auxiliary weights; it neither changes the source density nor its mass or original quotient norm.

## 5. OW.9–OW.13: determinant representation and original phases

For a_q>0 let `v_i=sqrt(a_i/a_q)`, i<q. The rank-one determinant expansion of `diag(τ_0,…,τ_(q−1))+τ_qvv*` equals `D_a(τ)/a_q` on positive diagonal entries. Equality on the whole simplex follows because both sides are polynomials. The represented matrix is positive semidefinite for nonnegative weights, and its determinant is positive exactly when it is positive definite.

For a Hermitian affine perturbation M+tE through positive definite matrices, differentiating the inverse identity gives `(M^(−1))'=−M^(−1)EM^(−1)`. Multilinearity of the determinant gives the first logarithmic derivative `Tr(M^(−1)E)` and the second derivative

`−Tr((M^(−1/2)EM^(−1/2))²)`.

The middle matrix is Hermitian, so the trace of its square is the sum of its real eigenvalues squared and is zero exactly when E=0. This proves the concavity used by the finite determinant argument. When p≥3, at least two v_i are nonzero. Their off-diagonal matrix entry determines τ_q, and the diagonal entries then determine the other weights. This proves the stated injectivity that yields strict concavity in that case. The p=1 and p=2 cases are explicitly treated by the separate polynomial factorization.

For the original matrix, monic remainder gives `E_q=Z[I_q,−ψ_<q]`. Keeping that minus sign yields

`A_q(τ)=Z B_0^(1/2)[diag(τ_<q)+τ_qzz*]B_0^(1/2)Z*`,

where `B_0=diag(β_0,…,β_(q−1))` and `z_i=−ψ_i sqrt(β_q/β_i)`. Its modulus square is `|z_i|²=η_i/η_q=a_i/a_q`. The diagonal matrix with entries `U_i=−ψ_i/|ψ_i|` for ψ_i≠0 and U_i=1 otherwise is unitary. It gives z=Uv and commutes with the real diagonal τ matrix. Hence the actual central matrix is `U M(τ) U*`. This proves OW.13 with every phase and the original minus sign present. The zero-coordinate convention is well-defined because both z_i and v_i are zero there; it does not change the row or column dimensions.

## 6. OW.25: original S/u quotient congruences

The analytic section uses integer k≥3, 0<b<π/2 and q≥1. It takes the sealed AU lower-comparison theorem as input, with its stated constants c_h>0, vartheta_h>0, B=42+2 deg h and α=π/2. This review checks the maps and inequalities using that proved measure domination. It does not independently rederive the preceding AU construction of those constants.

The density of ν_k is positive everywhere and has exponential tails, so all its polynomial moments exist. The original arithmetic density is positive almost everywhere with all moments. Hence both degree-N Gram matrices are positive definite: a nonzero polynomial vanishes on only finitely many real u values and has strictly positive squared norm. E_N has full row rank for every N≥q−1.

To specify precisely how the u presentation gives the original S quotient, let L_(N+1) map original S coefficients to coefficients of the same polynomial after S=c+iu. Its entries are `L_(a,j)=binom(j,a)c^(j−a)i^a` for a≤j. Its diagonal determinant is `i^(N(N+1)/2)`, of modulus one. Thus `H_N^S=L_(N+1)* H_N^u L_(N+1)`.

Let J_S be remainder modulo χ and J_u remainder modulo ψ, each in its monic coefficient basis. Substituting `Ψχ=i^qψ` into polynomial division gives the exact commutative identity

`J_u L_(N+1)=L_q J_S`.

The remainder has degree below q and its coefficient map is the same L_q. Inverting the Gram congruence and substituting this identity gives

`G_N^S=L_q*G_N^uL_q`, and therefore `det G_N^S=det G_N^u`.

This proves equality of the original quotient volumes through the exact coordinate map. It does not assert entrywise equality of two differently based Gram matrices.

The raw jet relation has its own precise phase map. If D_raw is diagonal with entry i^d on the derivative row (a,d), then `E_N^u L_(N+1)=D_raw E_N^S`. Consequently `K_N^{S,raw}=D_raw^(−1) K_N^{u,raw}D_raw^(−*)`; its determinant agrees because D_raw is unitary. These congruences connect the source, quotient and raw-jet presentations with all phases retained.

Within the u chart, E_N=ZJ_N because jets factor through the monic remainder and Z is the full jet map on degree-below-q representatives. Thus

`det K_N^raw = |det Z|² det(J_NH_N^(−1)J_N*) = |det Z|²/V_N`.

For a quotient vector x, `R_Nx=H_N^(−1)J_N*G_Nx` satisfies J_NR_Nx=x. If z is any relation vector, then `z*H_NR_Nx=(J_Nz)*G_Nx=0`. Every other lift differs by such a vector, so orthogonal Pythagoras proves this is the minimum-norm lift. The same formulas apply to ν with its own full mass. This establishes the original norms used in the volume conversion.

## 7. OW.26: both Loewner directions and the boundary extension

The input measure domination gives `H_N^ν≼H_N`. Conjugation by a positive square root shows inversion reverses this order, so `H_N^(−1)≼(H_N^ν)^(−1)`. Congruence by E_N then gives `K_N^raw≼K_N^{ν,raw}`.

`|Σ_(j=0)^q p_j u^j|² ≤ [Σ_j |p_j|²/(τ_jβ_j)] [Σ_j τ_jβ_j|u|^(2j)]`.

Integration and the individual moment estimates bound the second factor by M_kΣτ_j=M_k. This proves the written `H_q≼M_k diag((τ_jβ_j)^(−1))`, whose inverse and raw-jet congruence give `K_q^raw≽M_k^(−1)A_q(τ)`.

The volume identity yields

`C_k=log(V_q/V_(2q−1))=log det K_(2q−1)^raw−log det K_q^raw`.

Monotonicity of log determinant on positive definite matrices follows by conjugation and positive eigenvalues. Applying the upper comparison to the first term and the lower comparison to the second gives exactly OW.26, including q log M_k. The coefficient q is the dimension of each raw quotient kernel.

## 9. q=1 and the strict quartet gain

For q=1, Z=1, ψ=u+ψ_0, β_0=1 and β_1=b²/2. The determinant polynomial is the stated linear polynomial, and

`B_β d(η)=max{1,β_1|ψ_0|²}`.

Its relation denominator is the one-column Gram `∫|ψ|²dν`, which is strictly positive. There is no use of a degree-zero raw kernel with the wrong number of columns or of the q≥2 uniqueness proof.

For OW.23, each q-fold selected product is at most q^(−q), since its selected weights have sum at most one. Equality in a term omitting j requires τ_j=0 and every other weight 1/q. Two distinct positive terms cannot simultaneously have these equality conditions. Thus the upper value equality occurs exactly with one positive coefficient. At equal weights the gradient is `(q+1)^(−(q−1))(S_a−a_i)`; interior stationarity forces all coefficients equal. Conversely those equal coefficients give a stationary positive determinant and hence a maximum by concavity, including the direct linear q=1 case. These prove the precise two equality criteria used later.

For the complete quartet, the displayed roots `(2b−k)gamma−i(2a−k)delta` are pairwise distinct for the grid indices because delta,gamma>0. Negation corresponds to `(a,b)↦(k−a,k−b)` and preserves each complete length `1+k(m−1)`. Thus monicity gives `ψ(−u)=(−1)^qψ(u)`, forcing coefficients of the opposite parity to vanish. Since ψ_q=1, the η coefficients cannot all equal one common value. Also the grid contains a nonzero root, for example its (0,0) root, so ψ is not u^q. Therefore at least one additional coefficient is nonzero and at least two η coefficients are positive.

The two equality criteria now give exactly `0<g(η)<q log(1+1/q)<1`. The final inequality follows from the strict integral bound `log(1+x)<x` at x=1/q. The quotient indices for a complete quartet obey q≥(k+1)² with k≥3, so this case never relies on q=1. Since q→infinity, the bounded weight-only gain divided by q log k or q² tends to zero. ## 10. Additional proved bound from the full even-moment sum

The individual moment estimates used in OW.26 leave available a stronger joint inequality. With exactly the same β_j and M_k, the finite sum satisfies pointwise

`Σ_(j=0)^q β_j u^(2j) ≤ Σ_(j=0)^infinity b^(2j)u^(2j)/(2j)! = cosh(bu)`.

All terms are nonnegative on real u. Integrating and using the same two original Laplace integrals gives

`Σ_(j=0)^q β_j ∫u^(2j)m_k(u)du ≤ ∫cosh(bu)m_k(u)du = M_k/2`.

Apply Cauchy–Schwarz directly with β_j, before integration:

`|Σ_(j=0)^q p_j u^j|² ≤ [Σ_j |p_j|²/β_j][Σ_j β_j u^(2j)]`.

The joint integral bound therefore proves

`H_q ≼ (M_k/2) diag(β_j^(−1))`, and `K_q^raw ≽ (2/M_k) A_q(1,…,1)`.

Here `(1,…,1)` is an explicitly specified positive-cone argument of the already defined matrix A_q. This is not a claim that it lies on the simplex. The new proof uses the joint moment sum in place of the sum of the separate estimates, so its bound has no q+1 multiplier. It preserves every original coefficient, factorial, mass and source norm.

Cauchy–Binet at that argument gives `det A_q(1,…,1)=|det Z|² B_β Σ_jη_j`. Substituting the same upper comparator and determinant conversion now proves the stronger original arithmetic inequality

`C_k ≤ U_joint := q log(M_k/2) − log B_β − log(Σ_jη_j) − log V_(2q−1)^ν`.

Its exact relation to the two upper expressions at the reviewed source hash is

`U_eq−U_joint = q log(2(q+1))`,

`U_opt−U_joint = q log(2(q+1))−g(η) = q log2 + log((Ση_j)/d(η))`.

Consequently

`q log(2q) ≤ U_opt−U_joint ≤ q log(2(q+1))`.

For a complete quartet both inequalities are strict because its gain has the strict OW.29 bounds. This improvement is separate from the finite weight optimization of the existing AU.19 family. In particular it has size q log q and can affect a q log k comparison for the stated polynomially growing quartet q. It does not contradict the less-than-one result for the difference U_eq−U_opt, whose two expressions share the earlier individual-moment inequality. No asymptotic conclusion for V^ν or zero-location conclusion is inferred from this improvement.
