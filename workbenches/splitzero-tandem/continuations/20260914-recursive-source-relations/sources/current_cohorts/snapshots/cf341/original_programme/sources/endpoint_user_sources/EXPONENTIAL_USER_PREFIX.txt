## 2. Complete the cohomology of the curve already constructed

The constant coefficient connection restricts to the logarithmic complex

In the local frame \(w^m v\), \(w=1/z\), its coefficient operator is exactly

This is the logarithmic differential, with its boundary residue retained. The general residue-complex construction is standard; the following calculation is on this particular lattice. ([The Stacks Project][2])

### The calculated groups

As modules with the induced \(A\)-action,

$$
\boxed{
\mathbb H^0(X,\mathcal K_{F,m})=F,\qquad
\mathbb H^1(X,\mathcal K_{F,m})=0,\qquad
\mathbb H^2(X,\mathcal K_{F,m})=E/F.

The coefficient calculation is explicit. The compact component has Čech groups

The differential is

\boxed{z^{-a}\longmapsto-a\,z^{-(a+1)}dz.}

It is injective, and its cokernel is the retained residue line generated by \(z^{-1}dz\). The ordinary logarithmic component contributes its constants in degree zero. The exact sequence with subobject \(F\) and quotient \(E/F\) gives the displayed groups without choosing an \(A\)-invariant complement.

Restoring the original cochain degree \(k\) gives

\boxed{
\operatorname{Str}\!\left(f(A)\mid R\Gamma\mathcal K_{F,m}[-k]\right)

(-1)^k\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(f(A)\mid E\right).

Both \(F\) and \(E/F\), including their induced nilpotent structure, remain in that trace.

For the constant coefficient system on \(\mathbb A^1\), the actual compact-to-ordinary comparison therefore has

$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{im}\!\left(
H_c^1(\mathbb A^1,E)\longrightarrow H^1(\mathbb A^1,E)

$$

**This locates the preceding curve’s role:** it is a genuine metric and boundary comparison, but its logarithmic constant-coefficient realization does not yet put the arithmetic \(E\) into Deligne’s nonconstant degree-one image. The construction below supplies a degree-one family with \(E\) as a specified special fibre.

The curvature calculation is retained as well. Pulling back by \(z\mapsto z^m\) multiplies total curvature degree by \(m\), while the original endpoint logarithmic moment remains \(\log(V_i/V_j)\). Saturation contributes the explicit boundary current

$$
-ms_A\delta_\infty,\qquad s_A=\dim F-\dim E_0,

rather than producing a free improvement in the metric estimate.

## 3. Construct the degree-one family from the original cyclic relation

Keep the original

$$
g=2\xi,\qquad
E=\mathbb C[S]/(\chi),\qquad
\chi=\chi_{h,k},\qquad
A=M_S,\qquad q=\deg\chi>0.
$$

Here \(S=s_1+\cdots+s_k\) remains the sum coordinate. The full arithmetic inclusion remains

$$
\eta[P]
=
\upsilon_h^{\otimes k}P(A_k)1,
\qquad
\upsilon_h=j_h(g/h).
$$

Write

$$
\chi(S)=S^q+\sum_{a=0}^{q-1}c_aS^a.
$$

Introduce independent parameters \(u,t\), and retain the specified primitive

$$
\boxed{
\Phi(S)
=
\frac{S^{q+1}}{q+1}
+
\sum_{a=0}^{q-1}\frac{c_aS^{a+1}}{a+1},
\qquad
\Phi_t(S)=\Phi(S)-tS.
}
$$

Every coefficient is present. This constructs another object from \(\chi\); it does not replace \(g=2\xi\).

Over \(R=\mathbb C[u,t]\), define

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal C_{u,t}
=
\left[
R[S]\xrightarrow{\mathcal D_{u,t}}R[S]\,dS
\right],
\qquad
\mathcal D_{u,t}P
=
\bigl(uP'+(\chi-t)P\bigr)dS.
}
$$

### Free cohomology, including at repeated roots

There is a split coefficient-module decomposition

$$
\boxed{
R[S]
=
\bigl(\mathcal D_{u,t}(R[S])/dS\bigr)
\oplus R[S]_{<q}.
}
$$

Consequently,

$$
\boxed{
H^0(\mathcal C_{u,t})=0,\qquad
\mathcal H:=H^1(\mathcal C_{u,t})\cong R^q,
\qquad
\mathcal H/(u,t)\mathcal H\cong E.
}
$$

**Proof.** If \(P\) has degree \(a\) and leading coefficient \(b\), then

$$
uP'+(\chi-t)P
$$

has degree \(a+q\) and the same leading coefficient \(b\). It cannot vanish. Subtracting the corresponding differential of a monomial removes the highest term of any polynomial of degree at least \(q\). Iteration terminates in degree below \(q\), and the same leading-degree argument proves uniqueness.

The calculation stays in \(R\) and survives coefficient base change. At \(u=t=0\), the differential becomes multiplication by the literal \(\chi\), giving the original quotient—including every multiple-root jet.

The quotient here is a **coefficient-module quotient**. Its relationship to multiplication is the exact commutator

$$
\boxed{[\mathcal D_{u,t}/dS,M_S]=uI.}
$$

For example,

$$
[\chi-t]=0,\qquad
[S(\chi-t)]=-u[1].
$$

Thus multiplication by \(S\) alone does not descend for general \(u\). The parameter connection does.

## 4. The connection contains the original arithmetic operator

After inverting \(u\), define

$$
\boxed{
\nabla_t[P]
=
[\partial_tP-SP/u].
}
$$

The two commutators are

$$
[\mathcal D/dS,\partial_t]=I,\qquad
[\mathcal D/dS,-M_S/u]=-I.
$$

Their sum vanishes, proving that \(\nabla_t\) descends through the actual differential.

In the retained basis \(1,S,\ldots,S^{q-1}\),

$$
\boxed{
\nabla_t=\partial_t-\frac{A(t)}u,
\qquad
A(t)=A+t\mathcal R,
}
$$

where

$$
\mathcal R[P]
=
\bigl(\text{coefficient of }S^{q-1}\text{ in }\operatorname{rem}_\chi P\bigr)[1].
$$

The last companion column is exactly

$$
A(t)[S^{q-1}]
=
(-c_0+t)[1]-c_1[S]-\cdots-c_{q-1}[S^{q-1}].
$$

The operator

$$
-u\nabla_t=-u\partial_t+A(t)
$$

is regular in \(u\), with its explicit coefficient rule

$$
(-u\nabla_t)(fv)
=
f(-u\nabla_t)v-u(\partial_tf)v.
$$

Reducing at the specified special fibre gives

$$
\boxed{(-u\nabla_t)\bmod(u,t)=A.}
$$

Write

Use the existing full-jet observation followed by the retained equivariant cyclic retraction:

The map \(j_E\) kills the original theta boundaries; its kernel on the whole test-function space remains available.

=

Then

$$
j_ED_t^{(k)}=A(t)j_E,

and

\boxed{
D_t^{(k)}r_N-r_NA(t)
=

The original theta primitive is unchanged. At \(t=0\), the source operator is precisely the original \(D^{(k)}\).

The original metric also stays fixed:

This records the parameter’s exact effect on the existing control form.

## 5. The periods are explicit and proved to span

For a fixed \(u\ne0\), put \(d=q+1\) and choose the retained ray angles

$$
\theta_j=\frac{\arg u+\pi}{d}+\frac{2\pi j}{d},
\qquad 0\le j<d.
$$

Let \(\ell_j\) be the ray from zero to infinity with angle \(\theta_j\), and set

$$
\Gamma_j=-\ell_0+\ell_j,\qquad 1\le j\le q.
$$

Define

$$
\boxed{
\Pi_{jb}(u,t)
=
\int_{\Gamma_j}
e^{\Phi_t(S)/u}S^b\,dS,
\qquad 0\le b<q.
}
$$

Along these rays, the leading exponent is

$$
-\frac{r^d}{d|u|}.
$$

It dominates all lower-degree terms, uniformly on compact coefficient sets. This proves absolute convergence and permits differentiation in the polynomial parameters.

The original differential integrates to zero:

$$
\int_{\Gamma_j}
e^{\Phi_t/u}\bigl(uP'+(\chi-t)P\bigr)dS
=
u\int_{\Gamma_j}d(e^{\Phi_t/u}P)=0.
$$

The two origin values cancel because of the displayed contour orientation.

### Why the period matrix is invertible

At the monomial potential \(\Phi=S^d/d\), \(t=0\), its entries are

$$
\boxed{
\Pi_{jb}^{\mathrm{mon}}
=
\frac{(d|u|)^{(b+1)/d}}{d}
\Gamma\!\left(\frac{b+1}{d}\right)
e^{i(b+1)\theta_0}
\left(e^{2\pi ij(b+1)/d}-1\right).
}
$$

All factors and phases remain.

The last matrix is nonsingular. A dependence among its columns would produce a polynomial

$$
\sum_{l=1}^{q}a_l(z^l-1)
$$

of degree at most \(q\), vanishing at all \(q+1\) roots of unity. It is therefore zero.

To pass from that member to the actual polynomial, the coefficient-direction connections are calculated:

$$
\boxed{
\nabla_{c_a}
=
\partial_{c_a}+\frac{S^{a+1}}{(a+1)u},
\qquad
[\mathcal D/dS,\nabla_{c_a}]=0.
}
$$

Reducing their polynomial columns gives matrices \(B_a\) with

$$
\partial_{c_a}\Pi=\Pi B_a,\qquad
\partial_t\Pi=-\Pi A(t)/u.
$$

Along a straight coefficient path from the monomial to the actual potential,

$$
\Pi'=\Pi B,\qquad
(\det\Pi)'=(\operatorname{Tr}B)\det\Pi.
$$

The initial determinant is nonzero, so the scalar differential equation keeps it nonzero along the path.

Thus

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal H_{u,t}
\xrightarrow[\sim]{\text{the displayed contour integrals}}
\mathbb C^q
}
$$

for every \(u\ne0\), including when critical points coincide. Polynomial-exponential de Rham theory and rapid-decay periods are established machinery; the note gives this particular comparison’s contours, constants, and elementary proof rather than importing an unspecified period isomorphism. ([DOI][3])

## 6. Deligne’s infinity calculation applies to this potential

Let \(R_0\subset\mathbb C\) be the finitely generated ring containing the actual coefficients \(c_a\) and \(1/(q+1)!\).

For a specified specialization

$$
R_0\longrightarrow\mathbb F_Q,
\qquad \operatorname{char}\mathbb F_Q=p>q+1,
$$

and \(u_0\ne0\), the same polynomial data give

$$
\boxed{
Y^p-Y=\frac{\Phi(S)-t_0S}{u_0}.
}
$$

Here is the infinity chart needed in the family argument. Set \(w=1/S\). Then

$$
\frac{\Phi(S)-tS}{u}
=
w^{-d}b(w,u,t),
$$

where

$$
\boxed{
b(w,u,t)
=
\frac1u\left[
\frac1d+
\sum_{a=0}^{q-1}\frac{c_a}{a+1}w^{q-a}
-tw^q
\right].
}
$$

Its value at \(w=0\) is the unit \(1/(du)\).

Adjoin \(v\) by

$$
b\,v^d=1,
\qquad
x_\infty=wv.
$$

The cover is étale near the boundary: the derivative in \(v\) is \(dbv^{d-1}\), with inverse \(v/d\) on the cover. Direct substitution gives

$$
\boxed{
\frac{\Phi(S)-tS}{u}=x_\infty^{-d}.
}
$$

The root-of-unity action \(v\mapsto\zeta v\), \(\zeta^d=1\), is retained.

**That is the explicit local constant model at infinity used by the lissity argument.** The parameter open \(u\ne0\) remains part of its domain.

For the corresponding nontrivial Artin–Schreier eigensheaf, the degree is \(d=q+1\), prime to \(p\), and the leading projective zero locus in \(\mathbb P^0\) is empty and smooth. The displayed theorem in Deligne’s exponential-sum argument therefore gives

$$
\boxed{
H_c^a(\mathbb A^1,\mathcal L_\psi((\Phi-tS)/u))=0
\quad(a\ne1),
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
\dim H_c^1=q,
\qquad H_c^1\text{ is pure of weight }1.
}
$$

This is an application of his theorem to the constructed family, not a new proof of that theorem.

The realization diagram is a span:

$$
\boxed{
(R_0,\Phi,u,t)
\longrightarrow
\begin{cases}
(\mathbb C,\mathcal C_{u,t},\nabla_t,\Pi),\\
(\mathbb F_Q,\mathcal L_\psi((\Phi-tS)/u),R^1p_!).
\end{cases}
}
$$

Both arrows start at \(R_0\). There is no field map \(\mathbb C\to\mathbb F_Q\), and no identification of finite-field Frobenius with \(A\) is presumed.

## 7. Duality and the supported relation remain attached

On degree-below-\(q\) representatives, use the bilinear residue form

$$
\mathscr S_t(P,Q)
=
\sum_{\chi(\lambda)=t}
\operatorname{Res}_{S=\lambda}
\frac{P(S)Q(S)}{\chi(S)-t}\,dS.
$$

It is independent of \(t\).

Indeed,

$$
\frac1{\chi-t}-\frac1\chi
=
\frac{t}{\chi(\chi-t)}.
$$

Since \(\deg(PQ)\le2q-2\), the difference has no \(S^{-1}\) coefficient at infinity.

Its constant matrix \(\mathsf S\) satisfies

$$
\det\mathsf S=(-1)^{q(q-1)/2},
\qquad
A(t)^T\mathsf S=\mathsf S A(t).
$$

The exact dual comparison is between the connections at \(u\) and \(-u\):

$$
\boxed{
\partial_t(v^T\mathsf S w)
=
(\nabla_t^uv)^T\mathsf S w
+
v^T\mathsf S(\nabla_t^{-u}w).
}
$$

On the original special fibre, compose with the retained reflection \(f\mapsto f^{\dagger_k}\). The Jacobian contraction is

$$
\boxed{
\mathscr S_0(f^{\dagger_k},\chi'v)
=
\operatorname{Tr}_E M_{f^{\dagger_k}v}.
}
$$

The larger arithmetic trace still passes through \(\eta\) and its retained complement.

The split coefficient specialization is equally explicit:

$$
G(\mathbb C[u,t])\longrightarrow G(\mathbb C),
\qquad
u^\bullet,t^\bullet\longmapsto e,\qquad
\tau\longmapsto\tau.
$$

At each original label,

$$
(\ell,[P]_{\mathcal D})
\longmapsto
(\ell,[P(0,0,S)]_\chi).
$$

The family relation is

$$
\boxed{[\chi P]=t[P]-u[P'].}
$$

At \(u=t=0\), this becomes the original supported zero. The fibrewise quotient preserves the label and identifies precisely the original relation submodule. 

The already-formalized conormal derivative remains connected by

$$
[\partial_S(\chi P)]_\chi=[\chi'P]_\chi.
$$

That response is the Jacobian above; it is not replaced by the different \(-uP'\) term in the new family relation. The original derivative-between-levels construction remains intact. 

## 8. The norm comparison is now an actual matrix, not an unspecified period correspondence

Keep the original arithmetic \(G_N\). Give the explicit period-coordinate target its stated coordinate Gram \(I_q\), and define

$$
H_\Pi(u,t)=\Pi(u,t)^*\Pi(u,t),
\qquad
\boxed{B_N^{\mathrm{per}}=G_N^{-1}H_\Pi.}
$$

Then

$$
\|\Pi v\|^2=v^*G_NB_N^{\mathrm{per}}v,
$$

and at the same \((u,t)\),

$$
\boxed{
\log\frac{V_i}{V_j}
=
\log\frac{\det B_j^{\mathrm{per}}}{\det B_i^{\mathrm{per}}}.
}
$$

Thus the endpoint cost has been transported into two explicit period-to-source comparisons. Changing to period coordinates has not removed it.

The singular specialization matters even in rank one. For the declared calibration

$$
\chi(S)=S-\lambda,\qquad u>0,
$$

the contour \(i\mathbb R\), oriented upwards, gives

$$
\boxed{
\Pi(u,t)
=
i\sqrt{2\pi u}\,
e^{-(\lambda+t)^2/(2u)},
\qquad
-u\,\partial_t\log\Pi=\lambda+t.
}
$$

For real \(\lambda,t\), its squared norm is

$$
2\pi u\,e^{-(\lambda+t)^2/u}.
$$

Taking \(\lambda=3/4\) gives the exact original coefficient \(3/4\), while the associated good-characteristic exponential sheaves still have weight-one Frobenius. This is an algebraic calibration, not an asserted zeta packet. It demonstrates why the finite-field purity statement must be connected to the **specific theta metric and action**, rather than used as an automatic location theorem for the special coefficient.

The inherited off-line threshold remains

$$
\liminf_{k\to\infty}
\frac{\mathcal B_{h,k}}{q_k\log k}\ge4.

The formalization report retains both that conclusion and the absence of a contradicting upper estimate.
