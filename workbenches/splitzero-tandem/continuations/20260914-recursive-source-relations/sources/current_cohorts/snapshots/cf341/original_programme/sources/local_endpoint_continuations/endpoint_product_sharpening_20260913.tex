\begin{document}\maketitle
This continuation proves the product refinement in the delivered
four-endpoint calculation, then solves the associated scalar problem
with only the combined logarithmic budget retained. It gives explicit
maps from these quantities to the original source, relation and Schur
determinants. The new arithmetic consequence uses the proved norm
estimate of the accompanying \emph{Arithmetic endpoint bounds} source.
Its analytic proof remains an explicit dependency; the finite polynomial
calculations below do not certify that analytic theorem or an upper
estimate for the growing arithmetic relation volumes.

\section{Original source and a proof of its exact radius identity}
Fix the original monic packet $h$ of actual selected zeros, with all
their orders, of $g=2\xi$, and put
\[
 v_h=g/h,\quad w_h(t)=|v_h(1/2+it)|^2/(2\pi),\quad
 m_{h,k}=w_h^{*k},\quad c=k/2,
 \quad \int m_{h,k}=\mu_h^k,\quad \mu_h=\int w_h.\tag{EP.1}
\]
The complete source construction proves positivity almost everywhere
and all polynomial moments, also after multiplication by $e^{\theta u}$
for real $|\theta|<\pi/2$. Retain that literal density and the pairing
\[
 \langle P,Q\rangle_\theta=
 \int_\R\overline{P(c+iu)}Q(c+iu)e^{\theta u}m_{h,k}(u)\,du.
 \tag{EP.2}
\]
It is conjugate-linear in its first entry. Every nonzero polynomial has
positive squared norm since its zero set on the line is finite.
Let $\chi$ be the original degree-$q$ monic generator of
$(h(s_1),\ldots,h(s_k))\cap\C[S]$, where $S=\sum s_i$.
In this section $q\ge1$ and the packet is stable under
$\rho\mapsto1-\bar\rho$. Reflection preserves this ideal and gives
$\overline\chi(k-S)=(-1)^q\chi(S)$ by monicity. Hence
$\widehat\chi(u)=i^{-q}\chi(c+iu)$ is real monic.

Write $\mathcal P_N=\C[S]_{\le N}$, $\mathcal P_{-1}=0$, and
$C=\C[S]/(\chi)$ in the coordinates $1,S,\ldots,S^{q-1}$.
Monic division proves the exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal P_{N-q}\xrightarrow{\,\times\chi\,}
\mathcal P_N\xrightarrow{J_N}C\longrightarrow0\qquad(N\ge q-1).
\tag{EP.3}
\]
Let $H_N$ be the Gram in the stated monomial coordinates. The exact
least-lift matrices, with their full types, are
\[
 K_N=J_NH_N^{-1}J_N^*,\quad G_N=K_N^{-1},\quad
 R_N=H_N^{-1}J_N^*G_N:C\longrightarrow\mathcal P_N,
 \quad V_N=\det G_N.\tag{EP.4}
\]
Surjectivity of $J_N$ proves $K_N>0$. If $B_N$ is the multiplication
matrix for $\chi$, direct multiplication gives $J_NR_N=I$ and
$B_N^*H_NR_N=0$. Every lift of $x$ is $R_Nx+B_Ny$ and has squared
norm $x^*G_Nx+y^*B_N^*H_NB_Ny$. This proves both the minimum
property and $R_N^*H_NR_N=G_N$. Inclusions of the polynomial spaces
therefore give $G_{N+1}\preceq G_N$ and $V_{N+1}\le V_N$.

Let $p_j$ be the monic orthogonal family in $S$, with squared norms
$\omega_j>0$, and let $b_j=[p_j]_\chi$. The monic triangular
coefficient matrix conjugates $H_N$ to
$\diag(\omega_0,\ldots,\omega_N)$, so
\[
 K_N=\sum_{j=0}^N b_jb_j^*/\omega_j.\tag{EP.5}
\]
For $N\ge q$ define $\delta_N=V_N/V_{N-1}\in(0,1]$.
The determinant identity $\det(I+XY)=\det(I+YX)$ follows by
eliminating the two diagonal blocks of
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&X\\-Y&I\end{smallmatrix}\right)$
in the two orders. Applied to (EP.5), it gives
\[
 \frac{b_N^*G_Nb_N}{\omega_N}=1-\delta_N,\qquad
 \frac{b_{N+1}^*G_Nb_{N+1}}{\omega_{N+1}}
 =\delta_{N+1}^{-1}-1.\tag{EP.6}
\]
Both follow respectively by subtracting the last term of $K_N$ and
adding the next term; all the earlier kernels inverted here are positive.

The exact algebra map
\[
 \Phi:C\longrightarrow\C[u]/(\widehat\chi),\quad
 [P]\longmapsto[P(c+iu)],\qquad
 \Phi^{-1}[Q]=[Q((S-c)/i)]\tag{EP.7}
\]
has coefficient matrix $E$ triangular with diagonal $1,i,\ldots,i^{q-1}$.
Thus $|\det E|=1$. The quotient Gram becomes
$E^{-*}G_NE^{-1}$ and its determinant stays $V_N$.
Put $A=M_S:C\to C$ and $T=M_u$ in the real quotient, so
$\Phi A\Phi^{-1}=cI+iT$ and
$\sigma=\Tr T=(\Tr A-cq)/i\in\R$.
The real monic polynomials $Q_j=i^{-j}p_j(c+iu)$ have norms
$\omega_j$. Pairing $uQ_j$ with $Q_l$ proves
\[
 uQ_j=Q_{j+1}+a_j^{(0)}Q_j+
       (\omega_j/\omega_{j-1})Q_{j-1},\quad a_j^{(0)}\in\R;
 \qquad
 Sp_j=p_{j+1}+(c+ia_j^{(0)})p_j-
       (\omega_j/\omega_{j-1})p_{j-1}.\tag{EP.8}
\]
The last term is absent for $j=0$. Indeed pairing with degrees below
$j-1$ vanishes by self-adjoint multiplication by $u$, the leading
coefficient of $Q_{j+1}$ is one, and pairing with $Q_{j-1}$ gives
the displayed norm ratio.

For completeness the phase has its original derivative interpretation.
In the real coordinates let $d_j=[Q_j]$, and keep the transported
matrices denoted temporarily by $R,G$. Differentiating the minimum
norm shows $G'=R^*uR$: the two terms containing $R'$ vanish because
$J_NR'=0$ and $R$ is orthogonal to $\ker J_N$. The leading
coefficient of $Rx$ is $d_N^*Gx/\omega_N$. The polynomial
\[
 uRx-RTx-(Q_{N+1}-Rd_{N+1})d_N^*Gx/\omega_N
\]
is in $\ker J_N$: its leading degree cancels and its reduction is zero.
Pair it with $R$ and use $R^*Q_{N+1}=0$. Taking the trace gives
\[
 \partial_\theta\log V_N
 =\sigma-d_N^*Gd_{N+1}/\omega_N,
 \quad \phi_N:=\sigma-\partial_\theta\log V_N,
 \quad b_N^*G_Nb_{N+1}=i\omega_N\phi_N.\tag{EP.9}
\]
The factor $i$ is $\overline{i^N}i^{N+1}$; no phase has been removed.
Differentiation is justified by the exponential moment domination of
the original source.

Substitute (EP.8) into (EP.5). Terms with adjacent indices cancel
because $\omega_j/\omega_{j-1}$ is the exact recurrence coefficient;
diagonal terms cancel against $2cK_N$. Thus
\[
 AK_N+K_NA^*-2cK_N=
 (b_{N+1}b_N^*+b_Nb_{N+1}^*)/\omega_N.\tag{EP.10}
\]
The relative Hermitian defect
$\mathcal H_N=G_N^{-1/2}(A^*G_N+G_NA-2cG_N)G_N^{-1/2}$
has rank at most two and trace zero. For
$x=G_N^{1/2}b_N$, $y=G_N^{1/2}b_{N+1}$ its matrix is
$(xy^*+yx^*)/\omega_N$. Expanding its square gives trace
$2(\|x\|^2\|y\|^2-|x^*y|^2)/\omega_N^2$ because
$x^*y=i\omega_N\phi_N$. Its nonzero eigenvalues, if present,
are $\epsilon_N,-\epsilon_N$ with $\epsilon_N>0$; at rank zero
define $\epsilon_N=0$.
Consequently (EP.6) proves the full identity
\[
 \boxed{\epsilon_N^2+\phi_N^2=
 \frac{\omega_{N+1}}{\omega_N}(1-\delta_N)
                    (\delta_{N+1}^{-1}-1).}\tag{EP.11}
\]
For $q=1$ the trace-zero defect is the zero map; the identity retains
its possibly nonzero phase. We never form $V_{q-2}$.

\section{Window product, endpoint extremum, and phases}
Fix $n\ge q$, $r\ge1$, $m=n+r$, and put
\[
 d_j=\delta_{n+j}\ (0\le j\le r),\quad
 U=\omega_m/\omega_n,\quad \mathcal A=U^{1/(2r)},\quad
 E_* =\min_{0\le j<r}\epsilon_{n+j}.
\tag{EP.12}
\]
Multiplying every term of (EP.11), including both endpoint terms,
gives the exact overlapping product
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal K:=\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}(\epsilon_{n+j}^2+\phi_{n+j}^2)
 =U\frac{V_n}{V_m}(1-d_0)(1-d_r)
                 \prod_{j=1}^{r-1}(1-d_j)^2.}\tag{EP.13}
\]
Indeed the norm ratios telescope to $U$ and
$\prod_{j=1}^r d_j=V_m/V_n$. Each interior numerator occurs twice.
As $E_*^{2r}\le\prod\epsilon_{n+j}^2\le\mathcal K$ and
$0\le1-d_j<1$, this proves
\[
 E_*\le\left(UV_n/V_m\right)^{1/(2r)}.\tag{EP.14}
\]

For $r\ge2$ write $s=r-1$ and retain all four endpoint ratios
\[
 \alpha=d_0=V_n/V_{n-1},\quad
 \beta=d_r=V_m/V_{m-1},\quad t=\prod_{j=1}^s d_j=V_{m-1}/V_n.
\tag{EP.15}
\]
For $0<t<1$, set $v_j=-\log d_j$. The function
$f(v)=\log(1-e^{-v})$ has
$f''(v)=-e^{-v}/(1-e^{-v})^2<0$ on $v>0$.
The tangent-line inequality
$f(v)\le f(\bar v)+f'(\bar v)(v-\bar v)$ follows by integrating
the decreasing derivative on either side of $\bar v$. Summing at
$\bar v=(-\log t)/s$ and exponentiating proves
\[
 \prod_{j=1}^s(1-d_j)\le(1-t^{1/s})^s.\tag{EP.16}
\]
If one $d_j=1$, the left side is zero; if $t=1$, all are one and
both sides are zero. For $t<1$ with all $d_j<1$, strict concavity
shows equality exactly when all $d_j=t^{1/s}$.
Insert this result in (EP.13) to obtain
\[
 \boxed{E_*\le\mathcal U:=\mathcal A
 \left(\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)}{\beta t}\right)^{1/(2r)}
 (1-t^{1/(r-1)})^{(r-1)/r}.}\tag{EP.17}
\]
At $r=1$ the separate, zero-safe formula is
$E_*\le\mathcal A\sqrt{(1-\alpha)(\beta^{-1}-1)}$.
The four endpoint slots then overlap and no $t^{1/0}$ is defined.

This bound is attained in the relaxed scalar data class: choose
the equal interior contractions and, when $\alpha,\beta,t<1$, put
$f_j=(1-d_j)(d_{j+1}^{-1}-1)>0$ and
\[
 \omega_{n+j+1}/\omega_{n+j}=\mathcal U^2/f_j,
 \qquad\epsilon_{n+j}=\mathcal U,\qquad\phi_{n+j}=0.
\tag{EP.18}
\]
Their product is $U$ by (EP.17), so both norm endpoints are respected
and every radius identity holds. If an endpoint contraction is one,
or $t=1$, every compatible sequence has a zero radius at some step,
hence $E_*=0$ and the zero bound is attained. At $r=1$ choose the
sole norm ratio $U$. These scalar data are related to the arithmetic
objects by the evaluation map (EP.4)--(EP.12); the proof of scalar
attainment does not assert that this evaluation map is surjective.

For strictly interior contractions define the exact loss
\[
 \mathcal J=s\log(1-t^{1/s})-\sum_{j=1}^s\log(1-d_j)\ge0.
 \qquad \mathcal K=\mathcal U^{2r}e^{-2\mathcal J}.
\tag{EP.19}
\]
It also has the proved quantitative lower bound
\[
 \mathcal J\ge\frac{t}{2(1-t)^2}
 \sum_{j=1}^s\left(v_j+\frac{\log t}{s}\right)^2.
\tag{EP.20}
\]
To prove this, all $v_j$ and their mean lie in $(0,-\log t]$,
where $-f''(v)\ge t/(1-t)^2$. Integrating the derivative twice
gives the tangent inequality with the displayed quadratic remainder.
The linear terms sum to zero. This proves (EP.20), including $s=1$,
where both sides vanish. If an interior $d_j=1$ the exact product
(EP.13) applies with value zero; no logarithm of zero is evaluated.

Define the actual phase polynomial
\[
 P(y)=\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}(y+\phi_{n+j}^2),\quad y\ge0.
 \qquad P(E_*^2)\le\mathcal K.\tag{EP.21}
\]
It is continuous, tends to infinity, and is strictly increasing on
$[0,\infty)$: for $y_2>y_1\ge0$ every factor increases, and if a
factor vanishes at $y_1$, the product at $y_2$ is strictly positive.
The actual product guarantees $\mathcal K\ge P(0)$. There is
therefore exactly one $y_*\ge0$ with $P(y_*)=\mathcal K$, and
$E_*\le\sqrt{y_*}$. If $\mathcal K=P(0)$, then $y_*=0$.
If an externally supplied number were smaller than $P(0)$, it would
be incompatible with the displayed radius data; it would not define
a nonnegative threshold. In particular at $\mathcal K=0$ at least
one phase vanishes and $y_*=0$. Positive rational enclosures for
the phases' squares and $\mathcal K$ lead to polynomial evaluation
and rational bisection; positive powers and products suffice for each
comparison. Using $\mathcal U^{2r}$ in place of $\mathcal K$
gives the valid endpoint-only version, also with its phase retained.

\section{The sharp scalar bound with only the combined budget}
Put
\[
 \mathcal B=\log\frac{V_{n-1}V_n}{V_{m-1}V_m}
 =-\log\left(d_0d_r\prod_{j=1}^{r-1}d_j^2\right)\ge0.
\tag{EP.22}
\]
The product in (EP.13), divided by $U$, is the function
\[
 F(d)=\frac{(1-d_0)(1-d_r)\prod_{j=1}^{r-1}(1-d_j)^2}
 {d_r\prod_{j=1}^{r-1}d_j}.
\tag{EP.23}
\]
For $\mathcal B>0$ its unique maximum over these scalar contractions
with this budget is attained at
\[
 d_0=\frac1{1+2x},\qquad d_r=1-2x,\qquad
 d_j=\frac{1-x}{1+x}\ (1\le j<r),\tag{EP.24}
\]
where $x\in(0,1/2)$ is the unique solution of
\[
 \mathcal B=2\atanh(2x)+4(r-1)\atanh x.
\tag{EP.25}
\]
Here $\atanh x=\tfrac12\log((1+x)/(1-x))$.
The exact maximum and consequent bound are
\[
 F_{\max}=\frac{4^r x^{2r}}{(1-4x^2)(1-x^2)^{r-1}},\qquad
 \boxed{E_*\le\mathcal U\le
 \mathcal A\frac{2x}{[(1-4x^2)(1-x^2)^{r-1}]^{1/(2r)}}.}
\tag{EP.26}
\]
The middle expression for $r=1$ uses the separate expression after
(EP.17).

Here is a full extremization proof. Write $v_j=-\log d_j$.
The feasible simplex is $v_0+v_r+2\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}v_j=\mathcal B$,
$v_j\ge0$, a compact set. The product vanishes at every boundary
point and is positive at interior points, so a maximum is interior.
Its logarithm is
\[
 f(v_0)+f(v_r)+2\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}f(v_j)
                 +v_r+\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}v_j.
\tag{EP.27}
\]
Its Hessian is strictly negative diagonal, so it is strictly concave.
The Lagrange multiplier equations are
\[
 \frac1{e^{v_0}-1}=\lambda,\quad
 1+\frac1{e^{v_r}-1}=\lambda,\quad
 1+\frac2{e^{v_j}-1}=2\lambda\quad(1\le j<r).
\tag{EP.28}
\]
They imply $\lambda>1$ and yield (EP.24) with $x=1/(2\lambda)$.
The right side of (EP.25) is strictly increasing from zero to
infinity because its derivative is
$4/(1-4x^2)+4(r-1)/(1-x^2)>0$.
Thus it has exactly one solution and determines the unique maximum.
Substitution into (EP.23) gives (EP.26). The value $\mathcal B=0$
forces all $d_j=1$, and all bounds are zero; it is represented by
the continuous endpoint $x=0$. The norm-ratio construction (EP.18),
now using these contractions and $\mathcal A F_{\max}^{1/(2r)}$,
proves sharpness in the same scalar class for this reduced data.

We prove the comparison with the earlier hyperbolic-sine bound directly.
For $a,b\in(0,1]$ and $z=-\tfrac12\log(ab)$,
\[
 \sinh^2z-(1-a)(b^{-1}-1)
 =\frac{(1-2a+ab)^2}{4ab}\ge0.\tag{EP.29}
\]
Expansion after multiplying by $4ab$ proves the equality term by
term. Also $(\log\sinh z)''=-1/\sinh^2z<0$ for $z>0$,
so the same tangent-line proof used for (EP.16) shows
$\prod\sinh z_j\le\sinh((\sum z_j)/r)^r$.
If any $z_j=0$, the product is zero and the inequality is immediate.
Taking $a=d_j,b=d_{j+1}$ gives $\sum z_j=\mathcal B/2$,
and hence
\[
 \mathcal A F_{\max}^{1/(2r)}
 \le\mathcal A\sinh(\mathcal B/(2r)).\tag{EP.30}
\]
For $r>1$, $\mathcal B>0$ the inequality is strict. At a maximizer
every $z_j>0$. Equality in the concavity step requires all $z_j=z>0$,
so $d_jd_{j+1}=e^{-2z}=:p<1$. Equality in (EP.29) requires
$d_j=(1+p)/2$ for every $0\le j<r$. For $r>1$ this implies
$d_0=d_1$, whence $p=((1+p)/2)^2$ and $(1-p)^2=0$, a contradiction.
For $r=1$, (EP.25)--(EP.26) give exactly $\sinh(\mathcal B/2)$.
For geometric contractions $d_j=e^{-v}$ the fixed-endpoint bound
is $2\mathcal A\sinh(v/2)$ and the earlier bound is
$\mathcal A\sinh v$; their ratio is exactly $\cosh(v/2)$.

The optimizer can be certified without a transcendental equation.
The real endpoint datum $R=e^{\mathcal B}\ge1$ gives
\[
 (1+2x)(1+x)^{2(r-1)}
       =R(1-2x)(1-x)^{2(r-1)}.\tag{EP.31}
\]
The ratio of the two positive sides before subtraction is strictly
increasing on $[0,1/2)$ by (EP.25), so rational bisection isolates
its unique root using integer products when $R$ is rational. For a
general actual endpoint, certified rational bounds
$1\le R_-\le R\le R_+$ give enclosing roots
$x(R_-)\le x(R)\le x(R_+)$ by the same monotonicity. Positive-power bisection
then encloses (EP.26). This specifies the computational map exactly.

\section{Schur windows and the reduced source--relation determinant}
Split the source monomials of $\mathcal P_m$ at degree $n-1$ and set
\[
 H_m=\begin{pmatrix}H_{n-1}&C_D\\C_D^*&D_D\end{pmatrix},\quad
 W=D_D-C_D^*H_{n-1}^{-1}C_D,\quad
 F=J_D-J_{n-1}H_{n-1}^{-1}C_D.\tag{EP.32}
\]
The triangular map $(x,y)\mapsto(x-H_{n-1}^{-1}C_Dy,y)$,
from $\C^n\oplus\C^{r+1}$ onto $\C^{m+1}$, has determinant one
and takes $H_m$ to $\diag(H_{n-1},W)$ and the remainder map to
$[J_{n-1},F]$. Consequently $W>0$. For its first $j$ columns put
$W_j=W[1:j,1:j]$, $F_j=F[:,1:j]$, $Z_j=F_j^*G_{n-1}F_j$.
Block inversion and the determinant identity from (EP.6) prove
\[
 K_{n+j-1}=K_{n-1}+F_jW_j^{-1}F_j^*,\qquad
 R_j:=V_{n-1}/V_{n+j-1}
 =\frac{\det(W_j+Z_j)}{\det W_j},\quad R_0=1.
\tag{EP.33}
\]
This $R_j$ is a scalar and is distinct from the typed least-lift
$R_N$ by its explicit definition; the scalar is obtained from that
least-lift Gram by determinant. Write
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 w=(W_1)_{11},\quad p=\det W_r,\quad s_+=\det W_{r+1},\quad
 e=w+(Z_1)_{11},\\
 H_+=\det(W_{r+1}+Z_{r+1}),\qquad H_- =\det(W_r+Z_r).
 \end{gathered}\tag{EP.34}
\]
All six quantities are positive. Successive monic Schur complements
give $w=\omega_n$ and $\omega_m=s_+/p$. Thus the exact bridge
from the original EW matrices to the new endpoint quantities is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 U&=s_+/(pw),&\alpha&=w/e,&
 \beta&=H_-s_+/(pH_+),&t&=ep/(wH_-),\\
 U\frac{V_n}{V_m}&=\frac{H_+}{pe},&
 \mathcal B&=\log\frac{H_-H_+w}{ps_+e}.
 \end{aligned}\tag{EP.35}
\]
For example $\alpha=1/R_1$, $\beta=R_r/R_{r+1}$ and
$t=R_1/R_r$, and substitution gives each displayed equality.
Every individual contraction can also be recovered as
$d_j=R_j/R_{j+1}$ for $0\le j\le r$, so the exact product,
the loss $\mathcal J$ and every comparison use the same block.
For $r=1$, $p=w$, $H_-=e$, $t=1$, and the separate single-step
formula applies. At $n=q$ the base relation space is empty and
$G_{q-1}=H_{q-1}$; no inverse of an empty deficient kernel occurs.

To prove the determinant cancellation independently, define
\[
 \mathfrak D_j=\det H_{j-1},\quad\mathfrak D_0=1,\quad
 \mathfrak B_l=\det(B_{q+l-1}^*H_{q+l-1}B_{q+l-1}),
 \quad\mathfrak B_0=1.\tag{EP.36}
\]
The coefficient matrix with first columns the $q$ quotient monomials
and last columns $\chi,S\chi,\ldots,S^{N-q}\chi$ is triangular
with diagonal one. Subtracting boundary columns to replace the first
ones by $R_N$ keeps its determinant one and makes the Gram block
diagonal. Hence
\[
 \omega_N=\mathfrak D_{N+1}/\mathfrak D_N,\quad
 V_N=\mathfrak D_{N+1}/\mathfrak B_{N-q+1},\quad
 \Lambda_N:=\omega_N/V_N=\mathfrak B_{N-q+1}/\mathfrak D_N.
\tag{EP.37}
\]
The norm identity uses the analogous monic orthogonal triangular
basis. Equations (EP.14), (EP.35), and (EP.37) now prove
\[
 \boxed{E_*\le\left(\frac{\Lambda_m}{\Lambda_n}\right)^{1/(2r)}
 =\left(\frac{\mathfrak B_{m-q+1}\mathfrak D_n}
 {\mathfrak D_m\mathfrak B_{n-q+1}}\right)^{1/(2r)}
 =\left(\frac{H_+}{pe}\right)^{1/(2r)}.}\tag{EP.38}
\]
For $N\ge q$, multiplication by $\chi$ in (EP.3) also sends the original
leading-coefficient line at degree $N-q$ isomorphically to the
leading-coefficient line of its relation at degree $N$. Its leading
coefficient is one; the source norm of that relation is exactly the
form defining $\mathfrak B$. The quotient $J_N$ sends the same
relation to zero. These give the maps relating the determinant and
quotient observations.

For comparison only, multiplying the original source measure by a
positive scalar $a$ sends $H_N\mapsto aH_N$, $G_N\mapsto aG_N$,
$\omega_N\mapsto a\omega_N$, $V_N\mapsto a^qV_N$, and
$\Lambda_N\mapsto a^{1-q}\Lambda_N$. This follows directly from
(EP.4) and (EP.37). All window ratios displayed above are unchanged;
the actual measure throughout remains (EP.1).

\section{Full confluent transfer and its two-determinant window}
We now construct the transfer determinants in (EP.38) from their full
raw derivative maps. Retain $\psi=\widehat\chi=i^{-q}\chi(c+iu)$
and $\Pi=\psi^2$ under the reflection hypothesis of (EP.7). If
$\chi$ has distinct roots $\lambda_a$ of complete lengths $\ell_a$,
let $\zeta_a=(\lambda_a-c)/i$ and $r_a=2\ell_a$.
The width is $d=\sum_a r_a=2q$. The map
\[
 \mathcal J_2 f=(f^{(e)}(\zeta_a))_{a,\,0\le e<r_a}:
 \C[u]\longrightarrow\bigoplus_a\C^{r_a}\tag{EP.38a}
\]
uses the fixed order of nodes and then increasing raw derivative
orders. Its kernel is $(\Pi)$ by Taylor divisibility and the
coprimality of distinct linear factors. In these coordinates a product
uses $\sum_{b=0}^e\binom eb f^{(b)}g^{(e-b)}$, and multiplication
by $u$ has the block formula
$(X_\Pi v)_{a,e}=\zeta_av_{a,e}+e v_{a,e-1}$.
The original coordinate map has the exact raw-jet expression
\[
 (\partial_u^e\Phi P)(\zeta_a)=i^eP^{(e)}(\lambda_a),\qquad
 \C[S]/(\chi^2)\xrightarrow{\sim}\C[u]/(\Pi),\quad
 [P]\mapsto[P(c+iu)],\tag{EP.38b}
\]
since $\Phi(\chi^2)=i^{2q}\Pi$. Every $e!$ and $i^e$ is retained.

Let $v_j=\mathcal J_2Q_j$ for the same source polynomials used in
(EP.8), and form
\[
 \mathsf F_a=[v_a,\ldots,v_{a+d-1}],\qquad
 \mathsf V=[\mathcal J_2 1,\ldots,\mathcal J_2u^{d-1}].
\tag{EP.38c}
\]
Their full raw Vandermonde determinant is
\[
 \det\mathsf V=
 \left(\prod_a\prod_{e=0}^{r_a-1}e!\right)
 \prod_{a<b}(\zeta_b-\zeta_a)^{r_ar_b}\ne0.\tag{EP.38d}
\]
