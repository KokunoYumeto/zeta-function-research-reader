\section{Residue endomorphisms, metric variation, and the forced period Laplacian}
\label{sec:residue-constituent-extension}

The delivered residue--constituent note proves (RC1)--(RC53).
Its transverse-rank and period-curvature calculation is the same exact
calculation as (SC9)--(SC25), and its comparison with the original positive
form is already (LC13)--(LC15). We retain these shared results and prove
the additional endomorphism, variation, differential, and translation
maps below. The complete preceding determinant and constituent proofs
are included in the standalone edition. No original arithmetic source
norm, coefficient, multiplicity, or contour factor is changed.

\subsection{A universal inverse to the residue endomorphism basis}

Let \(B\) be an arbitrary commutative ring with identity. Fix the monic
polynomial and its original power-basis quotient
\[
 \chi(S)=S^q+\sum_{a=0}^{q-1}c_aS^a\in B[S],\quad q\ge1,\qquad
 E_B=B[S]/(\chi),\quad e_i=[S^i]\ (0\le i<q).
 \tag{RCX1}
\]
Polynomial division by the monic polynomial proves that these \(e_i\)
are a basis: subtract leading multiples until the degree is less than
\(q\); a nonzero multiple of a monic degree-\(q\) polynomial cannot
have degree less than \(q\), even when \(B\) has zero divisors.
Let \(A=M_S\), \(e=e_0\), and let
\(\ell:E_B\to B\) be the coefficient of \(S^{q-1}\) in this remainder.
Set
\[
 R=e\ell,\qquad
 \mathcal B_{ij}=\ell(e_ie_j),\qquad 0\le i,j<q.
 \tag{RCX2}
\]
The matrix \(\mathcal B\) is symmetric. Its entries with \(i+j<q-1\)
are zero, and its antidiagonal entries are one. Reversing its columns
therefore gives a triangular matrix with diagonal entries one. The
column-reversal permutation has \(q(q-1)/2\) inversions, so
\[
 \det\mathcal B=(-1)^{q(q-1)/2},\qquad
 \mathcal B^{-1}=(-1)^{q(q-1)/2}\operatorname{adj}(\mathcal B).
 \tag{RCX3}
\]
In particular, this inverse uses no discriminant, root separation, or
division by a coefficient. The pairing
\(\beta(x,y)=\ell(xy)\) induces an isomorphism
\(E_B\to\operatorname{Hom}_B(E_B,B)\) with matrix \(\mathcal B\).

For a matrix \(T\) in the original power basis, put
\(K(T)=T\mathcal B^{-1}\). The complete reconstruction is
\[
 \boxed{
 T=\sum_{i,j=0}^{q-1}K(T)_{ij}A^iRA^j,\qquad
 K(T)=T\mathcal B^{-1}.}
 \tag{RCX4}
\]
Indeed \(A^ie=e_i\), and the row \(\ell A^j\) has entries
\(\ell(e_je_b)=\mathcal B_{jb}\). Thus the matrix of the displayed
sum is \(K(T)\mathcal B=T\). Conversely, a zero sum has
\(K\mathcal B=0\), so \(K=0\). This proves, with explicit inverse,
the isomorphism of free \(B\)-modules
\[
 B^{q\times q}\longrightarrow\operatorname{End}_B(E_B),
 \qquad (K_{ij})\longmapsto\sum_{i,j}K_{ij}A^iRA^j.
 \tag{RCX5}
\]
Its image is contained in the unital \(B\)-algebra generated by \(A,R\);
hence that algebra is all of \(\operatorname{End}_B(E_B)\).
This includes \(q=1\), where \(R=I\). For a ring map \(B\to B'\),
monic division, \(\mathcal B\), its displayed inverse, and (RCX4)
all map to their corresponding formulas over \(B'\). Thus this is
a universal coefficient and base-change identity, including every
nonreduced fibre.

The residue dual rows give literal matrix units:
\[
 d^j=\sum_{b=0}^{q-1}(\mathcal B^{-1})_{jb}\ell A^b,\qquad
 U_{ij}=e_i d^j
       =\sum_b(\mathcal B^{-1})_{jb}A^iRA^b.
 \tag{RCX6}
\]
The equality \(d^j(e_k)=\delta_{jk}\) follows by multiplication of
\(\mathcal B^{-1}\mathcal B\). Consequently
\(U_{ij}U_{kl}=\delta_{jk}U_{il}\) and
\(\sum_iU_{ii}=I\). These equations specify the multiplication law,
not merely a dimension count.

For the original complex arithmetic source, retain exactly
\[
 j_Er_N=I,\quad j_ED^{(k)}=Aj_E,\quad j_Ed=0,\quad
 G_N=r_N^*r_N>0.
 \tag{RCX7}
\]
The maps have domains \(r_N:E\to\mathscr X\) and
\(j_E:\mathscr X\to E\) on the admitted strong-Schwartz source
\(\mathscr X\); \(d\) is its original boundary map. Every
\(T\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(E)\) has the canonical finite-rank
source lift
\[
 \widehat T=r_NTj_E,\quad
 j_E\widehat T=Tj_E,\quad
 \widehat T r_N=r_NT,\quad
 \widehat T d=0,\quad
 \widehat T\widehat U=\widehat{TU}.
 \tag{RCX8}
\]
Each identity follows by inserting \(j_Er_N=I\) or \(j_Ed=0\).
In particular \(\widehat I=r_Nj_E\), an idempotent on the full
source with image \(r_NE\). Thus the lift is a unital representation
in the corner with identity \(r_Nj_E\), and is injective because
\(j_E\widehat T r_N=T\). No assertion that \(j_E\) is bounded on
an unweighted completion is required or made. Taking \(T=U_{ij}\)
in (RCX8) gives every matrix unit at the original source, with the
same boundary kernel. Taking \(T=R\) gives the residue perturbation
\(r_NRj_E\) already used in (SC7)--(SC8).

\subsection{The original quotient-unit and residue metric product}

Now \(B=\mathbb C\), and \(I:F\hookrightarrow E\) is a fixed
nonzero proper \(A\)-invariant inclusion of dimension \(p\). Keep the
original unit \(e\) and row \(L=\ell I\). Both \(e\notin I(F)\)
and \(L\ne0\) follow directly from (RCX3): if \(e\in I(F)\), all
\(A^ie\) belong to \(I(F)\); if \(L=0\), invariance gives
\(\ell A^j I=0\) for all \(j\), and the invertible residue matrix
forces \(I=0\).

For every positive Hermitian form \(M\) on the original \(E\), define
\[
\begin{gathered}
 M_F=I^*MI,\quad P_M=I M_F^{-1}I^*M,\quad
 r_M=(1-P_M)e,\quad w_M=I M_F^{-1}L^*,\\
 q_M=r_M^*Mr_M,\qquad d_M=L M_F^{-1}L^*,\qquad
 \mathfrak c_F(M)=q_Md_M .
\end{gathered}\tag{RCX9}
\]
The equations \(P_M^2=P_M\), \(P_M^*M=MP_M\), and
\(\operatorname{im}P_M=I(F)\) follow by multiplication.
Thus \(I^*Mr_M=0\). Completing the square gives
\[
 q_M=\min_{v\in F}(e-Iv)^*M(e-Iv),\qquad
 d_M=w_M^*Mw_M
     =\sup_{v\ne0}\frac{|Lv|^2}{v^*M_Fv}.
 \tag{RCX10}
\]
For the supremum, write \(x=M_F^{-1}L^*\); then
\(Lv=x^*M_Fv\), and Cauchy--Schwarz in \(M_F\) proves the upper
bound \(x^*M_Fx=d_M\), attained at \(v=x\ne0\).
The minimum is attained at \(v=M_F^{-1}I^*Me\), with residual
\(r_M\ne0\). Consequently \(q_M,d_M>0\), and
\(r_M^*Mw_M=0\).
At \(M=G_N\), (RCX10) has exactly the original source expression
\[
 \mathfrak c_F(G_N)=
 \left(\min_{v\in F}\|r_N(e-Iv)\|^2\right)
 \left(\sup_{v\ne0}\frac{|Lv|^2}{\|r_NIv\|^2}\right).
 \tag{RCX11}
\]
In particular every original arithmetic Taylor unit and source mass
encoded in \(r_N\) remains in the two displayed factors.

For any invertible coefficient map \(C:E'\to E\), retain the transported
data \(I'=C^{-1}I\), \(e'=C^{-1}e\),
\(\ell'=\ell C\), and \(M'=C^*MC\). Direct substitution gives
\[
 M'_F=M_F,\quad P_{M'}=C^{-1}P_MC,\quad
 r_{M'}=C^{-1}r_M,\quad w_{M'}=C^{-1}w_M,\quad
 \mathfrak c_{F'}(M')=\mathfrak c_F(M).
 \tag{RCX12}
\]
For a positive real scalar \(s\), the formulas separately give
\(q_{sM}=s q_M\), \(d_{sM}=s^{-1}d_M\), and
\(\mathfrak c_F(sM)=\mathfrak c_F(M)\). These are proved
homogeneity identities with both factors retained; no form is replaced
by a rescaled form in the arithmetic construction.

\subsection{An exact rank-two differential for metric variation}

Let \(M(s)>0\) be a \(C^1\) path on a closed real interval; a dot denotes
its actual derivative. All inverses in (RCX9) are \(C^1\).
Differentiating \(q=r^*Mr\), the two terms with \(\dot r\) vanish:
\(\dot r\in I(F)\), whereas \(r\) is \(M\)-orthogonal to \(I(F)\).
Differentiating \(M_F^{-1}\) gives
\((M_F^{-1})^{\displaystyle\cdot}=-M_F^{-1}\dot M_F M_F^{-1}\).
Hence
\[
 \dot q=r^*\dot M r,\qquad
 \dot d=-w^*\dot M w,\qquad
 \boxed{\frac{d}{ds}\log\mathfrak c_F(M(s))
       =\frac{r^*\dot M r}{q}-\frac{w^*\dot M w}{d}.}
 \tag{RCX13}
\]
Define endomorphisms on the original \(E\), with its original form,
\[
 Q_r=\frac{rr^*M}{q},\quad Q_w=\frac{ww^*M}{d},\quad
 Z_M=Q_r-Q_w,\quad X=M^{-1}\dot M.
 \tag{RCX14}
\]
Each \(Q\) is its indicated rank-one \(M\)-orthogonal projection.
Their products in both orders vanish because \(r^*Mw=0\).
It follows that \(Z_M\) has eigenvalues \(1,-1\), and zero on the
remaining \(q-2\) dimensions. The complete differential is
\[
 \boxed{\frac{d}{ds}\log\mathfrak c_F(M(s))
            =\operatorname{Tr}(Z_M X).}
 \tag{RCX15}
\]
Indeed the trace of a column times a row is their row-column
contraction, giving exactly (RCX13). This also proves that scalar
metric directions cancel: \(X=\alpha I\) gives
\(\operatorname{Tr}Z_M=0\), while the original scalar \(\alpha\)
and both rank-one terms remain specified.

The operator \(X\) is self-adjoint in \(M\), since \(MX=\dot M\)
is Hermitian. Let \(\lambda_-(s)\) and \(\lambda_+(s)\) be its least
and greatest real eigenvalues. Each quotient in (RCX13) is a Rayleigh
quotient of this same \(X\), so
\[
 \left|\frac{d}{ds}\log\mathfrak c_F(M(s))\right|
       \le\lambda_+(s)-\lambda_-(s).
 \tag{RCX16}
\]
The positive equality holds exactly when \(r\) lies in the greatest
eigenspace and \(w\) in the least; negative equality reverses them.
To verify the equality statement, diagonalize the \(M\)-self-adjoint
operator: a Rayleigh quotient is a weighted mean of its eigenvalues,
with nonnegative weights and positive total weight, and attains an
extreme precisely on the corresponding eigenspace.
Integrating the actual continuous differential yields
\[
 \log\frac{\mathfrak c_F(M(1))}{\mathfrak c_F(M(0))}
   =\int_0^1\operatorname{Tr}(Z_{M(s)}M(s)^{-1}\dot M(s))\,ds .
 \tag{RCX17}
\]
This gives an exact observation on the metric path as well as the
bound obtained by integrating (RCX16).

For the nested original forms \(G_i\succeq G_j>0\), put
\[
 T=G_i^{-1}G_j,\quad
 0<\theta=\lambda_{\min}(T)\le
 \eta=\lambda_{\max}(T)\le1,\qquad
 M(s)=G_i+s(G_j-G_i).
 \tag{RCX18}
\]
No diagonalization of an arithmetic source is performed here:
the spectral theorem is used to evaluate the indicated relative
operator. Algebraic multiplication gives
\[
 M(s)=G_i(I+s(T-I)),\qquad
 M(s)^{-1}\dot M(s)=(I+s(T-I))^{-1}(T-I).
 \tag{RCX19}
\]
Thus its eigenvalues are
\((\lambda-1)/(1+s(\lambda-1))\). The derivative of this expression
in \(\lambda\) is \(1/(1+s(\lambda-1))^2>0\).
The spread therefore integrates exactly to
\[
 \int_0^1(\lambda_+(s)-\lambda_-(s))\,ds
   =\log\eta-\log\theta.
 \tag{RCX20}
\]
Combining (RCX16)--(RCX20), and keeping \(V_n=\det G_n\), proves
the sharpened endpoint estimate
\[
 \boxed{
 \left|\log\frac{\mathfrak c_F(G_j)}{\mathfrak c_F(G_i)}\right|
 \le\log\frac{\eta}{\theta}
 \le-\log\theta
 \le\log\frac{V_i}{V_j}.}
 \tag{RCX21}
\]
For the last inequality, all \(q\) eigenvalues of \(T\) lie in
\((0,1]\) and one is \(\theta\), so
\(\det T\le\theta\). Taking negative logarithms and
\(\det T=V_j/V_i\) proves it. This improves (RC36) by retaining
the greatest eigenvalue \(\eta\) as well as the least. It assigns
no unproved uniform bound to these actual finite quantities.

\subsection{All original volume factors in the coupling}

The ideal classification gives \(I(F)=(g)/(\chi)\), with \(g\) monic,
\(\chi=gh\), and \(\deg h=p\). Indeed \(A\)-invariance is invariance
under every polynomial in \(A\), so \(I(F)\) is an ideal; its preimage
in \(\mathbb C[S]\) is generated by a monic divisor \(g\) of \(\chi\).
The map \([a]_h\mapsto[ga]_\chi\) is bijective onto this ideal:
surjectivity is its definition and injectivity follows from
\(gh\mid ga\Rightarrow h\mid a\). Keep its columns and frame map
\[
 J_g=[g,Sg,\ldots,S^{p-1}g]=IC,\quad
 J_-=[g,\ldots,S^{p-2}g],\quad J_+=[J_g,e],\quad
 C=(I^*I)^{-1}I^*J_g.
 \tag{RCX22}
\]
Here \(C\) is invertible, \(LC=e_{p-1}^T\), and the empty
determinant for \(p=1\) is one. The last equality follows because
\(S^{p-1}g\) is monic of degree \(q-1\), while the preceding
columns have smaller degree. All displayed columns are independent,
including \(J_+\) because \(e\notin I(F)\).
For \(v_J(M)=\det(J^*MJ)\), the Schur complement for \(J_+\)
and the last diagonal inverse cofactor for \(J_g^*MJ_g\) give
\[
 q_M=\frac{v_{J_+}(M)}{v_{J_g}(M)},\qquad
 d_M=\frac{v_{J_-}(M)}{v_{J_g}(M)},\qquad
 \mathfrak c_F(M)=
       \frac{v_{J_+}(M)v_{J_-}(M)}{v_{J_g}(M)^2}.
 \tag{RCX23}
\]
For the first identity, the Schur complement is
\(e^*Me-e^*MJ_g(J_g^*MJ_g)^{-1}J_g^*Me=q_M\).
For the second, use \(LC=e_{p-1}^T\) and
\(J_g^*MJ_g=C^*M_FC\); the inverse last cofactor is precisely
the determinant of the first \(p-1\) Gram columns divided by the
full determinant. These proofs retain the frame determinant
\(\det(J_g^*MJ_g)=|\det C|^2\det M_F\).

Write \(v_{n,J}=v_J(G_n)\). The exact endpoint observation is
\[
\begin{split}
 \log\frac{\mathfrak c_F(G_j)}{\mathfrak c_F(G_i)}
  ={}&\log\frac{v_{j,J_+}}{v_{i,J_+}}
     +\log\frac{v_{j,J_-}}{v_{i,J_-}}
     -2\log\frac{v_{j,J_g}}{v_{i,J_g}} .
\end{split}\tag{RCX24}
\]
This supplies the original Toda-volume expression for the
rank-two differential (RCX15). It follows by taking logarithms of
the three positive factors in (RCX23); none is discarded.
In particular a scalar form change by \(s\) contributes
\((p+1)\log s+(p-1)\log s-2p\log s=0\) to (RCX24),
exactly matching (RCX12), with every dimension retained.

\subsection{The mixed fourth jet and the real-axis derivatives}

Fix the original \(u\in\mathbb C^\times\), the full period matrix
\(\Pi(t)\), and its equation \(\Pi'=-\Pi(A+tR)/u\).
The full determinant and contour convergence are proved in
(XD1)--(XD26) and (SC1)--(SC6b), included before this section.
Put \(Y=\Pi I\), \(H=\Pi^*\Pi\), \(H_F=Y^*Y\), and
\(\psi(t)=\log\det H_F(t)\). With
\(P=YH_F^{-1}Y^*\) and \(z=(1-P)\Pi e\), the exact equations are
\[
 (1-P)Y'=-\frac t u zL,\qquad
 \partial_t\partial_{\bar t}\psi
      =\frac{|t|^2}{|u|^2}\mathfrak c_F(H(t)).
 \tag{RCX25}
\]
For completeness, \(Y'=-YA_F/u-t\Pi eL/u\) gives the first.
Holomorphy gives \(\partial_tH_F=Y^*Y'\);
differentiate the inverse in
\(\partial_t\log\det H_F=\operatorname{Tr}(H_F^{-1}Y^*Y')\).
The result is
\(\operatorname{Tr}(H_F^{-1}Y'^*(1-P)Y')\).
Substitution of the first equation, (RCX10), and the period
quotient-norm minimum gives the second.

All entries are real analytic and \(H_F>0\), so \(\psi\) has a
convergent expansion in \(t,\bar t\) near zero. This can also
be seen by treating the two variables independently in
\(\det(I^*\Pi(\bar s)^*\Pi(t)I)\); its value at \((0,0)\)
is positive, so it has a holomorphic logarithm on a sufficiently
small polydisc. Equation (RCX25) then gives
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \psi_{t\bar t}(0)=\psi_{tt\bar t}(0)
  =\psi_{t\bar t\bar t}(0)=0,\qquad
 \psi_{ttt\bar t}(0)=\psi_{t\bar t\bar t\bar t}(0)=0,\\
 \boxed{\psi_{tt\bar t\bar t}(0)
       =\mathfrak c_F(H(0))/|u|^2>0,\qquad
 [t^2\bar t^2]\psi=\mathfrak c_F(H(0))/(4|u|^2).}
\end{gathered}\tag{RCX26}
\]
The first row is obtained by applying the indicated derivatives
to \(t\bar t\,\mathfrak c_F(H(t))/|u|^2\) and evaluating at zero.
The factorial in the second row is \(2!2!=4\).

Here is the exact map to real-axis derivatives, including the
terms a mixed derivative does not determine. Let
\[
 \phi(t)=\log\det\!\left(I^*\Pi(0)^*\Pi(t)I\right),
 \qquad \phi(0)=\log\det H_F(0)
 \tag{RCX27}
\]
on its local nonzero determinant neighbourhood. Its derivatives
of positive order are the pure \(t\)-derivatives of \(\psi\).
For \(x\in\mathbb R\), \(d/dx=\partial_t+\partial_{\bar t}\).
Reality of \(\psi\), the binomial theorem for commuting derivatives,
and (RCX26) prove
\[
\begin{split}
 \left.\frac{d^2}{dx^2}\psi(x)\right|_0
   &=2\operatorname{Re}\phi''(0),\\
 \left.\frac{d^4}{dx^4}\psi(x)\right|_0
   &=2\operatorname{Re}\phi^{(4)}(0)
       +6\,\frac{\mathfrak c_F(H(0))}{|u|^2}.
\end{split}\tag{RCX28}
\]
Thus the mixed observation and the real derivatives are related
by these exact equations, with the pure holomorphic jets retained.
In particular the first equation and
\(\Pi''(0)=\Pi(0)A^2/u^2-\Pi(0)R/u\) give
\[
 \phi'(0)=-\operatorname{Tr}A_F/u,\qquad
 \phi''(0)=-\frac1u L H_F(0)^{-1}I^*H(0)e .
 \tag{RCX29}
\]
To check the second identity, the second logarithmic derivative
is the trace of the second matrix derivative minus the square
of the first logarithmic matrix derivative. The \(A_F^2/u^2\)
terms cancel exactly, leaving the displayed rank-one trace.

\subsection{Every period derivative and the forced Laplacian}

All higher pure jets in (RCX28) are given by an explicit finite
noncommutative recursion in the original \(A,R,u\). Set
\[
 Q_0(t)=I,\qquad
 Q_{n+1}(t)=Q_n'(t)-\frac1u(A+tR)Q_n(t).
 \tag{RCX30}
\]
Induction using the product rule and the period equation proves
\(\Pi^{(n)}(t)=\Pi(t)Q_n(t)\) for every \(n\ge0\).
Every \(Q_n\) is a polynomial in \(t\) with coefficients in the
algebra over \(\mathbb C[u,u^{-1}]\) generated by \(A,R\).
In particular, with \(A_t=A+tR\),
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 Q_1=-A_t/u,\qquad Q_2=A_t^2/u^2-R/u,\\
 Q_3=-A_t^3/u^3+(RA_t+2A_tR)/u^2,\\
 Q_4=A_t^4/u^4
       -(RA_t^2+2A_tRA_t+3A_t^2R)/u^3+3R^2/u^2 .
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{RCX31}
\]
The order of \(RA_t\) and \(A_tR\) is retained: differentiating
\(A_t^2\) gives \(RA_t+A_tR\), and the product
\(-A_tQ_2/u\) contributes one additional \(A_tR/u^2\).
Differentiating \(Q_3\) gives
\(-(RA_t^2+A_tRA_t+A_t^2R)/u^3+3R^2/u^2\);
the additional \(-A_tQ_3/u\) contributes
\(A_t^4/u^4-(A_tRA_t+2A_t^2R)/u^3\).
Adding them proves the displayed \(Q_4\), including the \(3R^2\)
term. For \(q>1\), \(\ell e=0\) and \(R^2=0\); for \(q=1\),
\(R=I\) and that term remains \(3I/u^2\).

To evaluate the remaining fourth jet entirely from the displayed
data, put
\[
 D_n=H_F(0)^{-1}I^*H(0)Q_n(0)I\quad(1\le n\le4).
\]
Then
\[
 \phi^{(4)}(0)=
 \operatorname{Tr}\!\left(
 D_4-4D_1D_3-3D_2^2+12D_1^2D_2-6D_1^4\right).
 \tag{RCX32}
\]
Indeed the local matrix in (RCX27), after left multiplication by
\(H_F(0)^{-1}\), is
\(I+\sum_{n=1}^4D_nt^n/n!+O(t^5)\).
The convergent trace-log expansion
\(\operatorname{Tr}\log(I+Z)
=\sum_{m\ge1}(-1)^{m+1}\operatorname{Tr}(Z^m)/m\)
follows by differentiating both sides near zero and using their
common value zero at \(Z=0\).
Collecting the \(t^4\) coefficient gives respectively
\(D_4\), \(-4D_1D_3-3D_2^2\),
\(12D_1^2D_2\), and \(-6D_1^4\) after multiplication by \(4!\).
Cyclic trace, rather than a commutation assumption, combines the
three cubic terms. This proves (RCX32) and completely evaluates
the real fourth derivative in (RCX28).

For the original tensor degree \(k\ge1\), let \(L_t=A_t^2-kA_t\).
The following polynomial matrix identity also holds for every real
\(k\), as a separate algebraic parameter.
Equation (RCX31) proves the exact differential realization
\[
 \boxed{u^2\Pi''+ku\Pi'+u\Pi R=\Pi L_t.}
 \tag{RCX33}
\]
The added term is the same \(R=e\ell\) whose complete algebra
and normal action were computed above. On the admitted source
domain stable under \(D^{(k)}\) and its square, let
\(\widehat R=r_NRj_E\) and \(Y_x(t)=\Pi(t)j_Ex\) for a fixed
source vector \(x\). Applying (RCX33) at zero and twice using
\(j_ED^{(k)}=Aj_E\) gives
\[
 (u^2\partial_t^2+ku\partial_t)Y_x|_0
       +u\Pi(0)j_E\widehat R x
 =\Pi(0)j_E((D^{(k)})^2-kD^{(k)})x .
 \tag{RCX34}
\]
Every term has target \(\mathbb C^q\) with its specified period
coordinates; the source vector remains in the same admitted domain.

The primitive also has a complete formula. Let
\(\mathcal C_{\rm prim}\) be the original primitive source,
\(D_0:\mathcal C_{\rm prim}\to\mathcal C_{\rm prim}\) its Euler
action, and retain
\[
 D^{(k)}d=dD_0,\qquad
 D^{(k)}r_N-r_NA=dK,\qquad K:E\to\mathcal C_{\rm prim}.
\]
Put \(D_t=D^{(k)}+t\widehat R\). Equations (RCX8) give
\(D_td=dD_0\) and \(D_tr_N=r_NA_t+dK\).
Therefore direct expansion gives
\[
 \boxed{(D_t^2-kD_t)r_N-r_NL_t
       =d(D_0K+KA_t-kK).}
 \tag{RCX35}
\]
Indeed \(D_t(r_NA_t)=(r_NA_t+dK)A_t\) and
\(D_tdK=dD_0K\); subtract \(k(r_NA_t+dK)\).
This reproduces the complete primitive (LC12a) and attaches it
to the new forced period equation. In particular the term
\(tKR\) in \(KA_t\) is present with its original sign.

\subsection{Translation of the marked curvature and first derivative}

For a fixed complex \(a\), keep the actual substitution data
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \chi_a(S)=\chi(S-a),\quad
 \Phi_a(S)=\Phi(S-a)-\Phi(-a),\\
 (C_a)_{rb}=\binom br a^{b-r}\ (r\le b),\qquad
 f_a(t)=\exp((-\Phi(-a)-ta)/u).
\end{gathered}\tag{RCX36}
\]
The zero entries for \(r>b\) are retained. The literal substitution
\(P(S)\mapsto P(X+a)\) gives
\(C_a^{-1}=C_{-a}\), \(\det C_a=1\), \(C_ae=e\), and
\(\ell C_a=\ell\), because translation preserves the highest
coefficient in degree below \(q\). It follows in particular that
\(C_aRC_a^{-1}=R\). Also
\(C_aA_a(t)C_a^{-1}=A_t+aI\).

The original oriented exponential contour integral gives
\[
 \Pi_a(t)=f_a(t)\Pi(t)C_a.
 \tag{RCX37}
\]
