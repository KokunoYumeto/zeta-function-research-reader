\section{Finite arithmetic Gram transfer with the original minimum quotient metric}



This continuation proves a finite coefficient-to-metric map, its exact
boundary-resolvent remainder, and a two-degree determinant formula for
the consecutive volume ratio in the arithmetic Toda control. The original
Gamma mass, tensor sum, arithmetic amplitude, quotient coordinates and
relation polynomial remain in every formula. A bounded positive-weight
calibration at the end proves that the relative Gamma correction can
increase with degree even while the actual minimum volume decreases.
That calibration is explicitly an auxiliary measure, with no assertion
that its polynomial is an annihilator of actual zeta zeros.

The sources read for this calculation are the complete
\texttt{RESEARCH\_NOTE.md} and \texttt{NOTE.tex} in the delivered
Tau Gamma Convolution Descent, and the original arithmetic definitions and proofs
TVB.1--TVB.38 in \texttt{toda\_cv\_exact\_bridge\_20260912.tex}.
The finite transfer below is at the original observation $\theta=0$.
Its proof does not replace a nonzero exponential tilt by a polynomial
test. All interval claims here are finite rational inequalities; no
uniform estimate as packet, tensor degree or polynomial degree grow is
asserted.

\subsection{The original source, relation and coordinate maps}

Let $h(s)$ be a monic nonempty packet of actual zeros of
\[
 g(s)=s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)=2\xi(s),
\]
including the complete order at each selected zero. Put $v_h=g/h$,
$w_h(t)=|v_h(1/2+it)|^2/(2\pi)$, and $\mu_h=\int_\R w_h(t)\,dt$.
The quotient is entire: local Taylor division removes exactly the stated
vanishing order. Its value at each selected zero is nonzero. The full
Taylor remainder $[v_h]_h$ is therefore a unit of $E_h=\C[s]/(h)$.
Indeed in $\C[t]/(t^m)$ the inverse of $\sum_{j<m}a_jt^j$, $a_0\ne0$,
has coefficients $b_0=a_0^{-1}$ and
$b_n=-a_0^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^n a_jb_{n-j}$; the Chinese remainder map
from coprime factors is the exact algebra isomorphism obtained by
Bezout's identity. This retains every jet and multiplicity.

The original tensor coordinate is
\[
 k\ge1,\qquad c=k/2,\qquad S=s_1+\cdots+s_k=c+iu,
 \qquad u=t_1+\cdots+t_k.                                      \tag{GMT.1}
\]
The source observation is the conjugate-linear-first pairing
\[
 \langle P,Q\rangle_h=\int_\R
       \overline{P(c+iu)}Q(c+iu)m_{h,k}(u)\,du,
 \quad m_{h,k}=w_h^{*k},\quad\int m_{h,k}=\mu_h^k.                \tag{GMT.2}
\]
The change $(t_1,\ldots,t_k)\mapsto
(t_1,\ldots,t_{k-1},u)$ has determinant one. Thus Fubini gives (GMT.2)
from the literal tensor Mellin observation without a divisor or an
average coordinate. The complex Mellin amplitude remains
\[
 \prod_{i=1}^k\frac{v_h(1/2+it_i)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,
 P(c+i\textstyle\sum_i t_i).
\]

Let $I=(h(s_1),\ldots,h(s_k))$ and let $\chi$ be the monic generator
of $I\cap\C[S]$, of degree $q$. Existence follows from an annihilating
polynomial of multiplication by $S$ on the finite algebra
$\C[s_1,\ldots,s_k]/I$. Division by its least-degree monic member
proves that every member of the intersection is a multiple of $\chi$.
For $N\ge q-1$ define $\mathcal P_N=\C[S]_{\le N}$ and the exact maps
\[
 0\longrightarrow\mathcal P_{N-q}
 \xrightarrow{M_\chi}\mathcal P_N
 \xrightarrow{J_N}\mathcal C\longrightarrow0,
 \quad M_\chi P=\chi P,\quad
 \mathcal C=\C[S]/(\chi),\quad J_NP=[P]_\chi.                   \tag{GMT.3}
\]
Here $\mathcal P_{-1}=0$. All coordinate vectors of $\mathcal C$ are
in the written basis $1,S,\ldots,S^{q-1}$. Its map to the original
tensor jet is
\[
 \eta[P]=[v_h]_h^{\otimes k}[P(S)]_I.
\]
Its kernel is zero by the preceding intersection ideal and invertible
unit. No Gamma coefficient calculation changes this map.

Write $B_N$ for the coefficient matrix of the columns
$\chi,\chi S,\ldots,\chi S^{N-q}$ in $\mathcal P_N$. The matrix
$[E_N,B_N]$, where $E_N$ includes the first $q$ monomials, is triangular
with diagonal one. Thus its determinant is exactly one in this order;
putting the relation block first contributes $(-1)^{q(N-q+1)}$.

The original theta map is $\Theta\phi(x)=\sum_{n\ne0}\phi(nx)$,
with $D=-x\partial_x$, and $F_h$ satisfies
$\mathcal MF_h=v_h$, $h(D)F_h=\Theta\phi_0$, where
$\phi_0=(4\pi^2x^4-6\pi x^2)e^{-\pi x^2}$.
The exact primitive attached to a column $\chi P$ is retained as follows.
Successive monic division in $s_1,\ldots,s_k$ gives
$\chi(S)=\sum_i h(s_i)Q_i(\mathbf s)$. Its tensor primitive is
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^k(-1)^{i-1}Q_i(\mathbf D)P(\textstyle\sum_jD_j)
 (F_h^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes\phi_0\otimes
                F_h^{\otimes(k-i)}).                           \tag{GMT.4}
\]
The differential at the $i$th position has sign $(-1)^{i-1}$,
so differentiation gives precisely the original source vector
$\mathcal V_{h,k}(\chi P)$. This proves the relation-to-boundary
map, including its sign. The construction of $F_h$ uses the actual
zero-division operator
$S_\rho F(x)=x^{-\rho}\int_x^\infty F(y)y^{\rho-1}dy
=-x^{-\rho}\int_0^xF(y)y^{\rho-1}dy$ at a vanishing Mellin value.
The two integrals give respectively every large- and small-$x$ power
bound. Differentiation gives $(D-\rho)S_\rho F=F$, hence all Euler
derivative bounds and the Mellin quotient by $s-\rho$.
Starting from $\Theta\phi_0$ and successively dividing the complete
root list constructs the displayed $F_h$. The seed identity follows
by substituting $y=\pi x^2$ in its Mellin integral, which gives
$\mathcal M\phi_0(s)=\tfrac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)$
for $\Re s>-2$, followed by the absolutely convergent theta sum
for $\Re s>1$ and analytic continuation.

On each original support label $\ell$, a boundary still maps to
$(\ell,0)=e_\ell$; external absence $\tau$ still maps to $\tau$.
The linear polynomial maps (GMT.3)--(GMT.4) precede the quadratic map
$R\mapsto R^*H R$ from source representatives to Hermitian forms.
For the empty packet $h=1$, the analytic source remains, but
$\chi=1$, $q=0$, $\mathcal C=0$, $G_N$ is the zero-dimensional
form and its determinant is one. Every quotient formula below then
has its empty-block interpretation.

\subsection{Full Gamma mass and the finite coefficient evaluation}

For $\lambda>0$ set
\[
 \alpha=2\lambda,\quad \beta=k\alpha,\quad
 r_\lambda(t)=\frac{|\Gamma(\lambda+it/2)|^2}{2\pi},\quad
 c_\lambda=2^{1-2\lambda}\Gamma(2\lambda),\quad C_k=c_\lambda^k.
                                                                    \tag{GMT.5}
\]
The beta integral with its coordinate $x=2y$ gives
\[
 |\Gamma(\lambda+it/2)|^2
 =2\Gamma(2\lambda)\int_\R e^{ity}(2\cosh y)^{-2\lambda}dy.
\]
Fourier inversion therefore gives
$\int e^{iyt}r_\lambda(t)dt=c_\lambda(\cosh y)^{-2\lambda}$.
Both functions are integrable: the right integrand and all its
derivatives decay exponentially, so integration by parts bounds its
Fourier transform by every inverse power. Exponential integrability
of $r_\lambda$ on $|a|<\pi/2$ follows by rotating the gamma integral
by any $\varphi$ with $|a|<\varphi<\pi/2$; the squared modulus
has a bound $\Gamma(\lambda)^2(\cos\varphi)^{-2\lambda}
e^{-\varphi|t|}$. Analytic continuation now proves
\[
 \int e^{a u}r_{\lambda,k}(u)du=C_k(\cos a)^{-\beta},\qquad
 r_{\lambda,k}=r_\lambda^{*k}
             =\frac{C_k}{c_{k\lambda}}r_{k\lambda}.             \tag{GMT.6}
\]
The last identity is Fourier uniqueness with the exact product mass.

Let $b_j^{(\lambda)}$ denote the monic polynomials with
\[
 \sum_{j\ge0}\frac{b_j^{(\lambda)}(t)}{j!}z^j
 =(1-iz)^{-\lambda+it/2}(1+iz)^{-\lambda-it/2}
 =(1+z^2)^{-\lambda}e^{t\arctan z}.                             \tag{GMT.7}
\]
These agree with $j!P_j^{(\lambda)}(t/2;\pi/2)$ in the classical
generating function at
\href{https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.23.E7}{DLMF 18.23.7}.
The next proof fixes their scale without relying on that identification.
Differentiating (GMT.7) gives
\[
 b_{j+1}^{(\lambda)}=t b_j^{(\lambda)}
              -j(j+\alpha-1)b_{j-1}^{(\lambda)}.
\]
For small real $z,w$, integration of the product of generating
functions by (GMT.6) gives $c_\lambda(1-zw)^{-\alpha}$, since
$\cos(\arctan z+\arctan w)=(1-zw)/
\sqrt{(1+z^2)(1+w^2)}$. Local exponential domination justifies
every coefficient derivative. Thus
\[
 \int b_i^{(\lambda)}b_j^{(\lambda)}r_\lambda
 =\delta_{ij}c_\lambda j!(\alpha)_j,
 \qquad
 \int b_i^{(k\lambda)}b_j^{(k\lambda)}r_{\lambda,k}
 =\delta_{ij}C_kj!(\beta)_j.                                   \tag{GMT.8}
\]

Put $B(t)=|v_h(1/2+it)/\Gamma(\lambda+it/2)|^2$, so that
$w_h=B r_\lambda$ with its full complex amplitude still present
in the Mellin source. One may use the bounded reference constructed
in the Gamma descent: $\lambda=1/4$ for degree at least three,
or $\lambda=1/4+L$, $L\ge\max(0,3-\deg h)$ otherwise.
The relevant boundedness follows directly from the compact entire
quotient, $|h(1/2+it)|\ge(|t|/2)^{\deg h}$ beyond twice the root
radius, $|\zeta(1/2+it)|\le2(1+|t|)$, and the nonvanishing factors
$\prod_{j<L}(j+1/4+it/2)$ in the gamma shift. In particular
$B\in L^2(r_\lambda dt)$. All statements of this section also hold
for any specified nonnegative nonzero $B\in L^2(r_\lambda dt)$.
For that domain write $w_B=B r_\lambda$ and $m_{B,k}=w_B^{*k}$;
at the arithmetic input these equal the original $w_h,m_{h,k}$.
Cauchy--Schwarz with (GMT.6) gives all polynomial moments.
The calibration below uses this latter domain before it is explicitly
capped to a bounded function.

The sum adjoint is the actual conditional integral
\[
 C_\Sigma F(u)=\frac{1}{r_{\lambda,k}(u)}
 \int_{\R^{k-1}}F(t_1,\ldots,t_{k-1},u-\textstyle\sum_{i<k}t_i)
 \prod_{i<k}r_\lambda(t_i)r_\lambda(u-\textstyle\sum_{i<k}t_i)
 \,d^{k-1}t.                                                    \tag{GMT.9}
\]
Its adjoint inclusion $U_\Sigma f(\mathbf t)=f(\sum_i t_i)$
is isometric by Fubini; hence $C_\Sigma U_\Sigma=1$ and
$F\mapsto(C_\Sigma F,(1-U_\Sigma C_\Sigma)F)$ has inverse
$(f,n)\mapsto U_\Sigma f+n$ on
$L^2(r_{\lambda,k})\oplus\ker C_\Sigma$. At $k=1$ both maps
are the identity. The normal component and every support label
remain in this paired map. The specified sum density is
$m_{B,k}=r_{\lambda,k}B_k$, $B_k=C_\Sigma B^{\otimes k}$;
in particular it is $m_{h,k}$ at the arithmetic input.

Define the real coefficients $c_{B,j}$ (the original $c_{h,j}$
for the arithmetic multiplier $B=B_{h,\lambda}$) and formal coefficient power
\[
 c_{B,j}=\frac{\int b_j^{(\lambda)}w_B}{c_\lambda j!(\alpha)_j},
 \quad A(z)=\sum_{j\ge0}(\alpha)_j c_{B,j}z^j,
 \quad d_n=[z^n]A(z)^k.                                        \tag{GMT.10}
\]
Multiplying the finite generating coefficients in (GMT.7) gives
\[
 b_n^{(k\lambda)}(\textstyle\sum_i t_i)=
 \sum_{n_1+\cdots+n_k=n}\frac{n!}{\prod_i n_i!}
                   \prod_i b_{n_i}^{(\lambda)}(t_i).
\]
Pairing with $B^{\otimes k}$ gives the exact finite identity
\[
 \int b_n^{(k\lambda)}(u)m_{B,k}(u)du=C_k n!d_n.                \tag{GMT.11}
\]
Every term is integrable by Cauchy--Schwarz; no infinite formal-series
product was interchanged with an integral.

For a specified finite list of polynomials $p_0,\ldots,p_r$ in
$\mathcal P_N$, expand the actual tests
\[
 \overline{p_a(c+iu)}p_b(c+iu)
       =\sum_{n=0}^{2N}L_{ab,n}b_n^{(k\lambda)}(u).
\]
This expansion is a finite algorithm: subtract the top coefficient
times the monic polynomial of the same degree, then continue in
descending degree through zero. The resulting remainder is exactly
zero, by the decreasing degree. Equations (GMT.10)--(GMT.11) prove
\[
 \boxed{H(p)_{ab}=C_k\sum_{n=0}^{2N}n!L_{ab,n}d_n.}            \tag{GMT.12}
\]
The coefficient-to-form map is homogeneous of degree $k$ in
$c_{B,0},\ldots,c_{B,2N}$. Its full coordinate type is
$\R^{2N+1}\to\operatorname{Herm}(\mathcal P_N)$ on the actual
moment vectors, followed by congruence $H\mapsto P^*HP$ for a
polynomial coefficient matrix $P$. The nonnegative density has positive
mass. Its positivity set has positive Lebesgue measure, and a nonzero
polynomial has only finitely many real roots. Thus the integral of its
squared modulus is strictly positive, proving positivity of every finite
source Gram and of every injective relation Gram. Choosing $p_a=S^a$ gives
$H_N$; choosing the columns of $B_N$ gives exactly $B_N^*H_NB_N$.
Choosing actual representatives gives their actual Gram. Every relation
column has degree at most $N$, including its complete $\chi$ factor.

One may instead evaluate in the monic Gamma columns
$p_j^\Gamma(S)=i^j b_j^{(k\lambda)}((S-c)/i)$.
Their coefficient matrix $U_N$ is triangular with diagonal one,
and their reference Gram is
\[
 U_N^*H_N^\Gamma U_N
       =\diag\{C_kj!(\beta)_j:0\le j\le N\}.                  \tag{GMT.13}
\]
This is a stated invertible coordinate map, with all squared norms
retained. In particular it divides neither the measure nor the polynomials
by their mass or norms. The original reduction matrix becomes $J_NU_N$;
the original source form is recovered by $U_N^{-*}(\cdot)U_N^{-1}$.

\subsection{Finite coefficient errors and polynomial-test bounds}

Finite errors propagate before any inverse is taken. For real central
coefficients $\widehat c_{B,j}$ and radii $e_j\ge0$ enclosing the actual
$c_{B,j}$ for $0\le j\le2N$, define the finite polynomials
\[
 \widehat A=\sum_{j=0}^{2N}(\alpha)_j\widehat c_{B,j}z^j,
 \quad A_+=\sum_{j=0}^{2N}(\alpha)_j|\widehat c_{B,j}|z^j,
 \quad E_+=\sum_{j=0}^{2N}(\alpha)_je_jz^j,
\]
and set
\[
 \widehat d_n=[z^n]\widehat A^k,\qquad
 e^{(k)}_n=[z^n]\{(A_++E_+)^k-A_+^k\}.                         \tag{GMT.14}
\]
The multinomial expansion of each difference of products groups terms
by the nonempty set of factors containing a coefficient error. Taking
absolute values term by term proves
$|d_n-\widehat d_n|\le e^{(k)}_n$ for $n\le2N$. Thus the Hermitian
central matrix $\widehat H$ from (GMT.12) satisfies
\[
 |(H-\widehat H)_{ab}|\le
 \varepsilon_{ab}:=C_k\sum_{n=0}^{2N}n!|L_{ab,n}|e^{(k)}_n.
                                                                    \tag{GMT.15}
\]
The masses in this formula are exact constants. An interval evaluation
of those constants is propagated by the same product rule; it cannot
be replaced by setting $C_k=1$. Exact polynomial coefficients of
$\chi$ remain fixed in $L_{ab,n}$; any numerical enclosure of such
coefficients must likewise be propagated before reporting a matrix
certificate.

There is also a strictly polynomial-test refinement of the $L^2$ tail
bound in the supplied descent. Write
$B_L=\sum_{j\le L}c_{B,j}b_j^{(\lambda)}$ and
$E_k=B_k-C_\Sigma(B_L^{\otimes k})$. Orthogonality proves
$\|B_L\|\le\|B\|$. Expanding the tensor difference into $k$ terms
and using contraction of $C_\Sigma$ proves
$\|E_k\|\le k\|B\|^{k-1}\|B-B_L\|$ with the original measures.
Equation (GMT.11) applied degree by degree shows that $E_k$ is
orthogonal to all polynomials of degree at most $L$. Therefore
\[
 \left|\int\overline{p_a(c+iu)}p_b(c+iu)E_k(u)r_{\lambda,k}(u)du\right|
 \le\|E_k\|
 \left(C_k\sum_{n>L}^{2N}n!(\beta)_n|L_{ab,n}|^2\right)^{1/2}.
                                                                    \tag{GMT.16}
\]
This follows by discarding only the orthogonal test components whose
pairings are proved zero, and then applying Cauchy--Schwarz and
(GMT.8). It is exactly zero at $L\ge2N$. In particular a signed
truncated density has the same finite positive Gram at that degree;
pointwise positivity of the truncation is not required by this map.

An intrinsic finite polynomial-test bound is also available. The
reference evaluation kernel is
\[
 \mathcal K_N^\Gamma(u)=
       \sum_{j=0}^N\frac{b_j^{(k\lambda)}(u)^2}{C_kj!(\beta)_j}.
\]
Expansion in the original monic basis and Cauchy--Schwarz give
$|P(c+iu)|^2\le\|P\|_\Gamma^2\mathcal K_N^\Gamma(u)$.
It follows that the form induced by a real error $E_k$ obeys
\[
 |\langle P,P\rangle_{E_k}|
 \le\|P\|_\Gamma^2\|E_k\|_{L^2(r_{\lambda,k})}
       \|\mathcal K_N^\Gamma\|_{L^2(r_{\lambda,k})}.             \tag{GMT.17}
\]
The last squared norm is an exact Gamma integral of a degree-$4N$
polynomial, evaluable by (GMT.8) or (GMT.12) with the reference weight.
This is a finite evaluation-functional estimate, retaining every
Christoffel-kernel denominator and the full mass.

\subsection{Exact least lifts and the boundary-resolvent remainder}

For a positive definite source matrix $H$ in the fixed source coordinates,
define
\[
 K(H)=J_NH^{-1}J_N^*,\quad G(H)=K(H)^{-1},\quad
 R(H)=H^{-1}J_N^*G(H).                                         \tag{GMT.18}
\]
Surjectivity of $J_N$ makes $K(H)$ positive definite.
Multiplication gives $J_NR(H)=1$ and $B_N^*HR(H)=0$.
Every other lift is $R(H)x+B_Ny$, whose squared norm is
$x^*G(H)x+y^*B_N^*HB_Ny$. This proves minimum, uniqueness and
$R(H)^*HR(H)=G(H)$.

Let $H_0$ and $H_1$ be two actual positive finite source forms on
the same polynomial space. In particular they may be the Gamma and
arithmetic forms already constructed. Put
\[
 H_t=H_0+tE,\quad E=H_1-H_0,\quad0\le t\le1,\quad
 A_0=B_N^*H_0B_N,\quad F=B_N^*EB_N,\quad C=B_N^*ER_0,
\]
where $R_0=R(H_0)$ and $G_0=G(H_0)$. The whole interval of forms
is positive because it is the convex combination of the two actual
positive forms. Thus every nonempty $A_t=A_0+tF$ is positive.
The complete, signed resolvent formulas are
\[
 \boxed{R_t=R_0-tB_NA_t^{-1}C,\qquad
 G_t=G_0+tR_0^*ER_0-t^2C^*A_t^{-1}C.}                         \tag{GMT.19}
\]
To prove the first equation, its reduction is the identity and
$B_N^*H_tR_0=tC$, while $B_N^*H_tB_N=A_t$.
Consequently its boundary pairing is zero, so (GMT.18) identifies it.
Expanding its full $H_t$ norm gives
$R_0^*H_tR_0-2t^2C^*A_t^{-1}C+t^2C^*A_t^{-1}A_tA_t^{-1}C$.
The two negative terms and one positive term yield exactly (GMT.19).
In particular the unscaled matrix $C^*A_t^{-1}C$ is positive
semidefinite and vanishes precisely when $C=0$. Pairing $A_t^{-1}>0$
with every column of $C$ proves this criterion. The full quadratic
loss $t^2C^*A_t^{-1}C$ vanishes precisely when $t=0$ or $C=0$;
for an empty boundary block it is the zero form throughout.

The representative difference is a literal boundary with coefficient
vector $-tA_t^{-1}Cx$; inserting that polynomial into (GMT.4) gives
its full original theta primitive. The same jets $\eta x$ persist.
No statement of metric nonidentity leaves its comparison map unspecified.

For completeness the exact derivative identities are
\[
 R_t'=-B_NA_t^{-1}B_N^*ER_t,\qquad
 G_t'=R_t^*ER_t,\qquad
 G_t''=-2(B_N^*ER_t)^*A_t^{-1}(B_N^*ER_t).                     \tag{GMT.20}
\]
Differentiate $J_NR_t=1$ and $B_N^*H_tR_t=0$ to obtain the first
formula. The derivative $R_t'$ is a boundary, hence its two pairings
with $R_t$ in the derivative of $R_t^*H_tR_t$ vanish. This proves
the second formula. Differentiating it and substituting the first
proves the third. This is a proved concavity statement along the
actual finite metric segment, with the entire boundary coupling retained.

The determinant-line calculation has no hidden source-size exponent.
The change $[E_N,B_N]\mapsto[R(H),B_N]$ adds boundary columns to
the first $q$ columns and has determinant one. Its Gram is block
diagonal. Therefore
\[
 V_N(H)=\det G(H)=\frac{\det H}{\det(B_N^*HB_N)}.               \tag{GMT.21}
\]
For the original arithmetic and Gamma forms this is exactly
$V_N=V_N^\Gamma T_N$, with the same $T_N=X_{N+1}/Y_{N-q+1}$
as the supplied descent. Formula (GMT.19) gives its complete
matrix-level map and its nonlinear remainder.

\subsection{A two-degree map for the actual consecutive ratio}

Fix $N\ge q$. In original source monomials split degree $N+1$
into degrees $0,\ldots,N-1$ followed by $S^N,S^{N+1}$, and write
\[
 H_+=\begin{pmatrix}H_-&C_D\\ C_D^*&D_D\end{pmatrix},\quad
 W=D_D-C_D^*H_-^{-1}C_D,\quad
 J_+=[J_-,J_D],\quad F_D=J_D-J_-H_-^{-1}C_D.                   \tag{GMT.22}
\]
Here $H_-=H_{N-1}$, $H_+=H_{N+1}$, $W$ is $2\times2$, and
$F_D:\C^2\to\mathcal C$. Its columns are the quotient classes
of the two specified polynomials after subtracting their actual
orthogonal projections onto $\mathcal P_{N-1}$. The block map
\[
 (x,y)\longmapsto(x-H_-^{-1}C_Dy,y)
\]
has determinant one and carries the source Gram to
$\diag(H_-,W)$. Positivity proves $W>0$ and, by applying the
reduction map to this exact orthogonal direct sum,
\[
 K_+=K_-+F_DW^{-1}F_D^*,\quad
 Z_D=F_D^*G_-F_D\succeq0,\quad G_-=K_-^{-1}.                  \tag{GMT.23}
\]
Block elimination of $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&A\\-B&I\end{smallmatrix}\right)$
in its two orders proves $\det(I+AB)=\det(I+BA)$.
Applying this identity to (GMT.23) gives the exact scalar maps
\[
 \boxed{\frac{V_{N-1}}{V_{N+1}}
   =\frac{\det(W+Z_D)}{\det W}
   =1+\tr(W^{-1}Z_D)+\frac{\det Z_D}{\det W}.}                 \tag{GMT.24}
\]
The last equality is the complete characteristic polynomial of a
$2\times2$ matrix. All denominators are positive. This gives the
actual monotonicity because both eigenvalues of
$W^{-1/2}Z_DW^{-1/2}$ are nonnegative.

The same block also gives the adjacent source recurrence coefficient.
In the order in (GMT.22), the monic residual of $S^N$ has squared
norm $W_{11}$, and eliminating it from the residual of $S^{N+1}$
gives squared norm $\det W/W_{11}$. Therefore
\[
 \boxed{a_{N+1}=\frac{\omega_{N+1}}{\omega_N}
                   =\frac{\det W}{W_{11}^2}.}                 \tag{GMT.25}
\]
The first residual is monic because the subtracted projection has
smaller degree; so is the second after its additional elimination.
The norms have not been rescaled. The coefficient map
$S^j=\sum_{l\le j}\binom jl c^{j-l}i^lu^l$ has determinant
$i^{j(j-1)/2}$ in dimension $j$ and hence Gram determinant multiplier
one, which proves that these are also the source Hankel--Toda norms.

Apply (GMT.22)--(GMT.25) separately to the Gamma reference and
the arithmetic source, retaining their same $J_+$ and $\chi$. Then
\[
 \boxed{\frac{T_{N-1}}{T_{N+1}}
 =\frac{\det(W+Z_D)/\det W}
        {\det(W^\Gamma+Z_D^\Gamma)/\det W^\Gamma}.}           \tag{GMT.26}
\]
Every entry is a finite polynomial in the one-factor coefficients
through degree $2N+2$, followed by explicitly stated inverses of
positive matrices. In the supplied Toda upper estimate, substitute
the two positive scalars
$a_{N+1}=\det W/W_{11}^2$ and
$\mathcal R_N=\det(W+Z_D)/\det W$.
Its full right side becomes
\[
 \sqrt{\frac{\det W}{W_{11}^2}}\,
 \frac{\det(W+Z_D)/\det W-1}
      {2\sqrt{\det(W+Z_D)/\det W}}.                            \tag{GMT.27}
\]
This is an exact substitution into that existing inequality; its
discarded phase and volume-imbalance squares remain the ones proved
in TVB.32--TVB.34a. Formula (GMT.27) alone does not remove them or
assert equality. The two-degree determinant formula itself also
works at $q=0$, where $F_D$ and $Z_D$ are zero and the ratio is one.

\subsection{Certified finite envelopes and quotient-dimensional bounds}

Suppose a finite coefficient calculation supplies the entry bounds
(GMT.15), or the proved polynomial-test bounds (GMT.16).
Let $v_i>0$ be any specified positive rational weights and set
\[
 \Delta=\diag(\delta_i),\qquad
 \delta_i=\sum_j\varepsilon_{ij}\frac{v_j}{v_i},\qquad
 H^{\rm lo}=\widehat H-\Delta,\quad
 H^{\rm hi}=\widehat H+\Delta.                                \tag{GMT.28}
\]
The radii may be replaced by symmetric rational upper radii.
For each pair $i,j$, the inequality
$2|x_i||x_j|\le(v_j/v_i)|x_i|^2+(v_i/v_j)|x_j|^2$
gives $|x^*(H-\widehat H)x|\le x^*\Delta x$ after summation.
This proves $H^{\rm lo}\preceq H\preceq H^{\rm hi}$ without
using a pointwise lower bound on $B$.

The positive-pivot certificate for $H^{\rm lo}$ is entirely finite.
Eliminate successively its first diagonal pivot, recording
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}a&b^*\\b&C\end{pmatrix}
 \longmapsto \diag(a,C-bb^*/a),
 \qquad
 (x,y)\longmapsto(x-a^{-1}b^*y,y).
\]
The displayed congruence is invertible. Positive recorded rational
pivots prove positivity by induction; nonpositive pivots reject that
particular enclosure. Actual matrix positivity is already proved by
the source integral. For any sequence of valid entry intervals tending
to their actual entries, the lower envelopes tend to that positive
matrix. Its least unit-sphere quadratic value is positive by compactness,
and convergence in the finite sum of entry errors eventually makes
every lower envelope positive. Hence the certificate process succeeds
at every fixed finite degree when its actual input intervals are refined;
no success or interval refinement is asserted for absent arithmetic data.

For a positive certified lower envelope, calculate the actual finite
quotient forms $G^{\rm lo},G^{\rm hi}$ by (GMT.18), or by the
equivalent Schur formula
\[
 G(H)=E_N^*HE_N-E_N^*HB_N(B_N^*HB_N)^{-1}B_N^*HE_N.             \tag{GMT.29}
\]
The formula follows by substituting
$E_N-B_N(B_N^*HB_N)^{-1}B_N^*HE_N$ for the unique least lift.
The same radii certify the representative coefficients themselves.
Write $\widehat R=R(\widehat H)$,
$A^{\rm lo}=B_N^*H^{\rm lo}B_N$ and define, using any rational
upper bounds for the displayed complex moduli,
\[
 \begin{split}
 s_{ai}&=\sum_{r,l}|(B_N)_{ra}|\varepsilon_{rl}|\widehat R_{li}|,\\
 k_{ab}&=\tfrac12\bigl((A^{\rm lo})^{-1}_{aa}
                              +(A^{\rm lo})^{-1}_{bb}\bigr),\\
 e^R_{ri}&=\sum_{a,b}|(B_N)_{ra}|k_{ab}s_{bi}.
 \end{split}
\]
Then the full original-coordinate error and its quadratic loss obey
\[
 \boxed{|(R(H)-\widehat R)_{ri}|\le e^R_{ri},\qquad
 \widehat R^*H\widehat R-G(H)
 =(R(H)-\widehat R)^*H(R(H)-\widehat R).}                     \tag{GMT.29a}
\]
To prove the entry bound set $A=B_N^*HB_N$ and
$S_E=B_N^*(H-\widehat H)\widehat R$.
Formula (GMT.19) gives $R(H)-\widehat R=-B_NA^{-1}S_E$.
The triangle inequality gives $|(S_E)_{ai}|\le s_{ai}$.
Inversion reverses positive order: conjugating $A\succeq A^{\rm lo}$
by $(A^{\rm lo})^{-1/2}$, inverting its eigenvalues, and conjugating
back gives $A^{-1}\preceq(A^{\rm lo})^{-1}$.
Positivity of each two-coordinate Gram minor of $A^{-1}$ gives
$|A^{-1}_{ab}|\le\sqrt{A^{-1}_{aa}A^{-1}_{bb}}\le k_{ab}$,
where the last step is the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality and
the proved diagonal bounds. Matrix multiplication now proves the
coefficientwise bound in (GMT.29a). For its second identity,
$\widehat R-R(H)$ is an actual boundary, orthogonal to $R(H)$
in $H$. Expanding $\widehat R^*H\widehat R$ proves the equality.

This loss has an explicit $q$-dimensional rational upper form.
Choose positive rational weights $v_i$ in the original quotient
coordinates and set
\[
 \tau_{ij}=\sum_{a,b}s_{ai}|(A^{\rm lo})^{-1}_{ab}|s_{bj},\qquad
 \Delta_Q=\diag\left\{\sum_j\tau_{ij}\frac{v_j}{v_i}\right\}.
\]
The same entrywise quadratic inequality as (GMT.28) proves
\[
 \boxed{0\preceq\widehat R^*H\widehat R-G(H)\preceq\Delta_Q.}
                                                                    \tag{GMT.29b}
\]
Indeed the middle matrix is $S_E^*A^{-1}S_E\preceq
S_E^*(A^{\rm lo})^{-1}S_E$. Its value on $x$ is at most
$\sum_{i,j}\tau_{ij}|x_i||x_j|\le x^*\Delta_Qx$.
Every source row, relation coefficient and quotient coordinate is
retained in these sums. At an empty boundary block both error forms
are zero. Applying $\mathcal V_{h,k}$ to each representative difference
gives the theta primitive (GMT.4), so the certified coefficient
error also has its original source and quotient interpretation.

Minimizing the two proven source inequalities over the same affine
space of representatives gives
\[
 G^{\rm lo}\preceq G(H)\preceq G^{\rm hi},\qquad
 \det G^{\rm lo}\le V_N(H)\le\det G^{\rm hi}.                  \tag{GMT.30}
\]
For the determinant assertion, conjugate by the positive square root
of the lower form and multiply the eigenvalues, all of which are at
least one; apply the same argument for the upper form.

Construct the degree $N-1$ envelope by taking the corresponding
principal submatrix of the same degree $N+1$ envelope. Then
\[
 \frac{\det G_-^{\rm lo}}{\det G_+^{\rm hi}}
 \le\frac{V_{N-1}}{V_{N+1}}
 \le\frac{\det G_-^{\rm hi}}{\det G_+^{\rm lo}}.               \tag{GMT.31}
\]
Division by the exact positive Gamma ratio from (GMT.26) gives the
certified interval for $T_{N-1}/T_{N+1}$. These are determinants
of size $q$, with no independent numerator/denominator error estimate
on the larger source and relation determinants.

For example a relative certificate
$(1-\eta)\widehat H\preceq H\preceq(1+\eta)\widehat H$,
with a recorded rational $0\le\eta<1$ and $\widehat H>0$,
gives exactly
\[
 (1-\eta)^q\det G(\widehat H)\le V_N(H)
       \le(1+\eta)^q\det G(\widehat H),
\]
and a common such certificate for the two nested degrees gives ratio
multipliers $((1-\eta)/(1+\eta))^q$ and its reciprocal.
This follows because $R(aH)=R(H)$ and $G(aH)=aG(H)$ for $a>0$,
as direct substitution in (GMT.18) proves. The mass is scaled only
in this explicitly declared comparison, never assigned a new value
in the original arithmetic source.

\subsection{Exact calibration and a bounded relative-volume counterexample}

Take the explicitly auxiliary one-factor weight
\[
 \lambda=1,\quad k=2,\quad c=1,\quad

 \chi(S)=(S-1)^2-2.                                           \tag{GMT.32}

The positive polynomial $B$ lies in $L^2(r_1)$ by (GMT.6).

 A(z)=\tfrac32+\tfrac32z^2,\quad
 (d_0,d_2,d_4)=(\tfrac94,\tfrac92,\tfrac94),\quad
 B_2(u)=\frac{54}{35}+\frac{39}{280}u^2+\frac{3}{1120}u^4.
                                                                    \tag{GMT.33}

To verify the last equality, use
$b_2^{(2)}=u^2-4$,

The one-factor mass is $3/4$ and the sum mass is $9/16$.
Repeated multiplication by $t$ in the recurrence (GMT.8) gives

\subsection{Verification scope}

The accompanying exact checker constructs moments in two ways:
Jacobi recurrence and the literal two-factor binomial expansion,
then compares them with the Gamma coefficient formula. It verifies
the original $S$-coordinate Gram, every displayed lift and relation
identity, the full boundary-resolvent loss, five points on its actual
positive matrix segment, both two-degree formulas and the rational
envelope calculation. Its deliberate formula changes remove the
mass, omit the tensor cross term, reverse the boundary-loss sign,
remove the conjugation, or remove one power in the Schur denominator.
Each change is rejected by the corresponding exact check in ordinary
and optimized Python. The written proofs establish the all-degree
identities; these finite checks provide reproducible regression evidence.
There is no Lean execution or analytic interval claim for a nonempty
zeta packet in this verification.
