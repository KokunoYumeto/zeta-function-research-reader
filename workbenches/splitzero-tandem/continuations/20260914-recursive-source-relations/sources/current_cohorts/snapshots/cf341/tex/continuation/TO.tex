\section{The object, its actual elements, and its assembly}

Put $U=\{s:0<\Re s<1,\ \Re s\ne1/2\}$ and retain the original
$g=2\xi$, source $V$, target $\mathscr B$, operator $D=-x\partial_x$,
and map $\Theta$ defined in full in the following construction.
Let $\mathcal P_U$ consist of all monic polynomials whose roots lie in
$U$, including $1$.  The construction starts with actual functions:
\[
 \mathscr B_U=\{F\in\mathscr B:\exists p\in\mathcal P_U,\
                              p(D)F\in\Theta V\},\qquad
 C_U=[V\xrightarrow\Theta\mathscr B_U],\qquad
 \mathfrak T_U=\bigoplus_{k\ge0}C_U^{\otimes_{\mathbb C}k}.
 \tag{TO.1}
\]
The differential, multiplication, maps, topologies, full local
coordinates and every finite presentation of this object are proved
below.  In particular, (TO.1) specifies a subspace of the original
functions and a differential graded algebra, rather than introducing
free numerical variables to which necessary conditions are assigned.
An element is a finite sum of finite tensors of actual original
source functions and target functions satisfying the displayed
annihilator equation.  Its differential applies the original theta
map with the original Koszul signs.

The complete object includes its maps into the original two-leg
tau diagram and its arithmetic, geometric and norm observations.
We specify exactly what one object with these maps means.  Let
\[
 C_\tau^{\rm raw}
 =[V_+\oplus V_-\xrightarrow{(\phi,\psi)\mapsto
                    \Theta(\phi-\widehat\psi)}\mathscr B],
 \qquad
 i:C_U\longrightarrow C_\tau^{\rm raw},
 \tag{TO.2}
\]
where $i^0v=(v/2,-\widehat v/2)$ and $i^1F=F$.  The minus leg has
the action $t=1-D$, the plus and target legs have $t=D$, and Fourier
uses $e^{-2\pi ix\nu}$.  Thus $i$ is an injective cochain map:
its differential on $v$ is $\Theta(v/2+v/2)=\Theta v$, and Fourier's
Euler identity proves its $A=\mathbb C[t]$ linearity.  Tensoring
these literal inclusions over $\mathbb C$ gives the injective
homomorphism of differential graded algebras
\[
 T(i):\mathfrak T_U\hookrightarrow
       T_{\mathbb C}(C_\tau^{\rm raw}).                       \tag{TO.3}
\]
Injectivity follows because every inclusion of vector spaces admits
a linear retraction: extend a basis, project onto the original basis,
and tensor the retractions.  This is a proof of injectivity, not a
claim that these auxiliary retractions preserve the Euler action.

The arithmetic sheaf observation is the actual balanced diagram
\[

 C_g=[\mathcal O_U\xrightarrow{g}\mathcal O_U],

\]

sheafification of $\mathcal O_U\otimes_A$; it is a specified

single analytic stalk.

The connection from (TO.1) to this diagram is also explicit.  At a
polynomial $p$, a degree-one cycle of the original complex
\[

           \xrightarrow{(p(D),-\Theta)}\mathscr B]
\]

Its boundaries map to $\Theta V$.  Under the transitions $p\mid P$,

\[
 T_UQ=\bigcup_{p\in\mathcal P_U}\ker(p(D):Q\to Q),\qquad
 Q=\mathscr B/\Theta V.                                    \tag{TO.5}
\]
Composition with (TO.2), balancing, and Mellin is exactly the map
in (TO.4), as is checked on these representatives in CAU.

\section{The absolute base and the two distinct zero fibres}

The base of this represented object is the constructed pointed
monoid blueprint $\mathfrak b_\tau=\operatorname{Spec}\{\tau,1\}$,
with absorbing $\tau$ and empty-sum relation
$\varnothing\equiv\tau$.  At a commutative coefficient ring $R$,
its split carrier is $G(R)=\{\tau\}\sqcup R^\bullet$, with
$e_R=0_R^\bullet\ne\tau$.  Supported addition and multiplication
are the operations in $R$, while $\tau$ is the additive identity
and multiplicative absorber.  At a labelled linear arrow $f:E\to F$
the lifted arrow is
\[
 \tau\longmapsto\tau,\qquad
 (\lambda,x)\longmapsto(\lambda',f(x)).                      \tag{TO.6}
\]
For a linear arrow its additivity and supported scalar compatibility follow by
applying the linear identities for $f$; compositions give the
lift of the composite.  An element killed by an arrow lands at
$(\lambda',0)$ and retains support. For the conjugate-linear
reflected-conjugate operator and for conjugation, the scalar is
conjugated in the semilinear rule proved in (AG38). The Fourier
reflection retains its complex-linear map and the base involution
$s\mapsto1-s$.
For multiplication the rule is $x^\bullet y^\bullet=(xy)^\bullet$,
with absorbing $\tau$; the product identities prove compatibility.
These rules give the pointed structural arrows of $\mathbf C_U$.
Numerical norm evaluations remain the specified observations and
are not asserted additive.

For independent coefficients, the coordinatewise lift of a linear
map preserves every coefficient mask, including coordinates with
zero image. Face filling is a different specified arrow: for
$A\subseteq B$, zero insertion sends the zero $e_A$ of the
labelled additive face group to $e_B$. If $A$ is empty and $B$
is nonempty, it sends the all-absent coefficient tuple to a
supported-zero tuple. It is therefore not declared to preserve
the global absent point. Both maps occur in the represented
underlying-set category with their respective structural types.
The Cartesian product semiring has intersection of coefficient
supports under multiplication; the negacyclic semiring has their
cyclic sumset. These are two marked structures with their actual
formulas, not an identification of their products. The negacyclic
product is an arrow of the finite algebra observation. The
cochain tensor algebra uses its original concatenation into the
next tensor degree; no degree-zero cochain multiplication
$C_U\otimes C_U\to C_U$ is inferred from that finite product.

For the scalar carrier $G(R)$, the amplitude/support map is injective: support zero identifies
$\tau$, while support one and amplitude identify $r^\bullet$.
The scalar maps continue to be $G(\mathbb Z)\to G(\mathbb C)$

\section{Exactly what survives and how every offcritical zero enters}

The complete calculation gives an isomorphism with an explicit
inverse on original functions:
\[
 H^1(C_U)=T_UQ\xrightarrow[\ [F]\mapsto[\mathcal MF]\ ]{\sim}
 \bigoplus_{\substack{\rho\in U\\g(\rho)=0}}
               \mathcal O_{\mathbb C,\rho}/(g).
 \tag{TO.7}
\]
For finite support $Z$, put $h=\prod_{\rho\in Z}(s-\rho)^{m_\rho}$
with each actual full order.  A prescribed full jet $\alpha$ has
the unique remainder polynomial $P$ satisfying
$[P]_\rho=[(g/h)^{-1}\alpha_\rho]$; its preimage is
\[
 [F_\alpha],\qquad
 F_\alpha=S_h\Theta(P(D)\phi_*),\qquad
 \mathcal MF_\alpha=(g/h)P.                                 \tag{TO.8}
\]
Here $S_h$ is the iterated original Euler inverse and
$\phi_*=(4\pi^2x^4-6\pi x^2)e^{-\pi x^2}$.
CAU proves that every inverse acts on its required moment-zero
domain, that the result belongs to the original $\mathscr B$,
and that changes of representatives are actual theta boundaries.
The complete unit $g/h$ is retained in (TO.8).

The local element $1$ at any actual offcritical zero is nonzero
modulo $(g)$.  Formula (TO.8) therefore gives a nonzero arithmetic
class, not merely a parameter bearing that zero's name.  Conversely,
the original finite-jet kernel calculation proves that a nonzero
class in (TO.5) has a root of $g$ in its annihilator support.
Thus this construction is exhaustive in both directions.
The exclusion of zeros outside the open strip is the original
arithmetic input for $g=2\xi$; within that input, vanishing of
$H^1(C_U)$ excludes every counterexample to RH.

The entire object is retained when this marked cohomology vanishes:
its theta relations, scalar unit, diagonal tau source and meromorphic
comparison kernel remain actual parts of the represented diagram.
The precise arithmetic detector is (TO.7), not an assertion that
all those background spaces vanish.  Its tensor powers are
\[
 H^j(C_U^{\otimes k})=
 \begin{cases}(T_UQ)^{\otimes k},&j=k,\\0,&j\ne k.
 \end{cases}                                                \tag{TO.9}
\]
AG proves this with the full explicit tensor contraction.  For a
nonzero class $u$, a functional taking $u$ to $1$ proves
$u^{\otimes k}\ne0$.  A contradiction to any universally forced
component of $\mathbf C_U$ therefore excludes its originating
offcritical class.  The following amplification calculation uses
these exact nonvanishing maps.

\section{The norm observations are values on this same object}

All Hilbert, phase and finite quotient forms in the calculation
come from evaluating the original functions represented above.
For a full packet and polynomial $P$ they begin with the identity
\[
 \|P(D_1,\ldots,D_k)F_h^{\otimes k}\|_{L^2(d^kx)}^2
 =\int_{\mathbb R^k}|P(1/2+it_1,\ldots,1/2+it_k)|^2
       \prod_i\frac{|(g/h)(1/2+it_i)|^2}{2\pi}\,d\mathbf t.
 \tag{TO.10}
\]
Successive monic division gives their relation spaces and the
theta primitive (AG21).  Orthogonal minimization within precisely
these finite relation spaces defines their matrices $G$, their
canonical lifts and their volume determinants.  These quantities
are thereby coupled through the same $F_h$; none is an
independently adjustable entry in the construction.

Conormal logarithmic derivatives and other observations made
before the theta quotient retain their target in the full ambient
tensor source of (TO.3).  Closure of $\mathscr B_U$ under those
extra operations is not presumed.  Hilbert completions likewise
remain observations with their proved arrows.  In particular the
FKG calculation gives actual nonzero observations of canonical
lifts whose Hilbert norms tend to zero; it proves their exact
algebraic quotient and their Hausdorff reduction separately.
Replacing (TO.7) by that Hausdorff reduction is not an arrow
that preserves the arithmetic detector.
