\section{The matching passage in Weil
II}\label{the-matching-passage-in-weil-ii}

The local S20 text identifies a second, particularly close match to the
recollection about a bound involving a minus sign and two: \emph{Weil
II}, Lemma 1.8.1, its formulas 1.8.1.1--1.8.1.2, and the explicit return
to Corollary 1.4.3. The controlling text is
\href{workspace:/output/Deligne_Weil_II_S20_LaTeX/typed_latex/S20_EN_article_text.txt}{the
current English S20 article}, lines 1073--1095, checked against the
current French transcription. The
\href{https://archive.numdam.org/articles/10.1007/BF02684780/}{published
paper} is Deligne, \emph{La conjecture de Weil II}, IHÉS 52 (1980),
137--252.

Let \(j:U_0\hookrightarrow X_0\) be an open immersion of smooth
geometrically irreducible curves over \(\mathbb F_q\), with finite
complement \(S_0\). Fix \(\ell\ne\operatorname{char}\mathbb F_q\) and
the coefficient embedding
\(\iota:\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\to\mathbb C\) in Deligne's convention.
Let \(\mathcal F_0\) be lisse on \(U_0\) and pointwise \(\iota\)-pure of
weight \(\beta\). For \(N(x)=q^{\deg x}\) use the exact convention

\[w_{N(x)}(\alpha)=2\log_{N(x)}|\iota\alpha|.\]

After making \(X_0\) affine, the cohomological identity is

\[\begin{aligned}
&\prod_{x\in|U_0|}\iota\det(1-F_xt^{\deg x},\mathcal F_0)^{-1}
\prod_{x\in|S_0|}\iota\det(1-F_xt^{\deg x},j_*\mathcal F_0)^{-1}\\
&\hspace{15mm}=\frac{\iota\det(1-Ft,H_c^1(X,j_*\mathcal F))}
 {\iota\det(1-Ft,H_c^2(X,j_*\mathcal F))}.
\end{aligned}\tag{DII1}\]

The open-set Euler product is holomorphic and nonzero in
\(|t|<q^{-(\beta+2)/2}\). Corollary 1.4.3 excludes poles of the right
side there. The remaining finite product has numerator one, so no
denominator root can cancel inside that product. Dividing (DII1) by its
nonzero open-set factor therefore excludes each boundary local pole in
that disc.

For an eigenvalue \(\alpha\) in a boundary factor, a root of
\(1-\iota\alpha\,t^{\deg x}\) has modulus \(|\iota\alpha|^{-1/\deg x}\).
Pole exclusion gives

\[|\iota\alpha|^{-1/\deg x}\ge q^{-(\beta+2)/2},\qquad
|\iota\alpha|\le N(x)^{(\beta+2)/2},\qquad
w_{N(x)}(\alpha)\le\beta+2.\tag{DII2}\]

Now take tensor powers. Write \(W=\mathcal F_{\bar\eta}\) for the
geometric generic fibre near \(x\), and \(I_x\) for its local inertia
group. The boundary stalk is \((j_*\mathcal F)_{\bar x}=W^{I_x}\). The
relevant map is literally

\[(W^{I_x})^{\otimes k}\hookrightarrow(W^{\otimes k})^{I_x},
\qquad v_1\otimes\cdots\otimes v_k\longmapsto v_1\otimes\cdots\otimes v_k.\tag{DII3}\]

The tensor inclusion is injective by a basis extension. Its image is
invariant because inertia fixes every factor; it commutes with Frobenius
because Frobenius normalizes inertia. Consequently an eigenvector
\(v\ne0\) at the boundary has a nonzero image \(v^{\otimes k}\) with
eigenvalue \(\alpha^k\). The sheaf \(\mathcal F_0^{\otimes k}\) is
pointwise pure of weight \(k\beta\): eigenvalues in a tensor product are
products of eigenvalues, so their complex moduli multiply. Applying the
already-proved (DII2) to it yields

\[k\,w_{N(x)}(\alpha)=w_{N(x)}(\alpha^k)\le k\beta+2,
\qquad w_{N(x)}(\alpha)\le\beta+\frac2k.\tag{DII4}\]

A violation \(w_{N(x)}(\alpha)=\beta+\varepsilon\) with
\(\varepsilon>0\) contradicts (DII4) at the finite integer
\(k=\lfloor2/\varepsilon\rfloor+1\). Thus \(w_{N(x)}(\alpha)\le\beta\).
This lemma gives an upper bound at the boundary; it does not assert
equality of all boundary weights.

This is the exact pattern requested: first prove a larger forbidden
region by returning to an earlier cohomological exclusion; then map the
same offending class injectively into tensor powers until it would enter
that forbidden region. The earlier exclusion is reapplied to an object
proved to retain the offending eigenvalue.

The older English math-mode file has an incorrect \(H^0\) denominator in
its transcription of 1.8.1.1. The current S20 text has \(H_c^2\), as
used in (DII1). The historical file is retained unchanged and its status
is recorded in the source index.
