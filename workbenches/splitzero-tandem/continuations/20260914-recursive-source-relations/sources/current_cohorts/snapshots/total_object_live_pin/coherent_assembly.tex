\section{One anchored offcritical assembly of the original tau complex}
\label{sec:coherent-offcritical-assembly}

We construct a single diagram from the original source complexes, with a
marked arithmetic image and with its other fibres still attached.  Its
index set does not require naming an actual zero.  In particular, the
construction makes sense with no decision about the existence of an
offcritical zero.  The analytic input consists of the exact original
calculations (BK.1)--(BK.24), the finite derived comparison (DP.1)--(DP.8),
and the original two-leg maps (DC.17)--(DC.23).

\subsection{The source-indexed complex and its canonical directed limit}

Keep $A=\mathbb C[t]$, $D=-x\partial_x$, the original even Schwartz
space $V$ with $\phi(0)=0$ and $\int\phi=0$, and the original
rapid-at-both-ends space $\mathscr B$ of (BK.1).  All tensor products
in this section are algebraic.  The operators and entire functions are
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \Theta\phi(x)=2\sum_{n\ge1}\phi(nx),\qquad
 \mathcal MF(s)=\int_0^\infty F(x)x^s\frac{dx}{x},\\
 Q=\mathscr B/\Theta V,
 \qquad g(s)=2\xi(s),\qquad \mathcal M\Theta\phi=gH_\phi.
 \end{gathered}
                                                               \tag{CAU.1}
\]
Here $g=s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$ and
$H_\phi=2\pi^{s/2}M_+\phi/(s(s-1)\Gamma(s/2))$ with its original
entire continuation.  The Fourier transform uses $e^{-2\pi ix\nu}$.
Set
\[
 U=\{s\in\mathbb C:0<\Re s<1,\quad\Re s\ne\tfrac12\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal P_U=\{p\in\mathbb C[t]:p\text{ monic and every root lies in }U\}.
                                                               \tag{CAU.2}
\]
The constant polynomial $1$ belongs to this set.  Order it by divisibility.
It is directed because $pP$ is a common upper bound of $p$ and $P$.
No condition $p\mid g$ is imposed on this index set.

For every $p\in\mathcal P_U$ define the following original-source
complex in degrees zero, one, and two:
\[
 \mathscr K_p=
 [\,V\xrightarrow{v\mapsto(\Theta v,p(D)v)}
       \mathscr B\oplus V
     \xrightarrow{(F,\phi)\mapsto p(D)F-\Theta\phi}\mathscr B\,].
                                                               \tag{CAU.3}
\]
The differential squares to zero because $D\Theta=\Theta D$.
For $P=rp$, the transition $\iota_{p,P}$ is the identity on degree
zero and has the formulas
\[
 (F,\phi)\longmapsto(F,r(D)\phi),\qquad
 F\longmapsto r(D)F                                           \tag{CAU.4}
\]
in degrees one and two.  Applying both differentials verifies the
cochain identity, and $\iota_{P,R}\iota_{p,P}=\iota_{p,R}$ because
the polynomial multipliers multiply.  Thus (CAU.3)--(CAU.4) are one
specified functor from the directed category $\mathcal P_U$ to complexes
of $A$-modules, not a set of requested properties.

The original $\Theta$ is injective and every nonzero $p(D)$ is injective
on $\mathscr B$ and $V$.  The exact cohomology maps are
\[
 H^0\mathscr K_p=0,\qquad
 H^1\mathscr K_p\xrightarrow{\sim}Q[p],\quad[(F,\phi)]\mapsto[F],
 \qquad H^2\mathscr K_p\xrightarrow{\sim}Q/pQ.                 \tag{CAU.5}
\]
Indeed a cycle has $p(D)F=\Theta\phi$, exactly the torsion condition.
The source $\phi$ is unique.  If $F=\Theta v$, injectivity of $\Theta$
gives $\phi=p(D)v$, so the cycle is the displayed boundary.  Conversely
every torsion class has such a representative and gives a cycle.
The degree-two quotient is
$\mathscr B/(p(D)\mathscr B+\Theta V)$, which is exactly $Q/pQ$.
Under (CAU.5), (CAU.4) induces the literal inclusion $Q[p]\subset Q[P]$
in degree one, and $[q]\mapsto[r(D)q]$ in degree two.

Define the actual directed colimit complex
\[
 \mathscr K_U=\mathop{\rm colim}_{p\in\mathcal P_U}\mathscr K_p,
 \qquad T_UQ=\bigcup_{p\in\mathcal P_U}Q[p].                  \tag{CAU.6}
\]
Its universal property is explicit: a cochain map $\mathscr K_U\to L$
is uniquely the same as cochain maps $f_p:\mathscr K_p\to L$ satisfying
$f_P\iota_{p,P}=f_p$.  To construct it, represent a colimit vector by
$x\in\mathscr K_p$ and send it to $f_p(x)$.  The common-upper-bound
relation makes this independent of the representative, and the
cochain assertion follows at that stage.  Conversely restriction gives
the compatible family.  Filtered colimits of vector spaces are exact:
an element and any relation involving it occur at finitely many stages,
and a common upper stage witnesses the required kernel or image identity.
This also proves that cohomology commutes with this colimit.

In fact
\[
 H^1\mathscr K_U=T_UQ,
 \qquad H^i\mathscr K_U=0\quad(i\ne1).                        \tag{CAU.7}
\]
Only degree two remains to prove.  The original finite-jet calculation
(BK.8)--(BK.11) identifies
$Q/pQ$ with $A/(p,j_pg)$ by the Mellin jet.  At a root $\rho$ of $p$
of order $n$, let $m=\operatorname{ord}_\rho g$, allowing $m=0$.
The local factor of this cokernel is $\mathbb C[z]/(z^{\min(n,m)})$,
with $z=s-\rho$; this records the actual unit of $g$ in the Mellin map,
although the ideal alone is generated by this power.  For $P=p^L$, the
transition on degree two is multiplication by $p^{L-1}$.  At $\rho$
this multiplier has zero order $(L-1)n$, while the target cokernel is
killed by $z^{\min(Ln,m)}$.  Choose $L$ so that $(L-1)n\ge m$ at
every one of the finitely many roots with $m>0$.  Every local component
then maps to zero.  Components with $m=0$ were zero already.  Thus each
degree-two class is killed at a later stage, proving (CAU.7).
This argument never assumes that $p(D)$ is onto its original test space.

\subsection{The exact Mellin identification, including every actual zero}

Let $\mathcal N_U=(\mathcal O_{\mathbb C}/(g))|_U$.
The notation $\Gamma_c(U,\mathcal N_U)$ means sheaf sections with compact
support in $U$.  The zeros of the nonzero entire function $g$ form a
discrete closed set.  Each compact subset of $U$ meets that set in
finitely many points.  A local section at one of these points extends
uniquely by zero outside a sufficiently small disc containing no other
zero: on the punctured disc $g$ is a unit and the quotient sheaf is zero.
These extensions glue, since their restrictions agree on overlaps.
Consequently there is the canonical identification
\[
 \Gamma_c(U,\mathcal N_U)
   =\bigoplus_{\rho\in U:g(\rho)=0}\mathcal O_{\mathbb C,\rho}/(g).
                                                               \tag{CAU.8}
\]
The direct sum specifies finite support and keeps the full stalk quotient.

The original Mellin map gives a canonical isomorphism
\[
 \mathfrak m:T_UQ\xrightarrow{\sim}\Gamma_c(U,\mathcal N_U),
 \qquad [F]\longmapsto([\mathcal MF]_\rho)_\rho.              \tag{CAU.9}
\]
We prove all assertions, rather than presupposing a spectral
identification.  If $p(D)F=\Theta\phi$, then
\[
 p(s)\mathcal MF(s)=g(s)H_\phi(s).                            \tag{CAU.10}
\]
Away from the roots of $p$, this says that $\mathcal MF$ is locally a
multiple of $g$.  Hence the proposed section has finite support in $U$.
Changing $F$ by an original theta boundary changes its Mellin transform
by $gH$, so the map is well-defined and $A$-linear.

For injectivity, suppose the section in (CAU.9) is zero.  At a root
$\rho$ of $p$ of order $n$, write $p=a(z)z^n$ and $g=u(z)z^m$,
with $a(0)u(0)\ne0$, allowing $m=0$.  The vanishing section says that
$\mathcal MF=gB$ for an analytic germ $B$ if $m>0$; it says the same
when $m=0$ because $g$ is then a unit.  Equation (CAU.10) and cancellation
in the local analytic domain give $H_\phi=pB$.  Thus every jet of
$H_\phi$ of order less than the corresponding multiplicity in $p$ is
zero.  The proved original range identity (BK.8) gives
$\phi=p(D)\psi$ for $\psi\in V$.  Then
$p(D)(F-\Theta\psi)=0$, and injectivity on $\mathscr B$ gives
$F=\Theta\psi$.  This proves injectivity.

For surjectivity, take a finitely supported section $\alpha$ of (CAU.8),
with support $Z$, and put
\[
 p_Z(s)=\prod_{\rho\in Z}(s-\rho)^{m_\rho},\qquad
 m_\rho=\operatorname{ord}_\rho g,\qquad
 k_Z(s)=g(s)/p_Z(s).                                         \tag{CAU.11}
\]
The quotient $k_Z$ is entire, and its germ at each $\rho\in Z$ is a unit.
The function $g$, every multiplicity, and this entire quotient are retained.
Choose the unique polynomial $P$ of degree less than $\deg p_Z$ whose
jets satisfy
\[
 [P]_\rho=[k_Z^{-1}\alpha_\rho]
       \quad\text{in }\mathcal O_{\mathbb C,\rho}/(s-\rho)^{m_\rho}.
                                                               \tag{CAU.12}
\]
Existence and uniqueness follow from the polynomial Chinese remainder
map: relatively prime factors have Bezout polynomials, whose products
construct the orthogonal idempotents; multiplication by these idempotents
and summation realizes any prescribed finite set of Taylor polynomials.
The kernel is $(p_Z)$, and degree reduction gives the unique representative.

Use the original vector
$\phi_*=(4\pi^2x^4-6\pi x^2)e^{-\pi x^2}$, for which
$H_{P(D)\phi_*}=P$.  The Mellin transform of
$\Theta(P(D)\phi_*)$ is $gP$ and vanishes to every order specified by
$p_Z$.  The exact original range identity for $\mathscr B$ in (BK.8)
therefore supplies its unique inverse under $p_Z(D)$:
\[
 F_\alpha=S_{p_Z}\Theta(P(D)\phi_*)\in\mathscr B,
 \quad p_Z(D)F_\alpha=\Theta(P(D)\phi_*),\quad
 \mathcal MF_\alpha=k_ZP.                                    \tag{CAU.13}
\]
Here $S_{p_Z}$ is the composite of the original full-plane Euler inverses
at the roots with their full multiplicities.  Uniqueness makes the
composite independent of the order of those factors.  Formula (CAU.12)
gives $[\mathcal MF_\alpha]_\rho=\alpha_\rho$.  Away from $Z$ the
quotient by $p_Z$ is a unit at every zero of $g$, so the other stalk
classes are zero.  This proves surjectivity.  The empty support gives
the zero class.  Any alternative polynomial lift differing by $p_ZR$
changes $F_\alpha$ by $\Theta(R(D)\phi_*)$, so its class in $Q$ is
unchanged.  Thus (CAU.13) constructs the inverse to (CAU.9) on the
original functions and relations.

In particular, for every actual $\rho\in U$ the element $1$ of
$\mathcal O_{\mathbb C,\rho}/(g)$ has a nonzero preimage in $T_UQ$.
Indeed $g$ vanishes there, so $1\notin(g)$, and (CAU.9) is injective.
Every nilpotent coefficient at that zero is carried by the same inverse.
This exhaustiveness assertion is a proved map at every actual zero;
it is not an assertion that such a $\rho$ exists.

\subsection{The anchored sheaf diagram and its retained diagonal and kernel}

Sheafify $\mathcal O_U\otimes_A$ applied termwise to (CAU.6), and denote
the resulting complex by $\mathcal A_U$.  Its stalks are the corresponding
tensor products because tensor products commute with filtered colimits.
Every analytic stalk is torsion-free over the PID $A$, hence flat by
the directed-union-of-finite-free-modules proof in (BK.12).  Therefore
\[
 H^1\mathcal A_U=\mathcal O_U\otimes_A T_UQ,
 \qquad H^i\mathcal A_U=0\quad(i\ne1).                       \tag{CAU.14}
\]
The natural sheaf map induced by Mellin identifies this degree-one
sheaf with $\mathcal N_U$.  To check it exactly, (CAU.8)--(CAU.9)
identify $T_UQ$ as an $A$-module with the direct sum of
$A/(t-\rho)^{m_\rho}$.  At a point $s_0$, tensoring a summand gives
$\mathcal O_{s_0}/(s-\rho)^{m_\rho}$ through the original map
$a\otimes[P(t)]\mapsto[aP(s)]$.  It is zero if $s_0\ne\rho$,
and is the full quotient if $s_0=\rho$.  The Mellin identification
retains the unit $k_Z$ used in (CAU.12)--(CAU.13), so this is the
actual germ of the map to $\mathcal O_{s_0}/(g)$.  It is an isomorphism
at every stalk and hence an isomorphism of sheaves.

Use the original two-leg complex, with $t=D$ on $V_+$ and $t=1-D$
on $V_-$, and its analytic comparison:
\[
 C_\tau=[\mathcal V_+\oplus\mathcal V_-
       \xrightarrow{(\phi,\psi)\mapsto\Theta(\phi-\widehat\psi)}
               \mathcal B],\qquad
 C_g=[\mathcal O_U\xrightarrow{g}\mathcal O_U].                \tag{CAU.15}
\]
Their terms are in degrees zero and one and are restricted to $U$.
The comparison $c:C_\tau\to C_g$ sends $(\phi,\psi)$ to
$H_{\phi-\widehat\psi}$ and sends $F$ to $\mathcal MF$;
on balanced tensors these formulas are multiplied by their analytic
coefficient.  The identity in (CAU.1) proves it is a cochain map.

There is a cochain map from every $\mathscr K_p$ to the original
unbalanced joint complex, compatible with (CAU.4), given by
\[
 v\longmapsto\left(\tfrac12v,-\tfrac12\widehat v\right),
 \qquad(F,\phi)\longmapsto F,
 \qquad F\text{ in degree two}\longmapsto0.                  \tag{CAU.16}
\]
The differential of the first image is exactly $\Theta v$ because
Fourier squares to the identity on even functions.  The half factors
in (CAU.16) are part of this displayed map; no amplitude is discarded.
The map is compatible with every transition because their first
degree-one coordinate is unchanged.  Taking the colimit and balancing
therefore gives $a:\mathcal A_U\to C_\tau$.

Write $C_{\ker}=\ker c$.  As proved by the explicit lifts in (DC.20)--(DC.22),
there is a short exact sequence of the original complexes
$0\to C_{\ker}\to C_\tau\xrightarrow c C_g\to0$.
The single anchored diagram we use is
\[
 \mathfrak T_U^{\rm coh}:=
 \left(
 \mathcal A_U\xrightarrow{\ a\ }C_\tau\xrightarrow{\ c\ }C_g,
 \quad C_{\ker}\hookrightarrow C_\tau,
 \quad \{\mathcal O_U\otimes_A\mathscr K_p\to\mathcal A_U\}_{p\in\mathcal P_U}
 \right).                                                    \tag{CAU.17}
\]
The tensor notation in the final family means its sheafification.
This is a representation of the complex-linear path category of the
following fully specified quiver with relations: vertices $A,C,G,R$
and one vertex $P_p$ for every
$p\in\mathcal P_U$; arrows $P_p\to P_P$ for $p\mid P$, arrows
$P_p\to A$, and $A\to C\to G$, $R\to C$.  Relations identify
composites of divisibility arrows, require $P_p\to A$ to be compatible
with them, and require $R\to C\to G=0$.  The representation assigns
respectively $\mathcal A_U,C_\tau,C_g,C_{\ker}$ and the displayed
balanced source complexes, with precisely the maps above.  The quiver
is a set-sized category, since $\mathcal P_U$ is a set.  Its colimit
vertex has the proved universal property following (CAU.6).  This
specifies one object in a diagram category and its actual entries and
arrows, rather than an object defined by satisfying a list of conditions.

The composite $ca$ is a quasi-isomorphism.  Both its source and target
have cohomology only in degree one, and its degree-one map is the
isomorphism (CAU.14) to $\mathcal N_U$.  With the original notation
$\mathcal M=\mathcal O_U\otimes_AQ$, $\beta:\mathcal M\to\mathcal N_U$,
and $\mathcal K=\ker\beta$, its degree-one factorization is
\[
 \mathcal N_U\xrightarrow{\ j\ }\mathcal M
                 \xrightarrow{\beta}\mathcal N_U,
 \qquad\beta j=1.                                           \tag{CAU.18}
\]
It is the canonical original inclusion of the full torsion summand.
Indeed each local class in its image is annihilated by some polynomial,
whereas every nonzero polynomial is invertible on $\mathcal K$ by BK.
Thus it has zero $\mathcal K$ component, and $\beta$ is an isomorphism
on that torsion summand.  This identifies the actual map induced by
(CAU.16), not just an abstract summand of equal length.

For clarity the entire middle term is retained in (CAU.17).
On the original joint degree-zero amplitude make the reversible change
\[
 u=\phi-\widehat\psi,\qquad v=\tfrac12(\phi+\widehat\psi),
 \qquad\phi=v+\tfrac12u,\quad
 \psi=\widehat v-\tfrac12\widehat u.                          \tag{CAU.19}
\]
Its differential is $\Theta u$; the $v$ component is the full diagonal.
Together with the original canonical splitting
$\mathcal M=\mathcal N_U\oplus\mathcal K$, it proves
\[
 C_\tau\simeq\mathcal V_+[0]\oplus\mathcal N_U[-1]
                         \oplus\mathcal K[-1],\qquad
 C_{\ker}\simeq\mathcal V_+[0]\oplus\mathcal K[-1].           \tag{CAU.20}
\]
These are derived identifications of the explicitly retained complexes.
The marked arithmetic image of (CAU.17) is $\operatorname{im}H^1(a)$,
which by (CAU.18) is $j\mathcal N_U$.  Its support is therefore exactly
the actual zero divisor of $g$ in $U$, with its scheme multiplicities.
The support of the entire middle term is not being substituted for
this specified arithmetic support.  In particular the nonzero
meromorphic kernel and the diagonal are present even at points where
this arithmetic image is zero.

\subsection{Full finite observations and all tensor jets of the same diagram}

For each $p\in\mathcal P_U$ let $E_p=A/(p)$ and let $j_p$ denote full
Taylor reduction.  The original Mellin maps define the following
cochain map, with every factor of $g$ retained:
\[
 \mathscr K_p\longrightarrow
 [\,\mathcal O_U\xrightarrow{(g,p)}\mathcal O_U^2
                  \xrightarrow{(p,-g)}\mathcal O_U\,],
 \quad v\mapsto H_v,\quad(F,\phi)\mapsto(\mathcal MF,H_\phi),
 \quad F\mapsto\mathcal MF.                                 \tag{CAU.21}
\]
On balanced tensors it is $\mathcal O_U$-linear.  Applying either
differential gives the same expression by (CAU.1) and
$\mathcal M p(D)F=p(s)\mathcal MF$.  It induces the original maps
to the kernel and cokernel of multiplication by $j_pg$ on $E_p$;
equivalently its analytic degree-one target is $\mathcal N_U[p]$.
The local conversion between these two presentations, for
$p=a(z)z^n$ and $g=u(z)z^m$, is
\[
 [v]\longmapsto[(g/p)v],\qquad
 z^{\max(n-m,0)}O/z^nO\xrightarrow{\sim}
 z^{\max(m-n,0)}O/z^mO,                                     \tag{CAU.22}
\]
with the exact multiplier $(u/a)z^{m-n}$ and inverse
$(a/u)z^{n-m}$ on those specified powers.  This is a well-defined
isomorphism because changing a representative by $p$ changes the
product by $g$, and the source kernel condition makes the product
holomorphic.  It includes zero factors when $m=0$ and includes every
repeated-zero order.  Thus every finite factor of the all-polynomial
index is computed, including those which observe no actual zero.
In fact (CAU.21), after balancing, is a quasi-isomorphism at every
$p$: the degree-one map is (CAU.22), the degree-two map is the same
Mellin identification $O/(p,g)$, and both degree-zero groups vanish.
Flatness and (CAU.5) justify these cohomology calculations for the
original balanced source, so no chain-level kernel has been assumed zero.

For an actual finite monic divisor $h\mid g$ with roots in $U$, the
finite packet is the prescribed quotient
$A_h=A/(h)\simeq\Gamma(U,\mathcal O_U/(h))$.
It is obtained from the marked image by the reduction
$\mathcal N_U\to\mathcal O_U/(h)$ and from the original source by
$[F]\mapsto j_h\mathcal MF$.  Retain the entire quotient $k=g/h$;
its germ at a root of $h$ is a unit exactly when $h$ contains the
full multiplicity of $g$ there.
Both degrees of its derived observation are retained by
\[
 E_h^{\rm free}=[\mathcal O_U\xrightarrow{h}\mathcal O_U]
 \quad\text{in degrees }-1,0,
 \qquad -\otimes_{\mathcal O_U}E_h^{\rm free}.                \tag{CAU.23}
\]
This is a tensor operation on every vertex and arrow of (CAU.17),
so it is an observation of the single diagram.  On its analytic vertex
$C_g$ the total complex in degrees $-1,0,1$ is
\[
 \mathcal O_U\xrightarrow{a\mapsto(ha,ga)}\mathcal O_U^2
             \xrightarrow{(b,c)\mapsto gb-hc}\mathcal O_U.
                                                               \tag{CAU.24}
\]
It maps to $[\mathcal O_U/(h)\xrightarrow0\mathcal O_U/(h)]$
in degrees zero and one by $(b,c)\mapsto[b]$ and $d\mapsto[d]$.
This is a quasi-isomorphism because it tensors the free complex $C_g$
with the quasi-isomorphism $E_h^{\rm free}\to\mathcal O_U/(h)$;
alternatively its degree-zero cycles are exactly $(b,kb)$, modulo
$(ha,ga)$, and its degree-one quotient is $O/(g,h)=O/(h)$.
Under the map to the torsion in $\mathcal N_U$ its degree-zero class is
$[b]\mapsto[kb]$.  The multiplier $k=g/h$ is not dropped.

On the full joint vertex the same observation also retains its diagonal.
By (BK.8)--(BK.9), $\mathcal V_+/h\mathcal V_+\simeq\mathcal O_U/(h)$
through $H$, and $h$ is injective on $\mathcal V_+$.
Using (CAU.20) and the contractibility of
$[\mathcal K\xrightarrow{h}\mathcal K]$ therefore gives
\[
 H^0(C_\tau\otimes E_h^{\rm free})
       \simeq (\mathcal O_U/(h))^2,\qquad
 H^1(C_\tau\otimes E_h^{\rm free})\simeq\mathcal O_U/(h).
                                                               \tag{CAU.25}
\]
In the original plus/minus jet coordinates the degree-zero comparison
is $(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto\alpha-\beta$; its kernel is the full
diagonal $(\alpha,\alpha)$.  Thus the finite observation does not
confuse the joint source with the one-leg complex.

For $h\mid H$, the quotient transition of these observations is the
cochain map $E_H^{\rm free}\to E_h^{\rm free}$ equal to $H/h$ in
degree $-1$ and the identity in degree zero.  Its cochain identity is
$h(H/h)=H$.  All transitions of the total diagram are obtained by
tensoring this exact map.

To specify all endpoints of these arrows, first adjoin to the seed
diagram a vertex $(v,h)$ with value $D_v\otimes_{\mathcal O_U}E_h^{\rm free}$
for every existing vertex $v$ of value $D_v$ and every admitted $h$.
Adjoin the maps $f\otimes1$ for each original arrow $f$, the transition
maps $1\otimes(E_H^{\rm free}\to E_h^{\rm free})$, and their
cohomology vertices and induced arrows, including the finite quotient
vertices in (CAU.24)--(CAU.25).  Their relations are exactly the
commuting squares supplied by tensor functoriality and by cohomology.
This internal tensor operation over $\mathcal O_U$ is an explicitly
adjoined observation; it is not identified with the external tensor
operation in the next paragraph.

Finally close this represented diagram under finite ordered external
tensor products over $\mathbb C$, with the specified symmetry maps
and Koszul signs.  The ambient category is graded by the nonnegative
integer $r$: degree-$r$ values are sheaf complexes on $U^r$, with the
coefficient sheaf equal to the tensor product over $\mathbb C$ of the
pulled-back coefficient sheaves; degree zero is the point with unit
$\mathbb C$.  Permutations use their actual coordinate permutations
on $U^r$.  This is a single canonical enlargement: its vertices
are finite words of existing vertices, including the empty word;
arrows are generated by tensoring the existing arrows with identities,
by permutations, and by the displayed finite-observation maps.  Their
relations are composition, identity, tensor interchange, and the Koszul
permutation relations.  The value of a word is the literal external
tensor product of its original complexes; its differential is
\[
 d(x_1\otimes\cdots\otimes x_r)
 =\sum_{i=1}^r(-1)^{\sum_{j<i}|x_j|}
       x_1\otimes\cdots\otimes dx_i\otimes\cdots\otimes x_r.
                                                               \tag{CAU.26}
\]
This construction has the explicit free symmetric monoidal universal
property: a tensor-compatible extension of the original represented
diagram must have these values and arrows on every word, and the
displayed relations make that extension unique.  Pairwise interchange
of the two differential terms proves $d^2=0$ with these signs.
For finite packets $h_1,\ldots,h_r$, their tensor observation is exactly
\[
 A_{h_1}\otimes_{\mathbb C}\cdots\otimes_{\mathbb C}A_{h_r}
 =\frac{\mathbb C[s_1,\ldots,s_r]}
       {(h_1(s_1),\ldots,h_r(s_r))}.                          \tag{CAU.27}
\]
Successive monic division in each variable proves the isomorphism:
the monomials with $0\le j_i<\deg h_i$ give bases on both sides and
the map takes the tensor basis to that basis.  At specified actual
centres it retains all jets $0\le j_i<\operatorname{ord}_{\rho_i}h_i$;
when each selected divisor contains the full multiplicity, these are
exactly all jets $0\le j_i<m_{\rho_i}$.  It retains every
one-variable multiplier $g(s_i)/h_i(s_i)$ from (CAU.24).
The total derived observation is the tensor of the two-term observations,
so every degree and every tensor nilpotent survives in its prescribed
summand.  Tensoring over a field is exact: a basis extends a basis of
a subspace and splits every vector-space short exact sequence.
This proves the cohomological tensor assertion without an unproved
flatness claim about a completed analytic tensor product.
The tensor sheaves here are sheaves of modules over the literal tensor
product of the pulled-back analytic coefficient sheaves; no formal or
analytic completion of the original source is implicit.

\subsection{All proper source and original leg-support fibres, and the exact actions}

The diagram has its original label enlargement as part of the same
construction.  For every $D$-invariant subspace $W\subset V$ and every
nonempty original leg mask $S\subset\{+,-\}$, include the original complex
$C_{W,S}$ with central term $\mathscr B$, plus source $W$ if
$+\in S$, minus source $\widehat W$ if $-\in S$, and differential
$\Theta\phi-\Theta\widehat\psi$ on the present sources.
Give the two sources the actions $D$ and $1-D$, respectively.
Balancing and restriction to $U$ are the same operations used above.
For $W\subset W'$ and $S\subset S'$, inclusion on each present
source and the identity on $\mathscr B$ is a cochain map; the
differentials agree term by term.  These maps compose literally and
are included as arrows in the represented category of (CAU.17).
The map to $C_g$ is the original signed $H$ map in degree zero and
Mellin in degree one, so these arrows commute with that comparison.

For the joint mask, the change (CAU.19) has $u,v\in W$ and proves
$H^0C_{W,\{+,-\}}=W$ and $H^1C_{W,\{+,-\}}=Q_W$,
where $Q_W=\mathscr B/\Theta W$.  Each one-leg mask has zero
degree-zero cohomology and the same $Q_W$ in degree one; on the minus
leg the actual differential is $-\Theta\widehat\psi$.
The proper-label transition retains the exact original sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow V/W\xrightarrow{[\phi]\mapsto[\Theta\phi]}

\subsection{The exact attachment to the single arithmetic tensor algebra}

The arithmetic tensor algebra (AG14) is attached to (CAU.17) by an
explicit source-level map, with no claim that all operations on the
ambient test space preserve its arithmetic subspace.  Retain its
original definitions
\[
 \mathscr B_U=q^{-1}(T_UQ)\subset\mathscr B,\qquad
 C_U=[V\xrightarrow{\Theta}\mathscr B_U],\qquad
 \mathfrak T_U=\bigoplus_{k\ge0}C_U^{\otimes_{\mathbb C}k}.
                                                               \tag{CAU.34}
\]
These agree exactly with (AG2) and (AG14), since $[F]\in Q[p]$ means
$p(D)F\in\Theta V$.  Each term has the original topology as a
subspace when a topological observation is taken; the displayed DGA
itself uses the algebraic tensors specified in AG.

It is essential not to project all of $\mathscr K_p^1$ into
$\mathscr B_U$: an arbitrary $F$ in that degree need not have a torsion
class.  The exact connecting complex is the good truncation
\[
 Z_p=\{(F,\phi):p(D)F=\Theta\phi\},\qquad
 \tau_{\le1}\mathscr K_p=[V\xrightarrow{(\Theta,p(D))}Z_p].    \tag{CAU.35}
\]
The map $Z_p\to q^{-1}(Q[p])$, $(F,\phi)\mapsto F$, is a linear
isomorphism: existence of $\phi$ is the defining condition on its
target and uniqueness follows from injectivity of $\Theta$.
It intertwines the displayed differential with $\Theta$.  The
transitions (CAU.4) preserve it and become literal inclusions of these
target subspaces.  Directed exactness consequently proves the cochain
isomorphism and the anchored zigzag
\[
 \mathscr K_U\ \longleftarrow\
       \mathop{\rm colim}_p\tau_{\le1}\mathscr K_p
       \ \xrightarrow{\sim}\ C_U
       \ \xrightarrow{\ \sigma\ }\ C_\tau^{\rm orig},
 \quad
 \sigma^0(v)=(\tfrac12v,-\tfrac12\widehat v),\quad
 \sigma^1(F)=F.                                              \tag{CAU.36}
\]
Here $C_\tau^{\rm orig}$ is the original unbalanced joint complex;
its balancing is (CAU.15).  The left arrow is the inclusion of the
good truncation into the directed complex and is a quasi-isomorphism
by (CAU.7).  The middle arrow is the actual cochain isomorphism just
proved, since the union of $q^{-1}(Q[p])$ is $\mathscr B_U$.
The right arrow is injective in both degrees and is a cochain map
by (CAU.16).  The two maps from the middle complex into the full
tau complex agree literally.  Balancing preserves this zigzag and
its quasi-isomorphism because all the analytic stalks are flat over
$A$.  Thus it is the exact source-level attachment of AG's arithmetic
complex to the arithmetic vertex of (CAU.17).

Tensoring the right arrow and taking the direct sum gives one injective
unital differential graded algebra map
\[
 \mathfrak T_U\hookrightarrow
       T(C_\tau^{\rm orig})
       :=\bigoplus_{k\ge0}(C_\tau^{\rm orig})^{\otimes_{\mathbb C}k},
 \qquad x_1\otimes\cdots\otimes x_k
        \longmapsto\sigma(x_1)\otimes\cdots\otimes\sigma(x_k).
                                                               \tag{CAU.37}
\]
Its multiplicativity follows from concatenation, its compatibility
with the differential follows term by term from (CAU.26), and its
injectivity follows by extending bases over $\mathbb C$ in every
cochain degree before tensoring.  The degree-zero tensor unit maps
to the same unit.  It has the tensor-algebra universal property:
every cochain map from $C_U$ into the underlying complex of a unital
DGA extends uniquely to a unital DGA map by multiplying the images
of the tensor factors, since the Leibniz identity is exactly the
signed formula (CAU.26).  In particular (CAU.37) is the unique such
extension of the specified original source inclusion $\sigma$.
