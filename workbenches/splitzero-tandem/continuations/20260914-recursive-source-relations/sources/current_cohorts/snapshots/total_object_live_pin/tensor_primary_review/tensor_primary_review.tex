\section*{Ordered tensor primary boundary: independent calculation}

Fix the same actual zero \(\rho\) of its complete order \(m\geq1\),
put \(N=m+1\), and retain \(c=-(-\rho)^N/N\).
Fix an integer \(k\geq1\). Every tensor below is ordered; its top
form is \(ds_1\wedge\cdots\wedge ds_k\). There is no permutation
quotient or change of exterior convention. The coordinate change
is the specified \(y_i=s_i-\rho\) in each factor.

Let
\[
 V_y=\mathbb C[y_1,\ldots,y_k]/(y_1^m,\ldots,y_k^m),\qquad
 \mathcal A=\{0,\ldots,m-1\}^k,\qquad
 e_\alpha=y_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots y_k^{\alpha_k},\qquad
 H=k(m-1).
 \tag{TPR.1}
\]
Here \(H\) denotes a total degree bound. Let \(J_i\) multiply by
\(y_i\), and set \(T=\sum_iJ_i\). Let \(P\), \(W\) and \(D(u)\)
be the exact one-factor matrices proved in SP.3 and SP.13:
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 P_{ab}=\binom ba\rho^{b-a}\quad(a\leq b),\qquad
 W_{ja}=\zeta^{j(a+1)}-1,\qquad \zeta=e^{2\pi i/N},\\
 D(u)_{aa}
  =N^{(a+1)/N-1}e^{\pi i(a+1)/N}
       \Gamma((a+1)/N)u^{(a+1)/N}.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{TPR.2}
\]
Entries of \(P\) below the diagonal are zero. The original branch
of \(u\), all the gamma constants, and the ordered contours are
retained. Set \(S=P^{\otimes k}\), \(W_k=W^{\otimes k}\), and
\(D_k(u)=D(u)^{\otimes k}\). The coefficient relation and complete
ordered period matrix are
\[
 x_y=Sx_s,\qquad
 \Pi_k(u)=e^{kc/u}W_kD_k(u)S .
 \tag{TPR.3}
\]
Indeed the original phase is
\(\sum_i((s_i-\rho)^N/N+c)\), and its exponential and monomial
integrals factor in the displayed order. Each one-factor
translation was proved with decaying endpoint connectors in
SP.10--14. Absolute convergence permits iterated integration;
the original product orientation gives (TPR.3) without an extra
sign. Every factor is invertible for \(u\ne0\).

\subsection*{All character projectors in their precise coordinates}

For \(r\in\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\) define diagonal endomorphisms
of \(V_y\) by
\[
 Z e_\alpha=\zeta^{k+|\alpha|}e_\alpha,\qquad
 Q_r e_\alpha=
 \begin{cases}e_\alpha,&k+|\alpha|\equiv r\pmod N,\\
               0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{TPR.4}
\]
These projectors satisfy \(Q_rQ_t=\delta_{rt}Q_r\) and
\(\sum_rQ_r=I\). The original marked and period-coordinate
projectors are, respectively,
\[
 Q_{s,r}=S^{-1}Q_rS,\qquad
 E_r=W_kQ_rW_k^{-1},\qquad
 M=W_kZW_k^{-1},\qquad
 E_r=\frac1N\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\zeta^{-rj}M^j.
 \tag{TPR.5}
\]
To prove the last formula, evaluate its conjugate by \(W_k\)
on \(e_\alpha\); the geometric sum is one if
\(k+|\alpha|\equiv r\) and zero otherwise.
Continuation of the retained \(u\)-branch multiplies the
\(\alpha\)-entry of \(D_k(u)\) by \(\zeta^{k+|\alpha|}\).
Consequently \(\Pi_k^{\mathrm{cont}}=M\Pi_k\).
Moreover \(D_k(u)\) commutes with \(Q_r\), so the exact pullback is
\[
 \Pi_k(u)^{-1}E_r\Pi_k(u)=Q_{s,r}.
 \tag{TPR.6}
\]
Thus the marked projector is constant in \(u\), although it is
not the diagonal matrix (TPR.4) in the original \(s\)-basis.
The horizontal local system has the inverse monodromy convention;
its eigenvalue-one subspace has the same index condition.

The invariant dimension is computed by the same finite sum:
\[
 \dim Q_0V_y
   =\frac1N\left(m^k+m(-1)^k\right).
 \tag{TPR.7}
\]
For \(j=0\) the one-factor character sum is \(m\), while for
\(1\leq j<N\) it is
\(\sum_{\ell=1}^{N-1}\zeta^{j\ell}=-1\).
Taking its \(k\)-th power and then the average proves (TPR.7).
The same calculation gives
\(\dim Q_rV_y=(m^k-(-1)^k)/N\) for \(r\ne0\).
All these dimensions count the original ordered tensor basis;
no multiplicities have been suppressed.

\subsection*{Exact arithmetic action and failure of invariantness}

In the original marked coefficients the sum-coordinate action is
\[
 A_k=k\rho I+S^{-1}TS.
 \tag{TPR.8}
\]
This follows by applying \(s_i=\rho+y_i\) to every tensor monomial.
Let \(D_{k,0}\) be \(D_k(u)\) with each power of \(u\) removed,
and set
\[
 K=W_kD_{k,0}T D_{k,0}^{-1}W_k^{-1}.
 \tag{TPR.9}
\]
Each nonzero entry of \(T\) increases the total degree by one,
so conjugation by \(D_k(u)\) multiplies that entry by
\(u^{1/N}\). Therefore
\[
 \Pi_kA_k\Pi_k^{-1}=k\rho I+u^{1/N}K,\qquad
 M K M^{-1}=\zeta K.
 \tag{TPR.10}
\]
Both are exact identities on the original ordered tensor space.

The grade shift gives, for all residues \(r\),
\[
 Q_rT=TQ_{r-1},\qquad
 [Q_0,T]=T(Q_{N-1}-Q_0),\qquad Q_0TQ_0=0.
 \tag{TPR.11}
\]
For example, when \(Te_\alpha\ne0\), every term in it has
grade \(k+|\alpha|+1\); evaluating on every \(e_\alpha\) proves
the first identity, and the others follow because \(N\geq2\).
Conjugation gives the complete projector commutators:
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \relax[Q_{s,0},A_k]=S^{-1}T(Q_{N-1}-Q_0)S,\\
 [E_0,\Pi_kA_k\Pi_k^{-1}]
       =u^{1/N}K(E_{N-1}-E_0),\\
 Q_{s,0}A_kQ_{s,0}=k\rho Q_{s,0},\qquad
 E_0\Pi_kA_k\Pi_k^{-1}E_0=k\rho E_0.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{TPR.12}
\]
The scalar compression does not assert that the invariant subspace
is preserved by the full action.

Here is a complete classification of that invariantness.
Put
\[
 d_{\min}=N\left\lceil\frac{k}{N}\right\rceil-k
       \in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}.
 \tag{TPR.13}
\]
For integers \(c_i\geq0\), every integer between zero and
\(\sum_i c_i\) is the sum of integers \(b_i\) satisfying
\(0\leq b_i\leq c_i\). A proof is to choose
\(b_1=\min(c_1,d)\) for a target sum \(d\), and repeat with
the remaining target; if all capacities were exhausted before
reaching \(d\), their total would have been smaller than \(d\).
Applying this to \(c_i=m-1\) proves that every total degree
between zero and \(H\) occurs in (TPR.1).
Thus \(Q_0V_y\ne0\) exactly when \(d_{\min}\leq H\).
For a basis vector of degree less than \(H\), at least one
coordinate is below its cap and \(Te_\alpha\ne0\); its distinct
monomial terms cannot cancel. At degree \(H\) there is the sole
top monomial and \(T\) kills it. Hence
\[
 TQ_0\ne0
     \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d_{\min}<H.
 \tag{TPR.14}
\]
For \(k=1\), \(d_{\min}=m>m-1=H\), so \(Q_0=0\).
For \(m=1\), \(N=2\), \(H=0\), and \(T=0\); the invariant
space is one-dimensional exactly for even \(k\).
For \(m\geq2,k=2\), \(d_{\min}=m-1<2m-2=H\).
For \(m\geq2,k\geq3\), one has
\(H=k(m-1)\geq m+1=N\), and hence
\(d_{\min}\leq N-1<H\).
Combining these cases proves
\[
 \boxed{\ TQ_0\ne0
            \ \Longleftrightarrow\ m\geq2,\ k\geq2.\ }
 \tag{TPR.15}
\]
In those and only those cases, the original arithmetic action
fails to preserve the nonzero invariant subspace, as its
nilpotent part sends it to grade one.

\subsection*{Every return path and every compressed exponential coefficient}

For each integer \(r\geq0\), the commuting nilpotents give
the full matrix formula
\[
 (T^r)_{\beta\alpha}
 =
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\frac{r!}{\prod_i(\beta_i-\alpha_i)!},
    &\beta_i\geq\alpha_i\ \forall i,\quad
                    |\beta-\alpha|=r,\\[4pt]
 0,&\text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
 \qquad \alpha,\beta\in\mathcal A.
 \tag{TPR.16}
\]
The multinomial expansion of \((\sum_iJ_i)^r\) proves this:
a multiindex of increments \(\nu\) contributes
\(r!/\prod_i\nu_i!\) times \(J_1^{\nu_1}\cdots J_k^{\nu_k}\).
On \(e_\alpha\), it is zero if an increment exceeds
\(m-1-\alpha_i\), and otherwise its target is uniquely
\(\beta=\alpha+\nu\).
This both counts the ordered paths and retains the coordinatewise
truncations. In particular \(T^{H+1}=0\) and
\[
 T^H e_{\boldsymbol 0}
       =\frac{H!}{((m-1)!)^k}e_{(m-1,\ldots,m-1)}\ne0.
 \tag{TPR.17}
\]

The \((\beta,\alpha)\) entry of \(Q_0T^rQ_0\) is (TPR.16)
multiplied by the two indicators
\(\mathbf1_{N\mid k+|\alpha|}\mathbf1_{N\mid k+|\beta|}\).
It therefore vanishes identically if \(N\nmid r\). For an
integer \(q\geq0\), the exact nonzero criterion is
\[
 Q_0T^{qN}Q_0\ne0
       \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d_{\min}+qN\leq H .
 \tag{TPR.18}
\]
Necessity follows because the smallest possible invariant starting
degree is \(d_{\min}\), and the final degree cannot exceed \(H\).
For sufficiency choose \(\alpha\) of degree \(d_{\min}\).
Its remaining capacities sum to \(H-d_{\min}\). The elementary
capacity argument after (TPR.13) supplies increments \(\nu_i\)
bounded by these capacities, of total \(qN\).
Then \(\beta=\alpha+\nu\) is an allowed invariant index and
the coefficient in (TPR.16) is a strictly positive integer.
Thus each asserted return is an actual nonzero matrix entry.

On \(V_0=Q_0V_y\ne0\), define
\[
 q_*=\left\lfloor\frac{H-d_{\min}}N\right\rfloor
       =\left\lfloor\frac{km}N\right\rfloor
          -\left\lceil\frac{k}N\right\rceil
       =k-2\left\lceil\frac{k}N\right\rceil,\qquad
 R=(Q_0T^NQ_0)|_{V_0}.
 \tag{TPR.19}
\]
The equalities follow by inserting \(H=k(m-1)\) and
(TPR.13), and using
\(\lfloor k-k/N\rfloor=k-\lceil k/N\rceil\).
Since \(T^N\) commutes with every \(Q_r\), induction gives
\[
 R^q=(Q_0T^{qN}Q_0)|_{V_0},\qquad
 R^{q_*}\ne0,\quad R^{q_*+1}=0 .
 \tag{TPR.20}
\]
When \(q_*=0\), the nonzero zeroth power means \(I_{V_0}\),
and \(R=0\). If \(V_0=0\), every compressed map is the map
on the zero space, and (TPR.19)--(TPR.20) are not used to
declare a nilpotency index there.

For every \(z\in\mathbb C\), the exponential is a finite sum,
and its complete invariant compression is
\[
 F(z)=(Q_0e^{zT}Q_0)|_{V_0}
       =\sum_{q=0}^{q_*}\frac{z^{qN}}{(qN)!}R^q .
 \tag{TPR.21}
\]
In particular for a positive real dilation parameter \(a\),
set \(z=\log a\), with its real logarithm. The compression
of \(e^{(\log a)A_k}\) in the original invariant frame is
the conjugate of \(a^{k\rho}F(\log a)\).
For a nonzero complex \(a\), the same formula holds after
specifying its logarithm branch.
For period coefficients the corresponding compression is
\[
 \left(E_0e^{z u^{1/N}K}E_0\right)|_{E_0\mathbb C^{m^k}}
       =\sum_{q=0}^{q_*}
          \frac{z^{qN}u^q}{(qN)!}
             \left(E_0K^NE_0\right)^q .
 \tag{TPR.22}
\]
The factor \(u^q\) belongs to the period coordinate formula;
it is absent from the marked coefficient formula (TPR.21).

If \(q_*\geq1\), \(F(z)\) is nonconstant and, for \(z\ne0\),
is not the identity. Choose a nonzero entry of \(R\) joining
two invariant basis degrees with difference \(N\);
no higher power \(R^q\), \(q\geq2\), has that degree
difference, so its coefficient cannot cancel.
Furthermore the first nonzero homogeneous term of
\(F(z+w)-F(z)F(w)\), of total degree \(N\), is
\[
 \frac{(z+w)^N-z^N-w^N}{N!}\,R .
 \tag{TPR.23}
\]
It is a nonzero matrix polynomial: \(R\ne0\), \(N\geq2\),
and the coefficient of \(z w^{N-1}\) is \(R/(N-1)!\).
Thus this compressed family does not obey the additive
one-parameter group law. If \(q_*=0\), \(F(z)=I_{V_0}\)
for all \(z\), even though (TPR.15) can still give
nonzero departure from \(V_0\).
The exact composition difference before expansion is
\[
 Q_0e^{(z+w)T}Q_0-Q_0e^{zT}Q_0e^{wT}Q_0
    =Q_0e^{zT}(I-Q_0)e^{wT}Q_0 .
 \tag{TPR.24}
\]
This follows by inserting \(I=Q_0+(I-Q_0)\) between
the two commuting exponentials.

\subsection*{Required edge cases and nonvacuous terms}

The case \(k=1\) has \(V_0=0\). The cases \(m=1\) and
\(N=2\) are the same: \(T=0\), and for even \(k\) the
sole basis vector is invariant with \(F(z)=I\); for odd
\(k\) the invariant space is zero.
For \(k=2,m\geq2\), the invariant indices satisfy
\(\alpha_1+\alpha_2=m-1\). There are exactly \(m\) of
them. Their remaining total capacity is \(m-1<N\), so
\[
 \dim V_0=m,\qquad TQ_0\ne0,\qquad
 Q_0T^NQ_0=0,\qquad F(z)=I_{V_0}.
 \tag{TPR.25}
\]
For \(k=3,m\geq2\), \(d_{\min}=m-2\) and \(q_*=1\).
To exhibit a nonzero return explicitly, take
\[
 \alpha=(m-2,0,0),\qquad
 \nu=(1,m-1,1),\qquad
 \beta=(m-1,m-1,1).
 \tag{TPR.26}
\]
Every coordinate of \(\alpha,\beta\) lies between zero and
\(m-1\), their grades are respectively \(N\) and \(2N\),
and \(|\nu|=N\). The entry of \(Q_0T^NQ_0\) is
\(N!/(m-1)!\), and the entry of \(F(z)\) is
\(z^N/(m-1)!\). In particular \(m=2,N=3,k=3\)
gives \(T^3(1)=6y_1y_2y_3\).

Among \(m\geq2,k\geq2\), the condition \(q_*=0\) holds
exactly for \(k=2\), or for \((m,k)=(2,4)\).
To prove this, for \(N\geq4\) and \(k\geq3\), first
check \(k=3,4\) directly. For \(k\geq5\),
\[
 k-2\left\lceil\frac{k}N\right\rceil
 \geq k-2\left\lceil\frac{k}{3}\right\rceil\geq1;
 \tag{TPR.27}
\]
the last inequality follows by writing \(k=3b+r\),
\(r=0,1,2\), and evaluating the three possibilities
(the minimum \(k\geq5\) gives \(b\geq1\), and \(r=1\)
has \(b\geq2\)). For \(N=3\), direct evaluation gives
\(q_*=1\) at \(k=3\) and \(q_*=0\) at \(k=4\);
the same bound proves positivity at all \(k\geq5\).
For \(k=2\), (TPR.25) already proved \(q_*=0\).
This establishes exactly when the return terms in (TPR.21)
are present, rather than only displaying a possible series.

\subsection*{The full original Taylor unit and the source projector}

Retain every actual Taylor coefficient
\[
 a_j=\frac{g^{(m+j)}(\rho)}{(m+j)!}\quad(0\leq j<m),
       \qquad a_0\ne0,\qquad
 U_y=\prod_{i=1}^k\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}a_jJ_i^j\right),
       \qquad U_s=S^{-1}U_yS .
 \tag{TPR.28}
\]
Every factor is invertible by the finite inverse recursion in
SP.26. The matrices \(U_y\) and \(T\) commute, because all
\(J_i\) commute. The exact period image is
\[
 \Pi_kU_s\Pi_k^{-1}
  =\prod_{i=1}^k
       \left(\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}a_j u^{j/N}K_i^j\right),
 \quad
 K_i=W_kD_{k,0}J_iD_{k,0}^{-1}W_k^{-1},\quad K=\sum_iK_i .
 \tag{TPR.29}
\]
This follows by conjugating each factor and retaining its
complete coefficient list.

For \(\nu\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}^k\), put
\(a_\nu=\prod_i a_{\nu_i}\) and \(J^\nu=\prod_iJ_i^{\nu_i}\).
The full original unit compression and commutator are
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 Q_0U_yQ_0
   =\sum_{\substack{\nu\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}^k\\N\mid|\nu|}}
           a_\nu Q_0J^\nu Q_0,\\
 [Q_0,U_y]
    =\sum_{\nu\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}^k}
           a_\nu J^\nu(Q_{-|\nu|}-Q_0).
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{TPR.30}
\]
The minus subscript is a residue modulo \(N\).
Indeed \(Q_0J^\nu=J^\nu Q_{-|\nu|}\), by its total degree
shift. A particular compressed monomial with \(N\mid|\nu|\)
is nonzero precisely when \(d_{\min}\leq H-|\nu|\):
the allowed starting coordinates have capacities
\(m-1-\nu_i\), and the same capacity proof as (TPR.18)
applies. Its actual coefficient is still \(a_\nu\);
no coefficient beyond \(a_0\) has been assumed nonzero.
Different increments \(\nu\) join different pairs of
multiindices, so this matrix formula also records their
individual coefficients without hidden summation cancellation.

On \(V_0\ne0\), write the first expression as
\(C_U=a_0^k I_{V_0}+B_U\).
Every term of \(B_U\) increases the total degree by at
least \(N\). Hence
\[
 B_U^{q_*+1}=0,\qquad
 C_U^{-1}
   =a_0^{-k}\sum_{j=0}^{q_*}(-a_0^{-k}B_U)^j .
 \tag{TPR.31}
\]
Multiplying the finite geometric series proves the inverse.
It does not identify this inverse with
\((Q_0U_y^{-1}Q_0)|_{V_0}\).
In fact, with \(D_U=(Q_0U_y^{-1}Q_0)|_{V_0}\),
the exact defect obtained from \(U_yU_y^{-1}=I\) is
\[
 I_{V_0}-C_UD_U
      =(Q_0U_y(I-Q_0)U_y^{-1}Q_0)|_{V_0}.
 \tag{TPR.32}
\]
It retains all departing and returning unit terms.
The formula allows the defect to vanish for particular
actual coefficients; it does not assume they are generic.

Finally the original ordered tensor source has the exact jet
map \(x_s\mapsto q_s=U_sx_s\), by tensoring the original
theta/Euler source identities in their fixed order.
The period map on these source-weighted jet coordinates
is therefore \(\Pi_kU_s^{-1}\). Its pulled-back invariant
projector is
\[
 Q_{\theta,0}
   =(\Pi_kU_s^{-1})^{-1}E_0(\Pi_kU_s^{-1})
   =U_sQ_{s,0}U_s^{-1}.
 \tag{TPR.33}
\]
This identifies its image exactly as \(U_sQ_{s,0}V_s\);
it does not replace it by \(Q_{s,0}V_s\).
Because \(U_s\) commutes with \(A_k\), the arithmetic
commutator and its scalar compression transport as
\[
 [Q_{\theta,0},A_k]
     =U_s[Q_{s,0},A_k]U_s^{-1},\qquad
 Q_{\theta,0}A_kQ_{\theta,0}=k\rho Q_{\theta,0}.
 \tag{TPR.34}
\]
Thus the same invariantness classification holds on the
actual source-weighted jet image, with its full Taylor
unit and every original primary nilpotent retained.
There is also an exact coefficient criterion for when this
source projector equals the unweighted one. If \(m\geq2\)
and \(k\geq2\), then
\[
 \begin{split}
 U_yV_0\subseteq V_0
 &\ \Longleftrightarrow\ [Q_0,U_y]=0\\
 &\ \Longleftrightarrow\ Q_{\theta,0}=Q_{s,0}\\
 &\ \Longleftrightarrow\ a_1=\cdots=a_{m-1}=0 .
 \end{split}
 \tag{TPR.35}
\]
To prove the only non-immediate implication, suppose
\(a_j\ne0\) for some \(1\leq j<m\), and choose one
coordinate \(i\). The increment \(\nu=je_i\) in (TPR.28)
has nonzero coefficient \(a_ja_0^{k-1}\).
There exists an invariant starting monomial admitting this
increment: its available capacities have sum \(H-j\),
and \(d_{\min}\leq H-j\). For \(k=2\) this follows from
\(d_{\min}=m-1\) and \(j\leq m-1\).
For \(k\geq3\) it follows from
\(H-j\geq(k-1)(m-1)\geq2m-2\geq m\geq d_{\min}\).
The capacity argument proves the claimed starting
multiindex with its individual coordinate bounds.
Its target has character \(j\ne0\pmod N\).
No other increment contributes to that same matrix entry.
Thus \(U_yV_0\) is not contained in \(V_0\), and
\([Q_0,U_y]\ne0\).
Conversely, vanishing of all the positive coefficients
makes \(U_y=a_0^kI\), and every displayed property holds.
Finally \(Q_{\theta,0}=Q_{s,0}\) is equivalent to commutation
by its exact conjugation formula (TPR.33).
For \(k=1\), \(Q_0=0\); for \(m=1\), the unit is already
scalar. These excluded cases have the corresponding
trivial projector equality.
These calculations are exact complex tensor boundary and
source maps. They do not assert an identification with
arithmetic Frobenius or a conclusion about RH.
