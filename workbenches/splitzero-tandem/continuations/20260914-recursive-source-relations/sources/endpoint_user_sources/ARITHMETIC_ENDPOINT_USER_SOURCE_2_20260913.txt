## 2. The new analytic input is a lower bound after three actual convolutions

The earlier gamma comparison supplied an upper envelope. For the endpoint norm ratio, I constructed a lower envelope for the **unchanged arithmetic density**.

The starting lemma is

$$
\boxed{
\int_{T-1}^{T+1}

\ge c_\zeta(1+|T|)^{-42},
\qquad T\in\mathbb R,
}
$$

for a fixed positive constant \(c_\zeta\). The exponent \(42\) is a sufficient explicit exponent; no optimal local mean-square estimate is claimed.

### The local-mass proof

For \(|T|\ge10\), put

$$
f_T(z)=\zeta(\tfrac12+iT+iz).
$$

On the ellipse

$$
z=\frac12(w+w^{-1}),
\qquad |w|=8,
$$

the functional equation and the vertical-strip gamma estimate give

$$
|f_T(z)|\le M_T=C_0(1+|T|)^6.
$$

The relevant strip is fixed, and the zeta pole lies outside the ellipse. For the part with positive real component, the direct identity

$$
\zeta(s)=\frac{s}{s-1}
-s\int_1^\infty\{x\}x^{-s-1}\,dx
$$

gives the needed polynomial growth; reflection handles the remaining part. These use the classical zeta functional equation and gamma estimates, without RH. ([DLMF][1])

The absolutely convergent reciprocal Dirichlet series gives

$$
|\zeta(2+iT)|\ge\zeta(2)^{-1}.
$$

Let

$$
r_*=\frac{3+\sqrt{13}}2<4.
$$

Then

$$
\frac12\left(-ir_*+\frac1{-ir_*}\right)=-\frac{3i}{2},
$$

so \(f_T\) at this point is \(\zeta(2+iT)\).

Write

$$
m_T=\max_{x\in[-1,1]}|f_T(x)|.
$$

The maximum principle on the annulus \(1\le|w|\le8\), applied through the displayed coordinate map, gives

$$
\zeta(2)^{-1}
\le
m_T^\vartheta M_T^{1-\vartheta},
\qquad
\vartheta
=
1-\frac{\log r_*}{\log8}
>\frac13.
$$

Hence

$$
m_T\ge\zeta(2)^{-3}M_T^{-2}.
$$

Cauchy’s estimate also gives

$$
\sup_{[-1,1]}|f_T'|\le M_T.
$$

Around a point attaining \(m_T\), there is a one-sided interval of length \(m_T/(2M_T)\) on which \(|f_T|\ge m_T/2\). Therefore

$$
\int_{T-1}^{T+1}|\zeta(\tfrac12+it)|^2\,dt
\ge
\frac{m_T^3}{8M_T}
\ge
\frac{\zeta(2)^{-9}}{8C_0^7}(1+|T|)^{-42}.
$$

On the remaining compact range of \(T\), the integral is continuous and strictly positive. Its minimum supplies the remaining positive constant.

### Carry the bound through \(g/h\)

The original gamma factor and the elementary polynomial bound for \(h\) imply

$$

\ge
c_h^{(0)}
e^{-\pi|t|/2}(1+|t|)^{-2\deg h}

e^{-\pi|T|/2}(1+|T|)^{-B_h},
\qquad
B_h=42+2\deg h.

$$

Now \(w_h\) is continuous, integrable, and positive outside a discrete set. Thus \(w_h*w_h\) is continuous and strictly positive everywhere. Define the actual compact quantities

$$
b_h=\min_{|v|\le1}(w_h*w_h)(v)>0,
\qquad
\vartheta_h=\int_{-1}^{1}w_h(v)\,dv>0.

Restricting a nonnegative convolution integral gives

\ge

\qquad k\ge3.

$$

**The original mass has not been changed.** The complementary integration regions remain part of \(m_{h,k}\); this is a lower bound obtained from one positive contribution to that same density.

## 3. This proves the required endpoint norm bound

Fix \(0<b<\pi/2\), and retain the two original Laplace values

For the lower estimate, the exact least squared norm of a monic polynomial of degree \(n\) on \([-L,L]\) is

$$

=
\frac{2L^{2n+1}}{2n+1}
\left(\frac{2^n(n!)^2}{(2n)!}\right)^2.
}
$$

This follows from the leading coefficient and orthogonality of the Legendre polynomial. Under \(S=k/2+iu\), the retained leading phase is \(i^n\), whose squared modulus is one. ([DLMF][2])

Applying the lower convolution envelope on \([-L,L]\) yields

\ge
c_h\vartheta_h^{\,k-3}

\right]^{1/(2n)}.
\end{aligned}

$$

For \(n\ge k\ge3\), this is at most \(C_hn\).

The degree dependence follows directly from

$$
\binom{2n}{n}\le4^n,
\qquad
(4n)!\le(4n)^{4n},
\qquad
n+k-3\le2n.
$$

The factorial and monic-norm factors contribute \(O(n)\) after taking the \(2n\)-th root. The retained mass factors contribute bounded factors of the form \(\exp(O_h(k/n))\). The polynomial lower-envelope factor contributes

$$
(2n)^{B_h/(2n)},
$$

which is uniformly bounded.

Equation (36) of the note gives a literal sufficient \(C_h\), retaining all these constants. It is deliberately not optimized or presented as a numerically certified constant.

**This establishes a bound on the actual arithmetic norm sequence, uniformly in the required tensor and polynomial degrees.**
