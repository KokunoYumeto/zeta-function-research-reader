\subsection{Inputs and exact maps}\label{inputs-and-exact-maps}

Let g=2xi, k\textgreater=1, c=k/2, and retain

\[
w_h(t)=\frac{|(g/h)(1/2+it)|^2}{2\pi},\qquad
m(u)=w_h^{*k}(u),\qquad d\nu(u)=m(u)\,du,\qquad S(u)=c+iu.
\tag{CR1}
\]

HD1--4 prove for this actual measure that, for any fixed 0\textless b\textless pi/2, \(\int e^{b|u|}d\nu(u)<\infty\). The nonzero density is positive almost everywhere, and nu has no atoms. Its total mass remains \(\mu_h^k\). Fix the original nonzero monic cyclic polynomial \(\chi=\chi_{h,k}\), with degree q\textgreater=1 and every primary multiplicity retained. Define

\[
H=L^2(\mathbb R,d\nu),\quad H_\chi=L^2(\mathbb R,|\chi(S(u))|^2d\nu),
\quad E=\mathbb C[S]/(\chi),\quad J:\mathbb C[S]\longrightarrow E.
\tag{CR2}
\]

For N\textgreater=q-1, let \(\mathcal P_N=\mathbb C[S]_{\le N}\), \(\mathcal B_N=\chi\mathcal P_{N-q}\), with \(\mathcal P_{-1}=0\). The source maps, their kernels and their arithmetic class maps are exactly

\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal P_{N-q}\xrightarrow{M_\chi}
\mathcal P_N\xrightarrow{J_N}E\longrightarrow0.
\tag{CR3}
\]

No coefficient of chi or of the polynomial source has been rescaled. The original source norm is the norm of H through the specified Mellin observation. In particular all conclusions transport back through that original isometry, rather than selecting another Hilbert norm.

\subsection{Polynomial density, with its measure proof}\label{polynomial-density-with-its-measure-proof}

We first prove the exact density fact used twice below. Let eta be any of the two finite measures nu or \(|\chi(S)|^2\nu\). Both have a positive exponential moment. For the second, polynomial growth gives \(|\chi(c+iu)|^2\le C(1+|u|)^{2q}\); choosing 0\textless a\textless b absorbs this factor into \(e^{(b-a)|u|}\), so \(\int e^{a|u|}d\eta<\infty\). This bound is a consequence for the fixed original measure, not a new source hypothesis. All polynomial moments consequently exist.

If f in L2(eta) is orthogonal to all polynomials in S, it is orthogonal to all polynomials in u because \(u=(S-c)/i\) is the exact inverse coordinate. Set \(d\sigma=\overline f\,d\eta\). Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
\int e^{a|u|/2}\,d|\sigma|(u)
\le \|f\|_{L^2(\eta)}\left(\int e^{a|u|}d\eta(u)\right)^{1/2}<\infty.
\tag{CR4}
\]

Thus \(F(z)=\int e^{izu}d\sigma(u)\) is holomorphic for \(|\operatorname{Im}z|<a/2\). On each smaller closed strip, powers of \textbar u\textbar{} are absorbed by the remaining exponential margin, justifying every derivative under the integral. All derivatives at zero vanish by the orthogonality equations. Its Taylor series and the identity theorem give F=0 on the connected strip, including the real axis.

Here is the required uniqueness step explicitly. For t\textgreater0 put \(\gamma_t(x)=(4\pi t)^{-1/2}e^{-x^2/(4t)}\). Its Fourier integral is \(\gamma_t(x)=(2\pi)^{-1}\int e^{-t\zeta^2}e^{-i\zeta x}d\zeta\). Fubini, justified by the finite variation of sigma and the integrable Gaussian factor, gives \(\sigma*\gamma_t=0\). For every continuous compactly supported phi, \(\phi*\gamma_t\to\phi\) uniformly: uniform continuity controls \textbar y\textbar\textless epsilon, and the Gaussian tail controls its complement. Integrating against sigma gives \(\int\phi\,d\sigma=0\). Uniqueness of finite regular measures on continuous compactly supported test functions gives sigma=0. Hence f=0 eta-almost everywhere. The orthogonal complement of the polynomial span is zero, proving

\[
\overline{\mathbb C[S]}^{H}=H,\qquad
\overline{\mathbb C[S]}^{H_\chi}=H_\chi.
\tag{CR5}
\]

\subsection{The original relation multiplication is an onto isometry}\label{the-original-relation-multiplication-is-an-onto-isometry}

The map and its inverse on equivalence classes are

\[
U_\chi:H_\chi\longrightarrow H,\quad f\longmapsto\chi(S)f,
\qquad
U_\chi^{-1}v(u)=\frac{v(u)}{\chi(S(u))}\quad(\chi(S(u))\ne0).
\tag{CR6}
\]

At the finitely many real u where chi(S(u))=0, choose the inverse value zero. These points have nu-measure zero, so the choice does not affect either Hilbert equivalence class. Direct integration proves

\[
\|U_\chi f\|_H^2=\int|f|^2|\chi(S)|^2d\nu=\|f\|_{H_\chi}^2,
\quad
\|U_\chi^{-1}v\|_{H_\chi}^2=\|v\|_H^2.
\tag{CR7}
\]

The two composites are identities almost everywhere. Polynomial density in H\_chi and this actual multiplier therefore give

\[
\boxed{\overline{\chi\mathbb C[S]}^{H}=H,
\qquad H/\overline{\chi\mathbb C[S]}=0.}
\tag{CR8}
\]

The coordinate-level comparison from the original algebraic quotient is \([P]_\chi\mapsto P+\overline{\chi\mathbb C[S]}=0\), with kernel all of E. This identifies the map, not the two quotients. More precisely, complete both terms of \([\chi\mathbb C[S]\hookrightarrow\mathbb C[S]]\) in their inherited H norms. Both completions identify with H by CR5/CR8, and the extended inclusion is I\_H. The explicit degree-minus-one contraction of \([H\xrightarrow{I_H}H]\) is I\_H. Before completion, the cohomology is the full algebraic E; its induced map to this completed cohomology is zero with the stated kernel.

\subsection{Limit of the same finite canonical metric}\label{limit-of-the-same-finite-canonical-metric}

Use the fixed increasing-power remainder section \(s:E\to\mathcal P_{q-1}\), the inverse of J on that finite subspace. Let Pi\_N be the H-orthogonal projection onto the finite original relation subspace B\_N. The exact least-norm lift and quotient Gram are

\[
R_N=(I-\Pi_N)s,\qquad J_NR_N=I_E,\qquad
G_N=R_N^*R_N.
\tag{CR9}
\]

To verify minimality directly, every lift of x is s(x)+b with b in B\_N. The decomposition into B\_N and its orthogonal complement shows that the unique minimizing lift is R\_N x. The adjoints in the displayed Gram use the fixed original coefficient frame on E and the actual source H norm. If M\_N is the original source Gram, the equivalent coordinate formulas are

\[
G_N=(J_NM_N^{-1}J_N^*)^{-1},\qquad
R_N=M_N^{-1}J_N^*G_N.
\tag{CR10}
\]

Their equality with CR9 follows from J\_NR\_N=I and source orthogonality to ker J\_N; the minimizer is unique. Every finite G\_N is positive definite.

The nested B\_N have dense union by CR8. For any v in H and epsilon\textgreater0, choose b in some B\_n with \(\|v-b\|<\epsilon\). For N\textgreater=n, \(\|(I-\Pi_N)v\|\le\|v-b\|<\epsilon\). Thus Pi\_N converges strongly to I\_H. Applying this to each of the q fixed columns of s proves

\[
\boxed{G_N\downarrow0,\qquad V_N=\det G_N\longrightarrow0
\quad(N\longrightarrow\infty;\ h,k\text{ fixed}).}
\tag{CR11}
\]

The decrease is Loewner decrease, because enlarging the admitted relation space cannot increase a minimum. Convergence is in every fixed finite coordinate matrix norm: each column norm of R\_N tends to zero, and \(|(G_N)_{ij}|\le\|R_Ne_i\|\|R_Ne_j\|\). Determinant convergence then follows from its polynomial expression. This proves no growth rate.

For each nonzero x in E, the literal sequence R\_Nx tends to zero in H but has J(R\_Nx)=x for every N. Therefore the densely defined map \(J:\mathbb C[S]\subset H\to E\), with any fixed positive finite norm on its original coefficient target, is not closable. Every (0,x) lies in the closure of its graph. This is stronger than one evaluation witness, and records exactly why the algebraic class map has no continuous extension to this completed Hilbert quotient. It does not make J zero on its original polynomial domain.

In fact the graph closure is all of H times E. Given (v,y), choose polynomials p\_j tending to v by CR5, and put x\_j=y-Jp\_j. For each fixed j, CR9--11 allow a cutoff N\_j with \(\|R_{N_j}x_j\|_H<1/j\). Then the polynomial \(p_j+R_{N_j}x_j\) tends to v and its exact J value is y. This proves the full graph assertion without a bound uniform in j or an extension of J to H.

Under any fixed coefficient change x=Ux', the matrices and source lifts transform by \(G_N'=U^*G_NU\), \(R_N'=R_NU\), and the identity on classes transforms by \(J_N'=U^{-1}J_N\). Hence CR9--11 retain precisely the same morphisms in another coordinate frame; no N-dependent frame or metric normalization is used.

\subsection{The phase quotient and averaging comparison}\label{the-phase-quotient-and-averaging-comparison}

Fix the original circumference L. No assertion that every k=1 phase source Gram is invertible is needed. Write T\_theta for the original polynomial evaluation into its positive-weight sampled Hilbert space H\_theta. The precise finite receiving space and comparison map are

\[
Q_{N,\theta}=T_\theta(\mathcal P_N)/T_\theta(\mathcal B_N),\qquad
\rho_{N,\theta}:E\longrightarrow Q_{N,\theta},\quad
[P]_\chi\longmapsto[T_\theta P],\quad
\ker\rho_{N,\theta}=J_N(\ker(T_\theta|_{\mathcal P_N})).
\tag{CR12a}
\]

Both images are finite-dimensional closed subspaces of H\_theta and carry its actual norm. The map is onto. It is well defined because any two degree-N lifts differ by B\_N. If T\_theta P lies in T\_theta B\_N, subtract the corresponding b in B\_N to obtain an element of ker T\_theta with the same J\_N class; this proves the displayed kernel in both directions. Its pulled-back squared norm is the uniquely specified positive semidefinite coefficient form

\[
x^*G_{N,\theta}x
=\inf_{b\in\mathcal B_N}\|T_\theta(sx+b)\|_{H_\theta}^2.
\tag{CR12b}
\]

This is the same canonical quotient form: when the phase source Gram is positive, rho\_(N,theta) is an isomorphism and CR12b gives exactly the positive Gram \((J_NM_{N,\theta}^{-1}J_N^*)^{-1}\). When it is singular, CR12a states the actual additional kernel and CR12b uses no inverse. No zero-weight coordinate is inverted and no new metric is selected.

The form is measurable in theta: for each x its infimum may be taken over a countable dense set of relation coefficients, since its finite quadratic norm is continuous in those coefficients. Each such value is measurable from the original sampled measure. Polarization gives the matrix entries. Using the particular lift R\_N x in CR12b and the original all-phase source identity proves

\[
\int x^*G_{N,\theta}x\,\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}
\le\int\|T_\theta R_Nx\|_{H_\theta}^2\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}
=\|R_Nx\|_H^2=x^*G_Nx.
\tag{CR12c}
\]

In particular these integrals are finite. Define the actual mean form \(\bar G_N=\int G_{N,\theta}d\theta/(2\pi)\) and its difference \(D_N=G_N-\bar G_N\). Then, for all original k\textgreater=1,

\[
G_N=\bar G_N+D_N,\qquad D_N\succeq0,
\qquad 0\preceq\bar G_N\preceq G_N.
\tag{CR12}
\]

On an admitted positive phase family, D\_N agrees with the complete relation-gap formula HCD13, including its phasewise least-norm sections; CR12a--c extend the comparison through the specified kernels without asserting those inverse formulas at singular phases.

Consequently both \(\bar G_N\to0\) and \(D_N\to0\) in fixed coefficient norm, without interchanging any infinite-dimensional inverse and integral. The relative matrix \(G_N^{-1}D_N\) is not assigned a zero limit by this assertion: only the preceding absolute inequalities have been proved. The actual relative transfer remains the previously calculated metric/generator commutator, not this unscaled matrix limit.

The independent sampled density argument P53--58/CQ14--22 gives, at each fixed phase, the exact limit

\[
G_{N,\theta}\downarrow K_\theta
=J_{Z_\theta}^*\operatorname{diag}(\nu_{n,\theta})J_{Z_\theta},
\quad Z_\theta=\{n:\nu_{n,\theta}>0,\ \chi(S_{n,\theta})=0\},
\tag{CR13}
\]

where \(S_{n,\theta}=c+i(2\pi n+\theta)/L\) and \(\nu_{n,\theta}=(2\pi/L)m((2\pi n+\theta)/L)\). Its exact kernel is \((p_\theta)/(\chi)\), with p\_theta the squarefree product of the displayed sampled roots. Both full primary multiplicities and all masses remain in the domain/map. The target takes only the stated scalar values.

If Z\_theta is nonempty, choose an original class x whose evaluation at one of those roots is one (the class of 1 already suffices). Its canonical single-phase/source relative cost satisfies the explicit inequality

\[
\frac{x^*G_{N,\theta}x}{x^*G_Nx}
\ge\frac{\nu_{n,\theta}}{x^*G_Nx}\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{CR14}
\]

This strengthens HD's full-source evaluation bound to this precise quotient comparison. It needs an actual positive-mass sampled root; none is invented. For k=1 full-order cancellation supplies positivity at any selected sampled root; for k\textgreater=2 the original convolution is positive everywhere. If Z\_theta is empty, CR13 has zero limit but CR14 makes no relative-rate assertion.

For the fixed chi, the exceptional phases are finite. The completed phase quotient field is therefore zero almost everywhere for its actual phase measure, and its direct integral is zero. The exact source isometry over all phases, the finite quotient identity J\_NR\_N=I, CR11, CR12 and the exceptional positive quotient limit CR13 are all compatible through their displayed maps. No arithmetic class is declared absent before the specified completion. A retained split label is transported as \((\lambda,v)\mapsto(\lambda,fv)\); the zero comparison takes it to \((\lambda,0)\), fixes tau, and does not identify that receiving zero with external absence.
