\subsection{1. Fixed scalar support and the source

\[
S=G(\mathbb C)=\mathbb C\sqcup\{\tau\},\qquad e=0_{\mathbb C}\in S,\qquad 0_S=\tau.

Chapter 14 supplies the equivalence between \(S\)-semimodules and linear

\(\rho_{l,m}:W_l\to W_m\) coherent for \(l\le m\). Its carrier is

\[
x\boxplus y=\rho_{l,l\vee m}x+\rho_{m,l\vee m}y,\quad ex=0_{W_l},\quad \tau x=0_{W_{0_L}}.

All constructions below act on every \(W_l\) and every transport. No

\subsection{2. Test spaces, Fourier transform and
summation}\label{test-spaces-fourier-transform-and-summation}

Put \(D=-x\partial_x\) and fix

\[
\widehat\phi(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R}\phi(x)e^{-2\pi i x\xi}\,dx.
\]

The overlap test space is the Frechet space

\[
\mathscr B=\{F\in C^\infty(\mathbb R_{>0}):\sup_{x>0}x^b|D^kF(x)|<\infty\ \text{for every }b\in\mathbb Z,\ k\ge0\}.
\]

Its Mellin transform is

\[
\mathcal MF(s)=\int_0^\infty F(x)x^s\,\frac{dx}{x}.
\]

It is entire. Its derivatives are obtained by inserting \((\log x)^j\);
the defining seminorms give absolute locally uniform domination.
Integration by parts gives \(\mathcal M(DF)=s\mathcal MF\) and rapid
decay on vertical strips after any fixed finite number of
differentiations.

\subsubsection{2.1 Rational sector and the two boundary
observations}\label{rational-sector-and-the-two-boundary-observations}

Use

\[
\mathscr V_0=\{\phi\in\mathscr S(\mathbb R):\phi(-x)=\phi(x),\ \phi(0)=0,\ \widehat\phi(0)=0\},
\qquad E_0\phi(x)=\sum_{n\ge1}\phi(nx).
\]

Fourier transformation preserves \(\mathscr V_0\) and squares to the
identity there. Poisson summation gives the literal equality

\[
E_0\phi(x)=x^{-1}E_0\widehat\phi(1/x). \tag{2.1}
\]

For \(x\ge1\), differentiating the sum and using a Schwartz bound of
order \(N>1\) gives a bound by a constant times \(x^{-N}\sum n^{-N}\).
Equation (2.1), with the chain and product rules, gives arbitrarily high
positive powers at zero. Thus \(E_0:\mathscr V_0\to\mathscr B\) is
continuous.

The source's map is \(\mathcal E\phi=x^{1/2}E_0\phi\). The exact
comparison is

\[
W_{1/2}:\mathscr B\to\mathscr B,\quad F(x)\mapsto x^{1/2}F(x),
\quad
\mathcal F_\mu W_{1/2}F(z)=\mathcal MF(1/2-iz). \tag{2.2}
\]

No half-density factor or exponent sign is removed.

At the adelic level, for
\(\Phi=\phi\otimes\mathbf1_{\widehat{\mathbb Z}}\) and the idele with
positive real component \(x\) and all finite components one,

\[
\sum_{q\in\mathbb Q^\times}\Phi(qx,q_f)=2E_0\phi(x). \tag{2.3}
\]

The finite condition selects \(q\in\mathbb Z\); parity accounts for the
displayed factor two.

The current draft's framed arithmetic input makes (2.3) a section-count
observation. For an idele \(a=(a_f,a_\infty)\) set

\[
L_a=\{r\in\mathbb Q:a_fr\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}\},\qquad
h_a(r)=a_\infty r,\qquad
c_a=\prod_p p^{-v_p(a_p)},\quad \lambda_a=|a_\infty|c_a.
\]

Then \(L_a=c_a\mathbb Z\). Its bounded-section count is

\[
N_a(R)=\#\{r\in L_a:|h_a(r)|\le R\}=1+2\lfloor R/\lambda_a\rfloor.
\]

For an even Schwartz test \(\phi\), the exact weighted observation is

\[
\int_{(0,\infty)}\phi(tr)\,d(N_a(r)-1)
=\sum_{v\in L_a\setminus\{0\}}\phi(th_a(v))
=2E_0\phi(t\lambda_a).
\]

For \(q\in\mathbb Q^\times\), the map \(L_{qa}\to L_a\),
\(r\mapsto qr\), intertwines \(h_{qa}\) and \(h_a\); its split extension
fixes external absence. Thus the observation is compatible with the
actual principal-action arrows, not an unrelated theta function adjoined
afterward. This framed-module construction and its sheaf are source
inputs from the live appendix, not newly asserted here.

The exact boundary sequence is

\[
0\to\mathscr V_0\to\mathscr S(\mathbb R)^{\mathrm{ev}}
\xrightarrow{\partial}\mathbb C^2\to0,
\qquad \partial\phi=(\phi(0),\widehat\phi(0)). \tag{2.4}
\]

Surjectivity follows from \(g(x)=e^{-\pi x^2}\) and \(x^2g(x)\): their
images are \((1,1)\) and \((0,1/(2\pi))\). Dilation
\(\phi(x)\mapsto\phi(ax)\) acts on the boundary by
\((u,v)\mapsto(u,a^{-1}v)\); Fourier transformation swaps the two
coordinates. These are the retained pole modes at spectral parameters
zero and one.

\subsubsection{2.2 Every primitive Dirichlet
character}\label{every-primitive-dirichlet-character}

Let \(\chi\) be a primitive nonprincipal character modulo \(q>1\),
extended by zero on nonunits, and put \(\chi(-1)=(-1)^\epsilon\),
\(\epsilon\in\{0,1\}\). Set

\[
\mathscr V_\chi=\{\phi\in\mathscr S(\mathbb R):\phi(-x)=(-1)^\epsilon\phi(x)\},
\quad E_\chi\phi(x)=\sum_{n\ge1}\chi(n)\phi(nx).
\]

Retain \(\mathfrak g(\chi)=\sum_{a\bmod q}\chi(a)e^{2\pi i a/q}\).
Twisted Poisson summation is

\[
E_\chi\phi(x)=\frac{\mathfrak g(\chi)}{qx}
E_{\bar\chi}\widehat\phi\!\left(\frac1{qx}\right). \tag{2.5}
\]

To derive it, split the sum over all integers into residues
\(a\bmod q\), apply Poisson summation to each arithmetic progression,
and use \(\sum_a\chi(a)e^{2\pi i ma/q}=\bar\chi(m)\mathfrak g(\chi)\).
Parity makes each full sum twice its positive-index half; the factors
two cancel. The \(m=0\) Fourier coefficient is zero because \(\chi\) is
nonprincipal. Equation (2.5) proves \(E_\chi\phi\in\mathscr B\) exactly
as in the rational sector.

For completeness, the finite Gauss identity used here holds also for
nonunit \(m\). If \(d=\gcd(m,q)>1\), primitivity supplies a unit
\(u\equiv1\pmod{q/d}\) with \(\chi(u)\ne1\); invariance under
\(a\mapsto ua\) forces that Fourier sum to be zero. For unit \(m\),
substitution gives the stated \(\bar\chi(m)\) factor. Parseval for the
finite Fourier matrix then gives \(|\mathfrak g(\chi)|^2=q\), since
exactly \(\varphi(q)\) of these Fourier sums are nonzero and
\(\sum_a|\chi(a)|^2=\varphi(q)\).

Define

\[
J_qF(x)=\frac1{\sqrt q\,x}F\!\left(\frac1{qx}\right),\qquad J_q^2=1,
\]

and

\[
r_+=E_\chi,\qquad r_- =\frac{\mathfrak g(\chi)}{\sqrt q}J_qE_{\bar\chi}.
\]

Then \(r_-\widehat\phi=E_\chi\phi\), so \(r_-=E_\chi\mathcal F^{-1}\).
For the rational sector use \(q=1\), \(\mathfrak g=1\), \(r_-=J_1E_0\).

Every \(E_\chi\) here is injective: on \(\Re s>1\), absolute convergence
gives

\[
\mathcal M(E_\chi\phi)(s)=L(s,\chi)\mathcal M\phi(s). \tag{2.6}
\]

The Euler product is nonzero there. Vanishing of \(E_\chi\phi\)
therefore makes \(\mathcal M\phi\) zero on a vertical line, and Fourier
uniqueness in \(\log x\) gives \(\phi=0\). The rational case uses
\(L(s,1)=\zeta(s)\).

\subsection{3. A computed sheaf comparison, not a free spectral
shift}\label{a-computed-sheaf-comparison-not-a-free-spectral-shift}

Let \(X_\theta=\{0,\eta,\infty\}\) with opens \(\varnothing\),
\(\{\eta\}\), \(U_+=\{0,\eta\}\), \(U_-=\{\eta,\infty\}\), and
\(X_\theta\). This is an auxiliary two-chart space. The labels
\(0,\infty\) here identify charts; the scalar \(\tau\) is not being
substituted for either label.

Define a sheaf \(\mathcal T_\chi\) of complex vector spaces by

\[
\mathcal T_\chi(U_+)=\mathscr V_\chi,
\quad\mathcal T_\chi(U_-)=\mathscr V_{\bar\chi},
\quad\mathcal T_\chi(\{\eta\})=\mathscr B,
\]

with restrictions \(r_+,r_-\). On the full space its sections are
compatible pairs. This is a sheaf by its explicit gluing rule.

Sections over each of the three proper nonempty opens are evaluation at
a least point and are exact for sheaves of vector spaces on that open.
Consequently the two-open Cech complex computes sheaf cohomology:

\[
C_\chi^\bullet:
\mathscr V_\chi\oplus\mathscr V_{\bar\chi}
\xrightarrow{d_\chi}\mathscr B,
\quad d_\chi(\phi,\psi)=E_\chi\phi-r_-\psi
=E_\chi(\phi-\mathcal F^{-1}\psi). \tag{3.1}
\]

It follows that

\[
H^0(X_\theta,\mathcal T_\chi)=\{(\phi,\widehat\phi):\phi\in\mathscr V_\chi\},
\quad H^1(X_\theta,\mathcal T_\chi)=Q_\chi:=\mathscr B/E_\chi\mathscr V_\chi. \tag{3.2}
\]

Let \(j:U_+\hookrightarrow X_\theta\). The extension by zero
\(j_!j^*\mathcal T_\chi\) has values \(\mathscr V_\chi\) on \(U_+\),
zero on \(U_-\), and \(\mathscr B\) on their intersection. Its complex
is

\[
C_{+,\chi}^\bullet=[\mathscr V_\chi\xrightarrow{E_\chi}\mathscr B].
\]

The actual map \(j_!j^*\mathcal T_\chi\to\mathcal T_\chi\) is

\[
c^0(\phi)=(\phi,0),\qquad c^1(F)=F. \tag{3.3}
\]

\textbf{Theorem 3.1.} The induced map

\[
H^1(X_\theta,j_!j^*\mathcal T_\chi)\xrightarrow{c^1}H^1(X_\theta,\mathcal T_\chi)
\]

is the identity of \(Q_\chi\) and is therefore an isomorphism.

\textbf{Proof.} Equation (2.5) identifies the two restriction images, so
both cokernels equal \(\mathscr B/E_\chi\mathscr V_\chi\). More
explicitly, the change of chain coordinates
\((\phi,\psi)\mapsto(\phi-\mathcal F^{-1}\psi,\psi)\) writes
\(C_\chi^\bullet\) as \(C_{+,\chi}^\bullet\) plus
\(\mathscr V_{\bar\chi}\) in degree zero. This also proves the assertion
without any closure assumption. \(\square\)

This is extension-by-zero cohomology relative to the specified
compactification \(X_\theta\). It is not asserted to be the ordinary
compact-support cohomology of the real half-line.

The Frechet topology on \(\mathscr B\) also defines the Hausdorff
quotient

\[
Q_\chi\twoheadrightarrow Q_\chi^{\mathrm{H}}:=\mathscr B/\overline{E_\chi\mathscr V_\chi}.
\]

All finite spectral observations below factor through both quotients. No
equality of these two quotients or with the chapter-25 unspecified
topology is assumed.

The current draft uses \(\Theta=2E_0\) and seminorms
\(\sup_{x>0}(x^N+x^{-N})|(x\partial_x)^jF(x)|\). These define precisely
the same Frechet space \(\mathscr B\): each of its two summands is a
defining seminorm above, and for \(|b|\le N\) one has
\(x^b\le x^N+x^{-N}\). Since \(2E_0\mathscr V_0=E_0\mathscr V_0\), their
closed images also agree. The chain map

\[
[\mathscr V_0\xrightarrow{E_0}\mathscr B]
\xrightarrow{(\frac12\operatorname{id},\operatorname{id})}
[\mathscr V_0\xrightarrow{\Theta}\mathscr B]
\]

is an isomorphism, with inverse
\((2\operatorname{id},\operatorname{id})\). It induces the identity from
\(Q_0^{\mathrm H}\) to the actual \(Q_\Theta\) in that appendix. This
identifies our target topology with a specified workbench quotient, not
with the source's unnamed Hadamard topology.

\subsubsection{3.2 Dilation and the generator on this

\subsection{4. Lift this same comparison through every supported
fibre}\label{lift-this-same-comparison-through-every-supported-fibre}

For an input diagram \((L,W,\rho)\), define

\[
\mathcal T_{\chi,l}=W_l\otimes_{\mathbb C}\mathcal T_\chi,
\qquad \rho_{l,m}\otimes1:\mathcal T_{\chi,l}\to\mathcal T_{\chi,m}.
\]

This defines a functor from linear join diagrams to diagrams of sheaves
on \(X_\theta\). Over a field, tensoring by \(W_l\) is exact, so

\[
H^1(X_\theta,\mathcal T_{\chi,l})=W_l\otimes Q_\chi,
\quad H^1(c_l)=1_{W_l}\otimes1_{Q_\chi}. \tag{4.1}
\]

Reconstruct the supported carrier

\[
\widetilde H^1_\chi(M)=\bigsqcup_{l\in L}(W_l\otimes Q_\chi).
\]

The original join transports define its addition. The one scalar \(e\)
sends an element in label \(l\) to \(0_{W_l\otimes Q_\chi}\); \(\tau\)
sends it to the least-label origin. Higher labels have not been
identified. An arbitrary diagram morphism \((\varphi,f_l)\) is carried
to the maps \(f_l\otimes1\), and the original naturality squares remain
commutative.

For the two chart coefficients themselves the supported differential is

\[
\widetilde d(a,b)=\widetilde E_\chi(a)\oplus(-1)\widetilde r_-(b).
\]

Here each tilde is the linear-map extension sending external absence to
external absence. Both inputs absent gives \(\tau\). An active pair with
\(\phi=\mathcal F^{-1}\psi\) gives the supported zero of \(\mathscr B\).
The zero-amplitude fibre therefore retains the chart mask and the full
compatible pair.

The construction covers every finite cyclic-degree coefficient fibre
\(W_l\), and every compatible tower of such fibres, by the same formula
(4.1). It imposes no identification between the index of a character, a
dimension, and a support label.

\subsection{5. The spectral factor is obtained from an actual test
function}\label{the-spectral-factor-is-obtained-from-an-actual-test-function}

Start on the open strip \(\Omega=\{s:0<\Re s<1\}\), and then extend the
following multiplier maps to the whole plane as proved in section 5.3.
In that section \(\mathcal O=\mathcal O_{\mathbb C}\). The strip
restriction is available by pullback throughout.

\subsubsection{5.1 Rational generator}\label{rational-generator}

For \(g(x)=e^{-\pi x^2}\) put

\[
\phi_0(x)=(4\pi^2x^4-6\pi x^2)e^{-\pi x^2}.
\]

This is \(D(D-1)g\). It is even, has value zero at the origin, and has
zero integral, so it belongs to \(\mathscr V_0\). Mellin integration
gives

\[
\mathcal M\phi_0(s)=\frac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2).
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{\mathcal M(E_0\phi_0)(s)=\xi(s),\quad
\xi(s)=\tfrac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s).} \tag{5.1}
\]

This is an equality of entire functions, proved first on \(\Re s>1\) by
absolute convergence and then by analytic continuation. The Gaussian
Hermite-image result in chapter 25 is the source precedent; (5.1) is
used here to compute the sheaf image, not claimed as a new identity.

\subsubsection{5.2 Every primitive nonprincipal
character}\label{every-primitive-nonprincipal-character}

Retain the standard completed function

\[
\Lambda_\chi(s)=\left(\frac q\pi\right)^{(s+\epsilon)/2}
\Gamma\!\left(\frac{s+\epsilon}{2}\right)L(s,\chi).
\]

Take the actual Schwartz function

\[
\phi_\chi(x)=2x^\epsilon e^{-\pi x^2/q}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\mathcal M(E_\chi\phi_\chi)(s)=\Lambda_\chi(s).} \tag{5.2}
\]

The integral is \(\int_0^\infty2x^{s+\epsilon-1}e^{-\pi x^2/q}dx\).
Substituting \(u=\pi x^2/q\) gives exactly the displayed conductor and
gamma factors. No local factors at divisors of \(q\) are reinserted:
\(\chi(n)=0\) there is part of the primitive L-function.

Write \(\mathscr L_0=\xi\), \(\mathscr L_\chi=\Lambda_\chi\), and
\(m_\chi=\mathcal M\phi_\chi\). Each \(m_\chi\) is holomorphic and
nowhere zero on \(\Omega\).

\subsubsection{5.3 The sheaf map and its calculated
cokernel}\label{the-sheaf-map-and-its-calculated-cokernel}

The quotient \(\mathcal M\phi/m_\chi\) extends to an entire function.
Here is the needed continuation, retaining the apparent poles. For a
parity-\(\epsilon\) Schwartz function, Taylor subtraction at the real
origin continues \(\mathcal M\phi\) meromorphically, with possible
simple poles at \(s=-\epsilon-2j\). The simple zeros of
\(1/\Gamma((s+\epsilon)/2)\) cancel exactly those possible poles. In the
rational moment-zero space, the value condition removes the pole at
\(s=0\), while \(\int_0^\infty\phi=0\) gives \(\mathcal M\phi(1)=0\). In

\[
\frac{\mathcal M\phi}{m_0}
=\frac{2\pi^{s/2}\mathcal M\phi(s)}{s(s-1)\Gamma(s/2)},
\]

the simple zero of \(1/\Gamma(s/2)\) cancels the explicit denominator
\(s\); the moment condition cancels \(s-1\). For nonprincipal
characters, the reciprocal gamma factor alone handles all possible
poles, and the conductor exponential is entire and nonvanishing. This
proves the full-plane extension and agrees with the multiplier
calculation in the live arithmetic appendix. It does not divide by
\(\xi\) or \(\Lambda_\chi\) at a zero.

There is a commutative square of \(\mathcal O\)-module sheaves

\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
\mathcal O\otimes_{\mathbb C}\mathscr V_\chi&\xrightarrow{1\otimes E_\chi}&\mathcal O\otimes_{\mathbb C}\mathscr B\\
\alpha_\chi\downarrow&&\downarrow\beta\\
\mathcal O&\xrightarrow{\times\mathscr L_\chi}&\mathcal O,
\end{array} \tag{5.3}
\]

where

\[
\alpha_\chi(h\otimes\phi)=h\frac{\mathcal M\phi}{m_\chi},
\qquad\beta(h\otimes F)=h\mathcal MF.
\]

Equation (2.6) proves commutativity. The left map is surjective because
\(\alpha_\chi(1\otimes\phi_\chi)=1\). The right map is locally
surjective. Choose a nonnegative, nonzero
\(\psi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)\) and set \(F(e^y)=e^{-s_0y}\psi(y)\).
Then \(\mathcal MF(s_0)=\int\psi(y)dy>0\), so its Mellin germ is locally
invertible. No unit-integral choice is required.

\textbf{Theorem 5.1 (actual analytic presentation).} The image sheaf of
the composite \(\mathcal M E_\chi\) is exactly \((\mathscr L_\chi)\).
Therefore its presentation cokernel is

\[
\boxed{\mathcal Q_\chi=\mathcal O/(\mathscr L_\chi).} \tag{5.4}
\]

No zero locations are inserted in constructing the map. They are the
support of its calculated cokernel.

The analytic realization of the cohomology is the surjection

\[
\mathcal O\otimes Q_\chi\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal Q_\chi,
\qquad h\otimes[F]\mapsto[h\mathcal MF]. \tag{5.5}
\]

Its kernel is explicitly

\[
\boxed{\ker\beta\,/\,(1\otimes E_\chi)(\ker\alpha_\chi).} \tag{5.6}
\]

To verify (5.6), an element \(b\) with \(\beta b=\mathscr L_\chi h\) can
be replaced by \(b-(1\otimes E_\chi)(h\otimes\phi_\chi)\), which lies in
\(\ker\beta\). The intersection of \(\ker\beta\) with the image of
\(1\otimes E_\chi\) equals the image of \(\ker\alpha_\chi\), since
multiplication by \(\mathscr L_\chi\) is injective on \(\mathcal O\).
The precise pushout square is

\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
\mathcal O\otimes\mathscr B&\longrightarrow&\mathcal O\otimes Q_\chi\\
\beta\downarrow&&\downarrow\\
\mathcal O&\longrightarrow&\mathcal O/(\mathscr L_\chi).
\end{array}
\]

Indeed a map from \(\mathcal O\) compatible with a map from
\(\mathcal O\otimes Q_\chi\) must kill
\(\beta((1\otimes E_\chi)(\mathcal O\otimes\mathscr V_\chi))=(\mathscr L_\chi)\),
and hence factors uniquely through the lower-right quotient. Thus the
analytic realization is a computed pushout of the cokernel presentation,
not an assertion that extension of scalars alone lost no relations.

All these maps tensor by \(W_l\) and commute with every support
transport. This is the promised map from the support-retaining theta
cohomology to the actual completed spectral sheaf.

\subsection{6. Every finite spectral jet is reached, with no zero table
as
input}\label{every-finite-spectral-jet-is-reached-with-no-zero-table-as-input}

Let \(Z\) be any finite collection of zeros of \(\mathscr L_\chi\) in
\(\Omega\), and retain their actual orders \(m_\rho\). Define

\[
A_Z=\bigoplus_{\rho\in Z}\mathcal O_\rho/(\mathscr L_\chi),
\quad
J_Z(F)=\left(\frac{(\mathcal MF)^{(j)}(\rho)}{j!}\right)_{\rho\in Z,\,0\le j<m_\rho}.
\]

This is the local expansion in \(t=s-\rho\). Writing
\(\mathscr L_\chi=t^{m_\rho}U_\rho(t)\) identifies the ideal with
\((t^{m_\rho})\) because \(U_\rho\) is a unit. The unit is not discarded
from the residue pairing in section 7.

\textbf{Theorem 6.1.} For every \(R>0\), the map \(J_Z\) on
\(C_c^\infty((e^{-R},e^R))\) is surjective. It annihilates
\(E_\chi\mathscr V_\chi\) as a map on \(\mathscr B\), and factors
continuously through its closure quotient.

\textbf{Proof.} The vanishing follows from (5.3). The component
functionals are continuous on \(\mathscr B\). If a linear combination of
their restrictions to the compact interval vanished, changing to
\(y=\log x\) would make the analytic exponential polynomial

\[
\sum_{\rho,j}c_{\rho,j}e^{\rho y}y^j/j!
\]

zero throughout \((-R,R)\). Exponential polynomials with distinct
exponents are independent. One proof applies
\(\prod_{\eta\ne\rho}(\partial_y-\eta)^{m_\eta}\): it kills the other
blocks, while each operator \(\partial_y+\rho-\eta\) is invertible on
finite-degree polynomials. The remaining polynomial must vanish. Thus
the functionals are linearly independent and their finite-dimensional
joint map is onto. Continuity then gives vanishing on the closure.
\(\square\)

The surjectivity has a smooth, finite formula, not only a rank argument.
Fix

\[
\eta_R(y)=
\begin{cases}
\exp[-1/(R^2-y^2)],&|y|<R,\\
0,&|y|\ge R.
\end{cases}
\]

Index the finite jets by \(\alpha=(\rho,j)\) and put
\(r_\alpha(x)=x^{\bar\rho-1}(\log x)^j/j!\). The matrix

\[
B^R_{\alpha\beta}
=\ell_\alpha(\eta_R(\log x)r_\beta(x))
=\frac1{j!k!}\int_{-R}^R
\eta_R(y)e^{(\rho+\bar\sigma-1)y}y^{j+k}\,dy
\]

is positive definite by the same exponential-polynomial independence.
For a prescribed jet vector \(v\), the formula

\[
\boxed{F_v(x)=\eta_R(\log x)\sum_\beta((B^R)^{-1}v)_\beta r_\beta(x)}
\]

gives a function smooth on \(\mathbb R_{>0}\), supported in
\([e^{-R},e^R]\), and \(J_Z(F_v)=v\). This formula lies in
\(\mathscr B\). For support strictly inside \((e^{-R},e^R)\), replace
\(\eta_R\) by \(\eta_{R'}\) for any explicitly chosen \(0<R'<R\); the
same matrix argument applies. No infinite interpolation or selected zero
table is assumed.

On the local module, the arithmetic generator and dilation are exactly

\[
D\longmapsto M_s=\rho I+N,\qquad N(t^j)=t^{j+1},\quad N^{m_\rho}=0,
\]

\[
\boxed{R_a\longmapsto a^{-\rho}\exp(-(\log a)N).} \tag{6.1}
\]

Indeed \(\mathcal M(DF)=s\mathcal MF\) and
\(\mathcal M(R_aF)=a^{-s}\mathcal MF\). Consequently

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(h(M_s))=m_\rho h(\rho),
\qquad\operatorname{Tr}(R_a)=m_\rho a^{-\rho}.
\]

The entire nilpotent jet is retained by (6.1), even when an ordinary
trace sees only its multiplicity.

\subsection{7. Duality on the actual local
image}\label{duality-on-the-actual-local-image}

There is a canonical perfect residue pairing

\[
\mathcal O_\rho/(\mathscr L_\chi)\ \times\
\bigl(\mathscr L_\chi^{-1}\mathcal O_\rho\,ds/\mathcal O_\rho\,ds\bigr)
\longrightarrow\mathbb C,
\quad([a],[b\,ds/\mathscr L_\chi])\mapsto\operatorname{Res}_\rho\frac{ab}{\mathscr L_\chi}\,ds. \tag{7.1}
\]

If \(\mathscr L_\chi=t^mU(t)\), the matrix in the monomial numerator
bases is

\[
G_{ij}=[t^{m-1-i-j}]U(t)^{-1},\qquad0\le i,j<m.
\]

Entries with negative coefficient index are zero. Its anti-diagonal
equals \(U(0)^{-1}\), so

\[
\det G=(-1)^{m(m-1)/2}U(0)^{-m}.
\]

This proves nondegeneracy, including multiple zeros and the exact
analytic unit.

For an actual finite divisor invariant under \(s\mapsto1-\bar s\),
define \(b^\#(s)=\overline{b(1-\bar s)}\). The finite trace pairing is

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(M_{ab^\#})
=\sum_\rho m_\rho a(\rho)\overline{b(1-\bar\rho)}. \tag{7.2}
\]

For a non-real character, conjugation and reflection separately
interchange the character with its conjugate; their composition
\(s\mapsto1-\bar s\) preserves its zero divisor. Thus the stated
stable-divisor hypothesis specifies the sector of (7.2). Equation (7.2)
states the full finite test pairing; positivity is not inserted into
(7.1), whose perfectness is bilinear duality rather than a sign
assertion.

For an abelian extension \(K/\mathbb Q\), let \(\chi\) run through its
primitive associated Dirichlet characters, with conductors \(q_\chi\)
and parities \(\epsilon_\chi\). With
\(\Gamma_{\mathbb R}(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\) and
\(\Gamma_{\mathbb C}(s)=2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)\), the exact completion
relation is

\[
\prod_\chi\Lambda_\chi(s)
=\left(\prod_\chi q_\chi^{\epsilon_\chi/2}\right)
|D_K|^{s/2}\Gamma_{\mathbb R}(s)^{r_1}\Gamma_{\mathbb C}(s)^{r_2}\zeta_K(s). \tag{7.3}
\]

The ordinary trivial factor in (7.3) is
\(\Lambda_0=\Gamma_{\mathbb R}\zeta\), not \(\xi\). Replacing it by the
sheaf generator \(\xi\) multiplies the product by \(s(s-1)/2\). Formula
(7.3) uses the full primitive Euler factors and conductor-discriminant
formula;
\(\Gamma_{\mathbb R}(s)\Gamma_{\mathbb R}(s+1)=\Gamma_{\mathbb C}(s)\)
retains the complex-place factor two. General number-field Hecke
characters require their own adelic test spaces; that extension is not
claimed from the rational-character calculation.

\subsection{8. The actual finite-cutoff metric on this
map}\label{the-actual-finite-cutoff-metric-on-this-map}

The preceding finite-jet map gives a concrete metric computation. On the
interval \(I_R=(e^{-R},e^R)\) use exactly

\[
\mathcal H_R=L^2(I_R,dx),\qquad \langle F,G\rangle=\int_{I_R}F\bar G\,dx.
\]

This measure is tied to the retained source half-density map, not chosen
to manufacture a critical line: the map

\[
W_{1/2}:L^2(I_R,dx)\longrightarrow L^2(I_R,dx/x),\qquad F\mapsto x^{1/2}F
\]

is unitary because \(\int|x^{1/2}F|^2dx/x=\int|F|^2dx\). Equation (2.2)
is its Mellin/Fourier intertwining formula.

For the value functional \(\ell_\rho(F)=\mathcal MF(\rho)\), the Riesz
vector is \(x^{\bar\rho-1}\). Put \(\kappa=2\Re\rho-1\). Direct
integration gives

\[
\boxed{
\|\ell_\rho\|_{\mathcal H_R^*}^2
=\int_{-R}^R e^{\kappa y}\,dy
=\begin{cases}
(e^{\kappa R}-e^{-\kappa R})/\kappa,&\kappa\ne0,\\
2R,&\kappa=0.
\end{cases}} \tag{8.1}
\]

All constants are retained. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac1R\log\|\ell_\rho\|=|\Re\rho-\tfrac12|.} \tag{8.2}
\]

For a derivative jet,

\[
\ell_{\rho,j}(F)=\frac1{j!}\int_{I_R}F(x)x^{\rho-1}(\log x)^j\,dx,
\]

and, writing \(r_{\sigma,k}(x)=x^{\bar\sigma-1}(\log x)^k/k!\) for the
Riesz vector, the Gram entry \(\ell_{\rho,j}(r_{\sigma,k})\) is

\[
\boxed{
\frac1{j!k!}\int_{-R}^R e^{(\rho+\bar\sigma-1)y}y^{j+k}\,dy.
} \tag{8.3}
\]

The formula is entire in the combined exponent, with its value at zero
obtained from the integral. Linear independence from Theorem 6.1 makes
the finite Gram matrix positive definite. Writing
\(A_{\alpha\beta}=\ell_\alpha(r_\beta)\) for (8.3), the least-norm lift
in \(\mathcal H_R\) is

\[
F_v^{\min}=\sum_\beta(A^{-1}v)_\beta r_\beta,
\qquad \|F_v^{\min}\|^2=\bar v^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}v.
\]

It need not vanish at the interval endpoints, so it is not asserted to
belong to the original compactly supported smooth class. The infimum
over smooth compactly supported exact lifts is the same: approximate
\(F_v^{\min}\) in \(\mathcal H_R\) by smooth compactly supported
functions, and correct their finite jet errors using the fixed smooth
right inverse in section 6. That correction tends to zero in norm. Thus
this is a calculated quotient metric of the actual test functions, not a
claimed Hilbert norm on the complete arithmetic cohomology.

Equations (8.1)-(8.3) replace an unspecified weight comparison by an
exact metric of the map now constructed. Chen--Moriwaki's
HN-to-one-place comparison controls the degree of specified adelic
vector bundles. It has not been applied to these cutoff quotient norms:
the necessary adelic norm family and the relevant global two-sided
estimate are not supplied by that theorem. The local growth in (8.2) is
exactly what such a comparison would have to control, without replacing
an eigenvalue by its imaginary part.
