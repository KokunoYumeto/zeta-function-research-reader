\subsubsection{1. Original input and the fixed nonzero-parameter complex}\label{endpoint-analytic_pole--original-input-and-the-fixed-nonzero-parameter-complex}

Keep the monic polynomial \(h\) of degree \(d>0\) with every selected actual zero order, \(g=2\xi\), and the unique polynomial \(\nu\) of degree less than \(d\) representing the complete Taylor unit \(\upsilon_h=j_h(g/h)\). Thus \(\gcd(h,\nu)=1\), and no factor of \(h\) or Taylor coefficient of its unit has been removed. Let \(P_{\nu}\) be the set of distinct zeros of \(\nu\), and \(r\) its cardinality; \(r=0\) when \(\nu\) is constant. The multiplicities of \(\nu\) are retained in \(\nu\) itself throughout the gauge.

Fix the original primitive \(\Phi\) with \(\Phi'=h\) and its specified additive constant. For \(u\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\) and \(t\in\mathbb{C}\) define

\[
\begin{aligned}
D&=u\frac{d}{ds}+h(s)-t,\\
w(s;u,t)&=\exp((\Phi(s)-t s)/u),\\
B&=\mathbb C[s],\qquad B_{\mathrm{loc}}=\mathbb C[s,\nu^{-1}],
\qquad M=B_{\mathrm{loc}}/B.
\end{aligned}
\tag{AP1}
\]

Use complexes \([B\xrightarrow{D}B\,ds]\) and \([B_{\mathrm{loc}}\xrightarrow{D}B_{\mathrm{loc}}\,ds]\) in degrees \(0,1\). The equation \(w D F = u (w F)'\) is literal, with the constant in \(\Phi\) retained. Although \(w\) is entire and nowhere zero in \(s\), it is not asserted rational. The quotient \(M\) consists exactly of finite sums of principal parts at \(P_{\nu}\): partial fractions gives existence and uniqueness, with the polynomial part discarded only in this explicitly specified quotient.

\subsubsection{2. The entire additional pole cohomology is calculated}\label{endpoint-analytic_pole--the-entire-additional-pole-cohomology-is-calculated}

Define, for \([F]\in M\),

\[
\operatorname{Res}_w[F]
=\bigl(\operatorname{Res}_{s=p}w(s)F(s)\,ds\bigr)_{p\in P_\nu}.
\tag{AP2}
\]

Adding a polynomial changes none of these residues. Also \(\operatorname{Res}_w D=0\) by AP1, since a derivative of a meromorphic Laurent series has zero residue.

The exact sequence is

\[
0\longrightarrow M\xrightarrow{D}M
\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Res}_w}\mathbb C^{P_\nu}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{AP3}
\]

Here is a proof of every assertion. If \(F\) has pole order \(n\geq1\) at \(p\) and leading term \(c(s-p)^{-n}\), \(D F\) has leading pole term \(-u n c(s-p)^{-n-1}\). Multiplication by \(h-t\) has pole order at most \(n\), so it cannot cancel this term. Thus \(D[F]=0\) in \(M\) implies that \(F\) has no poles, and \([F]=0\). This proves injectivity.

For each \(p\), the rational function \(1/(w(p)(s-p))\) has residue vector equal to the \(p\)-th coordinate vector. It is analytic at every other point of \(P_{\nu}\). This proves surjectivity without suppressing the factor \(w(p)\).

Suppose \(\operatorname{Res}_w[F]=0\). In a disk at \(p\) containing no other point of \(P_{\nu}\), the Laurent series of \(wF\) has finitely many negative terms and no \((s-p)^{-1}\) term. Integrating term by term therefore gives a meromorphic primitive \(H_p\), with no logarithm. Put \(Q_p=H_p/(u w)\). Then \(D Q_p=F\) locally. Let \(Q\) be the rational function obtained by summing the finite principal parts of the \(Q_p\) at all \(p\). The difference \(Q-Q_p\) is holomorphic at \(p\), so \(DQ-F\) is holomorphic there. This rational function has no other possible finite poles, hence is a polynomial. Therefore \(D[Q]=[F]\) in \(M\), which proves exactness in the middle. Changing any integration constant changes only a holomorphic local function and does not change this construction of the principal part.

All higher pole orders are explicitly observed, rather than ignored:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Res}_p\frac{w(s)}{(s-p)^n}\,ds
&=\frac{w^{(n-1)}(p)}{(n-1)!},\\
D(s-p)^{-n}&=-u n(s-p)^{-n-1}\\
&\quad+\sum_{j=0}^d\frac{h^{(j)}(p)}{j!}(s-p)^{j-n}
-t(s-p)^{-n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{AP4}
\]

These formulas give the triangular reduction of each higher pole while retaining every derivative of \(h\) and every derivative of \(w\) that contributes. They explain why there is one quotient coordinate per distinct pole point; they do not replace \(h\) by its radical or discard its nilpotent zero jets.

\subsubsection{3. The original arithmetic classes inject into the analytic extension}\label{endpoint-analytic_pole--the-original-arithmetic-classes-inject-into-the-analytic-extension}

The polynomial complex has \(H^0=0\) and \(H^1\) of dimension \(d\), with basis \(1,s,\ldots,s^{d-1}\). Indeed, for a nonzero polynomial of degree \(n\), the highest term of \(DF\) has degree \(n+d\), with unchanged leading coefficient. Successive subtraction of such leading terms reduces every polynomial uniquely to degree less than \(d\). Write this unchanged remainder module as \(H_{\mathrm{pol}}\).

The same highest-pole argument shows \(H^0\) of the localized complex is zero: a rational solution of \(DF=0\) has no finite pole, and then the preceding polynomial argument applies. The short exact sequence of the three complexes, together with AP3, gives

\[
0\longrightarrow H_{\mathrm{pol}}\xrightarrow{i}H_{\mathrm{loc}}
\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Res}_w}\mathbb C^{P_\nu}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{AP5}
\]

One can check injectivity without a long exact sequence. If a polynomial \(F\) equals \(DQ\) for rational \(Q\), the leading-pole calculation forces \(Q\) to be a polynomial. Thus no original polynomial cohomology class becomes a boundary. The kernel and surjectivity assertions follow from AP3 by subtracting \(DQ\) and retaining the resulting polynomial.

An explicit basis of \(H_{\mathrm{loc}}\) is

\[
1,s,\ldots,s^{d-1},\qquad r_p=\frac{1}{s-p}
\quad\text{for }p\in P_\nu.
\tag{AP6}
\]

For existence, subtract \(\sum_p (\operatorname{Res}_w F)_p r_p/w(p)\). The remainder has zero weighted residues, and AP3 reduces it modulo \(D\) to a polynomial, then to degree less than \(d\). For independence, applying AP2 to a linear combination forces every pole coefficient to vanish because \(w(p)\ne0\); injectivity in AP5 then gives independence of the polynomial coefficients. Hence \(\dim H_{\mathrm{loc}}=d+r\). The original \(d\)-dimensional arithmetic fibre, with all repeated roots of \(h\), is present as the explicitly injected summand of this vector-space basis.

\subsubsection{4. Actual parameter connection and both explicit residue frames}\label{endpoint-analytic_pole--actual-parameter-connection-and-both-explicit-residue-frames}

\[

\tag{AP7}

\[
\begin{aligned}

A_{\mathrm{ext}}(t)&=\begin{bmatrix}A(t)&B_0\\0&P\end{bmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{AP8}

Here each pole basis vector satisfies the exact identity

\[

\tag{AP9}
\]

For the last polynomial vector, reduction of \(s^d\) uses \(D(1)=h-t\), so the polynomial block is exactly the already specified \(A(t)\), without a derivative correction. Thus in the full frame AP6 the connection is

\[

\tag{AP10}

All coefficients depend holomorphically on \((u,t)\) for \(u\ne0\). In fact \(A_{\mathrm{ext}}\) itself has no singular \(u\) coefficient; this statement about the matrix does not identify its rank-\((d+r)\) frame with the formal cohomology at \(u=0\).

\[

\tag{AP11}

\[

\tag{AP12}

Thus the quotient connection in weighted-residue coordinates is \(u\,d/dt\). The ordinary simple-pole coefficients instead have \(u\,d/dt-P\). Their exact transition is multiplication by \(W\), not deletion of an exponential phase.

For completeness use the second frame \(r_p/w(p)\). Its residue coordinates are the standard coordinate vectors, and

\[

\tag{AP13}
