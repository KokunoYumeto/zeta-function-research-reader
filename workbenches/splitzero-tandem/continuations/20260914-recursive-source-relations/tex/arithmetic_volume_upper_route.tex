\section{An arithmetic lower-envelope comparison for central quotient volumes: full jets, finite determinant bounds, and an exact quadratic benchmark}



This calculation constructs upper bounds for the original central volume
$\log(V_q/V_{2q-1})$. Its input is the proved lower envelope and Laplace
estimate of the complete \emph{Arithmetic Endpoint Bounds} source, equations
(29)--(32). The comparison acts on the original polynomial and quotient
maps, including all multiplicities and phases. The original matrix
bound retains an explicit positive matrix: its arithmetic moments stop at
degree $2q$, while its comparison moments continue to $4q-2$. A second
bound uses only the supplied envelope constants and two Laplace values.
The signed four-endpoint comparison is now propagated into (AU.31a)--(AU.31b);
it retains the endpoint losses and uses actual moments through degree $4q$.
The explicit scalar estimate has quadratic, rather than logarithmic,
growth per quotient direction. An exact auxiliary calculation determines
that scale and supplies a monic family relating its full jets to the
original quartet jets. No bound contradicting the quartet lower estimate
is asserted.

\subsection{Original source and every jet coordinate}

Fix a complete quartet $1/2\pm\delta\pm i\gamma$, with
$\delta,\gamma>0$, and common complete zero order $m\ge1$ of $g=2\xi$.
Keep its monic polynomial $h$, its entire quotient $v_h=g/h$, and
\[
 w_h(t)=|v_h(1/2+it)|^2/(2\pi),\qquad
 \mu_h=\int_\R w_h(t)\,dt,\qquad m_k=w_h^{*k},\qquad k\ge3.
 \tag{AU.1}
\]
Write $c=k/2$ and keep the original observation $S=c+iu$. The source
form is $\int\overline{P(c+iu)}Q(c+iu)m_k(u)\,du$, conjugate-linear
in its first argument. Its constant norm is $\mu_h^k$.
The coefficient map and its inverse are
\[
 \Psi:\C[S]\longrightarrow\C[u],\quad P\longmapsto P(c+iu),
 \qquad f\longmapsto f((S-c)/i).                             \tag{AU.2}
\]
These are inverse ring homomorphisms by substitution. Multiplication by
$S$ is carried to $c+iM_u$, and
$\partial_u^d\Psi(P)=i^d\Psi(P^{(d)})$. On the ordered degree-$N$
coefficient spaces the matrix is triangular with diagonal
$1,i,\ldots,i^N$, so its determinant has modulus one. The original
quotient volumes therefore have exactly the same values in the
transported remainder basis; no mass or norm is divided out.

Let $\ell=1+k(m-1)$ and use the ordered distinct grid
\[
 \lambda_{ab}=c+(2a-k)\delta+i(2b-k)\gamma,
 \quad \zeta_{ab}=(\lambda_{ab}-c)/i
     =(2b-k)\gamma-i(2a-k)\delta,
 \quad 0\le a,b\le k.                                      \tag{AU.3}
\]
The original cyclic relation and its transported monic polynomial are
\[
 q=\ell(k+1)^2,\quad
 \chi(S)=\prod_{a,b}(S-\lambda_{ab})^\ell,\quad
 \psi(u)=i^{-q}\chi(c+iu)=\prod_{a,b}(u-\zeta_{ab})^\ell.
 \tag{AU.4}
\]
The grid is invariant under complex conjugation in the $u$ chart, so
$\psi$ is real monic. Equivalently the original coefficient identity is
$\overline\chi(k-S)=(-1)^q\chi(S)$. In particular the original norm
multiplier is $\overline\chi(c-iu)\chi(c+iu)=\psi(u)^2$, with the
source multiplication still carrying the unit $i^q$.

For clarity the preceding cyclic degree also follows from the original
tensor algebra. In a tensor of $k$ length-$m$ local factors, the sum of
the $k$ nilpotent generators has power $k(m-1)$ nonzero: its coefficient
on the product of their top powers is $(k(m-1))!/((m-1)!)^k$, nonzero
in $\C$. Its next power is zero by total degree. Every grid sum in
(AU.3) occurs, since the two signs in each quartet factor can be chosen
independently. Direct products of local factors give the same maximal
length $\ell$ at every grid sum. Distinct pairs have distinct real and
imaginary parts. The minimal polynomial of the original sum is
therefore exactly (AU.4), with no lost multiplicity.

Put $\mathcal E=\C[u]/(\psi)$, with ordered remainder coefficients
$1,u,\ldots,u^{q-1}$. For $N\ge q-1$ let $J_N$ be remainder and
$B_N$ multiplication by $\psi$ in the ordered monomials. Monic division
gives the exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow\C[u]_{\le N-q}
 \xrightarrow{B_N}\C[u]_{\le N}
 \xrightarrow{J_N}\mathcal E\longrightarrow0.              \tag{AU.5}
\]
The source on the left is zero if $N=q-1$.
Precisely, the coefficient isomorphisms are
$\iota_N:\C^{N+1}\to\C[u]_{\le N}$,
$a\mapsto\sum_{j=0}^Na_ju^j$, and
$\kappa:\C^q\to\mathcal E$, $x\mapsto[\sum_{j<q}x_ju^j]$.
The polynomial quotient map is $\kappa J_N\iota_N^{-1}$ and the
polynomial multiplication map is
$\iota_N B_N\iota_{N-q}^{-1}$. When a matrix formula is evaluated
on a polynomial below, these displayed coefficient isomorphisms are
the specified identifications. The original $S$ chart uses the same
coefficient isomorphisms with $u^j$ replaced by $S^j$.
The complete raw derivative map is
\[
 \mathsf E_N f=(f^{(d)}(\zeta_{ab}))_{a,b,0\le d<\ell},
 \qquad
 (\mathsf E_N)_{(a,b,d),j}=
 \begin{cases}j!\zeta_{ab}^{j-d}/(j-d)!,&j\ge d,\\0,&j<d.
 \end{cases}                                                \tag{AU.6}
\]
Taylor expansion at each distinct centre shows that its kernel is
$(\psi)\cap\C[u]_{\le N}$. Let $\mathsf Z=\mathsf E_{q-1}$.
Then $\mathsf E_N=\mathsf ZJ_N$ and $\mathsf Z$ is invertible.
Indeed a degree below $q$ polynomial with all those zero raw jets is
divisible by degree-$q$ $\psi$ and is zero. For the fixed row order,
\[
 \det\mathsf Z=
 \left(\prod_{a,b}\prod_{d=0}^{\ell-1}d!\right)
 \prod_{(a,b)<(a',b')}(\zeta_{a'b'}-\zeta_{ab})^{\ell^2}.
 \tag{AU.7}
\]
One proof first regards all roots as distinct variables in the ordinary
Vandermonde determinant, groups each cluster, and replaces its rows by
successive divided differences. Each row operation divides by exactly
the corresponding within-cluster difference. Sending the nodes in a
cluster to its centre replaces its divided-difference row of order $d$
by $f^{(d)}/d!$, by the finite Taylor expansion of each monomial.
Multiplying those rows by $d!$ proves (AU.7). This is an identity of
polynomials after removal of the indicated factors; no source norm or
zero multiplicity is approximated. In the original $S$ coordinates the
same raw jet of order $d$ has its literal factor $i^d$ from (AU.2).

For any positive source matrix $H_N$, the least quotient form is
\[
 K_N=J_NH_N^{-1}J_N^*,\quad G_N=K_N^{-1},\quad V_N=\det G_N,
 \qquad R_N=H_N^{-1}J_N^*G_N.                               \tag{AU.8}
\]
Surjectivity proves $K_N>0$. Multiplication proves $J_NR_N=I$ and
$B_N^*H_NR_N=0$. Thus every lift $R_Nx+B_Ny$ has norm
$x^*G_Nx+y^*B_N^*H_NB_Ny$, proving the minimum property.
In raw jets the kernel is $\mathsf K_N=\mathsf E_NH_N^{-1}\mathsf E_N^*$,
and $\det\mathsf K_N=|\det\mathsf Z|^2/V_N$. Hence
\[
 C_k:=\log(V_q/V_{2q-1})
      =\log\det\mathsf K_{2q-1}-\log\det\mathsf K_q.
 \tag{AU.9}
\]
All factorials in (AU.7) cancel only in this specified ratio.

\subsection{The lower envelope as an explicit positive comparison form}

The supplied arithmetic proof establishes $c_h>0$,
$\vartheta_h=\int_{-1}^1w_h>0$, $B=42+2\deg h=42+8m$, and
\[
 m_k(u)\ge c_h\vartheta_h^{k-3}
 e^{-\alpha(|u|+k-3)}(1+|u|+k-3)^{-B},\qquad\alpha=\pi/2.
 \tag{AU.10}
\]
Set $a_k=c_h\vartheta_h^{k-3}e^{-\alpha(k-3)}$ and $d_k=k-2>0$.
Define the comparison measure, retaining its literal mass, by
\[
 d\nu_k(u)=a_k e^{-\alpha|u|}(d_k+|u|)^{-B}\,du.
 \tag{AU.11}
\]
The identity map $L^2(m_kdu)\to L^2(\nu_k)$ is well defined and a
contraction, since (AU.10) gives the squared-norm inequality for every
measurable function. It restricts to the identity on each polynomial
space and commutes with multiplication by $c+iu$, remainder (AU.5),
and every raw jet (AU.6). The comparison is therefore a map on the same
quotient, not a declaration that the measures coincide.

Every moment of $\nu_k$ is finite, and the following integral evaluates
it with positive integrands:
\[
 \nu_{k,j}:=\int_\R u^j\,d\nu_k(u)=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\frac{2a_kj!}{(B-1)!}
 \int_0^\infty\frac{t^{B-1}e^{-d_kt}}{(\alpha+t)^{j+1}}\,dt,
   &j\text{ even},\\
 0,&j\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}                                                \tag{AU.12}
\]
To prove it, repeated integration by parts gives
$\int_0^\infty t^{B-1}e^{-yt}dt=(B-1)!y^{-B}$ for $y>0$.
Insert $y=d_k+u$ on the positive half-line. Tonelli applies because
each integrand is nonnegative there. Integrating $u^je^{-(\alpha+t)u}$
gives $j!(\alpha+t)^{-j-1}$ by the same integration by parts. Evenness
supplies the factor two or the vanishing odd moment. The bound
$\nu_{k,j}\le2a_kd_k^{-B}j!\alpha^{-j-1}$ for even $j$ also proves
finiteness directly. In particular $\nu_{k,0}>0$ is retained, and
generally differs from $\mu_h^k$.

Let $H_N^\nu=(\nu_{k,i+j})_{0\le i,j\le N}$. Its quadratic form is
the $\nu_k$ integral of the polynomial's squared modulus. The weight
is strictly positive everywhere, so a nonzero polynomial has positive
norm. Thus $0<H_N^\nu\preceq H_N$. Minimizing on the identical
affine fibres in (AU.5) proves
\[
 0<G_N^\nu\preceq G_N,\qquad
 \mathsf K_N\preceq\mathsf K_N^\nu,
 \quad \mathsf K_N^\nu=\mathsf E_N(H_N^\nu)^{-1}\mathsf E_N^*.
 \tag{AU.13}
\]
The inverse inequality follows also by conjugating $H_N$ by
$(H_N^\nu)^{-1/2}$ and inverting its positive eigenvalues.

\begin{theorem}[Actual low-degree data control the higher quotient volume]
For the original quartet source and $k\ge3$, the finite upper bound is
\[
 \boxed{0\le C_k\le
 \log\det\mathsf K_{2q-1}^\nu-\log\det\mathsf K_q.}        \tag{AU.14}
\]
Its actual arithmetic inputs are the moments through degree $2q$ and
the stated envelope constants. The comparator moments through $4q-2$
are the explicit positive integrals (AU.12).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first inequality follows by minimizing the same quotient norm in
successively larger polynomial spaces. For the second use (AU.13) at
$N=2q-1$ and (AU.9). Determinant is increasing on positive Hermitian
forms: conjugate by the square root of the smaller one and multiply
eigenvalues at least one. $H_q$ uses precisely the original moments
$0,\ldots,2q$, and all the matrices in the second term of (AU.14) have
already specified finite algebraic maps from them. No higher actual
moment enters. Equivalently, if $\mathfrak D^\nu_j=\det H_{j-1}^\nu$
and $\mathfrak B^\nu_j$ is the Gram determinant of the original
$\psi$ times $1,u,\ldots,u^{j-1}$, the quotient-first coefficient
matrix $[1,u,\ldots,u^{q-1},\psi,u\psi,\ldots]$ is triangular with
diagonal one. Orthogonal elimination of its relation block gives
\[
 V_{2q-1}^\nu=\mathfrak D^\nu_{2q}/\mathfrak B^\nu_q,
 \qquad C_k\le\log V_q+
             \log\mathfrak B^\nu_q-\log\mathfrak D^\nu_{2q}.
 \tag{AU.15}
\]
This proves the same upper bound in the exact source/relation
determinants. Both the source mass and relation multiplication factors
remain in their respective determinants.
\end{proof}

The existing Gamma coefficient map also makes the cutoff in (AU.14)
explicit. With $\lambda>0$, $\alpha_\lambda=2\lambda$,
$c_\lambda=2^{1-2\lambda}\Gamma(2\lambda)$ and $C_\lambda=c_\lambda^k$,
define the original monic Gamma polynomials by
\[
 \sum_{j\ge0}b_j^{(\lambda)}(u)z^j/j!
 =(1+z^2)^{-\lambda}e^{u\arctan z},\quad
 c_{h,j}=\frac{\int b_j^{(\lambda)}w_h}
 {c_\lambda j!(\alpha_\lambda)_j}.
\]
Multiplying the $k$ generating functions and taking finite derivatives
at zero gives
\[
 d_l=\sum_{j_1+\cdots+j_k=l}\prod_a(\alpha_\lambda)_{j_a}c_{h,j_a},
 \qquad \int b_l^{(k\lambda)}m_k=C_\lambda l!d_l.          \tag{AU.16}
\]
All finite terms are integrable by the supplied exponential moments.
Expand $u^{i+j}=\sum_{l\le i+j}L_{ij,l}b_l^{(k\lambda)}(u)$ by monic
subtraction. Then $(H_q)_{ij}=C_\lambda\sum_l l!L_{ij,l}d_l$.
Thus the actual coefficient list $c_{h,0},\ldots,c_{h,2q}$ suffices
for (AU.14). At zero degree it gives
$C_\lambda c_{h,0}^k=\mu_h^k$, retaining the source mass.

\subsection{An upper determinant depending only on the two Laplace values}

Fix $0<b<\pi/2$ and set
$M_k=M_h(b)^k+M_h(-b)^k$, with the original
$M_h(\pm b)=\int e^{\pm bu}w_h(u)du$.
Positivity and convolution give $\int e^{b|u|}m_k(u)du\le M_k$.
The exponential series therefore gives
$\int |u|^{2j}m_k(u)du\le M_k(2j)!b^{-2j}$.
For arbitrary $\tau_0,\ldots,\tau_q>0$ define
\[
 D_q(\tau)=M_k\Bigl(\sum_{j=0}^q\tau_j\Bigr)
       \diag\left(\frac{(2j)!}{b^{2j}\tau_j}\right)_{j=0}^q.
 \tag{AU.17}
\]
For $f=\sum a_ju^j$, Cauchy--Schwarz applied to
$\sqrt{\tau_j}$ and $a_ju^j/\sqrt{\tau_j}$ proves
\[
 |f(u)|^2\le(\sum\tau_j)\sum |a_j|^2|u|^{2j}/\tau_j.
\]
Integration proves $H_q\preceq D_q(\tau)$. Inverting and applying
the same full jet map proves
\[
 \mathsf K_q\succeq\frac{1}{M_k\sum\tau_j}\mathsf A_q(\tau),
 \quad
 \mathsf A_q(\tau)=\mathsf E_q
       \diag\left(\frac{\tau_jb^{2j}}{(2j)!}\right)\mathsf E_q^*>0.
 \tag{AU.18}
\]
Consequently an upper bound without any unevaluated actual polynomial
moment is
\[
 \boxed{C_k\le q\log\left(M_k\sum_{j=0}^q\tau_j\right)
  +\log\det\mathsf K_{2q-1}^\nu-\log\det\mathsf A_q(\tau).}
 \tag{AU.19}
\]
Every positive choice of $\tau$ gives a proved bound, and the infimum
over such choices remains a bound. Multiplying all $\tau_j$ by the
same positive number cancels between the two written determinant
factors; it changes neither measure. No optimizer or numerical
enclosure is assumed in (AU.19).

The lower determinant has the exact positive expansion
\[
 \det\mathsf A_q(\tau)=
 \sum_{\substack{I\subset\{0,\ldots,q\}\\|I|=q}}
 |\det(\mathsf E_q)_{[:,I]}|^2
 \prod_{j\in I}\frac{\tau_jb^{2j}}{(2j)!}.                \tag{AU.20}
\]
Expand each determinant multilinearly in columns in the product
$\mathsf E_qD\mathsf E_q^*$: terms with repeated column indices
vanish by alternation, and grouping permutations of the same subset
gives (AU.20). Its subset $I=\{0,\ldots,q-1\}$ is strictly positive
by (AU.7). This expression retains full raw-jet minors and factorials
without estimating an inverse by its individual entries.

\subsection{The interval kernel and the actual comparison map}

For $T>0$, set
\[
 a_k(T)=a_ke^{-\alpha T}(d_k+T)^{-B}.
 \tag{AU.21}
\]
Restriction to $[-T,T]$ followed by the identity on polynomials maps
the actual source to $L^2([-T,T],a_k(T)du)$ contractively. The same
is true from $L^2(\nu_k)$, by the pointwise lower bound on that
interval. Let $H_N^I(T)$ be the Lebesgue interval Gram. Then
$a_k(T)H_N^I(T)\preceq H_N^\nu\preceq H_N$.
Let $P_j$ denote the Legendre polynomial specified by Rodrigues'
formula, $P_j(x)=(2^jj!)^{-1}(d/dx)^j(x^2-1)^j$.
Integration by parts $j$ times proves its orthogonality to lower
degrees; direct evaluation of the resulting beta integral gives its
squared norm $2/(2j+1)$. Its highest coefficient is
$(2j)!/(2^j(j!)^2)$. The endpoint boundary terms vanish because
$(x^2-1)^j$ has zeros of order $j$ at both endpoints.
Applying these constants to $P_j(u/T)$ gives the full interval jet
kernel
\[
 (\mathsf K_N^I(T))_{(a,b,d),(a',b',e)}
 =\sum_{j=0}^N\frac{2j+1}{2T^{1+d+e}}
 P_j^{(d)}(\zeta_{ab}/T)
 \overline{P_j^{(e)}(\zeta_{a'b'}/T)}.                    \tag{AU.22}
\]
This follows by writing the inverse Gram in the orthogonal Legendre
basis and applying (AU.6); each derivative contributes its stated
$T^{-d}$. In particular
\[
 \mathsf K_{2q-1}\preceq\mathsf K_{2q-1}^\nu
          \preceq a_k(T)^{-1}\mathsf K_{2q-1}^I(T).       \tag{AU.23}
\]
Equations (AU.14) and (AU.19) remain valid if their
$\log\det\mathsf K_{2q-1}^\nu$ is replaced by
$\log\det\mathsf K_{2q-1}^I(T)-q\log a_k(T)$.
The global envelope version is at least as strong as each such interval
version by the proved positive-matrix inequality (AU.23).

The restriction map has the exact orthogonal decomposition
$f\mapsto(f1_{[-T,T]},f1_{\R\setminus[-T,T]})$ in the original
weighted $L^2$ source; its two squared norms add to the original norm.
The interval bound drops a nonnegative term in that equality. It does
not assign the outside component to external absence.

\subsection{The explicit scalar bound and its finite gap}

The following explicit scalar estimate is the independent full calculation
VC.1--43, composed here with the preceding original quotient maps.
Put $R_0=\sqrt{\delta^2+\gamma^2}$ and define
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 D=\max\{1,2/b,2R_0\},\quad T=Dq,\quad r=kR_0/T\le1/2,\quad N=2q-1,\\
 U_k(T)=\mu_h^k+\frac{(2q-2)!}{(bT)^{2q-2}}M_k,\qquad
 A_N=\sqrt{\frac{2N+1}{2}}\frac{4^{N+1}-1}{3},\\
 B_q(r)=\left(\frac{1+r}{1-r}\right)^q,\qquad
 \mathcal C_{k,q}=
       \frac{U_k(T)A_{2q-1}^2B_q(r)^2}{T a_k(T)}.
 \end{gathered}                                             \tag{AU.24}
\]
All values are positive. In particular $q\ge k$ and $D\ge2R_0$
give the stated bound on $r$. These choices bound constants; they do
not change the original density.

\begin{theorem}[A full finite Hermitian upper bound]
In the transported ordered quotient coordinates, and by the explicit
congruence below also in the original $S$ coordinates,
\[
 \boxed{K_q\preceq K_{2q-1}\preceq\mathcal C_{k,q}K_q,
 \qquad 0\le C_k\le q\log\mathcal C_{k,q}.}               \tag{AU.25}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $\mathsf T_j$ be the coefficient matrix of the original map
$\Psi(P)=P(c+iu)$ in degree $j$. Write $H_j^S,J_j^S,K_j^S,G_j^S$
for matrices in the original $S$ monomials. Then
\[
 H_j^S=\mathsf T_j^*H_j\mathsf T_j,\quad
 J_j\mathsf T_j=\mathsf T_{q-1}J_j^S,\quad
 K_j=\mathsf T_{q-1}K_j^S\mathsf T_{q-1}^*.
\]
The first identity is the source substitution, the second is monic
division, and substitution in (AU.8) gives the third. Each
$\det\mathsf T_j$ has modulus one, as proved in (AU.2).
All source norms on $S$ polynomials in this proof use $H_j^S$.
The exact auxiliary coordinate map used in this proof is
$\Psi_TP(x)=P(c+iTx)$, with inverse $f((S-c)/(iT))$. Its target
measure is $T m_k(Tx)dx$ and retains mass $\mu_h^k$ by substitution.
The target monic relation is
$\chi_T(x)=(iT)^{-q}\chi(c+iTx)$, so
$\Psi_T(\chi Q)=(iT)^q\chi_T\Psi_TQ$.
Monic division therefore proves
$\operatorname{rem}_{\chi_T}\Psi_TP=\Psi_T\operatorname{rem}_\chi P$.
At every original centre $\lambda$, the full order-$d$ raw jet is
$(\Psi_TP)^{(d)}((\lambda-c)/(iT))=(iT)^dP^{(d)}(\lambda)$.
The coefficient matrix has diagonal $(iT)^j$ at degree $j$;
its quotient determinant is $(iT)^{q(q-1)/2}$. Consequently the
target quotient form is the original form transported by inverse
congruence, and its determinant is $T^{-q(q-1)}V_j$ at every source
degree $j$. This factor cancels in the central ratio, with all phases
and masses still present in the map.

For a polynomial $f(x)=\sum f_jx^j$ put
$\|f\|_{\mathrm{coeff},1}=\sum|f_j|$. The Rodrigues formula above,
expanded by the product rule, gives
\[
 P_j(x)=2^{-j}\sum_{a=0}^j\binom ja^2(x-1)^{j-a}(x+1)^a.
\]
The coefficient norm of a product is at most the product of the two
coefficient norms by convolution and the triangle inequality. Since
$\|(x\pm1)^a\|_{\mathrm{coeff},1}=2^a$, this proves
$\|P_j\|_{\mathrm{coeff},1}\le\sum_a\binom ja^2
=\binom{2j}{j}\le4^j$. The middle equality is the coefficient of
$z^j$ in $(1+z)^j(1+z)^j$. In the orthogonal expansion
$f=\sum_{j=0}^Nf_j^LP_j$, Cauchy--Schwarz and the squared norm
$2/(2j+1)$ give
$|f_j^L|\le\sqrt{(2j+1)/2}\|f\|_{L^2([-1,1])}$.
Summing $4^j$ therefore proves
\[
 \|f\|_{\mathrm{coeff},1}
       \le A_N\|f\|_{L^2([-1,1])},\qquad\deg f\le N.    \tag{AU.26}
\]

List all roots $z_1,\ldots,z_q$ of $\chi_T$, with repetitions;
each has modulus at most $r$. Define $e_a,h_j$ by
\[
 \prod_{a=1}^q(1-z_at)=\sum_{a=0}^q(-1)^ae_at^a,
 \quad
 \prod_{a=1}^q(1-z_at)^{-1}=\sum_{j\ge0}h_jt^j.
\]
The second identity is formal multiplication of geometric series.
It gives $|e_a|\le\binom qa r^a$ and
$|h_j|\le\binom{q+j-1}{j}r^j$: the latter counts exponent lists
of total degree $j$ by their $q-1$ separating bars.
For $0\le s\le q-1$, multiply those inverse series through order $s$.
It proves that the quotient of $x^{q+s}$ by $\chi_T$ is exactly
$Q_s=\sum_{j=0}^sh_jx^{s-j}$ and its remainder is
$x^{q+s}-\chi_TQ_s$, of degree below $q$.
Removing the leading term of $\chi_TQ_s$ removes precisely one
from its coefficient norm. All its remaining coefficients are the
negative remainder. Thus
\[
 \begin{split}
 \|\operatorname{rem}_{\chi_T}x^{q+s}\|_{\mathrm{coeff},1}
 &\le(1+r)^q\sum_{j=0}^s\binom{q+j-1}{j}r^j-1\\
 &\le((1+r)/(1-r))^q=B_q(r).
 \end{split}
\]
At degrees below $q$ the remainder is the original monomial and has
coefficient norm one, also bounded by $B_q(r)$. Linearity proves
\[
 \|\operatorname{rem}_{\chi_T}f\|_{\mathrm{coeff},1}
 \le B_q(r)\|f\|_{\mathrm{coeff},1},\quad\deg f\le2q-1.
 \tag{AU.27}
\]
Every root was retained in this proof. Its smaller literal finite
constant $\max\{1,(1+r)^q\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}\binom{q+j-1}{j}r^j-1\}$
can also replace $B_q(r)$.

For $v$ of degree at most $q-1$, each monomial is bounded by
$\max\{1,|x|^{q-1}\}$. Using $\max(1,y)\le1+y$ for $y\ge0$
and the proved Laplace moment bound gives
\[
 \int_\R|v(u/T)|^2m_k(u)du
             \le U_k(T)\|v\|_{\mathrm{coeff},1}^2.        \tag{AU.28}
\]
On the other hand, restriction to $[-T,T]$ and (AU.21) prove
$\|P\|_{H_N^S}^2\ge T a_k(T)\|\Psi_TP\|_{L^2([-1,1])}^2$.
Take $f=\Psi_TP$ and $v=\operatorname{rem}_{\chi_T}f$.
Equations (AU.26)--(AU.28) and the exact division map now give
\[
 \|\operatorname{rem}_\chi P\|_{H_{q-1}^S}^2
                      \le\mathcal C_{k,q}\|P\|_{H_N^S}^2.
 \tag{AU.29}
\]
The map estimated here is $s\pi_{\chi,N}:\mathcal P_N^S\to
\mathcal P_{q-1}^S$, where $\pi_{\chi,N}$ is the polynomial quotient
map and $s:\C[S]/(\chi)\to\mathcal P_{q-1}^S$ selects the unique
remainder representative. Their exact identities are
$\pi_{\chi,q-1}s=I$ and
$\pi_{\chi,q-1}s\pi_{\chi,N}=\pi_{\chi,N}$.
The composite $s\pi_{\chi,N}$ equals the identity on polynomials
of degree below $q$.

Apply (AU.29) to the least lift $R_N^Sx$ from the original matrices.

\subsection{A full-jet quadratic benchmark and its exact relation family}

Here the auxiliary source is explicitly Lebesgue measure on $[-1,1]$
and the auxiliary relation is $u^q$. Its mass is two. These data are
inserted into the same sequence (AU.5), kernel (AU.8), and central
ratio (AU.9). No assertion that they are an arithmetic density is
made. Write $D_d$ for its $d$ by $d$ monomial determinant, $D_0=1$,
and $B_j(q)$ for the $j$ by $j$ determinant of the relation measure
$u^{2q}du$ on that interval. The quotient-first triangular determinant
calculation proving (AU.15) gives exactly
\[
 \frac{V_q^{\rm aux}}{V_{2q-1}^{\rm aux}}
       =\frac{D_{q+1}B_q(q)}{B_1(q)D_{2q}},\qquad
 B_1(q)=\frac2{2q+1}.                                    \tag{AU.40}
\]
The orthogonal Legendre calculation gives
\[
 D_d=\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}\frac{2}{2j+1}
         \left(\frac{2^j}{\binom{2j}{j}}\right)^2.        \tag{AU.41}
\]
For $q=2s$, parity splits the relation Gram into two $s$ by $s$
blocks, and
\[
 B_{2s}(2s)=\mathcal C_s(2s+1/2)\mathcal C_s(2s+3/2),
 \quad
 \mathcal C_s(x)=\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}(j!)^2}
                       {\prod_{a,b=0}^{s-1}(x+a+b)}.
 \tag{AU.42}
\]
Indeed the even block has entries $1/(2s+a+b+1/2)$ and the odd
block entries $1/(2s+a+b+3/2)$. The Cauchy determinant formula
used here has numerator $\prod_{a<b}(b-a)^2=\prod_j(j!)^2$.
It follows by multiplying the determinant by all its denominators:
the numerator is alternating separately in its two lists of variables,
so contains their two Vandermonde factors. Its degree is their total
degree, and comparing one residue inductively gives constant one.
Substitution gives (AU.42) with no absolute-value or sign ambiguity.

\begin{theorem}[An explicit quadratic scale in the full-origin jet fibre]
For every even integer $q\ge2$,
\[
 \left|\log\frac{V_q^{\rm aux}}{V_{2q-1}^{\rm aux}}
       -\kappa_*q^2\right|\le12q\log(8q),\qquad
 \kappa_*:=\frac92\log3-6\log2
           =\frac32\log(27/16)>0.                        \tag{AU.43}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The elementary binomial bounds
$4^j/(2j+1)\le\binom{2j}{j}\le4^j$ hold because the central
coefficient is the largest of the $2j+1$ coefficients summing to
$4^j$. Therefore (AU.41) gives
\[
 \log D_d=-d(d-1)\log2+E_d,\qquad |E_d|\le d\log(4d)
 \quad(d\ge1).                                           \tag{AU.44}
\]
At $s\ge1$, expand the numerator of $\mathcal C_s(2s)$ as
$2\sum_{i=1}^{s-1}(s-i)\log i$. The function
$f(x)=(1-x)\log x$ is increasing on $(0,1]$, has integral $-3/4$,
and is nonpositive. Its right Riemann sum differs from its integral by
a number between zero and $(\log s+1)/s$: the difference is bounded
above by $-\int_0^{1/s}f(x)dx\le(\log s+1)/s$, using
$-f(x)\le-\log x$. It follows that this numerator is
$s^2\log s-3s^2/2$ plus an error of absolute value at most
$s\log s+2s$.

The denominator's logarithm is
$s^2\log s+\sum_{a,b=0}^{s-1}\log(2+a/s+b/s)$.
Both partial derivatives of the latter function lie in $[0,1/2]$.
Its left-square Riemann sum differs from its integral by at most
$1/s$, hence the sum by at most $s$. Two integrations give
\[
 \int_0^1\!\int_0^1\log(2+x+y)\,dx\,dy
       =18\log2-9\log3-3/2.
\]
For example use the second antiderivative
$F(t)=t^2\log t/2-3t^2/4$ and calculate $F(4)-2F(3)+F(2)$.
Thus
\[
 \left|\log\mathcal C_s(2s)-9s^2\log(3/4)\right|
          \le s\log s+3s.
\]
For $c>0$, the change to $\mathcal C_s(2s+c)$ has logarithm
$-\sum_{a,b}\log(1+c/(2s+a+b))$, between $-cs/2$ and zero.
Using $c=1/2,3/2$ in (AU.42) proves
\[
 \left|\log B_q(q)-\frac92q^2\log(3/4)\right|
          \le q\log(q/2)+\frac72q.                       \tag{AU.45}
\]
Insert (AU.44)--(AU.45) into (AU.40). The quadratic coefficient is
$3\log2+(9/2)\log(3/4)=\kappa_*$. The remaining absolute error
is at most
\[
 \begin{split}
 &3q\log2+(q+1)\log(4(q+1))+2q\log(8q)\\
 &\hspace{10mm}+q\log(q/2)+\tfrac72q+\log((2q+1)/2).
 \end{split}
\]
For $q\ge2$, use $q+1\le3q/2$, $4(q+1)\le8q$,
$\log(q/2)\le\log(8q)$ and $\log((2q+1)/2)\le\log(8q)$.
The two constant multiples of $q$ are bounded together by
$3q\log(8q)$, since $3\log(16)>3\log2+7/2$.
Their sum is then at most $8q\log(8q)$, hence certainly the
displayed $12q\log(8q)$. This proves (AU.43) for every indicated
finite $q$, not only along a limit.
\end{proof}
