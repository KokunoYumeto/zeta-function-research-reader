\section{The first three original source costs at every tensor degree}

Retain the exact theta seed, its Fourier coordinate and its measure
\[
 w(t)\dd t=|g(1/2+\ii t)|^2\frac{\dd t}{2\pi},\qquad
 g=2\xi,\qquad \mu_j=\int_{\mathbb R}t^j w(t)\dd t.
\]
Equations (GS.1)--(GS.18) prove that $w$ is even, has positive mass
$\mu_0$, and has finite moments of every order. The certificate of
(GS.36)--(GS.37) encloses the original $\mu_0,\mu_2,\mu_4,\mu_6$.
This section transports those existing certified integrals by exact
rational arithmetic. It performs no further quadrature.

For each integer $k\ge1$ put $u=t_1+\cdots+t_k$,
$m_k=w^{*k}$ and $M_j(k)=\int u^j m_k(u)\dd u$.
Fubini and evenness give the full first four even sum moments
\begin{align}
 M_0(k)&=\mu_0^k,\notag\\
 M_2(k)&=k\mu_2\mu_0^{k-1},\notag\\
 M_4(k)&=k\mu_4\mu_0^{k-1}
          +3k(k-1)\mu_2^2\mu_0^{k-2},\notag\\
 M_6(k)&=k\mu_6\mu_0^{k-1}
          +15k(k-1)\mu_4\mu_2\mu_0^{k-2}
          +15k(k-1)(k-2)\mu_2^3\mu_0^{k-3}.
 \tag{GS.41}
\end{align}
Here every missing term integrates to zero because at least one
factor has an odd moment. For $M_4$, the surviving exponent
partitions are $4$ and $2+2$. The second has
$\binom{k}{2}\,4!/(2!2!)=3k(k-1)$ terms. For $M_6$ the
partitions are $6$, $4+2$ and $2+2+2$; their numbers of terms
are respectively $k$, $k(k-1)6!/(4!2!)=15k(k-1)$ and
$\binom{k}{3}6!/(2!2!2!)=15k(k-1)(k-2)$.
The second-moment expansion has precisely $k$ surviving terms.
These finite counts prove (GS.41) with its full mass factors.
Because $\mu_0>0$, the displayed powers are defined even when
a zero combinatorial coefficient accompanies a negative exponent.

All nonzero polynomial norms in $L^2(m_k\dd u)$ are strictly
positive. Indeed the nonnegative density $m_k$ has positive mass
$\mu_0^k$, so its positivity set has positive Lebesgue measure.
A nonzero polynomial has finitely many real roots, as repeated
division by its distinct linear factors cannot exceed its degree.
Its squared modulus times $m_k$ is therefore positive on a set of
positive measure, and its integral is positive. This proves every
positivity and division below for the actual source.

Put $\nu=\mu_2/\mu_0$. With the norms taken in the unchanged
measure $m_k\dd u$, the first four monic orthogonal polynomials
and all their squared norms are
\begin{align}
 p_0(u)&=1,&h_0(k)&=M_0(k),\notag\\
 p_1(u)&=u,&h_1(k)&=M_2(k),\notag\\
 p_2(u)&=u^2-k\nu,&h_2(k)&=M_4(k)-\frac{M_2(k)^2}{M_0(k)},\notag\\
 p_3(u)&=u^3-\frac{M_4(k)}{M_2(k)}u,
 &h_3(k)&=M_6(k)-\frac{M_4(k)^2}{M_2(k)}.
 \tag{GS.42}
\end{align}
To verify orthogonality of $p_2$, its pairing with $1$ is
$M_2-k\nu M_0=0$, and its pairing with $u$ is zero by evenness.
For $p_3$, parity gives zero against $1$ and $p_2$; its pairing
with $u$ is $M_4-(M_4/M_2)M_2=0$. Expanding each squared
polynomial gives the norms in (GS.42). Uniqueness follows because
the difference of two monic orthogonal polynomials of the same
degree lies in the lower-degree space, is orthogonal to itself,
and hence is zero by the preceding positivity argument.

Define the positive original monic source costs $a_j^{(k)}=h_j(k)/h_{j-1}(k)$.
These are exactly the lower coefficients in the real-coordinate
three-term recurrence: in $u p_j$, its pairing with $p_{j-1}$
equals the pairing of $p_j$ with $u p_{j-1}$, which is $h_j$
because $u p_{j-1}-p_j$ has lower degree. Pairings with lower
degrees vanish by the same argument. The degree-$j$ coefficient
vanishes by evenness. Therefore $u p_j=p_{j+1}+a_j^{(k)}p_{j-1}$.
For $j=3$, the next monic polynomial $p_4$ exists by finite
Gram orthogonalization, whose Gram is positive definite by the
proved polynomial norm property; its full formula is not needed
to determine the already specified coefficient $h_3/h_2$.
Substituting (GS.41) into (GS.42) gives
\begin{align}
 a_1^{(k)}&=k\nu,\notag\\
 a_2^{(k)}&=\frac{\mu_4}{\mu_2}+(2k-3)\nu,\notag\\
 a_3^{(k)}&=
 \frac{M_6(k)-M_4(k)^2/M_2(k)}
      {M_4(k)-M_2(k)^2/M_0(k)}.\tag{GS.43}
\end{align}
For the second equality one obtains
$M_4/M_2=\mu_4/\mu_2+3(k-1)\nu$ and
$M_2/M_0=k\nu$, whose exact difference is the displayed cost.
For an explicit expanded version of the third equality, write
$r_4=\mu_4/\mu_0$ and $r_6=\mu_6/\mu_0$, solely as the
indicated scalar quotients of the original moments. Then
\begin{equation}
 a_3^{(k)}=
 \frac{r_6-r_4^2/\nu+9(k-1)r_4\nu
                  +3(k-1)(2k-7)\nu^3}
      {r_4+(2k-3)\nu^2}.\tag{GS.44}
\end{equation}
To check every term, divide the numerator of (GS.43) by
$k\mu_0^k$ using (GS.41). The square term contributes
$r_4^2/\nu+6(k-1)r_4\nu+9(k-1)^2\nu^3$; subtracting it
from $r_6+15(k-1)r_4\nu+15(k-1)(k-2)\nu^3$ gives
the numerator in (GS.44). The denominator of (GS.43), divided
by the same $k\mu_0^k$, is $r_4+(2k-3)\nu^2$.
Thus both original norms and all mass factors are recovered from
these scalar abbreviations.

The original spectral coordinate is $S=k/2+\ii u$. Let $c_k=k/2$
and define the algebra isomorphism
\begin{equation}
 \Psi_k:\mathbb C[S]\longrightarrow\mathbb C[u],\qquad
 (\Psi_kP)(u)=P(c_k+\ii u),\qquad
 \Psi_k^{-1}p(S)=p((S-c_k)/\ii).\tag{GS.45}
\end{equation}
Direct substitution proves the two inverse identities. Give
$\mathbb C[S]$ the source norm
$\int|P(c_k+\ii u)|^2m_k(u)\dd u$; then $\Psi_k$ is a
linear isometry onto the polynomial source in the real coordinate.
On the degree-$j$ monic affine space, its phase-preserving monic
inverse is $p\mapsto P_j=\ii^j\Psi_k^{-1}p$.
Since $|\ii^j|=1$, this map preserves the original norm and has
inverse $P\mapsto\ii^{-j}\Psi_kP$ on that affine space. In
particular the complete first four spectral monic polynomials are
\begin{align}
 P_0&=1,\quad P_1=S-c_k,\quad P_2=(S-c_k)^2+k\nu,\notag\\
 P_3&=(S-c_k)^3+
      \left\{\frac{\mu_4}{\mu_2}+3(k-1)\nu\right\}(S-c_k).
 \tag{GS.46}
\end{align}
Their squared norms are exactly $h_0,h_1,h_2,h_3$ from (GS.42).
Multiplying the real recurrence by $\ii^{j+1}$ proves the
spectral recurrence with its actual lower sign,
\begin{equation}
 S P_j=P_{j+1}+c_kP_j-a_j^{(k)}P_{j-1}.\tag{GS.47}
\end{equation}
Thus the positive norm ratio $a_j^{(k)}$ and the negative lower
coefficient in the $S$ presentation have their exact phase map.

To connect these costs to the existing Gamma determinant corrections,
let $D_n$ be the determinant of the source Gram on $1,u,\ldots,u^{n-1}$,
with $D_0=1$, and let $D_n^\Gamma$ use the Gamma sum measure
$r_{13/4}^{*k}\dd u$. Its mass is $c_{13/4}^k$, and its monic
norms are $c_{13/4}^k j!(13k/2)_j$, by (GS.23), Fourier
uniqueness and (GS.28). Specifically Fourier transformation of
the convolution gives $c_{13/4}^k\cosh^{-13k/2}y$; division by
the mass constant in (GS.23) identifies its density as a positive
scalar times $r_{13k/4}$, proving the asserted norms without any
change to $u$. Monic orthogonalization is triangular with diagonal
one, so $D_n=\prod_{j<n}h_j$ and the analogous Gamma product
holds. Define $X_n=D_n/D_n^\Gamma$ and
\begin{equation}
 Q_N=\frac{X_{N+2}X_N}{X_{N+1}^2},\qquad
 a_{N+1}^{(k)}=(N+1)(N+13k/2)Q_N.\tag{GS.48}
\end{equation}
Dividing the two consecutive determinant products proves the second
identity. In particular, with $\eta=\mu_4/\mu_2$,
\begin{align}
 Q_0&=\frac{2\nu}{13},\notag\\
 Q_1(k)&=\frac{\eta+(2k-3)\nu}{13k+2}
        =Q_0+\frac{\rho}{13k+2},\qquad
 \rho=\eta-\frac{43}{13}\nu,\notag\\
 Q_2(k)&=\frac{2a_3^{(k)}}{39k+12}.\tag{GS.49}
\end{align}
Every equality is obtained by substituting (GS.43) into (GS.48).
No volume of a nonzero packet quotient enters these source costs.

The pure rational checker reads the exact dyadic moment endpoints
from the pinned GS.36 certificate and propagates them through
(GS.43)--(GS.49) using \texttt{fractions.Fraction} exclusively.
For intervals $[a,b]$ and $[c,d]$, it uses endpoint addition,
$[a-d,b-c]$ for subtraction, the minimum and maximum of the four
endpoint products for multiplication, and multiplication by
$[1/d,1/c]$ for division after checking $c>0$. These rules follow
from linearity in each variable and the decreasing map $x\mapsto1/x$
on the positive axis. Thus they preserve inclusion without any
floating-point operation. Applying (GS.37) with exact rational
floor and ceiling yields, among the saved intervals,
\begin{align*}
 \nu\in[&10.2075647534857216888657612522375551749964,\\[-2pt]
         &10.2075647534857216888657612522375551749967],\\
 Q_0\in[&1.5703945774593417982870401926519315653839,\\[-2pt]
         &1.5703945774593417982870401926519315653842],\\
 a_2^{(1)}\in[&19.1496329281085602472067784658376980738791,\\[-2pt]
               &19.1496329281085602472067784658376980738794],\\
 \rho\in[&-4.4062857337815667270988244239412754068822,\\[-2pt]
          &-4.4062857337815667270988244239412754068819].
\end{align*}
All four widths are $3\cdot10^{-40}$. The receipt also encloses
$\mu_4/\mu_2$, $\mu_4\mu_0-\mu_2^2$, $a_2^{(2)}$, $Q_1(1)$,
$a_3^{(1)}$ and $Q_2(1)$. In particular $\rho<0$ and (GS.49)
give the exact fixed-degree all-$k$ comparison
\begin{equation}
 0<Q_1(1)\le Q_1(k)<Q_0,\qquad
 Q_1(k+1)-Q_1(k)=\frac{-13\rho}{(13k+2)(13k+15)}>0.
 \tag{GS.50}
\end{equation}
The positive lower bound follows either from $h_2/h_1>0$ or its
certified $k=1$ enclosure; the difference follows by literal
subtraction in (GS.49). The computation concerns original source
polynomial degrees at most three, at every specified tensor degree.
The analytic seed still has quotient $\mathbb C[S]/(1)=0$, its
quotient determinant is the empty determinant one, and every
nonzero source norm above remains before that zero quotient.
