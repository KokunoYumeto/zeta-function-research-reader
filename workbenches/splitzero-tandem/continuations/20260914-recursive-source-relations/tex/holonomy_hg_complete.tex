\subsubsection{The actual relation norm as a metric endomorphism}

Fix $N\ge q-1$ and let $J:\mathcal P_N\twoheadrightarrow E$ be the
original polynomial remainder map. Denote the literal multiplication
matrix of $\chi$ by $B_{\rm rel}:\mathbb C^{N+1-q}\hookrightarrow
\mathcal P_N$. For $N=q-1$ its domain is zero. Let $\Psi_N$ be the

\[
 (\mathcal U_k F)(r,z)=e^{(kr+\sum_{i<k}z_i)/2}
 F(e^{r+z_1},\ldots,e^{r+z_{k-1}},e^r),\qquad
 \mathcal K=L^2(\mathbb R^{k-1},dz).
 \tag{HG1}
\]
The logarithmic Jacobian is $d^kx=e^{kr+\sum z_i}\,dr\,dz$.
Thus the displayed map preserves the original source norm, and
$\mathcal U_kD^{(k)}=(-\partial_r+k/2)\mathcal U_k$.
For $L>0$, define
\[
\begin{gathered}
 (\mathcal Z_{L,\theta}\psi)(r)=
       \sum_{a\in\mathbb Z}e^{ia\theta}\psi(r+aL),\quad 0\le r<L,
 \qquad d\mu(\theta)=\frac{d\theta}{2\pi},\\
 M=\Psi_N^*\Psi_N,\qquad
 M_\theta=(\mathcal Z_{L,\theta}\Psi_N)^*
                    (\mathcal Z_{L,\theta}\Psi_N).
\end{gathered}\tag{HG2}
\]
For a square-integrable source, Fourier series in the translation index
give $\int\|\mathcal Z_{L,\theta}\psi\|^2d\mu=\|\psi\|^2$.
Polarization gives $\int M_\theta d\mu=M$. The inverse is
$\psi(r+aL)=\int e^{-ia\theta}\mathcal Z_{L,\theta}\psi(r)d\mu$,
first as a Fourier coefficient for almost every $r$, and hence in the
original Hilbert space. The actual finite columns have the weighted
regularity established in the source construction, so their sums and
derivatives converge in the stated norms.

Keep the two weighted source Grams and their actual comparison value
\[
 M_{a,\pm}=(e^{\pm ar}\Psi_N)^*(e^{\pm ar}\Psi_N),\qquad
 \kappa=\max_{v\ne0}\frac{v^*(M_{a,+}+M_{a,-})v}{v^*Mv},\qquad
 \delta=\frac{\kappa}{e^{aL}-1},\quad a>0.
 \tag{HG3}
\]
Their original coordinate weights are $x_k^{\pm2a}$. For
$\mathcal C(s)=\int\Psi_N(r)^*\Psi_N(r+s)\,dr$, weighted
Cauchy--Schwarz gives, for $s\ge0$,
$|v^*\mathcal C(s)v|\le e^{-as}
\sqrt{(v^*M_{a,-}v)(v^*M_{a,+}v)}$; the bound at $-s$ follows by
adjunction. Unfolding the periodization gives
$M_\theta=\sum_{b\in\mathbb Z}e^{ib\theta}\mathcal C(bL)$.
Sum the two tails and use $2\sqrt{uv}\le u+v$ to obtain
$-\delta M\preceq M_\theta-M\preceq\delta M$.
Choose $L$ as explicitly specified below so that $\delta<1$.
This proves positivity of every phase Gram in the admitted original
source, including the cases where individual sampled weights vanish.

Retain its canonical sections and Grams:
\[
\begin{gathered}
 G=(JM^{-1}J^*)^{-1},\quad C=M^{-1}J^*G,\\
 G_\theta=(JM_\theta^{-1}J^*)^{-1},\quad
 C_\theta=M_\theta^{-1}J^*G_\theta,\qquad
 \overline G=\int G_\theta\,d\mu .
\end{gathered}\tag{HG4}
\]
Completing the square on $J^{-1}(v)$ proves these are exactly the
minimum-norm representatives. In particular $JC=JC_\theta=I$ and
$B_{\rm rel}^*MC=B_{\rm rel}^*M_\theta C_\theta=0$.
There is a unique $X_\theta$ with
\[
\begin{gathered}
 C-C_\theta=B_{\rm rel}X_\theta,\qquad
 X_\theta=(B_{\rm rel}^*M_\theta B_{\rm rel})^{-1}
                       B_{\rm rel}^*M_\theta C,\\
 \mathscr D=\int (C-C_\theta)^*M_\theta(C-C_\theta)\,d\mu,
 \qquad G=\overline G+\mathscr D .
\end{gathered}\tag{HG5}
\]
The equality follows by expanding $C=C_\theta+B_{\rm rel}X_\theta$:
the two cross terms vanish by the displayed orthogonality, and
$\int C^*M_\theta C\,d\mu=C^*MC=G$. Empty relation columns give
$C=C_\theta=J^{-1}$ and $\mathscr D=0$ directly.
Every $B_{\rm rel}X_\theta v$ is multiplication by the original $\chi$;
it has the original monic-division theta primitive in the retained
tensor complex. In particular this positive norm is evaluated on actual
relations before passing to their cohomology class.

Here is a complete proof of the quadratic bound used below. For
$1-\delta\le x\le1+\delta$,
\[
 \frac{2-x}{1-\delta^2}-\frac1x
 =\frac{\delta^2-(x-1)^2}{x(1-\delta^2)}\ge0 .
 \tag{HG6}
\]
Apply the finite spectral theorem to the positive $M$-self-adjoint
endomorphism $M^{-1}M_\theta$ and then the original dual map $M^{-1}$.
This gives
$M_\theta^{-1}\preceq
(2M^{-1}-M^{-1}M_\theta M^{-1})/(1-\delta^2)$.
Consequently $\overline K=\int JM_\theta^{-1}J^*d\mu
\preceq G^{-1}/(1-\delta^2)$.
Each block matrix with rows $(G_\theta^{-1},I)$ and $(I,G_\theta)$
is positive: its quadratic form at $(x,y)$ is
$(x+G_\theta y)^*G_\theta^{-1}(x+G_\theta y)$.
Averaging and taking the Schur complement proves
$\overline G\succeq\overline K^{-1}\succeq(1-\delta^2)G$.
Together with (HG5) this proves
\[
 0\preceq\mathscr D\preceq\delta^2G,\qquad
 \mathsf D=G^{-1}\mathscr D=\mathsf D^{\dagger_G},\qquad
 0\preceq_G\mathsf D\preceq_G\delta^2I,\qquad
 G^{-1}\overline G=I-\mathsf D .
 \tag{HG7}
\]
The original inner product convention is $\langle v,w\rangle_G=v^*Gw$.
No metric or vector mass is replaced in this construction.

\subsubsection{The full commutator receives the quadratic relation cost}

Put $T_t=A+tR-kI/2$ for the original complex parameter $t$ and real
integer $k$. For any actual positive Gram $H$ on $E$, let
\[
 X_H=T_t^{\dagger_H}+T_t,
 \qquad \epsilon_H(t)=\|X_H\|_H,
 \qquad B=G^{-1}(H-G).
 \tag{HG8}
\]
The endomorphism $B$ is $G$-self-adjoint. Suppose its actual spectral
lower bound is $I+B\succeq_G\alpha I$ with $\alpha>0$; the concrete
holonomy and finite values of $\alpha$ are proved below. Let
$\mathsf S=(I+B)^{1/2}$ be the positive square root in the $G$ metric.
It is the exact isometry $(E,H)\to(E,G)$ because
$\mathsf S^*G\mathsf S=G(I+B)=H$. Direct multiplication gives
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \mathsf S X_H\mathsf S^{-1}-X_G=Z+Z^{\dagger_G},
 \qquad Z=[\mathsf S,T_t]\mathsf S^{-1},\\
 [\mathsf S,T_t]=\int_0^\infty
   e^{-r\mathsf S}[B,T_t]e^{-r\mathsf S}\,dr .
\end{gathered}\tag{HG9}
\]
For the first equality expand the left side as
$\mathsf S^{-1}T_t^{\dagger_G}\mathsf S+
\mathsf ST_t\mathsf S^{-1}-T_t^{\dagger_G}-T_t$.
Its two ordered terms are exactly $Z^{\dagger_G}$ and $Z$.
For the second equality, both sides solve the Sylvester equation
$\mathsf S Y+Y\mathsf S=[B,T_t]$.
The integral converges since
$\|e^{-r\mathsf S}\|_G\le e^{-r\sqrt\alpha}$.
Differentiate the integrand to verify the equation, with its boundary
value $[B,T_t]$ at zero and zero at infinity. In the spectral
decomposition of $\mathsf S$, a homogeneous solution has each block
multiplied by a strictly positive sum of two eigenvalues, so is zero.
This proves the claimed integral equality, including repeated eigenvalues.
Its norm is at most $\|[B,T_t]\|_G/(2\sqrt\alpha)$.
The isometry, reverse triangle inequality, and
$\|\mathsf S^{-1}\|_G\le\alpha^{-1/2}$ therefore prove
\[
 |\epsilon_H(t)-\epsilon_G(t)|
 \le\|Z+Z^{\dagger_G}\|_G
 \le\alpha^{-1}\|[B,A]+t[B,R]\|_G .
 \tag{HG10}
\]
This is the full actual-metric transfer supplied by the coordinating
Zeta task, here proved for the present maps and parameters.

For the exact phase mean set $H=\overline G$. Equations (HG5)--(HG7)
give $B=-\mathsf D$ and $\alpha=1-\delta^2$. Hence
\[
\begin{gathered}
 |\epsilon_{\overline G}(t)-\epsilon_G(t)|
 \le\|Z+Z^{\dagger_G}\|_G
 \le\frac{\|[\mathsf D,A]+t[\mathsf D,R]\|_G}{1-\delta^2},\\
 \frac{\|[\mathsf D,A]+t[\mathsf D,R]\|_G}{1-\delta^2}
 \le\frac{\delta^2}{1-\delta^2}\|A+tR-kI/2\|_G .
\end{gathered}\tag{HG11}
\]
Here is the full spectral argument for the last inequality. For any
$G$-self-adjoint $B$ with distinct eigenvalues
$b_1<\cdots<b_r$, retain its original spectral projections and put
$P_j=\mathbf1_{(b_j,\infty)}(B)$. The exact decomposition is
$B=b_1I+\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}(b_{j+1}-b_j)P_j$.
In the $G$-orthogonal decomposition into the image and kernel of
$P_j$, the commutator $[P_j,T]$ has block rows $(0,T_{12})$
and $(-T_{21},0)$. Its norm is
$\max(\|T_{12}\|,\|T_{21}\|)\le\|T\|_G$, as follows by
applying the two off-diagonal blocks to the orthogonal components of
an arbitrary vector. Consequently
\[
 \|[B,T]\|_G\le (b_r-b_1)\|T\|_G .\tag{HG11a}
\]
When $r=1$, the sum is empty and the commutator is zero.
This proof retains the complete eigenvalue data and every spectral
subspace. Applying it to (HG7) gives the last inequality in (HG11);
the exact commutator in its preceding line remains available.
Thus the original
source-relative phase error $\delta$ reaches the generator through
a proved quadratic allowance. If $[\mathsf D,T_t]=0$, the integral
in (HG9) is zero and the two control radii agree exactly. At $N=q-1$
this equality holds for every $t$ because $\mathscr D=0$.

\subsubsection{Exact residue contribution from two original Gram determinants}

For any $G$-self-adjoint $B$ in (HG8), retain $x=e_0$ and
$y=G^{-1}\ell^*$. Then $\ell(v)=\langle y,v\rangle_G$ and
$R=xy^{\dagger_G}$. Define the maps $U,V:\mathbb C^2\to E$ and
the six actual scalar moments
\[
\begin{gathered}
 U=[Bx,x],\qquad V=[y,-By],\qquad [B,R]=UV^{\dagger_G},\\
 a_0=\|Bx\|_G^2,\quad c_0=\|x\|_G^2,\quad
 d_0=\|y\|_G^2,\quad f_0=\|By\|_G^2,\\
 p_0=\langle x,Bx\rangle_G\in\mathbb R,\qquad
 r_0=\langle y,By\rangle_G\in\mathbb R .
\end{gathered}\tag{HG12}
\]
Self-adjointness proves the two reality statements. The signed
factorization follows on a vector $v$ from
$Bx\langle y,v\rangle_G-x\langle By,v\rangle_G
=Bx\ell(v)-x\ell(Bv)$. Its two coefficient Grams are
\[
\begin{gathered}
 P=U^{\dagger_G}U=\begin{pmatrix}a_0&p_0\\p_0&c_0\end{pmatrix},
 \quad Q=V^{\dagger_G}V=\begin{pmatrix}d_0&-r_0\\-r_0&f_0\end{pmatrix},\\
 T_{\rm res}=\operatorname{tr}(PQ)=a_0d_0+c_0f_0-2p_0r_0,\qquad
 \Delta_{\rm res}=(a_0c_0-p_0^2)(d_0f_0-r_0^2).
\end{gathered}\tag{HG13}
\]
Both negative cross entries and their complete contribution remain.
Let $K=UV^{\dagger_G}$. The nonzero eigenvalues of
$K^{\dagger_G}K=VPV^{\dagger_G}$, with multiplicities, are those
of $PQ$. To see this without a rank assumption, compute the determinant
of the block matrix with rows $(I,-zA_0)$ and $(B_0,I)$ by either
diagonal block elimination. This proves
$\det(I+zA_0B_0)=\det(I+zB_0A_0)$ for rectangular maps. Apply it to
$A_0=V$, $B_0=PV^{\dagger_G}$.
Applying the same identity in dimension two proves that $PQ$ has the
same characteristic polynomial as the positive semidefinite matrix
$P^{1/2}QP^{1/2}$, including when $P$ is singular. Its two eigenvalues
are therefore real and nonnegative. Their sum and product are (HG13).
It follows that
\[
 \boxed{\|[B,R]\|_G^2=
 \frac{T_{\rm res}+\sqrt{T_{\rm res}^2-4\Delta_{\rm res}}}{2}.}
 \tag{HG14}
\]
The square root is the nonnegative real one. The discriminant is
nonnegative by the positive spectrum just proved, and both factors of
$\Delta_{\rm res}$ are nonnegative Gram determinants.

There is no division by a vector mass or determinant. If
$T_{\rm res}=0$, positivity gives $K=0$ and $\Delta_{\rm res}=0$.
If $\Delta_{\rm res}=0<T_{\rm res}$, the rank is one and its norm
is $\sqrt{T_{\rm res}}$. If $\Delta_{\rm res}>0$, the rank is two;
a zero discriminant retains both equal singular-value squares
$T_{\rm res}/2$. For $q=1$, write $B=bI$ with $b$ real. Then
$a_0=b^2c_0$, $p_0=bc_0$, $f_0=b^2d_0$, $r_0=bd_0$, which give
$T_{\rm res}=\Delta_{\rm res}=0$ with the original masses unchanged.
Repeated roots of $\chi$ do not enter any inversion in this argument.

For the holonomy application use $B=-\mathsf D$ in (HG12)--(HG14).
One may bound the full allowance by
\[
 \frac{\|[\mathsf D,A]+t[\mathsf D,R]\|_G}{1-\delta^2}
 \le\frac{\|[\mathsf D,A]\|_G+
 |t|\sqrt{(T_{\rm res}+
 \sqrt{T_{\rm res}^2-4\Delta_{\rm res}})/2}}{1-\delta^2}.
 \tag{HG15}
\]
The left side retains cancellation between the arithmetic and residue
commutators. The right side is its triangle-inequality bound, while
(HG14) itself is an exact norm. The complete rank-two calculation and
degenerate cases incorporate the coordinating Zeta task's residue proof.
