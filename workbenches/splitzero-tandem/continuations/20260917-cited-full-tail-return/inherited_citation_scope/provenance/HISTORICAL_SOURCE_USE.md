# Human sources behind the historical construction

This additive attribution note identifies the human mathematical literature behind the programme's original web-session development. It is part of the effort to make the research legible and its provenance inspectable. It does not replace the mathematical papers or certify that every cited theorem has been independently reproved.

The programme's subsequent calculations should be read alongside their human sources. A project calculation can establish a new comparison or specialize a construction without thereby inventing the machinery on which it relies. We distinguish a theorem actually invoked, a framework used to define an object, a source that inspired a research route, and a historical direction that is no longer part of the active calculation.

## Deligne's results and the comparisons made with them

Pierre Deligne's *La conjecture de Weil II*, Publications Mathématiques de l'IHÉS 52 (1980), 137–252, DOI[10.1007/BF02684780](https://www.numdam.org/item/PMIHES_1980__52__137_0/), is a central human source. The historical construction explicitly consulted the compatible monodromy directions of §§1.7.8–1.7.10, the weight arguments of §3.2, the compact-support and duality results of §§3.3.1–3.3.6, and the derived and specialization formulations in §6.2. These result numbers must remain attached to the specific use; a generic citation to “Deligne” is insufficient.

The programme compares its own explicitly constructed maps and metrics with this machinery. Deligne's finite-field theorems are not themselves statements about every new complex operator introduced in the programme. Attribution must therefore show both the original human theorem and the separately proved project comparison, including its domain of validity. This distinction leaves the continuing research problem open and precisely located.

A corrected internal reference also leads to Deligne's *La conjecture de Weil I*, Publications Mathématiques de l'IHÉS 43 (1974), 273–307, DOI[10.1007/BF02684373](https://www.numdam.org/item/PMIHES_1974__43__273_0/), §8.11. It should not be replaced by a similarly numbered subsection of *Weil II*.

## The human chain does not stop at the last cited paper

The consulted *Weil II* passages themselves point to earlier work:3.2.15 cites SGA 4½, `[dualité]` 1.3 and 2.1; 3.3.2–3.3.3 cite SGA 7, Exposé XXI, §5 and 5.2.2; 6.2.11 cites the generic-acyclicity results in SGA 4½ `[Th.finitude]`. These are explicit source-to-source edges. Their exposé authors and collaborators belong in the resulting bibliography, rather than being replaced by the title of an AI-generated reading note.

The residue/trace calculation invokes SGA 5, Exposé III, §6.8. The appropriate credit is **Alexander Grothendieck, written up by Luc Illusie**, with Jean-Louis Verdier's pairing retained in the attribution. Illusie's [firsthand account](https://www.imo.universite-paris-saclay.fr/~luc.illusie/Chronology-SGA5-1.pdf) explains the respective contributions, and his [SGA 5, Exposé III errata](https://www.imo.universite-paris-saclay.fr/~luc.illusie/ErrSGA5III.pdf) form part of the source record. The historical review also identifies a reference from 6.8.5 to Raynaud's one-variable treatment. Its exact bibliographic identity remains unresolved in this pass and must not be guessed.

Different SGA volumes and exposés have different contributors. An acronym is not an adequate substitute for those humans. For example, the Arcata exposition of Deligne in SGA 4½ was written up by Jean-François Boutot; that volume also includes an appendix by Illusie and Verdier's derived-category text. The relevant contributor should be credited where the corresponding material is used.

## Coverage and remaining attribution work

This pass mechanically scanned all 215 readable records in the retained original transcript and six original source snapshots, and read selected named-source and dependency passages. It recovered 305 file-reference objects and 117 grouped web-reference objects. Ninety-eight file references are explicitly hidden or invalid in the retained metadata and remain unresolved here. The original share redacts 2,600 tool outputs; this note does not pretend to recover their contents.

The resulting private ledger distinguishes name matches from actual source use and preserves exact source locators. It finds 199 name, eponym or source candidates, not 199 verified human authors. For example, Fan Zheng and Ky Fan must not be merged, and a named mathematical eponym does not prove that its original historical paper was consulted. The bibliography must resolve those identities and roles from the actual cited work.

The broad recursive audit remains open where primary text, exact edition or theorem-level linkage is missing. Such gaps are recorded as gaps. They are neither silently filled with plausible references nor treated as a reason to stop the continuing mathematics.
