# Historical ideal-class scalar convention: exact correction already present in the successor

Checked 17 September 2026. This is a localized historical correction. It does not change the tau construction or attribute a defect to the current 692-page research edition.

[A Note on the Globalization Semiring G(Z), DOI 10.5281/zenodo.18976982](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.18976982), deposited `globalization_note.pdf`, p. 12, Definition 6.3 and Theorem 6.4; the proof continues on p. 13. The displayed relation is

\[
I_1\sim I_2\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists a,b\in S(\mathcal O_K)\setminus\{\tau\}:aI_1=bI_2.
\]

But \(S(\mathcal O_K)\setminus\{\tau\}\) is the entire supported copy of \(\mathcal O_K\), including its ring zero \(e=0_{\mathcal O_K}\). For each nonzero integral ideal \(J\subseteq\mathcal O_K\), let \(I_J=J\sqcup\{\tau\}\). The operation table gives

\[
eI_J=\{e,\tau\}.
\]

Indeed every supported element of \(J\) is sent to \(e\); \(\tau\) is sent to \(\tau\); and \(J\) is nonempty. Therefore \(a=b=e\) witnesses \(I_J\sim I_L\) for **every** pair of nonzero integral ideals \(J,L\). The displayed relation consequently cannot recover nontrivial ideal classes.

There is a second domain distinction. In the semiring, the zero ideal is \(\{\tau\}\). The ideal \(I_{(0)}=\{\tau,e\}\) is nonzero as a semiring ideal, but does not arise from a nonzero ring ideal. The target recovering the classical class group must be the lifted ideals \(I_J\) with \(J\ne(0)\), not merely all semiring ideals other than \(\{\tau\}\).

## Complete corrected comparison

Let \(R\) be an integral domain, \(K=\operatorname{Frac}(R)\), and let \(\mathcal I^+(R)\) denote the set of nonzero integral ideals. Define

\[
\mathcal I^+_G(R)=\{I_J=J\sqcup\{\tau\}:J\in\mathcal I^+(R)\}.
\]

On \(\mathcal I^+(R)\), define

\[
J\sim_R L\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists a,b\in R\setminus\{0\}:aJ=bL.
\]

On \(\mathcal I^+_G(R)\), use supported nonzero ring scalars:

\[
I_J\sim_G I_L\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists a,b\in R\setminus\{0\}:a^\bullet I_J=b^\bullet I_L.
\]

For every permitted \(a\), direct multiplication gives

\[
a^\bullet I_J=(aJ)^\bullet\sqcup\{\tau\}.
\]

Intersecting an equality of these lifted sets with the supported copy of \(R\) therefore proves

\[
a^\bullet I_J=b^\bullet I_L\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad aJ=bL.
\]

Thus the bijection \(J\mapsto I_J\), whose inverse is intersection with the supported copy, preserves and reflects the class relation. The relation is an equivalence: reflexivity uses \(a=b=1\), symmetry swaps the witnesses, and if \(aJ=bL\) and \(cL=dM\), then \(caJ=bdM\), with both witnesses nonzero because \(R\) is a domain.

The product of ideals means all finite sums of pairwise products. Consequently

\[
I_JI_L=I_{JL}.
\]

For the forward inclusion, each pairwise product is either \(\tau\) or the supported copy of a product \(jl\); a finite sum is accordingly \(\tau\) or a supported element of \(JL\). Conversely \(\tau\) is a product involving \(\tau\), and every element of \(JL\) is a finite sum of products \(jl\), so its supported copy lies in \(I_JI_L\). Nonzero ideals have nonzero product in a domain: choose \(j\ne0\) and \(l\ne0\); then \(jl\ne0\). The map is therefore an isomorphism of multiplicative monoids before taking classes. The relation respects multiplication, since \(aJ=bL\) implies \(aJH=bLH\). Hence it induces a monoid isomorphism

\[
\mathcal I^+(R)/\!\sim_R\ \xrightarrow{\sim}\
\mathcal I^+_G(R)/\!\sim_G.
\]

Equivalently, \(J\sim_R L\) exactly when \(\alpha J=L\) for some \(\alpha\in K^\times\). One direction takes \(\alpha=a/b\). Conversely write \(\alpha=a/b\) with nonzero \(a,b\in R\), and multiply \(\alpha J=L\) by \(b\). Thus the corrected relation is precisely scaling by a nonzero fraction, not by an arbitrary supported element.

When \(R=\mathcal O_K\) is a ring of integers, the classical ideal-class group is the group of invertible nonzero fractional ideals modulo the principal ideals generated by \(K^\times\); the standard Dedekind-domain theorem gives invertibility. Every nonzero fractional ideal \(F\) has a nonzero denominator \(d\in R\) with \(dF\) integral, so every fractional class has an integral representative. The displayed \(K^\times\)-criterion proves that two integral representatives define the same fractional class exactly when they are equivalent under \(\sim_R\). This identifies the left-hand side with the usual \(\mathrm{Cl}(K)\), and the right-hand side with it by the proved map. The human number-theory references are Jürgen Neukirch, *Algebraic Number Theory* (1999), and James S. Milne, *Algebraic Number Theory*, chapter 3 on Dedekind domains and chapter 4 on class groups. This report does not claim those authors used the project's semiring terminology.
