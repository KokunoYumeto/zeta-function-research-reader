\section{A polynomial forward bound for the full weighted conductor}
\label{sec:WCF}

The original Split-Zero programme and the deposited source notes retain
their existing attribution \cite{human:globalization2026,human:splitzero2026}.
The new calculation in this section acts on the literal SGT10 matrix. It
improves its forward bound at ranks of order $a^2$. Its exact triangular
determinant then controls every inverse exterior rank and supplies a
two-sided four-endpoint comparison for the entire conductor block.

\subsection{Original spaces, mass, phases and matrix}

Fix the original hypothetical simple quartet, its original period and
logarithm branch. Retain $0<\delta<1/2$, $\gamma>2$, and all original
coefficients $a_{uw}$, including repeated contributions. Put
\[
\begin{gathered}
 a=4l+1\ge9,\quad q=(a+1)^2,\quad q'=(a-7)^2,\quad
 \beta_{n,u,w}=n/2+(2u-n)\delta+i(2w-n)\gamma,\\
 E_A(z)=\sum_{u,w=0}^{8}a_{uw}e^{\beta_{8,u,w}z},\quad
 \mu_n=E_A^{(n)}(0),\quad v=\operatorname{ord}_0E_A\le80,
 \quad \mu_v\ne0,\quad A_{\rm abs}=\sum_{u,w=0}^{8}|a_{uw}|,\\
 s\in\{1,a\},\quad b=s/2,\quad c_0=a/2,\quad c_1=a/2-4,
 \quad N=D+v,\quad D\ge0.
\end{gathered}\tag{WCF1}
\]
The symbol and its coefficients are fixed as $a$ varies. The Hermitian
inner product is conjugate-linear in its first variable. The original
polynomial norm, orthonormal frame, and leading coefficient are
\[
\begin{gathered}
 dm_s(y)=\frac{(2\pi)^{s/2}2^{s/2}}{4\pi\Gamma(s/2)}
       |\Gamma(s/4+iy/2)|^2\,dy,\quad M_s=(2\pi)^{s/2},\\
 \|P\|_{s,c}^2=\int_{\mathbb R}|P(c+iy)|^2dm_s(y),\qquad
 p_{0,s}=1,\quad p_{1,s}=y,\\
 p_{n+1,s}=yp_{n,s}-n(n-1+s/2)p_{n-1,s},\quad
 h_{n,s}=M_sn!(s/2)_n,\\
 \phi_{n,s,c}(S)=\frac{p_{n,s}((S-c)/i)}{\sqrt{h_{n,s}}},\qquad
 [S^n]\phi_{n,s,c}=\frac{i^{-n}}{\sqrt{h_{n,s}}},\qquad
 \rho_n=\sqrt{\frac{n!}{(s/2)_n}}.
\end{gathered}\tag{WCF2}
\]
The classical dictionary is
$p_{n,s}(y)=n!P_n^{(s/4)}(y/2;\pi/2)$, with $dy=2\,d(y/2)$.
The weight, norm, recurrence and generating function are respectively
DLMF (18.19.8)--(18.19.9), (18.22.8), and (18.23.7)
\cite{human:dlmf18}. In the generating function take
$\lambda=s/4>0$, $x=y/2$, $\varphi=\pi/2$, and $z=t$, $|t|<1$.
The two powers take their branches with value one at $t=0$; their product is
\[
 \sum_{n\ge0}\frac{p_{n,s}(y)}{n!}t^n
   =(1+t^2)^{-s/4}e^{y\arctan t}.
 \tag{WCF3}
\]
This also follows by multiplying WCF2's recurrence by $t^n/n!$ and
solving $(1+t^2)\partial_t F=(y-(s/2)t)F$, $F(y,0)=1$.
Thus the stated generating identity holds at the complex polynomial
arguments below, without a change of phase or of the measure.

For $\mathcal P_d=\mathbb C[S]_{\le d}$, $\mathcal P_{-1}=0$, the
unscaled conductor map is
\[
 \mathcal T_A P(S')=\sum_{u,w=0}^{8}a_{uw}P(S'+\beta_{8,u,w}),\qquad
 A_{D,s}:(\mathcal P_{D+v}/\mathcal P_{v-1},\|\cdot\|_{s,c_0,\rm quot})
       \longrightarrow(\mathbb C[S']_{\le D},\|\cdot\|_{s,c_1}).
 \tag{WCF4}
\]
Indeed $\mathcal T_A S^n=\sum_{j=v}^n\binom nj\mu_j S'^{n-j}$.
For $n\ge v$ the leading coefficient is $\binom nv\mu_v\ne0$;
for $n<v$ the image is zero. Successive elimination in degree proves
that WCF4 is an isomorphism with precisely the displayed kernel.
Its quotient frame is represented by
$\phi_{v,s,c_0},\ldots,\phi_{D+v,s,c_0}$, the orthogonal complement
of that full kernel. Set
\[
\begin{gathered}
 w(t)=-i\arctan t=\tfrac12\log\frac{1-it}{1+it},\qquad
 g(t)=t^{-v}e^{-4w(t)}E_A(w(t)),\\
 g(0)=(-i)^v\mu_v/v!,\quad g(t)=\sum_{j\ge0}g_jt^j.
\end{gathered}
 \tag{WCF5}
\]
The singularity at zero is removable. The displayed logarithm is the
analytic branch equal to zero there. Apply the finite sum WCF4 to WCF3.
Since $c_0-c_1=4$, the result is $t^vg(t)$ times the generating
function centred at $c_1$. Comparing coefficients, including
$n!/\sqrt{h_{n,s}}=\rho_n/\sqrt{M_s}$, proves
\[
 A_{D,s}=\operatorname{diag}(\rho_0,\ldots,\rho_D)^{-1}
          T_D(g)\operatorname{diag}(\rho_v,\ldots,\rho_{D+v}),
 \quad
 (A_{D,s})_{ij}=\begin{cases}g_{j-i}\rho_{j+v}/\rho_i,&i\le j,\\0,&i>j.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{WCF6}
\]
Here $T_D(g)_{ij}=g_{j-i}$ above the diagonal. The two copies of
$M_s^{-1/2}$ cancel in this matrix because the source order is the same
on both sides. All norms and all endpoint Grams retain their original
mass. No scalar $v!/\mu_v$ is inserted in WCF4 or WCF6.

\subsection{Forward coefficient and weighted-shift estimates}

For $|t|\le r<1$ the Cayley ratio $z=(1-it)/(1+it)$ satisfies
\[
 \Re z=\frac{1-|t|^2}{|1+it|^2}>0,\qquad
 \frac{1-r}{1+r}\le|z|\le\frac{1+r}{1-r},\qquad
 |\arg z|<\pi/2.
 \tag{WCF7}
\]
The first identity follows by multiplying by $1-i\bar t$; the
modulus bounds follow from the triangle inequality and its reverse.
The branch in WCF5 is therefore exactly the logarithm on the right
half-plane. For $\beta_{8,u,w}-4=x+iy$, we have
$|x|\le8\delta$, $|y|\le8\gamma$. Consequently
\[
 \left|e^{(\beta_{8,u,w}-4)w(t)}\right|
 =\exp\!\left(\tfrac{x}{2}\log|z|-\tfrac{y}{2}\arg z\right)
 \le\left(\frac{1+r}{1-r}\right)^{4\delta}e^{2\pi\gamma}.
 \tag{WCF8}
\]
This estimate retains the imaginary shifts through $e^{2\pi\gamma}$;
it does not replace them by a real-root symbol.

For $D\ge1$, choose $r_D=D/(D+1)$ and define
\[
 C_D=e\,2^v A_{\rm abs}e^{2\pi\gamma}(2D+1)^{4\delta}.
 \tag{WCF9}
\]
On $|t|=r_D$, WCF5 and WCF8 bound $|g(t)|$ by
$r_D^{-v}A_{\rm abs}e^{2\pi\gamma}(2D+1)^{4\delta}$.
Cauchy's coefficient integral gives $|g_l|\le r_D^{-l}\max|g|$.
% BEGIN WCF_CITATION_INSERT C01_CAUCHY_FORWARD
This is the classical Cauchy coefficient formula on the positively
oriented circle, with expansion centre zero
\cite[eqs.~(1.9.30)--(1.9.31)]{human:dlmf1}.
% END WCF_CITATION_INSERT C01_CAUCHY_FORWARD
For $0\le l\le D$, $r_D^{-l}\le(1+1/D)^D\le e$ and
$r_D^{-v}\le2^v$. Hence
\[
 |g_l|\le C_D\qquad(0\le l\le D).
 \tag{WCF10}
\]

The $l$th upper diagonal in WCF6 is a weighted shift whose norm is
the maximum of $\rho_{i+l+v}/\rho_i$. Its nonzero columns have
orthogonal images, which proves this norm assertion directly. If
$s=a\ge9$, then $b>1$ and $\rho_{n+1}/\rho_n=
\sqrt{(n+1)/(n+b)}\le1$, so this norm is at most one.
If $s=1$, the factors in
\[
 \frac{\rho_{i+d}^2}{\rho_i^2}
 =\prod_{t=0}^{d-1}\left(1+\frac{1/2}{i+t+1/2}\right)
 \le\frac{d!}{(1/2)_d}\le2d+1
 \qquad(d\ge0)
 \tag{WCF11}
\]
decrease with $i$. For the last bound use induction: it is equality
at $d=0$, and multiplication by $(d+1)/(d+1/2)$ takes $2d+1$
to $2d+2\le2d+3$. The following integral calculation avoids paying
for the number of upper diagonals.

For $s=a$, so $b>1$, give a polynomial $P(z)=\sum p_nz^n$ the norm
\[
 \|P\|_{\rho}^2=\sum_n|p_n|^2\rho_n^2
  =(b-1)\int_0^1\int_0^{2\pi}|P(\sqrt{x}e^{i\theta})|^2
       \frac{d\theta}{2\pi}(1-x)^{b-2}\,dx.
 \tag{WCF11a}
\]
Angular integration kills unequal monomials. The radial identity
$(b-1)\int_0^1x^n(1-x)^{b-2}dx=n!/(b)_n$ follows either by
integration by parts and its value at $n=0$, or directly from Euler's
beta integral, DLMF (5.12.1), at the positive parameters $n+1,b-1$
\cite{human:dlmf5}. Thus WCF11a is an isometric coefficient
realization of these exact positive weights. It is an auxiliary proof
space for WCF6; the original Gamma measure WCF2 is unchanged.

% BEGIN WCF_CITATION_INSERT C02_MAXIMUM_MODULUS
The disk-bound step below uses the classical maximum-modulus principle
\cite[\S1.10(v)]{human:dlmf1}; the multiplier and compression calculations
are performed in the displayed auxiliary norm.
% END WCF_CITATION_INSERT C02_MAXIMUM_MODULUS
Multiplication by $g(rz)$, $0<r<1$, has norm at most
$\max_{|t|=r}|g(t)|$ in WCF11a, since that pointwise modulus bound
holds on the whole unit disk. Its power series converges uniformly
on the closed unit disk. Orthogonal projection onto degrees at most
$D$ contracts the norm. In the orthonormal basis $z^n/\rho_n$, the
transpose of this compressed multiplication matrix is
\[
 U_r=\operatorname{diag}(\rho_0,\ldots,\rho_D)^{-1}
            T_D(g(r\,\cdot))\operatorname{diag}(\rho_0,\ldots,\rho_D).
 \tag{WCF11b}
\]
Transposition preserves operator norm. For
$R_r=\operatorname{diag}(1,r,\ldots,r^D)$, the unscaled matrix is
$U=R_rU_rR_r^{-1}$. Hence
$\|U\|\le r^{-D}\max_{|t|=r}|g(t)|$. Finally
$A_{D,a}=U\operatorname{diag}(\rho_{i+v}/\rho_i)$, whose last
diagonal is a contraction. Taking $r=r_D$ and WCF8 proves
$\|A_{D,a}\|\le C_D$.

For $s=1$ apply the same angular integration with the unweighted
circle norm $\sum|p_n|^2$: multiplication and orthogonal compression
give $\|T_D(g)\|\le r^{-D}\max_{|t|=r}|g(t)|$ by the same radial
similarity. In this case $\rho_n$ increases, so the two diagonal
operator norms in WCF6 have product $\rho_{D+v}$, bounded by
$\sqrt{2(D+v)+1}$ in WCF11. We have therefore proved the sharper
finite estimate
\[
 \boxed{\quad \|A_{D,s}\|\le B^+_{D,s}:=
 \begin{cases}
 C_D\sqrt{2(D+v)+1},&D\ge1,\ s=1,\\
 C_D,&D\ge1,\ s=a,\\
 |\mu_v|\rho_v/v!,&D=0.
 \end{cases}\quad}
 \tag{WCF12}
\]
The $D=0$ case is the exact scalar modulus from WCF6; $\rho_0=1$.
The estimate therefore includes $v=0$ and the smallest cutoff.
In particular, uniformly for $s\in\{1,a\}$ and $D\le C a^2$,
\[
 \log^+B^+_{D,s}=O_{\rm actual,C}(\log(a+1)).
 \tag{WCF13}
\]
For $D\ge1$ this follows by taking the logarithm of WCF12 and
using fixed $v,\delta,\gamma,A_{\rm abs}$. At $D=0$, $\rho_v\le1$
for $s=a$ and is fixed for $s=1$. This proves the asserted uniformity.

\subsection{A finite inverse estimate with every principal part}

The following records the inverse terms needed at the negative endpoints.
It also prevents an upper-triangular estimate from being applied to a
lower-triangular correction. Retain the original SGT zero-free constants
\[
\begin{gathered}
 B_0=\max\{1,\max_{u,w}|\beta_{8,u,w}|\},\quad
 C_v=A_{\rm abs}B_0^v/|\mu_v|,\\
 R_0=B_0^{-1}\min\{1,(v+1)/(2eC_v)\},\quad
 a_0=\min\{1/2,\tanh(R_0/2)\},\\
 d_0=1-\frac{a_0^2}{3(1-a_0^2)},\qquad
 M_0=\frac{2v!}{|\mu_v|}d_0^{-v}e^{2R_0},\qquad
 S_D(r)=\sum_{l=0}^D r^{-l}.
\end{gathered}\tag{WCF14}
\]
Here $v!E_A(z)/(\mu_vz^v)$ differs from one by at most
$C_vB_0|z|e^{B_0|z|}/(v+1)$, by its exponential Taylor series.
This is at most $1/2$ for $|z|\le R_0$. On $|t|\le a_0$,
$|w(t)|\le R_0/2$ and $|w(t)/t|\ge d_0$, by the arctangent
series. Factoring $g$ as
$(\mu_v/v!)(w/t)^ve^{-4w}[v!E_A(w)/(\mu_vw^v)]$ proves
$|1/g(t)|\le M_0$ on that entire disk, including zero.

Fix any $0<\varrho<1$ with no zero of $g$ on $|t|=\varrho$.
Let its interior zeros be $\zeta$, of orders $m_\zeta$, and put
\[
\begin{gathered}
 H_\varrho=\sum_\zeta m_\zeta,\qquad
 A_{\zeta,h}=\frac1{(m_\zeta-h)!}
   \left.\frac{d^{m_\zeta-h}}{dt^{m_\zeta-h}}
       \frac{(t-\zeta)^{m_\zeta}}{g(t)}\right|_{t=\zeta},\\
 f_\varrho(t)=\frac1{g(t)}-
       \sum_{\zeta}\sum_{h=1}^{m_\zeta}\frac{A_{\zeta,h}}{(t-\zeta)^h},
 \quad W_\varrho=\operatorname{arctanh}\varrho,\\
 m_{E,\varrho}=\min_{|t|=\varrho}|E_A(w(t))|>0,\quad
 M_\varrho=\frac{\varrho^ve^{4W_\varrho}}{m_{E,\varrho}}
       +\sum_{\zeta,h}\frac{|A_{\zeta,h}|}{(\varrho-|\zeta|)^h}.
\end{gathered}\tag{WCF15}
\]
All these pole sums are finite. Zero is not a pole, and $f_\varrho$
is holomorphic on a neighbourhood of the closed circle and its
interior. Its modulus on the circle is at most $M_\varrho$.
% BEGIN WCF_CITATION_INSERT C03_FINITE_LAURENT
The analytic framework here is the classical Laurent expansion and
removal of its finite negative part at each pole
\cite[\S1.10(iii), eqs.~(1.10.6)--(1.10.7)]{human:dlmf1}.
The weighted matrix decomposition below is calculated separately.
% END WCF_CITATION_INSERT C03_FINITE_LAURENT

Truncated power-series multiplication gives
$T_D(g)^{-1}=T_D(1/g)$. The inverse weighted upper diagonals have
ratios $\rho_{i+l}/\rho_{i+v}$. WCF11 gives an upper bound
$\sqrt{2l+1}$ at $s=1$. At $s=a$, this ratio is at most
$\rho_i/\rho_{i+v}\le\sqrt{(b)_v/v!}$; the last inequality follows
by decreasing each factor $(i+t+b)/(i+t+1)$ to its maximum at $i=0$.
Thus set
\[
 W_{D,s}^-=
 \begin{cases}\sqrt{2D+1},&s=1,\\\sqrt{(s/2)_v/v!},&s=a,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad B^0_{D,s}=W_{D,s}^-M_0S_D(a_0).
 \tag{WCF16}
\]
Cauchy's bound on $1/g$ at radius $a_0$, followed by the sum of
these upper-diagonal norms, proves $\|A_{D,s}^{-1}\|\le B^0_{D,s}$.
% BEGIN WCF_CITATION_INSERT C04_CAUCHY_INVERSE
The coefficient estimate is again Cauchy's formula
\cite[eqs.~(1.9.30)--(1.9.31)]{human:dlmf1}, now applied to the reciprocal
on the displayed zero-free disk, with the original weights retained.
% END WCF_CITATION_INSERT C04_CAUCHY_INVERSE

For completeness, the Taylor coefficient of $A/(t-\zeta)^h$ is
$c_n=A(-1)^h\zeta^{-n-h}\binom{n+h-1}{h-1}$.
% BEGIN WCF_CITATION_INSERT C05_BINOMIAL
For nonnegative indices this is the classical binomial series
\cite[eq.~(1.10.4)]{human:dlmf1}; the following extension to integer
indices and its finite matrix corrections are computed explicitly here.
% END WCF_CITATION_INSERT C05_BINOMIAL
Extend the binomial polynomial to every integer $n$. The full matrix
$(c_{j-i})_{0\le i,j\le D}$ has rank at most $h$, by expanding its
polynomial in $i$. For all orders at one pole the combined column
space is contained in $\operatorname{span}\{(\zeta^ii^d)_i:
0\le d<m_\zeta\}$, hence its rank is at most $m_\zeta$.
For a lower-diagonal gap $l=i-j\ge1$, the omitted entry equals
zero if $l<h$ and equals
$-A\zeta^{l-h}\binom{l-1}{h-1}$ if $l\ge h$.
Its cancellation therefore requires a lower-triangular correction with
the opposite, positive displayed coefficient.

After multiplying by the actual inverse diagonal factors, that
lower-diagonal shift has weight $\rho_j/\rho_{j+l+v}$. Its norm is
at most one for $s=1$, and at most
$\sqrt{(b)_{l+v}/(l+v)!}$ for $s=a$, by the same decreasing-factor
argument. Define the finite, original-pole quantity
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \Theta_{l,s}=\begin{cases}1,&s=1,\\
       \sqrt{(s/2)_{l+v}/(l+v)!},&s=a,\end{cases}\\
 Z_{D,s,\varrho}=\sum_{\zeta,h}|A_{\zeta,h}|
      \sum_{l=h}^{D}|\zeta|^{l-h}\binom{l-1}{h-1}\Theta_{l,s},\\
 B^{\rm rem}_{D,s,\varrho}=W_{D,s}^-M_\varrho S_D(\varrho)
                              +Z_{D,s,\varrho},\qquad
 B^{\rm cap}_{D,s,\varrho}
     =\min\{B^0_{D,s},B^{\rm rem}_{D,s,\varrho}\}.
\end{gathered}\tag{WCF17}
\]
An empty inner sum is zero. The preceding exact principal-part
calculation and the coefficient bound on $f_\varrho$ prove a decomposition
of the entire inverse into a matrix of rank at most $H_\varrho$ and
a remainder of norm at most $B^{\rm rem}_{D,s,\varrho}$.
Diagonal multiplication leaves this rank bound unchanged. No pole,
lower-triangular correction, or original coefficient has been removed.

For $c$ orthogonal copies, $c\in\{1,4\}$, and a rank $r$ plane or
attained subquotient, put $h_c=\min\{r,cH_\varrho\}$ and
\[
 \mathcal L^{\rm wt}_{D,s,r,c}(\varrho)
 =2h_c\log^+B^0_{D,s}
       +2(r-h_c)\log^+B^{\rm cap}_{D,s,\varrho},\qquad
 \mathcal U^{\rm wt}_{D,s,r}=2r\log^+B^+_{D,s}.
 \tag{WCF18}
\]
To prove the inverse exterior bound, take the kernel of the exceptional
matrix. It has codimension at most $cH_\varrho$, and on it the full
inverse has norm at most the remainder bound. Diagonalizing its positive
squared norm shows that every singular value after the first
$cH_\varrho$ is at most that bound; all singular values are at most
$B^0_{D,s}$. Multiplying the largest $r$ singular values proves the
exterior bound in WCF18, with $\log^+$ giving a nonnegative allowance.

The older inverse estimate remains available without alteration. In its
literal NCT16--19 notation define
\[
\begin{gathered}
 L_{s,D,v}=\tfrac12\max\{1,s/2-1\}\sum_{n=1}^{D+v}\frac1n,
 \quad G_{0,D}=M_0S_D(a_0),\\
 P_\varrho=\sum_{\zeta,h}\frac{|A_{\zeta,h}|}{(1-|\zeta|)^h},
 \quad V_{\varrho,D}=M_\varrho S_D(\varrho)+P_\varrho,\\
 \mathcal L^{\rm old}_{D,s,r,c}(\varrho)
 =4rL_{s,D,v}+2h_c\log^+G_{0,D}
       +2(r-h_c)\log^+\min\{G_{0,D},V_{\varrho,D}\},\\
 \mathcal L^{\rm pole}_{D,s,r,c}=
  \inf_{\varrho\ {\rm admissible}}
  \min\{\mathcal L^{\rm wt}_{D,s,r,c}(\varrho),
        \mathcal L^{\rm old}_{D,s,r,c}(\varrho)\}.
\end{gathered}\tag{WCF19}
\]
The old bound follows from the same pole decomposition before diagonal
weighting: summing the lower shifts gives $P_\varrho$ by the binomial
series, and each of the two diagonal factors costs at most
$e^{L_{s,D,v}}$. The latter follows by writing $\rho_n$ as its
product and using $\log(1+x)\le x$. Applying the exterior argument
then gives exactly WCF19. Thus taking the displayed minimum and
infimum keeps two independently proved finite bounds. Admissible radii
exist arbitrarily close to one because zeros are isolated. The infimum
is finite and nonnegative and need not be attained.

\subsection{The full determinant controls every inverse exterior rank}

There is a stronger estimate that uses the entire original triangular
matrix and needs no separation of its poles. Put $d=D+1$ and
$M_{D,s}=\max\{1,B^+_{D,s}\}$. Triangularity of WCF6 proves the
exact complex determinant identity
\[
 \det A_{D,s}=g(0)^d\prod_{i=0}^{D}\frac{\rho_{i+v}}{\rho_i}.
 \tag{WCF19a}
\]
It retains the complete phase $g(0)=(-i)^v\mu_v/v!$. For $s=1$ the
product of positive ratios is at least one. For $s=a$ its reciprocal
satisfies
\[
 \begin{split}
 \log\prod_{i=0}^{D}\frac{\rho_i}{\rho_{i+v}}
 &=\frac12\sum_{i=0}^{D}\sum_{t=0}^{v-1}
           \log\left(1+\frac{s/2-1}{i+t+1}\right)\\
 &\le\frac12(s/2-1)v\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac1j.
 \end{split}\tag{WCF19b}
\]
For $v=0$ both sums are empty. Define the nonnegative finite quantity
\[
 J_{D,s}=d\log^+\frac1{|g(0)|}
       +\frac12(s/2-1)_+v\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac1j.
 \tag{WCF19c}
\]
Equations WCF19a--b prove $|\det A_{D,s}|^{-1}\le e^{J_{D,s}}$.

Let $B=(A_{D,s}^{\mathsf T})^{\oplus c}$, of dimension $cd$, with
singular values $\sigma_1\ge\cdots\ge\sigma_{cd}>0$. Its inverse
exterior norm is
\[
 \left\|\bigwedge^rB^{-1}\right\|
 =\frac{\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_{cd-r}}
           {\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_{cd}}
 =\frac{\|\bigwedge^{cd-r}B\|}{|\det B|}
 \le M_{D,s}^{cd-r}e^{cJ_{D,s}}\qquad(0\le r\le cd).
 \tag{WCF19d}
\]
The first equality multiplies the $r$ largest inverse singular values;
the denominator is exactly the modulus of the full determinant. Thus
this estimate includes every direction of the original inverse, including
all directions caused by every symbol zero. It has imposed no zero-free
unit disk or bound on its pole count.

For $r>0$ set
\[
 \mathcal L^{\rm det}_{D,s,r,c}
 =2\bigl((cd-r)\log M_{D,s}+cJ_{D,s}\bigr),\qquad
 \mathcal L^*_{D,s,r,c}
 =\min\{\mathcal L^{\rm pole}_{D,s,r,c},
                         \mathcal L^{\rm det}_{D,s,r,c}\}.
 \tag{WCF19e}
\]
At $r=0$ define both allowances to be zero, as the actual empty
exterior norm and determinant are one. This sharpens rather than
replaces the preserved finite principal-part bounds. For fixed $C_{\rm cut}>0$, if

 x^*D_{a,D}^{(s)}x
   &=\min_{\eta\in\mathbb C^Q}\|Jx+H\eta\|_2^2,
 &\eta_0(x)&=-K_0^{-1}H^*Jx,\\
 x^*\widehat D_{a,D}^{(s)}x
   &=\min_{\eta\in\mathbb C^Q}\|B(Jx+H\eta)\|_2^2,
 &\eta_1(x)&=-K_1^{-1}H^*B^*BJx.
\end{aligned}\tag{XIM16}
\]
Both minima retain every lower evaluation and all their cross terms.
Each minimizer is unique because $K_0,K_1$ are positive definite.
More explicitly, the difference between the first squared norm and
its displayed minimum is
$(\eta-\eta_0(x))^*K_0(\eta-\eta_0(x))$, and the difference for
the second is $(\eta-\eta_1(x))^*K_1(\eta-\eta_1(x))$.
These identities follow on expanding, using the two displayed
formulas for $\eta_0,\eta_1$; each cross term cancels exactly.
If either minimum were zero, invertibility of $B$ and independence
of $[H,J]$ would imply $x=0$. Thus both analytic metrics are positive
definite. The two minimizing coefficient columns need not coincide.

The induced map on the quotient has the following exact domain,
codomain, and inverse:
\[
\begin{gathered}
 Q_0=\mathscr Y_D\cap\mathscr K_D^\perp=\operatorname{im}W_0,
 \quad Q_1=B\mathscr Y_D\cap(B\mathscr K_D)^\perp
                         =\operatorname{im}W_1,\\
 \overline B:Q_0\longrightarrow Q_1,
       \quad \overline B=P_{Q_1}B|_{Q_0},\qquad
 \overline B^{-1}=P_{Q_0}B^{-1}|_{Q_1},\qquad
 \overline B(W_0x)=W_1x.
\end{gathered}\tag{XIM17}
\]
The quotient identifications are isometric because each representative
has a unique orthogonal minimum lift. Projection after $B$ subtracts
an element of $B\mathscr K_D$; applying $B^{-1}$ turns that into an
element of $\mathscr K_D$, which the second projection removes.
This proves both inverse identities. It also proves the last equation
using XIM14. In the orthonormal minimum-lift frames
$W_0(D_{a,D}^{(s)})^{-1/2}$ and
$W_1(\widehat D_{a,D}^{(s)})^{-1/2}$, the matrix is
\[
 [\overline B]=
 (\widehat D_{a,D}^{(s)})^{1/2}(D_{a,D}^{(s)})^{-1/2},\qquad
 [\overline B]^*[\overline B]=
 (D_{a,D}^{(s)})^{-1/2}\widehat D_{a,D}^{(s)}(D_{a,D}^{(s)})^{-1/2}.
 \tag{XIM18}
\]
Indeed $W_1^*BW_0=W_1^*W_1=\widehat D_{a,D}^{(s)}$, because
the discarded part lies in $B\mathscr K_D$. Thus the original TAC9
correction is exactly
\[
 \epsilon_{a,N,s}
 =\log\det\widehat D_{a,D}^{(s)}-\log\det D_{a,D}^{(s)}
 =2\log|\det[\overline B]|.
 \tag{XIM19}
\]

Write the established all-rank logarithmic exterior estimates for the
actual ambient matrix, including its original weights, as
\[
 \log\left\|\bigwedge^r\bigl((A_{D,s}^{\mathsf T})^{\oplus c}\bigr)\right\|
       \le F_{D,s,r,c},\qquad
 \log\left\|\bigwedge^r\big((A_{D,s}^{\mathsf T})^{\oplus c}\big)^{-1}
       \right\|\le I_{D,s,r,c},\qquad 0\le r\le cd.
 \tag{XIM20}
\]
The $F$ and $I$ in this display are logarithms of exterior norms,
without the Gram factor two. Orthogonal projections contract every
exterior power: in orthonormal bases their singular values are zero
or one. Restrictions and inclusions of Hilbert subspaces also
contract exterior powers. Applying XIM17 in both directions and
then XIM20 at $c=1$, $r=m$, proves the finite bound
\[
 \boxed{-2I_{D,s,m,1}\le\epsilon_{a,N,s}\le2F_{D,s,m,1}.}
 \tag{XIM21}
\]
There is no added $Q$-rank allowance: the complete denominator has
already been retained in the exact quotient map and in both minima.
For $c$ copies, the denominator has dimension $cQ$ and the full
analytic quotient dimension $cm$. Any further restriction followed
by an attained quotient has its full preimages $K'\subset Y'$ in
the ambient $c$ copies; necessarily $K'$ contains the original
$cQ$-dimensional denominator. Nested minimization over these fibres
equals minimization over $K'$, because the union of the original
affine fibres is the final affine fibre. XIM17 applies to $K'\subset Y'$
and proves the same determinant interval $[-2I_{D,s,r,c},2F_{D,s,r,c}]$
with its actual final rank $r$, including $r=0$ with empty determinant
one. No intermediate dimension is charged a second time.

Finally retain the actual TAC endpoints and dual sign orientation:
\[
 N_i=(q-1,q,2q-1,2q)_i,\quad D_i=N_i-v,\quad
 \Xi_{a,s}=-\epsilon_{a,N_0,s}-\epsilon_{a,N_1,s}
             +\epsilon_{a,N_2,s}+\epsilon_{a,N_3,s}.
 \tag{XIM22}
\]
For these four endpoints,
$d_i=(q-v,q+1-v,2q-v,2q+1-v)_i$ and
$t_i=(0,1,q,q+1)_i$. The annihilator dimensions remain exactly
$q-v$ and $Q$, and the analytic quotient rank remains $m$ at every
endpoint. Set $F_i=F_{D_i,s,m,1}$ and $I_i=I_{D_i,s,m,1}$.
Negating the two low-endpoint inequalities in XIM21 and adding the
two high-endpoint inequalities gives
\[
 \boxed{
 -2(F_0+F_1+I_2+I_3)
       \le\Xi_{a,s}\le
  2(I_0+I_1+F_2+F_3).}
 \tag{XIM23}
\]
These are bounds on the same four correlated matrices appearing in
TAC9. A separate radius infimum, minimum of established allowances,
or improved ambient estimate can be inserted in each $F_i$ or $I_i$
provided it bounds XIM20; no attainment of an infimum is used.
The source order remains $1$ or $a$ at the lower grid, its centre
remains $a/2-4$, and its cutoff remains $N_i-v$. In particular no
replacement by the order $a-8$ or by an unshifted cutoff occurs.
The explicit uniform bounds LT21--22 of the preceding transport proof
apply because $D_i\le3a^2$ for $a\ge9$. Inserting them in XIM23
proves $\Xi_{a,s}=O_{\rm actual}(q)$ for both original source orders.
The identical endpoint bound XIM21 holds at every cutoff
$q-1\le N\le2q$, including each growing-prefix endpoint $N=q+R$
with $0\le R<q$, with the same fixed constant.
\endgroup

\subsection{A uniform midpoint-quantile logarithmic discrepancy}

Let $A<B$ be real numbers, and let $\mu$ be the probability measure on
$[A,B]$ with density
\[
 d\mu(y)=H(y)\sqrt{(B-y)(y-A)}\,dy,
 \qquad H\in C^1([A,B]),\qquad H(y)>0.
\]
The probability normalization is part of the hypothesis; no scalar
rescaling of $\mu$ will be made. Put
\[
 c=\frac{B-A}{2}>0,\qquad m=\frac{A+B}{2},\qquad
 T(t)=m+ct\quad(-1\leq t\leq1).
\]
The map $T:[-1,1]\longrightarrow[A,B]$ is an increasing bijection,
with inverse $T^{-1}(y)=(y-m)/c$. Its inverse pushforward of $\mu$ has
the density
\[
 \rho(t)=h(t)\sqrt{1-t^2},\qquad h(t)=c^2H(T(t)).
\]
Indeed, $dy=c\,dt$ and
$\sqrt{(B-T(t))(T(t)-A)}=c\sqrt{1-t^2}$, so
$d\mu(T(t))=\rho(t)\,dt$. In particular,
$\int_{-1}^1\rho(t)\,dt=1$, $h>0$, and $h\in C^1([-1,1])$.
Define
\[
 F(t)=\int_{-1}^t\rho(s)\,ds\quad(-1\leq t\leq1),\qquad
 q=F^{-1}:[0,1]\longrightarrow[-1,1],
\]
and the constants
\[
 Q_h=\frac{\max_{[-1,1]}h}{\min_{[-1,1]}h},\qquad
 M_h=\max_{[-1,1]}\left|\frac{h'}h\right|,\qquad
 K_h=Q_h(1+M_h).
\]
These constants are finite; $Q_h\geq1$ and $K_h\geq1$. The function
$F$ is continuous and strictly increasing on $[-1,1]$, with endpoint
values $0,1$, because $\rho>0$ on $(-1,1)$. Thus its inverse $q$ is
well defined and continuous, and $q\in C^2((0,1))$.

For an integer $n\geq1$, define the original physical midpoint
probability quantiles by
\[
 u_j=\frac{j-\tfrac12}{n},\qquad y_j=T(q(u_j)),
 \qquad 1\leq j\leq n.
\]
We shall prove, for every real $x$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lrc-quantile-original}
 \sum_{j=1}^n\log|x-y_j|
 \leq n\int_A^B\log|x-y|\,d\mu(y)
       +24K_h^2+30Q_h.
\end{equation}
In fact the proof yields the stronger remainder $16K_h^2+24Q_h$.
As usual $\log0=-\infty$; the right-hand integral is finite for every
real $x$, since the density is bounded and $\log|x-y|$ is locally
Lebesgue integrable. Hence a coincidence $x=y_j$ immediately proves
\eqref{eq:lrc-quantile-original} in the extended-real sense.

\paragraph{Exact invariance of the discrepancy under the affine map.}
Let $z=T^{-1}(x)=(x-m)/c$, with $x$ allowed anywhere on $\mathbb R$.
For each $j$ and each $t$ away from the respective possible zero,
\[
 \log|x-y_j|=\log c+\log|z-q(u_j)|,
 \qquad
 \log|x-T(t)|=\log c+\log|z-t|.
\]
Consequently the $n\log c$ contributions to the sum and to $n$ times
the probability integral agree exactly. The original discrepancy
therefore equals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lrc-affine-exact}
 \sum_{j=1}^n\log|z-q(u_j)|
   -n\int_{-1}^1\log|z-t|\rho(t)\,dt.
\end{equation}
The correspondence also proves directly that $y_j$ are precisely
the midpoint quantiles of the original measure. It suffices to
bound \eqref{eq:lrc-affine-exact}; the original coordinates and
probability normalization are recovered by the displayed exact map.

\paragraph{The inverse-quantile derivative estimate.}
For $-1<t\leq0$, write $h_{\max}=\max h$ and $h_{\min}=\min h$.
Then
\[
 F(t)
 \leq h_{\max}\sqrt2\int_{-1}^t\sqrt{1+s}\,ds
 =\frac{2\sqrt2}{3}h_{\max}(1+t)^{3/2},
 \qquad
 \rho(t)\geq h_{\min}\sqrt{1+t}.
\]
Since $2\sqrt2/3<1$, it follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lrc-left-tail-ratio}
 \frac{F(t)}{\rho(t)}\leq Q_h(1+t)
 \qquad(-1<t\leq0).
\end{equation}
For $0\leq t<1$, integrating from $t$ to $1$ instead gives
\[
 1-F(t)
 \leq h_{\max}\sqrt2\int_t^1\sqrt{1-s}\,ds
 =\frac{2\sqrt2}{3}h_{\max}(1-t)^{3/2},
 \qquad \rho(t)\geq h_{\min}\sqrt{1-t},
\]
and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lrc-right-tail-ratio}
 \frac{1-F(t)}{\rho(t)}\leq Q_h(1-t)
 \qquad(0\leq t<1).
\end{equation}
On $(-1,1)$,
\[
 \frac{\rho'(t)}{\rho(t)}
 =\frac{h'(t)}{h(t)}-\frac{t}{1-t^2}.
\]
For $t\leq0$, using $\min(F(t),1-F(t))\leq F(t)$ and
\eqref{eq:lrc-left-tail-ratio}, we obtain
\[
 \min(F(t),1-F(t))\frac{|\rho'(t)|}{\rho(t)^2}
 \leq Q_h\left(M_h(1+t)+\frac{-t}{1-t}\right)
 \leq Q_h\left(M_h+\frac12\right)\leq K_h.
\]
For $t\geq0$, using $\min(F(t),1-F(t))\leq1-F(t)$ and
\eqref{eq:lrc-right-tail-ratio}, the corresponding calculation is
\[
 \min(F(t),1-F(t))\frac{|\rho'(t)|}{\rho(t)^2}
 \leq Q_h\left(M_h(1-t)+\frac{t}{1+t}\right)
 \leq Q_h\left(M_h+\frac12\right)\leq K_h.
\]
In particular, no assumption that $F(0)=1/2$ was made. Differentiating
$F(q(u))=u$ yields
\[
 q'(u)=\frac1{\rho(q(u))},\qquad
 \frac{q''(u)}{q'(u)}
 =-\frac{\rho'(q(u))}{\rho(q(u))^2}.
\]
We have therefore proved
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lrc-log-derivative}
 \left|\frac{d}{du}\log q'(u)\right|
 \leq\frac{K_h}{\min(u,1-u)}\qquad(0<u<1).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{A second-derivative bound away from the singular probability.}
Fix $z\in\mathbb R$, and put
\[
 v=\begin{cases}
 0,&z\leq-1,\\
 F(z),&-1<z<1,\\
 1,&z\geq1.
 \end{cases}
\]
Thus $v$ is the actual probability coordinate when $z$ is in the
support, and the nearest probability endpoint when $z$ is exterior.
For $u\in(0,1)\setminus\{v\}$, set
\[
 f(u)=\log|z-q(u)|,\qquad
 d(u)=\min\{u,1-u,|u-v|\}>0,
\]
and define
\[
 \ell=\min\left\{|u-v|,\frac{u}{2K_h},
                              \frac{1-u}{2K_h}\right\}>0.
\]
Consider the segment of length $\ell$ starting at $u$ and directed
towards $v$. Every point $w$ of this segment satisfies
$w\geq u/2$ and $1-w\geq(1-u)/2$, because $K_h\geq1$.
Integrating \eqref{eq:lrc-log-derivative} along any initial part of
this segment therefore gives
\[
 |\log q'(w)-\log q'(u)|
 \leq\frac{2K_h|w-u|}{\min(u,1-u)}\leq1.
\]
It follows that $q'(w)\geq e^{-1}q'(u)$ on the segment.
For $-1\leq z\leq1$, $q(v)=z$; for $z<-1$ or $z>1$,
\[
 |z-q(u)|\geq|q(v)-q(u)|.
\]
In all cases, monotonicity of $q$ shows that the distance on the
left is at least the change of $q$ along the chosen segment.
Consequently,
\[
 |z-q(u)|\geq e^{-1}q'(u)\ell,
 \qquad
 \frac{q'(u)}{|z-q(u)|}
 \leq\frac e\ell
 \leq\frac{2eK_h}{d(u)}.
\]
Since
\[
 f'(u)=-\frac{q'(u)}{z-q(u)},\qquad
 f''(u)=-\frac{q''(u)}{z-q(u)}
        -\frac{q'(u)^2}{(z-q(u))^2},
\]
equation \eqref{eq:lrc-log-derivative} gives
\[
 |f''(u)|
 \leq\frac{K_h}{d(u)}\frac{2eK_h}{d(u)}
       +\frac{4e^2K_h^2}{d(u)^2}
 =\frac{(2e+4e^2)K_h^2}{d(u)^2}
 \leq\frac{48K_h^2}{d(u)^2}.
\]
The last numerical inequality follows already from $e<3$, which
gives $2e+4e^2<42<48$. For completeness,
$e=\sum_{k\geq0}1/k!<1+1+\sum_{k\geq2}2^{-(k-1)}=3$.

\paragraph{Precise cell selection and the inverse-square sum.}
Let
\[
 I_j=\left[\frac{j-1}{n},\frac jn\right],\qquad1\leq j\leq n.
\]
Mark a cell if $j=1$, if $j=n$, or if
$\operatorname{dist}(v,I_j)\leq1/n$. The endpoint equalities in
this definition are included. With $w=nv$, the distance condition is
equivalent to
\[
 j\in[w-1,w+2].
\]
A closed interval of length $3$ contains at most four integers.
Together with the first and last cells, at most six distinct cells
are marked. This also includes $n=1$, when its only cell is marked.

Let $U$ be the set of unmarked indices, and for $j\in U$ write
\[
 e_j=\operatorname{dist}(nv,[j-1,j]),\qquad
 m_j=n\inf_{u\in I_j}d(u)
     =\min\{j-1,n-j,e_j\}.
\]
For these indices $2\leq j\leq n-1$, $e_j>1$, and $m_j>0$.
Therefore
\[
 \frac1{m_j^2}
 \leq\frac1{(j-1)^2}+\frac1{(n-j)^2}+\frac1{e_j^2}.
\]
The first two sums over $U$ are each at most $2$, since their
denominators run over distinct positive integers and
\[
 \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac1{k^2}
 \leq1+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac1{k(k-1)}=2.
\]
Every unmarked cell lies strictly on one side of $nv$. On the left
its distance is $nv-j$; on the right its distance is $j-1-nv$.
On either side, arrange the distances in increasing order
$a_1,\ldots,a_N$. The first is greater than $1$, and successive
distances differ by positive integers; thus $a_k>k$.
Each side contributes at most $2$ to $\sum_{j\in U}e_j^{-2}$.
It follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lrc-inverse-square}
 \sum_{j\in U}\frac1{m_j^2}\leq2+2+2+2=8.
\end{equation}
If there are no unmarked cells, this sum is zero and the assertion
remains valid.

\paragraph{The discrepancy on the unmarked cells.}
For a twice continuously differentiable function on $I_j$ with
$\sup_{I_j}|f''|\leq M$, Taylor's formula about the midpoint $u_j$
gives
\[
 |f(u)-f(u_j)-f'(u_j)(u-u_j)|\leq\frac M2(u-u_j)^2.
\]
The linear term integrates to zero, and
\[
 n\int_{I_j}(u-u_j)^2\,du=\frac1{12n^2}.
\]
Hence
\[
 \left|f(u_j)-n\int_{I_j}f(u)\,du\right|
 \leq\frac{M}{24n^2}.
\]
For every unmarked cell the preceding second-derivative bound
applies on the closed cell and implies
$M\leq48K_h^2n^2/m_j^2$. Summing and using
\eqref{eq:lrc-inverse-square} proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lrc-unmarked-total}
 \sum_{j\in U}\left(f(u_j)-n\int_{I_j}f(u)\,du\right)
 \leq2K_h^2\sum_{j\in U}\frac1{m_j^2}
 \leq16K_h^2.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Conditional physical densities on any cell.}
For any cell, marked or otherwise, put
\[
 a_j=q((j-1)/n),\qquad b_j=q(j/n),\qquad L_j=b_j-a_j>0.
\]
Its physical probability mass is exactly $1/n$, and the conditional
probability density on $[a_j,b_j]$ is
\[
 p_j(t)=n\rho(t)
       =\frac{h(t)\sqrt{1-t^2}}
               {\int_{a_j}^{b_j}h(s)\sqrt{1-s^2}\,ds}.
\]
The function $g(t)=\sqrt{1-t^2}$ is nonnegative and concave on
$[-1,1]$: in the interior its second derivative is
$g''(t)=-(1-t^2)^{-3/2}<0$, and it is continuous at the endpoints.
Let $t_0\in[a_j,b_j]$ be a point at which $g$ is maximal.
On each of $[a_j,t_0]$ and $[t_0,b_j]$, concavity places its graph
above the chord joining the endpoint values; these chords in turn
lie above the corresponding line segments with height zero at
$a_j,b_j$ and height $g(t_0)$ at $t_0$. Integrating the two triangles
(allowing a zero-length triangle when $t_0$ is an endpoint) gives
\[
 \int_{a_j}^{b_j}g(t)\,dt\geq\frac{L_j}2\max_{[a_j,b_j]}g.
\]
Since this maximum is positive, comparison of $h$ with its global
maximum and minimum proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lrc-cell-density}
 0\leq p_j(t)\leq\frac{2Q_h}{L_j}
 \quad(t\in[a_j,b_j]).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{An interval-logarithm bound for every evaluation point.}
For any $a<b$, $L=b-a$, and $z\in\mathbb R$, set
$d=\max\{|z-a|,|z-b|\}>0$. We claim
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lrc-interval-log}
 \int_a^b\log\frac d{|z-t|}\,dt\leq2L.
\end{equation}
All these improper integrals converge, and their integrands are
nonnegative. If $z\in[a,b]$, let $p=z-a$, $q_0=b-z$, and
$s=\min(p,q_0)$; then $p+q_0=L$ and $d=\max(p,q_0)$.
Using
\[
 \int_0^r\log(d/t)\,dt=r(1+\log(d/r))\quad(r>0)
\]
and its value $0$ at $r=0$, the integral equals
\[
 L+s\log(d/s),
\]
with the second summand interpreted as zero when $s=0$.
If $s>0$, the inequality $\log z_0\leq z_0-1$ for $z_0>0$ gives
\[
 L+s\log(d/s)\leq L+d-s=2d\leq2L.
\]
That elementary logarithm inequality follows, for example, by
minimizing $z_0-1-\log z_0$, whose derivative is $1-z_0^{-1}$.
If $s=0$, the integral is $L$. For an exterior point, let
$r=\operatorname{dist}(z,[a,b])>0$, so $d=r+L$. A direct integral
of the same primitive gives
\[
 \int_a^b\log\frac d{|z-t|}\,dt
 =L-r\log\frac{r+L}{r}\leq L.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:lrc-interval-log} for interior, endpoint, and
exterior points, with no restriction on how close $z$ is to the
interval.

\paragraph{The discrepancy on the marked cells and conclusion.}
Continue with $z$ not equal to a selected quantile, since all such
coincidences were already resolved. On a cell $I_j$ let
$d_j=\max\{|z-a_j|,|z-b_j|\}$. Because $q(u_j)\in[a_j,b_j]$,
$f(u_j)\leq\log d_j$. The exact change of variables $u=F(t)$ and
the fact that $p_j$ is a probability density give
\[
 \begin{split}
 f(u_j)-n\int_{I_j}f(u)\,du
 &\leq\log d_j-\int_{a_j}^{b_j}\log|z-t|p_j(t)\,dt\\
 &=\int_{a_j}^{b_j}\log\frac{d_j}{|z-t|}p_j(t)\,dt\\
 &\leq\frac{2Q_h}{L_j}
             \int_{a_j}^{b_j}\log\frac{d_j}{|z-t|}\,dt
 \leq4Q_h.
 \end{split}
\]
The penultimate inequality is legitimate because its logarithmic
integrand is nonnegative. At most six cells are marked, so their
total contribution is at most $24Q_h$. Combining this with
\eqref{eq:lrc-unmarked-total} proves
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^nf(u_j)-n\int_0^1f(u)\,du
 \leq16K_h^2+24Q_h\leq24K_h^2+30Q_h.
\]
Finally,
$\int_0^1f(u)\,du=\int_{-1}^1\log|z-t|\rho(t)\,dt$,
and \eqref{eq:lrc-affine-exact} returns this result to the original
measure and original physical quantiles. This proves
\eqref{eq:lrc-quantile-original} for every $n\geq1$ and every
$x\in\mathbb R$, including support endpoints and exterior points.

\subsection{The original EIQ density and the explicit LRC12 constant}

For the original EIQ equilibrium for
$V_{\alpha,\beta}(y)=\beta\sqrt y-\alpha\log y$, let its positive
support endpoints be $A=u^2$ and $B=v_*^2$, with $0<A<B$ and
$\beta>0$. The exact original density in EIQ10--EIQ11 is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lrc-eiq-density}
 \frac{d\mu}{dy}=H(y)\sqrt{(B-y)(y-A)},\qquad
 H(y)=\frac{\beta}{4\pi^2y}
       \int_0^\infty
       \frac{\sqrt s}{(y+s)\sqrt{(A+s)(B+s)}}\,ds.
\end{equation}
In the source notation the function called $h$ in EIQ10 is
$2\pi H$; the factor $1/(2\pi)$ in EIQ11 gives exactly
\eqref{eq:lrc-eiq-density}. No factor of $2\pi$, no mass, and no
term of the original potential has been changed.

Set
\[
 I(y)=\int_0^\infty
       \frac{\sqrt s}{(y+s)\sqrt{(A+s)(B+s)}}\,ds.
\]
This integral is finite and positive for $y>0$. On a compact
positive $y$ interval, its integrand is bounded near zero by a
constant times $\sqrt s$, and near infinity by a constant times
$s^{-3/2}$. Its derivative in $y$ is bounded on the same interval
near zero by a constant times $\sqrt s$ and near infinity by a
constant times $s^{-5/2}$. These bounds are integrable and uniform
in that compact interval; differentiation under the integral is
therefore justified and gives
\[
 I'(y)=-\int_0^\infty
       \frac{\sqrt s}{(y+s)^2\sqrt{(A+s)(B+s)}}\,ds.
\]
One may justify this differentiation directly by applying dominated
convergence to the difference quotient and the mean-value theorem
on a slightly larger compact positive $y$ interval. The same
majorants give continuity of both $I$ and $I'$. In particular
$H>0$ and $H\in C^1([A,B])$.

Define, for this calculation only, the probability measure on
$s\in(0,\infty)$
\[
 d\omega_y(s)=\frac1{I(y)}
       \frac{\sqrt s\,ds}{(y+s)\sqrt{(A+s)(B+s)}}.
\]
Its integral equals one by definition; it is an exact expression
for the logarithmic derivative of the original $I$, not a change
in the original equilibrium measure. Since $0<(y+s)^{-1}<y^{-1}$
for $s>0$, we obtain
\[
 \frac{H'(y)}{H(y)}
 =-\frac1y+\frac{I'(y)}{I(y)}
 =-\frac1y-\int_0^\infty\frac{d\omega_y(s)}{y+s},
 \qquad
 -\frac2y\leq\frac{H'(y)}{H(y)}\leq-\frac1y.
\]
Thus $H$ is decreasing. Writing $R=B/A>1$ and integrating the
displayed derivative bound over $[A,B]$ gives
\[
 \log\frac{H(A)}{H(B)}
 =-\int_A^B\frac{H'(y)}{H(y)}\,dy
 \leq2\log\frac BA,
 \qquad
 \frac{\max_{[A,B]}H}{\min_{[A,B]}H}\leq R^2.
\]
For the exact affine pushforward already established,
$h(t)=c^2H(m+ct)$, and hence
\[
 Q_h=\frac{\max H}{\min H}\leq R^2,\qquad
 \left|\frac{h'(t)}{h(t)}\right|
 =c\left|\frac{H'(m+ct)}{H(m+ct)}\right|
 \leq\frac{2c}{A}=R-1.
\]
It follows that
\[
 K_h=Q_h(1+M_h)\leq R^2(1+R-1)=R^3.
\]
Substitution in \eqref{eq:lrc-quantile-original} proves the exact
constant asserted in LRC12:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lrc-eiq-discrepancy}
 \sum_{j=1}^n\log|x-y_j|
 \leq n\int_A^B\log|x-y|\,d\mu(y)
       +C_{\mathrm{disc}},\qquad
 C_{\mathrm{disc}}=24(B/A)^6+30(B/A)^2.
\end{equation}
This holds for the original EIQ measure and its original midpoint
probability quantiles, simultaneously for every positive integer
$n$ and every real $x$. All endpoint and exterior cases have
already been covered by the preceding proof. The stronger constant
$16(B/A)^6+24(B/A)^2$ is also proved, but is not needed to preserve
the stated downstream formulas.

\subsection{Original full-polynomial comparison and single-norm audit}
\label{eqreview:section}

The scalar estimates below retain the real coordinate, the source order,
the original opposite roots, and the complete Gamma measure.  Their
providers in the cumulative source are EIQ1--33 and ECL1--14, restated
with the exact measure conventions in GRF1--5 and GRE9--31.  In particular,
\[
 d\sigma(y)=\sigma(y)\,dy
   =\frac{|\Gamma(1/4+iy/2)|^2}{2\pi}\,dy,\qquad
 \sigma(\mathbb R)=\sqrt{2\pi},
\]
\[
 c_\Gamma e^{-\pi|y|/2}(1+|y|)^{-1/2}
 \le \sigma(y)\le
 C_\Gamma e^{-\pi|y|/2}(1+|y|)^{-1/2},\qquad
 c_\Gamma=\sqrt2 e^{-7/6},\quad
 C_\Gamma=\sqrt{2\sqrt5}\,e^{2/3}.
\tag{EQ1}
\]
No probability renormalization of this measure is made.  GRE13--16
prove its mass from the beta integral and its Fourier transform; GRE10--11
derive these exact pointwise constants from Binet's integral formula.

\paragraph{The exact coordinate map and the original roots.}
For a grid of degree \(b\), put \(Q_b=(b+1)^2\), \(c_b=b/2\), and
\[
 \chi_b(S)=\prod_{a,d=0}^{b}
   \bigl(S-c_b-(2a-b)\delta-i(2d-b)\gamma\bigr).
\]
Here the packet is the stipulated simple quartet, so that every displayed
factor occurs once.  The polynomial
\[
 {\cal Q}_b(y)=i^{-Q_b}\chi_b(c_b+iy)
 =\prod_{a,d=0}^{b}
   \bigl(y-(2d-b)\gamma+i(2a-b)\delta\bigr)
\tag{EQ2}
\]
is monic in the same real coordinate \(y\).  Its roots occur in opposite
pairs, with their full multiplicities, by the involution
\((a,d)\mapsto(b-a,b-d)\), and have modulus at most
\(b\sqrt{\delta^2+\gamma^2}\).  For a monic degree-\(r\) polynomial \(P(S)\)
the map
\[
 \widetilde P(y)=i^{-r}P(c_b+iy),\qquad
 P(S)=i^r\widetilde P((S-c_b)/i)
\tag{EQ3}
\]
is a bijection of the two monic affine fibres.  The leading coefficients
are one in both directions; expansion in powers proves that the inverse
formula holds on the whole fibre.  The pointwise product gains the phase
\(i^{Q_b+r}\), of modulus one, so the norm is unchanged.  This explains
the real-coordinate notation in every norm below without deleting an
affine phase.

Keep \(s=1\) or the original upper degree \(a\), even when \(b=a-8\), and
put
\[
 \ell_s=(s-1)/4,\quad n=Q_b+\ell_s,\quad
 R=\max\{b\sqrt{\delta^2+\gamma^2},\,2\ell_s+1/2\},\quad
 \epsilon_*=2^{-16}.
\]
The exact recurrence of the original Gamma density is
\[
 dm_s(y)=\beta_s\prod_{t=0}^{\ell_s-1}
      (y^2+(2t+1/2)^2)\,d\sigma(y),\qquad
 \beta_s=\frac{(2\pi)^{s/2}}{\sqrt2\,\Gamma(s/2)}.
\tag{EQ4}
\]
In the original source convention the density before the recurrence is
\[
 dm_s(y)=\frac{(2\pi)^{s/2}2^{s/2-1}}{\Gamma(s/2)}
       \frac{|\Gamma(s/4+iy/2)|^2}{2\pi}\,dy .
\]
Indeed \(s/4=1/4+\ell_s\), and each use of the classical recurrence
\(\Gamma(z+1)=z\Gamma(z)\)
\cite[eq.~(5.5.1)]{human:dlmf5} produces
\(|1/4+t+iy/2|^2=(y^2+(2t+1/2)^2)/4\); the source normalization
therefore gives the exact multiplier
\[
 \frac{(2\pi)^{s/2}2^{s/2-1-2\ell_s}}{\Gamma(s/2)}
 =\frac{(2\pi)^{s/2}}{\sqrt2\,\Gamma(s/2)}.
\]
The empty product for \(s=1\) is one.

Let
\[
 {\cal M}(y)=|{\cal Q}_b(y)|^2
       \prod_{t=0}^{\ell_s-1}(y^2+(2t+1/2)^2).
\]
An opposite pair contributes \(|y^2-\zeta^2|^2\).  The triangle
inequality and its reverse therefore give, for real \(|y|>R\),
\[
 (1-R^2/y^2)^n
 \le \frac{{\cal M}(y)}{|y|^{2n}}
 \le (1+R^2/y^2)^n.
\tag{EQ5}
\]
Each Gamma factor is between \(y^2\) and \(y^2+R^2\); its lower bound
is also at least \(y^2-R^2\).  This proves the exponent \(n=Q_b+\ell_s\)
in both directions.  On the entire real line, including inside the root
disk, the upper triangle inequality gives
\[
 {\cal M}(y)\le(y^2+R^2)^n.
\tag{EQ6}
\]

\paragraph{A whole-coefficient inner-interval estimate.}
Assume
\[
 n\ge100,\qquad R\le\epsilon_*n/2.
\tag{EQ7}
\]
For every complex polynomial \(P\) of degree at most \(2n\), define
\[
 H_0(P)=\int_{\mathbb R}|y|^{2n}|P(y)|^2\,d\sigma(y),\qquad
 H_{\cal M}(P)=\int_{\mathbb R}{\cal M}(y)|P(y)|^2\,d\sigma(y).
\]
The zero polynomial is immediate, so suppose \(P\ne0\).
On \([n,2n]\), the polynomials
\[
 \phi_d(y)=\sqrt{(2d+1)/n}\,L_d(2y/n-3),\qquad 0\le d\le2n,
\]
are an orthonormal basis for the Lebesgue polynomial norm, where \(L_d\)
is the ordinary Legendre polynomial.  The Rodrigues formula used here is
the Legendre specialization of the classical formula
\cite[eq.~(18.5.5) and Table~18.5.1]{human:dlmf18}.
Their orthogonality and squared
norm \(2/(2d+1)\) on \([-1,1]\) follow from Rodrigues' formula by \(d\)
integrations by parts.  The same formula gives the finite identity
\[
 L_d(z)=2^{-d}\sum_{j=0}^d
       \binom dj^2(z-1)^{d-j}(z+1)^j.
\]
For \(|z|\le4\), Vandermonde's identity and
\(\binom{2d}{d}\le4^d\) give
\[
 |L_d(z)|\le (5/2)^d\binom{2d}{d}\le10^d.
\]
If \(|y|\le\epsilon_*n\), then \(|2y/n-3|<4\).  Expanding \(P\) in
this orthonormal basis and using the complex Cauchy--Schwarz inequality
therefore proves
\[
 |P(y)|^2\le
 \frac{(2n+1)(4n+1)100^{2n}}{n}
       \int_n^{2n}|P(t)|^2\,dt .
\tag{EQ8}
\]
This bounds every coefficient direction at once.

On the same inner interval, (EQ6)--(EQ7) give
\[
 {\cal M}(y)\le(5\epsilon_*^2n^2/4)^n,\qquad
 |y|^{2n}\le(\epsilon_*^2n^2)^n
            \le(5\epsilon_*^2n^2/4)^n .
\]
On \([n,2n]\), (EQ1) instead gives
\[
 H_0(P)\ge
 c_\Gamma n^{2n}e^{-\pi n}(1+2n)^{-1/2}
       \int_n^{2n}|P(t)|^2\,dt .
\tag{EQ9}
\]
Using the entire mass \(\sqrt{2\pi}\) to bound the inner integration
and cancelling (EQ9) proves each of the inequalities
\[
 \int_{|y|\le\epsilon_*n}
       {\cal M}(y)|P(y)|^2\,d\sigma(y)\le\eta_nH_0(P),\qquad
 \int_{|y|\le\epsilon_*n}
       |y|^{2n}|P(y)|^2\,d\sigma(y)\le\eta_nH_0(P),
\tag{EQ10}
\]
where
\[
 \eta_n=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{c_\Gamma}
 \frac{(2n+1)(4n+1)\sqrt{1+2n}}{n}\,16^{-n}.
\tag{EQ11}
\]
The base is valid because
\((5/4)e^\pi10^4\epsilon_*^2<1/16\).
For an entirely rational overestimate, use \(\pi<4\), \(e<3\), and
\(16(5/4)81\cdot10^4<2^{32}\).  Also
\(\sqrt{2\pi}/c_\Gamma=\sqrt\pi e^{7/6}<8\), whence
\(\eta_n\le240n^2\,16^{-n}<1/2\) already for \(n\ge4\).
The last sequence decreases there since \(((n+1)/n)^2<16\).
Thus no inner half-interval, root factor, or coefficient direction has
been removed.

\paragraph{The full form and all its original minima.}
For \(0\le z<1\), integration of \(1/(1-z)\) gives
\(-\log(1-z)\le z/(1-z)\), and \(\log(1+z)\le z\).
Consequently (EQ5) implies on \(|y|>\epsilon_*n\)
\[
 e^{-B_{\rm root}}|y|^{2n}\le{\cal M}(y)
       \le e^{B_{\rm root}}|y|^{2n},\qquad
 B_{\rm root}=\frac{nR^2}{\epsilon_*^2n^2-R^2}.
\]
Combine this with (EQ10), retaining the integrals over both half-lines:
\[
 \boxed{\;
 \beta_s e^{-B_{\rm root}}(1-\eta_n)H_0(P)
 \le \|\chi_bP\|_s^2
 \le \beta_s e^{B_{\rm root}}(1+\eta_n)H_0(P)
 \;}.
\tag{EQ12}
\]
Here (EQ3) identifies the notation on the left and right with the original
vertical-line coefficient space.  For the upper bound the intermediate
inequality is
\(H_{\cal M}\le e^{B_{\rm root}}H_{0,\mathrm{outer}}+\eta_nH_0
\le e^{B_{\rm root}}(1+\eta_n)H_0\).
For the lower bound it is
\(H_{\cal M}\ge e^{-B_{\rm root}}H_{0,\mathrm{outer}}
\ge e^{-B_{\rm root}}(1-\eta_n)H_0\).
This proves LRC14 with precisely its constants.

More generally fix any nonempty affine fibre \({\cal F}\) in the coefficient
space of degree at most \(2n\), including a fibre specified by all preceding
relation constraints.  Since (EQ12) holds at every point of this same
fibre, taking its infimum proves
\[
 \beta_s e^{-B_{\rm root}}(1-\eta_n)
       \inf_{P\in{\cal F}}H_0(P)
 \le\inf_{P\in{\cal F}}\|\chi_bP\|_s^2
 \le\beta_s e^{B_{\rm root}}(1+\eta_n)
       \inf_{P\in{\cal F}}H_0(P).
\tag{EQ13}
\]
The forms are positive definite: a nonzero polynomial cannot vanish on
an interval, and the weights are positive almost everywhere.  Their
finite coefficient Gram matrices are therefore positive definite.
Completing a square proves attainment and uniqueness on every closed
affine fibre, in particular the monic fibre.  Equation (EQ13) is the
exact quotient-norm comparison; it does not replace the old Gram inverse
by a diagonal matrix.

For the actual discrete family,
\(R^2=O_{\delta,\gamma}(a^2)\) and \(n=a^2+O(a)\).  Moreover (EQ7) gives
\[
 B_{\rm root}\le\frac{4R^2}{3\epsilon_*^2n}
       =O_{\delta,\gamma}(1).
\tag{EQ14}
\]
This statement keeps \(s=1\) or \(a\) at both grids.

\paragraph{Parity and the exact retained-Gamma monic norm.}
Put \(r=2h+\varepsilon\ge2\), \(h\ge1\), \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}\), and
\[
 \nu_r^0(n)=\min_{\deg P=r,\ P\ {\rm monic}}H_0(P),\qquad
 \alpha=\frac{n+\varepsilon-3/4}{h},\quad
 \beta=\frac{\pi n}{2h}.
\]
The even and odd polynomial subspaces are orthogonal for \(H_0\).
The parity opposite to \(r\) has no leading term and contributes a
nonnegative separate squared norm; its minimizer is zero.  On the
remaining parity fibre the exact monic bijection is
\[
 P(y)=n^r(y/n)^\varepsilon p(y^2/n^2),\qquad
 p\in\mathbb C[x]\ {\rm monic},\quad\deg p=h.
\tag{EQ15}
\]
Define, for \(y>0\),
\[
 {\mathfrak r}(y)=\frac{\sigma(y)e^{\pi y/2}\sqrt y}{\sqrt2},\qquad
 h_{h,n}(\alpha,\beta)
 =\min_{\deg p=h,\ p\ {\rm monic}}
   \int_0^\infty |p(x)|^2 e^{-h(\beta\sqrt x-\alpha\log x)}
                         {\mathfrak r}(n\sqrt x)\,dx.
\tag{EQ16}
\]
The two real half-lines in (EQ15) contribute equally.  Under
\(y=n\sqrt x\), their combined Jacobian is \(n/\sqrt x\), and
\(\sigma(n\sqrt x)=\sqrt2\,n^{-1/2}x^{-1/4}
e^{-\pi n\sqrt x/2}{\mathfrak r}(n\sqrt x)\).
It follows without a limiting step that
\[
 \boxed{\nu_r^0(n)=\sqrt2\,n^{2(n+r)+1/2}
                 h_{h,n}(\alpha,\beta).}
\tag{EQ17}
\]
In particular the shift is \(-3/4\).  The measures in (EQ16) have positive
density, finite moments, and a positive definite coefficient Gram matrix,
so their monic minima are attained uniquely as above.

\paragraph{Original equilibrium and the entire positive half-line.}
The accepted EIQ equilibrium for
\(V(x)=\beta\sqrt x-\alpha\log x\) has support
\([A,B]=[u^2,v_*^2]\), probability density
\[
 H(x)\sqrt{(B-x)(x-A)},\qquad
 H(x)=\frac{\beta}{4\pi^2x}
   \int_0^\infty
   \frac{\sqrt t}{(x+t)\sqrt{(A+t)(B+t)}}\,dt>0.
\tag{EQ18}
\]
Its logarithmic potential \(P_{\rm eq}(x)=\int\log|x-y|\,d\mu(y)\)
and original Robin constant satisfy
\[
 D(x)=V(x)-2P_{\rm eq}(x)-\lambda\ge0\quad(0<x<\infty),
 \qquad D(x)=0\quad(A\le x\le B).
\tag{EQ19}
\]
The endpoint equations and Robin identity are
\[
 uK(\kappa)=\frac{\alpha\pi}{\beta},\qquad
 v_*E(\kappa)=\frac{(\alpha+2)\pi}{\beta},\qquad
 \kappa^2=1-u^2/v_*^2,
\]
\[
 \lambda=2(\alpha+2)-2\alpha\log((u+v_*)/2)
                   -2\log((B-A)/4).
\tag{EQ20}
\]
These are exactly EIQ3--19 and ECL6, rather than a substituted potential.
The independent quantile proof accompanying this fragment establishes
LRC11--12 directly from (EQ18):
for the \(h\) midpoint probability quantiles \(x_i\),
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^h\log|x-x_i|
 \le hP_{\rm eq}(x)+C_{\rm disc}\quad(x\in\mathbb R),\qquad
 C_{\rm disc}=24(B/A)^6+30(B/A)^2.
\tag{EQ21}
\]
It applies in particular to \(h=1\).

From (EQ1),
\[
 0<{\mathfrak r}(y)\le r_+=C_\Gamma/\sqrt2,\qquad
 {\mathfrak r}(n\sqrt x)\ge r_-=
 \frac{c_\Gamma}{\sqrt2}\sqrt{\frac{nu}{1+nu}}
 \quad(A\le x\le B).
\tag{EQ22}
\]
For the full tail, set
\[
 T_*=\max\left\{1,v_*,
 \left[\frac{8(\alpha+2)}{\beta}\right]^2,
 \frac{4|\lambda|}{\beta}\right\},\qquad
 J_*=T_*^2+\frac8{\beta^2}.
\tag{EQ23}
\]
If \(t\ge v_*\) and \(y\in[A,B]\), then
\(0\le t^2-y\le t^2\).  Hence \(P_{\rm eq}(t^2)\le2\log t\),
including \(t=v_*\) by integrability of the endpoint logarithm.
Thus
\[
 D(t^2)\ge\beta t-2(\alpha+2)\log t-|\lambda|.
\]
For \(t\ge1\), \(\log t\le\sqrt t\), since
\(\max_{t\ge1}(\log t)/\sqrt t=2/e<1\).
The two nonconstant entries in (EQ23) imply for \(t\ge T_*\)
\[
 2(\alpha+2)\log t\le\beta t/4,\qquad
 |\lambda|\le\beta t/4,\qquad D(t^2)\ge\beta t/2 .
\]
Because \(h\ge1\) and (EQ19) holds all the way to zero,
\[
 \int_0^\infty e^{-hD(x)}\,dx
 \le T_*^2+\int_0^\infty2t e^{-\beta t/2}\,dt
 =J_* .
\tag{EQ24}
\]
This proves the displayed LRC15 cutoff itself.  No extra \(\log2\) is
needed: the stronger inequality \(t^2-y\le t^2\) at \(t\ge v_*\)
replaces the looser estimate \(t^2+y\le2t^2\).

\paragraph{The arcsine lower constant, including all complex roots.}
Put \(c_{\rm cap}=(B-A)/4\) and let
\(d\nu=dx/(\pi\sqrt{(B-x)(x-A)})\) be arcsine probability.
For every complex \(z\),
\[
 \int\log|x-z|\,d\nu(x)\ge\log c_{\rm cap},
\tag{EQ25}
\]
with equality for \(z\in[A,B]\).
Here is a direct proof.  Write \(x=(A+B)/2+d\cos\theta\),
\(d=(B-A)/2\), and factor
\(Z^2-2(z-(A+B)/2)Z/d+1=(Z-z_1)(Z-z_2)\).
On \(|Z|=1\),
\(|x-z|=(d/2)|Z-z_1||Z-z_2|\), while \(z_1z_2=1\).
The circle mean of \(\log|Z-z_j|\) is
\(\log\max(1,|z_j|)\): integrate the convergent logarithmic power
series inside the circle, factor out \(|z_j|\) outside it, and use
integrability of \(|\log|\theta||\) for the boundary limit.
Both means are nonnegative; both vanish exactly when the two roots
are on the circle, which occurs for \(z\in[A,B]\).  Symmetry in
\(\cos\theta\) identifies this circle mean with the arcsine integral.

Factor any complex monic degree-\(h\) polynomial root by root.
Equation (EQ25) gives \(\int\log|p|\,d\nu\ge h\log c_{\rm cap}\).
Integrating the Euler equality (EQ19), interchanging its logarithmic
integrals, and applying equality in (EQ25) gives
\(\int V\,d\nu=2\log c_{\rm cap}+\lambda\).
All the logarithms are integrable: the equilibrium density is bounded,
and arcsine endpoint singularities are integrable against a logarithm.
The entropy is exactly
\[
 \int\log(d\nu/dx)\,d\nu=-\log(\pi c_{\rm cap}).
\]
Indeed the same angular substitution reduces this to
\(\pi^{-1}\int_0^\pi\log\sin\theta\,d\theta=-\log2\);
the last identity follows by adding its sine and cosine integrals
on \([0,\pi/2]\) and doubling the angle.
The corresponding finite circle-weight Jensen inequality is recorded by
Szeg\H{o} \cite[\S12.3, eq.~(12.3.4), pp.~300--301]{human:szego1975};
the preceding angular map and entropy computation give its exact
interval constants here.  Probability Jensen applied to
\(|p|^2e^{-hV}/(d\nu/dx)\) now proves
\[
 \int_A^B|p(x)|^2e^{-hV(x)}\,dx
 \ge \pi c_{\rm cap} e^{-h\lambda}.
\tag{EQ26}
\]
Zeros cause only integrable logarithmic singularities, so this applies
to every complex monic polynomial.  Multiply by (EQ22) and then
take the same monic minimum.

For the upper bound use the actual quantile polynomial
\(p_h(x)=\prod_{i=1}^h(x-x_i)\).  Equations (EQ19)--(EQ21) give pointwise
on the entire positive half-line
\[
 |p_h(x)|^2e^{-hV(x)}
 \le e^{2C_{\rm disc}}e^{-h\lambda}e^{-hD(x)}.
\]
Integrating with (EQ22)--(EQ24) proves
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 c_-e^{-h\lambda}\le h_{h,n}(\alpha,\beta)
                \le c_+e^{-h\lambda},\qquad
 c_-=r_-\pi(B-A)/4,\quad
 c_+=r_+J_*e^{2C_{\rm disc}}.\quad}
\tag{EQ27}
\]
This is LRC15 with exactly the stated constants.

\paragraph{Explicit uniform constants when the degree starts at one.}
The following proof does not assume an upper bound on \(\alpha\).
It applies on the whole wedge
\[
 \alpha\ge1/2,\qquad \pi/3\le c:=\beta/\alpha\le\pi.
\tag{EQ28}
\]
Write \(s_0\in(0,1)\) for the unique solution
\[
 \frac{E(\sqrt{1-s_0^2})}
      {s_0K(\sqrt{1-s_0^2})}=5.
\tag{EQ29}
\]
Existence and uniqueness follow from the endpoint monotonicity in
EIQ6; they can also be checked from the derivative just below and the
limits \(1\) at \(s=1\) and \(+\infty\) at \(s=0\).
For the latter limit, \(E\ge1\) and
\(sK\to0\) by dominated convergence in its defining integral.
Use the abbreviations
\(K_s=K(\sqrt{1-s^2})\), \(E_s=E(\sqrt{1-s^2})\), \(f(s)=E_s/(sK_s)\).
The endpoint equation is
\[
 f(s)=1+2/\alpha,\qquad s=u/v_*.
\]
Direct differentiation under the integral and integration of the derivative
of
\[
\frac{\sin\theta\cos\theta}{\sqrt{\cos^2\theta+s^2\sin^2\theta}}
\]
give
\[
 -f'(s)=
 \frac{(K_s-E_s)(E_s-s^2K_s)}
      {(1-s^2)s^2K_s^2}>0.
\tag{EQ30}
\]
Here \(K_s-E_s\) is the integral of
\((1-s^2)\sin^2\theta/
\sqrt{\cos^2\theta+s^2\sin^2\theta}\);
\(E_s-s^2K_s\) is the same integral with \(\cos^2\theta\).
Each is between \((1-s^2)\pi/4\) and
\((1-s^2)\pi/(4s)\), whereas
\(\pi/2\le K_s\le\pi/(2s)\).
Since (EQ28) implies \(s\ge s_0\), these inequalities yield
\[
 \frac{1-s^2}{4}\le-f'(s)
       \le\frac{1-s^2}{4s_0^4}.
\]
Integration from \(s\) to \(1\) and
\[
 \frac1{16}\le\frac{s+2}{12(1+s)^2}\le\frac16
       \quad(0\le s\le1)
\]
give the explicit comparison
\[
 \frac{(1-s^2)^2}{16}\le\frac2\alpha
       \le\frac{(1-s^2)^2}{6s_0^4}.
\tag{EQ31}
\]
In particular this estimate includes \(\alpha\to\infty\) without
an asymptotic assumption.

Define the fixed, explicitly evaluable positive numbers
\[
 u_0=\frac{2s_0}{\pi},\quad v_0=\frac{30}{\pi s_0},\quad
 C_0=\frac{2\sqrt3\,s_0^4}{\pi^2},\quad
 C_1=\frac{900\sqrt2}{\pi^2s_0^2}.
\]
The original endpoint formulas, the bounds on \(K_s,E_s\), and
\(1+2/\alpha\le5\) prove
\[
 u\ge u_0,\qquad v_*\le v_0,\qquad
 B/A\le s_0^{-2},\qquad
 C_0\le c_{\rm cap}\sqrt\alpha\le C_1 .
\tag{EQ32}
\]
For the last inequality use

\subsection{The separate power-exponential reference and its moving centre}

\paragraph{The precise clipped-quantile estimate.}

and put \(P_d(x)=\prod_i(x-x_i)\).  The empirical distribution function
differs from the equilibrium distribution function by at most \(1/(2d)\),
and hence by at most \(1/d\).  For a fixed real \(x\), define on the
support

 f_x(y)=\log\max\{|x-y|,1/d\}.

The function is continuous and of bounded variation.  If \(x\) lies
inside the support, there are two monotone pieces, each having variation
at most \(\log(1+dW)\); if \(x\) lies outside it, the sole monotone
piece has that bound.  The latter statement follows from
\(\max(r+W,1/d)/\max(r,1/d)\le1+dW\), \(r\ge0\).

probability measures has zero endpoint term and gives

 \sum_{i=1}^d f_x(x_i)\le
 d\int f_x\,d\mu+2\log(1+dW).

The clipping error, using (EQ40), satisfies

 d\int(f_x(y)-\log|x-y|)\,d\mu(y)
 \le dM\int_{-1/d}^{1/d}\log\frac{1/d}{|t|}\,dt=2M.

the value \(-\infty\) at a root, this proves for every real \(x\)
\[
 \log|P_d(x)|\le dP_{\rm eq}(x)+2M+2\log(1+dW).
\tag{EQ41}

No unproved smoothness of a clipped kernel is used.

\section{Complete root--Gamma bounds for the two attained-tail monic fibres}
\label{sec:atgpa-audit}

Throughout this section retain the original parameters
\[
 0<\delta<\tfrac12,\qquad \gamma>2,\qquad
 j=4l+1\ge257,\qquad s\in\{1,j\},
\]
and the original fixed conductor order $v$, with
$0\le v\le\Delta$. Put
\[
\begin{gathered}
 q=(j+1)^2,\quad q'=(j-7)^2,\quad
 \Delta=16j-48,\quad m=\Delta-v,\quad
 c_+=j/2,\quad c_-=j/2-4,\\
 D=\sqrt{\delta^2+\gamma^2},\quad A=jD,\quad
 \epsilon=2^{-32},\quad T=\epsilon q,\quad
 A\le T/2,\quad 0\le r\le q+32j.
\end{gathered}\tag{ATG1}
\]
The letter $A$ here is a real root-radius bound, not the original
conductor operator. No parameter or root is changed by this notation.
Define
\[
 \ell_s=(s-1)/4,\quad M_s=(2\pi)^{s/2},\quad
 \beta_s=\frac{M_s}{\sqrt2\,\Gamma(s/2)},\quad
 \vartheta=\pi/2,\quad h=1/q.
\tag{ATG2}
\]
The integers used as monic degrees are $b_+=r$ and $b_-=m+r$;
the two root counts are $n_+=q$ and $n_-=q'$; and
$Q_\pm=n_\pm+\ell_s$.

Here are all finite constants in the comparison:
\[
\begin{gathered}
 C_L=\frac\pi{\Gamma(3/4)^2},\qquad
 C_U=\frac{\Gamma(1/4)^2}{2\pi},\qquad
 K_q=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{C_L}\frac{(2q+1)^2}{q}e^{-2q},\\
 E_j=\frac{q A^2}{T^2-A^2}+\log(1+K_q),\\
 S_\ell=\sum_{a=0}^{\ell-1}(2a+\tfrac12)^2
       =\frac{\ell(16\ell^2-12\ell-1)}{12},\\
 \rho_0=0,\qquad
 \rho_\ell=\frac{S_\ell}{T^2}+\log(1+K_q)\quad(\ell>0),\\
 L_h=C_L(\sin h)^{3/2},\qquad U_h=C_U(\sin h)^{-1/2},\\
 L_{b,Q}=b+\sqrt{b(b+2Q)},\qquad
 K_{b,Q}=2Q+1+2L_{b,Q},\\
 b^-_{n,b,s}=-E_j+\log L_h
              -K_{b,n+\ell_s}\log(1+h/\vartheta),\\
 b^+_{n,b,s}=E_j+\rho_{\ell_s}+\log U_h
              +K_{b,n+\ell_s}\log\frac1{1-h/\vartheta}.
\end{gathered}\tag{ATG3}
\]
The $E_j$ in both signs is exactly the common GPA root error, with
the original symbol $k$ replaced by its attained-tail index $j$.
In particular, the lower-root application still uses the unchanged
common outer scale $q$, cutoff $T$, and rate displacement $h$.

We prove below, for $\zeta\in\{+,-\}$, every $b\le2q$, and every
$f\in\mathbb C[S]$ of degree at most $b$, the whole-space inequalities
\[
\begin{split}
 \beta_s e^{b^-_{n_\zeta,b,s}}
  \int_{\mathbb R}|f(c_\zeta+iy)|^2
           |y|^{2(n_\zeta+\ell_s)}e^{-\vartheta|y|}\,dy
 &\le\int_{\mathbb R}|\chi_{j_\zeta}(c_\zeta+iy)
                 f(c_\zeta+iy)|^2\,dm_s(y)\\
 &\le\beta_s e^{b^+_{n_\zeta,b,s}}
  \int_{\mathbb R}|f(c_\zeta+iy)|^2
           |y|^{2(n_\zeta+\ell_s)}e^{-\vartheta|y|}\,dy,
\end{split}\tag{ATG4}
\]
where $j_+=j$, $j_-=j-8$, and the original source is
\[
 dm_s(y)=M_s\frac{2^{s/2}}{4\pi\Gamma(s/2)}
                 |\Gamma(s/4+iy/2)|^2\,dy.
\tag{ATG5}
\]
No restriction on the zeros, parity, coefficients, or extremality of
$f$ occurs in (ATG4).

\subsection{The exact original root and coefficient maps}

The complete original root polynomials, including every multiplicity,
are
\[
\begin{split}
 P_j(y)&=\prod_{a,t=0}^{j}
       [y-(2t-j)\gamma+i(2a-j)\delta],\\
 P_{j-8}(y)&=\prod_{a,t=0}^{j-8}
       [y-(2t-j+8)\gamma+i(2a-j+8)\delta].
\end{split}\tag{ATG6}
\]
Their exact original-coordinate identities are
$\chi_j(c_++iy)=i^qP_j(y)$ and
$\chi_{j-8}(c_-+iy)=i^{q'}P_{j-8}(y)$.
The respective involutions $(a,t)\mapsto(j-a,j-t)$ and
$(a,t)\mapsto(j-8-a,j-8-t)$ pair every root with its negative.
All roots have modulus at most $A$, and both degrees are even.
For fixed $b$, the map
\[
 (\Pi_{c,b}f)(y)=i^{-b}f(c+iy),\qquad
 \Pi_{c,b}^{-1}p(S)=i^bp((S-c)/i)
\tag{ATG7}
\]
is an exact linear isomorphism of the degree-at-most-$b$ coefficient
spaces. Its matrix entry in row $a$, column $t$ is
$\binom ta c^{t-a}i^{a-b}$ for $a\le t$, and zero otherwise.
It maps the original monic degree-$b$ affine fibre bijectively onto
the monic degree-$b$ fibre in $y$. The factors $i^{-b}$ and $i^{n_\zeta}$
have absolute value one; they therefore disappear only after the
squared norms are formed, without changing any norm or minimum.

\subsection{Full source mass and the positive Gamma recurrence}

The defining Euler integral is the classical formula recorded by
R.~A. Askey and R. Roy \cite[eq.~(5.2.1)]{human:dlmf5}.
For $a>0$, two such Euler integrals, the substitution $t=e^u$ in their
beta integral, and Fourier inversion give
\[
 \frac{\Gamma(a+ix)\Gamma(a-ix)}{\Gamma(2a)}
 =\int_{\mathbb R}e^{ixu}(2\cosh(u/2))^{-2a}\,du,
 \qquad
 \int_{\mathbb R}|\Gamma(a+ix)|^2dx
 =2\pi\Gamma(2a)2^{-2a}.
\tag{ATG8}
\]
To justify the inversion absolutely, rotate Euler's integration ray
through $\operatorname{sgn}(x)\theta$, with $0<\theta<\pi/2$.
The large connecting arc is bounded by a polynomial times an
exponential with negative real exponent; the small arc tends to zero
because $a>0$. Taking the modulus on the rotated ray proves
\[
 |\Gamma(a+ix)|\le e^{-\theta|x|}\Gamma(a)(\cos\theta)^{-a}.
\tag{ATG9}
\]
Thus the integrable Fourier transform in (ATG8) may be inverted at
$u=0$. With $a=s/4$ and $y=2x$, (ATG8) proves
$\int dm_s=M_s$, including its full original mass.

Let
$d\sigma(y)=|\Gamma(1/4+iy/2)|^2dy/(2\pi)$.
Integration by parts in Euler's integral gives
$\Gamma(z+1)=z\Gamma(z)$, the classical functional equation
\cite[eq.~(5.5.1)]{human:dlmf5}. Iterating exactly $\ell_s$ times gives
\[
 dm_s(y)=\beta_s\prod_{a=0}^{\ell_s-1}
                (y^2+(2a+\tfrac12)^2)\,d\sigma(y).
\tag{ATG10}
\]
Indeed each squared recurrence factor contributes
$(y^2+(2a+1/2)^2)/4$; since $s/2=2\ell_s+1/2$, the remaining
constant is precisely $\beta_s/(2\pi)$ before the Gamma square.
For $s=1$, $\ell_s=0$ and $\beta_1=1$, so
$\int d\sigma=\sqrt{2\pi}$.

The reflection identity used here is Euler's reflection formula,
as recorded by Askey and Roy \cite[eq.~(5.5.3)]{human:dlmf5}.
For completeness, the reflection product needed below follows
directly from the beta integral
$\Gamma(z)\Gamma(1-z)=\int_0^\infty t^{z-1}/(1+t)\,dt$,
$0<\Re z<1$. On the keyhole contour with argument in $(0,2\pi)$,
the radial integrals give the multiplier $1-e^{2\pi iz}$; the
residue at $-1$ is $e^{i\pi(z-1)}$; the two circular contributions
tend to zero as $O(r^{\Re z})$ and $O(R^{\Re z-1})$.
The residue theorem therefore evaluates that integral as
$\pi/\sin(\pi z)$. At $z=1/4+iy/2$ it yields
\[
 |\Gamma(1/4+iy/2)|^2|\Gamma(3/4-iy/2)|^2
       =\frac{2\pi^2}{\cosh(\pi y)}.
\tag{ATG11}
\]
The unrotated Euler integral bounds the second squared factor by
$\Gamma(3/4)^2$, and consequently
\[
 \frac{d\sigma}{dy}\ge C_Le^{-\pi|y|},\qquad
 \frac{dm_s}{dy}\ge\beta_s q^{2\ell_s}C_Le^{-2\pi q}
                 \quad(q\le y\le2q).
\tag{ATG12}
\]
Furthermore, without suppressing either original mass,
\[
 \frac{M_s}{\beta_s q^{2\ell_s}}
 =\sqrt2\,\Gamma(2\ell_s+1/2)q^{-2\ell_s}
 \le\sqrt{2\pi}.
\tag{ATG13}
\]
To see the inequality, expand the Gamma value as
$\sqrt\pi\prod_{a=0}^{2\ell_s-1}(a+1/2)$ and bound each factor
by $s/2\le q$.

\subsection{A bound on every polynomial direction, including the inner region}

For every $p\in\mathbb C[y]$ of degree at most $b$ and
$0<T\le q/2$,
\[
 \sup_{|y|\le T}|p(y)|^2
 \le\frac{(b+1)^2 10^{2b}}q\int_q^{2q}|p(u)|^2du.
\tag{ATG14}
\]
Here is a coefficient-space proof. The Rodrigues polynomials
$L_a$ are the classical Legendre polynomials; the formula and norm
are recorded by T.~H. Koornwinder, R. Wong, R. Koekoek,
R.~F. Swarttouw and W.~P. Reinhardt
\cite[eq.~(18.5.5), Table~18.5.1, and the Legendre and
shifted-Legendre rows of Table~18.3.1]{human:dlmf18}.
In precisely those conventions, the polynomials
$L_a(x)=(2^aa!)^{-1}(d/dx)^a(x^2-1)^a$ have squared norm
$2/(2a+1)$ on $[-1,1]$ and are orthogonal to lower degrees,
by $a$ integrations by parts. The boundary derivatives of orders
less than $a$ vanish. Their norm follows by pairing the leading
coefficient $(2a)!/(2^a(a!)^2)$ against $x^a$ and using
$\int_0^1 t^a(1-t)^a dt=(a!)^2/(2a+1)!$, itself obtained by
repeated integration by parts. Leibniz's rule gives
$L_a(x)=2^{-a}\sum_{t=0}^a\binom at^2(x-1)^{a-t}(x+1)^t$.
For $|x|\le4$, its modulus is at most
$(5/2)^a\sum_t\binom at^2\le10^a$.
Expand $p(qz)$ in the orthonormal basis
$\sqrt{2a+1}L_a(2z-3)$ on $1\le z\le2$.
For $|z|\le1/2$, Cauchy--Schwarz and
$\sum_{a=0}^b(2a+1)=(b+1)^2$ give (ATG14), after changing
variables back to $y$.

Let $P$ be either polynomial (ATG6), with degree $n\in\{q,q'\}$.
For $|y|\ge T$, pairing roots $\omega,-\omega$ gives
\[
 \left|\log\frac{|P(y)|^2}{|y|^{2n}}\right|
 \le-n\log(1-A^2/T^2)\le\frac{nA^2}{T^2-A^2}.
\tag{ATG15}
\]
This follows from $1-u\le|1-z|\le1+u$ for $|z|\le u<1$,
and $-\log(1-u)\le u/(1-u)$.
For all real $y$, the same pairing gives
$|P(y)|^2\le(y^2+A^2)^n$.
Set $B=\int_q^{2q}|y|^{2n}|p(y)|^2dm_s(y)$.
By (ATG12)--(ATG14), each of
\[
 \int_{|y|<T}|P(y)p(y)|^2dm_s(y),\qquad
 \int_{|y|<T}|y|^{2n}|p(y)|^2dm_s(y)
\]
is at most $\kappa B$, where
\[
 \kappa=\frac{M_s}{\beta_s q^{2\ell_s}C_L}
  \frac{(b+1)^2}{q}e^{2\pi q}10^{2b}
             \left(\frac{T^2+A^2}{q^2}\right)^n.
\tag{ATG16}
\]
The bounds $n\ge q/2$, $b\le2q$, $A\le T/2$ imply
\[
 \kappa\le\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{C_L}\frac{(2q+1)^2}{q}
 \exp\{q(2\pi+4\log10+\tfrac12\log(5/4)+\log\epsilon)\}
 \le K_q.
\tag{ATG17}
\]
For an exact elementary check of the last exponent,
$\pi<4$, $\log10<4\log2$, $\log(5/4)<1/4$, and
$\log2>2/3$ give
$2\pi+4\log10+\tfrac12\log(5/4)-32\log2
 <8-16\log2+1/8<-61/24<-2$.
The inequalities for $\pi$ and $\log2$ follow respectively from
$\pi/4=\int_0^1(1+x^2)^{-1}dx$ and
$\log2=2\int_0^{1/3}(1-t^2)^{-1}dt$.

Let $I_{\rm out}$ be the reference integral with $|y|^{2n}$
outside $(-T,T)$. Since $B\le I_{\rm out}$, both inner integrals
are bounded by $K_q I_{\rm out}$. The full reference norm lies
between $I_{\rm out}$ and $(1+K_q)I_{\rm out}$.
The full actual norm lies between
$e^{-nA^2/(T^2-A^2)}I_{\rm out}$ and
$e^{nA^2/(T^2-A^2)}(1+K_q)I_{\rm out}$.
Thus for every coefficient direction, including the zero polynomial,
\[
 e^{-E_j}\int|p|^2|y|^{2n}dm_s
 \le\int|pP|^2dm_s
 \le e^{E_j}\int|p|^2|y|^{2n}dm_s.
\tag{ATG18}
\]

We also prove the full-form recurrence comparison. Put $Q=n+\ell_s$.
Pointwise,
$|y|^{2n}\prod_a(y^2+(2a+1/2)^2)\ge |y|^{2Q}$.
For $|y|\ge T$ its logarithmic ratio to $|y|^{2Q}$ is at most
$S_{\ell_s}/T^2$. On $|y|<T$ its numerator is at most
$(T^2+(j/2)^2)^Q$.
Apply (ATG14), the $s=1$ case of (ATG12), and
$\int d\sigma=\sqrt{2\pi}$. Since $j/2\le A\le T/2$,
$Q\ge q/2$, and the base $(5/4)\epsilon^2$ is less than one,
the calculation (ATG16)--(ATG17) bounds the entire inner numerator
by $K_q$ times the reference integral on $[q,2q]$.
The full upper ratio is therefore at most
$e^{S_{\ell_s}/T^2}+K_q\le e^{\rho_{\ell_s}}$.
At $\ell_s=0$ the comparison is the exact identity. In all cases,
\[
 \beta_s\int|p|^2|y|^{2Q}d\sigma
 \le\int|p|^2|y|^{2n}dm_s
 \le\beta_s e^{\rho_{\ell_s}}\int|p|^2|y|^{2Q}d\sigma.
\tag{ATG19}
\]
In particular, no inner interval has been discarded in either
comparison.

\subsection{Global density bounds and their exact rate-change map}

The classical inputs are again Euler's integral and Euler's
reflection identity \cite[eqs.~(5.2.1), (5.5.3)]{human:dlmf5};
the complete inequalities following from them are proved here.
Apply (ATG9) with $\theta=\pi/2-h$, first at $a=1/4$.
After squaring and dividing by $2\pi$, it gives
$d\sigma/dy\le U_he^{-(\vartheta-h)|y|}$.
At $a=3/4$, (ATG9) bounds the second Gamma square in (ATG11)
by $\Gamma(3/4)^2(\sin h)^{-3/2}e^{-(\vartheta-h)|y|}$.
Dividing (ATG11) by that bound, then using
$\cosh(\pi y)\le e^{\pi|y|}$, proves on the whole real line
\[
 L_he^{-(\vartheta+h)|y|}
 \le\frac{d\sigma}{dy}(y)
 \le U_he^{-(\vartheta-h)|y|}.
\tag{ATG20}
\]

Write $\|p\|_{Q,\kappa}^2=\int|p(y)|^2|y|^{2Q}
e^{-\kappa|y|}dy$ and $\mathsf A p=yp'(y)$ on polynomials of
degree at most $b$. We claim
$\|\mathsf A\|\le L_{b,Q}$ for each $\kappa>0$.
On the positive half-line, set $x=\kappa y$ and $\alpha=2Q$.
The classical Laguerre Rodrigues formula, series, and squared norms
are recorded by Koornwinder, Wong, Koekoek, Swarttouw and Reinhardt
\cite[Table~18.3.1; eq.~(18.5.5), Table~18.5.1;
eq.~(18.5.12)]{human:dlmf18}. In those exact conventions, the polynomials
\[
 \mathcal L_a^{(\alpha)}(x)
 =\frac{x^{-\alpha}e^x}{a!}
          \left(\frac d{dx}\right)^a(e^{-x}x^{a+\alpha})
 =\sum_{t=0}^a\frac{(-1)^t}{t!}
                         \binom{a+\alpha}{a-t}x^t
\tag{ATG21}
\]
are orthogonal to lower degrees and have squared norm
$\Gamma(a+\alpha+1)/a!$ in $x^\alpha e^{-x}dx$.
These assertions follow by $a$ integrations by parts; every boundary
term vanishes, as the power at zero before the final integration is
at least $\alpha+1$ and the exponential controls infinity.
The coefficient formula in (ATG21) gives
$x(\mathcal L_a^{(\alpha)})'
=a\mathcal L_a^{(\alpha)}-(a+\alpha)\mathcal L_{a-1}^{(\alpha)}$.
In the orthonormal basis, this is the sum of a diagonal matrix of
norm $b$ and a one-step shift of norm $\sqrt{b(b+2Q)}$.
The same estimate applies to $p(-y)$ on the negative half-line;
adding the two squared estimates proves the claim on the full line.
The pullback $x=\kappa y$ preserves the operator and contributes
only the common positive factor $\kappa^{-2Q-1}$ to squared norms.

The exact dilation map is $p(y)\mapsto p(e^ty)=\exp(t\mathsf A)p$,
with diagonal monomial matrix $\operatorname{diag}(1,e^t,\ldots,e^{bt})$.
The finite matrix exponential bound, followed by the same bound for
its inverse, gives
$e^{-L_{b,Q}|t|}\|p\|_{Q,\vartheta}
\le\|p(e^t\cdot)\|_{Q,\vartheta}
\le e^{L_{b,Q}|t|}\|p\|_{Q,\vartheta}$.
With $u=\kappa/\vartheta$, the exact substitution is
\[
 \|p\|_{Q,\kappa}^2
 =u^{-(2Q+1)}\|p(u^{-1}\cdot)\|_{Q,\vartheta}^2.
\tag{ATG22}
\]
Retaining that scalar first and then bounding it gives
\[
 e^{-K_{b,Q}|\log(\kappa/\vartheta)|}\|p\|_{Q,\vartheta}^2
 \le\|p\|_{Q,\kappa}^2
 \le e^{K_{b,Q}|\log(\kappa/\vartheta)|}\|p\|_{Q,\vartheta}^2.
\tag{ATG23}
\]
Combining (ATG18)--(ATG20) and (ATG23), at rates
$\vartheta+h$ and $\vartheta-h$, proves (ATG4) with precisely
the constants (ATG3).

\subsection{Domain, explicit error control, and monic minima}

All uses of the preceding inequalities are in their proved domains.
Indeed
$2q'-q=j^2-30j+97>0$ for $j\ge257$, so $q'\ge q/2$;
$Q_\pm\le q+(j-1)/4\le2q$; and
\[
 r\le q+32j\le2q,\qquad
 0\le m+r\le q+48j-48\le2q.
\tag{ATG24}
\]
For the last two upper inequalities, subtract the left sides from
$2q$: the resulting lower bounds are respectively
$j^2-30j+1$ and $j^2-46j+49$, both positive for $j\ge257$.
Also $j/2\le A$ because $\gamma>2$, and $0<h<\vartheta$.
The nonnegativity of $m+r$ uses the original $m=\Delta-v\ge0$;
it is not inferred merely from the displayed numerical domain.

Set $B_q=\log(1+K_q)$ and
$G_q=\log q+\log(\pi/2)$. Then the following fully explicit
bounds apply simultaneously to both signs, both source orders,
both root counts, and all degrees $b\le2q$:
\[
\begin{split}
 |b^-_{n,b,s}|&\le
 \frac{4D^2}{3\epsilon^2}+B_q+|\log C_L|
       +\tfrac32G_q+\frac{17}{\vartheta-1},\\
 |b^+_{n,b,s}|&\le
 \frac{4D^2}{3\epsilon^2}+2B_q+
       \frac1{16\epsilon^2j}+|\log C_U|
       +\tfrac12G_q+\frac{17}{\vartheta-1}.
\end{split}\tag{ATG25}
\]
To verify these, $A^2\le T^2/4$ and $j^2/q\le1$ imply
$qA^2/(T^2-A^2)\le4D^2/(3\epsilon^2)$.
Further,
$S_{\ell_s}\le\ell_s j^2/4\le j^3/16$ and $q\ge j^2$.
Since $b,Q\le2q$,
$K_{b,Q}\le(8+4\sqrt3)q+1\le16q+1$.
Both absolute rate logarithms are at most
$h/(\vartheta-h)$; their products with $K_{b,Q}$ are at most
$(16q+1)/(\vartheta q-1)\le17/(\vartheta-1)$.
Finally $2h/\pi\le\sin h\le h$, so
$|\log\sin h|\le G_q$.
The exact $K_q$ is bounded, since
$K_q\le9\sqrt{2\pi}C_L^{-1}q e^{-2q}
\le9\sqrt{2\pi}C_L^{-1}e^{-2}$ for $q\ge1$.
This proves the uniform $O_{\delta,\gamma}(\log q)$ claim without
absorbing $\beta_s$ into the error.

The two monic minima are defined with their original centres by
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \nu^+_{r,s}=\min_{f\text{ monic},\,\deg f=r}
       \int|\chi_j(c_++iy)f(c_++iy)|^2dm_s(y),\\
 \nu^-_{m+r,s}=\min_{f\text{ monic},\,\deg f=m+r}
       \int|\chi_{j-8}(c_-+iy)f(c_-+iy)|^2dm_s(y).
\end{gathered}
\tag{ATG26}
\]
Each minimum exists uniquely: all moments are finite by (ATG9),
and the Gram form is positive definite because a nonzero polynomial
cannot vanish on a real set of positive measure. Its restriction to
the finite-dimensional lower-coefficient vector is consequently
strictly positive definite and coercive.
Define
$\mathfrak n(Q,b)=\min_{p\text{ monic},\,\deg p=b}
\|p\|_{Q,\vartheta}^2$.
The exact affine-fibre bijections (ATG7) allow minimization of both
sides of (ATG4) over exactly these fibres. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
 \nu^+_{r,s}\le\beta_s e^{b^+_{q,r,s}}
                       \mathfrak n(q+\ell_s,r),\qquad
 \nu^-_{m+r,s}\ge\beta_s e^{b^-_{q',m+r,s}}
                       \mathfrak n(q'+\ell_s,m+r).
}
\tag{ATG27}
\]
Both opposite inequalities also hold with their corresponding
$b^-$ and $b^+$. The positive original mass cancels only upon
forming the matched same-source ratio:
\[
 \frac{\nu^+_{r,s}}{\nu^-_{m+r,s}}
 \le e^{b^+_{q,r,s}-b^-_{q',m+r,s}}
       \frac{\mathfrak n(q+\ell_s,r)}
            {\mathfrak n(q'+\ell_s,m+r)}.
\tag{ATG28}
\]
No relation Gram, coefficient direction, source order, or root
coefficient is changed in this passage. The proof holds on all of
(ATG1); restricting to
$\lfloor j^{3/2}/\log j\rfloor<r\le q+32j$ preserves every
constant and makes the claimed tail uniformity immediate.

\subsection{Original objects and exact meaning of every cutoff}

fixed period and logarithm branch.  In particular retain

\begin{gathered}

 \|P\|_{s,d}^2=\int_{\mathbb R}|P(d+iy)|^2dm_s(y),\qquad

 (\mathcal T_AP)(S')=\sum_{a,b=0}^8a_{ab}P(S'+\beta_{8,a,b}),

\subsection{An affine bijection before the conductor and its attained slack}
Define the original upper minimum by
\[
 \nu^+_{r,s}=\min_{P\text{ monic},\ \deg P=r}
                      \|\chi_jP\|_{s,c}^2,
 \qquad P^+_{r,s}\text{ its unique minimizer}.           \tag{ATA5}
\]
For $P(S)=S^r+\sum_{t<r}p_tS^t$, linearity gives literally
\[
 \frac{\mathcal T_A(\chi_jP)}{\ell_r}
       =u_r+\sum_{t<r}p_t\frac{\ell_t}{\ell_r}u_t,
 \qquad p_t=z_t\frac{\ell_r}{\ell_t}.                  \tag{ATA6}
\]
These coefficient maps are mutually inverse affine maps between
the complete upper monic fibre and the complete original relation
fibre in ATA4.  Consequently
\[
 \eta_{r,s}=|\ell_r|^{-2}
       \min_{P\text{ monic},\ \deg P=r}
                         \|\mathcal T_A(\chi_jP)\|_{s,c'}^2.
                                                               \tag{ATA7}
\]
Let $E_r=\operatorname{span}(u_0,\ldots,u_{r-1})$, with $E_0=0$,
and let $\Pi_r$ be its orthogonal projection in ATA2.  Put
\[
 w_r=\mathcal T_A(\chi_jP^+_{r,s})/\ell_r,
 \qquad \tau_{r,s}=\|w_r\|_{s,c'}^2.
\]
Equation ATA6 gives $w_r-u_r\in E_r$, hence
$(I-\Pi_r)w_r=(I-\Pi_r)u_r$.  For every $e\in E_r$,
\[
 \|w_r+e\|_{s,c'}^2
 =\|(I-\Pi_r)u_r\|_{s,c'}^2+\|\Pi_rw_r+e\|_{s,c'}^2.
\]
Taking its exact minimum and then $e=0$ proves
\[
 \eta_{r,s}=\|(I-\Pi_r)u_r\|_{s,c'}^2,
 \qquad \tau_{r,s}=\eta_{r,s}+\|\Pi_rw_r\|_{s,c'}^2.    \tag{ATA8}
\]
For example if $G_{<r}=(\langle u_a,u_b\rangle)_{a,b<r}$,
$h_a=\langle u_a,w_r\rangle$, then
$\Pi_rw_r=\sum_{a<r}u_a(G_{<r}^{-1}h)_a$.
Thus this projection uses the entire original Gram inverse.
This is the complex Hermitian quadratic minimum underlying the
classical Schur calculation of Boyd and Vandenberghe
\cite[Appendix A.5.5, p.~650]{human:boyd-vandenberghe}; the displayed
coefficient map and square completion give its full specialization.

\subsection{A polynomial translation bound for the actual conductor}
The unchanged NTG3 polynomials and squared norms are
\[
 p_0=1,\quad p_1=y,\quad
 p_{n+1}=yp_n-n(n-1+s/2)p_{n-1},\qquad
 h_{n,s}=(2\pi)^{s/2}n!(s/2)_n.                          \tag{ATA9}
\]
Their generating function is
\[
 F_s(y,z)=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{p_{n,s}(y)}{n!}z^n
       =(1+z^2)^{-s/4}e^{y\arctan z}.                  \tag{ATA10}
\]
Indeed multiplication of the recurrence by $z^n/n!$ and summation
gives $(1+z^2)\partial_zF_s=(y-(s/2)z)F_s$ and $F_s(y,0)=1$;
the right side of ATA10 solves that equation.  Its Taylor coefficients
therefore agree recursively, and the right side is analytic for
$|z|<1$.  The human-authored NIST DLMF generating formula
\cite[eq.~(18.23.7)]{human:dlmf18} gives the same identity under
$p_{n,s}(y)=n!P_n^{(s/4)}(y/2;\pi/2)$.  The previously verified
DLMF dictionary also supplies exactly ATA9's norm, including
$dy=2\,dx$ and $(2\pi)^{s/2}$; see NTG3 and LRP3.

For a complex shift $z_0$ expand
$e^{z_0\arctan z}=\sum_{a\ge0}e_a(z_0)z^a$.
Coefficient comparison gives
\[
 p_{n,s}(y+z_0)=\sum_{a=0}^n\frac{n!}{a!}
                       e_{n-a}(z_0)p_{a,s}(y).         \tag{ATA11}
\]
Fix an integer $d\ge1$ and $0\le a\le d$.  On $|z|=d/(d+1)$,
the power series of $\arctan$ gives
$|\arctan z|\le\operatorname{artanh}(d/(d+1))
=\tfrac12\log(2d+1)$.  Cauchy's coefficient estimate and
$(1+1/d)^d\le e$ give
\[
 |e_a(z_0)|\le e(2d+1)^{|z_0|/2}.                      \tag{ATA12}
\]
In the original orthonormal coordinates the remaining entry factor is
\[
 \frac{n!}{a!}\sqrt{\frac{h_{a,s}}{h_{n,s}}}
  =\sqrt{\prod_{b=a+1}^n\frac{b}{b-1+s/2}}
  \le\sqrt{2d+1}\qquad(0\le a\le n\le d, s\ge1).     \tag{ATA13}
\]
For the inequality, $s\ge1$ bounds the product by
$\prod_{b=a+1}^nb/(b-1/2)$, and each omitted factor $b/(b-1/2)$
exceeds one.  Its enlargement from $b=1$ to $n$ equals
$4^n/\binom{2n}{n}\le2n+1$: the middle binomial coefficient
is the maximum among the $2n+1$ coefficients with sum $4^n$.
At $n=0$ the product equals one.
The translation matrix has at most $(d+1)^2$ entries.  Its operator
norm is bounded by its Frobenius norm, so ATA12--13 prove, for every
polynomial of degree at most $d$,
\[
 \|p(\,\cdot+z_0)\|_s
 \le e(d+1)(2d+1)^{(|z_0|+1)/2}\|p\|_s.                \tag{ATA14}
\]

The centres in ATA1 give the exact shift
\[
 z_{ab}=-i(\beta_{8,a,b}-4)
       =(2b-8)\gamma-i(2a-8)\delta,
 \quad c'+iy+\beta_{8,a,b}=c+i(y+z_{ab}).
\]
Thus the triangle inequality in the unchanged measure, applied to
each literal conductor summand, gives
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \mathfrak C_A(d)=e(d+1)\sqrt{2d+1}
                \sum_{a,b=0}^8|a_{ab}|(2d+1)^{|z_{ab}|/2},\\
 \|\mathcal T_AP\|_{s,c'}\le\mathfrak C_A(d)\|P\|_{s,c}
       \quad(\deg P\le d),\\
 \eta_{r,s}\le\tau_{r,s}
       \le\frac{\mathfrak C_A(q+r)^2}{|\ell_r|^2}\nu^+_{r,s}.
\end{gathered}\tag{ATA15}
\]
For the fixed actual conductor the finite sum has fixed coefficients
and exponents.  It has a nonzero term since $\mu_v\ne0$; hence
$|\log\mathfrak C_A(d)|=O_{\mathrm{actual}}(\log(d+1))$.
Likewise the exact finite product in $\binom{q+r}{v}$ gives
$|\log|\ell_r||=O_{\mathrm{actual}}(\log q)$ uniformly on
$0\le r\le q+32j$.  No inverse-conductor bound is used.

\subsection{Receiving the complete finite analytic endpoint}
Use the actual root-comparison domain
\[
 j\ge257,\qquad j\sqrt{\delta^2+\gamma^2}\le2^{-33}q,
 \qquad R_j<r\le q+32j,
 \quad R_j=\lfloor j^{3/2}/\log j\rfloor.                \tag{ATA16}
\]
The additive GPA reconstruction ATG3--28 proves the complete monic comparisons
with $Q_+=q+(s-1)/4$ and $Q_-=q'+(s-1)/4$:
\[
 \nu^+_{r,s}\le\beta_se^{b^+_{q,r,s}}\mathfrak n(Q_+,r),
 \quad
 \nu^-_{m+r,s}\ge\beta_se^{b^-_{q',m+r,s}}\mathfrak n(Q_-,m+r),

This proves the claimed logarithmic dependence without replacing the
unbounded-$\alpha$ family by a bounded one.

Set, with the original EIQ/ECL Robin convention,
\begin{equation}\tag{LRS10}
 \psi(t)=(1+t)(1-\log(1+t))
               -\frac t4\lambda(2/t,\pi/t),\quad t>0,
 \qquad \psi(0)=a_0=\log(4/\pi).
\end{equation}
Substitute LRS6--9 in LRS4.  The remaining difference of the main
Gamma terms is
\[
 2M(1-\log(M/n))-(r/2)\lambda(2n/r,\pi n/r)
      -\tfrac12\log(M/n)-\log(2\pi).
\]
Its first two terms equal $2n\psi(r/n)$, and the last two are bounded
for $r\le2n$.  The scalar moment estimates at $r=0,1$ give the same
bound: Stirling makes their centre
$2(n+r)a_0-\tfrac12\log(n+r)+O(1+(\ell_s+1)^2/(n+r))$,
and LRS14 below treats $\psi(r/n)$.  Thus, including both small ranks,
\begin{equation}\tag{LRS11}
 \tau_{b,s,r}=2n\psi(r/n)
 +O_{\rm actual}\!\left(1+\log^+\frac{n}{r+1}
                         +\frac{(\ell_s+1)^2}{n+r}\right),
 \qquad 0\le r\le2n.
\end{equation}
The bound is summable with cost $O_{\rm actual}(n)$: indeed
$\sum_{r=0}^{2n}\log^+(n/(r+1))\le n$ by comparison with
$\int_0^n\log(n/x)\,dx=n$, and
$\sum_{r=0}^{2n}(\ell_s+1)^2/(n+r)\le3(\ell_s+1)^2=O(n)$.
The constants are uniform for $b=a,a-8$ and $s=1,a$ on the actual
family.  In particular no $\tfrac12\log n$ has been charged once
per row.

\subsection{The exact primitive and both endpoint expansions}

Let $k(t)$ be the unique EIQ modulus at $(\alpha,\beta)=(2/t,\pi/t)$.
Write $r_k=\sqrt{1-k^2}$ only in the formulas involving this modulus.
EIQ5 gives $v_*=2(1+t)/E(k)$ and $u=r_kv_*$.  Applying ECL6 in
LRS10 and cancelling its explicitly displayed terms gives the exact
identity
\begin{equation}\tag{LRS12}
 \psi(t)=\log\frac{1+r_k}{E(k)}+t\log\frac{k}{E(k)},
 \qquad
 \psi'(t)=-\log(1+t)+\tfrac12\log c_{\rm cap}
             =\log\frac{k}{E(k)}.
\end{equation}
The derivative follows directly from LRS8, so no derivative of an
unstated minimizing selection is involved.  Since $E(k)>1>k$ for
$0<k<1$, $\psi$ decreases on $(0,\infty)$.

The convergent elliptic series obtained by expanding the integrands
in EIQ2 give
\[
 \frac{E(k)}{\sqrt{1-k^2}K(k)}
     =1+\frac{k^4}{16}+O(k^6).
\]
The endpoint equation makes its left side $1+t$.  Therefore
$k=2t^{1/4}(1+O(\sqrt t))$ as $t\downarrow0$.  LRS12 first gives
$\psi(0)=\log(4/\pi)$ and then
\begin{equation}\tag{LRS13}
 \psi'(t)=\tfrac14\log t+a_0+O(\sqrt t).
\end{equation}
Integrating from zero, where $\int_0^t\log x\,dx=t\log t-t$, proves
\begin{equation}\tag{LRS14}
 \psi(t)=a_0+\frac t4\log t+(a_0-\tfrac14)t+O(t^{3/2}).
\end{equation}

In the original opposite-energy convention $F=-E_{\min}$,
EIQ30--31 and ECL14 give
$F_\alpha=\mathcal L$, $\beta F_\beta=-2(\alpha+1)$ and
$F=-\lambda/2-(\alpha+1)+\alpha\mathcal L/2$.
Thus
\[
 \frac d{dt}\left[\frac{t^2}{2}F(2/t,\pi/t)\right]
   =tF-\frac t2(\alpha F_\alpha+\beta F_\beta)
   =-\frac t2\lambda(2/t,\pi/t).
\]
It follows that the continuous primitive with value zero at zero is
\begin{equation}\tag{LRS15}
 \mathfrak F(t)=\frac{t^2}{4}F(2/t,\pi/t)
 +\frac{(1+t)^2}{2}\left(\frac32-\log(1+t)\right)-\frac34,
 \qquad \mathfrak F'=\psi.
\end{equation}
The asserted value at zero follows either from ECL14 and the bounded
support, which give $F(2/t,\pi/t)=O(1/t+|\log t|)$, or directly by
integrating the continuous LRS14 and taking the displayed limit.
Put
\[
 a_1=\psi(1),\qquad C_B=4\mathfrak F(1)=9-8\log2+F(2,\pi).
\]
In ECL16 the original outer coefficient is
$C_\partial=4a_0-10-2F(2,\pi)+4\mathcal L(2,\pi)$.
Since $a_1=2(1-\log2)-\lambda(2,\pi)/4$ and
$F(2,\pi)=-\lambda(2,\pi)/2-3+\mathcal L(2,\pi)$, substitution gives
\begin{equation}\tag{LRS16}
 C_\partial=2C_B-8a_1+4a_0.
\end{equation}

\subsection{The displaced covariance and its finite error interval}

For integers $b_0\ge0,w\ge1,b_0+w\le2n$, define
\[
 \mathscr I(n;b_0,w)=4n^2\left[
 \mathfrak F((b_0+w)/n)-\mathfrak F(b_0/n)\right].
\]
The reference row sum with weights $1,2,\ldots,2,1$ differs from this
quantity by at most
\begin{equation}\tag{LRS17}
 A(n;b_0,w)=2n[\psi(b_0/n)-\psi((b_0+w)/n)].
\end{equation}
Indeed on each unit interval the integral of the decreasing function
$2n\psi(x/n)$ lies between its two endpoint values; its difference
from their average is at most half their difference.  Sum these
inequalities and multiply by two.  This proof applies also at zero,
where $\psi'$ is unbounded but $\psi$ is continuous and monotone.

For a completely finite interval, let $e_r^\pm$ be the two bounds in
LRS4 (or their scalar-moment counterparts) minus $2n\psi(r/n)$.
For $\vartheta_0=\vartheta_w=1$ and $\vartheta_j=2$ inside, the
actual weighted sum $S$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\tag{LRS18}
 \mathscr I+\sum_{j=0}^w\vartheta_je_{b_0+j}^--A
 \le S\le
 \mathscr I+\sum_{j=0}^w\vartheta_je_{b_0+j}^++A.
\end{equation}
This supplies directed finite errors, while LRS11 and LRS17 show
that their sizes are $O_{\rm actual}(q)$ on both original windows.

For the literal lower-grid covariance the cutoff $D=N-v$ has
$D-Q=m-1,m,m+q-1,m+q$ at $N=q-1,q,2q-1,2q$.
Telescoping LRS3 therefore gives exactly the weights just used, with
$n=Q+\ell_s,b_0=m,w=q$.  Consequently
\begin{equation}\tag{LRS19}
 \mathcal C^-_{a,s}=\mathscr I(Q+\ell_s;m,q)+O_{\rm actual}(q).
\end{equation}
No lower natural window or order $a-8$ has been inserted.

Set $n=Q+\ell_s$ and $H=m+q-n=2\Delta-v-\ell_s$.
Smoothness near one and integration of LRS14 at zero give
\[
\begin{split}
 \mathfrak F(1+H/n)&=C_B/4+(H/n)a_1+O(H^2/n^2),\\
 \mathfrak F(m/n)&=a_0m/n+\frac{m^2}{8n^2}\log(m/n)
                  +(a_0/2-3/16)m^2/n^2+O((m/n)^{5/2}).
\end{split}
\]
Here $m,H=O_{\rm actual}(a)$ and $n\asymp q\asymp a^2$.
Every displayed nonlogarithmic quadratic term is $O(q)$; the
last lower-end error after multiplication by $4n^2$ is
$O(m^{5/2}/\sqrt n)=O(a^{3/2})$.  Therefore
\begin{equation}\tag{LRS20}
 \mathcal C^-_{a,s}=C_Bn^2+4n(Ha_1-ma_0)
                    +\frac{m^2}{2}\log(n/m)+O_{\rm actual}(q).
\end{equation}
Because $m=16a+O_{\rm actual}(1)$ and $n=a^2+O(a)$,
\[
 \frac{m^2}{2}\log(n/m)
 =128a^2\log a+O_{\rm actual}(a^2+a\log a)
 =128q\log a+O_{\rm actual}(q).
\]
Expanding $n=q-\Delta+\ell_s$ in the other terms of LRS20 gives
\[
 C_Bq^2+q\Delta(-2C_B+8a_1-4a_0)
            +q\ell_s(2C_B-4a_1)+O_{\rm actual}(q).
\]
Use LRS16 and $\Delta=16a-48$.  We obtain
\begin{equation}\tag{LRS21}
\begin{split}
 \mathcal C^-_{a,1}
 &=C_Bq^2-16C_\partial aq+128q\log a+O_{\rm actual}(q),\\
 \mathcal C^-_{a,a}
 &=C_Bq^2+(-16C_\partial+C_B/2-a_1)aq
                +128q\log a+O_{\rm actual}(q).
\end{split}
\end{equation}

For the upper grid the original four cutoffs instead give
$n=q+\ell_s,b_0=0,w=q$.  Taylor expansion at
$q/n=1-\ell_s/n$ yields
\begin{equation}\tag{LRS22}
 \mathcal B_a^{(s)}=C_Bq^2+\ell_sq(2C_B-4a_1)
                         +O_{\rm actual}(q).
\end{equation}

\subsection{The original prefix and the correlated native tail}

At an integer cutoff $0\le R<q$ define
\[
 \mathscr P(n;b_0,R)=4n^2[
  \mathfrak F((b_0+R)/n)-\mathfrak F(b_0/n)]
       +2n\psi((b_0+R)/n),
\]
and retain the actual scalar centre
\begin{equation}\tag{LRS23}
 \mathfrak P_{a,s}(R)=\mathscr P(q+\ell_s;0,R)
                    -\mathscr P(Q+\ell_s;m,R).
\end{equation}
For a determinant $f(N)$ the exact prefix operation is
$\operatorname{pre}_Rf=2f(q+R)-f(q)-f(q-1)$.
Writing consecutive differences $\tau_r=f(q+r)-f(q+r-1)$ gives
$\operatorname{pre}_Rf=\tau_0+2\sum_{r=1}^R\tau_r$.
Thus the last $+2n\psi$ in LRS23 is necessary, including at $R=0$.
The scalar-prefix errors have finite intervals from LRS18 with
the final row's weight increased from one to two.  They are
$O_{\rm actual}(q)$ uniformly in every $R<q$.

At every endpoint the complete TAC6/TAC10 factorization, with the
original conductor and inverse-dual maps, gives
\[
 \mathcal B_a^{(s)}=
 \mathcal C^-_{a,s}+\mathcal R e^C
      +\mathcal T_{a,s}+F_{L_a}^{(s)}+\Xi_{a,s}.
\]
The all-cutoff exterior transport bounds LRC8--10 and LT26--28 apply to the same
restrictions and attained quotients; their complete proof gives
$\mathcal R e^C,\Xi_{a,s}=O_{\rm actual}(q)$.
LRP10--13 give $0\le F_{L_a}^{(s)}=O_{\rm actual}(q)$ for the
original nonzero quotient metric.  Subtract LRS21 from LRS22 in
this exact factorization to obtain
\begin{equation}\tag{LRS24}
 \mathcal T_{a,s}=16C_\partial aq-128q\log a+O_{\rm actual}(q),
 \qquad s=1,a.
\end{equation}
The two logarithmic terms have the opposite signs forced by the
same equality.

For specificity, if an endpoint error satisfies $-L_N\le e_N\le U_N$,
then the directed prefix and tail estimates are
\begin{equation}\tag{LRS25}
\begin{split}
 -2L_{q+R}-U_q-U_{q-1}
 &\le\operatorname{pre}_Re\le2U_{q+R}+L_q+L_{q-1},\\
 -L_{2q-1}-L_{2q}-2U_{q+R}
 &\le\operatorname{tail}_Re
       \le U_{2q-1}+U_{2q}+2L_{q+R},
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{tail}_Re=e_{2q-1}+e_{2q}-2e_{q+R}$.
These follow term by term, with no change of signs, and are uniform
$O_{\rm actual}(q)$ by the all-cutoff bounds.  The low-quotient
prefix is between zero and its full return: TAC9--10 proves the
quotient covariance increases with $N$, so each consecutive
log-determinant difference is nonnegative.  Its prefix and tail
partition its nonnegative full weighted sum.  Apply the exact
endpoint factorization to $\operatorname{pre}_R$, use this low bound,
LRS25 and the two scalar-prefix comparisons.  This proves
\begin{equation}\tag{LRS26}
 \mathcal T_{a,s}^{\le R}=\mathfrak P_{a,s}(R)+O_{\rm actual}(q),
 \qquad 0\le R<q.
\end{equation}
Subtract it from LRS24, preserving the same actual determinants, to get
\begin{equation}\tag{LRS27}
 \mathscr T_{a,s,R}=16C_\partial aq-128q\log a
                     -\mathfrak P_{a,s}(R)+O_{\rm actual}(q).
\end{equation}

We give the growing-prefix leading term with an error calculation.
Take $R=\lfloor a^{3/2}/\log a\rfloor$ and $n=Q+\ell_s$; then
$m/R\to0$, $R/n\to0$ and $n_+=n+\Delta$.
From LRS13--14, uniformly for $0<t\le(R+m)/n$,
\[
 \psi(t)-t\psi'(t)=a_0-t/4+O(t^{3/2}).
\]
Differentiate the integral expression for $\mathscr P(n;0,R)$ in
$n$ and integrate from $n$ to $n+\Delta$.  This bounds its change by
$O(\Delta R+\Delta R^2/n+\Delta)$.
For the displacement at fixed $n$, exact integration gives
\[
\begin{split}
 \mathscr P(n;0,R)-\mathscr P(n;m,R)
 &=-4\int_0^R\int_x^{x+m}\psi'(u/n)\,du\,dx\\
 &\quad-2\int_R^{R+m}\psi'(u/n)\,du.
\end{split}
\]
Insert LRS13.  Its double logarithmic integral is
$mR\log(n/R)+O(mR)$: for $0\le z\le m\le R$,
\[
 \int_0^R\log((x+z)/R)\,dx
 =(R+z)\log(1+z/R)-z\log(z/R)-R=O(R).
\]
The constant term costs $O(mR)$, the square-root remainder costs
$O(mR\sqrt{(R+m)/n})$, and the final single integral costs
$O(m(1+\log(n/R)))$.  All these errors and the preceding change
in $n$ are $o(a^{5/2})$.  Since
\[
 mR\log(n/R)
 =\frac{16a^{5/2}}{\log a}
      [\tfrac12\log a+\log\log a+O(1)](1+o(1)),
\]
LRS26 proves
\begin{equation}\tag{LRS28}
 \mathcal T_{a,s}^{\le R}\sim8a^{5/2}.
\end{equation}
For the precision of LRS27 the full LRS23 centre must still be used;
LRS28 alone does not bound its omitted terms by $O(q\log a)$.

The subscript $h$ here denotes the fixed original quartet polynomial

 \mathcal W_q^\sigma

 \Delta\mathcal C+\Delta\mathcal T+\Delta\Xi+\mathcal H_a
     =-\Delta F_L.
\]
Neither $C_Bq^2$ nor $\delta_{a,1}^{\rm ar}=\delta_a^\sigma$ has
been removed from the original action or its exterior benchmark.

\section{The finite envelope from degree two and a quantitative full-row profile}
\label{sec:AER}

Retain exactly ATA1--3 and the actual domain
$j\ge257$, $j\sqrt{\delta^2+\gamma^2}\le2^{-33}q$.
In particular $j=k+4\equiv1\pmod4$, $s\in\{1,j\}$,
$0\le v\le80$, $m=16j-48-v>0$, and the original quartet,
conductor, period and branch are fixed.  The following proof extends
ATA19 to every integer $2\le r\le q+32j$.  Its finite endpoint is
still the identical ATA17 expression: no trial, measure, conductor
coefficient, lower minimum or original flag is changed.

Write $\lambda(\alpha,\beta)$ for the EIQ Euler constant of
$V(x)=\beta\sqrt x-\alpha\log x$.  For its support $[u^2,v_0^2]$
write $w=(u+v_0)/2$ and $c_{\mathrm{cap}}=(v_0^2-u^2)/4$.
The exact Robin identities established by EQ20, EQ46 and EQ49 in
the accompanying analytic norm proof are
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \lambda=2(\alpha+2)-2\alpha\log w-2\log c_{\mathrm{cap}},
 \quad\lambda_\alpha=-2\log w,
 \quad\lambda_\beta=\frac{2(\alpha+2)}\beta,\\
 \lambda(\alpha,t\beta)
 =\lambda(\alpha,\beta)+2(\alpha+2)\log t
 \qquad(t>0).
\end{gathered}\tag{AER1}
\]
The last identity also follows directly by the measure and support
dilation $x\mapsto t^{-2}x$: it multiplies $w$ by $t^{-1}$ and
$c_{\mathrm{cap}}$ by $t^{-2}$ in the displayed first formula.

For the power-exponential minimum
\[
 \mathfrak n(Q,b)=\min_{p\text{ monic},\deg p=b}
       \int_{\mathbb R}|p(y)|^2|y|^{2Q}e^{-\pi|y|/2}dy
\]
write $b=2d+\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}$.
Every integer $b\ge2$ has $d\ge1$, so the exact parity map and
the substitution $y=q\sqrt x$ give
\[
 \mathfrak n(Q,b)=q^{2(Q+b)+1}
       \min_{P\text{ monic},\deg P=d}
       \int_0^\infty|P(x)|^2e^{-dV_{\alpha,\beta}(x)}dx,
 \quad \alpha=\frac{Q+\varepsilon-1/2}{d},\quad
 \beta=\frac{\pi q}{2d}.                               \tag{AER2}
\]
The finite Jensen and clipped-quantile proof EQ38--43 gives both endpoints
\[
 H^\pm(Q,b)=[2(Q+b)+1]\log q-d\lambda(\alpha,\beta)
                                  +R^\pm(Q,b),         \tag{AER3}
\]
where, with exactly the same parameters as in AER2,
\[
\begin{gathered}
 R^- =\log(\pi c_{\mathrm{cap}}),\quad
 R^+ =\log J_*+4M+4\log(1+dW),\quad W=v_0^2-u^2,
 \quad M=\frac{\alpha}{2\pi u^2},\\
 C=2\log(1+v_0^2)+|\lambda|,\quad
 T_* =\max\{1,v_0,[8(\alpha+2)/\beta]^2,4C/\beta\},\quad
 J_*=T_*^2+8/\beta^2.
\end{gathered}\tag{AER4}
\]
The proof of these finite endpoints requires $d\ge1$, with no
large-$d$ or small-$\alpha$ restriction.  This is the exact point
which permits the present extension down to $b=2$.

For $Q\in\{q+\ell_s,q'+\ell_s\}$, $\ell_s=(s-1)/4$, and
$2\le b\le2q$, the parameters of AER2 lie in one fixed wedge
\[
 \alpha\ge a_0>0,\qquad
 0<b_0\le\beta/\alpha\le b_1<\infty.                  \tag{AER5}
\]
The constants are independent of $j,s,b$: use $q/2\le Q\le2q$,
$1\le d\le q$ and $Q-1/2\le Q+\varepsilon-1/2\le Q+1/2$.
On the actual narrower wedge these ratios stay in a fixed
neighbourhood of $\pi/2$, although only AER5 is needed here.
The EIQ endpoint equations and their small-modulus expansion prove
uniformly on AER5
\[
\begin{gathered}
 0<u_0\le u<v_0\le v_1<\infty,\quad
 c_0(1+\alpha)^{-1/2}\le W\le c_1,\\
 |\lambda|\le C_0(1+\alpha),\quad
 |\lambda_\alpha|+|\lambda_\beta|\le C_1,
 \quad M\le C_2\alpha,\quad 0<J_*\le C_3.
\end{gathered}\tag{AER6}
\]
For clarity, the endpoint equation is
$\sqrt{1-\kappa^2}K(\kappa)/E(\kappa)=\alpha/(\alpha+2)$.
As $\alpha\to\infty$, its Taylor expansion gives
$2/\alpha=\kappa^4/16+O(\kappa^6)$,
$u,v_0=2\alpha/\beta+o(1)$, and
$v_0^2-u^2\asymp\alpha^{-1/2}$ uniformly for
$\beta/\alpha$ in the compact interval in AER5.
The remaining bounded-$\alpha$ region is compact, and the smooth
positive endpoints have positive lower bounds there.  These facts
prove the first line of AER6.  The remaining lines follow from AER1,
$\log(1+\alpha)=O(1+\alpha)$, and AER4.  In particular a value
$\alpha\asymp q$ at $b=2$ is allowed.
Consequently
\[
 |R^-(Q,b)|=O(\log q),\qquad
 |R^+(Q,b)|=O(q/b+\log q).                             \tag{AER7}
\]

Define the smooth centre
\[
 \mathcal H(Q,b)=2(Q+b)\log Q
                 -\frac b2\lambda(2Q/b,\pi Q/b).       \tag{AER8}
\]
At fixed scale $q$, put $\alpha_0=2Q/b$, $\beta_0=\pi q/b$.
The exact multiplied parity shifts are
\[
 d(\alpha-\alpha_0)=\varepsilon-\tfrac12
                         +\varepsilon Q/b,\qquad
 d(\beta-\beta_0)=\frac{\varepsilon\pi q}{2b}.           \tag{AER9}
\]
The straight parameter segment between these pairs stays in a fixed
wedge of the form AER5.  AER6, AER9 and $d=b/2-\varepsilon/2$ give
\[
 d\lambda(\alpha,\beta)
        =\frac b2\lambda(\alpha_0,\beta_0)+O(q/b).
\]
For example the coefficient change $\varepsilon\lambda/2$ is
$O(1+Q/b)$ by AER6, and the derivative terms have that same order.
There is no requirement that the parameter displacement itself
tend to zero.  By the exact dilation identity in AER1,
\[
 2(Q+b)\log q-\frac b2\lambda(2Q/b,\pi q/b)
                       =\mathcal H(Q,b).
\]
Combining this identity with AER3 and AER7 proves on the whole range
$2\le b\le2q$
\[
 H^\pm(Q,b)=\mathcal H(Q,b)+O(q/b+\log q).              \tag{AER10}
\]
The additional $\log q$ in AER3 is retained in this error.

Differentiate the explicit centre AER8 using AER1.  The result is
\[
 \partial_Q\mathcal H=2\log Q+2\log w,\quad
 \partial_b\mathcal H=2\log Q+\log c_{\mathrm{cap}},\quad
 (\partial_Q-\partial_b)\mathcal H=\mathfrak f(b/Q).
                                                               \tag{AER11}
\]
The last equality uses
$w^2/c_{\mathrm{cap}}=(u+v_0)/(v_0-u)$, so the function is exactly
the previously defined elliptic $\mathfrak f$.  These are derivatives
of an exact smooth formula, not derivatives of asymptotic errors.

For $Q_+=q+\ell_s$, $Q_-=q'+\ell_s$, integrate AER11 along the
straight line with constant $Q+b=Q_++r$:
\[
 \mathcal H(Q_+,r)-\mathcal H(Q_-,r+\Delta)
 =\int_{Q_-}^{Q_+}
       \mathfrak f\left(\frac{Q_++r-x}{x}\right)dx.      \tag{AER12}
\]
Let $t_x=(Q_++r-x)/x$ and $t_0=r/q$.  All of these arguments

\subsection{Exact derivative and uniform bounds for the elliptic profile}

For $0<\rho<1$, retain the original modulus
$k=\sqrt{1-\rho^2}$ and define
\[
 D_\rho(\theta)=\cos^2\theta+\rho^2\sin^2\theta,
 \qquad

 \qquad

\]
Put
\[
 A=\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\sin^2\theta}{\sqrt{D_\rho(\theta)}}\,d\theta,
 \qquad
 B=\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\cos^2\theta}{\sqrt{D_\rho(\theta)}}\,d\theta,
 \qquad \mathfrak q(\rho)=\frac{\rho K}{E}.
\]
All these functions are $C^\infty$ on $(0,1)$: on each compact
subinterval, $D_\rho$ has a positive lower bound, and every derivative
of each integrand has a uniform integrable bound. Directly,
\[
 A+B=K,\qquad K-E=(1-\rho^2)A,
 \qquad E-\rho^2K=(1-\rho^2)B,
 \qquad E_\rho=\rho A.
\]
The function $\sin\theta\cos\theta/\sqrt{D_\rho(\theta)}$
vanishes at both integration endpoints and has derivative
\[
 \frac{\cos^4\theta-\rho^2\sin^4\theta}
 {D_\rho(\theta)^{3/2}}.
\]
Its integral is zero. Since
\[
 \rho^2\sin^2\theta-\cos^2\theta D_\rho(\theta)
 =\rho^2\sin^4\theta-\cos^4\theta,
\]
this proves
\[
 \int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\sin^2\theta}{D_\rho(\theta)^{3/2}}\,d\theta
 =\frac B{\rho^2},\qquad K_\rho=-\frac B\rho.
\]

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak q'(\rho)=\frac{K-B}{E}-\frac{\rho^2KA}{E^2}
 =\frac{(1-\rho^2)AB}{E^2}
 =\frac{(K-E)(E-\rho^2K)}{(1-\rho^2)E^2}>0.}
\]
In particular this derivative never vanishes at an interior parameter.

and $\mathfrak q(\rho)\to1$. To check the other endpoint without an elliptic
asymptotic formula, Cauchy--Schwarz and $u=\tan\theta$ give
\[
 K^2\le\frac\pi2\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{d\theta}{D_\rho(\theta)}
 =\frac\pi2\int_0^\infty\frac{du}{1+\rho^2u^2}
 =\frac{\pi^2}{4\rho}.
\]

Thus $0<\mathfrak q(\rho)\le(\pi/2)\sqrt\rho\to0$ as $\rho\downarrow0$.
Strict monotonicity and these two limits show that $\mathfrak q$ is a
$C^\infty$ bijection from $(0,1)$ to $(0,1)$ with $C^\infty$ inverse.
Therefore the original equation
\[
 \mathfrak q(\rho(t))=\frac1{1+t},\qquad t>0,
\]
defines a unique $C^\infty$ function $\rho(t)$, strictly decreasing,
and $f(t)=\log((1+\rho(t))/(1-\rho(t)))$ is $C^\infty$.
Differentiating the exact equation gives
\[
 \rho'(t)=-\frac{\mathfrak q(\rho(t))^2}{\mathfrak q'(\rho(t))},\qquad
 f'(t)=-\frac{2\mathfrak q(\rho(t))^2}{(1-\rho(t)^2)\mathfrak q'(\rho(t))}.
\]
Using $t=(1-\mathfrak q)/\mathfrak q$, this becomes
\[
 \boxed{
 t f'(t)=-\frac{2\rho K(E-\rho K)}{(K-E)(E-\rho^2K)}<0.}
\]
Every $K,E,\rho$ in this expression has its original value at $t$.

Here is a uniform bound requiring no numerical elliptic evaluation.
Set $e_\rho=E/K$. The already proved inequality $\mathfrak q<1$ gives $e_\rho>\rho$.
For any positive $x,y$, $(x-y)(x^{-1/2}-y^{-1/2})\le0$.
Apply this with $x=D_\rho(\theta)$ and $y=D_\rho(\phi)$ and
integrate over $[0,\pi/2]^2$. Expanding the four terms yields
\[
 2\frac\pi2 E-2K\int_0^{\pi/2}D_\rho(\theta)\,d\theta\le0.
\]
Since the last integral is $\pi(1+\rho^2)/4$, this proves
\[
 \rho<e_\rho\le\frac{1+\rho^2}{2}<1.
\]
The polynomial $p(x)=(1-x)(x-\rho^2)$ has derivative
$1+\rho^2-2x\ge0$ on $[\rho,(1+\rho^2)/2]$.
It follows that
\[
 (1-e_\rho)(e_\rho-\rho^2)\ge p(\rho)=\rho(1-\rho)^2,
 \qquad 2\rho(e_\rho-\rho)\le\rho(1-\rho)^2.
\]
Substitution in the exact derivative proves
\[
 \boxed{0<-t f'(t)
 =\frac{2\rho(e_\rho-\rho)}{(1-e_\rho)(e_\rho-\rho^2)}\le1\qquad(t>0).}
\]
In particular, for any $0<t_1<t_2$, the fundamental theorem of calculus
gives the finite-difference estimate
\[
 0<f(t_1)-f(t_2)\le\log(t_2/t_1).
\]

For completeness, all constants in the small-$t$ assertion can also
be bounded directly from these integrals. Since $D_\rho\le1$,
$A,B\ge\pi/4$ and $E\le\pi/2$, whence $AB/E^2\ge1/4$.
On the other hand, $AB\le(A+B)^2/4=K^2/4$ and $E>\rho K$,
whence $AB/E^2\le1/(4\rho^2)$. Thus
\[
 \frac{1-\rho^2}{4}\le \mathfrak q'(\rho)
 \le\frac{1-\rho^2}{4\rho^2}.
\]
Write $d_\rho=1-\rho$ and $b_\rho=1-\mathfrak q(\rho)$.
Integration from $\rho$ to $1$ gives the exact bounds
\[
 \frac{d_\rho^2(3-d_\rho)}{12}\le b_\rho
 \le\frac{d_\rho^2}{4\rho}.
\]
Also $K\ge E$, so $\mathfrak q\ge\rho$ and
\[
 \frac{d_\rho^2(3-d_\rho)}{12}\le t=\frac{b_\rho}{\mathfrak q}
 \le\frac{d_\rho^2}{4\rho^2}.
\]
If $\rho\le1/2$, the lower bound for $b_\rho$ at $\rho=1/2$ and
monotonicity imply $t\ge b_\rho\ge5/96>1/20$.
Hence $0<t\le1/20$ implies $0<d_\rho<1/2$ and
\[
 d_\rho\le\sqrt{24/5}\,\sqrt t.
\]
Set $W=t(1+\rho)^2/d_\rho^2$. The preceding bounds give
\[
 W\ge\frac{(2-d_\rho)^2(3-d_\rho)}{12}
 =1-\frac43d_\rho+\frac7{12}d_\rho^2-\frac1{12}d_\rho^3
 \ge1-\frac43d_\rho,
 \qquad
 W\le\left(\frac{1+\rho}{2\rho}\right)^2.
\]
Using $\log(1+x)\le x$ for $x\ge0$ and
$\log(1-x)\ge-x/(1-x)$ for $0\le x<1$, with
$d_\rho\le1/2$, yields
\[
 -2d_\rho\le\frac12\log W
 =f(t)+\frac12\log t\le d_\rho.
\]
Thus, with its original constant term retained,
\[
 \boxed{
 \left|f(t)+\frac12\log t\right|
 \le2\sqrt{24/5}\,\sqrt t<\frac{22}{5}\sqrt t
 \qquad(0<t\le1/20).}
\]

To obtain the derivative remainder independently of differentiating
an $O$-term, set $T=-tf'(t)=2\mathfrak q b_\rho/((1-\rho^2)\mathfrak q')$.
The same bounds give
\[
 T\ge\frac{2\rho^3(2+\rho)}{3(1+\rho)^2}
 \ge\frac{\rho^3}{2},
 \qquad T\le\frac{2}{\rho(1+\rho)^2}.
\]
The second lower inequality follows from
$4(2+\rho)-3(1+\rho)^2=(1-\rho)(3\rho+5)\ge0$.
For $1/2\le\rho<1$, the lower estimate implies
$T-1/2\ge-3d_\rho/2$, since $(1-d_\rho)^3\ge1-3d_\rho$.
For the upper estimate,
\[
 \frac{2}{\rho(1+\rho)^2}-\frac12
 =\frac{d_\rho(\rho^2+3\rho+4)}{2\rho(1+\rho)^2}
 \le\frac{32}{9}d_\rho<4d_\rho.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \boxed{
 \left|t f'(t)+\frac12\right|
 \le4\sqrt{24/5}\,\sqrt t<9\sqrt t
 \qquad(0<t\le1/20),}
\]
equivalently $f'(t)=-1/(2t)+O(t^{-1/2})$ with the displayed
explicit constant. These arguments prove both
$f(t)=-\tfrac12\log t+O(\sqrt t)$ and its derivative assertion
directly, without differentiating an unproved remainder estimate.

Finally, when $0<t\le4$, one has $\mathfrak q\ge1/5$; the previously proved
$\mathfrak q\le(\pi/2)\sqrt\rho$ gives
$\rho\ge4/(25\pi^2)$. The lower bound $T\ge\rho^3/2$
therefore supplies, if a strictly positive uniform lower constant is needed,
\[
 \boxed{\frac{32}{15625\pi^6}\le -t f'(t)\le1
 \qquad(0<t\le4).}
\]

\paragraph{A completely explicit comparison allowance.}
Put $n_+=q+\ell_s$, $n_-=Q+\ell_s$ and
$P_1=-\psi'(1)>0$. The derivative calculation gives
$0<-t\mathfrak f'(t)\le1$ for all $t>0$.
Differentiating the exact identity PCB4 therefore gives
\[
 0<\psi''(t)=-\frac{\mathfrak f'(t)}{2(1+t)}
       \le\frac1{2t(1+t)}.
\]
Moreover $\psi'(t)=\log(k/E(k))<0$, since $0<k<1<E(k)$.
For $0<t\le1$, integrate the preceding upper bound from $t$ to $1$;
for $t\ge1$, use that $\psi'$ is increasing and negative. Thus
\[
 |\psi'(t)|\le P_1+\tfrac12\log^+(1/t),\qquad t>0.
\]
For $0\le y\le\Delta$, write
$z_y=(r+y)/(q+\ell_s-y)$. The logarithmic difference satisfies
\[
 \left|\log\frac{z_y}{r/q}\right|
 \le\log(1+y/r)+
      \left|\log\frac q{q+\ell_s-y}\right|
 \le\frac yr+\frac{\ell_s+y}{Q}.
\]
The last inequality uses
$|\log A-\log B|\le|A-B|/\min(A,B)$,
$q+\ell_s-y\ge Q+\ell_s\ge Q>0$, and
$\log(1+x)\le x$ for $x\ge0$.
Because $|t\mathfrak f'(t)|\le1$, integration on logarithmic coordinates
and then on $0\le y\le\Delta$ proves
\[
 \left|\int_0^\Delta\mathfrak f(z_y)\,dy
                   -\Delta\mathfrak f(r/q)\right|
 \le\frac{\Delta^2}{2r}
       +\frac{\Delta\ell_s}{Q}+\frac{\Delta^2}{2Q}.
\]
The fixed displacement has its original sign:
\[
 G(n_-,r+\Delta)-G(n_-,r+m)
       =2\int_{r+m}^{r+\Delta}\psi'(u/n_-)\,du\le0.
\]
Since $m=\Delta-v\ge0$, its absolute value is at most
$2vP_1+v\log^+(n_-/r)$. Consequently, for every integer $r\ge1$,
\[
 \left|G(n_+,r)-G(n_-,r+m)-\Delta\mathfrak f(r/q)\right|
 \le e^{\rm cmp}_{a,s}(r):=
 \frac{\Delta^2}{2r}+\frac{\Delta\ell_s}{Q}
       +\frac{\Delta^2}{2Q}+2vP_1+v\log^+(n_-/r).
\]
This compares the same two Robin centres; it does not replace either
original monic minimum. Its finite weighted sum can therefore be used as
\[
 E^{\rm cmp}_{a,s}(R)
    =\sum_{r=R+1}^{q}\vartheta_r e^{\rm cmp}_{a,s}(r).
\]
The harmonic term is at most
$\Delta^2\sum_{r=R+1}^{q}1/r$; the two constant rational terms have
sum at most $2q(\Delta\ell_s/Q+\Delta^2/(2Q))=O(q)$.
For the logarithmic term, put $N=\lfloor n_-\rfloor=n_-$ and use
\[
 \sum_{r=1}^{q}\log^+(n_-/r)
 \le\sum_{r=1}^{N}\log(N/r)
       =N\log N-\log(N!)\le N,
\]
where $\log(N!)\ge\int_1^N\log x\,dx=N\log N-N+1$.
The fixed $v$ term is therefore $O(q)$.
Because $\Delta^2=O(q)$, $\Delta\ell_s=O(q)$, $Q\asymp q$,
$n_-\asymp q$ and $0\le v\le80$, the finite allowance is
$O_{\rm actual}(q\log q)$ for every $0\le R<q$,
and has the sharper form $O_{\rm actual}(q\{1+\log(q/(R+1))\})$.

\section{Propagation into the earlier receiving equations}
\label{sec:DRU}

This section uses the unchanged domain PCB0, source orders $s=1,a$,
$q=(a+1)^2$, $Q=(a-7)^2$, $m=q-Q-v$, and the original complete
coefficient spaces and attained quotient metrics.  It records the
current receiving equations beside the earlier formulas.  In particular,
historical statements that a term had only an $o(aq)$ estimate are
strengthened by the estimates below; their original proofs remain valid.

\subsection{The conductor and covariance receiver}
The original WCF26 and TAC6 square, including its ordinary transpose,
identifies the transported covariance with the lower-root covariance
and its actual endpoint error.  The determinant phase and the two
complementary exterior maps are proved in LT1--28 and XIM1--23.
Their full-rank estimates imply
\[
 \mathcal R e^C=O_{\mathrm{actual}}(q),\qquad
 \Xi_{a,s}=O_{\mathrm{actual}}(q),\qquad
 F_{L_a}^{(s)}=O_{\mathrm{actual}}(q),
 \tag{DRU1}
\]
where the last estimate is the unchanged LRP10--13 bound.  Each symbol
is the actual term in the exact identity
\[
 \mathcal B_a^{(s)}=
 \mathcal C^-_{a,s}+\mathcal R e^C+
 F_{L_a}^{(s)}+\Xi_{a,s}+\mathcal T_{a,s}.
 \tag{DRU2}
\]
The directed finite intervals remain those in the transport proof;
DRU1 records their asymptotic sizes and does not choose their signs.
Substitution of the independently proved LRS21--22 in DRU2 gives
\[
 \mathcal T_{a,s}
 =16C_\partial aq-128q\log a+O_{\mathrm{actual}}(q),
 \qquad s=1,a.
 \tag{DRU3}
\]
Thus the lower-root $+128q\log a$ term and the tail's negative term
are related by the original covariance equality.  No diagonal
replacement of an attained norm is used in this substitution.

\subsection{Every original prefix and the correlated tail}
For the FTR11 operations, keep their exact coefficients:
\[
 \operatorname{pre}_Rh=2h(q+R)-h(q)-h(q-1),\qquad
 \operatorname{tail}_Rh=h(2q-1)+h(2q)-2h(q+R).
 \tag{DRU4}
\]
They sum to the same complete four-endpoint return.  Applying these
linear operations to the endpoint covariance identity underlying DRU2,
and using the directed LRS25 intervals,
proves, uniformly for every integer $0\le R<q$,
\[
 \mathcal T_{a,s}^{\le R}=\mathfrak P_{a,s}(R)+O_{\mathrm{actual}}(q),
 \qquad
 \mathscr T_{a,s,R}=16C_\partial aq-128q\log a
                   -\mathfrak P_{a,s}(R)+O_{\mathrm{actual}}(q).
 \tag{DRU5}
\]
Here $\mathfrak P$ is the complete LRS23 expression, including its
last single-row term.  The old growing-prefix statement is thereby
refined: at $R=\lfloor a^{3/2}/\log a\rfloor$ its leading size is
$8a^{5/2}$, while the full $\mathfrak P$ is retained whenever
$O(q)$ precision is used.  A claim that this prefix itself is
negligible at scale $q\log a$ would contradict this evaluated size.
Its occurrence in the complete action is controlled by the exact
partition in DRU4--5.

The FTR9--10 native tail is exactly
\[
 \tau_{\mathrm{total}}
 =\log\frac{\det\Omega_{q-1}\det\Omega_q}{(\det\Omega_R)^2}
 =\mathscr T_{a,s,R}.
 \tag{DRU6}
\]
For every original pair of row cutoffs $0\le p\le r\le q$, the
complete Gram update gives
\[
 \log\det(I+Y^*\Omega_p^{-1}Y)
 =16aq\int_{p/q}^{r/q}\mathfrak f(t)\,dt
       +O_{\mathrm{actual}}(q\log q),\qquad
 Y=[x_{p+1},\ldots,x_r].
 \tag{DRU7}
\]
The error is uniform in both cutoffs by PCB16--17.  Indeed the
determinant equals the sum of the same attained rows, whose full
absolute discrepancy is $O(q\log q)$; the decreasing integral
comparison costs $O(a\log q)$, and replacing $\Delta$ by $16a$
costs $O(q)$.  Thus the old inverse and all cross pairings remain
in the evaluated determinant.  DRU5 gives the finer correlated
total with its $q\log a$ coefficient; DRU7 supplies uniform interval
control with the displayed, larger allowance.

\subsection{The allocations, complements and proper source}
The exact receiving maps in FTR10 and ATA34 continue to read
\[
 S_K+S_R=\mathcal T,\qquad
 4S_K=\mathcal L_e+V_b+V_{\mathrm{rem}}+V_s+V_A.
 \tag{DRU8}
\]
These equalities apply to the same labelled row occurrences as well.
For a set of $d\le q$ occurrences, PCB18 therefore gives a common
finite budget of size
\[
 O_{\mathrm{actual}}\!\left(
 ad\{1+\log(q/d)\}+q\log q\right)
 \tag{DRU9}
\]
for each nonnegative single-copy allocation and four times that
budget for each original four-copy CPQ complement and signed
profile bound.  Empty sets give zero exactly; a possible row-zero
atom is handled by PCB13.  The proof is the unchanged extension of
the actual restriction and quotient frames: Schur factorization
splits the full increasing determinant into nonnegative increments,
so each quotient increment is bounded by the full increment.
This is the exact map transporting the row control into DRU8.
The sum in DRU8 is now evaluated by DRU3 or DRU5.  Its two summands
still retain their separate original subspaces; no individual
asymptotic coefficient follows from their sum.

At the independent proper degree $k$, retain its own source order
$s_0=1,k$, metric, centre and quotient frames, and its independent
finite guards $k\ge257$ and
$k\sqrt{\delta^2+\gamma^2}\le2^{-33}q_k$, together with its original
quartet domain.  They hold eventually for the fixed quartet and are
not inferred from the target degree's guards.  ATA35--39 proves
for the actual displaced-cutoff correction
\[
\begin{gathered}
 -E_Q^-\le\delta V_Q\le E_Q^+,\qquad
 E_Q^-=O_{\mathrm{actual}}(k^2\log k),\\
 E_Q^+=4(8k+24)(16k-48)\mathfrak f(1)
            +O_{\mathrm{actual}}(k\log q_k).
\end{gathered}
 \tag{DRU10}
\]
The two high intervals each have length $2(8k+24)$; the factor
four counts these rows in a single copy.  The proper source's own
standard-window return remains in PRD's original degree-$k$
Schur minimum.  DRU10 controls its displacement through precisely
that map and does not assign a value to the standard-window return.

\subsection{The complete arithmetic action}
Substituting DRU2 and the exact prefix--tail partition into WCF27
recovers the original TAC identity, without selecting any endpoint
extremizers independently:
\[
 J_a=\mathcal B_a^{(s)}+\delta_{a,s}^{\mathrm{ar}}
                          +W_a-P_{a,-}-P_{a,+}.
 \tag{DRU11}
\]
The evaluated original window LRS30 and order-one source LRS22 give
\[
 J_a-\delta_{a,1}^{\mathrm{ar}}
       =C_Bq^2+8q\log a+O_{\mathrm{actual},h}(q).
 \tag{DRU12}
\]
For the other source order the exact identity
$\delta_{a,a}^{\mathrm{ar}}-\delta_{a,1}^{\mathrm{ar}}
=\mathcal B_a^{(1)}-\mathcal B_a^{(a)}$ cancels its source change.
Here $h$ remains the fixed original quartet polynomial of LRS30.
The still unevaluated term is the same signed arithmetic correction
in DRU11--12.  The exterior lower benchmark is a statement about
$J_a$ itself; DRU12 therefore provides a precise receiving equation
for the next signed calculation, with no arithmetic sign inserted.
