# Completion of the retained Montgomery–Weil archimedean remainder

19 September 2026. This completes the specific identity explicitly left open in the Original Six Readers literature-foundations section. It does not prove a new pair-correlation estimate or replace complex zero coordinates by real ordinates.

Use exactly the source parameters

    1<sigma<2, t real, x>=1,
    a=sigma-1/2, U=log x,
    F(v)=exp(-a|v-U|) exp(-it(v-U)),
    C=F(0)=x^(1/2-sigma+it),
    z=1-sigma+it, w=sigma-it=1-z, p=sigma+it.

The source Mellin transform is

    Phi(s)=2a x^(s-1/2) / [a^2-(s-1/2-it)^2].           (A1)

The real-place kernel and exact finite-part convention are

    K(v)=exp(|v|/2)/|exp(v)-exp(-v)|,
    PF(FK)=lim_(Lambda->infinity) {
      integral_R (1-exp(-Lambda|v|)) F(v)K(v) dv
      - C log Lambda }.

Here |v|F(v)K(v) tends to C/2 at zero, so the subtraction is C log Lambda, not 2C log Lambda. Weil's real rational-field constant is A=(2pi)^(-1).

## 1. Compute the finite part directly

On v>0,

    K(v)=sum_(m=0)^infinity exp(-(2m+1/2)v).

For v<=U, F(v)=C exp((a-it)v); for v>=U,
F(v)=C x^(2a) exp(-(a+it)v); and F(-v)=C exp(-(a-it)v).

Integration on the two actual intervals, with the splitting point U retained, gives

    integral_0^infinity [F(v)+F(-v)] exp(-(2m+1/2)v) dv
     = C [1/(2m+w)+1/(2m+z)]
       - 2a x^(-2m-1/2)/[(2m+z)(2m+p)].                (A2)

The term 1/(2m+z) in (A2) is the algebraic result of the finite interval [0,U]; it is not the integral of an exponentially increasing function on the whole half-line. For m=0, Re z<0 and that distinction is necessary.

For finite Lambda>2, the factor (1-exp(-Lambda v)) subtracts the same expression with all 2m denominators shifted by Lambda and with x^(-2m-1/2) multiplied by x^(-Lambda). Consequently the harmonic part is exactly

    C/2 [psi((w+Lambda)/2)-psi(w/2)
         +psi((z+Lambda)/2)-psi(z/2)].                 (A3)

This follows from the convergent digamma partial-fraction difference, derivable by logarithmically differentiating Euler's Gamma product. Its continuation at z/2 is regular on -1/2<Re(z/2)<0. The other denominators likewise have no zeros on the source parameter domain.

The remaining series is absolutely convergent for every x>=1. The shifted remainder tends to zero: at x=1 its absolute sum is O_(sigma,t)(Lambda^(-1)); at x>1 it also has x^(-Lambda). The real-part Stirling asymptotic in (A3) gives log Lambda-log 2 before the subtraction C log Lambda. Thus

    PF(FK) = -C log 2 - C/2 [psi(w/2)+psi(z/2)]
             -2a x^(-1/2) sum_(m=0)^infinity
                   x^(-2m)/[(2m+z)(2m+p)].            (A4)

The exchange of sum and integral at finite Lambda is justified by the integrable absolute envelope: near zero the regularizer removes the 1/v singularity, and at infinity the actual split test has exponential decay. No zero sum or RH statement is needed for (A4).

For direct numerical verification, the same finite part is the ordinary convergent integral

    integral_0^infinity {
       [F(v)+F(-v)]K(v)-C exp(-v)/v } dv.               (A5)

Indeed the subtracted reference has regulated integral C log(1+Lambda), whose difference from C log Lambda tends to zero. This establishes the exact morphism between the finite part and the regularized ordinary integral used in the diagnostic.

## 2. Keep the negative-log prime series and use the exact functional equation

The negative-log prime-power part is

    sum_(n>=1) Lambda(n) n^(-1/2) F(-log n)
     = C sum_(n>=1) Lambda(n)n^(-w)
     = -C zeta'(w)/zeta(w).                            (A6)

The series converges absolutely because Re w=sigma>1.

The exact functional equation of pi^(-s/2)Gamma(s/2)zeta(s), differentiated at z and w=1-z, is

    zeta'(w)/zeta(w)-log pi
       +[psi(w/2)+psi(z/2)]/2
      = -zeta'(z)/zeta(z).                             (A7)

The logarithmic derivatives exist on this domain. In particular the functional equation transports the nonvanishing Euler product at w to z; no trivial zero lies in -1<Re z<0.

Finally (A1) gives the original other pole term

    Phi(0) = -2a x^(-1/2)/(zp).                        (A8)

It cancels precisely the m=0 term generated by (A4).

Combining (A4), (A6), (A7), (A8) proves the requested complete identity

    Phi(0)+C log((2pi)^(-1))-PF(FK)
       -sum_(n>=1) Lambda(n)n^(-1/2)F(-log n)
     = -C zeta'(1-sigma+it)/zeta(1-sigma+it)
       -x^(-1/2) sum_(m>=1)
          (2sigma-1)x^(-2m)
          /[(sigma-1-it-2m)(sigma+it+2m)].             (A9)

This is exactly the logarithmic-derivative term and complete trivial-zero series in the source's Montgomery formula. The finite part, the m=0 cancellation, both complex shifts, and the negative-log prime series are all present.

## 3. Endpoint and full explicit formula

The derivation is valid directly at x=1, not only by an omitted limiting argument. At x=1 the series in (A4) also reduces by partial fractions to

    PF(FK)=-log 2-[psi((sigma-it)/2)+psi((sigma+it)/2)]/2,

agreeing with the unshifted test integral (A5).

The positive-log prime values from the supplied coordinate map, Phi(1), and (A9) now reproduce the entire retained Montgomery–Weil identity. Its real-ordinate use still has the original source's RH domain; the commutator (A9) itself is an unconditional scalar identity on the displayed sigma,t,x domain. Nothing about a missing asymptotic or a conjectural zero location has been inserted into this calculation.
