\section{Independent residue--boundary verification and a sharper finite sign}
\label{sec:RBV-independent}

The scope of this derivation is RBV1--35, including the entire residue
space, the consecutive native chart, and every complete
conductor/native chart.  It does not assign the same witness to a
native block obtained by deleting the conductor component in an
arbitrary other chart. The expanded simple-root tensor calculation
RBV31--35 is verified in the last subsection below.
The original inputs are \texttt{02\_RESIDUE\_BOUNDARY\_VALUE.md},
RTM1--3 and GTM1/GTM7 in \texttt{RTM\_GTM\_original\_providers.tex},
and COB.1--14 in \texttt{COUPLED\_ORIGINAL\_CONE\_BOUNDARY.tex}.
The calculations below independently verify all constants and maps
used in RBV1--35. The original $7/32$ bound remains valid.

\subsection{Taylor estimate, sign, and exact asymptotic}

Retain $a\equiv1\pmod4$, $a\ge17$, $q=(a+1)^2$, $c=a/2$,
$m=16a-48-v$, $0\le v\le80$, $0<\delta<1/2$ and $\gamma>2$.
Then
$m-1-8a=8a-49-v\ge136-129>0$, and $8a<q$.
Thus the stated polynomial has the required degree and is nonzero.
Let $t_0=\pi/(2\gamma)$, $r_0=t_0\delta$.
At the original root $z=(2u-a)\delta+i(2b-a)\gamma$,
\[
 \cosh(t_0z)
 =i\sin(\pi(2b-a)/2)\sinh(r_0(2u-a)).
 \tag{RBR1}
\]
Both integer factors $2b-a,2u-a$ are odd.  Hence the sine has
modulus one and the real number in the last hyperbolic sine is
nonzero.
Moreover $t_0|z|\le a(\pi/2)\sqrt{1+(\delta/\gamma)^2}<2a$;
the numerical inequality follows already from
$\pi<22/7$, $\delta/\gamma<1/4$.
For $n=8a+1$,
\[
\begin{split}
 \sum_{j\ge n}\frac{(2a)^j}{j!}
 &\le\frac{(2a)^n e^{2a}}{n!}
 \le e^{2a}(2ae/n)^n\\
 &\le e^{2a}(e/4)^{8a+1}
 =(e/4)e^{-(8\log4-10)a}
 \le \epsilon_a .
\end{split}                                                    \tag{RBR2}
\]
The even omitted tail of $P_a$ is a subset of this exponential
tail.  The factorial bound used here follows by integrating
$\log x$ on $[1,n]$; no parameter-dependent constant was omitted.

The reflected form is Hermitian because
$\lambda\mapsto a-\overline\lambda$ is an involution of the full
grid.  Since $P_a$ is even with real coefficients about $c$,
$\overline{P_a(a-\overline\lambda)}=P_a(\lambda)$.
Conjugation also permutes the grid, so its sum of squares is real.
Write $s_u=\sinh(r_0(2u-a))$.  The finite sums are
\[
\begin{split}
 S_2:=\sum_{u=0}^a s_u^2
 &=\frac12\left[
 \frac{\sinh(2r_0(a+1))}{\sinh(2r_0)}-(a+1)\right],\\
 S_1:=\sum_{u=0}^a|s_u|
 &=\frac{\cosh(r_0(a+1))-1}{\sinh r_0}.
\end{split}                                                    \tag{RBR3}
\]
For $S_2$ use $2\sinh^2x=\cosh(2x)-1$ and sum the geometric
progression with exponents $2r_0(2u-a)$.
For $S_1$, the positive arguments are
$r_0,3r_0,\ldots,ar_0$ and each occurs twice in the absolute sum.
Summing their exponentials gives
$2\sinh^2((a+1)r_0/2)/\sinh r_0$, which is the second expression.

Here is a sharper finite sign calculation.  For real $s\ne0$ and
$|e|\le t|s|$, $0\le t\le1/2$, one has exactly
\[
 \sup_{|e|\le t|s|}\Re(is+e)^2=-(1-t)^2s^2 .
 \tag{RBR4}
\]
To prove it write $e/s=x+iy$ with $x^2+y^2\le t^2$.
Then
$\Re(is+e)^2/s^2=x^2-(1+y)^2
\le t^2-1-2y-2y^2$.
The latter is decreasing for $y\in[-t,t]$, because its derivative
is $-2-4y\le0$.  Its maximum is its value at $y=-t$,
namely $-(1-t)^2$, attained by $e=-its$.
Under the original guard $\epsilon_a\le r_0/4$ we have
$\epsilon_a/|s_u|\le1/4$.  Applying RBR4 separately to every root
therefore gives the optional strengthened finite endpoint
\[
\begin{split}
 \mathcal W(P_a,P_a)&\le-N_a^\sharp,\\
 N_a^\sharp
 &=(a+1)\sum_{u=0}^a(|s_u|-\epsilon_a)^2\\
 &=(a+1)[S_2-2\epsilon_a S_1+(a+1)\epsilon_a^2]\\
 &\ge\frac9{16}(a+1)S_2
 =\frac{9(a+1)}{32}
 \left[\frac{\sinh(2r_0(a+1))}{\sinh(2r_0)}-(a+1)\right]>0 .
\end{split}                                                    \tag{RBR5}
\]
In particular the simpler requested strengthening
$\Re(is+e)^2\le-s^2/2$ also holds; its resulting coefficient in
front of the bracket is $(a+1)/4$.  All of these imply the
incoming conservative coefficient $7(a+1)/32$.

The comparison trace is exactly $-(a+1)S_2$, and the absolute
Taylor error is at most
$2\epsilon_a(a+1)S_1+q\epsilon_a^2$.
For fixed $r_0>0$, RBR3 shows its ratio to $(a+1)S_2$ tends to zero;
the claimed looser bound
$O_{\delta,\gamma}(a e^{-(8\log4-10+r_0)a})$ is valid.
Expanding the numerator hyperbolic sine in $S_2$ gives precisely
\[
 \mathcal W(P_a,P_a)
 =-\frac{(a+1)e^{2r_0(a+1)}}{4\sinh(2r_0)}(1+o(1)).
 \tag{RBR6}
\]

\subsection{Original full source and its coefficient-form bound}

The constants in RBV12 match RTM2 and GTM1 at the original
multiplicity one: $B=50$, $p=7/2$, $M=B/2=25$.
Here is a direct derivation from those original bounds.
In the convolution of the three-factor lower bound with $a-3$
further copies, integrate each further source variable on $[-1,1]$.
Their sum $z$ has $|z|\le a-3$, and
\[
 e^{-\alpha|y-z|}\ge e^{-\alpha|y|}e^{-\alpha(a-3)},\qquad
 1+|y-z|\le(a-2)(1+|y|).
\]
This gives
\[
 w_h^{*a}(y)\ge
 c_h\vartheta_h^{a-3}e^{-\alpha(a-3)}(a-2)^{-50}
 e^{-\alpha|y|}(1+|y|)^{-50}.
\]
Divide by the original upper Gamma density, and use
$(1+|y|)^{50}\le2^{25}(1+y^2)^{25}$.
Discarding only the remaining factor $(1+|y|)^{1/2}\ge1$
proves exactly the lower measure in RBV12 with its stated $b_a$.

For the upper measure let $f(y)=e^{\alpha y}w_h(y)$.
In its $a$-fold convolution some coordinate has
$|y_j|\ge|y|/a$.  On that part of the integration domain,
$f(y_j)\le C_h^{\rm tilt}a^p(1+|y|)^{-p}$.
Integrating the other coordinates over the whole real line,
then summing over the $a$ possible choices, gives
$f^{*a}(y)\le C_h^{\rm tilt}a^{p+1}\mathfrak M^{a-1}
(1+|y|)^{-p}$.
For $y\ge0$, undo the tilt.  Evenness extends the resulting
bound with $e^{-\alpha|y|}$ to the other half-line.
Divide by the original lower Gamma density and use
$p\ge1/2$.  This proves the stated $A_a$ without changing the
full source mass.

The original Gamma recurrence GTM7, with its
Meixner--Pollaczek provenance \cite{human:dlmf18}, gives in the
orthonormal frame a raising entry
$\sqrt{(n+1)(n+1/2)}$ and a lowering entry
$\sqrt{n(n-1/2)}$.
On coefficient degree at most $D$ each shift has operator norm
at most $D+1$.  Thus
$\|yF\|_\sigma^2\le4(D+1)^2\|F\|_\sigma^2$.
For $F\ne0$ integrate the tangent inequality for the convex
function $x\mapsto(1+x)^{-25}$ against
$|F|^2d\sigma/\|F\|_\sigma^2$.  Its second derivative is
$25\cdot26(1+x)^{-27}>0$, so
\[
 \int(1+y^2)^{-25}|F|^2d\sigma
 \ge[1+4(D+1)^2]^{-25}\|F\|_\sigma^2 .
 \tag{RBR7}
\]
This scalar integration does not rescale either original form.
At $D=2q$ it gives exactly $L_a^-$; the density upper bound gives
$L_a^+=A_a$.  Each logarithmic source constant is fixed, the
exponential powers have size $O_h(a)$ and the polynomial powers
have size $O(\log q)$.  Hence
$\log(L_a^+/L_a^-)=O_h(a+\log q)$ as claimed.

\subsection{Complete relation-band concentration}

Retain $Y=2^{-16}q$, $R=a\sqrt{\delta^2+\gamma^2}\le Y/4$.
The original root factors satisfy
$|\chi(c+iy)|\le(Y+R)^q$ on $|y|\le Y$ and
$|\chi(c+iy)|\ge(q-R)^q$ on $[q,2q]$.
The shifted Legendre functions
$\sqrt{(2d+1)/q}\,L_d(2y/q-3)$, $0\le d\le q$,
are an orthonormal basis of the entire degree-$q$ polynomial
space on $[q,2q]$.  Their classical Rodrigues formula and norm
have the stated NIST DLMF provenance \cite{human:dlmf18}.
Leibniz expansion of Rodrigues' derivative gives
\[
 L_d(z)=2^{-d}\sum_{j=0}^d\binom dj^2
                              (z-1)^{d-j}(z+1)^j .
 \tag{RBR8}
\]
Since $|2y/q-3|<4$ centrally, each term has modulus at most
$5^d$, and $\sum_j\binom dj^2=\binom{2d}d\le4^d$.
This proves $|L_d|\le10^d$.
Expansion of any complex polynomial $P(c+iy)$ of degree at most
$q$ in that orthonormal basis, followed by Cauchy--Schwarz,
therefore gives
\[
 |P(c+iy)|^2\le q^{-1}(q+1)^2 100^q
                   \int_q^{2q}|P(c+it)|^2\,dt,\quad |y|\le Y .
 \tag{RBR9}
\]
This statement quantifies arbitrary linear combinations, not
individual relation polynomials.

For $F=\chi P$, bound the central numerator using RBR9,
$(Y+R)^{2q}$, $L_a^+$ and $\sigma(\mathbb R)=\sqrt{2\pi}$.
Bound its full denominator below by its Gamma integral on
$[q,2q]$, with $L_a^-$, $(q-R)^{2q}$ and
$c_\Gamma e^{-\pi q}(1+2q)^{-1/2}$.
Cancellation of the common integral of $|P|^2$ gives exactly
RBV17's $\eta_a$. The zero polynomial is handled directly.
The ratio satisfies
\[
 \frac{Y+R}{q-R}
 \le\frac{(5/4)2^{-16}}{1-2^{-18}}<2^{-15}.
 \tag{RBR10}
\]
Taking its $2q$-th power gives
$-30q\log2$, hence precisely
$c_1=30\log2-\pi-\log100>0$.
The other displayed factors have logarithm $O_h(a+\log q)$.

\subsection{Strict positivity of both original boundary forms}

For the original remainder representative $P\in\mathcal P_{q-1}$,
the entire fibre at cutoff $N$ is
$P+\chi\mathcal P_{N-q}$.  The full minimum is
$s_NP=P-\operatorname{Proj}_{\chi\mathcal P_{N-q}}P$.
Thus both projection identities RBV18 and
$G_{q-1}-G_{2q-1}=\Delta_0^*\Delta_0$,
$G_q-G_{2q}=\Delta_1^*\Delta_1$ follow by the orthogonal
decomposition into the nested relation subspaces. This is exactly
the source measure and minimum in COB.3--6.

Pairing the $u$ and $a-u$ root factors contributes a minus sign
per pair. There are $q/2$ pairs, and $q/2$ is even because $a+1$
is even. Hence the complete product on the source line is
\[
 \chi(c+iy)=
 \prod_{b=0}^a\prod_{0\le u<a/2}
 [(y-(2b-a)\gamma)^2+(2u-a)^2\delta^2]>0 .
 \tag{RBR11}
\]
The strict inequality retains $\delta>0$ and the oddness of $a$.
If $\Delta_0P=0$, its orthogonality to $\chi P$ proves
$\int\chi(c+iy)|P(c+iy)|^2d\mu_a=0$, so $P=0$.
For $\Delta_1P=0$, choose the scalar $b$ so that
$F=P-b\chi$ is orthogonal to $e_0$.
As $\chi$ is a multiple of $e_0$, $F$ is still orthogonal to
$e_1,\ldots,e_q$. Since $\deg F\le q$,
$\chi F$ belongs to their full span including $e_0$.
The same strictly positive integral now proves $F=0$;
$P=b\chi$ with $\deg P<q$ forces $P=b=0$.
This verifies both injectivity claims without an assumed
quantitative lower eigenvalue estimate.

\subsection{Boundary energy and the relative Rayleigh bound}

On the source line the original polynomial obeys
$|P_a(c+iy)|\le e^{t_0|y|}$.
Evenness and the exact convolution transform give
$\|P_a\|^2\le2M_h(2t_0)^a$ and, for
$\theta_1=(\pi/2-2t_0)/2>0$, $\theta_2=2t_0+\theta_1$,
\[
 \|1_{\{|y|>Y\}}P_a\|^2
       \le2e^{-\theta_1Y}M_h(\theta_2)^a.              \tag{RBR12}
\]
For every unit vector $f$ in either full boundary band,
splitting the inner product into the two original integration
regions gives
$|\langle f,P_a\rangle|
\le\sqrt{\min(1,\eta_a)}\|P_a\|
+\|1_{\{|y|>Y\}}P_a\|$.
The norm of the orthogonal projection is the supremum over
that entire unit sphere. Squaring and applying
$(x+y)^2\le2x^2+2y^2$ proves exactly RBV21's $B_a$, with
no additional factor equal to the band dimension.

In any stated coefficient domain with positive boundary Gram
$G$ and reflected matrix $W$, its actual witness $z$ has
$z^*Wz\le-N_a$ and $0<z^*Gz\le B_a$.
For $T=G^{-1/2}WG^{-1/2}$ its Rayleigh vector is
$G^{1/2}z$, so
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(T)\le
 \frac{z^*Wz}{z^*Gz}\le-\frac{N_a}{B_a}<0,\qquad
 \|I-T\|\ge1+\frac{N_a}{B_a}.                         \tag{RBR13}
\]
The sign of this division is essential and is correct in RBV22.
One may use $N_a^\sharp$ from RBR5 instead without changing
any map or denominator.
For fixed actual source,
$\log B_a\le-c_*q+O_h(a+\log q)$ with
$c_*=\min\{c_1,2^{-16}\theta_1\}>0$,
whereas $\log N_a=2r_0a+\log a+O_h(1)$.
Since $a/q\to0$ this proves RBV25.
The boundary energy is negligible relative to RBR6, and
$2r_0=\pi\delta/\gamma$, proving the exact limit RBV23.

\subsection{Every residue and conductor chart map}

Write $\chi(S)=\sum_{j=0}^qc_jS^j$, $c_q=1$, and
$p_n(S)=\sum_{j=n+1}^qc_jS^{j-n-1}$.
For the original Vandermonde
$V_{n,\lambda}=\lambda^n$, residues at its distinct roots show
\[
 [S^{-n-1}]\frac{\mathcal L\nu}{\chi}
       =\sum_\lambda\nu_\lambda\lambda^n=(V\nu)_n .
 \tag{RBR14}
\]
Also $p_n=(\chi/S^{n+1})_+$, so
$p_n/\chi=S^{-n-1}+O(S^{-q-1})$ as a Laurent series at infinity.
Thus the first $q$ Laurent coefficients of $p_n/\chi$ are
exactly the column $e_n$, proving
$F=\mathcal LV^{-1}$, $Fe_n=p_n$, and the full inverse in RBV26.
No adjoint replaces this ordinary coefficient inverse.

The original conductor moment polynomial at row $v+r$ is
$\sum_\beta a_\beta(S'+\beta)^{v+r}$.
Its leading nonzero coefficient is
$\binom{v+r}{v}\mu_v(S')^r$: all higher powers have coefficients
$\mu_0,\ldots,\mu_{v-1}=0$.
Evaluating these polynomials at the original lower roots gives
the consecutive minor determinant in RBV28 exactly, including
its original Vandermonde orientation. This proves its nonvanishing.

For any retained nonsingular row minor $J_I$, put
$H_I=J_I^{-1}E_I^{\mathsf T}$ and
$Q_I=E_{I^c}^{\mathsf T}-J_{I^c}H_I$.
On its $I$ rows the identity
$JH_I+E_{I^c}Q_I=I$ reads $J_IH_I=E_I^{\mathsf T}$;
on its $I^c$ rows it is the definition of $Q_I$.
The first $q-m=q'+v$ Laurent moments of $P_a/\chi$ vanish,
because $\deg P_a<m$. In particular the first $v$ vanish.
Multiplying the identity by $FP_v$ proves RBV27 exactly:
\[
 P_a=C_E\eta_I+L_Iz_I,\qquad
 \eta_I=H_IP_v^{\mathsf T}F^{-1}P_a,\quad
 z_I=Q_IP_v^{\mathsf T}F^{-1}P_a .                   \tag{RBR15}
\]
For the consecutive selector, the first $q'$ high moments also
vanish, so $\eta_{I_0}=0$.
Its native columns are $p_{v+q'},\ldots,p_{q-1}$, with distinct
monic degrees $m-1,\ldots,0$, hence span exactly $\mathcal P_{m-1}$.
Their coefficient inverse is precisely RBV29.

For any other chart, the matrix $[J,E_{I^c}]$ is invertible:
if $J\eta+E_{I^c}z=0$, restriction to $I$ gives
$J_I\eta=0$, hence $\eta=0$ and then $z=0$.
Its domain and codomain both have dimension $q-v$.
Thus $[C_E,L_I]=FP_v[J,E_{I^c}]$ is an injection onto the
same complete high-moment residue space.
Its positive Gram and reflected matrix are exactly the full
congruences in RBV30. The Rayleigh vector is
$(\eta_I,z_I)$ from RBR15, retaining both off-diagonal blocks.
This proves RBR13 on each complete chart.
The native-only vector $z_I$ alone maps to
$P_a-C_E\eta_I$; its trace and boundary energy include the
explicit removed-conductor cross terms. Therefore this review
makes no unproved extension of the bound to that different
native-only restriction.

\subsection{Full-unit tensor weights and the two signed pieces}

For the original quartet polynomial $h$ and full unit $v_h=2\xi/h$,
conjugation and the functional equation give
$h(\overline s)=\overline{h(s)}$,
$h(1-\overline s)=\overline{h(s)}$ and
$v_h(1-\overline s)=\overline{v_h(s)}$.
Differentiating the polynomial identity
$h(1-s)=h(s)$ proves
$h'(1-\overline s)=-\overline{h'(s)}$.
At each stipulated simple root $z_{\epsilon\eta}$ both
$v_h(z_{\epsilon\eta})$ and $h'(z_{\epsilon\eta})$ are nonzero.
Consequently the original weights
$w_{\epsilon\eta}=v_h(z_{\epsilon\eta})/h'(z_{\epsilon\eta})$
obey exactly
\[
 w_{\epsilon,1-\eta}=\overline{w_{\epsilon\eta}},
 \qquad
 w_{1-\epsilon,\eta}=-\overline{w_{\epsilon\eta}} .
 \tag{RBR16}
\]
There is one unchanged unit value in each weight, as in FTR.8--12,
not its modulus or its square.

Let $p(X,Y)=\sum_{\epsilon,\eta}w_{\epsilon\eta}X^\epsilon Y^\eta$,
and $c_{u,b}=[X^uY^b]p^a$. Taking the residue at each root in
each ordered tensor factor gives
$\mathcal B(f,g)=\sum_{u,b}c_{u,b}
f^{\sharp_a}(\lambda_{u,b})g(\lambda_{u,b})$.
Indeed the exponents count the two plus choices, and multiplication
enumerates every ordered tuple exactly once.
Conjugation in RBR16 gives the polynomial identities
$\overline p(X,Y)=Yp(X,Y^{-1})$ and
$\overline p(X,Y)=-Xp(X^{-1},Y)$.
Taking their $a$-th powers and equating coefficients yields
\[
 c_{u,a-b}=\overline{c_{u,b}},\qquad
 c_{a-u,b}=(-1)^a\overline{c_{u,b}}
                         =-\overline{c_{u,b}} .
 \tag{RBR17}
\]
Thus the actual nonzero set $\mathcal D$ and zero set $\mathcal Z$
are closed under both grid symmetries, with every possible
interior cancellation retained.

At a simple root, the cyclic residue weight is $1/\chi'(\lambda)$.
Hence its tensor multiplier has value
$b_{\rm ten}(\lambda_{u,b})=\chi'(\lambda_{u,b})c_{u,b}$.
The Chinese-remainder idempotents give the explicit class
$a_{\rm tr}=\sum_{\mathcal D}c_{u,b}^{-1}e_{u,b}$.
Evaluating at each simple root proves
$b_{\rm ten}a_{\rm tr}=e_{\mathcal D}\chi'$ and
\[
 \mathcal B(f,a_{\rm tr}g)
   =\sum_{\mathcal D}f^{\sharp_a}(\lambda)g(\lambda),
 \qquad
 \mathcal W(f,g)=\mathcal B(f,a_{\rm tr}g)
                   +\sum_{\mathcal Z}f^{\sharp_a}(\lambda)g(\lambda).
 \tag{RBR18}
\]
Evaluation is an algebra isomorphism on this original simple-root
packet, so these formulas prove the actual multiplier map.
The underlying multiplication trace has the cited classical
finite-algebra provenance \cite{human:stacks-trace}; the displayed
root computation gives its full specialization.
In particular the pairing in the first term is the form
$\mathcal B(\,\cdot\,,a_{\rm tr}\,\cdot\,)$.
It is not the diagonal value $\mathcal B(P_a,P_a)$ of the
original skew-Hermitian tensor residue.

For $P_a$, every term in each partial sum in RBR18 is
$P_a(\lambda)^2$. Conjugation stability from RBR17 makes each
partial sum real. RBR4 shows its real part is negative at
every root. Therefore the residual on $\mathcal Z$ is zero
when $\mathcal Z$ is empty and strictly negative otherwise;
the tensor term on $\mathcal D$ is strictly negative.
For its explicit finite magnitude, the edge coefficients are
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
 c_{0,b}=\binom ab w_{00}^{a-b}w_{01}^{b},&
 c_{a,b}=\binom ab w_{10}^{a-b}w_{11}^{b},\\
 c_{u,0}=\binom au w_{00}^{a-u}w_{10}^{u},&
 c_{u,a}=\binom au w_{01}^{a-u}w_{11}^{u}.
\end{array}                                                     \tag{RBR19}
\]
All factors are nonzero. In particular $\mathcal D$ contains
the two distinct full edges $u=0,a$, with $2(a+1)$ points.
At each point $|s_u|=\sinh(r_0a)$. RBR4 bounds its real
contribution by $-(\sinh(r_0a)-\epsilon_a)^2$.
Every other point of $\mathcal D$ contributes nonpositively,
so
\[
 \mathcal B(P_a,a_{\rm tr}P_a)
 \le-2(a+1)(\sinh(r_0a)-\epsilon_a)^2<0 .
 \tag{RBR20}
\]
This proves RBV35 without evaluating the interior zero set.
Its second argument is the specific class
$a_{\rm tr}P_a\bmod\chi$ with the full unit coefficients above;
no membership in a selected native section is inferred.
Transport by the already proved full-unit injection
$\mathcal I$ gives the exact endomorphism
$\mathcal I M_{a_{\rm tr}}\mathcal I^{-1}$ on its named
arithmetic image. Multiplication commutes with the original
sum action before injection, hence also after this transport.
The proper boundary map $\Psi_2$ has its distinct original
annihilator $\chi^{a+1}$, and its recovery composition remains
exactly COB.12. No infinite Weil distribution or native
minimum metric is substituted in these finite calculations.
