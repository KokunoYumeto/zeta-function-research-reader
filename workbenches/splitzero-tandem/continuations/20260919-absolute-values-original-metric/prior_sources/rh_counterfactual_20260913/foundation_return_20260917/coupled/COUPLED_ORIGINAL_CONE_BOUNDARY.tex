\section{The original coefficient cone inside the two proper boundary images}
\label{sec:coupled-original-cone-boundary}

This calculation couples FCI.33--39 and TWB1--45 on the same actual
simple-quartet branch.  The latter calculation is applied at tensor order
$j=k+4$, rather than identifying its order with the original coefficient
order $k$.  All maps below use the full original arithmetic units, the
original period coefficients, and the original polynomial coordinates.
The two source files are frozen, with exact hashes, in the accompanying
source manifest.  We write $i\in\{0,1\}$ for a boundary window; this index
is distinct from the invariant-comparison subscript in FCI.

\subsection{The common arithmetic packet and the exact two injections}

Retain the stipulated simple quartet
\[
 \rho_{\epsilon,\eta}=\tfrac12+\epsilon\delta+\mathrm i\eta\gamma,
 \quad 0<\delta<\tfrac12,\quad\gamma>0,\qquad
 h(s)=\prod_{\epsilon,\eta=\pm1}(s-\rho_{\epsilon,\eta}),
 \quad v_h=g/h,
 \tag{COB.1}
\]
with $g=2\xi$ and complete original multiplicity $m=1$.
Let $k=4\ell+1\ge9$, $j=k+4$, and
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 c=j/2,\quad q=(j+1)^2,\quad
 \lambda_{ab}=c+(2a-j)\delta+\mathrm i(2b-j)\gamma,\\
 \chi(S)=\prod_{a,b=0}^{j}(S-\lambda_{ab}),\quad
 E=\mathbb C[S]/(\chi),\quad A=M_S,\\
 \chi_2(S)=\prod_{a,b=0}^{j}(S-\lambda_{ab})^{j+1},\quad
 q_2=(j+1)q,\quad E_2=\mathbb C[S]/(\chi_2).
 \end{gathered}\tag{COB.2}
\]
All these centers are distinct: equality forces equality of their real
and imaginary offsets, hence of $a,b$.  The original arithmetic measure
and norm are
\[
 d\mu(y)=w_h^{*j}(y)\,dy,\quad
 w_h(y)=\frac{|v_h(1/2+\mathrm iy)|^2}{2\pi},\qquad
 \|P\|_\mu^2=\int|P(c+\mathrm iy)|^2\,d\mu(y).
 \tag{COB.3}
\]
Its mass is exactly $(\int w_h)^j$.  In contrast the native Gamma
coefficient metric retains its affine center $c'_j=j/2-4$ and its
original choice $s_1=1$ or $s_1=j$.

Let $d=m_j=16j-48-v$ in the rank-valid original CPQ domain, and put
$\mathcal H=\mathbb C^{4d}$.  The original period is
$E_A(z)=\sum a_{ab}e^{\beta_{8,a,b}z}$, with
$v=\operatorname{ord}_0E_A$, $\mu_r=E_A^{(r)}(0)$ and $\mu_v\ne0$.
For $z\in\mathbb C^d$ put $b=Z_{\exp,j}z$ and
\[
 L_jz=\sum_{a,b=0}^j\chi'(\lambda_{ab})b_{ab}p_{ab}\in E,
 \quad
 p_{ab}(S)=\frac{\chi(S)}{(S-\lambda_{ab})\chi'(\lambda_{ab})},
 \qquad M=I_4\otimes L_j.
 \tag{COB.4}
\]
The polynomial $p_{ab}$ has value one at its indicated center and zero
at the others.  Thus the values of $L_jz$ are the original coefficients
multiplied by their nonzero residue denominators.  They determine the
original functional
$\ell_z(P)=\sum b_{ab}P(\lambda_{ab})$ exactly by residues of
$(L_jz)P/\chi$.  The conductor has first been multiplied back:
$N_z=E_AF_z$ and
$\nu_{z,n}=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\binom nr\mu_r f_{z,n-r}$.
This retains every $\mu_r$ and the two different centers in COB.3;
it does not substitute the quotient coefficients for the numerator.
If $L_jz=0$, every $b_{ab}=0$, hence $N_z=0$, all the original
quotient rows are zero, and the positive native form has value zero
on $z$.  Positive definiteness on the CPQ domain gives $z=0$.
Consequently $L_j$ and $M$ are injective.

Apply TWB4--8 at order $j$.  To give the definitions explicitly,
let $J_n:\mathcal P_n\to E$ be monic remainder, let $H_n$ be the
Gram of COB.3, and set
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 G_n=(J_nH_n^{-1}J_n^*)^{-1},\quad
 R_n=H_n^{-1}J_n^*G_n,\\
 \Delta_0=R_{2q-1}-R_{q-1},\quad
 \Delta_1=R_{2q}-R_q,\quad
 M_0=G_{q-1}-G_{2q-1},\quad M_1=G_q-G_{2q}.
 \end{gathered}\tag{COB.5}
\]
Surjectivity of $J_n$ proves the inverse matrices exist.  The displayed
$R_n$ is the minimum lift since $J_nR_n=1$ and $R_nE$ is orthogonal
to $\ker J_n$.  On the line $S=c+\mathrm iy$, $Q(y)=\chi(c+\mathrm iy)$
is real, even, monic, and strictly positive: $q$ is divisible by four,
and its nonreal roots pair into the positive factors
$((y-(2b-j)\gamma)^2+((2a-j)\delta)^2)$ because $j$ is odd.
If $\Delta_i x=0$, the common minimum lift $P$ is orthogonal to
$\chi P$ within the larger polynomial space.  But
$\langle P,\chi P\rangle=\int Q|P|^2d\mu>0$ for $P\ne0$.
Thus $P=x=0$.  Both $\Delta_i$ are injective and
$M_i=\Delta_i^*\Delta_i\succ0$, where these adjoints use COB.3.

Let $d\nu=Q^2d\mu$, and let $\pi_r$ be its real monic orthogonal
polynomials of squared norms $\nu_r$.  Put
\[
 \mathfrak e_r=\frac{\chi(S)\pi_r((S-c)/\mathrm i)}{\sqrt{\nu_r}},
 \quad c_r(x)=-\langle\mathfrak e_r,R_{q-1}x\rangle_\mu,
 \quad C_0=(c_0,\ldots,c_{q-1})^{\mathsf T},\quad
 C_1=(c_1,\ldots,c_q)^{\mathsf T}.
 \tag{COB.6}
\]
These $\mathfrak e_r$ are orthonormal, and orthogonal projection gives
$\Delta_0=\sum_{r=0}^{q-1}\mathfrak e_rc_r$ and
$\Delta_1=\sum_{r=1}^{q}\mathfrak e_rc_r$.
Write $\boldsymbol\Delta_i=I_4\otimes\Delta_i$ and
$\mathbf M_i=I_4\otimes M_i$.  The two required coefficient maps are
\[
 \boxed{\beta_i=\boldsymbol\Delta_iM:\mathcal H
                 \hookrightarrow(\chi\mathcal P_q)^{\oplus4}
                 \hookrightarrow E_2^{\oplus4}.}
 \tag{COB.7}
\]
The first injection follows from those of its factors.  For the second,
all these polynomials have degree at most $2q$, whereas
$q_2=(j+1)q>2q+1$.  A polynomial of degree less than $q_2$ cannot
be a nonzero multiple of the monic $\chi_2$.  The next multiplication
edge by $S$ also injects by the same inequality.

Here is the literal proper-source arrow.  Retain the original
$h(D)F_h=\Theta\phi_*$, $\mathcal MF_h=v_h$,
$W_*=\mathbb C[D]\phi_*$ and $W_h=h(D)W_*$, and put
$Q_h=\mathscr B/\Theta W_h$.  The map
\[
 \Psi_2:E_2\hookrightarrow Q_h^{\otimes j},\qquad
 [P]\longmapsto[P(D_1+\cdots+D_j)F_h^{\otimes j}]
 \tag{COB.8}
\]
is injective.  Indeed one factor has annihilator exactly $h^2$:
$P(D)F_h=\Theta h(D)T(D)\phi_*$ implies
$Pv_h=g hT=h^2v_hT$, whence $P=h^2T$ by cancellation of
the nonzero entire function $v_h$; the reverse equality follows
from $h(D)F_h=\Theta\phi_*$.  Tensoring these injections over
$\mathbb C$ preserves injectivity.  On every ordered root tuple
the sum of the $j$ nilpotents of order two has last nonzero power
$j$, with coefficient $j!$ on their product.  Thus its annihilator
at that center is $(S-\lambda)^{j+1}$, giving exactly $\chi_2$.
The full Taylor observation of COB.8 is
$[v_h]_{h^2}^{\otimes j}P(s_1+\cdots+s_j)$; that full invertible
Taylor factor is retained.  In particular
\[
 \gamma_i=\Psi_2^{\oplus4}\beta_i:
       \mathcal H\hookrightarrow(Q_h^{\otimes j})^{\oplus4}
 \tag{COB.9}
\]
is injective and lands in the kernel of transition to the original
full-source packet, since every $\beta_i$ value is divisible by
$\chi$.  This is a calculated kernel image, not an identification
of the coefficient kernel with theta boundaries at its proper source.

\subsection{The entire original cone and its mixed image}

Retain without alteration the original CPQ subspaces
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 I=\operatorname{im}(B_{\rm cof}J_0),\quad
 B=\operatorname{im}B_{\rm cof},\quad
 S=\operatorname{im}[B_{\rm cof},F_e],\quad
 J=\ker(I_4\otimes B_j),\\
 K_i^{\rm flag}=I\cap J,\quad W_i^{\rm flag}=I+J,\quad
 W=B+J,\quad K_c=S\cap W,\quad T=S+J=S+W.
 \end{gathered}\tag{COB.10}
\]
The superscript on the two invariant flags only distinguishes their
original subscript from the window index; their spaces are unchanged.
Each $\gamma_i$ gives an isomorphism from the complete coefficient cone
onto the displayed relative cone inside the actual proper packet:
\[
 \begin{array}{ccccc}
 B&\xrightarrow{\ b\mapsto(b,-b)\ }&W\oplus S
                 &\xrightarrow{\ (w,s)\mapsto w+s\ }&\mathcal H\\
 \downarrow\gamma_i&&\downarrow(\gamma_i\oplus\gamma_i)
                 &&\downarrow\gamma_i\\
 \gamma_iB&\xrightarrow{\ b'\mapsto(b',-b')\ }
     &\gamma_iW\oplus\gamma_iS
                 &\xrightarrow{\ (w',s')\mapsto w'+s'\ }&\gamma_i\mathcal H.
 \end{array}\tag{COB.11}
\]
Linearity proves that both squares commute.  Injectivity proves that
intersections, sums and quotients are exactly their named images.
For example $\gamma_iS\cap\gamma_iW=\gamma_iK_c$:
equal image vectors have equal preimages.  Thus the middle and last
cohomologies are precisely $\gamma_iK_c/\gamma_iB$ and
$\gamma_i\mathcal H/\gamma_iT$, with zero degree-minus-one
cohomology.  This proves the maps realizing all the identifications,
including the middle kernel, rather than retaining only their ranks.
The invariant cone similarly maps
$[K_i^{\rm flag}\to J\oplus I\to W_i^{\rm flag}]$ to its exact
image, retaining its zero cohomology by the same intersection and sum.

On the ordinary source, let $\iota_j:E\hookrightarrow Q^{\otimes j}$
be FCI.35, including its full original $[v_h]_h^{\otimes j}$.
The original arithmetic injection remains $\mathfrak A=\iota_j^{\oplus4}M$.
There is an exact recovery on each named image, not an equivariance
assumption:
\[
 \mathfrak A
   =\iota_j^{\oplus4}\boldsymbol\Delta_i^{-1}
          (\Psi_2^{\oplus4}|_{\operatorname{im}\beta_i})^{-1}\gamma_i.
 \tag{COB.12}
\]
The inverse of $\boldsymbol\Delta_i$ is taken only on its labelled
band; in its orthonormal coordinates it is $I_4\otimes C_i^{-1}$.
The other inverse exists by COB.8 and is taken only on the specified
polynomial image.  Composing the maps cancels precisely those
injections and gives the written equality.  This recovers the original
nonzero ordinary arithmetic class from a labelled proper boundary image.
The ordinary source-transition arrow itself kills that boundary image;
it is not the recovery in COB.12.

Give these images the norm of their literal polynomial representatives
in COB.3.  It is well-defined on the displayed bands and their next
edge by COB.7; no norm on the entire quotient $E_2$ is being asserted.
The pullback forms on the unchanged coefficient coordinates are
\[
 \boxed{\Gamma_i=M^*\mathbf M_iM=\beta_i^*\beta_i\succ0.}
 \tag{COB.13}
\]
For the direct-sum cone metric, the middle cohomology norm is exactly
$2\|\cdot\|_{K_c/B,\Gamma_i}^2$, since the cocycle $(-s,s)$
has squared norm $2\|s\|_{\Gamma_i}^2$ and its boundary change is
$(-b,b)$.  The last cohomology has the attained norm on
$\mathcal H/T$ induced by $\Gamma_i$.  Thus the original factor two
is retained on each image cone.  The full joint boundary map has Gram
\[
 (\beta_0,-\beta_1)^*(\beta_0,-\beta_1)=
 \begin{pmatrix}\Gamma_0&-K_M\\-K_M&\Gamma_1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 K_M=M^*\left(I_4\otimes\sum_{r=1}^{q-1}c_r^*c_r\right)M.
 \tag{COB.14}
\]
This follows by orthonormality of the $\mathfrak e_r$ and retains
the full mixed covariance.  In particular
$\Gamma_1-\Gamma_0=M^*(I_4\otimes(c_q^*c_q-c_0^*c_0))M$;
it is not a positive row update of the native coefficient metric.

\subsection{The literal Euler defect with both remainders present}

On $E^{\oplus4}$ put $\mathsf A=I_4\otimes A$ and retain the
original arithmetic quotient Gram $G=I_4\otimes G_{q-1}$.  The
unchanged FCI action compression and remainder are
\[
 A_{\mathcal H}=(M^*GM)^{-1}M^*G\mathsf AM,\qquad
 R=\mathsf AM-MA_{\mathcal H},\qquad M^*GR=0.
 \tag{COB.15}
\]
The matrix $G_{q-1}$ is the actual minimum source Gram at that
cutoff, equal to the remainder Gram there.  None of the identities
below replaces $G$ by a boundary-energy matrix $\mathbf M_i$.

Let $\mathfrak a_r=\sqrt{\nu_r/\nu_{r-1}}$ for $r\ge1$.
Multiplication on the orthonormal relation vectors is
$S\mathfrak e_r=c\mathfrak e_r+\mathrm i\mathfrak a_{r+1}\mathfrak e_{r+1}
+\mathrm i\mathfrak a_r\mathfrak e_{r-1}$.
Indeed multiplication by $y$ is self-adjoint on real polynomials,
so its monic recurrence has off-diagonal coefficients
$1,\nu_r/\nu_{r-1}$; evenness makes its diagonal coefficient zero.
Let $P_i$ project onto the $i$-th band.  Its tangential matrix is
$A_i^{\rm tan}=C_i^{-1}(cI+\mathrm iJ_i)C_i$, where $J_i$ has the
just-displayed real symmetric off-diagonals.  Retain
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 C_i^{\rm act}=A_i^{\rm tan}-A,\qquad
 \mathcal N_0=\mathrm i\mathfrak a_q\mathfrak e_qc_{q-1},\qquad
 \mathcal N_1=\mathrm i\mathfrak a_1\mathfrak e_0c_1
              +\mathrm i\mathfrak a_{q+1}\mathfrak e_{q+1}c_q,\\
 S\Delta_i-\Delta_iA=\Delta_iC_i^{\rm act}+\mathcal N_i,
 \qquad\Delta_i^*\mathcal N_i=0.
 \end{gathered}\tag{COB.16}
\]
The last two formulas follow by projecting the displayed three-term
recurrence onto the band and its orthogonal complement.  All normal
edges occur before degree $q_2$, so COB.16 is also an identity in
the original proper packet, not only in an auxiliary polynomial space.
Write $\mathbf C_i=I_4\otimes C_i^{\rm act}$ and
$\mathbf N_i=I_4\otimes\mathcal N_i$.  Substitution of COB.15 into
COB.16 gives the complete coupled defect
\[
 \boxed{S\beta_i-\beta_iA_{\mathcal H}
       =\boldsymbol\Delta_i B_i+\mathbf N_iM,
       \qquad B_i=R+\mathbf C_iM.}
 \tag{COB.17}
\]
Here $S$ acts on each proper packet copy.  Its full cohomology defect
is obtained by applying the injective $\Psi_2^{\oplus4}$ to the right
side.  The first and second summands are orthogonal, giving the exact
positive matrix identity
\[
 \begin{split}
 &(S\beta_i-\beta_iA_{\mathcal H})^*
                      (S\beta_i-\beta_iA_{\mathcal H})\\
 &\quad=B_i^*\mathbf M_iB_i+M^*\mathbf N_i^*\mathbf N_iM,\\
 B_i^*\mathbf M_iB_i
 &\quad=R^*\mathbf M_iR+R^*\mathbf M_i\mathbf C_iM
       +M^*\mathbf C_i^*\mathbf M_iR
       +M^*\mathbf C_i^*\mathbf M_i\mathbf C_iM.
 \end{split}\tag{COB.18}
\]
The orthogonality $M^*GR=0$ does not remove the two mixed terms:
their metric is $\mathbf M_i$, exactly as written.  The complete
normal energies are
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \mathbf N_0^*\mathbf N_0=I_4\otimes
                      \mathfrak a_q^2c_{q-1}^*c_{q-1},\\
 \mathbf N_1^*\mathbf N_1=I_4\otimes
       (\mathfrak a_1^2c_1^*c_1+\mathfrak a_{q+1}^2c_q^*c_q).
 \end{gathered}\tag{COB.19}
\]
On the original integration line $\overline S+S=j$.  Pairing COB.17
with $\beta_i$ on both sides and using
$\boldsymbol\Delta_i^*\mathbf N_i=0$ proves
\[
 \boxed{A_{\mathcal H}^*\Gamma_i+\Gamma_iA_{\mathcal H}-j\Gamma_i
       =-M^*\mathbf M_iB_i-B_i^*\mathbf M_iM.}
 \tag{COB.20}
\]
Thus both the original coefficient compression remainder and the
boundary tangential correction contribute to the literal weight defect.
The normal leakage still occurs in COB.18--19.  No arithmetic
endomorphism of the CPQ cone has been deduced: even an action on its
coefficient space would have to preserve $B,S,W$ before inducing an
endomorphism of the cone.  COB.17 computes the actual maps without
assuming that preservation.

\subsection{The common native comparison and the exact four-copy spectrum}

Retain every original native cutoff and lower evaluation:
\[
 \mathcal D_N=I_4\otimes D_{j,N-v}^{(s_1)}\succ0,
 \quad q-1\le N\le2q,\qquad
 T_{i,N}=\mathcal D_N^{-1/2}\Gamma_i\mathcal D_N^{-1/2}.
 \tag{COB.21}
\]
These are the exact positive relative operators between the boundary
pullbacks and the common native form.  They give in particular
$\det T_{1,N}/\det T_{0,N}=\det\Gamma_1/\det\Gamma_0$.
There is an exact unitary equivalence, not merely that determinant
identity.  Put
\[
 V_N=\Gamma_0^{1/2}\mathcal D_N^{-1/2}T_{0,N}^{-1/2}.
\]
Then $V_N^*V_N=I$ by the definition of $T_{0,N}$; since the matrix
is square it is unitary.  Multiplication now proves
\[
 T_{0,N}^{-1/2}T_{1,N}T_{0,N}^{-1/2}
   =V_N^*(\Gamma_0^{-1/2}\Gamma_1\Gamma_0^{-1/2})V_N.
 \tag{COB.22}
\]
This is the shared native metric comparison.  It supplies no equality
of either $\Gamma_i$ with $\mathcal D_N$, and the native changes
with $N$ are not identified with the change of boundary window.

For completeness all two-window spectral constants are retained in
their actual arithmetic measure.  Orthogonalize the monic degree-$q$
polynomial $\widehat p_q$ for $d\eta=Qd\mu$, put
$\widehat\nu_q=\int\widehat p_q^2d\eta$, and write
$\widehat p_q=\pi_q+\sum_{r=0}^{q-1}a_r\pi_r$.
Its orthogonality to every degree-less-than-$q$ remainder implies
$c_q=tC_0$, where
\[
 t_r=-a_r\sqrt{\nu_r/\nu_q},\quad
 t_0=-\frac{\widehat\nu_q}{\sqrt{\nu_0\nu_q}},\quad
 \mathfrak s=\frac{\|\widehat p_q\|_\nu^2}{\nu_q},\quad
 \mathfrak p=\frac{\widehat\nu_q^2}{\nu_0\nu_q}>0.
 \tag{COB.23}
\]
To verify $a_0\nu_0=\widehat\nu_q$, integrate
$Q\widehat p_q$ against $d\eta$: $Q-\widehat p_q$ has degree
less than $q$, so the integral is $\widehat\nu_q$; it is also
$\int\widehat p_qd\nu=a_0\nu_0$.
Consequently $C_1=TC_0$ for
$T(x_0,\ldots,x_{q-1})=(x_1,\ldots,x_{q-1},tx)$, and
$T^*T=I-e_0e_0^*+t^*t$.  It is positive definite, has determinant
$|t_0|^2=\mathfrak p$, and equals the identity on the complement
of the span of $e_0,t^*$.  Its two remaining eigenvalues are
\[
 s_\pm=\frac{\mathfrak s\pm\sqrt{\mathfrak s^2-4\mathfrak p}}2,
 \quad0<s_-\le1\le s_+,\qquad
 \operatorname{spec}(\mathbf M_0^{-1/2}\mathbf M_1\mathbf M_0^{-1/2})
   =\{s_-^{[4]},1^{[4q-8]},s_+^{[4]}\}.
 \tag{COB.24}
\]
The trace of $T^*T$ is $q-1+\|t\|^2$ and
$\mathfrak s=1+\|t\|^2$, giving the displayed sum and product.
At $1$ its characteristic quadratic has value
$-\|t_{\rm tail}\|^2\le0$, proving the ordering of $s_\pm$.
Repeated unit eigenvalues in a degenerate case are included exactly
in this multiset.  In particular there are at most four eigenvalues
below one and at most four above one, independent of $q$.

\subsection{Sharp determinant bounds for every fixed subquotient}

The following argument applies simultaneously to restrictions,
observation images with their attained quotient norms, and the full
fixed flags of the CPQ cone.  Put $n=4q$.  For $0\le r\le n$ define
\[
 \begin{split}
 a(r)&=\min(4,r),\qquad b(r)=\max(0,r-(n-4)),\\
 L(r)&=a(r)\log s_-+b(r)\log s_+,\\
 U(r)&=a(r)\log s_++b(r)\log s_-.
 \end{split}\tag{COB.25}
\]
Let $X$ be any fixed subspace of $E^{\oplus4}$ and let $Z\subset X$
be any fixed subspace.  Give $X/Z$ the quotient of the restricted
$\mathbf M_i$ norm.  In the same fixed value coordinates for both
windows, let its Gram be $Q_i^{X/Z}$ and put $r=\dim(X/Z)$.
Then
\[
 \boxed{L(r)\le\log\frac{\det Q_1^{X/Z}}{\det Q_0^{X/Z}}\le U(r).}
 \tag{COB.26}
\]
The empty determinant at $r=0$ is one.  In particular every such
logarithmic variation lies in
\[
 [\,\min(4,r)\log s_-,\ \min(4,r)\log s_+\,]
 \ \subseteq\ [\,4\log s_-,\ 4\log s_+\,].
 \tag{COB.27}
\]
The stronger bounds COB.25 are necessary near full dimension;
for the entire packet $r=n$ they coincide and give the exact value
\[
 \log\frac{\det\mathbf M_1}{\det\mathbf M_0}
       =4\log(s_-s_+)=4\log\mathfrak p.
 \tag{COB.28}
\]

Here is a proof including quotient metrics.  Let the ordered
eigenvalues of the positive relative packet operator in COB.24 be
$\lambda_1\le\cdots\le\lambda_n$.  For a fixed full-rank frame
$X:\mathbb C^d\to E^{\oplus4}$, put
$H_i=X^*\mathbf M_iX$ and
$U=\mathbf M_0^{1/2}XH_0^{-1/2}$.  Then $U^*U=I$ and
\[
 H_0^{-1/2}H_1H_0^{-1/2}
   =U^*(\mathbf M_0^{-1/2}\mathbf M_1\mathbf M_0^{-1/2})U.
 \tag{COB.29}
\]
If $\theta_1\le\cdots\le\theta_d$ are its eigenvalues, the
minimum--maximum characterization gives
$\lambda_a\le\theta_a\le\lambda_{n-d+a}$.
One direct justification of the lower inequality is to intersect
any $a$-dimensional test subspace of $\operatorname{im}U$ with
the ambient span of eigenvectors of indices $a,\ldots,n$;
the dimensions force a nonzero intersection, on which the Rayleigh
quotient is at least $\lambda_a$.  Taking the minimum over those
test subspaces gives the lower bound.  Intersecting with the span
of the first $n-d+a$ ambient eigenvectors, or applying the lower
bound to the negative operator with reversed ordering, gives the
upper bound.  The minimum--maximum formula itself follows by taking
the spans of the first $a$ eigenvectors for the upper estimate and
the same dimension-intersection argument for the lower estimate.

Represent the fixed quotient by an onto matrix
$O:\mathbb C^d\to\mathbb C^r$.  Completing the square, or solving
its normal equations, gives its least lift
$H_i^{-1}O^*(OH_i^{-1}O^*)^{-1}$ and exact quotient Gram
\[
 Q_i^{X/Z}=(OH_i^{-1}O^*)^{-1}.
 \tag{COB.30}
\]
Let $V=H_0^{-1/2}O^*(OH_0^{-1}O^*)^{-1/2}$.  It obeys
$V^*V=I$, and the relative covariance is a compression of
$(H_0^{-1/2}H_1H_0^{-1/2})^{-1}$ by $V$.
Its ordered eigenvalues $\kappa_a$ therefore satisfy
$1/\theta_{d+1-a}\le\kappa_a\le1/\theta_{r+1-a}$.
The eigenvalues of the relative quotient metric are the reciprocal
values in reverse order: this follows directly because the
covariance pair is $(Q_0^{-1},Q_1^{-1})$ and the metric pair is
$(Q_0,Q_1)$.  Hence their ordered values $\rho_a$ satisfy
\[
 \lambda_a\le\theta_a\le\rho_a
       \le\theta_{d-r+a}\le\lambda_{n-r+a},\qquad1\le a\le r.
 \tag{COB.31}
\]
Taking products and then logarithms bounds the determinant by the
product of the $r$ smallest and of the $r$ largest packet eigenvalues.
Those products are exactly $\exp L(r)$ and $\exp U(r)$ from COB.24.
This proves COB.26 including all near-full-dimension cases.  It also
shows directly that on every such subquotient there are at most four
relative eigenvalues below one and at most four above one; the
remaining relative eigenvalues equal one.  This last conclusion
uses the bounds in COB.31, not an assumption that a chosen subspace
is invariant for either metric.

Every fixed coefficient subquotient $X_0/Z_0$ inside $\mathcal H$
is covered by this result with $X=MX_0$, $Z=MZ_0$, because $M$ is
injective and COB.13 pulls the metrics back exactly.  In particular
it applies to the restriction to any original kernel flag, to every
onto observation of such a flag with its complete attained metric,
and to every original flag and its intersections in COB.10.  The
literal middle cochain term is an orthogonal direct sum of two such
restrictions, so its logarithmic determinant change is the sum of
the two restriction changes.  It is not assigned a single four-copy
bound.  The middle cohomology has the single subquotient metric
multiplied by two as proved after COB.13, and that unchanged factor
cancels between windows, so COB.26 does apply to its determinant
change.  For a fixed
observation $O:\mathcal H\to\mathcal Y$ onto its actual image,
the exact image metric is
\[
 Q_{i,O}=(O\Gamma_i^{-1}O^*)^{-1}.
 \tag{COB.32}
\]
For a joint observation $(\Lambda,F)^{\mathsf T}$ its covariance
retains both cross blocks
$\Lambda\Gamma_i^{-1}F^*$ and $F\Gamma_i^{-1}\Lambda^*$.
If its displayed ambient rows are redundant, take coordinates on
