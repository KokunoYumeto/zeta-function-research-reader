\section{The original native tail as an attained relation Gram matrix}
\label{sec:NTG}

\subsection{Original data and the classical tools being used}
Fix the original simple quartet $0<\delta<1/2$, $\gamma>2$,
its nonzero amplitude, its original period, its logarithm branch,
and its complete nonreduced conductor. The period is fixed before
any degree limit. Retain
\[
\begin{gathered}
 k=4l+1\ge9,\quad j=k+4,\quad q=(j+1)^2,\quad q'=(j-7)^2,
 \quad c'=j/2-4,\\
 \beta_{a,u,w}=a/2+(2u-a)\delta+i(2w-a)\gamma,\quad
 \chi_a(S)=\prod_{u,w=0}^{a}(S-\beta_{a,u,w}),\quad
 \chi_-=\chi_{j-8},\\
 E_A(z)=\sum_{u,w=0}^{8}a_{uw}e^{\beta_{8,u,w}z},\quad
 \mu_d=E_A^{(d)}(0)=\sum_{u,w=0}^{8}a_{uw}\beta_{8,u,w}^{\,d},
 \quad v=\operatorname{ord}_0E_A,\quad\mu_v\ne0,\\
 L=q-v,\quad m=L-q'=16j-48-v>0,\qquad s\in\{1,j\}.
\end{gathered}                                                    \tag{NTG1}
\]
The original bounds on $v$ and the original admissible period
domain remain in force; in particular no exceptional finite-period
fibre is replaced by $v=0$. Every $\mu_d$ is a derivative,
not a Taylor coefficient. The accepted original relation
calculation NIB1--9 supplies
\[
\begin{gathered}
 (\mathcal T_AP)(S')=\sum_{u,w=0}^{8}a_{uw}P(S'+\beta_{8,u,w}),
 \quad g_t=\mathcal T_A(\chi_j S^t),\\
 \ell_t=\mu_v\binom{q+t}{v},\quad u_t=g_t/\ell_t,\quad
 \deg u_t=L+t,\quad\operatorname{lc}u_t=1,\quad \chi_-\mid u_t .
\end{gathered}                                                    \tag{NTG2}
\]
It also proves that $u_0,\ldots,u_t$ span the complete original
functional annihilator at degree $L+t$. We keep all original
analytic columns $F_b=N_b/E_A$ of NSO12, $1\le b\le m$, with
the original complex-linear functional convention
$\Lambda_F(S'^d)=F^{(d)}(0)$.

The scalar measure and polynomial recurrence are
\[
\begin{gathered}
 dm_s(y)=\frac{M_s2^{s/2}}{4\pi\Gamma(s/2)}
              |\Gamma(s/4+iy/2)|^2\,dy,\qquad M_s=(2\pi)^{s/2},\\
 p_{0,s}=1,\quad p_{1,s}=y,\quad
 p_{n+1,s}=yp_{n,s}-n(n-1+s/2)p_{n-1,s},\\
 h_{n,s}=M_s n!(s/2)_n,\quad
 \phi_n(S')=p_{n,s}((S'-c')/i)/\sqrt{h_{n,s}},\quad
 \langle P,Q\rangle_s=\int\overline{P(c'+iy)}Q(c'+iy)\,dm_s(y).
\end{gathered}                                                    \tag{NTG3}
\]
Inner products are conjugate-linear in the first argument.
These Gamma-weight orthogonal polynomials belong to the classical
Meixner--Pollaczek theory, not to a new family introduced here.
One retained historical source record is Josef Meixner,
\emph{Orthogonale Polynomsysteme mit einer besonderen Gestalt der
erzeugenden Funktion}, J.\ London Math.\ Soc.\ 9 (1934), 6--13,
\href{https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s1-9.1.6}{doi:10.1112/jlms/s1-9.1.6}.
The precise recurrence, phase and mass used in this calculation
are the displayed original NTG3; its orthonormality is retained
from NIB2 and the cumulative source's full Gamma calculation.
The directly checked modern formula reference is NIST DLMF,
\href{https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.19.E8}{18.19.8},
\href{https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.19.E9}{18.19.9}, and
\href{https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.22.E8}{18.22.8}
\cite[eqs.~(18.19.8)--(18.19.9), (18.22.8)]{human:dlmf18}.
The exact parameter map is
$p_{n,s}(y)=n!P_n^{(s/4)}(y/2;\pi/2)$.
Substitution in the displayed DLMF recurrence gives NTG3.
Its norm under the original density is
\[
 (n!)^2\,\frac{M_s2^{s/2}}{2\pi\Gamma(s/2)}
       \frac{2\pi\Gamma(n+s/2)}{2^{s/2}n!}
       =M_sn!(s/2)_n .
\]
The first factor of $2$ in the density conversion is the
Jacobian $dy=2\,dx$, so this dictionary also checks the
original mass. DLMF is used as a verified formula reference;
the Meixner record preserves a historical attribution trail whose
exact relation to these displayed formulas has not been verified here
from the complete article. It is not used as a checked theorem provider.

The orthogonal projections and attained least-squares minima below
use the classical Gram--Schmidt construction. The retained historical
record includes Erhard Schmidt, \emph{Zur Theorie der linearen und
nichtlinearen Integralgleichungen. I}, Math.\ Ann.\ 63 (1907),
433--476,
\href{https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01449770}{doi:10.1007/BF01449770},
with the earlier J.\ P.\ Gram attribution retained for the historical
audit to trace in the original pages. The block determinant eliminations
are Schur-complement calculations; their retained historical trail includes
Issai Schur,
\emph{\"Uber Potenzreihen, die im Innern des Einheitskreises
beschr\"ankt sind}, J.\ Reine Angew.\ Math.\ 147 (1917), 205--232,
\href{https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.1917.147.205}
{doi:10.1515/crll.1917.147.205}.
Every finite projection, determinant identity and inequality
needed below is proved explicitly. The contribution here is their
application to NTG1--3 with the full original conductor and
functional frames, including the precise native phase.
For the actual minimum and determinant identities we also cite the
directly read derivations in Boyd and Vandenberghe,
Appendix A.5.5, p.~650, and Appendix C.4.1, p.~673
\cite{human:boyd-vandenberghe}; the formulas below provide the
complete complex Hermitian specialization used in this programme.
The publisher records for Schmidt and Schur were checked for
their bibliographic metadata. Their complete historical proofs
were not read as external theorem providers in this calculation;
the finite proofs below are self-contained.

\subsection{Finite evaluation of every coefficient and every pairing}
Write $\chi_j(S)=\sum_{a=0}^{q}c_aS^a$, with $c_q=1$ and all
coefficients obtained from the original root product. Direct
binomial expansion gives, for $0\le h\le q+t$,
\[
 [S'^h]g_t
   =\sum_{\substack{0\le a\le q\\a+t\ge h}}
             c_a\binom{a+t}{h}\mu_{a+t-h}.
                                                               \tag{NTG4}
\]
Thus every coefficient above degree $L+t$ vanishes because
$\mu_d=0$ for $d<v$, and the coefficient at degree $L+t$ is
exactly $\ell_t$. This verifies the derivative factorials in
the original translation map. It does not replace that map
by a derivative of order $v$.

For each integer $d\ge0$ let $J^{(d)}_s$ be the matrix on indices
$0,\ldots,d$ with zero diagonal and off-diagonal entries
$J_s^{(d)}[n,n+1]=J_s^{(d)}[n+1,n]=\sqrt{(n+1)(n+s/2)}$.
Use the convention that the zeroth power is the identity.
The exact moments are
\[
 M_{d,s}:=\int y^d\,dm_s(y)=M_s((J_s^{(d)})^d)_{00}.
                                                               \tag{NTG5}
\]
Indeed multiplication by $y$ in the orthonormal basis of NTG3
has exactly those adjacent entries. A path of $d$ multiplication
steps starting at index zero never exceeds index $d$.
Taking its inner product with the constant unit polynomial
$1/\sqrt{M_s}$ proves NTG5. In particular it preserves the
entire original mass.

Put $\mathfrak H_s[h,b]=\langle S'^h,S'^b\rangle_s$. Expansion
on the original vertical line gives the completely finite formula
\[
 \mathfrak H_s[h,b]
 =\sum_{\alpha=0}^{h}\sum_{\beta=0}^{b}
 \binom h\alpha\binom b\beta
 (c')^{h+b-\alpha-\beta}(-i)^\alpha i^\beta
 M_{\alpha+\beta,s}.
                                                               \tag{NTG6}
\]
Consequently, for arbitrary polynomials $P,Q$ with coefficient
columns $p,q$, their inner product is $p^*\mathfrak H_s q$
at their finite degree cutoff. This formula evaluates every
cross pairing below without quadrature, unlisted constants, or
an asymptotic replacement of the Gamma density.

Set
\[
 B_s[a,b]=\langle\chi_-S'^a,\chi_-S'^b\rangle_s.
                                                               \tag{NTG7}
\]
NTG6 and the full lower root coefficients calculate its entries.
Every finite leading principal block is strictly positive:
a nonzero polynomial has positive squared integral against the
strictly positive Gamma density outside its finite zero set.
For $b\ge1$, let $B_{<b}=(B_s[a,d])_{a,d<b}$ and
$z_b=(B_s[a,b])_{a<b}$. Define
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \pi_b^-(S')=S'^b-\sum_{a<b}S'^a(B_{<b}^{-1}z_b)_a,\qquad
 \nu_{b,s}^-=B_s[b,b]-z_b^*B_{<b}^{-1}z_b,\\
 \pi_0^-=1,\quad\nu_{0,s}^-=B_s[0,0],\qquad
 e_b(S')=\chi_-(S')\pi_b^-(S')/\sqrt{\nu_{b,s}^-}.
\end{gathered}                                                    \tag{NTG8}
\]
Completing the coefficient square proves that $\pi_b^-$ is
the unique monic minimizer of degree $b$ in the lower weighted
norm and $\nu_{b,s}^-$ is its strictly positive squared norm.
Its residual is orthogonal to every preceding monomial. It
follows by successive degrees that the $e_b$ are orthonormal,
and $e_0,\ldots,e_d$ span the complete ideal
$\chi_-\mathbb C[S']_{\le d}$. This is the actual Gram--Schmidt
operation just cited, with its entire finite coefficient map
given in NTG7--8.

For later use the analytic entries are also a finite calculation
from the same nonreduced conductor. Write
$n_{d,b}=N_b^{(d)}(0)=\sum_\lambda Z_{\lambda b}\beta_{j,\lambda}^d$
with the original numerator frame $Z$, and
$f_{d,b}=F_b^{(d)}(0)$. The original vanishing conditions
$n_{d,b}=0$ for $d<v$ make $N_b/E_A$ analytic at zero.
Leibniz's formula gives the recursion
\[
 f_{d,b}=
 \frac{n_{d+v,b}
       -\displaystyle\sum_{h=v+1}^{d+v}
              \binom{d+v}{h}\mu_h f_{d+v-h,b}}
      {\binom{d+v}{v}\mu_v}
       \quad(d\ge0).
                                                               \tag{NTG9}
\]
Each index in the sum is smaller than $d$, so the recursion
starts with $f_{0,b}=n_{v,b}/\mu_v$ and determines every required
derivative in finitely many operations. If $e_a=\sum_h e_{ha}S'^h$,
then $\Lambda_{F_b}(e_a)=\sum_h e_{ha}f_{h,b}$, with no
conjugation of this complex-linear functional value.

\subsection{The actual relation graph and its full attained Gram}
For $Q\ge0$ form the coefficient matrix of $u_0,\ldots,u_Q$
in the original orthonormal lower ideal basis:
\[
\begin{gathered}
 {\cal R}_Q[b,t]=\langle e_b,u_t\rangle_s,\quad
       0\le b\le m+Q,\ 0\le t\le Q,\\
 {\cal R}_Q=\begin{bmatrix}{\cal L}_Q\\ H_Q\end{bmatrix},
 \qquad {\cal L}_Q[b,t]={\cal R}_Q[b,t]\ (b<m),\quad
 H_Q[a,t]={\cal R}_Q[m+a,t].
\end{gathered}                                                    \tag{NTG10}
\]
NTG4,6,8 evaluate every entry. If $a>t$, $e_{m+a}$ is
orthogonal to $u_t$ by degree. For $a=t$, the difference
$u_t-\chi_-\pi_{m+t}^-$ has smaller degree in the same
lower ideal, so
\[
 H_Q[a,t]=0\ (a>t),\qquad
 H_Q[t,t]=\sqrt{\nu_{m+t,s}^-}>0.
                                                               \tag{NTG11}
\]
In particular $H_Q$ is invertible and is upper triangular
in the unchanged degree order. Define
\[
 X_Q={\cal L}_QH_Q^{-1}=[x_0,\ldots,x_Q],\qquad
 \Omega_Q=I_m+X_QX_Q^*,\qquad
 X_{-1}\text{ empty},\quad\Omega_{-1}=I_m.
                                                               \tag{NTG12}
\]
The first $Q+1$ columns of $X_{Q+1}$ equal $X_Q$. To prove
this, partition $H_{Q+1}$ as
$\left[\begin{smallmatrix}H_Q&h\\0&d\end{smallmatrix}\right]$;
its first $Q+1$ inverse columns are
$\left[\begin{smallmatrix}H_Q^{-1}\\0\end{smallmatrix}\right]$.
Multiplication by $[{\cal L}_Q,\ell]$ proves the assertion.
Thus the columns $x_t$ are actual compatible original-data
columns and are not selected anew at each cutoff.

In the coefficient frame $e_0,\ldots,e_{m+Q}$ the complete
functional relation space has the exact graph frame
\[
 {\cal R}_QH_Q^{-1}
     =\begin{bmatrix}X_Q\\I_{Q+1}\end{bmatrix}.
                                                               \tag{NTG13}
\]
Both sides are injective maps from $\mathbb C^{Q+1}$ into
the same original ideal. If $c$ is the original $u_t$
coefficient column and $z$ is its high-degree coordinate
column, the two mutually inverse coordinate maps are
$z=H_Qc$ and $c=H_Q^{-1}z$. The inverse of the graph
map itself is simply high-degree coordinate projection.
Thus this calculation preserves every original relation.

Let $G_Q=(\langle u_a,u_b\rangle_s)_{0\le a,b\le Q}$.
Orthonormality and NTG13 give
\[
\begin{gathered}
 G_Q=H_Q^*(I_{Q+1}+X_Q^*X_Q)H_Q,\\
 \frac{\det G_Q}{\prod_{t=0}^{Q}\nu_{m+t,s}^-}
       =\det(I_{Q+1}+X_Q^*X_Q)=\det\Omega_Q .
\end{gathered}                                                    \tag{NTG14}
\]
For completeness the last determinant equality follows by
eliminating either diagonal identity block in
$\left[\begin{smallmatrix}I_m&X_Q\\-X_Q^*&I_{Q+1}
\end{smallmatrix}\right]$. These two eliminations use block
unitriangular factors of determinant one. This proves the
identity without an assumption on singular values or ranks.
The scalar denominator in NTG14 is exactly
$|\det H_Q|^2$ by NTG11; none of the lower-ideal minima
has been replaced by a trial polynomial.

Successively completing the squares of $G_Q$ identifies its
last Schur complement with the original $\eta_{Q,s}$ of NIB5.
Induction gives $\det G_Q=\prod_{t=0}^Q\eta_{t,s}$, including
$Q=0$. NIB9 and NTG14 therefore imply
\[
 \log\frac{\det\Omega_Q}{\det\Omega_R}
   =\sum_{t=R+1}^{Q}\log\frac{\eta_{t,s}}{\nu_{m+t,s}^-}
   =\sum_{t=R+1}^{Q}\log(1+r_{L+t})
        \qquad(-1\le R<Q).
                                                               \tag{NTG15}
\]
This is a relation-Gram identity on the actual source, with
dimension $m=O(j)$ on its left. No asymptotic bound for this
left side is inferred merely from that dimension.

\subsection{The literal analytic quotient metric and the native phase}
Define the square matrix and its succeeding rows by
\[
 A[a,b]=\Lambda_{F_b}(e_a)\quad(0\le a<m,\ 1\le b\le m),
 \qquad F_{\rm hi}[t,b]=\Lambda_{F_b}(e_{m+t}).
                                                               \tag{NTG16}
\]
All entries are evaluated by NTG8--9. The matrix $A$ is
invertible. Indeed an ideal polynomial of degree at most
$q'+m-1=L-1$ annihilated by all $F_b$ also vanishes at all
lower nodes. The full original functional map at degree
$L-1$ is invertible by NIB4's Vandermonde argument.
Thus that polynomial is zero. In the $m$-dimensional basis
$e_0,\ldots,e_{m-1}$ its functional map is $A^{\mathsf T}$,
which is consequently injective and hence invertible.

Applying each complex-linear functional to NTG13 gives,
with ordinary transpose,
\[
          X_Q^{\mathsf T}A+F_{\rm hi}=0 .
                                                               \tag{NTG17}
\]
Here $F_{\rm hi}$ contains rows $0,\ldots,Q$. All signs,
conjugations and the complete lower ideal are fixed by this
actual polynomial equation.

Let $D_n$ be the original native quotient Gram after observing
rows $0,\ldots,n$:
\[
 D_n=T_n^*(I-V_n(V_n^*V_n)^{-1}V_n^*)T_n,\quad n\ge L-1.
                                                               \tag{NTG18}
\]
The columns of $V_n$ are all original lower evaluations and
those of $T_n$ are all original analytic NSO functionals
evaluated on $\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_n$. Their full ranks follow
from the same original Vandermonde argument. Let $C_n$ be
the matrix of coefficients of $e_0,\ldots,e_{n-q'}$ in
that orthonormal native basis. Then $C_n^*C_n=I$ and
$V_n^{\mathsf T}C_n=0$. Polynomial division by $\chi_-$
and the dimension count show that these are all vectors
in $\ker V_n^{\mathsf T}$. Thus
\[
 I-V_n(V_n^*V_n)^{-1}V_n^*
       =\overline{C_n}C_n^{\mathsf T}.
                                                               \tag{NTG19}
\]
Both sides are the orthogonal projector onto
$\ker V_n^*=\overline{\ker V_n^{\mathsf T}}$; the identity
also follows directly by applying them to their image and
its orthogonal complement. Since $C_n^{\mathsf T}T_n$
is the matrix of actual functional values on the $e_b$,
substitution of NTG17 into NTG18 proves the full matrix equality
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 D_{L+Q}
   =A^*(I_m+\overline{X_Q}X_Q^{\mathsf T})A
   =A^*\overline{\Omega_Q}A,\qquad D_{L-1}=A^*A .
 \quad}                                                        \tag{NTG20}
\]
This is equality in the unchanged analytic coefficient frame,
not only a determinant equality. It proves the exact map to
every original restriction and quotient: in four copies use
$\operatorname{diag}(A,4)$ on the original coefficient frame,
retain $\operatorname{diag}(\overline{\Omega_Q},4)$ as its
metric, and perform the original restriction or Schur
minimum on the original affine coefficient fibre. The
conjugation in NTG20 is forced by the complex-linear
functional pairing in NTG17 and NTG19.

There is also an exact row phase. Put $n=L+t$ and write
$v_n,t_n$ for the appended lower and analytic rows. At
cutoff $n-1$, retain
\[
 \zeta_n=v_n(V_{n-1}^*V_{n-1})^{-1}v_n^*,\quad
 \rho_n=t_n-v_n(V_{n-1}^*V_{n-1})^{-1}V_{n-1}^*T_{n-1}.
                                                               \tag{NTG21}
\]
The last native coefficient of $e_{m+t}$ is
$c_n=i^n\sqrt{h_{n,s}/\nu_{m+t,s}^-}
     =i^n/\sqrt{1+\zeta_n}$ by its leading coefficient
and NIB8. Its old coefficient column is the minimum-norm
solution of the lower evaluation equation, because it is
orthogonal to the old lower ideal. This solution is
$-c_n\overline{V_{n-1}}(V_{n-1}^{\mathsf T}
\overline{V_{n-1}})^{-1}v_n^{\mathsf T}$.
Multiplying its ordinary transpose by $T_{n-1}$ and adding
$c_nt_n$ gives
$\Lambda_F(e_{m+t})=c_n\rho_n$. Equation NTG17 therefore
proves
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \frac{\rho_n}{\sqrt{1+\zeta_n}}
        =-i^{-n}x_t^{\mathsf T}A,\qquad
 r_n=x_t^*\Omega_{t-1}^{-1}x_t .
 \quad}                                                        \tag{NTG22}
\]
For the last equality substitute NTG20 and the row equality
into $r_n=\rho_nD_{n-1}^{-1}\rho_n^*/(1+\zeta_n)$.
It yields $x_t^{\mathsf T}\overline{\Omega_{t-1}^{-1}}
\overline{x_t}$, the conjugate of the real nonnegative
number $x_t^*\Omega_{t-1}^{-1}x_t$. This supplies the
original phase-sensitive row to each of the eight fixed
flag compressions; zeros and all eigenvalues stay present.

\subsection{Joint minimization of the complete later relations}
Fix the accepted prefix endpoint
$R=R_j=\lfloor j^{3/2}/\log j\rfloor<q$ and any $R<Q\le q$.
Write $X_Q=[X_R,Y]$, with
$Y=[x_{R+1},\ldots,x_Q]$, and partition the upper triangular
matrix of NTG11 as
\[
 H_Q=\begin{bmatrix}H_R&H_{12}\\0&H_{22}\end{bmatrix}.
                                                               \tag{NTG23}
\]
Let $S_{R,Q}$ be the full attained Gram on the later relation
coefficients after minimizing over every earlier coefficient:
\[
 z^*S_{R,Q}z
   =\min_{a\in\mathbb C^{R+1}}
       \left\|\sum_{t=0}^Ra_tu_t+
                    \sum_{t=R+1}^Qz_{t-R-1}u_t\right\|_s^2 .
                                                               \tag{NTG24}
\]
The minimum is attained uniquely since the preceding relation
Gram is positive. In the graph coordinates NTG13, the
preceding columns are $[X_R;I_{R+1};0]$ and the new columns
are $[Y;0;I_{Q-R}]$. Their two diagonal Grams are
$I+X_R^*X_R$ and $I+Y^*Y$, and their cross Gram is
$X_R^*Y$. Completing the entire preceding square gives
\[
\begin{gathered}
 S_{R,Q}
  =H_{22}^*\bigl(I_{Q-R}+Y^*\Omega_R^{-1}Y\bigr)H_{22},\\
 \frac{\det S_{R,Q}}
      {\displaystyle\prod_{t=R+1}^Q\nu_{m+t,s}^-}
  =\det(I_{Q-R}+Y^*\Omega_R^{-1}Y)
  =\frac{\det\Omega_Q}{\det\Omega_R}.
\end{gathered}                                                    \tag{NTG25}
\]
The $H_{12}$ component is in the preceding relation span
and is minimized over, so it disappears only through the
displayed full minimum. To verify its inverse factor,
direct multiplication proves
$I-X_R(I+X_R^*X_R)^{-1}X_R^*=(I+X_RX_R^*)^{-1}$.
Conjugating by $Y$ proves the first line. The determinant
identities follow by the same two identity-block
eliminations used in NTG14. This calculation jointly
minimizes every later linear combination and preserves
every off-diagonal pairing between those combinations.

\subsection{Finite analytic improvement from the retained cross pairings}
All the following quantities have already been evaluated by
the finite original-data operations NTG4--12:
\[
\begin{gathered}
 a_t=x_t^*\Omega_R^{-1}x_t,\quad
 c_{th}=x_t^*\Omega_R^{-1}x_h,\quad R<t,h\le Q,\\
 d=Q-R,\quad d_0=\min(m,d),\quad
 T_{R,Q}=\sum_{t=R+1}^Qa_t .
\end{gathered}                                                    \tag{NTG26}
\]
The exact unweighted tail value $Z_{R,Q}:=
\log\det\Omega_Q-\log\det\Omega_R$ satisfies
\[
 0\le Z_{R,Q}\le
 d_0\log\left(1+\frac{T_{R,Q}}{d_0}\right).
                                                               \tag{NTG27}
\]
Indeed the nonzero eigenvalues of
$\Omega_R^{-1/2}YY^*\Omega_R^{-1/2}$ are nonnegative,
number at most $d_0$, and sum to $T_{R,Q}$.
The determinant equality NTG25 identifies $Z_{R,Q}$
with the sum of $\log(1+\lambda)$ over those eigenvalues.
Append zeros to reach $d_0$ terms. The elementary
concavity inequality for $\log$, proved by its negative
second derivative and finite weighted averaging, gives
NTG27. This bound charges the actual dimension $m$ rather
than the number of remaining rows when $Q-R>m$.
It does not bound $T_{R,Q}$ by an unproved small number.

There is an explicit second improvement that measures
the retained interaction of distinct later relations.
Partition the actual consecutive indices $R+1,\ldots,Q$
into adjacent pairs and at most one final singleton;
denote the pairs by $\mathscr P$. Define
\[
 \Delta_{R,Q}:=
 -\sum_{(t,h)\in\mathscr P}
       \log\left(1-
         \frac{|c_{th}|^2}{(1+a_t)(1+a_h)}\right)\ge0 .
                                                               \tag{NTG28}
\]
Every term is finite. Minimizing
$\|x_h-zx_t\|_{\Omega_R^{-1}}^2$ over complex $z$ gives
the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality in this exact positive metric
(if $x_t=0$ the mixed pairing is already zero). Thus
$|c_{th}|^2\le a_ta_h<(1+a_t)(1+a_h)$.
It follows that
\[
 Z_{R,Q}\le
       \sum_{t=R+1}^{Q}\log(1+a_t)-\Delta_{R,Q}.
                                                               \tag{NTG29}
\]
Here is the determinant proof, including the full interaction
discarded by a scalar bound. The positive matrix
$M=I+Y^*\Omega_R^{-1}Y$ has diagonal entries $1+a_t$
and off-diagonal entries $c_{th}$. For a two-block
partition, elimination gives
$\det M=\det M_{11}\det(M_{22}-M_{21}M_{11}^{-1}M_{12})
\le\det M_{11}\det M_{22}$.
The inequality follows since the second positive factor
is bounded above by $M_{22}$; conjugating by
$M_{22}^{-1/2}$ gives eigenvalues at most one and
therefore a determinant at most one. Applying this
successively to the prescribed pairs bounds $\det M$
by the product of the actual pair determinants and
the possible singleton entry. Each pair determinant
is exactly $(1+a_t)(1+a_h)-|c_{th}|^2$.
Taking logarithms proves NTG29.

This is the familiar determinant inequality associated with
Hadamard and its block refinements, applied to an explicitly
computed original Gram. The retained historical source record is
Jacques Hadamard, \emph{R\'esolution d'une question relative
aux d\'eterminants}, Bull.\ Sci.\ Math.\ (2) 17 (1893),
240--246; a scan of the original article is preserved at
\href{https://zenodo.org/records/18674602}{the primary-article archive}.
This archive's bibliographic metadata was consulted here;
its full original proof was not used as a verified theorem.
The proof above supplies the precise positive-block version
used here rather than importing an unstated variant.

For the first two remaining indices $a=R+1$, $b=R+2$,
the entire joint calculation is especially explicit:
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \log(1+r_{L+a})=\log(1+a_a),\\
