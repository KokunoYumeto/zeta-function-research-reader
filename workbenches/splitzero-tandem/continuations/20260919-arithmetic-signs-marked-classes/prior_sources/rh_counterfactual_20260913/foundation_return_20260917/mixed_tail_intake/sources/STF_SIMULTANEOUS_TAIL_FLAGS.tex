\section{The simultaneous original tail and its eight coefficient flags}
\label{sec:STF}

The Schur minimum and determinant factorization used below are classical:
see Boyd and Vandenberghe, Appendix A.5.5, p.~650, and Appendix C.4.1,
p.~673 \cite{human:boyd-vandenberghe}. We prove the required complex
Hermitian formulas explicitly. The application is to the unchanged
NIB/IPR rows and the original CPQ flags; no generic subspace of the same
dimension replaces a programme map.

Fix the original simple quartet, its period and logarithm branch, and its
complete conductor. Retain
\[
 j=k+4,\quad q=(j+1)^2,\quad q'=(j-7)^2,\quad
 L=q-v,\quad m=L-q',\quad s\in\{1,j\},\quad c'=j/2-4 .       \tag{STF1}
\]
Here $k=4l+1\ge9$, $v=\operatorname{ord}_0 E_A$ and the full
nonzero derivative $\mu_v=E_A^{(v)}(0)$ remain those of NIB1.
In particular $m>0$ on its original domain. The complete native basis is
$\phi_n(S')=p_{n,s}((S'-c')/i)/\sqrt{M_sn!(s/2)_n}$, with
$M_s=(2\pi)^{s/2}$ and the original Meixner--Pollaczek dictionary
HPS1--4 \cite[eqs.~(18.19.8)--(18.19.9), (18.22.8)]{human:dlmf18}.
For all original lower roots $\beta_{j-8,u,w}$, put
$V_d[n,(u,w)]=\phi_n(\beta_{j-8,u,w})$; for each original analytic
numerator functional $\Lambda_{F_b}$ put
$T_d[n,b]=\Lambda_{F_b}(\phi_n)$, $0\le n\le d$, $1\le b\le m$.
The functional pairing is complex-linear, as in NIB; inner products
are conjugate-linear in their first entry. The original target form is
\[
 D_N=T_{N-v}^*\bigl(I-V_{N-v}(V_{N-v}^*V_{N-v})^{-1}
                         V_{N-v}^*\bigr)T_{N-v},
 \qquad \mathcal D_N=\operatorname{diag}(D_N,4).             \tag{STF2}
\]
All inverses exist on $q-1\le N\le2q$ by the full original rank
proof NIB4. In particular the original masses and phases occur in
every entry of these matrices.

At $N=q+r$, $n=L+r$, let $V_0=V_{n-1}$, $T_0=T_{n-1}$, and
write $v_n,t_n$ for their original next rows. Retain
\[
 \zeta_n=v_n(V_0^*V_0)^{-1}v_n^*,\qquad
 \rho_n=t_n-v_n(V_0^*V_0)^{-1}V_0^*T_0,
 \qquad U_r=\operatorname{diag}
              (\rho_n/\sqrt{1+\zeta_n},4).                \tag{STF3}
\]
IPR1 proves the exact update
$\mathcal D_{q+r}=\mathcal D_{q+r-1}+U_r^*U_r$.
The old lower Gram, conductor and analytic functionals in STF3 are
retained at their actual changing cutoff.

\subsection{Two matrices for the entire original tail}
Choose any original integer $0\le R<q$ and let
$N_0=q+R$, $H=\mathcal D_{N_0}$.
For $t\in\{q-1,q\}$, define the actual stacked rows and relative forms
\[
 A_t=\begin{bmatrix}U_{R+1}\\ \vdots\\ U_t\end{bmatrix},
 \quad C_t=A_tH^{-1/2},\quad M_t=C_t^*C_t,
 \quad \mathcal D_{q+t}=H^{1/2}(I_{4m}+M_t)H^{1/2}.          \tag{STF4}
\]
An empty stack gives $C_t$ with zero rows and $M_t=0$.
Summing STF3 proves STF4, including $t=R=q-1$.
The map $z\mapsto H^{1/2}z$ is an isometry from the original
coefficient space with metric $H$ to Euclidean $\mathbb C^{4m}$,
with inverse $H^{-1/2}$. Formula STF4 records both directions and
the entire original $H$; it does not divide a source measure by its mass.
The matrices $M_t$ have size $4m$, independent of the number of tail rows.

For any fixed original subspace $X$ with independent column frame $X_0$,
set
\[
 Z_X=H^{1/2}X_0(X_0^*HX_0)^{-1/2},\quad
 P_X=Z_XZ_X^*,\quad K_{X,t}=C_tP_XC_t^*,\quad
 F_t(X)=\log\det(I+K_{X,t}).                              \tag{STF5}
\]
For $X=0$ use empty $Z_X$, $P_X=0$ and $F_t(X)=0$.
Since $Z_X^*Z_X=I$, $P_X$ is exactly the orthogonal projector onto
$H^{1/2}X$. Direct multiplication of STF4 gives
\[
 \frac{\det(X_0^*\mathcal D_{q+t}X_0)}{\det(X_0^*HX_0)}
 =\det(I+Z_X^*M_tZ_X)=\det(I+K_{X,t}).                    \tag{STF6}
\]
For the last equality, elimination of either diagonal identity block in
$\left[\begin{smallmatrix}I&B\\-A&I\end{smallmatrix}\right]$
proves $\det(I+AB)=\det(I+BA)$ for rectangular $A,B$.
Apply it with $A=C_tZ_X$ and $B=Z_X^*C_t^*$.
Thus every original fixed-frame determinant cancels only in its own
same-frame ratio. No cutoff-dependent frame is substituted.

The nonzero spectrum of $K_{X,t}$ can be calculated in $4m$ dimensions:
\[
             \widetilde K_{X,t}=M_t^{1/2}P_XM_t^{1/2}.     \tag{STF7}
\]
Here is the exact map, including singular $M_t$. On
$\operatorname{im}M_t^{1/2}$ define
$W_t(M_t^{1/2}z)=C_tz$. It is well defined since
$\|C_tz\|^2=z^*M_tz=\|M_t^{1/2}z\|^2$, and is an isometry onto
$\operatorname{im}C_t$. Extend it by zero on $\ker M_t$.
Then $C_t=W_tM_t^{1/2}$, and
$K_{X,t}=W_t\widetilde K_{X,t}W_t^*$.
Both operators vanish on the corresponding complementary kernels;
on their ranges this is a unitary equivalence with inverse $W_t^*$.
It preserves every nonzero eigenvalue with multiplicity. The zero
eigenvalues contribute zero to both functions $\log(1+x)$ and
$\log\max(1,x)$ used below.

\subsection{Each original quotient, with its cross terms}
Let $K\subset Y$ be fixed original coefficient spaces. Choose independent
$X_K$ for $K$ and fixed lifts $X_Q$ of the specified quotient values, so
$[X_K,X_Q]$ frames $Y$. Let $Q_0$ be its exact quotient metric in $H$,
and retain the attained base representative
\[
 Z=X_Q-X_K(X_K^*HX_K)^{-1}X_K^*HX_Q,\quad
 Q_0=Z^*HZ,\quad E=H^{1/2}ZQ_0^{-1/2}.                   \tag{STF8}
\]
For $K=0$ omit its entire term. For $Y=K$ the quotient value space
is zero-dimensional: its determinant is $1$, its log determinant is $0$,
and its inverse and square root are the unique operators on that space.
These conventions also apply to empty frames throughout.
The columns of $E$ are orthonormal,
orthogonal to $H^{1/2}K$, and span its orthogonal complement in
$H^{1/2}Y$. Thus $EE^*=P_Y-P_K$. Let $Z_K$ be an orthonormal frame
for $H^{1/2}K$. Every vector representing quotient value $x$ has
transformed coordinate $EQ_0^{1/2}x+Z_Ka$. Its new quadratic form is
\[
 \|Q_0^{1/2}x\|^2+\|a\|^2+
       \|C_tEQ_0^{1/2}x+C_tZ_Ka\|^2 .                   \tag{STF9}
\]
For any matrix $B$ and vector $y$, differentiation or square completion
gives the unique minimum of $\|a\|^2+\|y+Ba\|^2$ at
$a=-(I+B^*B)^{-1}B^*y$. Its value is
$y^*(I+BB^*)^{-1}y$: multiplication verifies
$I-B(I+B^*B)^{-1}B^*=(I+BB^*)^{-1}$.
Applying this complete minimum to STF9 proves the literal original
quotient form at the later endpoint,
\[
 Q_t=Q_0^{1/2}\left[I+E^*C_t^*(I+C_tP_KC_t^*)^{-1}C_tE\right]Q_0^{1/2}.
                                                               \tag{STF10}
\]
In particular $Q_t\succeq Q_0$ and no kernel direction or cross pairing
has been removed without minimizing over it. The expression inside
STF10 also has the finite coefficient-space realization
\[
 C_t^*(I+C_tP_KC_t^*)^{-1}C_t
 =M_t^{1/2}(I+M_t^{1/2}P_KM_t^{1/2})^{-1}M_t^{1/2}.       \tag{STF11}
\]
To prove it, use the isometry $W_t$ just constructed. On its range the
two inverses are intertwined; on the complementary range both surrounding
$C_t$ factors vanish. This proves STF11 even at rank loss.

Block elimination in the fixed frame $[X_K,X_Q]$ gives
$\det\operatorname{Gram}(Y)=\det\operatorname{Gram}(K)\det Q$.
Taking its ratio at $q+t$ and $N_0$, all frame constants cancel, yielding
\[
 \log\frac{\det Q_t}{\det Q_0}=F_t(Y)-F_t(K)\ge0.        \tag{STF12}
\]
Consequently STF10 evaluates every complementary tail at the same two
matrices $M_{q-1},M_q$, preserving the dependence between the quotients.

\subsection{The eight flags and the exact allocation identity}
Use precisely the CPQ coefficient maps on $\mathcal U=\mathbb C^{4m}$:
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 B=\operatorname{im}B_{\rm cof},\quad
 I=\operatorname{im}(B_{\rm cof}J_0),\quad
 S=\operatorname{im}[B_{\rm cof},F_e],\quad
 J=\ker\operatorname{diag}(B_j,4),\\
 K_i=I\cap J,\quad W_i=I+J,\quad W=B+J,\quad
 K_c=S\cap W,\quad T=S+J,
 \qquad e\in\{0,1\}.                                    \tag{STF13}
 \end{gathered}
\]
Since $B\subset S$, this $T$ is the same $S+W$ as CPQ.
The original inclusions give
$J\subset W_i\subset W\subset T$ and
$K_i\subset I\subset K_c\subset S$.
The actual pairs for the four CPQ complementary forms are
\[
 \begin{array}{c|cccc}
 \text{form}&Q_b^E&Q_{{\rm rem},e}&G_{s,e}&A_{\rm inv}\\ \hline
 (Y,K)&(W,W_i)&(\mathcal U,T)&(S,K_c)&(I,K_i).
 \end{array}                                                   \tag{STF14}
\]
These identifications are the exact same-value CPQ maps, not merely
dimension equalities: $W/J$ identifies with the original image of
$\Phi$, $W_i/J$ with its invariant image; $\mathcal U/T$ is CPQ8's
remaining target quotient; $S/K_c$ is CPQ7's connecting source quotient;
and $I/K_i$ is the source pullback quotient in CPQ9. The corresponding
fixed lift changes are independent of cutoff and therefore cancel in
STF12. All their original value metrics are recovered by STF8--10.

For the logarithm of a restriction determinant in a fixed frame,
$f_X(N)=\log\det(X_0^*\mathcal D_NX_0)$, IPR3 gives exactly
\[
 \mathcal R_{>R}f_X=F_{q-1}(X)+F_q(X).                    \tag{STF15}
\]
Indeed both terms on the right subtract the same $f_X(q+R)$.
It follows that the original signed tail is
\[
\begin{gathered}
\mathcal L_e^{>R}=\sum_{t=q-1,q}\sum_{a=1}^8\sigma_a F_t(X_a),\\
(X_1,\ldots,X_8)=(T,K_c,W_i,K_i,W,S,J,I),\qquad
(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_8)=(1,1,1,1,-1,-1,-1,-1).
\end{gathered}
                                                               \tag{STF16}
\]
The tuple consists of labelled occurrences: coincident subspaces are
retained in every position with its indicated sign. All flag sums below
use this tuple, including when a quotient is zero-dimensional.
Every complementary tail is $\sum_{t=q-1,q}[F_t(Y)-F_t(K)]$
using its exact pair in STF14. The four-copy kernel restriction and
target quotient allocations are
\[
 4S_R^{>R}=\sum_{t=q-1,q}F_t(J),\qquad
 4S_K^{>R}=\sum_{t=q-1,q}[F_t(\mathcal U)-F_t(J)].         \tag{STF17}
\]
The latter is the minimum over the actual full kernel of
$\operatorname{diag}(B_j,4)$. Substitution of STF14 into STF16 cancels
the same terms algebraically, proving
\[
 4S_K^{>R}=\mathcal L_e^{>R}+V_b^{>R}+V_{\rm rem}^{>R}
                        +V_s^{>R}+V_A^{>R},\qquad
 S_K^{>R}+S_R^{>R}=\mathcal T^{>R}.                       \tag{STF18}
\]
Thus the joint calculation supplies every term of IPR7 through common
finite matrices and its exact quotient operations.

\subsection{Finite comparison with the original row-threshold profile}
Define the simultaneous endpoint threshold value
\[
\mathcal B_e^{>R}=\sum_{t=q-1,q}\sum_{a=1}^8\sigma_a
             \sum_b\log\max(1,\lambda_b(K_{X_a,t})),
 \qquad d_t=\operatorname{rank}C_t\le4m.                 \tag{STF19}
\]
All matrices $K_{X,t}$ are supported on the same $d_t$-dimensional
space $\operatorname{im}C_t$. Compress to it by the exact isometry in
STF7. Nested $X$ give ordered projectors and ordered $K_{X,t}$, hence
ordered eigenvalues: for each test subspace every Rayleigh quotient of
the larger Hermitian matrix dominates the smaller one, and successive
minima and maxima preserve that inequality. This proves the required
eigenvalue ordering without assuming a common eigenbasis.

For $x\ge0$, put $h(x)=\log(1+x)-\log\max(1,x)=u(x)-v(x)$,
where
$u(x)=\log(1+\min(x,1))$ and $v(x)=0$ for $x\le1$,
$v(x)=\log(2x/(1+x))$ for $x\ge1$.
Both $u,v$ are increasing from zero and at most $\log2$.
Along any four-element ordered eigenvalue chain, the sum of the first
and last disjoint increments of either function lies in $[0,\log2]$:
adding its omitted middle nonnegative increment proves the upper bound.
The corresponding sum of $h$ increments lies in $[-\log2,\log2]$.
The two chains in STF13 therefore give a difference bounded by
$2\log2$ per eigenvalue. Summing over the common range proves
\[
 |\mathcal L_e^{>R}-\mathcal B_e^{>R}|
             \le2(d_{q-1}+d_q)\log2\le16m\log2.         \tag{STF20}
\]
This is a bound for the simultaneous endpoint representation, whose
eigenvalues differ from the original successive four-row compressions.
The exact map to the latter scalar profile is as follows. At each
original row the same two-chain argument uses four eigenvalues, hence
its signed logarithmic and threshold increments differ by at most
$8\log2$. The tail weights sum to $2(q-R)-1$, so the original
row-threshold tail $\mathcal P_e^{>R}$ satisfies
\[
 |\mathcal P_e^{>R}-\mathcal B_e^{>R}|
 \le\bigl[8\{2(q-R)-1\}+2(d_{q-1}+d_q)\bigr]\log2.      \tag{STF21}
\]
This proves the relation between the two threshold constructions with
their precise finite discrepancy; it asserts no equality of individual
row and endpoint eigenvalues.

For a positive semidefinite finite matrix let
$n_X(t,a)=\#\{\lambda_b(K_{X,t})>a\}$, counting multiplicity.
The elementary identity
$\log\max(1,\lambda)=\int_1^\infty\mathbf1_{a<\lambda}\,da/a$
and the finite eigenvalue sum give the exact remaining integral
\[
 \mathcal B_e^{>R}
   =\sum_{t=q-1,q}\int_1^\infty
                    \sum_{b=1}^8\sigma_b n_{X_b}(t,a)\,\frac{da}{a}. \tag{STF22}
\]
No limiting interchange is needed; each integrand vanishes beyond
the largest eigenvalue of its finite matrices. It is the signed
spectral count of the original correlated flag compressions.

Finally choose only the already-proved original prefix
$R=R_j=\lfloor j^{3/2}/\log j\rfloor<q$.
NIB19--20 and IPR8 bound the removed signed and threshold prefixes
by $4\mathfrak E_{s,R_j}=o(kq_k)$. Since $m=16j-48-v$ and
$q=(j+1)^2$, STF20--21 are respectively $O(j)$ and $O(j^2)$.
Thus the full signed and original threshold returns each differ
from the exact finite expression STF22 by $o(kq_k)$, with explicit
finite errors given by the prefix budget and STF20--21. This evaluates
the complete source-to-expression map and its error; the growing-degree
value of STF22 itself is still to be calculated. Both source orders,
both primitive indices, the independent proper source and the full
action retain their existing domains and receiving identities.
