The earlier circle heat construction in \cite{oldcircle} has an exact
dyadic map to the theta source of the current programme. Equations D1--D3
of the corpus receiver \cite{corpus} prove the source identity and its
Gram error. Here we extend that construction to the full finite relation
minimum, the original additive tensor source, its complete primary
polynomial and every fixed restriction or quotient of the attained metric.
All coordinate maps and the finite error constants are specified below.
The truncations need not span the same finite relation subspace; the
comparison is proved on the full coefficient space before taking either
minimum. The original polynomial and both regions of every source integral
are retained throughout.

\section{The source, the physical measure and exact division}
Retain $x>0$, $D=-x\partial_x$, $\Hh=L^2(\R_{>0},dx)$ and
\begin{equation}\tag{HM1}
 \begin{gathered}
 \phi_*(x)=(4\pi^2x^4-6\pi x^2)e^{-\pi x^2},\qquad
 \Theta\phi(x)=2\sum_{n\ge1}\phi(nx),\qquad f_j=D^j\Theta\phi_*,\\
 V=\{\phi\in\mathcal S(\R):\phi(-x)=\phi(x),\ \phi(0)=0,
                           \ \int_\R\phi=0\},\\
 \B=\{F\in C^\infty(\R_{>0}):
         \sup_{x>0}x^a|D^bF(x)|<\infty\text{ for all }a\in\R, b\ge0\},\\
 \M F(w)=\int_0^\infty F(x)x^{w-1}\,dx,\qquad
 g(w)=2\xi(w)=w(w-1)\pi^{-w/2}\Gamma(w/2)\zeta(w).
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The completed-zeta convention is Apostol's convention as recorded in
\cite{DLMF25}. Poisson summation gives $\Theta V\subset\B$:
the theta sum of an even Schwartz function has near zero the two terms
$x^{-1}\int\phi-\phi(0)$ and a remainder decreasing faster than every
power, and those two terms vanish on $V$. The same argument applies after
every $D$ derivative. Since $D(D-1)e^{-\pi x^2}=\phi_*$ and $D$ preserves
both zero conditions on $V$, every $f_j$ belongs to $\B$.
For $\Re w>1$, absolute integration of the theta series and integration
by parts give
\begin{equation}\tag{HM2}
 \M f_j(w)=w^j g(w).
\end{equation}
Rapid decrease at both ends extends this identity to the whole plane.

Mellin Parseval in the physical measure is
\begin{equation}\tag{HM3}
 \norm F_{\Hh}^2=\frac1{2\pi}\int_\R
                   |\M F(1/2+iy)|^2\,dy .
\end{equation}
For precision, put $x=e^u$ and $\widetilde F(u)=e^{u/2}F(e^u)$.
Its $L^2(du)$ norm equals $\norm F_{\Hh}$ and its Fourier transform
with positive exponential is $(2\pi)^{-1/2}\M F(1/2+iy)$.
Fourier Plancherel proves (HM3), including complex functions and polarized
inner products. This is the convention of \cite[\S1.14(iv)]{DLMF1}.

Let $Z$ be any nonempty finite set of actual zeros of $g$, with their
full multiplicities $m_\rho$, and put
\begin{equation}\tag{HM4}
 h(w)=\prod_{\rho\in Z}(w-\rho)^{m_\rho},\qquad d=\deg h,\qquad
 E_h=\prod_{\rho\in Z}\C[w]/(w-\rho)^{m_\rho}.
\end{equation}
The map $j_h$ takes every divided Taylor coefficient through order
$m_\rho-1$ in the literal coordinate $w-\rho$. In particular
$j_h(g/h)$ is a unit in $E_h$; no root difference or Taylor coefficient
of this unit is omitted.

\begin{lemma}[Division in the original source space]
For $F\in\B$ with $\M F(\rho)=0$, define
\[
 (S_\rho F)(x)=x^{-\rho}\int_x^\infty F(t)t^{\rho-1}\,dt.
\]
Then $S_\rho F\in\B$,
$(D-\rho)S_\rho F=F$, and
$\M(S_\rho F)(w)=\M F(w)/(w-\rho)$ with its removable value at $\rho$.
Consequently there is a unique $a_h\in\B$ with $\M a_h=g/h$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At infinity rapid decrease of $F$ bounds the integral and each derivative
by arbitrarily high negative powers. At zero the vanishing total integral
rewrites it as $-x^{-\rho}\int_0^xF(t)t^{\rho-1}dt$, giving arbitrarily
high positive powers. Differentiation gives
$D(S_\rho F)=\rho S_\rho F+F$ and proves the same estimates for all
derivatives. Mellin integration by parts gives the transform formula for
$w\ne\rho$, and both sides are entire after removal. Starting from
$f_0$, divide successively by the complete multiset of roots of $h$.
Each required Mellin zero remains until its corresponding factor is
removed. The result has transform $g/h$. Mellin injectivity, which
follows from (HM3), proves uniqueness and independence of the chosen
division order.
\end{proof}

For $u\in E_h$, let $r_h(u)\in\PP_{d-1}$ be the unique polynomial with
\begin{equation}\tag{HM5}
 j_h r_h(u)=j_h(h/g)u,
 \qquad R_hu=r_h(u)(D)a_h.
\end{equation}
Here $h/g$ is only used as its holomorphic germ at the specified zeros;
the notation means the inverse of $j_h(g/h)$. Then
$\M R_hu=g r_h(u)/h$ and $j_h\M R_hu=u$ exactly.

\section{The full finite prequotient and the heat replacement}
Fix $N\ge d-1$. Write $\PP_n=\C[w]_{\le n}$ and $\PP_{-1}=0$.
The following are mutually inverse coordinate maps:
\begin{equation}\tag{HM6}
 \begin{aligned}
 L_N:E_h\oplus\PP_{N-d}&\longrightarrow\PP_N,
      &(u,a)&\longmapsto r_h(u)+ha,\\
 L_N^{-1}:P&\longmapsto
 \left(j_h((g/h)P),\ \frac{P-r_h(j_h((g/h)P))}{h}\right).
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Indeed the first component of the inverse recovers $u$ since $j_h g=0$;
the second is a polynomial since its numerator has all the required
Taylor zeros. Both compositions are the identity, including when the
relation summand is zero. Use the original jet coordinates and the
ordinary power coefficients for every matrix below. Define
\begin{equation}\tag{HM7}
 \begin{gathered}
 F_{N-d}a=a(D)f_0,\quad
 \mathcal R_N=[R_h,F_{N-d}],\quad
 \mathcal P_N=[0,F_{N-d}],\quad
 \mathbb G_N=\mathcal R_N^*\mathcal R_N>0,\\
 \M(\mathcal R_NL_N^{-1}P)=\frac{g}{h}P,\qquad
 \norm{\mathcal R_NL_N^{-1}P}_{\Hh}^2
 =\int_\R |P(1/2+iy)|^2 w_h(y)\,dy,\\
 w_h(y)=\frac1{2\pi}|(g/h)(1/2+iy)|^2.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
This is the original complete one-factor polynomial source. Positivity
follows because the nonzero entire function $g/h$ cannot vanish on an
interval, so a zero source norm forces $P=0$ and then $(u,a)=0$.

On the circle $z\in\R/\mathbb Z$, keep
$h_0=\mathbf1_{[0,1/2)}-\mathbf1_{[1/2,1)}$ and
$\mathcal N=-(4\pi^2)^{-1}\partial_z^2$. The old construction
\cite{oldcircle} is $C(t)=\partial_z(e^{-t\mathcal N}h_0)(0)$.
Its Fourier coefficients give $C(t)=4\sum_{n\ {\mathrm{odd}}}e^{-tn^2}$,
where the sum is over all odd integers. To avoid any dependence on a
formal infinite rearrangement, for every integer $J\ge0$ put
$s=2^{J+1}$ and use the finite identity
\begin{equation}\tag{HM8}
 \frac14\sum_{j=0}^J C(\pi4^jx^2)
  =\vartheta(x^2)-\vartheta(s^2x^2),\qquad
 \vartheta(v)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}e^{-\pi n^2v}.
\end{equation}
This follows by separating odd from even integers at each step.
Applying $D^{j+1}(D-1)$ (with the derivative index $j$ independent of
the summation index in (HM8)) gives the exact source replacement
\begin{equation}\tag{HM9}
 \begin{gathered}
 T_sF(x)=F(sx),\qquad T_s^*T_s=s^{-1}I,\\
 f_j^{[J]}=(I-T_s)f_j
   =\frac14D^{j+1}(D-1)\sum_{\ell=0}^J C(\pi4^\ell x^2),\\
 \mathcal R_N^{[J]}=\mathcal R_N-T_s\mathcal P_N,
 \qquad \mathbb G_N^{[J]}=(\mathcal R_N^{[J]})^*\mathcal R_N^{[J]}.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
All columns of $T_s\mathcal P_N$ lie in $\Theta V$. Therefore the
two full column maps have the same image in $\B/\Theta V$ and the
same full jets at every actual zero. This equality of quotient classes
alone makes no assertion about either finite metric; those metrics
are compared next.

\section{An explicit comparison before taking any minimum}
When $N\ge d$, partition the full original Gram as
\begin{equation}\tag{HM10}
 \mathbb G_N=\begin{pmatrix}A&C\ C^*&S\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 H=A-CS^{-1}C^*>0,
 \qquad
 c_N^2=1+\norm{H^{-1/2}CS^{-1/2}}^2.
\end{equation}
All norms on finite coordinate spaces here are Euclidean operator norms;
the positive square roots are those of the actual matrices. When $N=d-1$
there is no relation block and define $c_N=0$.

\begin{theorem}[Full finite heat comparison]
For every $N\ge d-1$ and every $J\ge0$, let
$\delta_{N,J}=2^{-(J+1)/2}c_N$. For $\delta_{N,J}<1$,
\begin{equation}\tag{HM11}
 (1-\delta_{N,J})^2\mathbb G_N
 \preceq\mathbb G_N^{[J]}
 \preceq(1+\delta_{N,J})^2\mathbb G_N.
\end{equation}
In particular the actual minima over all relation coefficients obey
\begin{equation}\tag{HM12}
 (1-\delta_{N,J})^2H\preceq H^{[J]}
                 \preceq(1+\delta_{N,J})^2H,
 \quad
 u^*H^{[J]}u=\min_a\norm{R_hu+(I-T_s)F_{N-d}a}_{\Hh}^2.
\end{equation}
The matrices in (HM10) are sufficient to choose a finite $J$ for every
specified positive error tolerance; no growth assumption on $c_N$ is used.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For the nonempty relation block,
$\mathcal P_N^*\mathcal P_N=\operatorname{diag}(0,S)$.
The nonzero eigenvalues of
$\mathbb G_N^{-1/2}\mathcal P_N^*\mathcal P_N\mathbb G_N^{-1/2}$
are those of the bottom principal block of $\mathbb G_N^{-1}$
conjugated by $S^{1/2}$. Direct block elimination gives that block as
$S^{-1}+S^{-1}C^*H^{-1}CS^{-1}$. Its conjugate is
$I+S^{-1/2}C^*H^{-1}CS^{-1/2}$, whose largest eigenvalue is $c_N^2$.
This is also obtained by completing the square
\[
 \norm{\mathcal R_N(u,a)}^2
 =u^*Hu+(a+S^{-1}C^*u)^*S(a+S^{-1}C^*u).
\]
Thus, for every coefficient vector $v$,
\begin{equation}\tag{HM13}
 \norm{T_s\mathcal P_Nv}_{\Hh}
 \le s^{-1/2}c_N\norm{\mathcal R_Nv}_{\Hh}.
\end{equation}
The two triangle inequalities for
$\mathcal R_N^{[J]}v=\mathcal R_Nv-T_s\mathcal P_Nv$
prove (HM11). The zero-relation case is an equality.
To prove (HM12), use (HM11) on every vector $(u,a)$ and take the
infimum over the same coefficient $a$ on each side. Positive definiteness
ensures the minima are attained. This proves the comparison even though
$F_{N-d}\PP_{N-d}$ and $(I-T_s)F_{N-d}\PP_{N-d}$ need not be the
same subspace of $\Hh$.
\end{proof}
For reference, the exact correction, with both cross terms retained, is
\begin{equation}\tag{HM14}
 \mathbb G_N^{[J]}-\mathbb G_N
 =-\mathcal R_N^*T_s\mathcal P_N
  -\mathcal P_N^*T_s^*\mathcal R_N+s^{-1}\mathcal P_N^*\mathcal P_N.
\end{equation}
Completing a positive quadratic form and its minimum-norm lift is the
classical Schur-complement calculation of \cite[Appendix C.4.1]{Boyd};
the preceding proof specifies its matrices in this arithmetic source.

\section{The exact additive tensor source and its full primary quotient}
Let $k\ge1$ and retain each separate Mellin coordinate $w_j=1/2+iy_j$.
On the original $S$-power coefficients define the literal multinomial map
\begin{equation}\tag{HM15}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal S_{k,N}:\C[S]_{\le N}&\longrightarrow\PP_N^{\otimes k},\\
 S^n&\longmapsto
 \sum_{n_1+\cdots+n_k=n}\frac{n!}{n_1!\cdots n_k!}
                     w_1^{n_1}\cdots w_k^{n_k},\\
 \mathcal C_{k,N}&=(L_N^{-1})^{\otimes k}\mathcal S_{k,N},\\
 \mathcal A_{k,N}&=\mathcal R_N^{\otimes k}\mathcal C_{k,N},\qquad
 \mathcal A_{k,N}^{[J]}=(\mathcal R_N^{[J]})^{\otimes k}\mathcal C_{k,N}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
These maps target $\Hh^{\otimes k}=L^2((\R_{>0})^k,dx_1\cdots dx_k)$.
In particular no new product, root polynomial or coefficient normalization
is inserted. The multiple Mellin transform of the first map is exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{HM16}
 \M^{\otimes k}(\mathcal A_{k,N}P)(w_1,\ldots,w_k)
       =P(w_1+\cdots+w_k)\prod_{j=1}^k(g/h)(w_j).
\end{equation}
Tensor Parseval and Tonelli, followed by the definition of convolution,
therefore prove
\begin{equation}\tag{HM17}
 \begin{aligned}
 \norm{\mathcal A_{k,N}P}^2
 &=\int_{\R^k}|P(k/2+i\textstyle\sum_jy_j)|^2
                                  \prod_j w_h(y_j)\,dy_j\\
 &=\int_\R|P(k/2+iy)|^2w_h^{*k}(y)\,dy.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The entire real line is retained. All moments are finite by (HM7) and
the rapid vertical decrease of Mellin transforms of $\B$. The form in
(HM17) is the original tensor source of \cite{fullwindow}; denote its
power-basis matrix by $\mathcal H_{k,N}>0$ and the corresponding heat
matrix by $\mathcal H_{k,N}^{[J]}$.

The finite tensor quotient class is preserved as well. Indeed, expanding
the difference of the tensor maps in (HM15) by replacing factors one at
a time expresses it as a sum of tensors with one factor in $\Theta V$.
Consequently both maps induce the same map into the algebraic tensor
quotient $\B^{\otimes k}/\sum_i\B^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes\Theta V
\otimes\B^{\otimes(k-i)}$. No identification with a completed tensor
quotient is needed for the following finite matrix comparison.

\begin{theorem}[The original tensor minima receive the heat error]
For $\delta=\delta_{N,J}<1$ one has
\begin{equation}\tag{HM18}
 (1-\delta)^{2k}\mathcal H_{k,N}
 \preceq\mathcal H_{k,N}^{[J]}
 \preceq(1+\delta)^{2k}\mathcal H_{k,N}.
\end{equation}
Let $\chi(S)$ be the original complete primary polynomial of the packet,
$q=\deg\chi$, and take $N\ge\max(d-1,q-1)$. Keep the remainder map
$J_{\chi,N}:\C[S]_{\le N}\to E_\chi=\C[S]/(\chi)$ in its original
coordinates. The two actual attained quotient forms satisfy
\begin{equation}\tag{HM19}
 \begin{gathered}
 G_{k,N}=(J_{\chi,N}\mathcal H_{k,N}^{-1}J_{\chi,N}^*)^{-1},\quad
 G_{k,N}^{[J]}=(J_{\chi,N}(\mathcal H_{k,N}^{[J]})^{-1}
                                                   J_{\chi,N}^*)^{-1},\\
 (1-\delta)^{2k}G_{k,N}preceq G_{k,N}^{[J]}
                                      \preceq(1+\delta)^{2k}G_{k,N}.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
All original multiplicities, roots and the complete relation space
$\chi\C[S]_{\le N-q}$ remain unchanged in this comparison.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Conjugating (HM11) by $\mathbb G_N^{-1/2}$ puts every eigenvalue
between $(1-\delta)^2$ and $(1+\delta)^2$. The eigenvalues of its
$k$-fold tensor power are the products of $k$ such eigenvalues. Returning
to the original coordinate forms gives the corresponding bounds on
$(\mathbb G_N^{[J]})^{\otimes k}$. Pull them back through the exact
map $\mathcal C_{k,N}$ to obtain (HM18).

For completeness, for any positive matrix $M$ and any surjection $J$,
the minimum of $p^*Mp$ subject to $Jp=v$ is
$v^*(JM^{-1}J^*)^{-1}v$. Its unique minimum-norm representative is
$p_*=M^{-1}J^*(JM^{-1}J^*)^{-1}v$; every other representative is
$p_*+z$ with $Jz=0$ and $z^*Mp_*=0$. This proves both formulas in
(HM19). Taking the infimum in (HM18) over the identical affine fibre
$J_{\chi,N}p=v$ gives its comparison. Its kernel is exactly the
specified full polynomial ideal by Euclidean division by $\chi$.
\end{proof}

For the quartet used in the current programme, this theorem retains
literally
\begin{equation}\tag{HM20}
 \begin{gathered}
 \rho=\tfrac12+\delta_\rho+i\gamma,\quad
 0<\delta_\rho<\tfrac12,\quad\gamma>2,\quad m_0\ge1,\\
 k\equiv1\pmod4,\quad k\ge5,\qquad
 h(w)=\prod_{\sigma\in\{\rho,\bar\rho,1-\rho,1-\bar\rho\}}
                         (w-\sigma)^{m_0},\\
 e=1+k(m_0-1),\quad q=e(k+1)^2,\\
 \chi(S)=\prod_{a,b=0}^k
 [S-k/2-(2a-k)\delta_\rho-i(2b-k)\gamma]^e.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
In particular the dyadic $s$ is a heat truncation parameter and is not
identified with $k,q$, a period, a root displacement or the physical
coordinate $S=k/2+iy$.

\section{Restrictions, further quotients and an attained finite budget}
Let $M$ be any one of the forms just compared, and let $M^{[J]}$ be
its heat counterpart in the same coefficient space. For every fixed
injective frame $I$ and fixed surjection $\Lambda$, define
\begin{equation}\tag{HM21}
 M_I=I^*MI,\qquad
 Q_\Lambda=(\Lambda M^{-1}\Lambda^*)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Multiplication by $I$ preserves both inequalities in (HM18)--(HM19).
Taking the infimum over $\Lambda v=u$ preserves them by the proof of
(HM19). Induction gives the same constants for every finite sequence of
these fixed restrictions and quotients, including the original conductor
kernel and attained observation when their original coefficient maps
are used. This statement fixes the maps before changing the metric;
it does not substitute metric-dependent orthonormal frames for them.

If the resulting form has rank $r>0$, each eigenvalue relative to its
original form lies between $(1-\delta)^{2k}$ and $(1+\delta)^{2k}$.
Hence its determinant in the same coordinate frame obeys
\begin{equation}\tag{HM22}
 \left|\log\frac{\det M_X^{[J]}}{\det M_X}\right|
 \le 2kr[-\log(1-\delta)].
\end{equation}
Rank zero contributes determinant $1$ and error zero. For a finite
specified return $\mathcal L=\sum_\ell a_\ell\log\det M_{X_\ell}$,
let $B=\sum_\ell|a_\ell|r_\ell$. If its cutoff family is finite, use
$c_* =\max_N c_N$ over exactly those original prequotients, the same
$J$ for the whole family, and $\delta=2^{-(J+1)/2}c_*$. Then
\begin{equation}\tag{HM23}
 |\mathcal L^{[J]}-\mathcal L|\le2kB[-\log(1-\delta)].
\end{equation}
This includes a full original cutoff window with its literal endpoint
weights, once their ranks and coefficients are inserted into $B$.
It does not discard a rank factor from an absolute determinant estimate.

Here is a finite choice that actually attains every prescribed error
$\varepsilon>0$. If $B=0$ or $c_*=0$, the error is zero. Otherwise put
\begin{equation}\tag{HM24}
 \delta_* =\min\left\{\frac14,\frac{\varepsilon}{4kB}\right\},\qquad
 J=\max\left\{0,
       \left\lceil2\log_2(c_*/\delta_*)\right\rceil-1\right\}.
\end{equation}
This gives $\delta\le\delta_*\le1/4$. The integral identity
$-\log(1-\delta)=\int_0^\delta(1-t)^{-1}dt$
gives $-\log(1-\delta)\le4\delta/3$, so (HM23) is at most
$\varepsilon$ (indeed at most $2\varepsilon/3$). All matrices defining
$c_*$ are the full original finite integral Grams in (HM7), with the
same arithmetic unit and the two cross blocks in (HM10).
Thus (HM24) proves finite convergence of these geometric heat sources to
every specified original metric return. It does not assert a bound on
the growth of $c_N$, and in particular makes no assertion that this
choice of $J$ is logarithmic in $q$.

\section{The actual nilpotent graded metrics and their singular values}
The recovered jet-ideal chain also has the following precise receiver.
For the full original sum polynomial $\chi$, write its distinct roots as
$\kappa$ with their original multiplicities $e_\kappa$. Its confluent
matrix in the original power basis is
\[
 \mathsf V_{(\kappa,j),n}=\binom nj\kappa^{n-j}
 \quad(0\le n<q,\ 0\le j<e_\kappa),
\]
with zero entries when $n<j$. Let $N_\kappa$ multiply by $t=S-\kappa$
on $\C[t]/t^{e_\kappa}$ and put
$\mathsf N=\mathsf V^{-1}(\bigoplus N_\kappa)\mathsf V$.
Define the finite increasing filtration
\begin{equation}\tag{HM25}
 M_aE_\chi=\mathsf V^{-1}\bigoplus_\kappa
 \operatorname{span}\{t^j:e_\kappa-1-2j\le a\}.
\end{equation}
This is the explicit single-Jordan-block construction in
Deligne \cite[(1.6.1), (1.6.7)]{Deligne}. Indeed $\mathsf N$ lowers
the displayed weight by two. For $a\ge0$, a nonzero source grade at
$\kappa$ occurs precisely when $a\le e_\kappa-1$ and
$a\equiv e_\kappa-1\pmod2$, and its vector is
$t^{(e_\kappa-1-a)/2}$. Multiplication by $\mathsf N^a$ sends it
with coefficient one to $t^{(e_\kappa-1+a)/2}$. Thus the exact induced
map $T_a:\operatorname{Gr}_a^M\to\operatorname{Gr}_{-a}^M$ is an
isomorphism on the stated original coordinate vectors. This proves
the nilpotent identities directly for this packet.

Order the columns $\mathsf V^{-1}e_{\kappa,j}$ by increasing weight,
with the same fixed order within ties, and call the resulting matrix $C_M$.
For $\mathcal B=C_M^*G_{k,N}C_M$, let $<$ mean all weights below $a$.
The actual metric on $M_a/M_{a-1}$ and its heat counterpart are
\begin{equation}\tag{HM26}
 \mathcal G_a=\mathcal B_{aa}
       -\mathcal B_{a<}\mathcal B_{<<}^{-1}\mathcal B_{<a},\qquad
 \mathcal G_a^{[J]}=\mathcal B_{aa}^{[J]}
       -\mathcal B_{a<}^{[J]}(\mathcal B_{<<}^{[J]})^{-1}
                                      \mathcal B_{<a}^{[J]}.
\end{equation}
For the first grade the second term is absent. These are precisely
restrictions followed by the quotient minima in (HM21): minimizing
$(v,u)^*\mathcal B(v,u)$ over $v$ gives the unique lift
$v=-\mathcal B_{<<}^{-1}\mathcal B_{<a}u$ and (HM26). Consequently
\begin{equation}\tag{HM27}
 l\mathcal G_a\preceq\mathcal G_a^{[J]}\preceq u\mathcal G_a,
 \qquad l=(1-\delta)^{2k},\quad u=(1+\delta)^{2k}.
\end{equation}
In particular no cross block between different primary states or
different nilpotent grades is suppressed. The frame here is a specified
complex coefficient frame, without an assertion that it is an
automorphism of a free split-semiring module.

Let $\lambda_{a,i}>0$ be the ordered squared singular values of $T_a$
in the actual two graded metrics, and use $\lambda_{a,i}^{[J]}$ for
the heat metrics. Explicitly the former are the eigenvalues of
$\mathcal G_a^{-1/2}T_a^*\mathcal G_{-a}T_a\mathcal G_a^{-1/2}$.
Equation (HM27) bounds the Rayleigh quotient for every nonzero vector
between $l/u$ and $u/l$ times its original value. Applying the finite
min--max formula (minimizing the maximum over each subspace of the
appropriate dimension) gives, for each ordered eigenvalue,
\begin{equation}\tag{HM28}
 \frac lu\lambda_{a,i}\le\lambda_{a,i}^{[J]}
        \le\frac ul\lambda_{a,i},\qquad
 \left|\log\frac{\lambda_{a,i}^{[J]}}{\lambda_{a,i}}\right|
 \le2k\log\frac{1+\delta}{1-\delta}.
\end{equation}
This bounds the singular values of the actual induced maps, as well as
the separate graded determinants. Iterated block elimination also gives
\begin{equation}\tag{HM29}
 \det G_{k,N}=|\det\mathsf V|^2\prod_a\det\mathcal G_a,
 \qquad
 \det G_{k,N}^{[J]}=|\det\mathsf V|^2\prod_a\det\mathcal G_a^{[J]}.
\end{equation}
Indeed $|\det C_M|=|\det\mathsf V|^{-1}$, and each elimination factor
in (HM26) is the actual Schur factor of the same full Gram.
These constructions implement Deligne's nilpotent filtration and its
metric receivers in the original sum packet. They specify the graded
operators whose estimates a mixed-cohomology argument can use. No
arithmetic Frobenius operator or weight-purity theorem is inferred from
the integers in (HM25).

On the literal quartet sum (HM20), all primary multiplicities are $e$.
Each nonzero grade therefore has dimension $(k+1)^2$, and for $a\ge0$
with $a=e-1\pmod2$, $a\le e-1$, the same ordering of roots makes
$T_a$ the identity between those two different graded spaces. Its
squared singular values are still the eigenvalues of
$\mathcal G_a^{-1/2}\mathcal G_{-a}\mathcal G_a^{-1/2}$; the two
metrics need not coincide. In particular, for the simple-quartet case
$m_0=1$, one has $e=1$, and the exact specialization is
\begin{equation}\tag{HM30}
 M_{-1}=0,\quad M_0=E_\chi,\quad \mathsf N=0,\quad
 \mathcal G_0=\mathsf V^{-*}G_{k,N}\mathsf V^{-1},\quad
 T_0=I,\quad\lambda_{0,i}=1.
