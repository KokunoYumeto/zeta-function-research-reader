\title{Split-Zero Support, Quotient--Lift Parity, and CUE Shifted-Hurwitz Defect Stacks}
\author{Working preprint draft}
\date{April 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We assemble a constructive framework connecting the split-zero semiring
$G(R)=R\sqcup\{\tau\}$, completed shifted-Hurwitz averages, fixed-$N$ CUE
first-derivative Mellin measures, finite Weyl-Heisenberg phase space, and the
first nontrivial $N=3$ phase-cover obstruction.  The central technical point is a
three-layer parity distinction: function parity, differential parity, and endpoint
residue-sector parity are not the same operation.  The residue map reverses the
parity of the quotient/lift functions because the involution $s\mapsto 1-s$ sends
$ds$ to $-ds$.  This identifies the finite two-end defect of the completed shifted
Hurwitz transform with the anti-invariant lift pole $v/u$, not with the quotient
pole $1/u$.

For compactly supported measures $\mu$, we prove the principal-part formula
\[
\PP_{\{0,1\}}\,\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\int\zeta(s,1+ty)\,d\mu(y)
=(1+t m_1(\mu))\frac1u-tm_1(\mu)\frac vu,
\]
where $u=s(s-1)$ and $v=2s-1$.  We then prove all-order local zero-motion
recursions for compact Hurwitz averages, and specialize them to the CUE
first-derivative measures.  The fixed-$N$ CUE measure realizes
$M_N(k)=\mathbb E_{U(N)}|\Phi_N'(1)|^{2k}$ as a compact Mellin transform on
$[0,N^24^{N-1}]$ and admits a Bernstein-Hausdorff finite atomic reconstruction.
The associated finite Hurwitz approximants converge locally uniformly away from
$s=1$, and their zeros converge by Hurwitz/Rouche theory.

We also prove the finite phase-space theorem: for a finite abelian group $A$ of
order $r$, the nonidentity Weyl operators indexed by $A\times\widehat A$ form an
orthogonal basis of $\mathfrak{sl}_r(\C)$.  For first-derivative $s$-fold CUE
moments, the Grover--Mezzadri--Simm microscopic exponent is
$s^2+2s=\dim\mathfrak{sl}_{s+1}$, and it is realized by the first-jet block
triplet $\End(W)\oplus W\oplus W^\vee\cong\mathfrak{sl}(W\oplus\C)$,
$\dim W=s$.  Finally, at $N=3$, we record the quadratic phase-cover trace algebra
$\R[u,X]\oplus\R[u,X]J$, $J^2=-S$, and prove the trace-orthogonal real/imaginary
splitting and the quotient obstruction: the descended kernel $\Psi_2^\#(u,v)$ is
not a sum of squares in $\R[u,v]$ although the lifted phase-cover kernel is a
finite Hermitian-square sum.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The purpose of this note is to turn a collection of compatible constructions into a
single precise preprint-level framework.  Three themes are kept separate.

First, the split-zero semiring
\[
G(R)=R\sqcup\{\tau\},\qquad 0_{G(R)}=\tau,
\]
separates the unsupported zero $\tau$ from the supported arithmetic zero
$0_R\in R\subset G(R)$.  This is not a linguistic distinction: it is measured by two
canonical maps, the arithmetic reflection $p_R:G(R)\to R$ and the support
character $\chi_R:G(R)\to\B$.  The secondary split-zero note proves, beyond the
baseline ideal and localization package, the support-diagram form of semimodules,
the Boolean-cubical form of free semimodules, the product theorem
$G(A\times B)\simeq G(A)\times_{\B}G(B)$, and the universal shifted-zeta operator
$D=sT$ governing the all-order deformation of the separated quotient-size sheet
\cite{SZ2}.

Second, the quotient/lift involution
\[
 u=s(s-1),\qquad v=2s-1,
\]
produces an exact parity calculus.  The quotient function $1/u$ is invariant under
$s\mapsto 1-s$ and the lift function $v/u$ is anti-invariant.  However, residues are
residues of differentials.  Since $ds\mapsto -ds$, the residue vector of $1/u$ is
anti-invariant and the residue vector of $v/u$ is invariant.  This is the sign hinge
which aligns the visible two-end endpoint defect with the anti-invariant lift
coefficient.

Third, the CUE first-derivative moment problem supplies a compact-measure
incarnation of the same quotient/lift pattern.  The recent CUE derivative moment
paper of Grover, Mezzadri, and Simm proves, for higher derivative lists, determinant
asymptotics with Kostka coefficients and a microscopic exponent
$|\mu|+|\nu|+s^2$ when $\ell(\mu)=\ell(\nu)=s$ \cite{GMS}.  The companion
compact Mellin peeling note proves that, for fixed $N$, the noninteger family
$k\mapsto M_N(k)=\mathbb E|\Phi_N'(1)|^{2k}$ is not guessed from the integer
determinant formula; it is the Mellin transform of an explicitly compactly
supported probability measure on $[0,N^24^{N-1}]$, reconstructed from its integer
moments by Bernstein-Hausdorff inversion \cite{CMP}.  At $N=3$, the same note
identifies a phase cover where positivity is represented by Hermitian squares, while
the descended quotient polynomial $\Psi_2^\#$ is not a real sum of squares
\cite{CMP}.

The paper is deliberately algebraic.  No claim is made here that the CUE phase
cover is equal to an $A_2$ lattice, nor that the first-derivative moment exponent by
itself proves a representation theorem.  What is proved is the following rigorous
package: a parity reversal theorem; a completed Hurwitz principal-part theorem;
a residue-inflow theorem; all-order local zero deformation recursions; finite
Bernstein-Hurwitz zero approximation; a finite Heisenberg/Weyl basis theorem for
$\mathfrak{sl}_r$; a first-jet block realization of the moment-order exponent; and a
quadratic trace-algebra model for the $N=3$ phase-cover obstruction.

\section{The split-zero support package}

\subsection{The split-zero semiring and the unique support character}

\begin{definition}
Let $R$ be a nonzero commutative ring with identity.  Define
\[
G(R):=R\sqcup\{\tau\}
\]
with addition and multiplication
\[
r\oplus s=r+s,\qquad r\oplus\tau=\tau\oplus r=r,\qquad \tau\oplus\tau=\tau,
\]
\[
rs=rs,
\qquad r\tau=\tau r=\tau,
\qquad \tau^2=\tau.
\]
Then $0_{G(R)}=\tau$ and $1_{G(R)}=1_R$.  We write
\[
e:=0_R\in R\subset G(R).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[coordinate normal form]
There is a canonical semiring isomorphism
\[
G(R)\simeq D_R:=\{(0,0)\}\cup(R\times\{1\})\subset R\times\B
\]
given by
\[
\tau\mapsto(0,0),\qquad r\mapsto(r,1).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The indicated map is bijective.  Addition and multiplication in $R\times\B$ restrict
to
\[
(r,1)+(s,1)=(r+s,1),\quad (r,1)+(0,0)=(r,1),\quad (0,0)+(0,0)=(0,0),
\]
\[
(r,1)(s,1)=(rs,1),\quad (r,1)(0,0)=(0,0),\quad (0,0)^2=(0,0),
\]
which are precisely the defining operations of $G(R)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}
The arithmetic reflection and support character are
\[
p_R:G(R)\to R,
\qquad p_R(\tau)=0_R,\quad p_R(r)=r,
\]
and
\[
\chi_R:G(R)\to\B,
\qquad \chi_R(\tau)=0,
\qquad \chi_R(r)=1\quad(r\in R).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[unique Boolean support character]
There is exactly one semiring homomorphism $G(R)\to\B$.  It is $\chi_R$.
Consequently
\[
\chi_R(\tau)=0,
\qquad
\chi_R(0_R)=1,
\qquad
\chi_R(r)=1\quad(r\in R).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $f:G(R)\to\B$ be a semiring homomorphism.  Since $\tau$ is the semiring zero,
$f(\tau)=0$.  Since $1_R$ is the unit, $f(1_R)=1$.  Inside the supported copy $R$ one
has $0_R=1_R+(-1_R)$, hence in $G(R)$,
\[
0_R=1_R\oplus(-1_R).
\]
Therefore
\[
f(0_R)=f(1_R)+f(-1_R)=1+f(-1_R)=1
\]
in the Boolean semiring.  If $r\in R$ and $f(r)=0$, then
\[
1=f(0_R)=f(r0_R)=f(r)f(0_R)=0,
\]
which is impossible.  Hence $f(r)=1$ for every $r\in R$, so $f=\chi_R$.
\end{proof}

Thus $p_R^{-1}(0_R)=\{\tau,0_R\}$, while
\[
\chi_R^{-1}(0)=\{\tau\},\qquad \chi_R^{-1}(1)=R.
\]
The ring-zero $0_R$ is a supported zero; $\tau$ is the unique unsupported element.
The excluded coordinates $(r,0)$, $r\ne0$, are precisely the forbidden unsupported
nonzero states.


\subsection{Separated quotient-size zeta sheet}

For $S=G(\Z)$, the ordinary ideal sheet and the separated support sheet differ.
Let
\[
I_n:=n\Z\cup\{\tau\}\qquad (n\ge 1).
\]
On the collapsed arithmetic sheet, the quotient-size norm is $N(I_n)=n$, so the
ordinary ideal zeta sheet is
\[
\zeta^{\rm idl}_{G(\Z)}(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-s}=\zeta(s).
\]
On the separated support sheet, the quotient $G(\Z/n\Z)$ has $n+1$ elements: the
$n$ supported residue classes and the unsupported point $\tau$.  Hence
\[
\zeta^{\rm sep}_{G(\Z)}(s)
=
\sum_{n\ge1}(n+1)^{-s}
=
\zeta(s)-1.
\]
This sheet is not Eulerian: the weight $n\mapsto n+1$ is not multiplicative.  Thus
the support deformation is not a deformation of classical ideal multiplication; it is
a deformation of support bookkeeping.

\subsection{Support of free semimodules}

The full classification of $G(R)$-semimodules is a support-diagram theorem in the
secondary note \cite{SZ2}.  We only need the following finite free consequence.

\begin{proposition}[Boolean support cube of a free semimodule]
Let $S=G(R)$ and consider $S^n$.  For $x=(x_1,\dots,x_n)\in S^n$, define its support
\[
\operatorname{supp}(x):=\{i:x_i\in R\}\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}.
\]
For each $I\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}$, the fiber
\[
(S^n)_I:=\{x\in S^n: \operatorname{supp}(x)=I\}
\]
is naturally identified with $R^I$.  If $I\subseteq J$, adding supported zeros in the
coordinates $J\setminus I$ gives the transport map $R^I\to R^J$.  Thus the support
skeleton of $S^n$ is the Boolean cube of subsets $I\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}$ carrying
ordinary $R$-modules on its fibers.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A coordinate of $S$ is either unsupported, namely $\tau$, or supported, namely an
element of $R$.  Fixing the set $I$ of supported coordinates leaves exactly one
unsupported value $\tau$ in all complementary coordinates and arbitrary values of $R$
in coordinates belonging to $I$.  This identifies the fiber with $R^I$.
If $I\subseteq J$, the map $R^I\to R^J$ sends $(r_i)_{i\in I}$ to the vector whose
coordinates in $I$ are $r_i$ and whose coordinates in $J\setminus I$ are $0_R$.
The addition law in $G(R)$ makes $\tau\oplus 0_R=0_R$, so this is exactly the effect
of adding supported zeros in the new coordinates.
\end{proof}

This support-cube observation is used later to distinguish three different operations
which are often conflated informally: a zero scalar coefficient, an unsupported sector,
and trace removal of an identity sector.

\section{Quotient/lift parity and residue reversal}

Let
\[
u=s(s-1),\qquad v=2s-1.
\]
Then
\[
u(1-s)=u(s),\qquad v(1-s)=-v(s),\qquad v^2=1+4u.
\]
Set
\[
K=\C(u),\qquad L=\C(s)=K(v),
\]
with deck involution $\sigma(s)=1-s$ and $\sigma(v)=-v$.

Let
\[
\mathcal P=\left\{\frac a s+\frac b{s-1}:a,b\in\C\right\}.
\]
Define
\[
q:=\frac1u=\frac1{s-1}-\frac1s,
\qquad
\ell:=\frac vu=\frac1{s-1}+\frac1s.
\]
Then $\sigma(q)=q$ and $\sigma(\ell)=-\ell$.  Hence
\[
\mathcal P=\mathcal P^+_{\func}\oplus\mathcal P^-_{\func},
\qquad
\mathcal P^+_{\func}=\C q,
\qquad
\mathcal P^-_{\func}=\C\ell.
\]

Let $E=\C e_0\oplus\C e_1$ and let $\tau_E(e_0)=e_1$, $\tau_E(e_1)=e_0$.
Define
\[
\operatorname{res}:\mathcal P\,ds\to E
\]
by
\[
\operatorname{res}(P(s)\,ds)=\Res_{s=0}(P(s)\,ds)e_0+
\Res_{s=1}(P(s)\,ds)e_1.
\]

\begin{theorem}[residue parity reversal]
One has
\[
\operatorname{res}(q\,ds)=-e_0+e_1,
\qquad
\operatorname{res}(\ell\,ds)=e_0+e_1.
\]
Consequently the residue map reverses the function parity:
\[
q\in\mathcal P^+_{\func}\mapsto E^- ,
\qquad
\ell\in\mathcal P^-_{\func}\mapsto E^+ .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The partial-fraction formulae give
\[
q=\frac1{s-1}-\frac1s,
\qquad
\ell=\frac1{s-1}+\frac1s.
\]
Thus
\[
\Res_{s=0}q\,ds=-1,
\quad
\Res_{s=1}q\,ds=1,
\]
and
\[
\Res_{s=0}\ell\,ds=1,
\quad
\Res_{s=1}\ell\,ds=1.
\]
Therefore
\[
\operatorname{res}(q\,ds)=-e_0+e_1,
\qquad
\operatorname{res}(\ell\,ds)=e_0+e_1.
\]
The vector $-e_0+e_1$ is anti-invariant under endpoint transposition, while
$e_0+e_1$ is invariant.

The structural reason is that residues belong to differentials.  Since
\[
\sigma^*(ds)=d(1-s)=-ds,
\]
we have
\[
\sigma^*(q\,ds)=-q\,ds,
\qquad
\sigma^*(\ell\,ds)=(-\ell)(-ds)=\ell\,ds.
\]
Thus differential parity is function parity multiplied by the orientation character.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[finite endpoint defect detects the lift]
Define
\[
\mathfrak D_{\Fin}(P)=\Res_{s=0}(P\,ds)+\Res_{s=1}(P\,ds).
\]
Then
\[
\mathfrak D_{\Fin}(q)=0,
\qquad
\mathfrak D_{\Fin}(\ell)=2.
\]
Thus the visible two-end residue vector $e_0+e_1$ is the residue-sector image of the
function-anti-invariant lift pole $v/u$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The result is immediate from the previous theorem:
$(-1)+1=0$ and $1+1=2$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Projective residue inflow}

Since $u=s(s-1)$ as a rational function on $\mathbb P^1_s$ has divisor
\[
(u)=[0]+[1]-2[\infty],
\]
and $du=v\,ds$, one has
\[
\ell\,ds=\frac vu\,ds=d\log u.
\]

\begin{theorem}[projective inflow]
For every $B\in\C$, the differential $B\ell\,ds=Bd\log u$ has finite endpoint
residue sum $2B$ and residue at infinity $-2B$.  Hence the total projective residue
is zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The logarithmic differential $d\log u$ has residues equal to the orders of $u$ in its
divisor.  Therefore
\[
\Res_{0}d\log u=1,
\quad
\Res_{1}d\log u=1,
\quad
\Res_{\infty}d\log u=-2.
\]
Multiplying by $B$ gives the result.
\end{proof}

The finite two-end defect is therefore not destroyed; it is balanced by projective
infinity.  This is the analytic counterpart of a global support boundary.


\section{Universal shifted Dirichlet deformation}

Let
\[
L(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}a_n n^{-s}
\]
be a Dirichlet series in a half-plane of absolute convergence, and for $|t|<1$ set
\[
L_t(s):=\sum_{n\ge1}a_n(n+t)^{-s}.
\]
Let $T$ be the shift operator $(Tf)(s)=f(s+1)$ and define
\[
D:=sT.
\]

\begin{theorem}[universal shift flow]
In every common domain of absolute convergence,
\[
L_t(s)=\sum_{m\ge0}(-1)^m\frac{(s)_m}{m!}t^mL(s+m)=e^{-tD}L(s),
\]
and, for every $n\ge0$,
\[
\partial_t^nL_t(s)=(-1)^n(s)_nL_t(s+n).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each $n\ge1$,
\[
(n+t)^{-s}=n^{-s}\left(1+\frac tn\right)^{-s}
=n^{-s}\sum_{m\ge0}(-1)^m\frac{(s)_m}{m!}\left(\frac tn\right)^m
\]
when $|t|<1$.  Absolute convergence permits summation over $n$, giving the first
identity.  The operator identity follows because $D^mL(s)=(s)_mL(s+m)$.  The
derivative formula follows by differentiating $(n+t)^{-s}$ termwise:
\[
\partial_t^n(n+t)^{-s}=(-1)^n(s)_n(n+t)^{-s-n}.
\]
Summing over $n$ gives the assertion.
\end{proof}

The shifted-Hurwitz and split-zero support sheets below are specializations of this
universal flow.  The endpoint $t=1$ is singular from the Euler-product viewpoint even
when the meromorphic function itself is elementary, e.g. $\zeta(s,2)=\zeta(s)-1$.

\section{Completed shifted-Hurwitz averages}

Let $\mu$ be a compactly supported probability measure on $[0,\infty)$ and write
\[
m_n(\mu)=\int y^n\,d\mu(y).
\]
For $t\ge0$, define
\[
Z_{\mu,t}(s)=\int_0^\infty \zeta(s,1+ty)\,d\mu(y),
\]
and
\[
\Lambda_{\mu,t}(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)Z_{\mu,t}(s).
\]

\begin{theorem}[residues of the completed shifted-Hurwitz average]
One has
\[
\Res_{s=1}\Lambda_{\mu,t}=1,
\qquad
\Res_{s=0}\Lambda_{\mu,t}=-1-2t m_1(\mu).
\]
Consequently
\[
\Res_{s=0}\Lambda_{\mu,t}+\Res_{s=1}\Lambda_{\mu,t}=-2t m_1(\mu).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $a>0$, the Hurwitz zeta function satisfies
\[
\zeta(s,a)=\frac1{s-1}+O(1)\quad(s\to1)
\]
with residue $1$.  Since $\mu$ is a probability measure, $Z_{\mu,t}$ has residue $1$
at $s=1$.  Moreover
\[
\pi^{-1/2}\Gamma(1/2)=1,
\]
so the residue of $\Lambda_{\mu,t}$ at $s=1$ is $1$.

At $s=0$,
\[
\zeta(0,a)=\frac12-a,
\]
so
\[
Z_{\mu,t}(0)=\int\left(\frac12-(1+ty)\right)d\mu(y)=-\frac12-tm_1(\mu).
\]
Also
\[
\Gamma(s/2)=\frac2s+O(1),
\qquad
\pi^{-s/2}=1+O(s).
\]
Thus
\[
\Res_{s=0}\Lambda_{\mu,t}=2Z_{\mu,t}(0)=-1-2t m_1(\mu).
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[quotient/lift principal part]
The principal part at $\{0,1\}$ is
\[
\PP_{\{0,1\}}\Lambda_{\mu,t}
=(1+t m_1(\mu))\frac1u-tm_1(\mu)\frac vu.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\PP_{\{0,1\}}\Lambda_{\mu,t}=A_{\mu,t}q+B_{\mu,t}\ell,
\qquad
A_{\mu,t}=1+t m_1(\mu),
\qquad
B_{\mu,t}=-t m_1(\mu).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write the principal part as
\[
P(s)=\frac a s+\frac b{s-1}.
\]
By the previous theorem,
\[
a=-1-2t m_1(\mu),
\qquad
b=1.
\]
Since
\[
Aq+B\ell=A\left(\frac1{s-1}-\frac1s\right)+B\left(\frac1{s-1}+\frac1s\right),
\]
we have
\[
a=-A+B,
\qquad
b=A+B.
\]
Solving gives
\[
A=\frac{b-a}{2}=1+t m_1(\mu),
\qquad
B=\frac{a+b}{2}=-t m_1(\mu).
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[finite and projective defects]
The finite endpoint defect of the principal part is
\[
\mathfrak D_{\Fin}(\PP_{\{0,1\}}\Lambda_{\mu,t})=-2t m_1(\mu),
\]
and the residue at projective infinity of the lift term is $+2t m_1(\mu)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The finite defect of $Aq+B\ell$ is $2B$ by the residue parity theorem, so it equals
$-2tm_1(\mu)$.  The infinity residue of $B\ell\,ds=Bd\log u$ is $-2B=2tm_1(\mu)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[critical-line splitting]
At $s=1/2+i\gamma$,
\[
u=-\left(\gamma^2+\frac14\right),
\qquad
v=2i\gamma.
\]
Hence
\[
\PP_{\{0,1\}}\Lambda_{\mu,t}\left(\frac12+i\gamma\right)
=-\frac{1+t m_1(\mu)}{\gamma^2+1/4}
+i\frac{2t m_1(\mu)\gamma}{\gamma^2+1/4}.
\]
The quotient term is real and the lift term is imaginary.
\end{corollary}

\section{Quadratic trace orthogonality}

\begin{lemma}[quadratic trace form]
Let $A=R\oplus R\eta$ with $\eta^2=\Delta\in R$ and involution
$\sigma(a+b\eta)=a-b\eta$.  Define
\[
\langle x,y\rangle_A=\frac12\Tr_{A/R}(x\sigma(y)).
\]
Then for $x=a+b\eta$ and $y=c+d\eta$,
\[
\langle x,y\rangle_A=ac-bd\Delta.
\]
In particular $A^+=R$ and $A^-=R\eta$ are orthogonal.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We compute
\[
(a+b\eta)(c-d\eta)=ac-bd\eta^2+(bc-ad)\eta=ac-bd\Delta+(bc-ad)\eta.
\]
Since $\Tr_{A/R}(r+s\eta)=2r$, the formula follows.  Orthogonality follows by
setting either $b=c=0$ or $a=d=0$.
\end{proof}

Apply this to
\[
A_z=\R[u,v]/(v^2-1-4u),
\qquad \sigma(v)=-v.
\]
On the critical line,
\[
A_{z,\gamma}=\R[v]/(v^2+4\gamma^2).
\]
For $\gamma\ne0$, this is $\C$ by $v\mapsto 2i\gamma$.

\begin{corollary}
For $\gamma\ne0$,
\[
A_{z,\gamma}^+=\R,
\qquad
A_{z,\gamma}^-=\R v=i\R,
\]
and
\[
\langle a+bv,a+bv\rangle=a^2+4\gamma^2b^2.
\]
In particular $1/u$ and $v/u$ are trace-orthogonal on the critical-line fiber.
\end{corollary}

\section{All-order zero deformation for compact Hurwitz averages}

Let $\mu$ be compactly supported on $[0,L]$.  For $|t|L<1$, one has
\[
Z_{\mu,t}(s)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty (-t)^n\frac{(s)_n}{n!}m_n(\mu)\zeta(s+n),
\]
where $(s)_n=s(s+1)\cdots(s+n-1)$.

\begin{theorem}[recursive motion of a simple zero]
Let $\rho$ be a simple nontrivial zero of $\zeta(s)$.  Define
\[
F_n(s)=(-1)^n\frac{(s)_n}{n!}m_n(\mu)\zeta(s+n).
\]
Then, for sufficiently small $t$, there is a unique holomorphic branch
\[
\rho_\mu(t)=\rho+\sum_{N\ge1}c_Nt^N
\]
such that
\[
Z_{\mu,t}(\rho_\mu(t))=0.
\]
Let
\[
a_{n,j}=\frac1{j!}F_n^{(j)}(\rho).
\]
Then $a_{0,1}=\zeta'(\rho)\ne0$, and the coefficients satisfy
\[
c_N=-\frac1{\zeta'(\rho)}
\sum_{\substack{n,j\ge0\\(n,j)\ne(0,1)}}
 a_{n,j}\,[t^N]\left(t^n\left(\sum_{m=1}^{N-1}c_mt^m\right)^j\right).
\]
In particular
\[
c_1=m_1(\mu)\frac{\rho\zeta(\rho+1)}{\zeta'(\rho)},
\]
and
\[
c_2=-\frac{\frac12\zeta''(\rho)c_1^2+F_1'(\rho)c_1+F_2(\rho)}{\zeta'(\rho)},
\]
where
\[
F_1(s)=-s m_1(\mu)\zeta(s+1),
\qquad
F_2(s)=\frac{s(s+1)}2m_2(\mu)\zeta(s+2).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Taylor expansion in $t$ is locally normally convergent near $(\rho,0)$ because
$|t|L<1$ and $\rho+n\ne1$ for all $n\ge0$.  At $t=0$ we have
$Z_{\mu,0}(s)=\zeta(s)$ and
\[
\partial_sZ_{\mu,0}(\rho)=\zeta'(\rho)\ne0.
\]
The holomorphic implicit function theorem gives the unique branch.
Write
\[
w(t)=\rho_\mu(t)-\rho=\sum_{N\ge1}c_Nt^N.
\]
Expanding at $s=\rho$ gives
\[
0=Z_{\mu,t}(\rho+w(t))=\sum_{n,j\ge0}a_{n,j}t^n w(t)^j.
\]
Since $a_{0,0}=\zeta(\rho)=0$ and $a_{0,1}=\zeta'(\rho)$, the coefficient of $t^N$ is
\[
\zeta'(\rho)c_N+
\sum_{\substack{n,j\ge0\\(n,j)\ne(0,1)}}
a_{n,j}[t^N](t^nw(t)^j).
\]
The second term depends only on $c_1,\dots,c_{N-1}$, so solving gives the
recursion.  The displayed formulae for $c_1$ and $c_2$ are the cases $N=1,2$.
\end{proof}

Thus the pole principal part sees only $m_1(\mu)$, while zero motion sees the full
moment sequence $m_1(\mu),m_2(\mu),\dots$.

\section{CUE fixed-$N$ compact Mellin measures and Hurwitz zero stacks}

Let
\[
\Phi_N(z)=\det(zI-U),\qquad U\sim\Haar(U(N)),
\]
and
\[
X_N(U)=|\Phi_N'(1)|^2,
\qquad
\nu_N=(X_N)_*\Haar.
\]
Let
\[
M_N(k)=\mathbb E_{U(N)}|\Phi_N'(1)|^{2k}=\int t^k\,d\nu_N(t)
\]
whenever the moment exists.

\begin{theorem}[exact compact support]
For $N\ge2$,
\[
\supp(\nu_N)=[0,L_N],
\qquad
L_N=N^24^{N-1}.
\]
Consequently, for every $k\ge0$,
\[
M_N(k)=\int_0^{L_N}t^k\,d\nu_N(t).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $z_1,\dots,z_N\in S^1$ be the eigenvalues of $U$.  Then
\[
\Phi_N(z)=\prod_{j=1}^N(z-z_j),
\]
so
\[
\Phi_N'(1)=\sum_{m=1}^N\prod_{j\ne m}(1-z_j).
\]
Since $|1-z_j|\le2$,
\[
|\Phi_N'(1)|\le N2^{N-1},
\]
hence $0\le X_N\le N^24^{N-1}=L_N$.
At $U=I_N$, $\Phi_N(z)=(z-1)^N$, so $\Phi_N'(1)=0$ for $N\ge2$.
At $U=-I_N$, $\Phi_N(z)=(z+1)^N$, so $|\Phi_N'(1)|=N2^{N-1}$.
The group $U(N)$ is compact and connected, and $X_N$ is continuous.  Hence
$X_N(U(N))$ is a compact connected subset of $\R$ containing both endpoints, so it
is $[0,L_N]$.  Haar measure has full support, so the support of the pushforward is
the full image.
\end{proof}

Normalize $\widetilde\nu_N=(t\mapsto t/L_N)_*\nu_N$ and set
\[
c_{N,n}=\int_0^1x^n\,d\widetilde\nu_N(x)=\frac{M_N(n)}{L_N^n}.
\]
For $m\ge0$ and $0\le j\le m$, define
\[
\lambda_{N,m,j}:=\binom mj(-1)^{m-j}\Delta^{m-j}c_{N,j}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Bernstein-Hausdorff reconstruction]
One has
\[
\lambda_{N,m,j}=\binom mj\int_0^1x^j(1-x)^{m-j}\,d\widetilde\nu_N(x)\ge0,
\qquad
\sum_{j=0}^m\lambda_{N,m,j}=1.
\]
The atomic measures
\[
\widetilde\nu_{N,m}=\sum_{j=0}^m\lambda_{N,m,j}\delta_{j/m}
\]
converge weakly to $\widetilde\nu_N$.  Equivalently, for every continuous
$f:[0,1]\to\C$,
\[
\int_0^1 f(x)\,d\widetilde\nu_N(x)
=\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{j=0}^m f(j/m)\lambda_{N,m,j}.
\]
Consequently, for every $k\ge0$,
\[
\frac{M_N(k)}{L_N^k}=\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{j=0}^m\left(\frac jm\right)^k\lambda_{N,m,j}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $r\ge0$,
\[
(-1)^r\Delta^r c_{N,n}=\int_0^1x^n(1-x)^r\,d\widetilde\nu_N(x).
\]
Indeed,
\[
(-1)^r\Delta^r c_{N,n}
=\sum_{j=0}^r\binom rj(-1)^j c_{N,n+j}
=\int_0^1x^n\left(\sum_{j=0}^r\binom rj(-x)^j\right)d\widetilde\nu_N(x).
\]
The bracket equals $(1-x)^r$.  Taking $r=m-j$ gives the formula for
$\lambda_{N,m,j}$.  Summing over $j$ uses the binomial identity
\[
\sum_{j=0}^m\binom mjx^j(1-x)^{m-j}=1.
\]
For the weak convergence, let $B_mf$ be the Bernstein polynomial
\[
(B_mf)(x)=\sum_{j=0}^m f(j/m)\binom mjx^j(1-x)^{m-j}.
\]
Then
\[
\sum_{j=0}^m f(j/m)\lambda_{N,m,j}=\int_0^1(B_mf)(x)\,d\widetilde\nu_N(x).
\]
The Bernstein approximation theorem gives $\|B_mf-f\|_\infty\to0$, proving the
claim.  Taking $f(x)=x^k$ gives the final formula.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The CUE shifted-Hurwitz stack}

Let $\mu_N$ be either the unpeeled measure $\nu_N$ or the peeled measure
$(x\mapsto x/N^2)_*\nu_N$.  For a compact support interval $[0,L]$ define
\[
Z_{N,t}(s)=\int_0^L\zeta(s,1+tx)\,d\mu_N(x).
\]
The completed transform is treated by the principal-part theorem above.

For a finite Bernstein approximant, normalize $\mu_N$ to a probability measure
$\widetilde\mu_N$ on $[0,1]$, with Bernstein weights $\lambda_{N,m,j}$, and set
\[
Z_{N,t}^{[m]}(s)=\sum_{j=0}^m\lambda_{N,m,j}\,
\zeta\left(s,1+tL\frac jm\right).
\]

\begin{theorem}[finite Bernstein-Hurwitz zero stack]
For fixed $N$ and fixed $t\ge0$,
\[
Z_{N,t}^{[m]}(s)\to Z_{N,t}(s)
\]
locally uniformly on compact subsets of $\C\setminus\{1\}$.  Hence if
$\Omega\Subset\C\setminus\{1\}$ has piecewise smooth boundary and $Z_{N,t}$ has no
zeros on $\partial\Omega$, then for all sufficiently large $m$, $Z_{N,t}^{[m]}$ and
$Z_{N,t}$ have the same number of zeros in $\Omega$, counted with multiplicity.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a compact $K\subset\C\setminus\{1\}$, the function
\[
f_s(x)=\zeta(s,1+tLx)
\]
is continuous on $[0,1]$, holomorphic in $s$, and uniformly bounded on
$K\times[0,1]$.  Weak convergence of $\widetilde\nu_{N,m}$ to $\widetilde\nu_N$ gives
uniform convergence of the integrals on $K$.  The zero-counting assertion follows from
Rouche's theorem applied on $\partial\Omega$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{First CUE moment and peeled endpoint defect}

\begin{proposition}[exact first CUE derivative moment]
For every $N\ge2$,
\[
M_N(1)=\mathbb E|\Phi_N'(1)|^2=\frac{N(N+1)(2N+1)}6.
\]
For the peeled measure $Y_N=X_N/N^2$, one has
\[
\mathbb E Y_N=\frac{(N+1)(2N+1)}{6N}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write
\[
\Phi_N(z)=\sum_{j=0}^N(-1)^j e_j(U)z^{N-j},
\]
where $e_j$ is the $j$th elementary symmetric polynomial in the eigenvalues of $U$.
Then
\[
\Phi_N'(1)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(-1)^j(N-j)e_j(U).
\]
The functions $e_j$ are irreducible $U(N)$-characters and are orthonormal under Haar
measure, so
\[
\mathbb E[e_j(U)\overline{e_\ell(U)}]=\delta_{j\ell}.
\]
Therefore
\[
M_N(1)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(N-j)^2=\sum_{m=1}^Nm^2=\frac{N(N+1)(2N+1)}6.
\]
Dividing by $N^2$ gives the peeled first moment.
\end{proof}

Thus, for the peeled shifted-Hurwitz CUE measure, the primitive lift coefficient at
time $t$ is
\[
-t\frac{(N+1)(2N+1)}{6N},
\]
and the finite two-end residue defect is
\[
-2t\frac{(N+1)(2N+1)}{6N}.
\]
For $N=3$, this is $-14t/9$ at the primitive lift and $-28t/9$ at the finite two-end
defect.

\section{Finite Weyl phase space and the first-jet triplet}

\subsection{Finite Heisenberg-Weyl basis}

Let $A$ be a finite abelian group of order $r$, let $\widehat A=\Hom(A,\C^\times)$,
and set $H_A=\C[A]$.  For $a\in A$ and $\chi\in\widehat A$, define
\[
T_a e_x=e_{a+x},
\qquad
M_\chi e_x=\chi(x)e_x,
\qquad
W_{a,\chi}=T_aM_\chi.
\]

\begin{theorem}[finite phase-space basis]
The operators $W_{a,\chi}$ form an orthogonal basis of $\End(H_A)$ for the
Hilbert-Schmidt trace form.  More precisely,
\[
\Tr(W_{a,\chi}^*W_{b,\psi})=r\delta_{a,b}\delta_{\chi,\psi}.
\]
Moreover $W_{0,1}=I$, every other $W_{a,\chi}$ is traceless, and
\[
\Span_\C\{W_{a,\chi}:(a,\chi)\ne(0,1)\}=\mathfrak{sl}(H_A)\simeq\mathfrak{sl}_r(\C).
\]
The commutator is
\[
[W_{a,\chi},W_{b,\psi}]=(\chi(b)-\psi(a))W_{a+b,\chi\psi}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since $M_\chi T_b=\chi(b)T_bM_\chi$,
\[
W_{a,\chi}W_{b,\psi}=T_aM_\chi T_bM_\psi=\chi(b)T_{a+b}M_{\chi\psi}
=\chi(b)W_{a+b,\chi\psi}.
\]
Interchanging the pairs gives the commutator formula.

Also
\[
W_{a,\chi}e_x=\chi(x)e_{a+x}.
\]
This contributes to the trace only if $a=0$.  If $a=0$, then
\[
\Tr(W_{0,\chi})=\sum_{x\in A}\chi(x),
\]
which is $r$ for $\chi=1$ and $0$ otherwise.  Applying the same trace computation
to $W_{a,\chi}^*W_{b,\psi}$ gives Hilbert-Schmidt orthogonality.  There are $r^2$
operators, equal to $\dim\End(H_A)$, so they form a basis.  The nonidentity basis
elements have trace zero, and their number is $r^2-1=\dim\mathfrak{sl}_r(\C)$.
\end{proof}

For $A=\Z/r\Z$, with $\omega=e^{2\pi i/r}$,
\[
Xe_j=e_{j+1},\qquad Ze_j=\omega^j e_j,
\qquad ZX=\omega XZ,
\]
and
\[
X^aZ^b=\sum_{j\in\Z/r\Z}\omega^{bj}E_{j+a,j}.
\]
Thus the $a=0$ sectors Fourier-span the diagonal Cartan subalgebra, while each
$a\ne0$ sector Fourier-spans the fixed-displacement root-space slice.

\subsection{First-derivative moment exponent and the $\mathfrak{sl}_{s+1}$ triplet}

Grover--Mezzadri--Simm prove that, in the microscopic/unit-circle regime with
$\ell(\mu)=\ell(\nu)=s$, the leading exponent of $N$ is
\[
|\mu|+|\nu|+s^2
\]
with leading constants expressed by Kostka-number determinant formulae and strict
positivity established through Schur-positive Andreief integrals \cite{GMS}.

\begin{theorem}[first-derivative exponent]
For the $s$-fold first-derivative moment
\[
\mu=\nu=(1^s),
\]
the microscopic CUE exponent is
\[
s^2+2s=(s+1)^2-1=\dim\mathfrak{sl}_{s+1}(\C).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $\mu=\nu=(1^s)$, one has $|\mu|=|\nu|=s$.  Substituting in the
Grover--Mezzadri--Simm exponent gives
\[
|\mu|+|\nu|+s^2=s+s+s^2=s^2+2s.
\]
The identity $s^2+2s=(s+1)^2-1=\dim\mathfrak{sl}_{s+1}(\C)$ is immediate.
\end{proof}

This identity is numerical until a functor from the Kostka determinant expression to a
specific $\mathfrak{sl}_{s+1}$ representation-theoretic invariant is constructed.  The next
result gives the exact block object whose dimension is the same exponent.

\begin{theorem}[first-jet block triplet]
Let $W=\C^s$.  The map
\[
\Theta:\End(W)\oplus W\oplus W^\vee\to\mathfrak{sl}(W\oplus\C)
\]
given by
\[
\Theta(A,u,\varphi)=
\begin{pmatrix}
A & u\\
\varphi & -\Tr(A)
\end{pmatrix}
\]
is a vector-space isomorphism.  Hence
\[
\dim(\End(W)\oplus W\oplus W^\vee)=s^2+2s=\dim\mathfrak{sl}_{s+1}(\C).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every traceless endomorphism of $W\oplus\C$ has a unique block form
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
B & u\\
\varphi & \lambda
\end{pmatrix},
\]
where $B\in\End(W)$, $u\in W$, $\varphi\in W^\vee$, and $\lambda\in\C$, with trace
condition $\Tr(B)+\lambda=0$.  Thus $\lambda=-\Tr(B)$ and the displayed map is
bijective.  The dimension count is $s^2+s+s=s^2+2s$.
\end{proof}

Taking $r=s+1$, the finite phase-space theorem supplies a second realization of the
same number:
\[
|\Z_r\times\widehat{\Z_r}|-1=r^2-1=(s+1)^2-1.
\]
The exact statement is not that the CUE constants are already an $\mathfrak{sl}_{s+1}$
character.  The exact theorem is that the exponent is the dimension of the first-jet
triplet and of the nonidentity finite Weyl phase space.

\section{The $N=3$ phase cover and quotient obstruction}

This section formalizes the first nontrivial finite-$N$ phase-cover layer developed in
\cite{CMP}.

\subsection{Quadratic phase cover}

Set
\[
u=|\lambda|^2,
\qquad
v=|c|^2,
\qquad
w=|d|^2=\frac{1-u+2v}{2},
\]
and
\[
z:=c\overline d\,\overline\lambda.
\]
The phase-cover trace and norm are
\[
z+\overline z=R(u,v):=\frac{9u^2-16uv-10u-16v+1}{16},
\]
\[
z\overline z=P(u,v):=uv\frac{1-u+2v}{2}.
\]
Hence $z$ satisfies
\[
T^2-R(u,v)T+P(u,v)=0.
\]
Define
\[
J:=z-\frac{R}{2}.
\]
Then $\overline J=-J$ and
\[
J^2=-S(u,v),
\qquad
S(u,v):=P(u,v)-\frac{R(u,v)^2}{4}.
\]
Let
\[
X:=9u-16v+1,
\qquad
\Delta:=36u-X^2.
\]
A direct simplification gives
\[
S(u,X)=\frac{(u-1)^2\Delta}{1024}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[quadratic trace extension]
The algebra
\[
\mathcal C=\R[u,X,J]/(J^2+S(u,X))
\]
with involution $\overline u=u$, $\overline X=X$, $\overline J=-J$ is a free
rank-two $\R[u,X]$-module
\[
\mathcal C=\R[u,X]\oplus\R[u,X]J.
\]
For
\[
\langle A,B\rangle_{\mathcal C}:=\frac12\Tr_{\mathcal C/\R[u,X]}(A\overline B),
\]
and $A=a+bJ$, $B=c+dJ$, one has
\[
\langle A,B\rangle_{\mathcal C}=ac+bdS.
\]
In particular $1\perp J$, and on the chamber $S>0$ this is a positive
real/imaginary splitting.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since $J^2=-S$ is monic quadratic, $\mathcal C$ is free with basis $1,J$ over
$\R[u,X]$.  The involution is well-defined because $(-J)^2+S=J^2+S$.  Now
\[
\overline B=c-dJ,
\]
so
\[
A\overline B=(a+bJ)(c-dJ)=ac-bdJ^2+(bc-ad)J=ac+bdS+(bc-ad)J.
\]
The trace kills the $J$-coefficient and doubles the scalar coefficient.  Dividing by
$2$ gives the formula.  Positivity on $S>0$ is immediate from
$\langle a+bJ,a+bJ\rangle=a^2+Sb^2$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Hermitian squares upstairs, non-SOS downstairs}

In the phase gauge
\[
\lambda=a,
\qquad
c=b,
\qquad
d=\rho e^{i\delta},
\qquad a,b,\rho\ge0,
\]
put
\[
P_{s,m}(z)=(1-az)^{s-m}(b+\rho e^{-i\delta}z)^m
=\sum_{n=0}^s p_{s,m,n}z^n.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Hermitian-square formula on the phase cover]
For every integer $s\ge0$, the first-peeled $N=3$ inner kernel satisfies
\[
\Psi_s(a,b,\rho,\delta)=\sum_{m=0}^s\binom sm^2\sum_{n=0}^s
\frac{|p_{s,m,n}|^2}{n+1}.
\]
Thus $\Psi_s$ is a finite sum of Hermitian squares on the phase cover.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For integer $s$,
\[
{}_2F_1(-s,-s;1;x)=\sum_{m=0}^s\binom sm^2x^m.
\]
The inner integrand becomes
\[
\sum_{m=0}^s\binom sm^2|1-a\beta|^{2(s-m)}|b+\rho e^{i\delta}\beta|^{2m}
=\sum_{m=0}^s\binom sm^2|P_{s,m}(\beta)|^2.
\]
Writing $P_{s,m}(\beta)=\sum_np_{s,m,n}\beta^n$ and using
\[
\frac1\pi\int_D \beta^n\overline{\beta}^{\ell}\,d^2\beta=\frac{\delta_{n\ell}}{n+1}
\]
gives the formula.
\end{proof}

Define trace polynomials
\[
T_0=2,
\qquad
T_1=R,
\qquad
T_{n+1}=RT_n-PT_{n-1}.
\]
Then $T_n=z^n+\overline z^n$ by induction.

\begin{theorem}[trace descent]
For every $s\ge0$, $\Psi_s$ descends to a polynomial
\[
\Psi_s^\#(u,v)\in\Q[u,v].
\]
Moreover $\deg\Psi_s^\#=s$, and
\[
[v^s]\Psi_s^\#(u,v)=\frac1{s+1}\binom{2s+1}{s}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Expanding the Hermitian-square formula yields monomials in $u,v,w$ multiplied by
phase factors $z^n\overline z^{m}$.  Pairing conjugate terms replaces a phase difference
of size $\ell$ by $z^\ell+\overline z^\ell=T_\ell(u,v)$.  Since
\[
w=\frac{1-u+2v}{2},\quad R\in\Q[u,v],\quad P\in\Q[u,v],
\]
all $T_\ell$ lie in $\Q[u,v]$, proving descent.
Every term has total degree at most $s$.  The coefficient of $v^s$ comes only from the
$m=s$ diagonal part and equals
\[
\sum_{q=0}^s\frac{\binom sq^2}{q+1}.
\]
Using
\[
\frac1{q+1}\binom sq=\frac1{s+1}\binom{s+1}{q+1}
\]
and Vandermonde's identity gives
\[
\sum_{q=0}^s\frac{\binom sq^2}{q+1}
=\frac1{s+1}\binom{2s+1}{s},
\]
which is nonzero.  Hence the total degree is exactly $s$.
\end{proof}

The first two descended kernels are
\[
\Psi_1^\#(u,v)=\frac{5+u+6v}{4},
\]
and
\[
\Psi_2^\#(u,v)=\frac{47}{24}+\frac{11}{4}u+\frac{28}{3}v-
\frac{11}{8}u^2+4uv+\frac{10}{3}v^2.
\]
The next two, recorded for reference, are
\[
\Psi_3^\#=\frac{105}{32}+\frac{465}{32}u+\frac{75}{2}v-\frac{225}{32}u^2+
\frac{195}{4}uv+\frac{405}{8}v^2-\frac{65}{32}u^3-\frac{15}{2}u^2v+
\frac{225}{8}uv^2+\frac{35}{4}v^3,
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Psi_4^\#=\frac1{320}(&1759u^4-16224u^3v-11812u^3+3024u^2v^2
-12816u^2v-2334u^2\\
&+50176uv^3+165984uv^2+117312uv+18732u\\
&+8064v^4+78848v^3+121296v^2+40752v+1719).
\end{aligned}
\]

\begin{theorem}[quotient non-SOS obstruction]
The polynomial $\Psi_2^\#(u,v)$ is not a sum of squares in $\R[u,v]$, although its
pullback to the phase cover is a Hermitian-square sum.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A quadratic polynomial in $\R[u,v]$ is a sum of squares only if it is a sum of
squares of affine linear forms.  Writing
\[
\Psi_2^\#=(1\;u\;v)
Q
\begin{pmatrix}1\\u\\v\end{pmatrix}
\]
gives the unique symmetric Gram matrix
\[
Q=\begin{pmatrix}
47/24 & 11/8 & 14/3\\
11/8 & -11/8 & 2\\
14/3 & 2 & 10/3
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The principal minor
\[
\det\begin{pmatrix}47/24&11/8\\ 11/8&-11/8\end{pmatrix}=-\frac{55}{12}
\]
is negative.  Hence $Q$ is not positive semidefinite, so no real SOS representation
exists.  The Hermitian-square representation upstairs was proved above.
\end{proof}

This is the precise quotient/lift obstruction: positivity is present on the phase cover
but is not represented by real sums of squares after quotient descent.

\section{Consolidated structural theorem}

\begin{theorem}[support-parity-CUE package]
The following structures are compatible.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The split-zero semiring separates arithmetic value and support:
\[
p_R^{-1}(0_R)=\{\tau,0_R\},
\qquad
\chi_R^{-1}(0)=\{\tau\},
\qquad
\chi_R(0_R)=1.
\]
\item The quotient/lift principal-part basis
\[
q=1/u,
\qquad
\ell=v/u
\]
has function parity $q$ even and $\ell$ odd.
\item Residues reverse function parity:
\[
q\,ds\mapsto -e_0+e_1,
\qquad
\ell\,ds\mapsto e_0+e_1.
\]
\item The lift differential is logarithmic:
\[
\ell\,ds=d\log u,
\]
and the finite two-end residue is canceled by projective infinity.
\item For every compact probability measure $\mu$,
\[
\PP_{\{0,1\}}\Lambda_{\mu,t}
=(1+t m_1(\mu))\frac1u-tm_1(\mu)\frac vu.
\]
\item For the peeled CUE first-derivative measure,
\[
m_1=\frac{(N+1)(2N+1)}{6N},
\]
so the primitive lift coefficient is $-tm_1$ and the visible finite defect is $-2tm_1$.
\item For finite abelian $A$ with $|A|=r$, the nonidentity Weyl operators indexed by
$A\times\widehat A$ form an orthogonal vector-space basis of
$\mathfrak{sl}_r(\C)$.
\item For the $s$-fold first-derivative CUE moment, the microscopic exponent is
\[
s^2+2s=\dim\mathfrak{sl}_{s+1}(\C),
\]
and the exact first-jet block triplet is
\[
\End(\C^s)\oplus\C^s\oplus(\C^s)^\vee\simeq\mathfrak{sl}_{s+1}(\C).
\]
\item At $N=3$, the phase-cover algebra
\[
\R[u,X]\oplus\R[u,X]J,
\qquad J^2=-S,
\]
has trace-orthogonal real and imaginary sectors; integer kernels are Hermitian-square
sums upstairs, but $\Psi_2^\#$ is not a sum of squares on the quotient base.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Items (i) through (iv) are the split-zero and residue-parity theorems above.  Items
(v) and (vi) are the completed shifted-Hurwitz principal-part theorem and its CUE
first-moment specialization.  Item (vii) is the finite phase-space basis theorem.  Item
(viii) is the Grover--Mezzadri--Simm exponent specialization together with the
first-jet block theorem.  Item (ix) is the quadratic phase-cover trace theorem and the
non-SOS obstruction theorem.
\end{proof}

The exact sign identity is therefore
\[
\boxed{\text{visible finite two-end residue defect}+\text{projective-infinity inflow}=0.}
\]
The exact quotient/lift identity is
\[
\boxed{\text{quotient real sector}\perp\text{anti-invariant imaginary lift sector}.}
\]
The exact phase-cover identity is
\[
\boxed{\text{Hermitian-square positivity upstairs need not descend as real SOS downstairs}.}
\]

\section{Further problem: beyond the first-jet block}

The first-jet block theorem supplies a rigorous triplet
\[
\End(W)\oplus W\oplus W^\vee.
\]
This is the minimal algebraic object behind the exponent
\[
\dim\mathfrak{sl}(W\oplus\C).
\]
The Grover--Mezzadri--Simm determinant formula contains substantially more structure:
partitions, Kostka coefficients, Andreief positivity, and microscopic kernels.  The natural
next step is not to assert an $SU(s+1)$ representation theorem, but to construct one.

\begin{problem}
Construct, if it exists, a functor from the Kostka determinant category generated by the
microscopic CUE first-derivative moment data to a representation-theoretic category for
$\mathfrak{sl}_{s+1}$ such that the scalar exponent $s^2+2s$ is lifted to an invariant of
that functor, and such that the block triplet
$\End(W)\oplus W\oplus W^\vee$ is recovered as the first-jet truncation.
\end{problem}

This problem is independent of any physical beta-function or holographic interpretation.
Those analogies may motivate a later comparison, but they are not premises of the
mathematics proved here.

\section{Exact constant-term check for $M_3(s)$}\label{app:script}

The following script implements the Weyl constant-term formula for
\[
M_3(s)=\mathbb E_{U(3)}|\Phi_3'(1)|^{2s},
\qquad
\Phi_3'(1)=3-2e_1+e_2.
\]

\begin{verbatim}
from collections import defaultdict
import itertools, math

def mul(p, q, N):
    r = defaultdict(int)
    for a, ca in p.items():
        for b, cb in q.items():
            r[tuple(a[i] + b[i] for i in range(N))] += ca * cb
    return {k: v for k, v in r.items() if v}

def pow_poly(p, n, N):
    r = {tuple([0] * N): 1}
    base = p
    while n:
        if n & 1:
            r = mul(r, base, N)
        n >>= 1
        if n:
            base = mul(base, base, N)
    return r

def inv(p):
    return {tuple(-x for x in k): v for k, v in p.items()}

def moment_N_s(N, s):
    # Phi_N'(1)=sum_{r=0}^{N-1}(N-r)(-1)^r e_r.
    p = defaultdict(int)
    p[tuple([0] * N)] = N
    for r in range(1, N):
        coeff = (N - r) * ((-1) ** r)
        for subset in itertools.combinations(range(N), r):
            e = [0] * N
            for i in subset:
                e[i] = 1
            p[tuple(e)] += coeff
    p = dict(p)

    # Vandermonde modulus squared.
    V = {tuple([0] * N): 1}
    for i, j in itertools.combinations(range(N), 2):
        factor = defaultdict(int)
        factor[tuple([0] * N)] += 2
        e = [0] * N
        e[i] += 1
        e[j] -= 1
        factor[tuple(e)] -= 1
        e = [0] * N
        e[j] += 1
        e[i] -= 1
        factor[tuple(e)] -= 1
        V = mul(V, dict(factor), N)

    ps = pow_poly(p, s, N)
    f = mul(ps, inv(ps), N)
    FV = mul(f, V, N)
    return FV.get(tuple([0] * N), 0) // math.factorial(N)

for s in range(1, 6):
    print(s, moment_N_s(3, s))
\end{verbatim}

\]
