The programme's originator proposes the following complete square. Each equation quantifies over the carrier on which the specified role is imposed.
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{Addition}\backslash\text{Multiplication}&\text{identity}&\text{absorber}\\\hline
\text{identity}&
\begin{gathered}x+\varepsilon=x\\x\varepsilon=x\end{gathered}&
\begin{gathered}x+\mathcal T=x\\x\mathcal T=\mathcal T\end{gathered}\\\hline
\text{absorber}&
\begin{gathered}x+u=u\\xu=x\end{gathered}&
\begin{gathered}x+\Omega=\Omega\\x\Omega=\Omega\end{gathered}
\end{array}
\]
Here $\mathcal T$ is the original ``trivial number'' of the 2025 construction \cite{origin}. Its adjoined element is distinct from the original integer zero. The earlier sequential manuscript \cite{sequential} also explicitly defines $\Omega$. The $u$ and $\varepsilon$ roles complete the proposed square. The notation records the roles rather than an assertion of four globally distinct constants in a single algebra.

\begin{theorem}[Every corner has a nontrivial realization]
Let $X$ be a nonempty set. With $+$ interpreted as union and multiplication as intersection on $\mathcal P(X)$, the element $\varnothing$ has the $\mathcal T$ role and $X$ has the $u$ role. With both operations interpreted as union, $\varnothing$ has the $\varepsilon$ role and $X$ has the $\Omega$ role. Both choices satisfy associativity, commutativity, and distributivity, and have the indicated additive and multiplicative identities.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Union and intersection are associative and commutative by their elementwise membership definitions. The identities $A\cup\varnothing=A$, $A\cap\varnothing=\varnothing$, $A\cap X=A$, and $A\cup X=X$ give the first pair of roles. Distributivity follows elementwise: membership in $A\cap(B\cup C)$ is equivalent to membership in $(A\cap B)\cup(A\cap C)$.
For two union operations, $\varnothing$ is the identity for each and $X$ is an absorber for each. Distributivity is the equality
\[
A\cup(B\cup C)=(A\cup B)\cup(A\cup C),
\]
which follows from associativity, commutativity, and $A\cup A=A$. In the latter structure the additive identity is also the multiplicative identity, and is not required to be a multiplicative absorber. Nonemptiness of $X$ gives at least two distinct elements in either realization.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact global and local scope]
If four elements have the displayed roles globally for the same pair of operations on a nonempty carrier, the carrier is a singleton. Nevertheless, the original sequential construction $H_R=R\sqcup\{\mathcal T,\Omega\}$ retains a nonzero ring $R$ injectively by keeping the role equations on their specified domains: $\mathcal T$ is a multiplicative absorber on $R\sqcup\{\mathcal T\}$, while $\Omega$ is the global multiplicative absorber and $\mathcal T\Omega=\Omega$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Two identities for a binary operation coincide: applying the operation to them returns each by the other identity law. Two absorbers likewise coincide by applying the operation to them. Thus the displayed global roles imply $\varepsilon=\mathcal T$ from addition, $\varepsilon=u$ from multiplication, and $u=\Omega$ from addition. An element that is both an additive identity and absorber satisfies $x=x+\varepsilon=\varepsilon$ for every $x$, proving the singleton assertion.
For the sequential construction, retain the ring operations on $R$, make $\mathcal T$ its external additive identity and multiplicative absorber, then extend both operations by the rule that an argument $\Omega$ returns $\Omega$. No old operation changes. Associativity and commutativity reduce to the old laws when $\Omega$ is absent and to $\Omega$ when it is present. In a distributive expression containing $\Omega$, both sides evaluate to $\Omega$; otherwise the old distributive law applies. The additive identity remains $\mathcal T$, and the multiplicative identity remains $1_R$. This verifies the asserted object and its injective inclusion of the old carrier. In particular, the two multiplication rules have exactly the domains stated in the theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact receiver for adjoining a fresh common identity]
Let $S_R=R\sqcup\{\mathcal T\}$ with its original operations. Consider data $(D,j)$ where $D$ has associative and commutative addition and multiplication, distributivity, and a common additive and multiplicative identity $\varepsilon$, and $j:S_R\to D$ preserves the binary operations. The old identities are allowed to become local; $j$ is not required to preserve them.

Every such $j$ is constant on $S_R$. The universal receiving object is
\[
D_2=\{\varepsilon<c\},\qquad x+y=xy=\max(x,y),
\]
with $j_0(s)=c$ for all $s\in S_R$. Every $(D,j)$ factors uniquely through a morphism $D_2\to D$ preserving both operations and the common identity.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $a=j(s)$ and $t=j(\mathcal T)$. The original equations give $at=t$ and $a+t=a$. Since $\varepsilon+t=t$ in $D$, distributivity yields
\[
t=at=a(\varepsilon+t)=a\varepsilon+at=a+t=a.
\]
Thus every $j(s)$ equals $t$. Also $t+t=t$ and $t^2=t$, by applying $j$ to the corresponding equations for $\mathcal T$. The two-element object $D_2$ has both operations maximum, so is associative, commutative and distributive by idempotence, with common identity $\varepsilon$. The map $j_0$ preserves both operations because $c+c=c^2=c$. Define the factor map by $\varepsilon\mapsto\varepsilon_D$ and $c\mapsto t$. Products and sums with $\varepsilon$ satisfy the identity laws; products and sums of $c$ are preserved by the two idempotence equations for $t$. These check the complete tables. Uniqueness follows from the values of the common identity and of $j_0$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The four adjunction calculations.}
The direct definition checks above show why the original $\mathcal T$ and the subsequent $\Omega$ adjunction retain the ring carrier and its operations, with their global identities and absorbers changing scope. For a fresh $u$, the companion proof \emph{Adjoining an Additive Absorber That Is a Multiplicative Identity} computes the universal receiver as the three-element chain with addition maximum and multiplication minimum: all ring elements merge into its middle element, $\mathcal T$ remains its bottom, and $u$ is its top. Requiring the old unit to remain global gives its Boolean quotient. For a fresh common identity $\varepsilon$, the theorem above gives the two-element join/join receiver and shows the forced identification of the whole old carrier. These are exact answers about what each branch preserves. The square remains a coherent method for generating and comparing the questions.
