\begin{abstract}
The two boundary projection energies in the original response ellipse
are evaluated through the full retained eigenclass, its consecutive
Cauchy transforms, and its actual source-norm drops. The eigen-equation
determines the complete opposite-parity masses. Their exact transport
between adjacent attained sections expresses each class-norm drop in
the original directed phase errors. Together with the original
arithmetic covariance and multiplication estimates, this proves
\(p_2=\Theta_h(a^2/q^2)\) on the even cutoff subsequence and
\(p_1=O_h(q^{-2})\) on the odd subsequence, with explicit finite
bounds and complete primary multiplicity retained. The same directed
error extends to the previously uncovered final receiving cutoff.
The observation-kernel projection remains its original map; none
of its individual projected signs is inferred from these energies.
\end{abstract}

Indeed pair the eigen-equation from (RBE4) with \(f_N\).

\section{Exact individual Cauchy-transform formulas}

The empty sum at \(r=0\) is zero. Orthogonal projection onto
all old relations gives the full polynomial
\begin{equation}\tag{RBE13}
 f_N(S)=\frac{\chi(S)}{H_\lambda}

       -\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\frac{C_j(\lambda)}{\nu_j}\pi_j(S)\right\},
 \qquad E_N=A_r/|H_\lambda|^2.
\end{equation}
Each term is either the original terminal polynomial or an original
\(\chi\)-multiple. The orthogonality follows directly from
the definition of \(C_j\); positivity of \(A_r\) is the

This also proves the exact source-norm drop

and the leading coefficients give
\begin{equation}\tag{RBE15}
 \begin{gathered}

 F_{22}=\nu_r-\omega_{N+1},\\
 F_{13}=-\frac{\omega_NC_{r-1}}{H_\lambda\nu_{r-1}},
 \qquad F_{23}=-\frac{C_r}{H_\lambda}.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
To verify the first identity, pair \(p_N\) with the old relation

and \(F_{13}=\omega_{q-1}/H_\lambda\); the remaining entries

 =\frac{|C_r/C_{r-1}|^2\nu_{r-1}(\nu_{r-1}-\omega_N)}

 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
None of the positive multipliers in the ratio is discarded.

\(\widehat\chi(y)=i^{-q}\chi(c+iy)\) and
