\section{Independent parity-block calculation and the inverse equilibrium moment}
\label{sec:igb-independent-parity}

Fix the original packet parameters $0<\delta<1/2$, $\gamma>2$,
and complete multiplicity $m_0\geq1$. Throughout,
\[
 k=4l+1,\qquad e=1+k(m_0-1),\qquad q=e(k+1)^2,\qquad c=k/2.
 \tag{IGB1}
\]
Thus $q$ is even, $q\geq(k+1)^2$, and $k/q\to0$.
Retain the original polynomial and the entire fixed Gamma measure:
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \chi(S)=\prod_{a,b=0}^k
 \{S-c-(2a-k)\delta-i(2b-k)\gamma\}^{e},\\
 Q(y)=i^{-q}\chi(c+iy)
 =\prod_{a,b=0}^k(y-\omega_{ab})^e,\qquad
 \omega_{ab}=(2b-k)\gamma-i(2a-k)\delta,\\
 d\sigma(y)=\frac{|\Gamma(1/4+iy/2)|^2}{2\pi}\,dy .
 \end{gathered}\tag{IGB2}
\]
Every root and every repetition is retained. The maps
$P(S)\mapsto P(c+iy)$ and $f(y)\mapsto f((S-c)/i)$ are inverse
coefficient substitutions. Their diagonal factors have modulus one.
The additional factor $i^{-q}$ makes $Q$ monic in $y$ and also has
modulus one. Consequently these maps preserve the literal source
Gram determinants, including the mass $\sqrt{2\pi}$ of $\sigma$.
Conjugation and negation permute the displayed roots, so $Q$ is real
and even. Since $k$ is odd, no $2a-k$ is zero, and $Q$ has no real root.

Write
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \mathcal R_d(Q)=
 \det\left[\int_{\mathbb R}y^{i+j}|Q(y)|^2\,d\sigma(y)\right]_{i,j=0}^{d-1},
 \qquad \mathcal R_0(Q)=1,\\
 \mathcal H_{n,\epsilon}(Q)=
 \det\left[\int_{\mathbb R}
 y^{2i+2j+2\epsilon}|Q(y)|^2\,d\sigma(y)\right]_{i,j=0}^{n-1},
 \qquad \epsilon\in\{0,1\}.
 \end{gathered}\tag{IGB3}
\]
These are full relation determinants. Their entries are not products
of unminimized relation norms. Reordering the original monomials into
even and odd powers makes the Gram matrix block diagonal. Applying
the same permutation to its rows and columns contributes determinant
one. For $N=q/2$ this proves the exact identity
\[
 \log\mathcal R_q+\log\mathcal R_{q+1}
 =\log\mathcal H_{N,0}+
   \log\mathcal H_{N+1,0}+2\log\mathcal H_{N,1}.
 \tag{IGB4}
\]

\subsection{The full rectangular root contribution}

Direct summation gives
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^k(2j-k)=0,\qquad
 \sum_{j=0}^k(2j-k)^2=\frac{(k+1)k(k+2)}3 .
\]
The mixed product in the square of $\omega_{ab}$ therefore vanishes
after the two independent sums. In particular
\[
 \sum_{a,b=0}^k e\,\omega_{ab}^2
   =\frac{qk(k+2)}3(\gamma^2-\delta^2),\qquad
 \eta_h=\frac{\gamma^2-\delta^2}{3}>0,\qquad
 \tau_k=\frac{\eta_h k(k+2)}q\in[0,\eta_h].
 \tag{IGB5}
\]
Negation of all roots proves that every odd power sum is zero.
Set $R_k=k\sqrt{\delta^2+\gamma^2}$. On any fixed complex
neighbourhood of a compact interval $[\varepsilon,M]\subset(0,\infty)$,
with its square root positive on the interval, eventually
$|\omega_{ab}/(q\sqrt{x})|<1/2$. The power series of the logarithm,
continued from the positive value of $|Q(q\sqrt{x})|^2$ on the
real interval, then gives
\[
 \log\frac{|Q(q\sqrt{x})|^2}{q^{2q}x^q}
 =-\frac{\tau_k}{x}+r_k(x),\qquad
 \sup|r_k(x)|\leq C_h\frac{k^4}{q^3}.
 \tag{IGB6}
\]
To specify the analytic expression in this display, on real $x$ it is
$2\sum_{a,b}e\log(1-\omega_{ab}/(q\sqrt{x}))$ with the
branches continued from zero root displacement. Conjugate pairing
makes the sum real. The quadratic term is minus the second power sum
divided by $q^2x$, giving IGB5. After that term, the absolute sum is
bounded by
$Cq(R_k/q)^4\sum_{j\geq0}(R_k/(q\sqrt{\varepsilon/2}))^{2j}$.
This proves the stated bound while retaining all roots.
For a determinant of size $n=q/2+s$, $s\in\{0,1\}$, the resulting
logarithmic error is
\[
 O_h(nk^4/q^3)=O_h(k^4/q^2)=O_h(1).
 \tag{IGB7}
\]
The same conclusion holds for every fixed multiplicity; no expansion
of $k$ in fractional powers of the determinant size is needed.

\subsection{The coefficient substitution and its whole-form tail bound}

The substitution $x=(y/q)^2$ on the two halves of the real line is
used only after the even and odd blocks have been separated.
For a polynomial $P$ in $x$, the exact block measure before factoring
the root and Gamma asymptotics is
\[
 q^{2\epsilon+1}x^{\epsilon-1/2}|Q(q\sqrt{x})|^2
             \sigma(q\sqrt{x})\,dx,\qquad x>0,
 \quad
 \sigma(t)=\frac{|\Gamma(1/4+it/2)|^2}{2\pi}.
 \tag{IGB8}
\]
The two real half-lines together give the factor $q$ in this formula.
The row and column changes $y^{2j}=q^{2j}x^j$ give the further
determinant factor $q^{2n(n-1)}$. These are explicit invertible
coefficient maps, rather than changes of the original source mass.

Here is a whole-form restriction argument sufficient to apply the
analytic theorem on a fixed compact interval. The endpoints
$a=u^2,b=v^2$ of the EIQ measure are fixed positive numbers.
Choose $0<\varepsilon<\min(a,1)$ and $M>\max(b,2)$, making
$\varepsilon$ smaller and $M$ larger below.
Uniform Stirling estimates on the imaginary ray, combined with
continuity on a bounded segment, give constants $C,c>0$ such that
\[
 c(1+t)^{-1/2}e^{-\pi t/2}
 \leq\sigma(t)\leq
 C(1+t)^{-1/2}e^{-\pi t/2}\qquad(t\geq0).
 \tag{IGB9}
\]
All constants here are independent of $q$.

For a polynomial of degree $d\leq q/2$, expansion in the
orthonormal shifted Legendre polynomials on $[1,2]$ and
Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
 |P(x)|^2\leq C(d+1)^2
        [C_1(1+x)]^{2d}\int_1^2|P(t)|^2\,dt
       \qquad(x\geq0).
 \tag{IGB10}
\]
For completeness, the literal Legendre polynomial is
$P_j(z)=2^{-j}\sum_{r=0}^j\binom jr^2
(z-1)^{j-r}(z+1)^r$.
Using $\sum_r\binom jr^2=\binom{2j}j\leq4^j$ gives
$|P_j(z)|\leq[2(1+|z|)]^j$.
At $z=2x-3$, this is at most $[8(1+x)]^j$.
The shifted orthonormal polynomial is
$\sqrt{2j+1}P_j(2x-3)$; its squared evaluations summed over
$0\leq j\leq d$ are therefore at most
$(d+1)^2[8(1+x)]^{2d}$.
Cauchy--Schwarz proves IGB10, for example with $C=1,C_1=8$,
for every polynomial in the entire space, including complex coefficients.

Pairing opposite roots proves on $x\geq0$
\[
 q^{-2q}|Q(q\sqrt{x})|^2
 \leq \{x+(R_k/q)^2\}^{q}.
 \tag{IGB11}
\]
On $[1,2]$, IGB6 and $\tau_k\leq\eta_h$ give the lower bound
$q^{-2q}|Q(q\sqrt{x})|^2\geq c_hx^q$.
After removing the common factor $q^{2q+2\epsilon+1}$,
the weight in IGB8 on this interval is consequently bounded
below by $c_hq^{-1/2}e^{-C_2q}$. On $[0,\varepsilon]$,
eventually $(R_k/q)^2\leq\varepsilon$, so IGB9--11 bound the
whole lower-tail form by
\[
 C_h(q+1)^C(C_3\,2\varepsilon)^q
       \int_0^\varepsilon x^{\epsilon-1/2}\,dx
       \int_1^2|P(t)|^2\,dt.
\]
The integral at zero is finite for both original values of
$\epsilon$. Choose $\varepsilon$ so that this expression
is at most $e^{-c_3q}$ times the $[1,2]$ source norm.

For $x\geq M$, IGB11 gives
$(x+(R_k/q)^2)^q\leq e^{C_h}x^q$, because
$R_k^2/q$ is bounded. Equations IGB9--10 bound the upper tail
by a fixed polynomial in $q$ times
\[
 \int_M^\infty x^{\epsilon-3/4}
  \exp\{q[2\log(1+x)+\log C_1-(\pi/2)\sqrt{x}]\}\,dx
       \int_1^2|P(t)|^2\,dt .
\]
Choose $M$ so that the square-bracketed expression is at most
$-c_4\sqrt{x}$ and $c_4\sqrt M>C_2+1$. The substitution
$r=\sqrt{x}$ bounds this integral by a polynomial in $q$
times $e^{-c_4q\sqrt M}$. It too is exponentially smaller
than the original norm on $[1,2]$.
Both choices are fixed independently of $q$ and of the three
parity-block types.

Let $H^{\rm full}$ and $H^{[\varepsilon,M]}$ denote any one of
the block Grams in its actual polynomial coefficient frame.
The preceding estimates prove the Loewner inequalities
\[
 H^{[\varepsilon,M]}\preceq H^{\rm full}
 \preceq(1+e^{-c_hq})H^{[\varepsilon,M]}
 \tag{IGB12}
\]
for all sufficiently large $q$, after adjusting $c_h>0$.
Congruence by the positive square root of the restricted Gram
shows that every relative eigenvalue lies in the same interval.
Thus the logarithmic determinant difference is
$O(qe^{-c_hq})$. This argument retains all polynomial norms
before quotient minimization, rather than just controlling
individual entries.

On the fixed interval the real part of Stirling's expansion yields
\[
 \sigma(q\sqrt{x})=
 \sqrt2\,q^{-1/2}x^{-1/4}
 e^{-(\pi q/2)\sqrt{x}}\{1+O(q^{-2})\}.
 \tag{IGB13}
\]
The potential inverse-$q$ term is imaginary before taking the
modulus and cancels. Equivalently, conjugating the expansion
at $1/4+iq\sqrt{x}/2$ cancels its odd inverse powers in the
logarithm. The resulting bound is uniform on the interval.
Replacing this factor changes the block log determinant by
$O(nq^{-2})=O(q^{-1})$, by the same relative-form argument.

\subsection{Fixed-potential factors and a uniform convex estimate}

Use exactly the EIQ potential and its probability measure,
\[
 V_0(x)=\pi\sqrt{x}-2\log x,\qquad
 \rho=\rho_{2,\pi},\qquad
 F=-\min_\mu\left\{\int V_0\,d\mu-
                    \iint\log|x-y|\,d\mu(x)d\mu(y)\right\}.
 \tag{IGB14}
\]
Here EIQ's proved support is $[u^2,v^2]$, with
$uK(\kappa)=2$, $vE(\kappa)=4$ and
$\kappa^2=1-u^2/v^2$. Set
\[
 L=\int\log x\,\rho(x)\,dx,\qquad
 M_{-1}=\int x^{-1}\rho(x)\,dx,\qquad
 M_{1/2}=\int\sqrt{x}\rho(x)\,dx=\frac6\pi .
 \tag{IGB15}
\]
The last equality also follows directly by pushing the minimizing
measure forward under $x\mapsto t^2x$: its energy change is
$\pi(t-1)M_{1/2}-6\log t$. Its derivative at the minimum
$t=1$ is zero. This proves the factor six in the following
calculation without a change of measure normalization.

On $[\varepsilon,M]$, put
\[
 D_n(W)=\det\left[
 \int_\varepsilon^M x^{i+j}e^{-nV_0(x)+W(x)}\,dx
                         \right]_{i,j=0}^{n-1}.
 \tag{IGB16}
\]
The analytic input is Borot--Guionnet,
\emph{Asymptotic expansion of beta matrix models in the one-cut regime},
\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.1167}, version 3,
Proposition 1.2, equations (1-3)--(1-6), and Proposition 5.2.
For this fixed potential their results give
\[
 \log D_n(0)=n^2F+n\log(2\pi)+O(\log n),\qquad
 \log\frac{D_n(W)}{D_n(0)}
       =n\int W\rho+O_W(1)
 \tag{IGB17}
\]
for each fixed real analytic $W$ on the interval.
The hypotheses hold here: the two integration boundaries are
strictly outside the EIQ support, the potential is holomorphic
on a neighbourhood avoiding zero, the density coefficient is
strictly positive including at its two soft endpoints, and EIQ's
exterior variational inequality is strict.

We spell out the determinant normalization in the first formula.
Expanding the Vandermonde determinant and using Fubini gives
$D_n(W)=Z_n(W)/n!$ for the paper's index-two integral.
The Gaussian determinant at weight $e^{-nx^2/2}$ equals
\[
 (2\pi)^{n/2}n^{-n^2/2}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}j!.
\]
This follows either from monic Hermite norms or from the paper's
displayed Gaussian product. To check its linear coefficient,
write the log product as
$n\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\log j-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}j\log j$.
Stirling's formula for the first sum and Euler summation for
the second give
$\tfrac12n^2\log n-\tfrac34n^2+
\tfrac12n\log(2\pi)+O(\log n)$.
Thus the Gaussian determinant has linear term $n\log(2\pi)$.
The index-two potential-dependent odd coefficient vanishes
in (1-6), and the support-length exponent in (1-3) is exactly
$n^2$. This proves the same linear term for $V_0$.
Its quadratic coefficient is the negative equilibrium energy
by the actual Vandermonde integral. These observations fix
the constants and signs in IGB17.

For $n=q/2+s$ the exact fixed-potential factor after IGB6 and
IGB13 is
\[
 W_{s,\epsilon,t}(x)=
 (\epsilon-2s-3/4)\log x+\pi s\sqrt{x}-t/x .
 \tag{IGB18}
\]
Indeed $-nV_0+W_{s,\epsilon,\tau_k}$ equals
$(q+\epsilon-3/4)\log x-(\pi q/2)\sqrt{x}-\tau_k/x$.
This is an exact identity. In particular $V_0$ is independent
of $n$, even at higher multiplicity.

Here is a uniform version of the second formula of IGB17 using only
fixed factors. For each of the three pairs $(s,\epsilon)$, define
\[
 f_n(t)=\log\frac{D_n(W_{s,\epsilon,t})}{D_n(0)}
                  -n\int W_{s,\epsilon,t}\rho .
\]
Differentiation under the finite positive integral is valid on
every bounded $t$ interval and gives
$f_n''(t)=\operatorname{Var}_t(\sum_i x_i^{-1})\geq0$.
By IGB17 the three values at $t=-1,0,\eta_h+1$ are bounded
in absolute value by a constant independent of $n$.
For $0\leq t\leq\eta_h$, convexity bounds $f_n(t)$ above by
the chord from $0$ to $\eta_h+1$, and below by
$f_n(0)+t(f_n(0)-f_n(-1))$.
Both bounds are uniform constants. Consequently
\[
 \log D_n(W_{s,\epsilon,\tau_k})
 =n^2F+n\log(2\pi)+
 n\{(\epsilon-2s-3/4)L+6s-\tau_kM_{-1}\}
 +O_h(\log q).
 \tag{IGB19}
\]
No derivative of an unknown asymptotic error is taken.
The negative fixed value $t=-1$ is used only on the compact
interval, where its factor is analytic and bounded.

Combining the explicit coefficient factors of IGB8 with
IGB6--7, IGB12--13 and IGB19 proves, for each original block,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \log\mathcal H_{n,\epsilon}(Q)
 ={}&\{2(q+\epsilon)n+2n^2-\tfrac32n\}\log q
       +\tfrac n2\log2+n^2F+n\log(2\pi)\\
 &+n\{(\epsilon-2s-\tfrac34)L+6s-\tau_kM_{-1}\}
       +O_h(\log q),\qquad n=q/2+s .
 \end{aligned}\tag{IGB20}
\]
The error includes $O_h(k^4/q^2)$ from the full roots, which is
bounded for every fixed multiplicity. This proves the logarithmic
error at higher multiplicity as well as at simple multiplicity.

\subsection{The inverse moment from the exact positive density}

For this calculation allow the original EIQ parameters
$\alpha,\beta>0$, and use $a=u^2$, $b=v^2$,
$R_s=\sqrt{(a+s)(b+s)}$, $R_0=uv$ and $m_e=(a+b)/2$.
The EIQ density and endpoint relation give
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \rho_{\alpha,\beta}(x)=
 \frac{\sqrt{(b-x)(x-a)}}{2\pi}
 \left\{\frac{\alpha}{xR_0}
  -\frac{\beta}{2\pi}
       \int_0^\infty\frac{ds}{\sqrt{s}(x+s)R_s}\right\},\\
 \frac{\alpha}{R_0}
   =\frac{\beta}{2\pi}\int_0^\infty\frac{ds}{\sqrt{s}R_s}.
 \end{gathered}\tag{IGB21}
\]
All uses below are on $a\leq x\leq b$, so the factors $1/x$
are bounded and exchanges of the displayed convergent
integrals are justified by absolute integrability.
The EIQ arcsine computation gives, for $s\geq0$,
\[
 \frac1\pi\int_a^b\frac{\sqrt{(b-x)(x-a)}}{x+s}\,dx
      =m_e+s-R_s .
 \tag{IGB22}
\]
Differentiating at $s=0$ gives
$\pi^{-1}\int_a^b\sqrt{(b-x)(x-a)}/x^2\,dx
=m_e/R_0-1$. Subtracting the values at $s$ and zero and
dividing by $s$ gives the integral with denominator $x(x+s)$.
Substitution into IGB21 therefore yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 M_{-1}(\alpha,\beta)
 ={}&\frac{\alpha}{2R_0}
                  \left(\frac{m_e}{R_0}-1\right)
 -\frac{\beta}{4\pi}
   \int_0^\infty
    \frac{(R_s-R_0)/s-1}{\sqrt{s}R_s}\,ds\\
 ={}&\frac{\alpha(a+b)}{4ab}
 -\frac{\beta}{4\pi}\int_0^\infty
                    \frac{1-R_0/R_s}{s^{3/2}}\,ds .
 \end{aligned}\tag{IGB23}
\]
The cancellation on the second line is precisely the endpoint
relation in IGB21. In the remaining integral,
$1-R_0/R_s=O(s)$ at zero and is bounded at infinity.
Integration by parts has zero boundary terms and gives
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \int_0^\infty\frac{1-R_0/R_s}{s^{3/2}}\,ds
 =R_0J(a,b),\\
 J(a,b)=\int_0^\infty
 \frac{a+b+2s}{\sqrt{s}\{(a+s)(b+s)\}^{3/2}}\,ds .
 \end{gathered}\tag{IGB24}
\]
The integral in $J$ is positive, $O(s^{-1/2})$ at zero
and $O(s^{-5/2})$ at infinity.

The substitution $s=a\tan^2\theta$ in the convergent integral
gives, with $\kappa^2=1-a/b$,
\[
 I(a,b):=\int_0^\infty\frac{ds}{\sqrt{s}R_s}
       =\frac{2K(\kappa)}{\sqrt b}.
 \tag{IGB25}
\]
Differentiating under this integral is allowed uniformly on
compact positive endpoint sets; hence
$J=-2(\partial_a+\partial_b)I$.
Explicitly,
\[
 (\partial_a+\partial_b)I
 =-b^{-3/2}\{K(\kappa)+\kappa K'(\kappa)\}
 =-\frac{E(\kappa)}{a\sqrt b}.
 \tag{IGB26}
\]
The second equality follows from
$K'=E/(\kappa(1-\kappa^2))-K/\kappa$, whose proof by
differentiating the defining elliptic integral is included
in EIQ2--6. Thus $J=2E(\kappa)/(u^2v)$.
Finally $\beta vE(\kappa)=(\alpha+2)\pi$ proves the exact
general endpoint formula
\[
 M_{-1}(\alpha,\beta)
 =\frac{\alpha(a+b)-2(\alpha+2)uv}{4ab}.
 \tag{IGB27}
\]
In particular, for the actual limiting measure used above,
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 M_{-1}=\frac{u^2+v^2-4uv}{2u^2v^2}>0.}
 \tag{IGB28}
\]
Its sign follows from its original strictly positive integral
$\int_a^b\rho(x)/x\,dx$; the closed endpoint formula is equal
to that integral, rather than an independently assigned constant.

\subsection{All four block sums and the absolute Gamma coefficient}

The three block types in IGB4 have multiplicities
$(s,\epsilon)=(0,0)$ once, $(1,0)$ once, and $(0,1)$ twice.
Keeping those repetitions gives the following exact sums:
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \sum n=2q+1,\qquad \sum n^2=q^2+q+1,\qquad
 \sum n\epsilon=q,\qquad \sum ns=q/2+1,\\
 \sum n(\epsilon-2s-3/4)=-3q/2-11/4,\\
 \sum\{2(q+\epsilon)n+2n^2-3n/2\}
                         =6q^2+3q+1/2.
 \end{gathered}\tag{IGB29}
\]
For example these follow by inserting $n=q/2$ in the first and
third types and $n=q/2+1$ in the second; hence the $q+1$
relation dimension is kept throughout. Summing IGB20 gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \log\mathcal R_q+\log\mathcal R_{q+1}
 ={}&(6q^2+3q+\tfrac12)\log q+(q^2+q+1)F\\
 &+(q+\tfrac12)\log2+(2q+1)\log(2\pi)
 -(3q/2+11/4)L+3q+6\\
 &-(2q+1)\tau_kM_{-1}+O_h(\log q).
 \end{aligned}\tag{IGB30}
\]
In particular the same formula, grouped only by its proved orders, is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \log\mathcal R_q+\log\mathcal R_{q+1}
 ={}&(6q^2+3q+\tfrac12)\log q+Fq^2\\
 &+q\{F+3-\tfrac32L+3\log2+2\log\pi\}\\
 &-2\eta_hM_{-1}k(k+2)+O_h(\log q).
 \end{aligned}\tag{IGB31}
\]
Here the omitted extra $-\tau_kM_{-1}$ is bounded, as are the
other explicitly constant terms of IGB30.

The original fixed-Gamma monic squared norms are
$\gamma_n=\sqrt{2\pi}\Gamma(2n+1)/4^n$.
The coefficient frame consisting of the first $q$ monomials
and the monic relations $\chi,\chi S,\ldots,\chi S^{N-q}$
is triangular with determinant one. Its Schur complement is
the original quotient Gram. Thus its determinant is
$V_N=\prod_{j=0}^N\gamma_j/\mathcal R_{N-q+1}(Q)$.
At the four original cutoffs this proves
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal B_k[\sigma]
 :={}&\log V_{q-1}+\log V_q-\log V_{2q-1}-\log V_{2q}\\
 ={}&\log\mathcal R_q+\log\mathcal R_{q+1}
          -\log\mathcal R_1
       -\sum_{n=q}^{2q}c_{n-q}\log\gamma_n,\\
 &c_0=c_q=1,\qquad c_j=2\ (1\leq j\leq q-1).
 \end{aligned}\tag{IGB32}
\]
The empty relation determinant at the first cutoff is exactly one.

The degree-zero case of the whole-form tail comparison above
also localizes $\mathcal R_1$. On that fixed compact interval,
IGB6 bounds its root factor above and below by fixed positive
constants. The power comparison over the original real line is
\[
 2\sqrt2\int_0^\infty
              y^{2q-1/2}e^{-\pi y/2}\,dy
 =\frac{2\sqrt2\,\Gamma(2q+1/2)}
                       {(\pi/2)^{2q+1/2}}.
\]
The analogous tail proof for this power weight and IGB13 show
that its log differs from $\log\mathcal R_1$ by $O_h(1)$.
Stirling's formula therefore gives, in particular,
\[
 \log\mathcal R_1
 =2q\log q+(4\log2-2\log\pi-2)q+O_h(\log q).
 \tag{IGB33}
\]
This use of the power integral retains the full root contribution
as a bounded error at the low determinant; it assigns no such
error to either high determinant.

At the source degrees $q\leq n\leq2q$, the exact norm gives
\[
 \log\gamma_n
 =2n\log n-2n+\tfrac12\log n+\tfrac32\log2+\log\pi+O(1/n).
 \tag{IGB34}
\]
The $c_j$ are twice the trapezoidal weights.
For the leading part, twice its integral from $q$ to $2q$ is
$6q^2\log q+(8\log2-9)q^2$.
For $\tfrac12\log n$ it is
$q\log q+(2\log2-1)q$.
The constant gives $(3\log2+2\log\pi)q$.
The trapezoidal error is bounded by a constant times the integral
of the absolute second derivative, which is $O(1)$ here;
the sum of the $O(1/n)$ errors is also $O(1)$.
Consequently
\[
 \sum_{n=q}^{2q}c_{n-q}\log\gamma_n
 =6q^2\log q+(8\log2-9)q^2+
   q\log q+(5\log2+2\log\pi-1)q+O(\log q).
 \tag{IGB35}
\]
All endpoint weights remain those of IGB32.
Substitution of IGB31, IGB33 and IGB35 proves that the
$q\log q$ coefficient is exactly $3-2-1=0$.
The remaining coefficient calculation is
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 C_B=9-8\log2+F,\qquad
 C_\sigma=F+6-\tfrac32L-6\log2+2\log\pi,\\
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \mathcal B_k[\sigma]
 =C_Bq^2+C_\sigma q
        -2\eta_hM_{-1}k(k+2)+O_h(\log q).}
 \end{gathered}\tag{IGB36}
\]
The proof covers every fixed $m_0\geq1$ on IGB1.
For $m_0=1$, $k(k+2)=q-1$, so the root term contributes
$-2\eta_hM_{-1}$ to the coefficient of $q$.
For each fixed $m_0\geq2$ it is of order $k^2=o(q)$;
