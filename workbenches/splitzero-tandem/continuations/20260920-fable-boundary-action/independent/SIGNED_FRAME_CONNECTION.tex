The four signed inverse states are those of the original ES--Fable
map \cite{ES488}, with their exact inverse coordinates proved in
\cite{Global}. This note differentiates their actual odd frame in
the unchanged coordinates $(a,y,z,w)$. It then transports the
result through one fixed original conductor receiving map. No
moving conductor, diagonal replacement of the frame, or change
of its original Gamma norm is used.

\section{The original odd frame and its derivative}
Fix $\gamma>2$ and let $0<\delta<1/2$. Order the labels as
$(\mathrm M,\mathrm N,\mathrm T,\mathrm E)$, with corresponding
$(\varepsilon,\eta)=(1,1),(1,-1),(-1,1),(-1,-1)$. Retain
\begin{equation}
 r_j=\tfrac12+\varepsilon_j\delta+i\eta_j\gamma,
 \qquad A=-\tfrac12,\qquad
 d_j=h_\rho'(r_j)=4\delta\gamma(\varepsilon_j\gamma-i\eta_j\delta),
 \qquad a_j^2=d_j^{-1}.\tag{SF1}
\end{equation}
Each of the two sign choices is retained; take either continuous
choice on the displayed positive interval when differentiating.
The exact half-difference of its two original source points is
\begin{equation}
 o_j=a_j\,v_j,\qquad
 v_j=\begin{pmatrix}
 1\\-i d_j\\-d_j^2/2+2r_jd_j\\7ir_j^2d_j-13id_j^2
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 N=(v_1\ v_2\ v_3\ v_4),\quad
 O=N\diag(a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4).\tag{SF2}
\end{equation}
For example these expressions follow from the complete inverse
by using $a^{-1}=ad$, $a^{-2}=d$, $a^{-3}=ad^2$,
$a^{-4}=d^2$ and subtracting the values at $a$ and $-a$.
All constants, including $A=-1/2$, remain in SF2.

The determinant and branch logarithmic derivatives are
\begin{align}
 \det N&=-28672\delta^4\gamma^4(\delta^2+\gamma^2)
                         (\gamma^2+(2\gamma^2-1)\delta^2),\nonumber\\
 \frac{a_j'}{a_j}&=-\frac1{2\delta}
       +\frac{i\eta_j}{2(\varepsilon_j\gamma-i\eta_j\delta)}.
 \tag{SF3}
\end{align}
The determinant is obtained from the four explicit columns SF2
by expansion; the independent coefficient identity and exact
polynomial numerator certificate are supplied below. Every factor
displayed in this determinant is nonzero on the original interval.
The second equation follows by differentiating $a_j^2d_j=1$;
it is independent of the retained choice of sign.
Writing $L=\diag(a_j'/a_j)$, ordinary product differentiation gives
the exact original-coordinate connection
\begin{equation}
 \Omega_\delta=O'O^{-1}=(N'+NL)N^{-1}.\tag{SF4}
\end{equation}
In particular the square roots and their signs cancel from the
connection. This is an identity of the original matrices, not an
identification of the signed source points with each other.

\section{Every entry of the rational matrix}
For readable formulas only, abbreviate
$t=\delta^2$, $g=\gamma^2$, $Q=t+g$ and $F=g+(2g-1)t$.
These abbreviations do not change any coordinate of the four-vector.
The complete matrix is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\quad\Omega_\delta=
             \frac{B(\delta^2,\gamma^2)}{448\delta QF}.\quad}
 \tag{SF5}
\end{equation}
Here all sixteen entries of the polynomial matrix $B(t,g)$ are
as follows, in the original row and column order $(a,y,z,w)$:
\begin{align*}
 B_{11}&=-224(5t^2g-2t^2+tg^2+3tg+g^2),&
 B_{12}&=-28i,&B_{13}&=28,&B_{14}&=0,\\
 B_{21}&=2880it gQ^2,&&&&\\
 B_{22}&=4(280t^2g-140t^2+56tg^2-82tg+7t+28g^2-7g),&&&&\\
 B_{23}&=-8i(104tg-7t+7g),& B_{24}&=16F,&&&\\
 B_{31}&=64t gQ^2(84tg-56Q+39),&&&&\\
 B_{32}&=-4i(224t^2g-84t^2+448tg^2-86tg-7t+28g^2+7g),&&&&\\
 B_{33}&=8(252t^2g-112t^2+252tg^2+20tg-7t+28g^2+7g),&&&&\\
 B_{34}&=16iF,&&&&\\
 B_{41}&=112it gQ^2(1248tg-1012t+68g+123),&&&&\\
 B_{42}&=-7\bigl(1120t^3g-560t^3+2240t^2g^2-2336t^2g+112t^2&&&&\\
 &\hspace{15mm}+1120tg^3-1440tg^2+254tg+7t+560g^3+112g^2-7g\bigr),&&&&\\
 B_{43}&=14i(832t^2g-28t^2+832tg^2-64tg-7t-140g^2+7g),&&&&\\
 B_{44}&=28F(28Q-1).&&&&
\end{align*}
These expressions include the complete polynomial numerators;
no coefficient has been fitted at a sample quartet.

To verify SF5 from the original definition, insert the four
columns SF2 and the four entries of $L$ from SF3. Direct
multiplication yields the exact matrix identity
\begin{equation}
 B(\delta^2,\gamma^2)N
 =448\delta(\delta^2+\gamma^2)
       (\gamma^2+(2\gamma^2-1)\delta^2)(N'+NL).\tag{SF6}
\end{equation}
Every entry is a rational expression with a nonzero denominator
on the original domain. Multiplication by those denominators
reduces SF6 to sixteen polynomial identities over $\mathbb Q(i)$.
The supplied certificate constructs these polynomials from the
displayed expressions and verifies that every coefficient is zero.
The nonzero determinant SF3 then implies SF5. This verification
also proves the determinant in SF3 directly from SF2, so the
calculation does not require an inferred frame rank.

The exact trace provides a separate scalar check:
\begin{equation}
 \tr\Omega_\delta=\frac2\delta+
        \frac{2\delta(2\gamma^2-1)}{\gamma^2+(2\gamma^2-1)\delta^2}.
 \tag{SF7}
\end{equation}
It follows either by adding the diagonal entries SF5 or by
differentiating the actual determinant. In fact, with
$\Delta_\rho=-64\delta^2\gamma^2(\delta^2+\gamma^2)$,
the preserved sign invariant
$\chi=A^2\Delta_\rho\prod_ja_j\in\{1,-1\}$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \det O=1792\chi\delta^2\gamma^2
                    (\gamma^2+(2\gamma^2-1)\delta^2).
 \tag{SF8}
\end{equation}
Indeed $A^2=1/4$, so multiplication of SF3 by
$\prod a_j=4\chi/\Delta_\rho$ gives SF8 with its full sign.
Its logarithmic derivative is precisely SF7.

\section{The exact pole and retained Laurent remainder}
The pole at $\delta=0$ is simple. Its residue, still in the
unchanged original coordinates and with $g=\gamma^2$, is
\begin{equation}
 R_\gamma=\lim_{\delta\to0}\delta\Omega_\delta
 =\begin{pmatrix}
 -\tfrac12&-\dfrac{i}{16g^2}&\dfrac1{16g^2}&0\\[5pt]
 0&\dfrac{4g-1}{16g}&-\dfrac{i}{8g}&\dfrac1{28g}\\[5pt]
 0&-\dfrac{i(4g+1)}{16g}&\dfrac{4g+1}{8g}&\dfrac{i}{28g}\\[5pt]
 0&-\dfrac{7(80g^2+16g-1)}{64g}&\dfrac{7i(1-20g)}{32g}
                                   &\dfrac{28g-1}{16g}
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{SF9}
\end{equation}
It is $B(0,g)/(448g^2)$ by SF5. Define the polynomial matrix
\[
 C(t,g)=\frac{g^2B(t,g)-(t+g)(g+(2g-1)t)B(0,g)}{t}.
\]
The numerator vanishes at $t=0$, entry by entry, so this is
exact polynomial division. The complete remainder identity is
\begin{equation}
 \Omega_\delta=\frac{R_\gamma}{\delta}
       +\delta\,\frac{C(\delta^2,\gamma^2)}
        {448\gamma^4(\delta^2+\gamma^2)
                        (\gamma^2+(2\gamma^2-1)\delta^2)}.
 \tag{SF10}
\end{equation}
The second term is analytic and of order $\delta$ near zero.
No higher terms have been suppressed: its exact rational
numerator and denominator remain specified by SF5 and SF10.
The entry $R_{11}=-1/2$ proves that this pole does not cancel.

For completeness the entire finite complex-$\delta$ pole set
of SF5 is
\begin{equation}
 0,\quad\pm i\gamma,\quad
 \pm\frac{i\gamma}{\sqrt{2\gamma^2-1}}.\tag{SF11}
\end{equation}
All five poles are simple when $\gamma>2$. The denominator in
SF5 has these distinct simple zeros, and its $(1,2)$ numerator
is the nonzero constant $-28i$, so none can be removed from the
matrix. At the pair with $Q=0$ the common residue is
$B(-g,g)/(1792g^2(g-1))$; at the pair with $F=0$ it is
$-(2g-1)B(-g/(2g-1),g)/(1792g^2(g-1))$.
These formulas follow by differentiating the scalar denominator
$448\delta QF$ at its simple zeros. The original positive
interval contains none of these poles except its excluded
endpoint zero.

As a rational matrix function, SF5 has pole order three at
infinity, with exact leading term
\[
 \lim_{\delta\to\infty}\delta^{-3}\Omega_\delta
 =\frac{g}{2g-1}\bigl(4(3g-2)E_{31}
                         +i(312g-253)E_{41}\bigr).
\]
Here $E_{jk}$ is the matrix with its only nonzero entry, equal
to one, in position $(j,k)$. The limit follows from the highest
powers in SF5; its $(3,1)$ entry is nonzero for $g>4$.
If the connection is regarded as the one-form
$\Omega_\delta\,d\delta$, its order at infinity is five,
because $d\delta=-\zeta^{-2}d\zeta$ for $\zeta=1/\delta$.
This statement of complex pole orders does not extend the
original real arithmetic parameter domain.

\section{Residue identities without replacing the original matrix}
Direct expansion of SF9 gives
\begin{equation}
 \det(\lambda I-R_\gamma)
   =(\lambda+\tfrac12)(\lambda-\tfrac12)^2(\lambda-\tfrac32),
 \qquad
 (2R_\gamma+I)(2R_\gamma-I)(2R_\gamma-3I)=0.
 \tag{SF12}
\end{equation}
The characteristic determinant and each entry of the latter
identity are verified as polynomial numerator identities in
$g$ by the exact certificate. The annihilating polynomial has
three distinct roots; its Lagrange interpolation polynomials
in $R_\gamma$ give complementary spectral idempotents.
This proves semisimplicity as a property of the displayed
matrix, rather than assuming a diagonal frame.

Two eigenvectors needed for the native metric statement are
particularly explicit:
\begin{equation}
 e_a=(1,0,0,0)^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 x_+=(0,1,i,35\gamma^2-7/4)^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 R_\gamma e_a=-\tfrac12e_a,\quad
 R_\gamma x_+=\tfrac32x_+.
 \tag{SF13}
\end{equation}
The first follows from the first column of SF9; substitution
in its other three rows proves the second. No coordinates of
the operator have been changed to use these identities.

\section{Exact fixed-conductor transport and Hermitian defect}
Fix one admitted reference conductor and its exact receiving
isomorphism $\Psi_*:\C^4\to W=\cP_3$, as constructed in
\cite{Bridge,Global}. Hold it fixed as $\delta$ varies here.
In the original target monomial basis let $H_{\Gamma,*}$ be
the full original Gram matrix of the norm
\[
 \|p\|_{\Gamma,*}^2=\int_{\mathbb R}|p(c_*+iy)|^2
 \frac{(2\pi)^{s/2}2^{s/2}}{4\pi\Gamma(s/2)}
 |\Gamma(s/4+iy/2)|^2\,dy,
 \qquad c_*=a_{\rm amp,*}/2-4.
\]
The source order $s\in\{1,a_{\rm amp,*}\}$ and every mass
in this expression remain fixed. Put
$G=\Psi_*^*H_{\Gamma,*}\Psi_*>0$. This is an exact pullback
of the original norm, not a replacement by a coefficient norm.

The transported frame and connection are
\begin{equation}
 O_W=\Psi_*O,\qquad
 \Omega_W=O_W'O_W^{-1}=\Psi_*\Omega_\delta\Psi_*^{-1},
 \qquad R_W=\Psi_*R_\gamma\Psi_*^{-1}.
 \tag{SF14}
\end{equation}
The identities follow by differentiation because the reference
map is fixed. In particular every coefficient of the original
receiving map remains in these formulas; no derivative of a
moving reference period has been silently omitted.

The entire original-coordinate Hermitian defect is
\begin{equation}
 \mathscr H_\delta=G\Omega_\delta+\Omega_\delta^*G
    =\frac{G B(\delta^2,g)+B(\delta^2,g)^*G}
                 {448\delta(\delta^2+g)(g+(2g-1)\delta^2)}
 \quad(\delta>0).
 \tag{SF15}
\end{equation}
The denominator is real and positive on the original interval,
so taking the conjugate transpose gives exactly this expression.
For the actual moving-frame Gram matrix $K_\delta=O^*GO$,
ordinary differentiation yields
\begin{equation}
 K_\delta'=O^*\mathscr H_\delta O.
 \tag{SF16}
\end{equation}
Its native determinant is also exact:
\[
 \det K_\delta=1792^2\delta^4\gamma^4
       (\gamma^2+(2\gamma^2-1)\delta^2)^2\det G,
 \qquad
 \partial_\delta\log\det K_\delta
  =\frac4\delta+
    \frac{4\delta(2\gamma^2-1)}{\gamma^2+(2\gamma^2-1)\delta^2}.
\]
The first equality is $\det K=|\det O|^2\det G$ with SF8;
the second is its derivative. Thus the native metric and its
complete mass are retained even where a scalar trace is used.

SF10 proves the operator-norm limits
\begin{equation}
 \delta\Omega_\delta\longrightarrow R_\gamma,
 \qquad
 \delta\mathscr H_\delta\longrightarrow
       G R_\gamma+R_\gamma^*G,
 \qquad
 \delta\|\Omega_W\|_{\Gamma,*}
       \longrightarrow\|R_\gamma\|_G\ge\tfrac32.
 \tag{SF17}
\end{equation}
Continuity of matrix norms in these fixed finite-dimensional
positive inner products gives the limits; the lower bound
follows by applying SF13 to $x_+$. Both signs of the leading
Hermitian defect are present for \emph{every} such fixed
original metric, as the exact identities
\begin{equation}
 e_a^*(G R_\gamma+R_\gamma^*G)e_a=-\|e_a\|_G^2<0,
 \qquad
 x_+^*(G R_\gamma+R_\gamma^*G)x_+=3\|x_+\|_G^2>0
 \tag{SF18}
\end{equation}
show. They follow from the two eigenvector equalities in SF13
and conjugate symmetry; they do not assert that $R_\gamma$
is self-adjoint or normal in this original metric. Equivalently
the physical test vectors are $\Psi_*e_a$ and $\Psi_*x_+$,
and the physical Hermitian defect pulls back exactly to SF15.
The full remainder in all these limits remains the explicit
rational remainder SF10, not an unspecified discarded term.

\section{Exact verification and source use}
The accompanying script
\texttt{derive\_signed\_frame\_connection.py} differentiates
the four labelled columns SF2, forms SF4, and saves their full
rational expressions. The independent display certificate
\texttt{verify\_signed\_frame\_connection.py} reconstructs
the sixteen entries in SF5, checks all sixteen entries of
SF6 by their polynomial numerators over $\mathbb Q(i)$,
checks the determinant SF3, both identities SF12, both vectors
SF13, the trace SF7, and the exact divisibility used in SF10.
No decimal approximation or sample parameter is used.
The captured output is
\texttt{verify\_signed\_frame\_connection\_output.txt}.
