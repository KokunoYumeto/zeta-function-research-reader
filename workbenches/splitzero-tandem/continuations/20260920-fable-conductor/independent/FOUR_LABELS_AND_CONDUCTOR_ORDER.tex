This calculation uses the four \emph{Time} points of the ES--Fable reader,
not its separate binary-character tetrahedron. The two inputs are
\cite[Theorem \texttt{explicit-four-time-Jacobian-collision}]{ES}
and \cite[WCF1--6 and WCF19a]{WCF}. The original conductor coefficients
are retained. No coefficients are fitted to the four points. The result
identifies the precise rank-loss test for a pullback of those coefficients;
it does not assert that an unprovided coefficient map has been constructed.

\section{The four marked source points and their common image}
Fix $\iota=i$ and the positive real square root $r=\sqrt2$. Use the
original source coordinates $(a,y,z,w)$. The four columns are
\begin{align*}
q_{\mathrm M}&=(0,0,i/2,0)^{\mathsf T},&
q_{\mathrm N}&=(i,-1,-3i,13)^{\mathsf T},\\
q_{\mathrm T}&=(1/r,-1-ri,2r+6i,-34-19ri)^{\mathsf T},&
q_{\mathrm E}&=(1/r,1-ri,-2r+6i,34-19ri)^{\mathsf T}.
\end{align*}
Their common target under the reader's polynomial map $P_i$ is
$h=(0,0,-1,0)^{\mathsf T}$. For completeness the exact polynomial map is
\begin{align*}
P_0={}&a^3z+2a^2y-ia,\\
P_2={}&-a^3y^2z-2ia^2yz+a^2w-2a^2y^3-10iay^2+3az+y,\\
P_3={}&2a^3y^3z+6ia^2y^2z+2a^2wy+4a^2y^4+2iaw
       -4iay^3-2ayz+2iz+7y^2,\\
P_4={}&2a^3y^4z+8ia^2y^3z+a^2wy^2+4a^2y^5+2iawy
       +7iay^4-10ay^2z-4iyz-w-3y^3.
\end{align*}
The reader proves $\det DP_i=-2$. Its four evaluations and that complete
polynomial determinant have additionally been checked by the exact
symbolic certificate accompanying this note; no floating-point test is
used. The coordinate calculation below does not require a claim about
the global conjecture to which the reader relates this map.

\begin{proposition}[The full labelled collision algebra]
The matrix $Q=[q_{\mathrm M}\ q_{\mathrm N}\ q_{\mathrm T}\ q_{\mathrm E}]$
is invertible and
\begin{equation}
 \det Q=\frac{77\sqrt2\,i}{2}.\tag{FC1}
\end{equation}
Let $X$ be the reduced scheme consisting of these four points and let
$Y=\{h\}$ be their reduced common image. In the order
$(\mathrm M,\mathrm N,\mathrm T,\mathrm E)$ the exact pullback of functions
is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal O(Y)=\C\longrightarrow\mathcal O(X)=\C^4,
 \qquad c\longmapsto(c,c,c,c).\tag{FC2}
\end{equation}
The dual pushforward of point masses is
\begin{equation}
 \C^4\longrightarrow\C,\qquad
 (c_{\mathrm M},c_{\mathrm N},c_{\mathrm T},c_{\mathrm E})
 \longmapsto c_{\mathrm M}+c_{\mathrm N}+c_{\mathrm T}+c_{\mathrm E}.
 \tag{FC3}
\end{equation}
Its kernel is the three-dimensional space with the ordered basis
$e_{\mathrm N}-e_{\mathrm M},e_{\mathrm T}-e_{\mathrm M},
e_{\mathrm E}-e_{\mathrm M}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Expansion along the first column of $Q$ gives
\[
 \det Q=\frac i2\det\begin{pmatrix}
 i&1/r&1/r\\-1&-1-ri&1-ri\\13&-34-19ri&34-19ri
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Subtract its second column from its third. The last column becomes
$(0,2,68)^{\mathsf T}$. Expansion yields
\[
 i\{68(-1-ri)-2(-34-19ri)\}
 -r^{-1}\{-68-26\}=30r+94/r=77r.
\]
This proves FC1. The four columns are consequently distinct.
For each pair of distinct points choose a coordinate taking different
values at them. Products of the corresponding affine linear factors
give a polynomial which is one at a selected point and zero at the
other three. Evaluation of polynomials therefore surjects onto
$\C^4$, with kernel the ideal of $X$. All four values of $P_i$ equal
$h$, so composition with $P_i$ gives the constant column, proving FC2.
Duality gives FC3 and the stated kernel directly.
\end{proof}

Thus the linear map of \emph{point masses} has a kernel although all
four differential maps $DP_i(q_T)$ are invertible. These are exact
different arrows, related by the displayed pullback and duality. A
permutation $\pi$ of the four labels acts on $\C^4$ by its permutation
matrix $\Pi_\pi$. In the original ambient coordinates its action on
the four columns is $Q\Pi_\pi Q^{-1}$. FC3 is invariant under this
action. This assertion concerns the four marked points and makes no
unproved equivariance assertion for $P_i$ away from them.

\section{The original weighted conductor and its order of vanishing}
Use all of WCF1's original coefficients $a_{uw}$ and its original shifts
\[
 \beta_{8,u,w}=4+(2u-8)\delta+i(2w-8)\gamma,
 \qquad
 E(z)=\sum_{u,w=0}^8a_{uw}e^{\beta_{8,u,w}z}.
\]
Write $\mu_j=E^{(j)}(0)$ and $v=\operatorname{ord}_0E$.
Every finite $v$ has $\mu_v\ne0$; set $v=\infty$ when $E=0$.
The shift operator, before taking any quotient, is
\begin{equation}
 TP(S')=\sum_{u,w=0}^8a_{uw}P(S'+\beta_{8,u,w}).\tag{FC4}
\end{equation}
Here $\cP_N=\C[S]_{\le N}$ and $\cP_{-1}=0$. Direct use of the
binomial theorem gives
\begin{equation}
 T(S^n)=\sum_{j=0}^n\binom nj\mu_j(S')^{n-j}.\tag{FC5}
\end{equation}

The original Hermitian norms are those of WCF2, with
$s\in\{1,a\}$, $a=4l+1\ge9$, $c_0=a/2$, $c_1=a/2-4$:
\begin{gather*}
 dm_s(y)=\frac{(2\pi)^{s/2}2^{s/2}}{4\pi\Gamma(s/2)}
 |\Gamma(s/4+iy/2)|^2\,dy,\qquad
 \|P\|_{s,c}^2=\int_{\mathbb R}|P(c+iy)|^2dm_s(y),\\
 h_{n,s}=(2\pi)^{s/2}n!(s/2)_n,\qquad
 \rho_n=\sqrt{n!/(s/2)_n},\\
 p_{0,s}=1,\quad p_{1,s}=y,\quad
 p_{n+1,s}=yp_{n,s}-n(n-1+s/2)p_{n-1,s},\qquad
 \phi_{n,s,c}(S)=p_{n,s}((S-c)/i)/\sqrt{h_{n,s}}.
\end{gather*}
The Gamma mass and the phase $i^{-n}$ have not been divided out.
The classical polynomial identities and their human provenance are
given in WCF2--3 and DLMF 18.19, 18.22 and 18.23 \cite{DLMF}.

\begin{proposition}[Exact fixed-cutoff rank, including order jumps]
Fix integers $v_0,D\ge0$ and $N=D+v_0$. The original operator FC4
induces the displayed arrow
\begin{equation}
 A^{[v_0]}:\cP_N/\cP_{v_0-1}\longrightarrow\cP_D\tag{FC6}
\end{equation}
if and only if $\mu_0=\cdots=\mu_{v_0-1}=0$.
When it is defined and $v<\infty$, its rank, kernel and image are
\begin{align}
 \rk A^{[v_0]}&=\max\{D+1-(v-v_0),0\},\tag{FC7}\\
 \ker A^{[v_0]}&=\cP_{\min(v-1,N)}/\cP_{v_0-1},\tag{FC8}\\
 \operatorname{im}A^{[v_0]}&=\cP_{N-v}\quad(v\le N),\tag{FC9}
\end{align}
and its image is zero for $v>N$. If $E=0$, the map is zero.
It is invertible exactly when $v=v_0$. In the full orthonormal
quotient frame of WCF2 its determinant is
\begin{equation}
 \det A^{[v_0]}=
 \left(\frac{(-i)^{v_0}\mu_{v_0}}{v_0!}\right)^{D+1}
 \prod_{j=0}^D\frac{\rho_{j+v_0}}{\rho_j}.\tag{FC10}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
To descend to the quotient the operator must vanish on
$1,S,\ldots,S^{v_0-1}$. Evaluation of FC5 at $S'=0$ proves that
this requires $\mu_j=0$ for $j<v_0$, and the same formula proves
sufficiency both for descent and for the asserted codomain.
If $n\ge v$, the leading coefficient of FC5 is
$\binom nv\mu_v$, which is nonzero, and its degree is $n-v$.
If $n<v$ its image is zero. Triangular elimination, in the degrees
$v,\ldots,N$, proves FC7--9 without any assumption on later moments.

For the weighted assertion put
\[
 w(t)=-i\arctan t,\qquad
 g_{v_0}(t)=t^{-v_0}e^{-4w(t)}E(w(t)).
\]
The moment vanishing already proved is exactly the condition that this
function is holomorphic at zero. The generating identity WCF3 applied
to FC4, with the original centre difference $c_0-c_1=4$, gives
\[
 A^{[v_0]}_{ij}=\begin{cases}
 [t^{j-i}]g_{v_0}(t)\,\rho_{j+v_0}/\rho_i,&i\le j,\\
 0,&i>j.
 \end{cases}
\]
Its constant coefficient is $(-i)^{v_0}\mu_{v_0}/v_0!$.
Multiplication of the diagonal entries proves FC10. All factors
$\rho_j$ are positive. When $v>v_0$, the first nonzero coefficient
of $g_{v_0}$ is in degree $v-v_0$, also recovering FC7 in the exact
weighted frame.
\end{proof}

The preceding proposition is a complete finite calculation, applicable
pointwise to every actual coefficient evaluation. It is not an
assumption that the moment order is constant. For any actual parameter
space carrying the original coefficients and shifts, the order-$v$
stratum is precisely
\[
 \{\mu_0=\cdots=\mu_{v-1}=0,\ \mu_v\ne0\}.
\]
The singular set of the fixed arrow FC6 is precisely the additional
equation $\mu_{v_0}=0$ on its domain. If $\mu_j\ne0$ for some
$j<v_0$, FC6 is undefined there; it is not a noninvertible instance
of that arrow. Replacing the source kernel by the \emph{actual} kernel
gives, for $v\le N$, the always invertible map
\[
 \cP_N/\cP_{v-1}\longrightarrow\cP_{N-v}.
\]
This changes the displayed source and target dimensions and must be
recorded, rather than identifying two different cutoffs.

Because there are at most 81 exponential terms, the identically zero
case is detected by the first 81 moments. Indeed first collect equal
shifts, leaving distinct values $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_m$, $m\le81$,
with collected coefficients $b_j$. The equations
$\mu_0=\cdots=\mu_{m-1}=0$ read
$(\alpha_j^n)_{0\le n<m,1\le j\le m}b=0$. This Vandermonde matrix
has determinant $\prod_{j<k}(\alpha_k-\alpha_j)\ne0$, so every
$b_j=0$. This proof retains repeated contributions by collecting their
exact sum and works when shifts themselves coincide.

\section{The exact four-label receiving map}
The original WCF map has a canonical extension to the four labelled
points: scalar extension from $\C$ to $\mathcal O(X)=\C^4$. It is
\begin{equation}
 A^{[v_0]}_X=A^{[v_0]}\otimes I_4.\tag{FC11}
\end{equation}
This expression keeps all four points even when their common image is
represented by the same coordinates. Its determinant and rank are
\begin{equation}
 \det A^{[v_0]}_X=(\det A^{[v_0]})^4,\qquad
 \rk A^{[v_0]}_X=4\rk A^{[v_0]}.\tag{FC12}
\end{equation}
These follow by reordering the tensor basis into four blocks. The
four-copy inner product is the direct sum of the four original WCF
inner products; the corresponding source mass is not changed.
The map obtained by forgetting the labels is induced by FC3 and has
the exact commuting square
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
 (\cP_N/\cP_{v_0-1})\otimes\C^4
 &\xrightarrow{A^{[v_0]}\otimes I_4}&\cP_D\otimes\C^4\\
 \downarrow I\otimes\Sigma&&\downarrow I\otimes\Sigma\\
 \cP_N/\cP_{v_0-1}&\xrightarrow{A^{[v_0]}}&\cP_D,
\end{array}
\qquad \Sigma(c)=\sum_Tc_T.
\]
Equality of the two compositions is bilinearity. The vertical kernels
are precisely the tensor products with $\ker\Sigma$. If $v=v_0$,
the upper arrow restricts to an isomorphism on these two kernels.
Thus the collision's three directions survive as labelled directions
and do not become a kernel of the horizontal conductor map.

In original coordinate columns, use $Q$ from FC1. This is a change of
frame for the four labels, and it transports the label metric to
$(Q^{-1})^*Q^{-1}$ on the raw coordinate space: the equality
$\|Q^{-1}x\|^2=x^*(Q^{-1})^*Q^{-1}x$ proves the assertion. The
identity
\[
 (I\otimes Q)(A^{[v_0]}\otimes I_4)(I\otimes Q^{-1})
 =A^{[v_0]}\otimes I_4
\]
follows by tensor multiplication. Hence the actual nonorthogonal
coordinates of the four points change neither FC10 nor FC12.

FC11 is the exact base-change relation supplied by the four-point
collision alone. A nonconstant map from those source coordinates into
the programme's original conductor coefficients is additional data.
Once that concrete map is supplied, FC5 and FC10 give its exact
singularity test, including every moment-order jump. This note has not
invented such a coefficient map or replaced the original symbol by an
interpolating polynomial.

\section{The four original conductor incidences inside every moment}
Retain the original ordered quartet
\[
 (\rho_1,\rho_2,\rho_3,\rho_4)
 =(\tfrac12+\delta+i\gamma,\tfrac12+\delta-i\gamma,
 \tfrac12-\delta+i\gamma,\tfrac12-\delta-i\gamma).
\]
Write the original degree-eight conductor, in its original period
coordinates, as
\[
 P(t)=A_H(Ht)=\sum_{|m|=8}c_m t^m.
\]
No entry of the original matrix $H$ has been removed. Substituting
$t_j=e^{\rho_jz}$ sends a monomial to the WCF exponential indexed by
$u=m_1+m_2$, $w=m_1+m_3$. Consequently the full collected coefficients
are
\begin{equation}
 a_{u,w}=\sum_{\substack{|m|=8\\m_1+m_2=u\\m_1+m_3=w}}c_m.
 \tag{FC13}
\end{equation}
The four corner index pairs have only one monomial each. Therefore
\begin{equation}
 P(e_1)=a_{8,8},\quad P(e_2)=a_{8,0},\quad
 P(e_3)=a_{0,8},\quad P(e_4)=a_{0,0}.\tag{FC14}
\end{equation}
Define $\mathcal D_\rho=\sum_j\rho_jt_j\partial_{t_j}$.
The chain rule gives
\[
 \frac{d}{dz}P(e^{\rho_1z},\ldots,e^{\rho_4z})
 =(\mathcal D_\rho P)(e^{\rho_1z},\ldots,e^{\rho_4z}).
\]
Applying this identity successively to $\mathcal D_\rho^jP$ proves,
for every $n\ge0$, the complete original moment formula
\begin{equation}
 \mu_n=\left.\mathcal D_\rho^nP\right|_{t=(1,1,1,1)}
 =\sum_{j=1}^4 P(e_j)(8\rho_j)^n
 +\sum_{(u,w)\notin\{(8,8),(8,0),(0,8),(0,0)\}}
     a_{u,w}\beta_{8,u,w}^{\,n}.\tag{FC15}
\end{equation}
The second equality follows by applying the diagonal operator to every
monomial and using FC13--14. It includes every repeated contribution.

Thus the four original point-conductor incidences are four exact
coordinates in the original moment test, with an explicit remaining
77-coordinate sum. They are not another choice of conductor. Their
simultaneous vanishing removes precisely those four corner terms.
For a nonzero symbol supported on $r$ distinct remaining exponents,
the Vandermonde proof above gives $v\le r-1$; in particular deleting
the four corners gives $v\le76$. This is support information supplied
by the four points, not a kernel of the map with its actual vanishing
order. FC10 tests a fixed old quotient after all retained terms of
FC15 have been evaluated.
