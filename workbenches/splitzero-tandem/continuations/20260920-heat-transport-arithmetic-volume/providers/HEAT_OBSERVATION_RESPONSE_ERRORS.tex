We carry HM19 into the original responses of ORE8 and the three actual
centered pairings of ORE11--12. The source classes, observation map,
period, arithmetic coefficient and physical coordinate remain fixed.
Only the metric in their explicit evaluation is replaced by its stated
heat approximation. In particular the approximating boundary columns
below are the \emph{original} columns, not newly chosen monic polynomials
for the heat source.

\section{Every observation cross term receives a finite bound}
At an original cutoff $N$ write $G=G_{k,N}$ and
$G'=G_{k,N}^{[J]}$. Retain $\Lambda:E\to B=\operatorname{im}\Lambda$,
$K=\ker\Lambda$ and the same original two boundary vectors
$a_1=b_N$, $a_2=b_{N+1}$ and terminal class $v$. HM19 proves
\begin{equation}\tag{HR1}
 \alpha G\preceq G'\preceq\beta G,
 \quad \alpha=(1-\delta)^{2k},\quad\beta=(1+\delta)^{2k},
 \quad\delta=2^{-(J+1)/2}c_N<1.
\end{equation}
The full prequotient constant $c_N$ and all its arithmetic cross blocks
are those of HM10. Put $\eta=\max(1-\alpha,\beta-1)=\beta-1$;
the equality follows from the even terms in the binomial expansion
of $(1+\delta)^{2k}+(1-\delta)^{2k}\ge2$.
Use the exact original quotient and kernel forms
\begin{equation}\tag{HR2}
 Q=(\Lambda G^{-1}\Lambda^*)^{-1},\quad
 \Omega=\Lambda^*Q\Lambda,\quad L=G-\Omega,
\end{equation}
and define $Q',\Omega',L'$ with $G'$ and the same $\Lambda$.
These formulas include zero-dimensional image or kernel with the
usual empty-matrix conventions. The exact minimum-norm lift proves
$0\preceq\Omega\preceq G$ and $L=GP_K$, where $P_K$ is the
$G$-orthogonal projection onto the original kernel. Indeed the lift
of $b\in B$ is $G^{-1}\Lambda^*Qb$, whose difference from any
other lift is orthogonal to it. This is the original Schur minimum,
also described by Boyd and Vandenberghe \cite[Appendix C.4.1]{Boyd}.

\begin{theorem}[Full cross-term error]
For all original coefficient vectors $x,y\in E$,
\begin{equation}\tag{HR3}
 \begin{aligned}
 |x^*(G'-G)y|&\le\eta\sqrt{x^*Gx}\sqrt{y^*Gy},\\
 |x^*(\Omega'-\Omega)y|&\le\eta\sqrt{x^*Gx}\sqrt{y^*Gy},\\
 |x^*(L'-L)y|&\le2\eta\sqrt{x^*Gx}\sqrt{y^*Gy}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The observed fraction of the retained norm has the sharper ratio bound
\begin{equation}\tag{HR4}
 \frac\alpha\beta(1-\theta)\le1-\theta'
                 \le\frac\beta\alpha(1-\theta),\qquad
 \theta=\frac{v^*Lv}{v^*Gv},\quad
 \theta'=\frac{v^*L'v}{v^*G'v}.
\end{equation}
If $\Lambda v=0$, both observed fractions are exactly zero.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Conjugating (HR1) by $G^{-1/2}$ bounds the Hermitian operator norm of
$G^{-1/2}(G'-G)G^{-1/2}$ by $\eta$. Cauchy--Schwarz gives the
first inequality. Taking the minimum over each unchanged fibre
$\Lambda x=b$ in (HR1) gives $\alpha Q\preceq Q'\preceq\beta Q$.
Pulling back gives
$-\eta\Omega\preceq\Omega'-\Omega\preceq\eta\Omega$.
As $0\preceq\Omega\preceq G$, the same conjugation proves the
second inequality. Subtract the two corrections in (HR2), retaining
both, to obtain the third. For (HR4) use
$v^*\Omega v=(v^*Gv)(1-\theta)$ and its primed identity. The
numerator and denominator each have factors between $\alpha$ and
$\beta$, giving the stated ratios. Their numerator is zero in both
metrics exactly when $\Lambda v=0$.
\end{proof}

\section{The original response ratios and their amplification}
Write $E=v^*Gv$, $d_i=a_i^*Ga_i$ and retain the original quantities
\begin{equation}\tag{HR5}
 F_i=a_i^*Gv,\quad K_i=a_i^*Lv,\quad
 p_i=\frac{|F_i|^2}{d_iE},\quad
 U=\frac{K_1}{F_1},\quad V=\frac{K_2}{F_2}.
\end{equation}
On the original terminal-class domain the two $F_i$ are nonzero by
the full source calculation RBE12--19 and FW4--5, so $0<p_i\le1$.
All these numbers use the full original minimum. Define $F'_i,K'_i$
and $U',V'$ by the same vectors and (HR2), now evaluated in $G'$.
For an explicit finite guard choose the heat depth so
$\eta<\min(\sqrt{p_1},\sqrt{p_2})$. Such a depth always exists by
HM24, with all original constants retained. Then
\begin{equation}\tag{HR6}
 \begin{gathered}
 |F'_i|\ge(\sqrt{p_i}-\eta)\sqrt{d_iE}>0,\\
 |U'-U|\le e_U:=\frac{\eta(2+|U|)}{\sqrt{p_1}-\eta},\qquad
 |V'-V|\le e_V:=\frac{\eta(2+|V|)}{\sqrt{p_2}-\eta}.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Indeed (HR3) gives $|F'_i-F_i|\le\eta\sqrt{d_iE}$ and
$|K'_i-K_i|\le2\eta\sqrt{d_iE}$. The exact identity
\[
 \frac{K'_i}{F'_i}-\frac{K_i}{F_i}
 =\frac{(K'_i-K_i)-(K_i/F_i)(F'_i-F_i)}{F'_i}
\]
proves every factor and denominator in (HR6).

There is also a bound using only original source energies and the
observed fraction. Positivity of $L$, $a_i^*La_i\le d_i$, and
$v^*Lv=\theta E$ give
\begin{equation}\tag{HR7}
 |U|\le\sqrt{\theta/p_1},\qquad |V|\le\sqrt{\theta/p_2}.
\end{equation}
Thus the right sides in (HR6) can be bounded with the original
$p_i,\theta$. Whenever the sharper original conductor bounds FW50
are used, they can replace (HR7) in exactly the same formula; no
denominator or amplification is removed. For example, the finite
guard $\eta\le\tfrac12\min(\sqrt{p_1},\sqrt{p_2})$ gives the
coarser completely explicit bounds $e_U\le6\eta/p_1$,
$e_V\le6\eta/p_2$, since $\theta\le1$ and $p_i\le1$.

\section{The three original centered pairings}
Retain the original complex arithmetic coefficient
$\mathfrak c=\mathfrak c_{N,\lambda}$ from ORE12. This is fixed
in the following approximation of the \emph{original} evaluation;
it is not recomputed from a different source action. Put
\begin{equation}\tag{HR8}
 \begin{gathered}
 P_K=\overline U V,\quad
 P_B=(1-\overline U)(1-V),\quad
 P_\times=1-P_K-P_B,\\
 \Phi_X=2E\operatorname{Re}(\mathfrak cP_X),\qquad
 \Phi'_X=2E'\operatorname{Re}(\mathfrak cP'_X),\quad
 E'=v^*G'v,
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
where each primed product uses $U',V'$ and the same displayed
complex conjugations. These are the original kernel, observation
and complementary cross products. Expanding each product without
discarding its quadratic correction proves
\begin{equation}\tag{HR9}
 \begin{aligned}
 |P'_K-P_K|&\le b_K:=|V|e_U+|U|e_V+e_Ue_V,\\
 |P'_B-P_B|&\le b_B:=|1-V|e_U+|1-U|e_V+e_Ue_V,\\
 |P'_\times-P_\times|&\le b_\times:=b_K+b_B.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Consequently the actual current evaluation has the explicit error
\begin{equation}\tag{HR10}
 |\Phi'_X-\Phi_X|
 \le 2E|\mathfrak c|\,[\eta|P_X|+\beta b_X],
 \qquad X\in\{K,B,\times\}.
\end{equation}
To check (HR10), write its difference as
$2(E'-E)\operatorname{Re}(\mathfrak cP_X)
 +2E'\operatorname{Re}(\mathfrak c(P'_X-P_X))$.
Use $|E'-E|\le\eta E$ and $E'\le\beta E$ from (HR1).
The identities $\sum_XP_X=\sum_XP'_X=1$ are exact. In particular
\begin{equation}\tag{HR11}
 \sum_X\Phi_X=2E\operatorname{Re}\mathfrak c,\quad
 \sum_X\Phi'_X=2E'\operatorname{Re}\mathfrak c,\quad
 \left|\sum_X(\Phi'_X-\Phi_X)\right|
 \le2\eta E|\operatorname{Re}\mathfrak c|.
\end{equation}
For every prescribed finite cutoff family these estimates also sum
with the original absolute weights, by the triangle inequality.
All terms on the right are original coefficients or their proved
bounds, so the errors tend to zero as $J\to\infty$ while this finite
family is fixed. The small denominators in (HR6) remain visible.
These formulas provide the required error control for an evaluation
of the original projected signs; they do not assign those signs
without evaluating the original responses.
