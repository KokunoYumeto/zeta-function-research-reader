\section{The original conductor on every chart and at the vertex}
Work first over an algebraically closed field $K$ of characteristic
different from five.  Let $\zeta$ have order five and let the cyclic
group act on $S=K[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4]$ by $g x_i=\zeta^i x_i$.
The inverse-action convention gives the same proof with every character
negated.  Let $Q$ be the unchanged invertible linear transport of
$t_1t_4-t_2t_3$, assume its five translates $Q_a=g^aQ$ are pairwise
nonassociate, and retain
\[
 P=\prod_{a=0}^4Q_a,\qquad A=\prod_{a=1}^4Q_a,\qquad
 R=S/(Q),\qquad B=\im(S^G\to R).                              \tag{CR1}
\]
The Segre determinant is irreducible in every such characteristic:
view it as a primitive degree-one polynomial in $t_1$ over the
factorial ring $K[t_2,t_3,t_4]$.  Its two coefficients are coprime,
and it is irreducible over that ring's fraction field.  An invertible
linear substitution preserves irreducibility.  Thus each $Q_a$ is
prime.  If an invariant polynomial is divisible by $Q$, applying
the action shows it is divisible by all $Q_a$, hence by $P$.
After cancellation, its quotient by the invariant $P$ is invariant.
Therefore
\[
 B=S^G/(P S^G).                                                \tag{CR2}
\]

For $h\in S$, the invariant orbit sum $\sum_{a=0}^4g^a(Ah)$ has
restriction $Ah$ to $Q=0$.  Indeed for every $a\ne0$ its product
$g^aA$ contains $Q$, while the $a=0$ term is the displayed one.
There is no factor of five.  Hence $AR\subset B$ and, since it
is an ideal of $R$, it is contained in the conductor.

To prove the reverse containment, choose a coordinate $x_i$ and set
$T=S[x_i^{-1}]$, $U=T^G$.  The exact character decomposition is
\[
 T=\bigoplus_{r=0}^4 U x_i^r
   \simeq U[t]/(t^5-x_i^5).                                   \tag{CR3}
\]
For a monomial of character $j$, choose the unique $r$ with
$ir\equiv j\pmod5$ and divide it by $x_i^r$; the quotient is
invariant.  Character independence proves the direct sum.  The
derivative $5t^4$ is a unit.  Thus CR3 is finite free, faithfully
flat and etale of degree five, with its original cyclic action.
Modulo the invariant $P$, it remains a faithfully flat map
$B_i=U/(P)\to D_i=T/(P)$.  Here localizing $B$ at $x_i^5$
gives exactly $B_i$, and localizing $R$ gives $T/(Q)$.

The torsor isomorphism
\[
 T\otimes_U T\longrightarrow\prod_{a=0}^4T,
 \qquad f\otimes h\longmapsto(fg^a(h))_a
\]
can be proved by expressing both sides in the basis CR3: its
matrix is the Fourier matrix times invertible powers of $x_i$.
After imposing $Q$ on the second factor and $P$ on the first,
the base change of $B_i\subset R_i$ is precisely
\[
 D_i\longrightarrow\prod_{a=0}^4T/(Q_a),
 \qquad f\longmapsto(f\bmod Q_a)_a.                            \tag{CR4}
\]
The conductor of CR4 on component $a$ is the image of
$\prod_{b\ne a}Q_b$.  To prove it, multiply any conductor element
by the idempotent selecting component $a$.  Its resulting one-component
tuple is in the image exactly when it lifts to a polynomial divisible
by every other $Q_b$.  In the localization of the original polynomial
UFD their ideal intersection equals the product ideal: divisibility
by each pairwise nonassociate prime is equivalent to divisibility by
their product.  Conversely every such lift gives a one-component
tuple, and addition handles all components.

This equality descends faithfully to the original chart, giving
the conductor $AR_i$.  One may see the descent without an implicit
invariance assertion: choose finitely many generators of $R_i$ as
a $B_i$-module; the condition $fR_i\subset B_i$ is the simultaneous
vanishing of their products with $f$ in $R_i/B_i$.  Flat base change
preserves these finite kernels, and faithful flatness detects both
the vanishing and equality of the resulting submodules.  The base
change of $AR_i$ is exactly the component product ideals in CR4.

The conductor is homogeneous, so consider a homogeneous $f\in R_d$.
If it is in the conductor then the chart result gives $f\in AR_i$
for every nonzero-coordinate chart.  Under the original Segre map,
$R_d=K[Z_0,Z_1,W_0,W_1]_{d,d}$ and $A$ is the complete biform of
degree $(8,8)$.  Every irreducible bihomogeneous factor of $A$
defines a projective divisor on $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$.
Chartwise divisibility implies that the factor's multiplicity in
$f$ is at least its full multiplicity in $A$.  UFD divisibility
in this four-variable biform ring gives $f=Ah$, with bidegree
$\deg h=(d-8,d-8)$.  For $d<8$ this forces $f=0$; otherwise $h$
is an element of $R_{d-8}$.  There is therefore no additional
term supported only at the affine vertex, and
\[
 \boxed{\{f\in R:fR\subset B\}=AR.}                            \tag{CR5}
\]
This proof retains repeated factors of the restrictions of the
different $Q_a$; it never assumes those restrictions are coprime.

\section{All sixteen cofactor observations and the four-step test}
Put $J_d=B_d/(AR)_d$.  The sixteen maps are the actual products
\[
 X_d:J_d\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^4\bigoplus_{r=1}^4
                          (R/B)_{d+4r},
 \qquad [f]\longmapsto([x_i^{4r}f])_{i,r}.                       \tag{CR6}
\]
They are well-defined because $AR$ is an ideal contained in $B$.
If $f\in B$ and all sixteen products belong to $B$, then on the
$i$th chart the products of $f$ with
$1,x_i^4,x_i^8,x_i^{12},x_i^{16}$ all belong to $B_i$.
These five powers have distinct characters $0,4i,3i,2i,i$ and
generate $T$ over $U$; powers differing by five differ by the
invariant unit $x_i^5$.  Their restrictions therefore generate
$R_i$ over $B_i$.  Thus $fR_i\subset B_i$ on every chart, and
CR5 implies $f\in AR$.  This proves $X_d$ injective in every
degree and in every characteristic different from five.

The one-step condition uses only $x_i^4$.  Write
\[
 \mathcal T=B+x_1^4B+x_2^4B+x_3^4B+x_4^4B\subset R.            \tag{CR7}
\]
For an invariant representative of the original proper-source
class, the four tests modulo $AR$ are equivalent to literal
$x_i^4f\in B$, since $AR\subset B$.  Thus they give
$f\mathcal T\subset B$.

The original ring $R$ is finite over $B$: $S$ is finite over its
invariants, as witnessed by the equations $x_i^5\in S^G$, and
passing to $S/(Q)$ preserves this finite generation.  On a nonzero
coordinate chart, a geometric fibre of $R/B$ is a reduced subset
of the five points of the etale torsor CR3.  If it has $r$ points
and its representative has $l$ nonzero coordinates, the values of
$1$ and those $l$ products $x_i^4$ form nonzero column scalings
of the five-character Fourier matrix, restricted to $r$ rows.

Every square minor of this Fourier matrix is nonzero.  In sizes
one and two the determinants are respectively a root of unity
and a nonzero root multiple of $1-\zeta^a$ with $a\ne0\pmod5$.
Its inverse has entries $\zeta^{-ij}/5$; this formula is algebraic
and applies in residue fields, where a complex-conjugation symbol
would not be the correct definition.  The complementary-minor
identity for an invertible matrix proves sizes three and four
from sizes two and one of this inverse.  The full determinant
is the nonzero Vandermonde.  Hence whenever $r\le l+1$, these
sections span the whole fibre.  The finite cokernel of
$\mathcal T\subset R$ vanishes at that base point: if a finite
generating column $v$ satisfies $v=Mv$ with the entries of $M$
in the local maximal ideal, the adjugate of $I-M$ and its unit
determinant give $v=0$.

For a projective divisor, all remaining failures are now explicit.
If $l=4$, $r\le5=l+1$.  If $l=3$, failure requires $r=5$.
The six quadratic monomials in the three active coordinates
have five distinct characters, exactly one repeated.  Fourier
inversion of the five equations $Q(g^a x)=0$ makes every
singleton-character coefficient vanish, since its active-coordinate
monomial is nonzero.  Thus the coordinate-plane restriction of
$Q$ must be a single-character conic.  If $l=2$, failure requires
$r\ge4$; the three binary quadratic monomials have distinct
characters and the corresponding three-column Fourier matrix
has full rank on any three rows.  All three coefficients vanish,
so the coordinate line is contained in $Q$.  The locus $l=1$
contains only finitely many projective points and no divisor of
the smooth quadric surface.

Exclude these six line containments and four single-character
plane restrictions.  Then $\mathcal T=R$ at the generic point
of every divisor.  The relation $f\mathcal T\subset B$ makes
$f$ a conductor element there.  The full multiplicity argument
at the end of CR5 again gives $f\in AR$.  This proves vanishing
of the original one-step proper-source kernel on exactly the
stated locus, with every original low-jet restriction still imposed.

\section{The complete period transformation and limiting gates}
For the complex original period, retain
\[
 H(u)=e^{\mathfrak a/u}G_u\mathcal R(1/u)V^{-1}U,
 \quad U=\diag(a,\bar a,\bar a,a),\quad a\ne0,\quad z=1/u.
                                                                    \tag{CR8}
\]
The exact coordinate map $X=G_u^{-1}x$ commutes with the original
diagonal cyclic action.  Multiplication of the actual quadric
equation by $e^{2\mathfrak a/u}$ gives
\[
 \widetilde Q_z(X)=Q_0(U^{-1}V\mathcal R(z)^{-1}X).              \tag{CR9}
\]
The cofactor products are multiplied by the actual nonzero
entries $(G_u)_{ii}^{4r}$, so their kernels transport in both
directions.  This is not asserted to be an isometry.

Retain $r=\delta+i\gamma$, $R_0=r^2$, $T_0=\bar r^2$,
$\beta=-(R_0+T_0)>0$, $\eta=R_0T_0>0$, $p=a^{-2}$,
$b=\bar a^{-2}$, and
$u_0=(2R_0-T_0)/3$, $v_0=(2T_0-R_0)/3$.
The original centered Vandermonde has rows
$(1,r,r^2,r^3)$, $(1,\bar r,\bar r^2,\bar r^3)$,
$(1,-\bar r,\bar r^2,-\bar r^3)$,
$(1,-r,r^2,-r^3)$.  Multiplication by
$J^{-1}=I+\beta E_{13}/3+2\beta E_{24}/3$ and expansion of the
two original products in $Q_0$ gives
\[
 \widetilde Q_0=p(X_1+u_0X_3)^2-b(X_1+v_0X_3)^2
      -pR_0(X_2-v_0X_4)^2+bT_0(X_2-u_0X_4)^2.                   \tag{CR10}
\]
Thus, in the even and odd index blocks, its symmetric entries are
\[
 \begin{array}{lll}
 e_0=p-b,&e_1=pu_0-bv_0,&e_2=pu_0^2-bv_0^2,\\
 o_0=-pR_0+bT_0,&o_1=pR_0v_0-bT_0u_0,
                    &o_2=-pR_0v_0^2+bT_0u_0^2.
 \end{array}                                                     \tag{CR11}
\]
Expanding each two-by-two determinant proves
\[
 e_0e_2-e_1^2=-pb(R_0-T_0)^2\ne0,\qquad
 o_0o_2-o_1^2=-pbR_0T_0(R_0-T_0)^2\ne0.                         \tag{CR12}
\]
Here $R_0-T_0=4i\delta\gamma\ne0$ and
$R_0+T_0=2(\delta^2-\gamma^2)\ne0$ on the actual domain.

The possible repeated-character pairs of the four coordinate
planes, in order of the omitted coordinate, are
\[
 (X_3^2,X_2X_4),\quad (X_1^2,X_3X_4),\quad
 (X_4^2,X_1X_2),\quad (X_2^2,X_1X_3).
\]
For the first plane, single-character failure would require
$o_0=o_2=0$, which forces $u_0^2=v_0^2$, contrary to
$(u_0-v_0)(u_0+v_0)=(R_0-T_0)(R_0+T_0)/3\ne0$.
For the fourth plane, $e_0=e_2=0$ gives the same contradiction.
For the second plane $e_1,e_2$ cannot both vanish by CR12; they
are singleton-character coefficients.  For the third plane the
corresponding pair is $o_0,o_1$.  This proves a nonzero
constant-term gate on every plane.

The exact sufficient condition here is that one singleton-character
coefficient be nonzero: on the locus where all three coordinates are
active its monomial is nonzero, so the five Fourier equations cannot
all hold.  This argument does not require that the restriction have
two different characters at every later period.  For completeness,
the limiting restriction itself cannot have only a singleton character.
On the plane omitting $X_2$ it is
$e_0X_1^2+2e_1X_1X_3+e_2X_3^2+o_2X_4^2$, whose four terms have
distinct characters.  Only the $e_1$ term could by itself retain a
nonzero even-block determinant, but that would require
$e_0=e_2=0$, already proved impossible from
$(R_0-T_0)(R_0+T_0)\ne0$.  Every other singleton-only case
contradicts CR12.  On the plane omitting $X_3$, the same argument
uses the odd block, with its only potential cross-term case
$o_0=o_2=0$, also already excluded.  More generally a singleton-only
quadratic monomial has no zero on the three-active-coordinate locus,
so could not support the divisorial failure under examination.

The coordinate lines $(1,3)$ and $(2,4)$ cannot be contained by
CR12, and $(1,2)$ would require $e_0=o_0=0$, hence $R_0=T_0$.
For $(1,4)$ and $(2,3)$ the necessary vanishing contradicts
\[
 T_0u_0^2-R_0v_0^2
  =-(R_0-T_0)(R_0^2-7R_0T_0+T_0^2)/9\ne0.                     \tag{CR13}
\]
Indeed $(R_0^2+T_0^2)/(R_0T_0)$ is twice the real part of the
unit complex number $R_0/T_0$, and lies in $[-2,2]$; subtracting
seven is strictly negative.  Only line $(3,4)$ can remain, and
then exactly $e_2=o_2=0$ while $e_1o_1\ne0$.

\section{All first-derivative entries and the exceptional line}
The original PCL coefficient formula is
\[
 [z^n]\mathcal R_{ar}(z)=
 \sum_{2p+4h=5n+r+1-a}
 \frac{\beta^p\eta^h}{3^p p!h!}(-1)^L5^L(a/5)_L,
 \quad L=p+h-n,\quad 1\le a\le4,\quad0\le r\le3.              \tag{CR14}
\]
The sum is finite at each degree, with $L\ge0$ for every term.
At degree one, direct summation of these terms yields the entire
matrix, in ordinary one-based row and column indices,
\[
 \mathcal R'(0)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&\beta(\beta^2-9\eta)/27&0&
          -(11\beta^4-108\beta^2\eta+162\eta^2)/324\\
 -(\beta^2-9\eta)/9&0&\beta(7\beta^2-54\eta)/81&0\\
 0&-(\beta^2-6\eta)/6&0&\beta(4\beta^2-27\eta)/27\\
 \beta/3&0&-(2\beta^2-9\eta)/9&0
 \end{pmatrix}.                                                   \tag{CR15}
\]
For example the $(2,3)$ entry uses $(p,h)=(3,0),(1,1)$
in CR14.  Their terms are $7\beta^3/81$ and
$-2\beta\eta/3$, respectively.  The other entries follow from
the same displayed finite sum, and all zero entries have no
admissible pair because its left side is even.

Put $K=\mathcal R'(0)J^{-1}$.  Multiplication of CR15 gives
\[
 K_{23}=\beta(4\beta^2-27\eta)/81<0,\qquad
 K_{14}=-(\beta^4-12\beta^2\eta+54\eta^2)/108<0.                 \tag{CR16}
\]
The first sign follows from $0<\beta/\sqrt\eta<2$; the second
from $\beta^4-12\beta^2\eta+54\eta^2
=\eta^2((\beta^2/\eta-6)^2+18)$.
The exact identity
$\widetilde Q_z(X)=\widetilde Q_0(J\mathcal R(z)^{-1}X)$ has
derivative $-K^T\widetilde Q_0-\widetilde Q_0K$ in symmetric
matrix notation, since
$[J\mathcal R(z)^{-1}]'_{z=0}=-\mathcal R'(0)J^{-1}$.
On the possible line stratum this gives
\[
 \widetilde Q'_{34}(0)
       =-e_1\bigl(K_{14}+(o_1/e_1)K_{23}\bigr).                  \tag{CR17}
\]

For a direct exact derivation of the exceptional equations,
$e_2=0$ gives $b=p u_0^2/v_0^2$.  Here neither $u_0$ nor $v_0$
is zero, because either zero combined with $e_2=0$ would make
both zero, contrary to $R_0\ne T_0$.  Substitution into $o_2=0$
gives $T_0u_0^4=R_0v_0^4$.  The full factorization is
\[
 T_0u_0^4-R_0v_0^4
 =-\frac{R_0-T_0}{81}
  (R_0^4-23R_0^3T_0+33R_0^2T_0^2-23R_0T_0^3+T_0^4).           \tag{CR18}
\]
The quartic factor equals
$(R_0+T_0)^4-27(R_0+T_0)^2R_0T_0+81R_0^2T_0^2$.
Writing $b_0=\beta/\sqrt\eta\in(0,2)$ therefore gives
\[
 b_0^4-27b_0^2+81=0,\qquad
 b_0=3(\sqrt5-1)/2,\qquad b_0^2+3b_0-9=0.                     \tag{CR19}
\]
The other positive squared root is greater than four and cannot
occur in this interval.  Moreover, after $b=p u_0^2/v_0^2$
is substituted, direct expansion gives
\[
 o_1^2-R_0T_0e_1^2=
 \frac{p^2(R_0-T_0)^2(R_0^2-7R_0T_0+T_0^2)}{9(R_0-2T_0)^4}
 (R_0^4-23R_0^3T_0+33R_0^2T_0^2-23R_0T_0^3+T_0^4).
                                                                    \tag{CR20}
\]
Thus $(o_1/e_1)^2=\eta$ on this stratum, with either original
sign still retained.  The plus sign makes the bracket in CR17
strictly negative by CR16.  For the minus sign, substitution of
CR19 gives exactly
\[
 K_{14}-\sqrt\eta K_{23}
       =\eta^2(108-45b_0)/324>0.                                \tag{CR21}
\]
The inequality uses $b_0<2$, so its numerator exceeds eighteen.
Since $e_1\ne0$, the exceptional line has nonzero first variation
for every retained original unit phase.  The ordinary polynomial
coefficient of $X_3X_4$ is twice the symmetric entry in CR17;
this factor is required if it is used as the coefficient gate.

\section{Finite period gates and the original proper-source minimum}
Choose the actual nonzero coefficient just proved on each of the
six lines and four planes.  Each coefficient is an entire
function $g_\nu(z)$, because the original PCL matrix and its
inverse are entire.  Its first nonzero order $r_\nu$ is zero,
apart from the possible line $(3,4)$, whose order is one and
whose coefficient is the nonzero value $2\widetilde Q'_{34}(0)$.
With $c_\nu=[z^{r_\nu}]g_\nu$, put
\[
 C_\nu=1+\sum_{n>r_\nu}|[z^n]g_\nu|,\quad
 \epsilon_\nu=\min(1,|c_\nu|/(2C_\nu)),\quad
 R_{\rm prop}=\max(R_*,(\min_\nu\epsilon_\nu)^{-1}).             \tag{CR22}
\]
The sums converge absolutely by entireness.  For
$0<|z|\le\epsilon_\nu$, every tail term is bounded after factoring
$|z|^{r_\nu+1}$, so
$|g_\nu(z)|\ge |c_\nu||z|^{r_\nu}/2$.  All fixed original periods
$|u|\ge R_{\rm prop}$ therefore satisfy the zero proper-source
kernel statement, without a degree-dependent drift in period.
More generally the only possible exceptions on $|u|\ge R_*$
belong to the zero set of $\prod_\nu g_\nu(1/u)$ there.
Divide this product by its proved zero order at zero.  The quotient
is entire and nonzero at zero; its zeros in the compact disk
$|z|\le R_*^{-1}$ are finite.  Hence the exceptional superset is
finite and independent of the source degree.  A selected coefficient
zero is only a failed sufficient gate, and does not assert actual
kernel failure at that period.  CR6 remains injective there.

For the original source-$E$ minimum, the NC24 form is
\[
 Q_N^p=Z_R^*J_0^*DJ_0Z_R
 -Z_R^*J_0^*DJ_0Z_E(Z_E^*J_0^*DJ_0Z_E)^{-1}Z_E^*J_0^*DJ_0Z_R.
\]
When the proved proper-source kernel $E$ is zero, its frame has
zero columns and this subtraction is exactly absent:
$Q_N^p=Z_R^*J_0^*DJ_0Z_R$.  Completing the square in the
different original target direction space still gives
\[
 H_N^p=F_R^*D_{\rm target}F_R
 -F_R^*D_{\rm target}J_*(J_*^*D_{\rm target}J_*)^{-1}
                         J_*^*D_{\rm target}F_R.                 \tag{CR23}
\]
This proves CP20 directly from the actual two fibre direction
spaces.  It does not remove $J_*$ or evaluate its determinant
from the sixteen-cofactor injectivity result.

\section{Good coefficient specialization, with the actual graded images}
Let $\mathcal O$ be the finitely generated subring of the original
complex coefficient field specified in CP: it contains
$\mathbb Z[1/5]$, all entries of $H,H^{-1}$, $\zeta$, and the
five original quadric coefficients.  Localize only at their chosen
nonzero leading coefficients and two nonzero coefficients of $Q$
in distinct cyclic characters.  These are finitely many fixed
numbers, independent of degree.  In every geometric residue
field, $\Phi_5(\zeta)=0$ and $5$ invertible give exact order five;
$H$ remains invertible.  The two retained character coefficients
prevent any nonidentity translate from being proportional to $Q$,
since their character ratios differ by a nontrivial fifth root.
Thus the geometric fibre satisfies every algebraic hypothesis of
CR1--6, including in characteristic two.

All differences $1-\zeta^a$, $1\le a\le4$, are units: their
product is five, a unit, and in a commutative ring every factor
of a unit is a unit.  Consequently the invariant monomials are
exactly those of character zero, over $\mathcal O$ and after any
base change.  With the selected leading coefficients units,
division by the single polynomial $Q$ gives free modules of
normal monomials in each degree for $R_\mathcal O$; division by
the invariant $P$ gives the same assertion for
\[
 B_\mathcal O=S_\mathcal O^G/(P S_\mathcal O^G).
\]
For the latter, the leading monomial of $P$ has character zero,
so every quotient monomial and every subtraction in the division
of an invariant stays invariant.  Linear independence of the
normal remainders follows because a nonzero multiple of the
monic leading term has its largest monomial divisible by that
leading term.

The restriction $B_{\mathcal O,d}\to R_{\mathcal O,d}$ is
injective over the fraction field by the prime-factor argument
CR2, hence over $\mathcal O$ since its free source has no
$\mathcal O$-torsion.  Its reduction is injective in every geometric
residue field by that same argument.  A full-column-rank minor
in each such fibre is invertible near its point.  Invert that
literal square block, eliminate the remaining entries, and obtain
a local left inverse and a free complementary cokernel.  Thus
$(R/B)_d$ is finite locally free, and the exact sequence remains
exact after arbitrary base change.

Multiplication by the original $A$ sends $R_{d-8}$ into $B_d$
by the orbit-sum identity.  Its every geometric fibre is injective
since $R$ there is a domain and $A$ has nonzero restriction to
$Q=0$.  The same minor proof therefore applies to its cokernels
$J_d=B_d/A R_{d-8}$ and $(R/AR)_d$.  These are the actual graded
images in the fibre, rather than replacements obtained after
an unexamined tensor kernel.

Finally CR6 is a map of finite locally free modules whose every
geometric fibre is injective.  Its maximal minors again prove
a locally split injection and locally free cokernel.  Since
finite locally free modules over the affine coefficient ring are
projective, this exact sequence also has a module splitting.
One can construct it by patching: on a finite principal cover,
clear denominators of local left inverses by sufficiently large
powers of the cover elements; these powers still generate the
unit ideal, and a linear combination equal to one patches the
multiplied identities.  In particular for every $\mathcal O$-algebra
$T$, not only every residue field,
\[
 \ker(X_d\otimes_\mathcal O T)=0.                              \tag{CR24}
\]
This strengthening follows from the actual splitting.  It does
not supply one chosen degree-uniform inverse or a Hermitian
isometry.

\section{Exact specialized receivers and the separate interpolation kernel}
In a residue field of characteristic $p\ne5$, let $X$ denote
the matrix of the original cofactor map in any local bases.
Entrywise Frobenius obeys the exact identity
\[
 F(Xv)=X^{(p)}F(v),                                             \tag{CR25}
\]
by its additive and multiplicative polynomial identities.  The
matrix on the right is that of the Frobenius-pulled-back map;
the relation retains the original source and target scalar twists.
It does not identify this semilinear operation with the arithmetic
sum action or its exponential.

For the separately defined sum polynomial module the original
evaluation matrix is
$V_k[(a,b),r]=\lambda_{ab}^{r}$, $0\le r<(k+1)^2$.
