\begin{abstract}
The effective heat approximation of D.~H.~J.~Polymath is transferred to
the original source $g_t(s)=\pmreceiverMell(U_tf_0)(s)=16H_t((2s-1)/i)$ without
dividing that source by a gamma factor or a polynomial divisor.
An exact finite cutoff correction gives a holomorphic comparison
function across Riemann--Siegel cutoff changes. Explicit finite
inequalities bound every coordinate jet, the complete exterior
logarithmic derivative of a packet, its traces, and its trace derivative.
The inequalities are actually checked for a nonempty moving packet:
for $1023/4096\le t\le1025/4096$ there is exactly one zero in
$1/10<\pmreceiverRea s<9/10$, $507/4<\pmreceiverIma s<513/4$.
Its simplicity and location on $\pmreceiverRea s=1/2$ follow from the certified
count and an exact symmetry, not from an assumed real-zero theorem.
No assertion about all zeros or the endpoint $t=0$ is made.
\end{abstract}

\section{The original objects and the source that supplies the error}

The Mellin convention is
\[
 \pmreceiverMell f(s)=\int_0^\infty f(x)x^s\frac{\pmreceiverdd x}{x}.
\]
The original source and its multiplier are retained literally:
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH1-objects}
\begin{split}
 f_0(x)&=2\sum_{n\ge1}
 (4\pi^2n^4x^4-6\pi n^2x^2)e^{-\pi n^2x^2},\qquad x>0,\\
 (U_tf)(x)&=e^{t(\log x)^2/4}f(x),\\
 g_t(s)&=\pmreceiverMell(U_tf_0)(s)=16H_t(z(s)),\qquad
 z(s)=\frac{2s-1}{i}=-2i(s-\tfrac12).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Here Polymath's original conventions, \pmreceiverSrc{phidef} and \pmreceiverSrc{htdef}, are
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH1-Phi}
 \Phi(u)=\sum_{n\ge1}(2\pi^2n^4e^{9u}-3\pi n^2e^{5u})
 e^{-\pi n^2e^{4u}},\qquad
 H_t(z)=\int_0^\infty e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)\cos(zu)\pmreceiverdd u.
\end{equation}
The theta identity $\Phi(-u)=\Phi(u)$, stated with these conventions
immediately after \pmreceiverSrc{phidef}, and its super-exponential decay justify
the following exact coordinate calculation:
\[
 f_0(e^{2u})=4e^{-u}\Phi(u),\quad
 \frac{\pmreceiverdd x}{x}=2\pmreceiverdd u,\quad
 e^{t(\log x)^2/4}=e^{tu^2},
\]
and therefore
\[
 \pmreceiverMell(U_tf_0)(s)
 =8\int_\pmreceiverR e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)e^{(2s-1)u}\pmreceiverdd u
 =16\int_0^\infty e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)\cos(z(s)u)\pmreceiverdd u.
\]
This calculation only identifies the receiving object. The full entire
theta transport and the full-divisor Volterra construction belong to
the earlier programme result SZ-20260920-046 and are not replaced here.
Differentiation under this rapidly convergent integral gives
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH1-heat}
 \partial_tg_t=\tfrac14\partial_s^2g_t,
 \qquad \partial_t^k\partial_s^jg_t
 =4^{-k}\partial_s^{j+2k}g_t.
\end{equation}
The sign is also checked from Polymath's $\partial_tH_t=-H_t''$
(\pmreceiverSrc{back}): $(-2i)^2=-4$.

Write $s=\sigma+i\tau$. The complete coordinate dictionary is
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH1-coordinate}
 x=2\tau,\quad y=1-2\sigma,\quad
 s=\frac{1+iz}{2},\quad 1-s=\frac{1-iz}{2},\quad
 \pmreceiverdd z=-2i\pmreceiverdd s,\quad |\pmreceiverdd z|=2|\pmreceiverdd s|.
\end{equation}
The Euclidean metric is being used in both planes. The affine map
rotates by $-\pi/2$, scales length by $2$ and area by $4$, and preserves
positive orientation. No root multiplicity changes under this map.

\subsection{Literal gamma/Stirling factors and all source constants}

On $\pmreceiverC\setminus(-\infty,1]$, use the principal logarithm, with argument
in $(-\pi,\pi)$. The values needed below have nonzero imaginary part,
so no endpoint convention for the cut is used. Polymath's exact
definitions \pmreceiverSrc{M-def}, \pmreceiverSrc{logM}, \pmreceiverSrc{alpha-form}, and \pmreceiverSrc{Mt-def}
are
\begin{align}
 M_0(q)&=\frac18\frac{q(q-1)}2\pi^{-q/2}\sqrt{2\pi}
 \exp\left((\tfrac q2-\tfrac12)\pmreceiverLog\tfrac q2-\tfrac q2\right),
 \label{pmreceiver:PMH1-M0}\\
 \log M_0(q)&=\pmreceiverLog q+\pmreceiverLog(q-1)-\tfrac q2\log\pi
 +\log\tfrac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{16}
 +(\tfrac q2-\tfrac12)\pmreceiverLog\tfrac q2-\tfrac q2,
 \label{pmreceiver:PMH1-logM}\\
 \alpha(q)&=\frac1{2q}+\frac1{q-1}+\frac12\pmreceiverLog\frac q{2\pi},
 \qquad M_t(q)=e^{t\alpha(q)^2/4}M_0(q),
 \label{pmreceiver:PMH1-alpha}\\
 b_n^t&=e^{t\log^2n/4},\qquad
 T_{t,n}(q)=M_t(q)\frac{b_n^t}{n^{q+(t/2)\alpha(q)}}.
 \label{pmreceiver:PMH1-term}
\end{align}
Powers of a positive integer mean $n^v=e^{v\log n}$ with the real
logarithm of $n$. In particular every complex phase in
\eqref{pmreceiver:PMH1-term} is retained. The source factor called $B_t$ in
\pmreceiverSrc{bo-def} is, in the original Mellin coordinate,
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH1-B}
 B_t(z(s))=M_t(1-s).
\end{equation}
It is a literal nonzero factor; it is not a new definition of $g_t$.

For $0\le t\le1/2$, $0\le\sigma\le1$, $\tau\ge100$, set
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH1-cutoff}
 T'=\tau+\frac{\pi t}{8},\qquad
 a=\sqrt{\frac{T'}{2\pi}},\qquad
 N=N(t,\tau)=\left\lfloor\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{2\pi}+\frac t{16}}\right\rfloor.
\end{equation}
For $q=s$ or $q=1-s$, so $|\pmreceiverIma q|=\tau$, define the following
positive quantities:
\begin{align}
 W_{t,n}(q)&=|M_t(q)|
 e^{t\log^2n/4-\pmreceiverRea(q+(t/2)\alpha(q))\log n},
 \label{pmreceiver:PMH1-W}\\
 \epsilon_{t,n}(q)&=
 \exp\left(\frac{(t^2/8)|\alpha(q)-\log n|^2+t/4+1/6}
 {\tau-3.33}\right)-1,
 \label{pmreceiver:PMH1-eps}\\
 \widetilde\epsilon_t(q)&=
 \left(\frac{0.397\,9^{\pmreceiverRea q}}{a-0.865}
       +\frac5{3(\tau-6)}\right)e^{3.49/(\tau-4)},
 \label{pmreceiver:PMH1-tilde}\\
 E_{A,N}&=\sum_{n=1}^N W_{t,n}(s)\epsilon_{t,n}(s),
 \qquad E_{B,N}=\sum_{n=1}^N W_{t,n}(1-s)\epsilon_{t,n}(1-s),
 \label{pmreceiver:PMH1-EAB}\\
 E_{C,0}&=e^{t\pi^2/64}|M_0(iT')|
       \left(1+\widetilde\epsilon_t(s)+\widetilde\epsilon_t(1-s)\right),
 \qquad E_N=E_{A,N}+E_{B,N}+E_{C,0}.
 \label{pmreceiver:PMH1-EC}
\end{align}
All decimal constants here are the literal terminating decimals in
\pmreceiverSrc{eps-def}, \pmreceiverSrc{epsp-def}, and Corollary \texttt{ab-cor}; hence they
are exact rational constants, not rounded measurements made here.
For negative $\pmreceiverIma q$, \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH1-eps}--\eqref{pmreceiver:PMH1-tilde} mean the
reflected positive-imaginary estimates. This reflection is justified
in the next proof, rather than silently inserting a negative $T$ in a
positive-$T$ source estimate.

\begin{pmreceivertheorem}[PMH1: the imported approximation in the original coordinate]
\label{pmreceiver:PMH1}
With the literal quantities \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH1-M0}--\eqref{pmreceiver:PMH1-EC},
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH1-bound}
 \left|g_t(s)-16\sum_{n=1}^{N(t,\tau)}
       \bigl(T_{t,n}(s)+T_{t,n}(1-s)\bigr)\right|\le16E_N
\end{equation}
for $0\le t\le1/2$, $0\le\pmreceiverRea s\le1$, $\pmreceiverIma s\ge100$.
\end{pmreceivertheorem}
\begin{proof}
For $0<t\le1/2$ and $0\le\sigma\le1/2$,
\eqref{pmreceiver:PMH1-coordinate} gives Polymath's source region
$0<t\le1/2$, $0\le y\le1$, $x\ge200$ (\pmreceiverSrc{region}).
Corollary \texttt{ab-cor}, with the definitions in
Corollary \texttt{abc-cor}, says
$|H_t-A_{t,N}-B_{t,N}|\le E_A+E_B+E_{C,0}$.
Its $s_-$ is exactly $s$, its $s_+$ is exactly $1-s$, and its
$x/2$ is exactly $\tau$. Substitution gives
\eqref{pmreceiver:PMH1-EAB}--\eqref{pmreceiver:PMH1-bound}, including the factor $16$.
The functions $M_0$, $M_t$, and $\alpha$ respect conjugation away
from the cut. Moreover $H_t(\bar z)=\overline{H_t(z)}$ for real $t$,
by \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH1-Phi}. Thus $1/2\le\sigma\le1$ follows by reflecting
$z$ in the real axis, which replaces $s$ by $1-\bar s$ and exchanges
the two summands and their error bounds. This also proves the claimed
reflected interpretation of $\epsilon$ and $\widetilde\epsilon$.
At any fixed $\tau$, the integer $N(t,\tau)$ is constant for all
sufficiently small $t\ge0$, including when $\tau/(2\pi)$ is a square:
at that endpoint the floor is already the larger integer.
The rapidly convergent integral for $H_t$ and every displayed finite
quantity are continuous as $t\downarrow0$. Taking this limit proves
the stated $t=0$ bound. No statement at negative $t$ is obtained.
The intermediate source-proof slips in the $R_{t,N}$ estimate are
repaired independently in PMH11 below, which reconfirms exactly
the final constants used in \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH1-tilde}.
\end{proof}

For completeness, the source's refined correction and its phases are
not lost in \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH1-EC}. In the original upper-$z$ half-strip,
\pmreceiverSrc{abc-cor}, \pmreceiverSrc{C0-def}, and \pmreceiverSrc{U-def} give
\begin{align}
 p&=1-2(a-N),\qquad
 U=\exp\left[-i\left(\frac{T'}2\log\frac{T'}{2\pi}
                         -\frac{T'}2-\frac\pi8\right)\right],
 \label{pmreceiver:PMH1-phase}\\
 C_0(p)&=\frac{e^{\pi i(p^2/2+3/8)}-i\sqrt2\cos(\pi p/2)}
                 {2\cos(\pi p)},
 \label{pmreceiver:PMH1-C0}\\
 C_t(x+iy)&=2e^{-\pi iy/8}(-1)^N e^{t\pi^2/64}
       \pmreceiverRea\left(M_0(iT')C_0(p)Ue^{\pi i/8}\right).
 \label{pmreceiver:PMH1-C}
\end{align}
The apparent singularities at $p=\pm1/2$ in \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH1-C0} are
removed by continuity, as in the source. The refined approximation
is $H_t=A_{t,N}+B_{t,N}-C_t$ with error
$E_A+E_B+e^{t\pi^2/64}|M_0(iT')|
(\widetilde\epsilon_t(s)+\widetilde\epsilon_t(1-s))$.
The source bound $|C_0(p)|\le1/2$ (\pmreceiverSrc{cop}) gives
$|C_t|\le e^{t\pi^2/64}|M_0(iT')|$, which is precisely the
additional term retained in $E_{C,0}$.
The displayed $C_t$ depends on real coordinates, a floor, and a real
part. No holomorphic derivative of that displayed expression is used.
The receiver below uses the proved $A+B$ bound, not a deletion of its
$C$ contribution.

\section{A fixed cutoff and a finite box receiver}

Let
\[
 \mathcal D=\{s\in\pmreceiverC:0<\pmreceiverRea s<1,\ \pmreceiverIma s>100\}.
\]
For a fixed nonnegative integer $K$, define
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH2-Q}
 Q_{t,K}(s)=16\sum_{n=1}^K
       \bigl(T_{t,n}(s)+T_{t,n}(1-s)\bigr).
\end{equation}
This function is holomorphic on the open upper half-plane. Indeed
$s$ and $1-s$ then avoid the logarithm cuts and the points $0,1$.
The source error estimate is asserted only on the smaller source
strip; holomorphy itself has this stated open domain.
Write $F_{t,K}(z)=Q_{t,K}((1+iz)/2)/16$.

\begin{pmreceivertheorem}[PMH2: exact repair of a moving cutoff]
\label{pmreceiver:PMH2}
At each source point define
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH2-Delta}
 \Delta_{N,K}(s,t)=\sum_{n=1}^N
 (T_{t,n}(s)+T_{t,n}(1-s))-
 \sum_{n=1}^K(T_{t,n}(s)+T_{t,n}(1-s)).
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH2-bound}
 |g_t(s)-Q_{t,K}(s)|\le
 16\bigl(E_{N(t,\tau)}(s,t)+|\Delta_{N(t,\tau),K}(s,t)|\bigr).
\end{equation}
This estimate holds across every cutoff jump. The function being
differentiated is $g_t-Q_{t,K}$, never the floor-dependent sum.
\end{pmreceivertheorem}
\begin{proof}
Subtract the exact identity
$Q_{t,N}-Q_{t,K}=16\Delta_{N,K}$ from $g_t-Q_{t,K}$ and apply
\eqref{pmreceiver:PMH1-bound} and the triangle inequality. Every term between
the two cutoffs is present with its exact sign in
\eqref{pmreceiver:PMH2-Delta}. If only an absolute majorant is needed,
\[
 |\Delta_{N,K}|\le\sum_{n=\min(N,K)+1}^{\max(N,K)}
           (W_{t,n}(s)+W_{t,n}(1-s)).
\]
This is an estimate of that exact finite difference, not an alteration
of either source function.
\end{proof}

Here is an explicitly finite prescription for a uniform right-hand
side. Fix a closed real interval $I\subset[0,1/2]$ and a compact
rectangle $\mathcal B\subset\{0\le\pmreceiverRea s\le1,\pmreceiverIma s\ge100\}$.
Enclose $\tau/(2\pi)+t/16$ over $I\times\mathcal B$ in a positive
rational interval $[A_-,A_+]$. Choose a finite integer list
$\mathcal J$ containing every $\lfloor\sqrt A\rfloor$ for
$A\in[A_-,A_+]$. This list is computed using integer squares;
including both candidates at a boundary is valid and avoids an
undecidable equality test on a general computable real.

Using outward-rounded real or complex interval arithmetic, form
upper bounds $w_{n,q}$, $e_{n,q}$, $c$, and $d_{N,K}$ for,
respectively, $W_{t,n}(q)$, $\epsilon_{t,n}(q)$,
$E_{C,0}$, and $|\Delta_{N,K}|$, over the specified box and time
interval. All $n$ range only up to $\max(K,\max\mathcal J)$.
Then the following is a finite, explicit error receiver:
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH2-box}
 \eta(\mathcal B,I,K)=16\max_{N\in\mathcal J}
 \left\{\sum_{n=1}^N
 (w_{n,s}e_{n,s}+w_{n,1-s}e_{n,1-s})+c+d_{N,K}\right\}.
\end{equation}
It bounds $|g_t-Q_{t,K}|$ everywhere on $I\times\mathcal B$ by
PMH2. This prescription uses only finitely many evaluations of
rational operations, $\pi$, $\log$, $\exp$, square root, and the
principal complex logarithm on explicitly cut-free rectangles.
There is no hidden supremum to be numerically guessed.

In particular, the exact magnitude formula retaining the Stirling
decay, for $q=u+iv$ in this domain, is
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH2-magnitude}
 |M_t(q)|=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{16}|q||q-1|\pi^{-u/2}
 \exp\left((\tfrac u2-\tfrac12)\log\frac{|q|}{2}
 -\frac v2\arg q-\frac u2+\frac t4\pmreceiverRea(\alpha(q)^2)\right).
\end{equation}
Its proof is taking absolute values in \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH1-M0} and
\eqref{pmreceiver:PMH1-alpha}, using
$\pmreceiverRea((q/2-1/2)\pmreceiverLog(q/2))=(u/2-1/2)\log(|q|/2)-(v/2)\arg q$.
One may use \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH2-magnitude} or directly use complex balls in
\eqref{pmreceiver:PMH1-M0}; both retain the same factor and phase information.

\section{Every jet, with its coordinate factors and source factor}

\begin{pmreceivertheorem}[PMH3: Cauchy error jets]
\label{pmreceiver:PMH3}
Let $\overline{D(s_0,R)}\subset\mathcal D$ and let
$|g_t-Q_{t,K}|\le\eta$ on this disk, uniformly for $t\in I$.
For $|s-s_0|\le h<R$ and every integer $j\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH3-sjet}
 |g_t^{(j)}(s)-Q_{t,K}^{(j)}(s)|
 \le\eta_j:=\frac{j!\eta}{(R-h)^j}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, if $|H_t-F_{t,K}|\le\mathcal E$ on the closed
$z$-disk of radius $r$, then at its center
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH3-zjet}
 \left|g_t^{(j)}(s)-16(-2i)^jF_{t,K}^{(j)}(z(s))\right|
 \le\frac{16\,2^j j!\mathcal E}{r^j}.
\end{equation}
The corresponding approximation to the exact mixed heat jet is
$4^{-k}Q_{t,K}^{(j+2k)}$ with error
$4^{-k}(j+2k)!\eta/(R-h)^{j+2k}$.
\end{pmreceivertheorem}
\begin{proof}
The error is holomorphic on an open neighborhood of every closed
disk in question. Apply Cauchy's derivative formula to the error on
the circle centered at $s$ of radius $R-h$. At a boundary case use
slightly smaller radii and pass to the limit. The integral bound is
$j!\eta/(R-h)^j$. The chain rule and
$z'(s)=-2i$ give the exact factor $16(-2i)^j$;
the metric conversion in \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH1-coordinate} gives $r=2R$.
The mixed jet assertion follows from \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH1-heat}.
In particular it does not assert that $Q_{t,K}$ itself solves the
heat equation.
\end{proof}

The comparison jets themselves are finite expressions. Set
$\ell_n=\log n$ and
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH3-lambda}
 \lambda_{t,n}(q)=\alpha(q)-\ell_n
       +\frac t2(\alpha(q)-\ell_n)\alpha'(q).
\end{equation}
For every $r\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH3-alpha-jets}
 \alpha^{(r)}(q)=\frac{(-1)^r r!}{2q^{r+1}}
 +\frac{(-1)^r r!}{(q-1)^{r+1}}
 +\frac{(-1)^{r-1}(r-1)!}{2q^r}.
\end{equation}
Let $\mathcal B_{0,n}=1$ and, recursively,
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH3-Bell}
 \mathcal B_{j+1,n}
 =\sum_{a=0}^j\binom ja\lambda_{t,n}^{(a)}\mathcal B_{j-a,n}.
\end{equation}
Every $\lambda_{t,n}^{(a)}$ is explicitly computed by Leibniz's rule
from \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH3-lambda} and \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH3-alpha-jets}. The exact jets are
\begin{align}
 T_{t,n}^{(j)}(q)&=T_{t,n}(q)\mathcal B_{j,n}(q),
 \label{pmreceiver:PMH3-term-jets}\\
 Q_{t,K}^{(j)}(s)&=16\sum_{n=1}^K
 \left(T_{t,n}(s)\mathcal B_{j,n}(s)
 +(-1)^jT_{t,n}(1-s)\mathcal B_{j,n}(1-s)\right),
 \label{pmreceiver:PMH3-Qjets}\\
 \partial_tQ_{t,K}(s)&=16\sum_{n=1}^K
 \left(T_{t,n}(s)\frac{(\alpha(s)-\ell_n)^2}{4}
 +T_{t,n}(1-s)\frac{(\alpha(1-s)-\ell_n)^2}{4}\right).
 \label{pmreceiver:PMH3-timeQ}
\end{align}
Indeed logarithmic differentiation of the literal term
\eqref{pmreceiver:PMH1-term} gives \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH3-lambda}; differentiating
$T'=\lambda T$ $j$ times proves \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH3-Bell} by induction.
Differentiating its full exponent with respect to $t$ gives
$\alpha^2/4-\alpha\ell_n/2+\ell_n^2/4$, which is exactly the
factor in \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH3-timeQ}. No gamma or Stirling derivative was
omitted in this calculation.

One can explicitly see the source factor in the alternative
identity $Q_{t,K}=16M_t(1-s)f_{t,K}(z(s))$, where
$f_{t,K}=F_{t,K}/B_t$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH3-Bjet}
 Q_{t,K}^{(j)}(s)=16\sum_{a=0}^j\binom ja(-1)^a
 M_t^{(a)}(1-s)(-2i)^{j-a}f_{t,K}^{(j-a)}(z(s)).
\end{equation}
In particular, wherever the two sides are defined,
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH3-logB}
 \frac{Q_{t,K}'}{Q_{t,K}}(s)
 =-\left[\alpha(1-s)+\frac t2\alpha(1-s)\alpha'(1-s)\right]
       -2i\frac{f_{t,K}'}{f_{t,K}}(z(s)).
\end{equation}
The first term is not dispensable. The use of a quotient in this
identity does not replace $g_t$ by a normalized source.

\section{A finite stopping inequality for a whole contour and time interval}

Let $\Gamma$ be a positively oriented polygonal Jordan contour
whose closed interior lies in $\mathcal D$. Its successive segments
have midpoints $c_i$, lengths $L_i$, and half-lengths $h_i=L_i/2$.
Choose $R_i>h_i$ so that
$\overline{D(c_i,R_i)}\subset\mathcal D$, and use the same fixed
cutoff $K$ on every segment. Let $I=[t_0-d_t,t_0+d_t]\subset[0,1/2]$.
The box construction \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH2-box} gives a verified bound $\eta_i$
on each outer disk, uniform in $I$. Finite comparison jets give
verified upper bounds $U_{j,i}$ for $|Q_{t,K}^{(j)}|$ and $V_i$ for
$|\partial_tQ_{t,K}|$ there. A computed complex ball for
$Q_{t_0,K}(c_i)$ has center $A_i$ and radius $u_i$; a direct complex
ball absolute lower bound can be used in place of $|A_i|-u_i$.

\begin{pmreceivertheorem}[PMH4: the stopping inequality]
\label{pmreceiver:PMH4}
The finitely many explicitly evaluated inequalities
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH4-stop}
 a_i:=|A_i|-u_i-h_iU_{1,i}-d_tV_i,\qquad
 \mu_i:=a_i-\eta_i>0\quad\text{for every }i
\end{equation}
certify simultaneously for all $t\in I$ that
\[
 |Q_{t,K}|\ge a_i>\eta_i,\qquad |g_t|\ge\mu_i
 \quad\hbox{on the entire $i$th segment.}
\]
Consequently $g_t$ and $Q_{t,K}$ have the same number of zeros
inside $\Gamma$, counted with full algebraic multiplicity, and that
number is constant on $I$. This is a finite certificate rule, not an
assertion that untested input satisfies \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH4-stop}.
\end{pmreceivertheorem}
\begin{proof}
Join $(t_0,c_i)$ to $(t,c_i)$ and then to $(t,s)$ along the real time
interval and the straight subsegment in the outer disk. The
fundamental theorem of calculus and the stated derivative bounds
give
$|Q_{t,K}(s)-Q_{t_0,K}(c_i)|\le d_tV_i+h_iU_{1,i}$.
This proves the first bound and, by the source error, the second.
The strict inequality $|g_t-Q_{t,K}|<|Q_{t,K}|$ on all of $\Gamma$
gives equality of root counts by Rouch\'e's theorem. As $t$ varies in
$I$, the continuous nonzero boundary values of $g_t$ form a homotopy
in $\pmreceiverC\setminus\{0\}$; their integer winding number is constant.
Every claim uses an inequality actually tested on finite arithmetic
data. There is no initial assumption of a zero-free boundary.
\end{proof}

The number itself can be determined by a second finite calculation.
At $t_0$, the image of each segment under $Q_{t_0,K}$ and the chord
between its endpoint images lie in the disk centered at
$Q_{t_0,K}(c_i)$ of radius $h_iU_{1,i}$. Inequality
\eqref{pmreceiver:PMH4-stop} keeps that disk away from zero. The segment image

\subsection{What terminates and what does not}

There is a finite computation for every prescribed finite cover,
cutoff and arithmetic precision. A strict positive result in
\eqref{pmreceiver:PMH4-stop} is a stopping certificate. For fixed comparison
function and fixed outer-disk error bounds, if the limiting exact
arithmetic inequalities have positive margins, convergence of
outward interval evaluations and sufficiently fine finite
subdivision eventually exhibit positive margins: each of the
finitely many strict inequalities has a positive distance from
zero, and all evaluation widths can be made smaller than half that
distance. This statement is only the termination property of the
arithmetic verification of a strict inequality.
The analytical source error $E_N$ does not tend to zero when one
merely raises machine precision. A failed inequality may remain
failed forever with this approximation. No general termination on
every genuinely zero-free contour, and no eventual success at
$t=0$ or at all heights, is claimed.

After PMH4 has passed, write $\eta_{j,i}=j!\eta_i/(R_i-h_i)^j$.

 \delta_{j,i}=\frac{\eta_{j,i}}{\mu_i}

\begin{pmreceivertheorem}[PMH5: quotient errors, without a zero-reality premise]
\label{pmreceiver:PMH5}
On the $i$th contour segment, uniformly for $t\in I$,
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH5-ratio}
 \left|\frac{g_t^{(j)}}{g_t}-\frac{Q_{t,K}^{(j)}}{Q_{t,K}}\right|
 \le\delta_{j,i},\qquad j=1,2.
\end{equation}
\end{pmreceivertheorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $e=g_t-Q_{t,K}$. Exact subtraction gives
\[
 \frac{g_t^{(j)}}{g_t}-\frac{Q_{t,K}^{(j)}}{Q_{t,K}}
 =\frac{e^{(j)}}{g_t}
       -\frac{Q_{t,K}^{(j)}e}{Q_{t,K}g_t}.
\]
Use PMH3, $|g_t|\ge\mu_i$ and $|Q_{t,K}|\ge a_i$.
\end{proof}

At a fixed certified time, let $\rho_a$ be the distinct enclosed
zeros of $g_t$, and let $m_a$ be their actual positive integer
multiplicities. Define exactly
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH6-divisor}
 P_t(s)=\prod_a(s-\rho_a)^{m_a},\qquad
 m=\sum_am_a,\qquad R_t(s)=\frac{g_t(s)}{P_t(s)},\qquad
 b_t(s)=\frac{R_t'(s)}{R_t(s)}.
\end{equation}
No zero outside $\Gamma$ is included in $P_t$, and no enclosed zero
is omitted or assigned a smaller multiplicity. The divisor is
monic; the original scalar mass, all gamma/Stirling contributions,
and the entire exterior factor remain in $R_t$.

\begin{pmreceivertheorem}[PMH6: a finite-contour receiver for the complete exterior term]
\label{pmreceiver:PMH6}
The function $R_t$ is holomorphic and nonzero throughout the closed
interior of $\Gamma$, after removing its apparent singularities at
the enclosed zeros. For every interior $w$, including an enclosed
zero, and every integer $r\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH6-exact}
 b_t^{(r)}(w)=\frac{r!}{2\pi i}\oint_\Gamma
       \frac{g_t'(\zeta)/g_t(\zeta)}{(\zeta-w)^{r+1}}\pmreceiverdd\zeta.
\end{equation}
If $d_i$ is any positive lower bound for the Euclidean distance
from $w$ to the $i$th segment, the comparison integral
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH6-approx}
 \widehat b_{t,K}^{(r)}(w)=\frac{r!}{2\pi i}\oint_\Gamma
       \frac{Q_{t,K}'(\zeta)/Q_{t,K}(\zeta)}{(\zeta-w)^{r+1}}\pmreceiverdd\zeta
\end{equation}
satisfies the fully explicit bound
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH6-error}
 |b_t^{(r)}(w)-\widehat b_{t,K}^{(r)}(w)|
 \le\frac{r!}{2\pi}\sum_i\frac{L_i\delta_{1,i}}{d_i^{r+1}}.
\end{equation}
\end{pmreceivertheorem}
\begin{proof}
At each zero $\rho_a$, Taylor's theorem writes
$g_t(s)=(s-\rho_a)^{m_a}v_a(s)$ with $v_a(\rho_a)\ne0$.
Removal at the exact multiplicities in \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH6-divisor} proves
the assertion about $R_t$. Logarithmic differentiation gives
\[
 \frac{g_t'}{g_t}=\sum_a\frac{m_a}{s-\rho_a}+b_t.
\]
For $w$ different from the $\rho_a$, the integral of each rational
term $m_a/((\zeta-\rho_a)(\zeta-w))$ is zero: its residues at
$\rho_a$ and $w$ are opposite. The remaining integral is the
Cauchy formula for $b_t(w)$. Both sides are holomorphic in $w$
throughout the interior, so this equality extends over all the
$\rho_a$. Differentiate under the contour integral $r$ times;
$\partial_w^r(\zeta-w)^{-1}=r!(\zeta-w)^{-r-1}$ has a positive
sign. This proves \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH6-exact}. PMH5 and the length estimate
for a contour integral prove \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH6-error}.
\end{proof}

For a function $\psi$ holomorphic on a neighborhood of the closed
interior, the corresponding trace and its source error are
\begin{align}
 \mathscr T_\psi(t)&=\sum_am_a\psi(\rho_a)
    =\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_\Gamma\psi(s)\frac{g_t'}{g_t}(s)\pmreceiverdd s,
 \label{pmreceiver:PMH7-trace}\\
 \left|\mathscr T_\psi(t)-\frac1{2\pi i}
 \oint_\Gamma\psi(s)\frac{Q_{t,K}'}{Q_{t,K}}(s)\pmreceiverdd s\right|
 &\le\frac1{2\pi}\sum_iL_i\sup_{\Gamma_i}|\psi|\,\delta_{1,i}.
 \label{pmreceiver:PMH7-traceerror}
\end{align}
The first equality is the residue theorem, with residue $m_a$ at a
zero of order $m_a$; the second follows from PMH5. The previously
proved moving-packet identity in SZ-20260920-046 reads, in exactly
these coordinates and orientations,
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH7-dynamics}
 \mathscr T_\psi'(t)=-\frac1{8\pi i}\oint_\Gamma
       \psi'(s)\frac{g_t''(s)}{g_t(s)}\pmreceiverdd s.
\end{equation}
Its use here needs no new root-by-root motion hypothesis. For the
reader to check the exact sign and factor, \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH1-heat} gives
$\partial_t(g_t'/g_t)=\tfrac14\partial_s(g_t''/g_t)$ on the
certified boundary; integration of the derivative of
$\psi g_t''/g_t$ along the closed contour yields
\eqref{pmreceiver:PMH7-dynamics}. Thus this identity also holds through
collisions of interior roots.

\begin{pmreceivertheorem}[PMH7: effective trace-dynamics receiver]
\label{pmreceiver:PMH7}
The finite source error on the right side of
\eqref{pmreceiver:PMH7-dynamics}, when $g_t''/g_t$ is replaced by
$Q_{t,K}''/Q_{t,K}$, is at most
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH7-timeerror}
 \frac1{8\pi}\sum_iL_i\sup_{\Gamma_i}|\psi'|\,\delta_{2,i}.
\end{equation}
The result is an approximation to the derivative of the original
trace. It is not asserted to be the time derivative of the trace
of the comparison function $Q_{t,K}$.
\end{pmreceivertheorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply PMH5 with $j=2$ directly to the already exact contour
identity \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH7-dynamics} and bound each segment integral by
its length times its integrand bound. In general
$\partial_tQ_{t,K}\ne Q_{t,K}''/4$, so identifying the two
different derivatives would introduce an unbounded heat defect;
no such identification was made.
\end{proof}

The full polynomial packet can also be enclosed without locating

 e_k=\frac1k\sum_{j=1}^k(-1)^{j-1}e_{k-j}p_j,
 \quad 1\le k\le m,\qquad
 P_t(s)=\sum_{k=0}^m(-1)^ke_ks^{m-k}.
\end{equation}
To prove the recurrence, form

Comparing the coefficient of $v^{k-1}$ in this identity gives

\section{Full repeated-root jets, without discarding their mass}

At any enclosed zero $\rho$ of actual multiplicity $m_\rho$, write
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH8-local}
 g_t(\rho+h)=h^{m_\rho}\sum_{r\ge0}J_rh^r,
 \qquad J_r=\frac{g_t^{(m_\rho+r)}(\rho)}{(m_\rho+r)!},
 \qquad J_0\ne0.
\end{equation}
Every one of the first $m_\rho$ vanishing derivatives and every
nonzero higher jet is part of this local object. In particular,
PMH3 gives the exact comparison centers
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH8-jets}
 \widehat J_r=
 \frac{16(-2i)^{m_\rho+r}}{(m_\rho+r)!}
 F_{t,K}^{(m_\rho+r)}(z(\rho)),\qquad
 |J_r-\widehat J_r|\le\frac{\eta}{d^{m_\rho+r}}
\end{equation}
whenever a Cauchy error disk of available $s$-radius $d$ contains
the evaluation point with error $\eta$. The factorial and every
power of $-2i$ have not been removed from the center.

\begin{pmreceivertheorem}[PMH8: complete local logarithmic-derivative jets]
\label{pmreceiver:PMH8}
For $v(h)=\sum_{r\ge0}J_rh^r$, write
$v'(h)/v(h)=\sum_{r\ge0}b_rh^r$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH8-recursion}
 b_r=\frac{(r+1)J_{r+1}-\sum_{k=1}^rJ_kb_{r-k}}{J_0},
 \qquad r\ge0.
\end{equation}
The full exterior derivative jets for \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH6-divisor} are
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH8-exterior}
 b_t^{(r)}(\rho)=r!b_r
       -\sum_{\rho_a\ne\rho}
          \frac{m_a(-1)^r r!}{(\rho-\rho_a)^{r+1}}.
\end{equation}
These identities apply to every actual multiplicity. If a numerical
local jet division is desired, it is performed only after a finite
ball check excludes zero from $J_0$; it is not an assumed check.
PMH6 remains available at all interior points without such a
local division or prior knowledge of an individual root location.
\end{pmreceivertheorem}
\begin{proof}
Multiply the two convergent series in $v\sum_rb_rh^r=v'$ and
compare coefficients of $h^r$. This gives
$\sum_{k=0}^rJ_kb_{r-k}=(r+1)J_{r+1}$ and proves the recursion
because $J_0\ne0$ by the definition of exact multiplicity.
The local function $v=g_t/(s-\rho)^{m_\rho}$ equals
$R_t(s)\prod_{\rho_a\ne\rho}(s-\rho_a)^{m_a}$.
Logarithmic differentiation, followed by $r$ derivatives, gives
\eqref{pmreceiver:PMH8-exterior}. This proof preserves the original nonzero
mass $J_0$ throughout; it never sets $J_0$ to one.
\end{proof}

The local algebra is $\pmreceiverC[h]/(h^{m_\rho})$, not only its constant

The same full multiplicities are the ones in
\eqref{pmreceiver:PMH7-trace} and \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH7-Newton}.

\begin{pmreceiverlemma}[PMH9: finite midpoint enclosure]
\label{pmreceiver:PMH9}
For a straight complex segment with displacement $v_i$, length
$L_i$, midpoint $c_i$, and a differentiable holomorphic integrand
$f$ with $|f'|\le D_i$ on it,
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH9-midpoint}
 \left|\int_{\Gamma_i}f(s)\pmreceiverdd s-v_if(c_i)\right|
 \le\frac{L_i^2D_i}{4}.
\end{equation}
An evaluation-ball radius $\nu_i$ for $f(c_i)$ adds $L_i\nu_i$.
\end{pmreceiverlemma}
\begin{proof}
Parameterize the segment by arclength $u\in[-L_i/2,L_i/2]$;
its unit tangent has modulus one. The fundamental theorem of
calculus gives $|f(s(u))-f(c_i)|\le D_i|u|$.
Integrating and using
$\int_{-L_i/2}^{L_i/2}|u|\pmreceiverdd u=L_i^2/4$ proves the claim.
The evaluation-ball contribution follows by multiplying its radius
by $|v_i|=L_i$.
\end{proof}

For the exterior receiver \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH6-approx}, write

 |L_Q'|\le U_{2,i}/a_i+U_{1,i}^2/a_i^2.

Thus for $f(s)=L_Q(s)/(s-w)^{r+1}$ one may take

       +\frac{(r+1)U_{1,i}}{a_i d_i^{r+2}}.
\end{equation}
Combine PMH9, multiplied by $r!/(2\pi)$, with
\eqref{pmreceiver:PMH6-error} for a finite arithmetic ball enclosing

\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH9-traceD}
 D_i=\sup|\psi'|\frac{U_{1,i}}{a_i}
 +\sup|\psi|\left(\frac{U_{2,i}}{a_i}
                         +\frac{U_{1,i}^2}{a_i^2}\right).
\end{equation}
For the trace derivative, use $f=\psi'Q''/Q$ and the cap

\end{equation}
Multiply its midpoint and evaluation errors by $1/(8\pi)$ and add

 |C_k(p,u)|\le\frac{2^{1/2-u}}{2\pi}
       \frac{\Gamma(k/2)}{2\pi((3-2\log2)\pi)^{k/2}}
       \quad(u\le0),
\]
\[
 |RS_K(u+iT')|\le\frac17 2^{3u/2}\Gamma((K+1)/2)(1.1/a)^{K+1}
       \quad(u\ge0),
\]
\[
 |RS_K(u+iT')|\le\frac12(9/10)^{\lceil-u\rceil}
       \Gamma((K+1)/2)(1.1/a)^{K+1}
       \quad(u<0,\ K+u\ge2).

We also retain the source input $|C_0(p)|\le1/2$. All subsequent
integrations and numerical majorants are proved below.

Set $D=T-3$ and $c=11/12$. The source effective Stirling estimate
\texttt{elem-lem}(v) bounds its exponent error at $u+iT'$ by
$1/(6(|u+iT'|-0.66))\le1/(6(T'-0.66))$.
Taylor's formula for \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH1-logM} and the source derivative
bound \texttt{alpha-deriv-bound} give
\[
 |\log M_0(u+iT')-\log M_0(iT')-u\alpha(iT')|
 \le\frac{u^2}{4(T'-3)}.
\]
The exact rational terms of $\alpha$ give
\[
 \left|\alpha(iT')-\log a-\frac{\pi i}{4}\right|
 =\left|\frac1{2iT'}+\frac1{iT'-1}\right|\le\frac3{2T'}.
\]
The combined exponent error is therefore at most
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH11-pointwise}
 \frac{u^2}{4(T'-3)}+\frac{3|u|}{2T'}+\frac1{6(T'-0.66)}
 \le\frac{u^2+6|u|+2/3}{4D}
 \le\frac{u^2+c}{D}.
\end{equation}
The last step is $6|u|\le3u^2+3$. The numerator $2/3$, not
$1/3$, accounts for the complete Stirling error.

For $u>0$ use $K_u=1$. For $u<0$ use
$K_u=\max(\lfloor-u\rfloor+3,\lfloor2a^2\rfloor)$, so
$K_u+u>2$. Put
\[
 S(u)=\sum_{k=1}^{K_u}\frac{|C_k(p,u)|}{a^k}
            +|RS_{K_u}(u+iT')|.
\]
For $u>0$, the imported bounds give
\[
 S(u)\le\frac{0.2\,9^u}{a}+\frac{0.173\,2^{3u/2}}{a^2}
 \le\frac{0.2\,9^u}{a-0.865}.
\]
Here $2^{3u/2}\le9^u$, $0.2(0.865)=0.173$, and the geometric
series bounds the two reciprocal powers. The value at the single
point $u=0$ has no effect on any integral below.

For $u<0$, the exact constants satisfy
$\sqrt2/(4\pi^2)<0.036$ and
$((3-2\log2)\pi)^{-1/2}<0.445$. Moreover
\[
 \frac{\Gamma((k+2)/2)}{a^{k+2}}
 =\frac{k}{2a^2}\frac{\Gamma(k/2)}{a^k}.
\]
For indices up to $2a^2$ this permits geometric majorants in the
even and odd subsequences. They give
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH11-low}
 \sum_{1\le k\le2a^2}\frac{|C_k(p,u)|}{a^k}
 \le0.036\,2^{-u}
 \left(\frac{0.445\sqrt\pi}{a}+\frac{0.247}{a^2}
                         +\frac{0.098}{a^3}\right)
 \le\frac{0.0285\,2^{-u}}{a-0.353}.
\end{equation}
The first constants follow from
\[
 \frac{0.445^2}{1-0.445^2}<0.247,\qquad
 \frac{0.445^3\sqrt\pi}{2(1-0.445^2)}<0.098.
\]
For the last inequality compare the first three coefficients of
the geometric series for $0.0285/(a-0.353)$, using
\[
 0.036(0.445)\sqrt\pi<0.0285,\quad
 0.036(0.247)<0.0285(0.353),\quad
 0.036(0.098)<0.0285(0.353)^2.
\]

The remainder when $K_u=\lfloor2a^2\rfloor$ must still be kept.
Let $r=K_u+1\ge32$; then $a^2\ge(r-1)/2$. Its imported bound,
multiplied by $a2^u$, is at most
\[
 v_r=\frac12(1.1)^r\Gamma(r/2)
       \left(\frac{r-1}{2}\right)^{(1-r)/2}.
\]
Indeed $(9/10)^{\lceil-u\rceil}\le1\le2^{-u}$.
The ratio of successive same-parity terms is exactly
\[
 \frac{v_{r+2}}{v_r}=1.21\frac r{r+1}
       \left(\frac{r-1}{r+1}\right)^{(r-1)/2}
 \le1.21e^{-(r-1)/(r+1)}\le1.21e^{-31/33}<1.
\]
The finite checks $v_{32}<4.897\times10^{-6}$ and
$v_{33}<3.268\times10^{-6}$ are both below $0.0005$.
Thus this complete branch of the remainder is at most
$0.0005\,2^{-u}/a\le0.0005\,2^{-u}/(a-0.353)$.
Together with \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH11-low} it is covered by
$0.029\,2^{-u}/(a-0.353)$; no remainder has been dropped.

If $K_u>\lfloor2a^2\rfloor$, the high coefficients and final
remainder all have integer indices in $2a^2\le k\le-u+4$.
The coefficient inequality $0.036(0.445)^k\le(1/2)(1.1)^k$
puts both into one positive sum. Adding unused positive terms in
the two sign ranges gives the uniform bound
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH11-S}
 S(u)\le\frac{0.2\,9^u}{a-0.865}+\frac{0.029\,2^{-u}}{a-0.353}
       +\frac{2^{-u}}2
          \sum_{2a^2\le k\le-u+4}(1.1)^k\frac{\Gamma(k/2)}{a^k}.
\end{equation}

Now use the original shift \texttt{RTN-def} with $\beta_N=\pi i/4$
and $u=\sigma+\sqrt t\,v$. The shift keeps $e^{t\pi^2/64}$;
the factors $a^{-u}$ and $e^{u\log a}$ cancel; the phases
$e^{\pi iu/4}$ and $e^{-\pi i\sqrt t v/4}$ leave
$e^{\pi i\sigma/4}$. With
$\mathrm d\nu(u)=(\pi t)^{-1/2}e^{-(u-\sigma)^2/t}\pmreceiverdd u$,

\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH11-epsdelta}
 \tfrac12\epsilon+\delta,\quad
 \epsilon=\int_\pmreceiverR(e^{(u^2+c)/D}-1)\mathrm d\nu(u),\quad

\end{equation}
This uses \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH11-pointwise}, $|e^w-1|\le e^{|w|}-1$ and
the unchanged $|C_0|\le1/2$.

For real $l$, completing the square gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH11-Gaussian}
 J(l):=\int_\pmreceiverR e^{(u^2+c)/D+2lu}\mathrm d\nu(u)
 =e^{2l\sigma+tl^2}(1-t/D)^{-1/2}
       \exp\left(\frac cD+\frac{(\sigma+tl)^2}{D-t}\right).
\end{equation}
Every occurrence of $D$ is $T-3$. Expanding the quadratic exponent
gives leading coefficient $-(D-t)/(tD)$; integrating that Gaussian
proves the identity. Since $-\log(1-v)\le v/(1-v)$ for

\[
 \log J(0)\le\frac{c+\sigma^2+t/2}{D-t}
 \le\frac{13/6}{T-3.5}\le\frac{10}{3(T-6)}.

With $v=10/(3(T-6))$, $e^v-1\le ve^v$, hence
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH11-epsilon}
 \tfrac12\epsilon\le\frac5{3(T-6)}e^{3.49/(T-4)}.
\end{equation}
The exponent comparison $10/(3(T-6))\le3.49/(T-4)$ is verified

\[\delta_1=0.2J(\log3)/(a-0.865),\qquad
\delta_2=0.029J(-\log\sqrt2)/(a-0.353).\]
Put

       =3.5670161955\ldots.
\]
After its factor $9^\sigma e^{t\log^23}$, the remaining

\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH11-absorb}

 <0.365986<0.366
\end{equation}

\[

 \sum_{k\ge2a^2}\frac{(1.1)^k\Gamma(k/2)}{a^k}

 \exp\left(\frac{u^2+c}{D}-\frac{(u-\sigma)^2}{t}
                          -u\log2\right)\pmreceiverdd u.
\]
Here $k\ge32$, hence $k\ge14$ and $u\le-10$. Since
$\sigma\ge0$, $t\le1/2$, $D\ge97$, and
$(1+c/100)/97<1$, this exponent is at most
$-u^2/(2t)-u\log2$. For $u\le4-k$ one has
$-u^2/(2t)\le(k-4)u/(2t)$. Integration and
$((k-4)/(2t)-\log2)\ge(k-6)/(2t)$ give
\[
 \delta_3\le\frac{\sqrt t}{\sqrt\pi}
 \sum_{k\ge2a^2}\frac{(1.1)^k\Gamma(k/2)}{(k-6)a^k}
            e^{-(k-4)^2+(k-4)\log2}.
\]
Set $f_k=(1.1)^k\Gamma(k/2)e^{-(k-4)^2+(k-4)\log2}$.
The two finite bases satisfy $f_{14}<1.042\times10^{-37}$ and
$f_{15}<4.516\times10^{-46}$, both below $10^{-30}$, and
\[
 \frac{f_{k+2}}{f_k}=2.42k e^{12-4k}<1\quad(k\ge14).
\]
Indeed $k e^{-4k}$ decreases there and the case $k=14$ passes.

now gives
\begin{equation}\label{pmreceiver:PMH11-delta3}
 \delta_3\le\sum_{k\ge14}\frac{10^{-30}}{a^k}
       \le\frac{2\times10^{-30}}{a^{14}}.
\end{equation}
The complete denominator slack covers this term:
\[
 \frac{0.031\,2^{-\sigma}}{a-0.865}
 -\frac{0.031\,2^{-\sigma}}{a-0.353}
 =\frac{0.031(0.512)2^{-\sigma}}{(a-0.865)(a-0.353)}
 \ge\frac{0.007936}{a^2}
 >\frac{2\times10^{-30}}{a^{14}}.
\]
Thus
\[
 \delta\le\frac{0.366\,9^\sigma+0.031\,2^{-\sigma}}
                  {a-0.865}e^{3.49/(T-4)}
 \le\frac{0.397\,9^\sigma}{a-0.865}e^{3.49/(T-4)}.
\]
Together with \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH11-epsdelta} and \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH11-epsilon}
this proves \eqref{pmreceiver:PMH11-final}. Every finite transcendental
comparison in this proof is checked in
\texttt{corrected\_rtn\_constant\_checks()}.
\end{proof}

\section{Cutoff admissibility and the previously missing remainder}

Write $b=2a^2=T'/\pi$. We have $b\ge100/\pi>31$ and $a>3$.
Choose the source cutoff
\[
 K_u=1\quad(u\ge0),\qquad
 K_u=\max(\lfloor-u\rfloor+3,\lfloor b\rfloor)\quad(u<0).
\]
This is a measurable, integer-valued function. For $u<0$,
$\lfloor-u\rfloor+u>-1$, hence $K_u+u>2$, which verifies
the only additional condition in \eqref{pmindependent:rsneg}. Define
\[
 S(u)=\sum_{k=1}^{K_u}|C_k(p,u)|a^{-k}+|RS_{K_u}(u+iT')|.
\]
For $u>0$, \eqref{pmindependent:ckpos} and \eqref{pmindependent:rspos} give
\[
 S(u)\le\frac{0.2\,9^u}{a}+\frac{0.173\,2^{3u/2}}{a^2}
 \le0.2\,9^u\left(\frac1a+\frac{0.865}{a^2}\right)
 \le\frac{0.2\,9^u}{a-0.865}.
\]
Indeed $\sqrt{2\pi}/(4\pi)<0.2$, $1.21/7<0.173$,
$2^{3/2}<9$, and $0.2(0.865)=0.173$.
At $u=0$, use \eqref{pmindependent:ckneg}; its $k=1$ coefficient is below
$0.036(0.445)\sqrt\pi<0.0285<0.2$. Thus the same bound holds
also at $u=0$, without extending the domain of \eqref{pmindependent:ckpos}.

For $u<0$, put $q=0.445$. The exact constants in \eqref{pmindependent:ckneg}
satisfy
\[
 \frac{\sqrt2}{4\pi^2}<0.036,\qquad
 ((3-2\log2)\pi)^{-1/2}<q.
\]
The identity
\[
 \frac{\Gamma((k+2)/2)}{a^{k+2}}
 =\frac{k}{b}\frac{\Gamma(k/2)}{a^k}
\]
shows that on each parity subsequence, as long as both indices remain
at most $b$, successive terms $q^k\Gamma(k/2)/a^k$ have ratio at
most $q^2$. Consequently
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{1\le k\le b}\frac{|C_k(p,u)|}{a^k}
 &\le0.036\,2^{-u}\left(
 \frac{q\sqrt\pi}{a}+
 \frac{q^2}{1-q^2}\frac1{a^2}+
 \frac{q^3\sqrt\pi}{2(1-q^2)}\frac1{a^3}\right)\\
 &\le0.036\,2^{-u}\left(
 \frac{0.445\sqrt\pi}{a}+\frac{0.247}{a^2}+\frac{0.098}{a^3}\right)
 \le\frac{0.0285\,2^{-u}}{a-0.353}.\tag{L}\label{pmindependent:low}
\end{align*}
For the last step, expand $(a-0.353)^{-1}$ as a positive geometric
series and compare its first three terms, using
\[
 0.036(0.445)\sqrt\pi<0.0285,\quad
 0.036(0.247)<0.0285(0.353),\quad
 0.036(0.098)<0.0285(0.353)^2.
\]

If $K_u=\lfloor b\rfloor$, its remainder index is
$r=K_u+1=\lfloor b\rfloor+1\ge32$. Since
$a^2\ge(r-1)/2$, \eqref{pmindependent:rsneg} gives
\begin{equation}\label{pmindependent:vr}
 a2^u|RS_{K_u}(u+iT')|
 \le v_r:=\frac12(1.1)^r\Gamma(r/2)
                           ((r-1)/2)^{(1-r)/2}.
\end{equation}
Here $2^u\le1$ and $(9/10)^{\lceil-u\rceil}\le1$.
Direct gamma recurrence gives the exact ratio
\[
 \frac{v_{r+2}}{v_r}=1.21\frac r{r+1}
     \left(\frac{r-1}{r+1}\right)^{(r-1)/2}
 \le1.21\exp\!\left(-\frac{r-1}{r+1}\right)
 \le1.21e^{-31/33}<1.
\]
The first inequality uses $\log(1-x)\le-x$ with $x=2/(r+1)$;
the last follows already from $e^{31/33}>1+31/33>1.21$.
The exact squared base expressions are
\[
 v_{32}^2=\frac14(11/10)^{64}(15!)^2(2/31)^{31},\qquad
 v_{33}^2=\frac14(11/10)^{66}
 \left(\frac{32!}{4^{16}16!}\right)^2\pi\,16^{-32}.
\]
Their certified upper bounds are respectively
$(4.897\cdot10^{-6})^2$ and $(3.268\cdot10^{-6})^2$.
Both are less than $(0.0005)^2$. The two parity inductions now prove
\[
 |RS_{K_u}(u+iT')|\le\frac{0.0005\,2^{-u}}a
 \le\frac{0.0005\,2^{-u}}{a-0.353}
 \quad\hbox{when }K_u=\lfloor b\rfloor.
\]
This is the remainder absent from the source's displayed bound
after \texttt{laf}. Together with \eqref{pmindependent:low}, it fits
$0.029\,2^{-u}/(a-0.353)$.

If $K_u>\lfloor b\rfloor$, necessarily $K_u=\lfloor-u\rfloor+3$.
Every coefficient index above $b$ satisfies $b\le k\le-u+4$;
the final remainder index $K_u+1$ also satisfies those inequalities.
By \eqref{pmindependent:ckneg}, its coefficient majorant is at most
$(1/2)2^{-u}(1.1)^k\Gamma(k/2)/a^k$, and \eqref{pmindependent:rsneg}
puts the remainder in the same sum. The coefficient indices and
the remainder index are distinct, so no index is counted twice
for this upper bound. If $b$ is an integer, the extra index $k=b$
in the enlarged high sum is harmless. Ties
$\lfloor-u\rfloor+3=\lfloor b\rfloor$ were already covered by
\eqref{pmindependent:vr}. Therefore, for every real $u$,
\begin{equation}\label{pmindependent:Sbound}
 S(u)\le\frac{0.2\,9^u}{a-0.865}
       +\frac{0.029\,2^{-u}}{a-0.353}
       +\frac{2^{-u}}2\sum_{b\le k\le-u+4}
                              \frac{(1.1)^k\Gamma(k/2)}{a^k}.
\end{equation}
All sums here and below are over integers. Empty sums are zero.

\section{The pointwise exponent and the exact Gaussian integral}

Let $D=T-3\ge97$ and $c=11/12$. Source Lemma
\texttt{elem-lem}(v), attributed to Boyd \cite{pmindependent:polymath,pmindependent:boyd},
states an exponential Stirling error at $z=(u+iT')/2$ of modulus
at most $1/(12(|z|-0.33))$. Thus its contribution is at most
$1/(6(T'-0.66))$. Taylor's formula using \eqref{pmindependent:alphabound}
and the horizontal segment from $iT'$ to $u+iT'$ bounds the
logarithmic Taylor remainder by $u^2/(4(T'-3))$. Finally
\[
 \alpha(iT')=\log a+\frac{\pi i}4+\frac1{2iT'}+\frac1{iT'-1},
 \qquad
 \left|\frac1{2iT'}+\frac1{iT'-1}\right|\le\frac3{2T'}.
\]
There is consequently a complex exponential error $E(u)$ with
\begin{equation}\label{pmindependent:Ebound}
 |E(u)|\le\frac{u^2}{4(T'-3)}+\frac{3|u|}{2T'}
                      +\frac1{6(T'-0.66)}
 \le\frac{u^2+6|u|+2/3}{4D}
 \le\frac{u^2+c}{D}.
\end{equation}
The last step uses $6|u|\le3u^2+3$. In particular the Stirling
numerator is $2/3$, not the source's $1/3$.

For $t>0$, the source contour shift \texttt{RTN-def} with
$\beta_N=\pi i/4$ reads
\[
 R_{t,N}(\sigma+iT)=e^{t\pi^2/64}
 \int_\pmindependentR e^{-\sqrt t\,v\pi i/4}
 R_{0,N}(\sigma+\sqrt t\,v+iT')
                         \frac{e^{-v^2}}{\sqrt\pi}\pmindependentdd v.
\]
The shift is admissible because $T,T'>0$; the source justifies
this by slower-than-Gaussian growth on the connecting horizontal
strips. Set $u=\sigma+\sqrt t\,v$. Formula \eqref{pmindependent:RSexp} contains
$a^{-u}$, which cancels the factor $e^{u\log a}$ from $M_0$.
The two phases $e^{\pi iu/4}$ and $e^{-\sqrt t\,v\pi i/4}$
leave exactly $e^{\pi i\sigma/4}$. The real probability measure is
\[
 \pmindependentdd\nu(u)=\frac1{\sqrt{\pi t}}e^{-(u-\sigma)^2/t}\pmindependentdd u,
 \qquad \int_\pmindependentR\pmindependentdd\nu=1.
\]
Using $|C_0|\le1/2$ and $|e^z-1|\le e^{|z|}-1$, the left
side of \eqref{pmindependent:result} is bounded by
\begin{equation}\label{pmindependent:epsdelta}
 \frac12\epsilon+\delta,\qquad
 \epsilon=\int_\pmindependentR(e^{(u^2+c)/D}-1)\pmindependentdd\nu(u),\qquad
 \delta=\int_\pmindependentR e^{(u^2+c)/D}S(u)\pmindependentdd\nu(u).
\end{equation}

For every real $l$, completing the square gives exactly
\begin{equation}\label{pmindependent:J}
 J(l):=\int_\pmindependentR e^{(u^2+c)/D+2lu}\pmindependentdd\nu(u)
 =e^{2l\sigma+tl^2}(1-t/D)^{-1/2}
       \exp\!\left(\frac cD+\frac{(\sigma+tl)^2}{D-t}\right).
\end{equation}
To check the algebra, the exponent before integration is
\[
 -\frac{D-t}{tD}u^2+2(\sigma/t+l)u+c/D-\sigma^2/t.
\]
Its Gaussian integral contributes $(1-t/D)^{-1/2}$ and exponent
$c/D-\sigma^2/t+tD(\sigma/t+l)^2/(D-t)$.
Subtracting $2l\sigma+tl^2+c/D$ leaves
$(\sigma+tl)^2/(D-t)$, proving \eqref{pmindependent:J}. Since $D>t$,
all these integrals converge. No occurrence of $D$ in this identity
can be replaced by $T'-0.33$.

The elementary inequality $-\log(1-x)\le x/(1-x)$ for
$0\le x<1$ yields
\[
 \log J(0)\le\frac{c+\sigma^2+t/2}{D-t}
 \le\frac{13/6}{T-3.5}\le\frac{10}{3(T-6)}.
\]
The last inequality follows from $13(T-6)\le20(T-3.5)$.
Since $e^v-1\le ve^v$ for $v\ge0$ and
\[
 \frac{10}{3(T-6)}\le\frac{3.49}{T-4}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 0.47T\ge22.82,
\]
we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{pmindependent:epsilonfinal}
 \frac12\epsilon=\frac12(J(0)-1)
       \le\frac5{3(T-6)}e^{3.49/(T-4)}.
\end{equation}

\section{The first two integrated coefficient terms}

By \eqref{pmindependent:Sbound} write $\delta\le\delta_1+\delta_2+\delta_3$,

 A=c+(1+L/2)^2+1/4=3.5670161955\ldots.

 \frac cD+\frac{(\sigma+tL)^2+t/2}{D-t}

Since $A>3.49$ and $T-4\ge96$,

 0.2e^{tL^2}e^{(A-3.49)/(T-4)}

This absorption is necessary: merely replacing $5/6$ by $11/12$

\section{The exact tail cutoff and its enlargement}

Tonelli's theorem for nonnegative measurable functions gives the
exact expression
\begin{equation}\label{pmindependent:tail}
 \delta_3=\frac1{2\sqrt{\pi t}}
 \sum_{k\ge b}\frac{(1.1)^k\Gamma(k/2)}{a^k}
 \int_{-\infty}^{4-k}
 \exp\!\left(\frac{u^2+c}D-\frac{(u-\sigma)^2}t-u\log2\right)\pmindependentdd u.
\end{equation}
The cutoff is exactly $b=T'/\pi$, not $T'/(2.2\pi)$.
Here $k\ge32$ and $u\le-28$. To prepare an upper bound enlarged
to $k\ge14$, it is enough to use $u\le-10$. Because $\sigma\ge0$,
$(u-\sigma)^2\ge u^2$. Moreover, $u^2\ge100$, $D\ge97$,
and $1/(2t)\ge1$ imply
\[
 \frac{u^2+c}D
 \le u^2\frac{1+c/100}{97}\le\frac{u^2}{2t}.
\]
Thus the exponent in \eqref{pmindependent:tail} is at most
$-u^2/(2t)-u\log2$. For $u\le4-k$,
$-u^2/(2t)\le(k-4)u/(2t)$, so
\begin{align*}
 \int_{-\infty}^{4-k}e^{-u^2/(2t)-u\log2}\pmindependentdd u
 &\le\frac{e^{-(k-4)^2/(2t)+(k-4)\log2}}
                {(k-4)/(2t)-\log2}\\
 &\le\frac{2t}{k-6}e^{-(k-4)^2+(k-4)\log2}.
\end{align*}
Both denominators are positive, and the last step follows from
$2t\le1$, $2t\log2\le2$. Therefore
\[
 \delta_3\le\frac{\sqrt t}{\sqrt\pi}
 \sum_{k\ge b}\frac{f_k}{(k-6)a^k},\qquad
 f_k=(1.1)^k\Gamma(k/2)e^{-(k-4)^2+(k-4)\log2}.
\]
The two bases are
\[
 f_{14}=(1.1)^{14}6!2^{10}e^{-100}<1.042\cdot10^{-37},
\]
\[
 f_{15}=(1.1)^{15}\frac{14!\sqrt\pi}{4^7 7!}2^{11}e^{-121}
                                         <4.516\cdot10^{-46}.
\]
Exact gamma recurrence gives
\[
 \frac{f_{k+2}}{f_k}=2.42k e^{12-4k}<1\qquad(k\ge14).
\]
Indeed $x e^{-4x}$ decreases for $x\ge14$ and the ratio at $14$
is $33.88e^{-44}<1$, already implied by $e^{44}>45$.
Parity induction proves $f_k<10^{-30}$ for every integer $k\ge14$.
Since $\sqrt t/\sqrt\pi<1$ and $1/(k-6)<1$ throughout the
original range, enlarging the nonnegative sum now gives
\begin{equation}\label{pmindependent:tailfinal}
 \delta_3\le\sum_{k\ge14}\frac{10^{-30}}{a^k}
 =\frac{10^{-30}}{a^{14}(1-a^{-1})}
 \le\frac{2\cdot10^{-30}}{a^{14}}.
\end{equation}
This is an inequality, with no altered identity or cutoff.

For $0\le\sigma\le1$ and $a>3$ the available denominator slack is
\begin{align*}
 \frac{0.031\,2^{-\sigma}}{a-0.865}
 -\frac{0.031\,2^{-\sigma}}{a-0.353}
 &=\frac{0.031(0.512)2^{-\sigma}}
          {(a-0.865)(a-0.353)}\\
 &\ge\frac{0.007936}{a^2}
 >\frac{2\cdot10^{-30}}{a^{14}}.
\end{align*}
The last step follows from $a^{12}>3^{12}$ and
$0.007936\,3^{12}>2\cdot10^{-30}$. Multiplying the slack by
$e^{3.49/(T-4)}>1$ only increases it. Equations \eqref{pmindependent:deltaone},
\eqref{pmindependent:deltatwo}, and \eqref{pmindependent:tailfinal} consequently give
\[
 \delta\le\frac{0.366\,9^\sigma+0.031\,2^{-\sigma}}{a-0.865}
                       e^{3.49/(T-4)}
 \le\frac{0.397\,9^\sigma}{a-0.865}e^{3.49/(T-4)}.
\]
Together with \eqref{pmindependent:epsdelta} and \eqref{pmindependent:epsilonfinal}, this proves
Theorem \ref{pmindependent:main} for $t>0$.

\section{Exact rational certificates for the finite comparisons}

The companion \texttt{exact\_certificates.py} uses Python integer
arithmetic, \texttt{Fraction}, and factorials only. Every comparison
is exact. No floating-point comparison is used. Its rational
enclosures follow from these elementary series arguments.

For $0<z<1$, alternating integration of the finite geometric series
gives
$\arctan z=\sum_{j\ge0}(-1)^jz^{2j+1}/(2j+1)$,
between a partial sum and that sum plus its next term. Machin's
identity $\pi=16\arctan(1/5)-4\arctan(1/239)$ follows from
the tangent addition formula and the fact that the relevant angle
is in $(0,\pi/2)$: $\tan(2\arctan(1/5))=5/12$,
$\tan(4\arctan(1/5))=120/119$, and subtracting
$\arctan(1/239)$ gives tangent $1$. Using partial sums through
$j=40$ and $j=10$, respectively, proves
\[
 3.1415926535<\pi<3.1415926536,
 \qquad \sqrt\pi<1.772453851,
\]
where the square-root assertion is checked by squaring positive
rationals.

For $x>1$, $z=(x-1)/(x+1)$ satisfies
\[
 \log x=2\sum_{j=0}^n\frac{z^{2j+1}}{2j+1}+R_n,
 \qquad 0<R_n\le\frac{2z^{2n+3}}{(2n+3)(1-z^2)}.
\]
This follows by integrating the geometric expansion of
$2/(1-z^2)$ and bounding every remaining denominator below by
$2n+3$. With $n=60$ for $x=2,3$, these give the stated enclosures
for logarithms and hence for $A$.

For rational $x\ge0$ and integer $n$ with $x<n+2$, write
$E_n(x)=\sum_{j=0}^n x^j/j!$. The ratios of successive terms
after the first omitted term are at most $x/(n+2)$, whence
\[
 E_n(x)\le e^x\le E_n(x)+
 \frac{x^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}\frac1{1-x/(n+2)}.
\]
The script uses $n=40$ for the two small positive exponential
arguments, and lower sums with $n=300$ at $x=100$ and $n=330$
at $x=121$ for the negative-exponential tail bases. This proves
all comparisons used above, in particular
\[
 3.5670161955<A<3.5670161956,
\]
\[
 0.2\exp\!\left((\log3)^2/2+(A-3.49)/96\right)<0.365986,
 \qquad 0.029e^{(\log\sqrt2)^2/2}<0.031.
\]
The script's 23 explicit checks all passed in both ordinary and
optimized Python mode on 20 September 2026. Failed comparisons and
invalid series domains raise exceptions explicitly, so optimization
cannot disable the checks.
They supplement the recurrence and monotonicity proofs; they do
not substitute a finite sample for a bound over the unbounded
