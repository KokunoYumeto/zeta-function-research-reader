This note independently derives the movable-pole portion of the supplied
full-Weil continuation, including its complete zero sums, the original
physical measure, all prime powers, all Gamma terms, and finite truncation
errors. It proves the identities corresponding to incoming equations
(18), (24)--(32), and (40)--(49). It does not claim a negative numerical
certificate or an off-critical zero. The separate Gaussian estimates and
the Fable determinant comparison are outside this note.

The classical analytic input is the completed Riemann function
\begin{equation}
 \xi(s)=\tfrac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s),\qquad
 \swpoleLL(s)=\frac{\xi'(s)}{\xi(s)}.\tag{MP1}
\end{equation}
It is entire of order one, has zeros precisely at the nontrivial zeta zeros
with their multiplicities, has no zero at zero or one, and satisfies
$\xi(1-s)=\xi(s)$ and $\xi(\bar s)=\overline{\xi(s)}$.
The source for the full genus-one product used below is Lagarias
\cite[Lemma 4.1 and its proof]{swpole:Lagarias}; absolute convergence of reciprocal
power sums of order at least two is stated and proved in
\cite[Lemma 2.1]{swpole:Lagarias}. The original author TeX, version 4, was read at
these locators and at the definition of the Weil form in Section 3.
Lagarias uses a form linear in its first slot. Our convention is its
transposition: $\swpoleW(F,G)=\langle G,F\rangle_{\mathcal W}$.
The moving-pole calculations below are proved here, rather than attributed
to that source. DLMF 25.2.12 \cite{swpole:DLMFProduct} also gives the
genus-one zeta product explicitly; its original formula TeX was read
and yields the completed-xi constants recorded in MP6a below.

Throughout, $\rho$ ranges over distinct nontrivial zeros, and $m_\rho$ is
their actual multiplicity. Every zero sum includes all these zeros.
We use $0<\swpoleRea\rho<1$ and
$\sum_\rho m_\rho(1+|\rho|)^{-2}<\infty$. These statements impose no
Riemann-hypothesis assumption.

\section{The original form, Mellin transform, and kernel}
Define $R(s)=1-\bar s$. Whenever the displayed series is absolutely
convergent, set
\begin{equation}
 \swpoleW(F,G)=\sum_\rho m_\rho\,\overline{F(R\rho)}G(\rho).
 \tag{MP2}
\end{equation}
This form is conjugate-linear in the first variable. Reflection permutes
the zeros with their multiplicities. Replacing $\rho$ by $R\rho$ in an
absolutely convergent sum therefore proves
$\overline{\swpoleW(G,F)}=\swpoleW(F,G)$. Under RH, and only when using that stated
assumption, $R\rho=\rho$, and MP2 becomes the positive sum
$\sum m_\rho\overline{F(\rho)}G(\rho)$.

Fix, without changing its physical or spectral coordinates,
\begin{equation}
 \omega=\sigma+i\tau,\quad \sigma>1,\quad h=2\sigma-1,
 \qquad U_\omega(s)=\frac{s+\bar\omega-1}{\omega-s},\qquad
 \varphi_j^\omega(s)=\frac{\sqrt h}{\omega-s}U_\omega(s)^j
 \quad(j\geq0).\tag{MP3}
\end{equation}
The square root of $h$ is the positive real square root. For every fixed
$j$, the function is holomorphic across the closed critical strip and is
$O((1+|\swpoleIma s|)^{-1})$ there. The constant may depend on $j$ and $\omega$.
Consequently MP2 converges absolutely on every finite linear span of
these functions, including between families with different such poles.

Use the Mellin convention $\mathcal Mf(s)=\int_0^\infty f(x)x^{s-1}\swpoledd x$.
Define the Laguerre polynomials here by their finite coefficients,
$L_j(u)=\sum_{\ell=0}^j(-1)^\ell\binom j\ell u^\ell/\ell!$.
Their classical generating function is also recorded in
Koornwinder--Wong--Koekoek--Swarttouw--Reinhardt \cite{swpole:Laguerre};
the transform and orthogonality needed here are proved below.
Then the physical source is exactly
\begin{equation}
 f_j^\omega(x)=\sqrt h\,(-1)^j x^{-\omega}L_j(h\log x)
                    \mathbf1_{\{x\geq1\}}.
 \tag{MP4}
\end{equation}
The value assigned at the single endpoint does not affect the Mellin
integral or the $L^2(\swpoledd x)$ class. These functions need not be smooth
across that endpoint.

\begin{swpolelemma}
For $\swpoleRea s<\sigma$ one has $\mathcal Mf_j^\omega(s)=\varphi_j^\omega(s)$.
For all $i,j\geq0$, with the original physical measure unchanged,
\begin{equation}
 \int_1^\infty\overline{f_i^\omega(x)}f_j^\omega(x)\swpoledd x=\delta_{ij}.
 \tag{MP5}
\end{equation}
\end{swpolelemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $a=\omega-s$. Termwise integration of the finite polynomial gives,
for $\swpoleRea a>0$,
\[
 \int_0^\infty e^{-at}L_j(ht)\swpoledd t
 =\sum_{\ell=0}^j(-1)^\ell\binom j\ell\frac{h^\ell}{a^{\ell+1}}
 =\frac{(a-h)^j}{a^{j+1}}.
\]
The substitution $x=e^t$ in the Mellin transform and
$h-a=s+\bar\omega-1$ prove MP3--MP4 with the stated sign.
The same substitution in the physical norm, now retaining its factor
$\swpoledd x=e^t\swpoledd t$, gives
\[
 h(-1)^{i+j}\int_0^\infty e^{-ht}L_i(ht)L_j(ht)\swpoledd t
 =(-1)^{i+j}\int_0^\infty e^{-u}L_i(u)L_j(u)\swpoledd u.
\]
For completeness, summing the finite definition of $L_j$ gives
\[
\sum_{j\geq0}L_j(u)t^j=(1-t)^{-1}\exp(-ut/(1-t)).
\]
For $|t|,|v|$ sufficiently small its product can be integrated against
$e^{-u}\swpoledd u$, by exponential domination. The result is
\[
 \frac1{(1-t)(1-v)}
 \int_0^\infty\exp\left[-u\left(1+\frac t{1-t}
                         +\frac v{1-v}\right)\right]\swpoledd u
 =\frac1{1-tv}.
\]
Comparison of coefficients proves that the last integral of the two
Laguerre polynomials is $\delta_{ij}$. Its accompanying phase is then
$(-1)^{2j}=1$, proving MP5.
\end{proof}

The full genus-one product, with constants $A,B$, is
\begin{equation}
 \xi(s)=e^{As+B}\prod_\rho
       \left[(1-s/\rho)e^{s/\rho}\right]^{m_\rho}.
 \tag{MP6}
\end{equation}
For the particular completion MP1, the exact constants may be retained
as well. Multiplying the original DLMF 25.2.12 product by
$\tfrac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)$ and using
$\Gamma(s/2+1)=(s/2)\Gamma(s/2)$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \xi(s)=\frac12\exp\!\left[
  \left(\log2+\tfrac12\log\pi-1-\tfrac12\gamma_E\right)s\right]
 \prod_\rho\left[(1-s/\rho)e^{s/\rho}\right]^{m_\rho}.
 \tag{MP6a}
\end{equation}
The factors initially cancel away from their displayed special points;
entire continuation gives the same identity there. The DLMF source
credits Titchmarsh (1986), equations (2.12.6) and (2.12.8), pages 30--31.
An earlier integration warning that this locator did not exist was
incorrect; the incoming locator is valid.
Its logarithmic derivative is locally normally convergent off the zeros
when the paired summand $1/(s-\rho)+1/\rho$ is kept intact. Subtracting
two such derivatives cancels both $A$ and the regularizers. Thus, for
$\swpoleRea\omega,\swpoleRea\nu>1$,
\begin{align}
 \swpoleLL(\nu)-\swpoleLL(1-\bar\omega)
 &=\sum_\rho m_\rho
       \left(\frac1{\nu-\rho}+\frac1{\rho+\bar\omega-1}\right),\tag{MP7}\\
 \swpoleW\left(\frac1{\omega-s},\frac1{\nu-s}\right)
 &=\frac{\swpoleLL(\nu)+\overline{\swpoleLL(\omega)}}{\nu+\bar\omega-1}.
 \tag{MP8}
\end{align}
Indeed the MP7 summand is
$(\nu+\bar\omega-1)/((\nu-\rho)(\rho+\bar\omega-1))$ and is
$O(|\rho|^{-2})$. The denominator $\nu+\bar\omega-1$ is nonzero.
Differentiating the two functional symmetries gives
$\swpoleLL(1-\bar\omega)=-\overline{\swpoleLL(\omega)}$. These facts prove MP8
with every Hadamard constant accounted for.

\section{Exact Toeplitz coefficients and the fixed-pole criterion}
At a zero put $\alpha=\omega-\rho$ and $\beta=\rho+\bar\omega-1$;
both are nonzero and $\alpha+\beta=h$. Direct conjugation, retaining the
same reflection as MP2, gives
$\overline{U_\omega(R\rho)}=U_\omega(\rho)^{-1}$.
It follows that the entire finite Weil matrix has the Toeplitz form
\begin{align}
 \swpoleW(\varphi_i^\omega,\varphi_j^\omega)&=c_{j-i}(\omega),
   &c_{-n}&=\overline{c_n},\tag{MP9}\\
 c_0&=\sum_\rho m_\rho\frac h{\alpha\beta}
             =2\swpoleRea\swpoleLL(\omega),\tag{MP10}\\
 c_n&=\sum_\rho m_\rho\frac{h\beta^{n-1}}{\alpha^{n+1}}
       \quad(n\geq1).\tag{MP11}
\end{align}
All these series converge absolutely: for fixed positive or negative
$n$, $U_\omega(\rho)$ and its inverse tend to $-1$ at large height,
while $h/(\alpha\beta)=O(|\rho|^{-2})$.

There is also an unconditional positive diagonal. Write
$d=\sigma-\tfrac12>\tfrac12$, $\rho=\tfrac12+\delta+i\gamma$, and
$y=\gamma-\tau$. Since $|\delta|<\tfrac12$,
\begin{equation}
 \swpoleRea\frac h{\alpha\beta}
 =\frac{h(d^2-\delta^2+y^2)}
        {(d^2-\delta^2+y^2)^2+4\delta^2y^2}>0.
 \tag{MP12}
\end{equation}
Hermiticity makes the sum MP10 real. Absolute convergence permits taking
its real part term by term. The nonempty zero set therefore gives
$c_0>0$ without RH.

Differentiating MP6 at least once after taking its logarithmic derivative
gives, for $\ell\geq1$,
$\swpoleLL^{(\ell)}(\omega)=(-1)^\ell\ell!\sum_\rho
m_\rho\alpha^{-\ell-1}$. These sums are absolutely convergent.
Expansion of $(h-\alpha)^{n-1}$ in MP11 therefore proves
\begin{equation}
 c_n=(-1)^n\sum_{\ell=1}^n\binom{n-1}{\ell-1}
               \frac{h^\ell}{\ell!}\swpoleLL^{(\ell)}(\omega).
 \tag{MP13}
\end{equation}
In particular $c_1=-h\swpoleLL'$, $c_2=h\swpoleLL'+h^2\swpoleLL''/2$, and
$c_3=-h\swpoleLL'-h^2\swpoleLL''-h^3\swpoleLL'''/6$, all at the original $\omega$.

For a finite vector $b=(b_0,\ldots,b_n)$ define
$F_b=\sum_{j=0}^n b_j\varphi_j^\omega$ and
$T_n=(c_{j-i})_{0\leq i,j\leq n}$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \swpoleW(F_b,F_b)=b^*T_nb,\qquad
 \swpoleW\left(\varphi_0^\omega-
             \frac{\bar c_n}{|c_n|}\varphi_n^\omega,
          \varphi_0^\omega-
             \frac{\bar c_n}{|c_n|}\varphi_n^\omega\right)
       =2(c_0-|c_n|)\quad(c_n\ne0).
 \tag{MP14}
\end{equation}
This follows by expansion using the conjugate-linear-first convention;
it fixes the complex phase rather than replacing it by a sign.

The relevant generating function is initially an identity near zero:
\begin{equation}
 s(z)=\frac{\omega+(1-\bar\omega)z}{1+z}
     =\omega-\frac{hz}{1+z},\qquad
 \swpoleLL(s(z))=\swpoleLL(\omega)+\sum_{n=1}^\infty c_n z^n.
 \tag{MP15}
\end{equation}
To prove it, use the Taylor series for $\swpoleLL$ at the zero-free point
$\omega$. For $1\leq\ell\leq n$ the coefficient of $z^n$ in
$(-hz/(1+z))^\ell$ is
$(-1)^n h^\ell\binom{n-1}{\ell-1}$. This gives MP13.
Unconditionally one must not presume that this Taylor series converges
on the whole unit disk.

\begin{swpoletheorem}
For each one fixed pole $\swpoleRea\omega>1$, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item RH;
\item $|c_n(\omega)|\leq c_0(\omega)$ for every $n\geq1$;
\item all finite matrices $T_n(\omega)$ are positive semidefinite.
\end{enumerate}
If RH is false, already a two-mode vector in MP14 is negative for some
finite $n$. This assertion evaluates no particular finite $n$ or phase.
\end{swpoletheorem}
\begin{proof}
Under RH, $\beta=\bar\alpha$, $|U_\omega(\rho)|=1$, and
MP10--MP11 give
\[
 c_n=\sum_\rho m_\rho\frac h{|\omega-\rho|^2}
                                      U_\omega(\rho)^n,
 \qquad c_0=\sum_\rho m_\rho\frac h{|\omega-\rho|^2}.
\]
The triangle inequality proves (2), and MP2 proves (3).
Conversely (3) implies (2) by the principal minor on indices $0,n$.
Under (2) the series in MP15 is holomorphic on $|z|<1$.
The map $s(z)$ takes that disk biholomorphically onto $\swpoleRea s>1/2$,
since
\begin{equation}
 \swpoleRea(s(z)-\tfrac12)=\frac{h(1-|z|^2)}{2|1+z|^2},\qquad
 s'(z)=-\frac h{(1+z)^2}.\tag{MP16}
\end{equation}
The composition $\swpoleLL\circ s$ is meromorphic there and agrees with the
series near zero. The identity theorem for meromorphic functions on the
connected disk makes it holomorphic on that disk. But a zero $\rho$ with
$\swpoleRea\rho>1/2$ would give a pole at
\begin{equation}
 z_\rho=\frac{\omega-\rho}{\rho+\bar\omega-1},\qquad
 |\rho+\bar\omega-1|^2-|\omega-\rho|^2
                       =h(2\swpoleRea\rho-1)>0.\tag{MP17}
\end{equation}
The residue of the composition there is
$m_\rho/s'(z_\rho)\ne0$. Distinct zeros give distinct points because
$s$ is injective, so the pole cannot cancel. This is a contradiction.
Reflection then also excludes zeros with $\swpoleRea\rho<1/2$, proving RH.
If RH fails, the contrapositive gives $|c_n|>c_0>0$ for some $n$,
and MP14 supplies the asserted vector.
\end{proof}

A precise strengthening records the degree dependence without assigning
an uncomputed degree. If there is a right-of-line zero, put
\begin{equation}
 r_\omega=\min_{\swpoleRea\rho>1/2}|z_\rho|<1.
 \quad\text{Then}\quad
 \limsup_{n\longrightarrow\infty}|c_n|^{1/n}=r_\omega^{-1}>1.
 \tag{MP18}
\end{equation}
The minimum exists and is positive: $\omega$ is not a zero, zeros are
discrete, and $z_\rho\to-1$ as $|\swpoleIma\rho|\to\infty$. There are
therefore finitely many zeros whose images lie in any fixed smaller disk.
The composition has no singularity inside $|z|<r_\omega$ and has a
genuine pole on its boundary. Thus the Taylor radius is $r_\omega$;
the Cauchy--Hadamard theorem proves MP18.

\section{Prime and Gamma evaluation with all constants retained}
For $\swpoleRea s>1$, the Euler product and MP1 give
\begin{equation}
 \swpoleLL(s)=\frac1s+\frac1{s-1}-\frac12\log\pi
           +\frac12\psi(s/2)-\sum_{m=2}^\infty\swpolevM(m)m^{-s},
 \qquad \psi=\Gamma'/\Gamma.\tag{MP19}
\end{equation}
Here $\swpolevM(p^a)=\log p$ for a prime power, and $\swpolevM(m)=0$ otherwise.
Indeed unique factorization expands the absolutely convergent Euler
product on this half-plane. Its logarithm is
$\sum_p\sum_{a\geq1}p^{-as}/a$; termwise differentiation, uniformly
on compact subsets of the half-plane, gives the displayed prime-power
series. The bound $0\leq\swpolevM(m)\leq\log m$ proves the needed local
uniform convergence of every differentiated series.

We spell out the Gamma formulas so their constants and domains are
explicit. For $\swpoleRea z>0$, repeated integration by parts gives
\[
 \int_0^1t^{z-1}(1-t)^M\swpoledd t
       =\frac{M!}{z(z+1)\cdots(z+M)}.
\]
After multiplying by $M^z$ and substituting $u=Mt$, dominated
convergence to the defining Gamma integral gives Euler's product limit
$\Gamma(z)=\lim_{M\to\infty}M!M^z/[z(z+1)\cdots(z+M)]$.
The domination follows from $(1-u/M)^M\leq e^{-u}$ on $0\leq u\leq M$,
uniformly for $z$ in a compact subset of the right half-plane, with
integrable bounds for the required powers of $\log u$.
Equivalently the regularized reciprocal product converges normally,
since its logarithmic summands after removal of the linear term are
$O(M^{-2})$ on such compact subsets. Its factors do not vanish there.
Logarithmic differentiation therefore gives
\begin{equation}
 \psi(z)=-\gamma_E+\sum_{r=0}^\infty
                   \left(\frac1{r+1}-\frac1{r+z}\right),
 \qquad
 \frac12\psi(s/2)=-\frac{\gamma_E}2+
       \sum_{r=0}^\infty\left(\frac1{2r+2}-\frac1{s+2r}\right).
 \tag{MP20}
\end{equation}
These sums converge locally normally. The $r=0$ pole in the second
sum is exactly $-1/s$, cancelling the first term of MP19.

For a fixed complex $a$ with $\omega\ne a$, direct expansion yields
\begin{equation}
 [z^n]\frac1{s(z)-a}
 =\frac h{(\omega-a)^2}
       \left(\frac{\bar\omega+a-1}{\omega-a}\right)^{n-1}
 \quad(n\geq1).\tag{MP21}
\end{equation}
To check the coefficient, write the rational function as
$(1+z)/((\omega-a)+(1-\bar\omega-a)z)$ and use its geometric series
at zero. Its $n$th coefficient is the displayed one since the sum of
$\omega-a$ and $\bar\omega+a-1$ is $h$.

Define $L_0^{(-1)}(x)=1$ and, for $n\geq1$,
\begin{equation}
 L_n^{(-1)}(x)=\sum_{\ell=1}^n(-1)^\ell
             \binom{n-1}{\ell-1}\frac{x^\ell}{\ell!},\qquad
 e^{xz/(1+z)}=\sum_{n=0}^\infty(-1)^nL_n^{(-1)}(x)z^n.
 \tag{MP22}
\end{equation}
The second identity follows by expanding the exponential and extracting
coefficients, using the same binomial coefficient as in MP15.
Choose a sufficiently small disk around zero so $\swpoleRea s(z)>1$.
The prime and Gamma series there converge locally uniformly, and so
coefficients may be extracted termwise. Combining MP19--MP22 gives the
exact formula, for each fixed $n\geq1$,
\begin{align}
 c_n={}&\frac h{(\omega-1)^2}
                \left(\frac{\bar\omega}{\omega-1}\right)^{n-1}
 -h\sum_{r=1}^\infty\frac1{(\omega+2r)^2}
             \left(\frac{\bar\omega-1-2r}{\omega+2r}\right)^{n-1}
 \notag\\[-2pt]
 &\hspace{4mm}-(-1)^n\sum_{m=2}^\infty
           \swpolevM(m)m^{-\omega}L_n^{(-1)}(h\log m).
 \tag{MP23}
\end{align}
The Gamma sum is absolutely convergent since
\begin{equation}
 |\omega+2r|^2-|\bar\omega-1-2r|^2=h(4r+1)>0.
 \tag{MP24}
\end{equation}
The prime sum is absolutely convergent by its fixed polynomial degree in
$\log m$ and $\sigma>1$. MP23 retains the phase $m^{-i\tau}$ and
every prime power. No assertion of prime-series convergence throughout
the Möbius unit disk is needed or made.

\section{Finite tail bounds and an exact computable certificate}
For integers $N\geq1$, $j\geq0$, define
\begin{equation}
 I_j(N,\sigma)=N^{1-\sigma}\sum_{r=0}^j
       \frac{j!}{(j-r)!}\frac{\log(N+1)^{j-r}}{(\sigma-1)^{r+1}}.
 \tag{MP25}
\end{equation}
For every such $N$, without a monotonicity threshold for a logarithmic
weight,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{m>N}(\log m)^j m^{-\sigma}\leq I_j(N,\sigma).
 \tag{MP26}
\end{equation}
To prove it, for $x\in[m-1,m]$ and $m>N$ use
$m^{-\sigma}\leq x^{-\sigma}$ and
$\log m\leq\log x+\log(1+1/N)$. The latter upper bound is
nonnegative on $x\geq N\geq1$. The sum is bounded by the integral of
$x^{-\sigma}(\log x+\log(1+1/N))^j$ from $N$ to infinity.
Putting $x=Ne^t$ evaluates that integral as
\[
 N^{1-\sigma}\int_0^\infty e^{-(\sigma-1)t}
                     (\log(N+1)+t)^j\swpoledd t=I_j(N,\sigma),
\]
by a finite binomial expansion and the elementary factorial integral.

Consequently the prime tail in MP23 after $m=N$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 E_{\mathrm{prime},n}\leq
 \sum_{\ell=1}^n\binom{n-1}{\ell-1}\frac{h^\ell}{\ell!}
                                     I_{\ell+1}(N,\sigma).
 \tag{MP27}
\end{equation}
This follows from MP22, $\swpolevM(m)\leq\log m$, and the triangle inequality,
including every term of the finite polynomial. The Gamma tail after
$r=M$, for an integer $M\geq0$, satisfies
\begin{equation}
 E_{\Gamma,n}\leq h\sum_{r>M}(\sigma+2r)^{-2}
       \leq h\int_M^\infty(\sigma+2x)^{-2}\swpoledd x
       =\frac h{2(\sigma+2M)}.\tag{MP28}
\end{equation}
The first inequality uses MP24, and the second uses a decreasing positive
function; both are valid for $n=1$ and for $M=0$.

The diagonal can also be enclosed without discarding a Gamma constant.
Let $B_1(t)=\{t\}-1/2$ off the integers, with arbitrary endpoint values.
For $\swpoleRea z>0$ the exact identity is
\begin{equation}
 \psi(z)=\log z-\frac1{2z}
       +\int_0^\infty\frac{B_1(t)}{(z+t)^2}\swpoledd t.
 \tag{MP29}
\end{equation}
Here and below the logarithm is its analytic branch on the right
half-plane, real on the positive real axis. To prove MP29, integrate
$B_1(t)f'(t)$ separately on each interval $[r,r+1]$ for
$f(t)=(z+t)^{-1}$. Summing the endpoint values gives, for integral $L>0$,
\[
 \sum_{r=0}^{L-1}\frac1{z+r}
 =\log(z+L)-\log z+\frac1{2z}-\frac1{2(z+L)}
                  -\int_0^L\frac{B_1(t)}{(z+t)^2}\swpoledd t.
\]
The Euler product above gives
$\psi(z)=\lim_{L\to\infty}(\log L-\sum_{r=0}^{L-1}(z+r)^{-1})$;
the harmless final term in that limit tends to zero. The integral
converges absolutely. Passing to the limit proves MP29 and its sign.

Use the exact finite recurrence
$\psi(z)=\psi(z+M)-\sum_{r=0}^{M-1}(z+r)^{-1}$.
For $a=\swpoleRea(z+M)>0$ and $b=\swpoleIma z$, the integral omitted after applying
MP29 at $z+M$ has absolute value at most
\begin{equation}
 J(a,b)=\frac12\int_0^\infty\frac{\swpoledd t}{(a+t)^2+b^2}
 =\begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\frac{\arctan(|b|/a)}{2|b|},&b\ne0,\\[5pt]
 \displaystyle\frac1{2a},&b=0.
 \end{cases}\tag{MP30}
\end{equation}
Use this with $z=\omega/2$. Since $c_0=2\swpoleRea\swpoleLL(\omega)$, the
factor $1/2$ multiplying $\psi$ in MP19 cancels that factor $2$:
the diagonal Gamma error is at most $J(a,b)$, not $2J(a,b)$.
The diagonal prime tail is at most $2I_1(N,\sigma)$.
The explicit rational terms, $\log\pi$, finite sums, and all rounding
errors must also be enclosed in the computed diagonal.

Suppose real $e_0,e_n\geq0$ and finite computations give
\begin{equation}
 |c_n-\widehat c_n|\leq e_n,\qquad
 |c_0-\widehat c_0|\leq e_0,\qquad
 \widehat c_0\in\mathbb R,\quad \widehat c_n\ne0.
 \tag{MP31}
\end{equation}
The nonzero condition makes the computed phase defined. If the initial
diagonal approximation is complex, replacing it by its real part does
not increase its error against the real number $c_0$.
Set
\begin{equation}
 \widehat F_n=\varphi_0^\omega-
                 \frac{\overline{\widehat c_n}}{|\widehat c_n|}
                      \varphi_n^\omega.
 \quad\text{Then}\quad
 \swpoleW(\widehat F_n,\widehat F_n)
 \leq2(\widehat c_0+e_0-|\widehat c_n|+e_n).
 \tag{MP32}
\end{equation}
Indeed the exact cross term is
$-2\swpoleRea(\overline{\widehat c_n}c_n/|\widehat c_n|)$.
Replacing $c_n$ by $\widehat c_n+(c_n-\widehat c_n)$ and applying
MP31 proves MP32. If the right side is strictly negative, the original
full zero form is negative, which contradicts its RH-positive expression.
This is an exact implication for the specified computed vector; there
is no negative certificate asserted by this note.

For all entries through degree $n$, take a Hermitian Toeplitz computed
matrix $\widehat T_n$ with real diagonal and the corresponding entry
errors $e_0,\ldots,e_n$. Each row and column of its error matrix has
absolute sum at most $e_0+2\sum_{r=1}^n e_r$. Hence
\begin{equation}
 \|T_n-\widehat T_n\|_{2\to2}
       \leq e_0+2\sum_{r=1}^n e_r,\qquad
 |b^*(T_n-\widehat T_n)b|
       \leq\left(e_0+2\sum_{r=1}^n e_r\right)\|b\|_2^2.
 \tag{MP33}
\end{equation}
For example, the operator bound follows from the square root of the
product of the maximum row and column sums. This proves a finite-vector
certificate without relying on an unenclosed floating-point eigenvalue.

\section{Exact height modulation and its amplification}
For $\rho=\tfrac12+\delta+i\gamma$ and the original pole
$\omega=\sigma+i\tau$, put $d=\sigma-\tfrac12$. Directly from MP3,
\begin{equation}
 \log|U_\omega(\rho)|
 =\frac12\log\frac{(d+\delta)^2+(\gamma-\tau)^2}
                         {(d-\delta)^2+(\gamma-\tau)^2}.
 \tag{MP34}
\end{equation}
For $\omega=2$ and fixed $0<\delta<1/2$, expanding the two logarithms
at $\gamma^{-2}=0$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \log|U_2(\rho)|
 =\frac{3\delta}{\gamma^2}
   -\frac{3\delta(9/4+\delta^2)}{\gamma^4}
   +O_\delta(\gamma^{-6}).\tag{MP35}
\end{equation}
In contrast, at the original height-matched pole $2+i\gamma$ the exact
value is
\begin{equation}
 \log|U_{2+i\gamma}(\rho)|
       =\log\frac{3/2+\delta}{3/2-\delta}.
 \tag{MP36}
\end{equation}
Both numerator and denominator are positive in the stated range, so
there is no unmentioned absolute-value or branch choice.

The physical comparison is the exact map
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 M_\tau:L^2([1,\infty),\swpoledd x)\longrightarrow L^2([1,\infty),\swpoledd x),
 \\ (M_\tau f)(x)=x^{-i\tau}f(x),
 \qquad M_\tau^{-1}=M_{-\tau},\qquad
 f_j^{\sigma+i\tau}=M_\tau f_j^\sigma.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{MP37}
\end{equation}
For real $x>0$, $|x^{-i\tau}|=1$, proving unitarity for the original
measure, and multiplication of the two phases proves the inverse.
The parameter $h=2\sigma-1$ stays fixed in this map. This proves precisely
how the physical test family changes while the zero set in MP2 remains
the same.

The arithmetic quantity detected by the two-mode form is therefore
\begin{equation}
 D_n(\sigma,\tau)=|c_n(\sigma+i\tau)|-
            2\swpoleRea\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(\sigma+i\tau).
 \tag{MP38}
\end{equation}
Its exact coefficients and finite errors are MP19--MP33. MP34--MP37
describe their spectral and physical dependence on the movable pole.
No low-degree positive computation supplies an estimate for every
degree or every pole, and no uncomputed $D_n$ is assigned a sign here.

\section{Original source, full zero sum and the human inputs}
Retain the completed zeta function and the actual Gaussian source
\[
 \xi(s)=\Phi(s)\zeta(s),\qquad
 \Phi(s)=\tfrac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2),\qquad
 \phi_*(x)=(4\pi^2x^4-6\pi x^2)e^{-\pi x^2}.                 \tag{WP1}
\]
For $D=-x\,d/dx$, $P(s)=\sum_{j=0}^3p_js^j$ and
$f_P=P(D)\phi_*$, integration by parts gives
\[
 F_P(s)=\int_0^\infty f_P(x)x^{s-1}\,dx=P(s)\Phi(s),
                  \qquad \Re s>-2.                            \tag{WP2}
\]
Indeed the two terms of the Mellin integral of $\phi_*$ are
$2\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2+2)$ and
$-3\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2+1)$; the Gamma recursion gives WP1.
Every integration by parts has zero boundary term on the stated
half-plane, since $D^j\phi_*(x)=O(x^2)$ at zero and is a polynomial
times a Gaussian at infinity. The even extension of every $f_P$ is
Schwartz, vanishes at zero, and has integral zero, since $\Phi(1)=0$.

All sums below run over the actual nontrivial zeros $\rho$ of $\zeta$,
with their actual multiplicities $m_\rho$. Define
\[
 \swcertW(F,G)=\sum_\rho m_\rho\,
       \overline{F(1-\bar\rho)}G(\rho).                         \tag{WP3}
\]
The functional equation and conjugation preserve this multiset.
The estimates below prove absolute convergence for the sources
used here, so reflection and conjugation may be applied without
an ordering convention. At a critical-line zero each summand of
$\swcertW(F,F)$ is $m_\rho|F(\rho)|^2$.

The finite-height input is Platt--Trudgian's Theorem~1
\cite{swcert:PlattTrudgian}: every zero up to the stated height
$3\,000\,175\,332\,800$ is on the critical line. We use only
\[
                         T_0=3\cdot10^{12}.                    \tag{WP4}
\]
For the unverified tail use Hasanalizade--Shen--Wong's
Corollary~1.2 \cite{swcert:HSW}, in its stated original version:
\[
 \left|N(T)-\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi e}\right|
 \le .1038\log T+.2573\log\log T+9.3675\quad(T\ge e).          \tag{WP5}
\]
$N(T)$ counts positive-height zeros with multiplicity. In particular
$N(T)\le T\log T$ for $T\ge100$: the right-hand error in WP5 is
less than $\log T+10\le T$, while
$1/(2\pi)+1/\log T<1$. These inequalities hold at $100$ and
persist because $T-\log T-10$ increases and $1/\log T$ decreases.
The proof uses no hypothesis on the location of zeros above WP4.

The functional equation and conjugation show that this function

5.11(ii) \cite{swcert:Gamma}. In the open right half-plane,

 \log\Gamma z=(z-\tfrac12)\log z-z+\tfrac12\log(2\pi)+R(z),
 \quad |R(z)|\le\frac{\sec^2(\arg z/2)}{12|z|}
                   \le\frac1{6|z|}.                           \tag{WP7}

The first inequality is the remainder after omitting all
Bernoulli terms. Put $z=\beta/2+it/2$ with $0\le\beta\le1$,

 \begin{split}

 &\le \sqrt{2\pi}\,b^{\beta/2-1/2}
       (1+.09)^{1/2}e^{1/30}e^{-\pi b/2}

 \end{split}                                                   \tag{WP8}

Here $|w|^{a-1/2}=b^{a-1/2}(1+a^2/b^2)^{(a-1/2)/2}$,

\section{The full cubic Gaussian inequality, with all high zeros retained}
Let $V_{j\ell}=\rho_j^\ell$ for $0\le\ell\le3$ and let
$H=V^*\swcertdiag(|\Phi(\rho_j)|^2)V$ be the contribution of one
copy of each of the four zeros. It is positive definite, and
\[
 \det H=\prod_{j=1}^4|\Phi(\rho_j)|^2
                   \prod_{i<j}|\rho_j-\rho_i|^2>e^{-160},
 \qquad \operatorname{tr}H<400\cdot485^5<e^{40}.              \tag{WP10}
\]
The trace estimate follows from WP8 and
$\sum_{\ell=0}^3|\rho_j|^{2\ell}\le4(1+\gamma_j^2)^3$,
discarding only the factor $e^{-\pi|\gamma_j|/2}\le1$ in this
upper bound. Hence
\[
                         \lambda_{\min}(H)>e^{-280}.           \tag{WP11}
\]
Indeed the product of its three largest eigenvalues is at most
$(\operatorname{tr}H)^3$.

For a unit coefficient vector $p$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$|P(\beta+it)|\le2(1+t^2)^{3/2}$ uniformly in the strip.
The absolute value of each unknown zero summand in WP3 is at
most

                  u(t)=100(1+t^2)^5e^{-ct},\qquad c=\pi/2.
                                                                  \tag{WP12}
\]
For $t\ge T_0$, $u$ decreases and
$0\le-u'(t)\le c u(t)\le3200c\,t^{10}e^{-ct}$.
Stieltjes integration by parts, WP5 and $\log t\le t$ show

 \begin{split}
 2\sum_{\Im\rho>T_0}m_\rho u(\Im\rho)
 &\le2\int_{T_0}^{\infty}N(t)(-u'(t))\,dt\\
 &\le6400e^{-cT_0}\sum_{j=0}^{12}
             \frac{12!}{(12-j)!}\frac{T_0^{12-j}}{c^j}
 <e^{-T_0}.
 \end{split}                                                   \tag{WP13}
\]
For the last inequality the sum is at most
$T_0^{12}\sum_{j=0}^{12}(12/(cT_0))^j<2T_0^{12}$.
Thus its logarithm including $6400$ is less than
$10+12\log T_0<370$, whereas $(c-1)T_0>10^{12}$.
The factor $2$ includes negative heights; the boundary term in
integration by parts is nonpositive and was discarded in an
upper bound. WP12--13 also prove absolute convergence.

All other zeros below WP4 contribute nonnegative terms, by

proves the complete, unconditional inequality

 \boxed{\swcertW(\Phi P,\Phi P)\ge e^{-300}\sum_{j=0}^3|p_j|^2.}
                                                                  \tag{WP14}
\]
The margin follows from $e^{-280}-e^{-T_0}>e^{-300}$.
The unknown high zeros have been estimated, not placed on the
critical line. WP14 is a statement about this entire original
cubic Gaussian source space, not only four sampled vectors.

\section{Exact translation and the coefficient-space receiver}
The original target coordinate is $S'=c_*+iy$, whereas the
zeta coordinate on the same physical line is $s=1/2+iy$.
Thus $S'=s+c_*-1/2$. Retain this exact affine map, and define
the actual coefficient translation $C_{c_*}$ by
$p(s+c_*-1/2)=\sum_i(C_{c_*}p)_i s^i$:
\[
 (C_{c_*})_{ij}=\begin{cases}\binom ji(c_*-1/2)^{j-i},&i\le j,\\
                  0,&i>j,\end{cases}\qquad
 \det C_{c_*}=1,\quad C_{c_*}^{-1}=C_{1-c_*}.                \tag{WP15}
\]
The entry formula is the binomial theorem; composing the two
translations proves the inverse. For the original conductor
coordinate map $y=\Psi q$ of FC26--32 \cite{swcert:FC} into the coefficient vector of the
cubic $p$, the original lift is exactly $p(D+c_*-1/2)\phi_*$.
Explicitly, $\Psi=YK^{-1}$, where $K$ is the retained collision

 y_a(S')=\sum_{u,w=0}^{8}a_{uw}

\]
Here $\ell_a(S)=\prod_{b\ne a}(S-\rho_b)/(\rho_a-\rho_b)$
and $v$ is the actual order of the conductor symbol, exactly as
in FC17 and FC26; its periods and original coefficients remain

 \boxed{\mathsf W_{\rm original}

This identity follows by applying WP2 to each original

\section{A rational cubic test and its unknown-tail bound}
Retain the pole $\omega=2$, its $h=3$, and the four functions
\[
 U(s)=\frac{s+1}{2-s},\qquad
 \varphi_j(s)=\frac{\sqrt3}{2-s}U(s)^j\quad(0\le j\le3).
                                                                  \tag{WP17}
\]
The complete movable-pole proof MP in the companion source \cite{swcert:MP}
establishes that their full-Weil Gram is the Hermitian Toeplitz
matrix $T_3(2)$; equivalently this matrix is defined directly by
WP3 applied to these four functions. The present positivity
proof uses WP3 and therefore does not depend on a truncated
zero expansion or on a Taylor approximation to its entries.

At a critical height $t$, the weight of one row is
$w_t=3/(9/4+t^2)>1/200$ for $14<|t|<22$, and $|U(1/2+it)|=1$.
For two of our four heights,
\[
 |U(1/2+it)-U(1/2+iu)|
 =\frac{3|t-u|}{\sqrt{9/4+t^2}\sqrt{9/4+u^2}}
 >\frac{18}{487}>\frac1{30}.                                \tag{WP18}
\]
Consequently the four-row Vandermonde Gram $H_R$ satisfies
\[

 \operatorname{tr}H_R\le\frac{16\cdot3}{9/4+14^2}<1,
 \qquad\lambda_{\min}(H_R)>b_*.                              \tag{WP19}
\]
On $0\le\Re s\le1$, $|\Im s|=t\ge10$, one has
$|U(s)|^2\le1+3/t^2<1.02^2$ and
$|U'(s)|\le3/t^2$. Differentiation of WP17 gives, for each
$0\le j\le3$,
\[
 |\varphi_j'(s)|
 \le\frac{\sqrt3}{t^2}
       \left(1.02^3+\frac9t\,1.02^2\right)<\frac4{t^2}.
\]
Thus for $F=\sum_{j=0}^3p_j\varphi_j$ with $\|p\|_2=1$,
\[
 |F'(s)|\le8/t^2,\qquad
 |F(\rho)-F(1-\bar\rho)|\le8/t^2.                           \tag{WP20}
\]
The second bound integrates on the horizontal segment of length
$|2\Re\rho-1|\le1$ inside the same strip. A noncritical reflected
pair with equal multiplicity $m$ contributes
\[
 2m\Re(\overline{F(1-\bar\rho)}F(\rho))
 =m\left(|F(\rho)|^2+|F(1-\bar\rho)|^2
        -|F(\rho)-F(1-\bar\rho)|^2\right)
 \ge-64m/t^4.                                                 \tag{WP21}
\]
Each positive-height reflected pair accounts for $2m$ in $N$;
the same bound at negative heights therefore gives a total
possible negative contribution of at most
\[
 \begin{split}
 64\sum_{\Im\rho>T_0}\frac{m_\rho}{(\Im\rho)^4}
 &\le\frac{256}{T_0^3}
                    \left(\frac{\log T_0}{3}+\frac19\right)
 <10^{-33}<\frac{b_*}{2}.
 \end{split}                                                   \tag{WP22}
\]

comparison follows already from $\log T_0<30$ and
$200^4 30^{12}<10^{27}$. Absolute convergence of WP3 also follows

nonnegatively. Hence
\[
                      \boxed{T_3(2)\succeq\tfrac12 b_* I.}    \tag{WP23}
\]
This directly bounds all four rational coefficients, including
their arbitrary relative phases.

\section{Unchanged arithmetic measure, mass, and physical source}

Retain the original complete finite divisor
\[
 h(s)=\prod_{\rho\in Z}(s-\rho)^{m_\rho},\qquad
 g(s)=2\xi(s)=s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s).
\]
Here $Z$ is the original nonempty actual set of nontrivial zeros,
closed under conjugation and $s\mapsto1-s$, and every $m_\rho$
is its complete order. This stipulates the original actual packet;
it does not assert the existence of an off-line packet. For its
unchanged tensor degree $k\geq1$ put
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:measure}
 \begin{gathered}
 w_h(u)=\frac{|(g/h)(1/2+iu)|^2}{2\pi},\qquad
 m(u)=m_{h,k}(u)=w_h^{*k}(u),\\
 \mu_h=\int_\swlieR w_h(u)\,du,\qquad
 M=\int_\swlieR m(u)\,du=\mu_h^k,\qquad s_0=k/2,\qquad S=s_0+iu.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The Hilbert space in this note is the actual
$\swlieHH_m=L^2(\swlieR,m(u)\,du)$, with
$\langle f,g\rangle_m=\int\overline f g\,m\,du$.
No probability density is substituted for $m$.

The density is even and positive almost everywhere. Indeed
$g(\bar s)=\overline{g(s)}$, and the same is true of $h$,
so $w_h$ is even. The zeros of the nonzero entire function
$g/h$ are discrete, hence $w_h>0$ almost everywhere.
Convolution preserves evenness and positivity almost everywhere,
and Tonelli gives the exact mass in \eqref{swlie:eq:measure}.
For every $0<t<\pi/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:expmoment}
 \int_\swlieR e^{t|u|}m(u)\,du<\infty.
\end{equation}
Here is a direct sufficient bound. On $\Re s=1/2$ the integral
representation of the alternating series gives
$|\zeta(s)|\leq2|s|/(\sqrt2-1)$: use
$\eta(s)=s\int_1^\infty A(x)x^{-s-1}\,dx$, $|A(x)|\leq1$,
and $\eta(s)=(1-2^{1-s})\zeta(s)$.
The vertical-line Gamma estimate gives a polynomial times
$e^{-\pi|u|/4}$ for $g(1/2+iu)$. Outside a fixed compact interval
$|h(1/2+iu)|$ is bounded below by a positive constant times
$(1+|u|)^{\deg h}$. On that compact interval the quotient
$g/h$ has its removable values and is bounded. Squaring proves
exponential integrability of $w_h$ for $t<\pi/2$.
Since $e^{t|\sum u_j|}\leq\prod_j e^{t|u_j|}$, Tonelli proves
\eqref{swlie:eq:expmoment} for its actual convolution.

The original physical source has $D=-x\partial_x$,
$\Theta\phi(x)=2\sum_{n\geq1}\phi(nx)$, and
$\phi_*(x)=(4\pi^2x^4-6\pi x^2)e^{-\pi x^2}$.
Let $F_h\in\swlieB$ be its original divided function,
$\swlieM F_h=g/h$, where $\swlieM F(s)=\int_0^\infty F(x)x^{s-1}\,dx$
and $\swlieB$ has all seminorms
$\sup_{x>0}x^b|D^jF(x)|$, $b\in\mathbb Z$, $j\geq0$.
For completeness, the Fourier transform with kernel
$e^{-2\pi ixy}$ fixes $\phi_*$: differentiating the Gaussian
Fourier identity twice and four times gives
$\widehat{\phi_*}=\phi_*$. Also $\phi_*(0)=\int_\swlieR\phi_*=0$.
Poisson summation therefore gives
$\Theta\phi_*(x)=x^{-1}\Theta\phi_*(1/x)$.
The Gaussian bound at infinity and this identity at zero,
after every $D$ derivative, prove $\Theta\phi_*\in\swlieB$.
The elementary Gaussian Mellin integral and the Gamma
recurrence give
$\swlieM(2\phi_*)(s)=s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)$.
Termwise integration on $\Re s>1$ and analytic continuation
then give $\swlieM(\Theta\phi_*)=g$ with every constant retained.
This construction can be made explicitly: starting from
$\Theta\phi_*$, divide once at each root occurrence using
$S_\rho F(x)=x^{-\rho}\int_x^\infty F(y)y^{\rho-1}\,dy$.
The vanishing Mellin moment rewrites this as the negative
integral from zero to $x$, proving all rapid endpoint bounds;
$(D-\rho)S_\rho F=F$ and integration by parts give the Mellin
division. Thus the complete multiplicity produces this
unique $F_h$, with no change of physical coordinate.

For $P\in\swlieC[S]$ define the unchanged tensor source
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:physical}
 \mathcal A P=P(D_1+\cdots+D_k)F_h^{\otimes k}.
\end{equation}
Its multiple Mellin transform is
$P(s_1+\cdots+s_k)\prod_j(g/h)(s_j)$.
Mellin Parseval on each original $dx_j$ axis, followed by
Tonelli and convolution, therefore proves
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:isometry}
 \swlienorm{\mathcal A P}_{L^2(dx_1\cdots dx_k)}^2
  =\int_\swlieR|P(s_0+iu)|^2m(u)\,du.
\end{equation}
All polynomial pairings follow by polarization.
In particular the constant-source squared norm is $M$, not one.
The finite polynomial maps also retain the original strong
source topology, independently of this Hilbert identity.
On the original $k$-variable rapid space write
\[
 p_{\boldsymbol b,\boldsymbol d}(F)
 =\sup_{\boldsymbol x>0}
       \left|\prod_{\ell=1}^k x_\ell^{b_\ell}
                    D_\ell^{d_\ell}F(\boldsymbol x)\right|.
\]
For $P(S)=\sum_{j=0}^L p_jS^j$ the multinomial theorem gives
the explicit finite strong bound
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:strongfinite}
 p_{\boldsymbol b,\boldsymbol d}(\mathcal A P)
 \leq\sum_{j=0}^L|p_j|
       \sum_{\substack{\boldsymbol\alpha\geq0\\|\boldsymbol\alpha|=j}}
       \frac{j!}{\prod_\ell\alpha_\ell!}
       \prod_{\ell=1}^k
                  p_{b_\ell,d_\ell+\alpha_\ell}(F_h).
\end{equation}
Thus every finite coefficient map used below is a continuous
map into the unchanged strong physical source.
The Hilbert completion constructed below instead has
codomain the physical Hilbert space; it is not asserted
to take every Hilbert vector into the rapid source.

\section{Native orthogonal polynomials and exact coefficient maps}

Let $Q_n(u)$ be the unique real monic orthogonal polynomials for
$m(u)\,du$, and retain their full norms
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:norms}
 \omega_n=\int_\swlieR Q_n(u)^2m(u)\,du>0,\qquad
 \omega_0=M,\qquad
 \phi_n=Q_n/\sqrt{\omega_n},\qquad
 a_n=\sqrt{\omega_{n+1}/\omega_n},\quad a_{-1}=0.
\end{equation}
Positive density makes every finite moment Gram positive
definite, proving existence and uniqueness by Gram--Schmidt.
Evenness and uniqueness give $Q_n(-u)=(-1)^nQ_n(u)$.
The exact recurrence is
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:recurrence}
 uQ_n=Q_{n+1}+\frac{\omega_n}{\omega_{n-1}}Q_{n-1}
 \quad(n\geq1),\qquad uQ_0=Q_1;
 \qquad
 u\phi_n=a_{n-1}\phi_{n-1}+a_n\phi_{n+1}.
\end{equation}
To verify the recurrence, pair $uQ_n$ with $Q_j$ for $j<n-1$
and move $u$ to $Q_j$, whose degree is at most $n-1$.
The diagonal pairing vanishes by parity. The leading
coefficient fixes $Q_{n+1}$, and pairing with $Q_{n-1}$
gives $\omega_n/\omega_{n-1}$.

The $\phi_n$ are complete in $\swlieHH_m$. If $f$ is orthogonal
to every polynomial, Cauchy--Schwarz and \eqref{swlie:eq:expmoment}
make $\int\overline f(u)e^{zu}m(u)\,du$ holomorphic on a
strip containing the imaginary axis. Every derivative at
zero vanishes, so the function vanishes on that strip.
Fourier uniqueness for the finite measure $\overline f m\,du$
gives $f=0$. Hence the map
\[
 U:\ell^2(\mathbb N_0)\longrightarrow\swlieHH_m,\qquad Ue_n=\phi_n
\]
is unitary. The multiplication operator by $u$ on its natural
domain corresponds to the Jacobi operator $J$,
$Je_n=a_{n-1}e_{n-1}+a_ne_{n+1}$ on the finite-support core.
Indeed the same exponential-moment density argument applied
to $(1+u^2)m(u)\,du$ proves density of polynomials in the
graph norm of multiplication by $u$, so this is a core.
The number operator $\mathsf N e_n=ne_n$ is self-adjoint
on $\{\sum x_ne_n:\sum n^2|x_n|^2<\infty\}$.
All products and brackets below are asserted on the
invariant polynomial/finite-support core; no self-adjoint
extension of a prolate expression is selected by them.

The original monic $S$-polynomials, including their phases, are
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:physical-polys}
 p_n(S)=i^nQ_n((S-s_0)/i),\qquad
 p_n(s_0+iu)=i^nQ_n(u),\qquad \swlienorm{p_n}_m^2=\omega_n.
\end{equation}
The constant vector $1$ is $\sqrt M\,\phi_0$.
The corresponding unit cyclic vector, when the convention of
a unit cyclic pair is wanted, is explicitly $1/\sqrt M$
in this same measure; the physical vector $1$ and its mass
are never replaced.

Here are full finite coordinate maps. Let $B_L$ have as its
$n$th column the $u$-power coefficients of $\phi_n$, $0\leq n\leq L$.
Let
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:coordinates}
 (T_L)_{rj}=\binom jr s_0^{j-r}i^r\quad(r\leq j),\qquad
 (T_L)_{rj}=0\quad(r>j),\qquad
 D_L=B_L^{-1}T_L.
\end{equation}
Since the diagonal of $B_L$ is $1/\sqrt{\omega_n}$,
it is invertible, and
$P(s_0+iu)=\sum_{n=0}^L(D_L\operatorname{coeff}P)_n\phi_n(u)$.
Thus the complete original source Gram in increasing $S$ powers is
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:sourcegram}
 H_{S,L}=D_L^*D_L.
\end{equation}
The native grading in those exact coordinates is
$D_L^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(0,\ldots,L)D_L$.
The rectangular map of multiplication by $S$ from degree
$\leq L$ to degree $\leq L+1$ is the corresponding rectangular
matrix $s_0I+iJ$, with the outgoing $a_L$ edge included.
These equalities prove all physical phases and mass factors,
not just equivalence of the two polynomial spans.

Equation \eqref{swlie:eq:isometry} extends the map
$f(u)\mapsto\mathcal A(f((S-s_0)/i))$ from polynomials to
a unitary map from $\swlieHH_m$ onto the closed cyclic Hilbert
subspace generated by the original tensor vector.
It does not identify that subspace with the whole tensor
Hilbert space. On its polynomial domain it transports
$-iJ$ to $s_0-(D_1+\cdots+D_k)$ exactly.

\section{The prolate polynomial action and the phase dictionary}

Connes--Consani--Moscovici \cite{swlie:CCM}, Definition
\texttt{prolop}, attach the formal expression $-J^2+\eta^2\mathsf N$
to an even cyclic pair. Their actual archimedean prolate
formula in Theorem \texttt{mainstart}(v) is instead
$-J^2+2\pi\lambda^2(4\mathsf N+1)-1/4$.
Retaining all those constants, define for real $\lambda$
its native arithmetic receiver
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:W}
 W_\lambda=-J^2+8\pi\lambda^2(\mathsf N+1/4)-1/4
 \quad\text{on the finite-support core}.
\end{equation}
This is a densely defined symmetric operator.
It is a polynomial-domain receiver, not an assertion about
a self-adjoint prolate extension or its spectrum.
Its entries are, with missing negative indices zero,
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:Wentries}
 \begin{split}
 W_\lambda e_n={}&-a_{n-1}a_{n-2}e_{n-2}
  +w_ne_n-a_na_{n+1}e_{n+2},\\
 w_n={}&-a_n^2-a_{n-1}^2+8\pi\lambda^2(n+1/4)-1/4.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
These follow by applying \eqref{swlie:eq:recurrence} twice.
The parity involution $\Gamma e_n=(-1)^ne_n$ commutes with
$W_\lambda$ and $\mathsf N$ and anticommutes with $J$.
Consequently on the parity-$\varepsilon$ sector
($n=2r+\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon=0,1$), the adjacent off-diagonal
is $-a_{2r+\varepsilon}a_{2r+\varepsilon+1}$ and the
diagonal is $w_{2r+\varepsilon}$.

Let $\mathcal N$ be the polynomial grading
$\mathcal Np_n=np_n$ in the original $S$ coordinate.
The physical polynomial-domain action of \eqref{swlie:eq:W} is
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:Wphysical}
 (\mathcal W_\lambda P)(S)
 =(S-s_0)^2P(S)+8\pi\lambda^2(\mathcal N+1/4)P(S)-P(S)/4.
\end{equation}
It maps degree $\leq L$ into degree $\leq L+2$.
Its actual source vector is $\mathcal A\mathcal W_\lambda P$.
In the orthonormal physical frame $p_n/\sqrt{\omega_n}$,
the two off-diagonals in \eqref{swlie:eq:Wentries} have the opposite
sign, namely $+a_na_{n+1}$: the exact diagonal change of
frame is $e_n\mapsto i^ne_n$, and $i^{n}/i^{n+2}=-1$.
This explains the positive off-diagonals in the archimedean
phase convention of \cite{swlie:CCM}; no phase is discarded.

\section{All Lie brackets, with the literal generator factors}

Fix a real scalar $c$, distinct from the physical centre $s_0$,
and keep precisely
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:generators}
 H=-iJ,\qquad K=\mathsf N+c,\qquad
 B=\frac{[J,\mathsf N]}{2i},\qquad
 E_+=\frac i2(K+B),\qquad E_-=\frac i2(-K+B).
\end{equation}
Define the actual diagonal discrepancy
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:delta}
 \mathcal D e_n=(a_n^2-a_{n-1}^2)e_n,\qquad
 \Delta_c=\mathcal D-2K,\qquad
 \delta_n^{(c)}=a_n^2-a_{n-1}^2-2n-2c.
\end{equation}

\begin{swlietheorem}[Complete bracket calculation]\label{swlie:thm:brackets}
For every positive native recurrence sequence,
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:basicbrackets}
 [H,K]=2B,\qquad [H,B]=2K+\Delta_c,\qquad [K,B]=-H/2.
\end{equation}
Consequently the literal three generators satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:literalbrackets}
 \boxed{
 [H,E_+]=2E_++\tfrac i2\Delta_c,\quad
 [H,E_-]=-2E_-+\tfrac i2\Delta_c,\quad
 [E_+,E_-]=H/4.}
\end{equation}
The remaining mixed brackets are
$[K,E_\pm]=-iH/4$, $[B,E_+]=iH/4$,
$[B,E_-]=-iH/4$, with reverse brackets their negatives.
In particular the literal standard-$\mathfrak{sl}_2$
third-bracket defect is $-3H/4$, even if $\Delta_c=0$.

The first two weight relations hold without defect if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:allc}
 a_n^2=(n+1)(n+2c)\quad(n\geq0).
\end{equation}
Positivity is equivalent to $c>0$ within this family.
For every such $c$, the explicitly different generators
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:morphism}
 F_+=2E_+,\qquad F_-=2E_-,\qquad K_{\rm std}=2K
\end{equation}
satisfy
$[H,F_\pm]=\pm2F_\pm$, $[F_+,F_-]=H$,
$i(F_--F_+)=K_{\rm std}$.
These are an exact change of Lie-algebra basis, not an
unannounced change in \eqref{swlie:eq:generators}.
\end{swlietheorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $Re_n=a_ne_{n+1}$ and $Le_n=a_{n-1}e_{n-1}$.
Then $J=L+R$, $[J,\mathsf N]=L-R$,
$B=(L-R)/(2i)$, and $[L,R]=\mathcal D$.
Also $[\mathsf N,L]=-L$ and $[\mathsf N,R]=R$.
Direct substitution gives $[J,K]=2iB$,
$[J,B]=i\mathcal D$, $[K,B]=iJ/2$, proving
\eqref{swlie:eq:basicbrackets}. For example
$[H,E_+]=(i/2)(2B+2K+\Delta_c)
 =2E_++i\Delta_c/2$; the negative case is the same
substitution with the sign on $K$ reversed.
Finally
\[
 [E_+,E_-]=-\tfrac14[K+B,-K+B]
          =-\tfrac12[K,B]=H/4.
\]
This verifies the third bracket independently of any
recurrence hypothesis. The mixed brackets follow by the
same two-term expansions.

The vanishing of the two weight defects is $\Delta_c=0$.
Sum $a_n^2-a_{n-1}^2=2(n+c)$ from $0$ to $n$, with
$a_{-1}=0$, to obtain \eqref{swlie:eq:allc}. Conversely taking
the difference of its two consecutive values gives
the required diagonal equality. Its first value is
$a_0^2=2c$, and all values are positive exactly for $c>0$.
Multiplying both $E$'s by two proves every identity
in \eqref{swlie:eq:morphism}.
\end{proof}

This computation supplies a precise correction at the scope
of \cite{swlie:CCM}. It applies to equations \texttt{h} and
\texttt{eplusminusricu}, and to Theorem \texttt{dzero}(i).
The asserted uniqueness of $c=1/4$ does not follow from the
written relations. With the literal generators there is
also the third-bracket factor in \eqref{swlie:eq:literalbrackets}.
The archimedean even representation has an additional
specified compact spectrum $2n+1/2=2(n+1/4)$, which fixes
$c=1/4$ after the exact map \eqref{swlie:eq:morphism}.
That extra representation datum is not a theorem about
the native arithmetic recurrence.

\begin{swlieproposition}[Quadratic operator and exact Casimir receiver]
\label{swlie:prop:casimir}
The standard-basis quadratic expression, retaining the
literal $E$'s, is
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:Cas}
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal Q_c
 &=H^2+8(E_+E_-+E_-E_+)
   =4K^2-J^2-4B^2,\\
 \mathcal Q_ce_n
 &=\bigl(4(n+c)^2-2(a_n^2+a_{n-1}^2)\bigr)e_n.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
For arbitrary coefficients its commutator defects are
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:Casdef}
 [\mathcal Q_c,H]=4\swlieac B{\Delta_c},\quad
 [\mathcal Q_c,K]=0,\quad
 [\mathcal Q_c,B]=\swlieac H{\Delta_c},\quad
 [\mathcal Q_c,E_\pm]=\tfrac i2\swlieac H{\Delta_c}.
\end{equation}
Here $\swlieac AB=AB+BA$. Under \eqref{swlie:eq:allc},
$\mathcal Q_c=4c(c-1)I$, which is $-3I/4$ at $c=1/4$.
The unmodified expression
$C=H^2+2(E_+E_-+E_-E_+)$ instead obeys
$C=(\mathcal Q_c+3H^2)/4$; even under
\eqref{swlie:eq:allc} it is not central.
\end{swlieproposition}
\begin{proof}
Expand $E_+E_-+E_-E_+=(K^2-B^2)/2$.
Then $J^2+4B^2=2(LR+RL)$, which proves the diagonal
formula. Expanding commutators of squares using
\eqref{swlie:eq:basicbrackets} gives, for example,
\[
 [\mathcal Q_c,H]
  =-8\swlieac KB+4\swlieac B{2K+\Delta_c}
  =4\swlieac B{\Delta_c}.
\]
The $K$ terms cancel; the $B$ calculation is
$\swlieac H{2K+\Delta_c}-2\swlieac KH=\swlieac H{\Delta_c}$.
The $E$ equations follow by linearity.
For \eqref{swlie:eq:allc},
$a_n^2+a_{n-1}^2=2n^2+4cn+2c$,
so the diagonal is $4c(c-1)$.
The equation for $C$ follows by direct subtraction.
Its noncentrality is explicit:
$[C,K]=(3/2)\swlieac HB=(3/2)(R^2-L^2)$,
whose value on $e_0$ is $(3/2)a_0a_1e_2\ne0$.
\end{proof}

\section{All masses in the zero-discrepancy family}

The free parameter in \eqref{swlie:eq:allc} corresponds to an exact
family of measures, not merely a formal sequence. For $c>0$ let
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:gamfamily}
 dm_{c,M}(u)=
 \frac{M\,2^{2c}}{4\pi\Gamma(2c)}
       |\Gamma(c+iu/2)|^2\,du .
\end{equation}
Its mass is $M$ and its moment-generating function is
$M(\cos t)^{-2c}$ for $|t|<\pi/2$.
For a verification with the factors retained, the beta integral
after $x=e^y$ is
\[
 \frac{\Gamma(c+iv)\Gamma(c-iv)}{\Gamma(2c)}
  =\int_\swlieR (2\cosh(y/2))^{-2c}e^{ivy}\,dy .
\]
Fourier inversion, then $u=2v$, gives
$\int|\Gamma(c+iu/2)|^2e^{iux}\,du
 =4\pi\Gamma(2c)2^{-2c}(\cosh x)^{-2c}$.
The Gamma exponential bound permits analytic continuation
to $x=-it$, proving the asserted mass and transform.

Define monic polynomials by
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:genfamily}
 \sum_{n\geq0}Q_n^{(c)}(u)\frac{z^n}{n!}
       =(1+z^2)^{-c}e^{u\arctan z}.
\end{equation}
Differentiating in $z$ proves the recurrence
$uQ_n^{(c)}=Q_{n+1}^{(c)}+n(n-1+2c)Q_{n-1}^{(c)}$.
For sufficiently small real $z,w$, multiplying the two
generating functions and integrating against
\eqref{swlie:eq:gamfamily} gives
\[
 M(1+z^2)^{-c}(1+w^2)^{-c}
       \bigl(\cos(\arctan z+\arctan w)\bigr)^{-2c}
       =M(1-zw)^{-2c}.
\]
Exponential domination justifies coefficient extraction,
so the full norms are
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:gamnorms}
 \int Q_n^{(c)}Q_j^{(c)}\,dm_{c,M}
       =\delta_{nj}M\,n!(2c)_n .
\end{equation}
Thus \eqref{swlie:eq:allc} is realized for every $c>0$ with the
same prescribed mass $M$.

For the native arithmetic measure the following classification
is exact for $c>0$:
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:exacttest}
 \begin{gathered}
 \Delta_c=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m(u)\,du=dm_{c,M}(u)\\
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \left(\int_\swlieR e^{tu}w_h(u)\,du\right)^k
          =\mu_h^k(\cos t)^{-2c}\quad(|t|<\pi/2).
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Indeed equal recurrences and the same $\omega_0=M$ give
identical moments:
$\int u^r\,dm=M\langle e_0,J^re_0\rangle$ is a finite
weighted-path calculation determined by the coefficients.
Both measures have exponential moments, so equality of
all moments gives equality of their analytic Laplace
transforms and then their Fourier transforms and measures.
The reverse implication follows from \eqref{swlie:eq:gamnorms};
the convolution Laplace identity proves the last equivalence.

\begin{swlietheorem}[The native arithmetic Lie defect never vanishes]
\label{swlie:thm:nonzero}
For every actual $h$ and $k$ in \eqref{swlie:eq:measure} and every
real $c$, the operator $\Delta_c$ is nonzero. In particular,
there is at least one finite index $n$ with
$a_n^2-a_{n-1}^2-2n-2c\ne0$.
\end{swlietheorem}
\begin{proof}
For $c\leq0$, its first entry is
$\delta_0^{(c)}=a_0^2-2c>0$.
Suppose $c>0$ and $\Delta_c=0$.
For real $|t|<\pi/2$ the native Laplace transform is positive.
Taking its unique positive $k$th root in
\eqref{swlie:eq:exacttest} gives
\[
 \int_\swlieR e^{tu}w_h(u)\,du
       =\mu_h(\cos t)^{-2c/k}.
\]
Analytic Laplace uniqueness as proved above, with
$d=c/k>0$, forces the exact equality of densities
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:impossiblegamma}
 w_h(u)=\frac{\mu_h\,2^{2d}}{4\pi\Gamma(2d)}
                \Gamma(d+iu/2)\Gamma(d-iu/2).
\end{equation}
The equality is first almost everywhere and then everywhere
on the real axis, since both densities are continuous.
But $F=g/h$ is entire, and
\[
 W_h(z)=\frac{F(1/2+iz)F(1/2-iz)}{2\pi}
\]
is an entire function agreeing with $w_h$ on the real axis.
The right side of \eqref{swlie:eq:impossiblegamma} is holomorphic
on $|\Im z|<2d$. The identity theorem makes it equal to
$W_h$ throughout that strip. At $z_0=2id$ its meromorphic
continuation has the nonzero residue
\[
 \frac{\mu_h2^{2d}}{4\pi\Gamma(2d)}
       \frac{2}{i}\Gamma(2d)
       =-\frac{i\mu_h2^{2d-1}}{\pi}.
\]
Approaching $z_0$ from inside the strip makes the right
side unbounded, whereas the entire $W_h$ is bounded near
$z_0$. This contradiction proves the theorem.
\end{proof}

Thus the receiving formulas describe an actual nonzero
arithmetic defect, not an unverified defect-free
representation. The theorem does not identify its first
nonzero index; every entry is exactly evaluated by the
original moment determinants below. Nonvanishing alone
gives no growing-degree lower bound and no uniform bound
as $h$, $k$, or the cutoff varies.
In particular, at $c=1/4$ the original arithmetic discrepancy
is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:archdefect}
 \boxed{\quad
 \Delta_{1/4}e_n=
       \left(\frac{\omega_{n+1}}{\omega_n}
        -\frac{\omega_n}{\omega_{n-1}}-2n-\frac12\right)e_n,
 \quad}
\end{equation}
where the second ratio is zero for $n=0$.
Its first entry retains the actual convolution variance:
$\delta_0^{(1/4)}
 =k\bigl(\int u^2w_h(u)\,du/\mu_h\bigr)-1/2$.
No Gamma replacement of that integral is made.

\section{Original Hankel determinants and Toda differences}

Let the unscaled moments and their exponential deformation be
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:tau}
 \mu_j(t)=\int_\swlieR u^je^{tu}m(u)\,du,\qquad
 \tau_n(t)=\det(\mu_{r+s}(t))_{0\leq r,s<n},\quad \tau_0(t)=1 .
\end{equation}
These are well-defined and analytic for real $|t|<\pi/2$
and holomorphic locally there. For real $t$ the Grams are
positive definite. At zero $\tau_1(0)=M$, with no mass
cancellation. These are also exactly the original
$S$-coordinate Gram determinants, not replacement volumes:
if
\[
 H_{S,n-1}(t)_{rj}
 =\int_\swlieR\overline{(s_0+iu)^r}(s_0+iu)^j e^{tu}m(u)\,du
 \quad(0\leq r,j<n),
\]
then \eqref{swlie:eq:coordinates} gives
$H_{S,n-1}(t)=T_{n-1}^*(\mu_{r+j}(t))T_{n-1}$.
Its triangular coordinate determinant is
$\det T_{n-1}=i^{n(n-1)/2}$, so
$\det H_{S,n-1}(t)=\tau_n(t)$ exactly.
Gram--Schmidt using monic polynomials gives
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:hankelratios}
 \omega_n=\frac{\tau_{n+1}(0)}{\tau_n(0)},\qquad
 a_n^2=\frac{\tau_{n+2}(0)\tau_n(0)}{\tau_{n+1}(0)^2}.
\end{equation}
The determinant of the monic triangular change of basis is
one, so the Gram determinant is precisely
$\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\omega_j$; this proves both formulas.
Therefore every entry of the native Lie defect is the
explicit original Gram expression
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:hankeldefect}
 \delta_n^{(c)}
  =\frac{\tau_{n+2}\tau_n}{\tau_{n+1}^2}
    -\frac{\tau_{n+1}\tau_{n-1}}{\tau_n^2}-2n-2c,
 \qquad (n\geq1),
 \quad \delta_0^{(c)}=\frac{\tau_2}{\tau_1^2}-2c,
\end{equation}
where unlabelled $\tau$'s in this display are evaluated at zero.

\begin{swlietheorem}[Exact Toda receiver]\label{swlie:thm:toda}
For $n\geq1$ and real $|t|<\pi/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:toda}
 \tau_n\tau_n''-(\tau_n')^2=\tau_{n+1}\tau_{n-1},
 \qquad \partial_t^2\log\tau_n
                    =\frac{\tau_{n+1}\tau_{n-1}}{\tau_n^2}.
\end{equation}
Let $\omega_n(t)=\tau_{n+1}(t)/\tau_n(t)$,
$\beta_n(t)=\partial_t\log\omega_n(t)$ and
$a_n(t)^2=\omega_{n+1}(t)/\omega_n(t)$, with $a_{-1}(t)=0$.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:todarecurrence}
 \begin{gathered}
 \beta_n'(t)=a_n(t)^2-a_{n-1}(t)^2,\qquad
 \partial_t\log a_n(t)^2=\beta_{n+1}(t)-\beta_n(t),\\
 \beta_n(0)=0,\qquad
 \delta_n^{(c)}
   =\left.\partial_t^2\log\frac{\tau_{n+1}(t)}{\tau_n(t)}
                                               \right|_{t=0}-2n-2c .
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
\end{swlietheorem}
\begin{proof}
Differentiation under the integral gives
$\mu_j'(t)=\mu_{j+1}(t)$. Differentiate $\tau_n$ by rows:
every differentiated row except the last repeats the next
row, so $\tau_n'$ is the minor with row indices
$0,\ldots,n-2,n$ and column indices $0,\ldots,n-1$.
Differentiate that expression by columns to obtain
$\tau_n''$ with both row and column indices
$0,\ldots,n-2,n$. Apply the Desnanot--Jacobi identity
to the $(n+1)$-square Hankel matrix with indices $0,\ldots,n$,
deleting the last two indices. It gives exactly
$\tau_n\tau_n''-(\tau_n')^2=\tau_{n+1}\tau_{n-1}$.
This identity itself follows by two-row/two-column
Schur elimination on an invertible upper-left block;
that block is positive definite here. Dividing by
$\tau_n^2$ proves the logarithmic formula.
Taking the difference at $n+1$ and $n$ proves the first
identity in \eqref{swlie:eq:todarecurrence}; differentiating
the defining norm ratio proves the second.
Evenness makes $\mu_j(-t)=(-1)^j\mu_j(t)$ and thus
$\tau_n(-t)=\tau_n(t)$, since the row and column signs
multiply to $(-1)^{n(n-1)}=1$. Hence $\beta_n(0)=0$.
The last identity is \eqref{swlie:eq:delta} with the first
Toda identity substituted.
\end{proof}

The first two discrepancies can therefore be evaluated
without an orthogonalization ambiguity:
\[
 \delta_0^{(c)}=\frac{\mu_2}{\mu_0}-2c,\qquad
 \delta_1^{(c)}=\frac{\mu_4}{\mu_2}
              -2\frac{\mu_2}{\mu_0}-2-2c,
\]
with moments at zero; here
$\tau_2=\mu_0\mu_2$ and
$\tau_3=\mu_2(\mu_0\mu_4-\mu_2^2)$.
The full sequence is retained in \eqref{swlie:eq:hankeldefect},
not replaced by these initial entries.

\section{Every finite cutoff, including excursions beyond the edge}

Let $\Pi_L$ project to $e_0,\ldots,e_L$, write
$P_L^\partial=e_Le_L^*$, and put $\alpha_L=a_L^2$.
Compressed single generators carry a subscript $L$.
Because $\mathsf N$ preserves the cutoff,
$B_L=[J_L,\mathsf N_L]/(2i)$ exactly.

\begin{swlietheorem}[Complete finite edge corrections]\label{swlie:thm:cutoff}
The finite diagonal discrepancy is
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:cutdelta}
 \Delta_{c,L}=\Pi_L\Delta_c\Pi_L-\alpha_LP_L^\partial.
\end{equation}
All identities \eqref{swlie:eq:basicbrackets} and
\eqref{swlie:eq:literalbrackets} hold with the finite matrices and
this $\Delta_{c,L}$. In particular
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:cutbracket}
 [H_L,E_{\pm,L}]
 =\pm2E_{\pm,L}+\tfrac i2\Pi_L\Delta_c\Pi_L
                         -\tfrac i2\alpha_LP_L^\partial,
 \qquad [E_{+,L},E_{-,L}]=H_L/4.
\end{equation}
The exact compressed prolate action is
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:cutprolate}
 \boxed{\quad
 W_{\lambda,L}:=\Pi_LW_\lambda\Pi_L
 =-J_L^2-\alpha_LP_L^\partial
        +8\pi\lambda^2(\mathsf N_L+1/4)-1/4.\quad}
\end{equation}
The quadratic expression built from the finite generators
instead satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:cutcas}
 \mathcal Q_{c,L}=\Pi_L\mathcal Q_c\Pi_L
                                   +2\alpha_LP_L^\partial.
\end{equation}
Even when the infinite discrepancy vanishes,
the finite endpoint terms in these equations remain.
\end{swlietheorem}
\begin{proof}
The truncated raising map sends $e_L$ to zero.
Therefore $[L_L,R_L]=\Pi_L[L,R]\Pi_L-\alpha_LP_L^\partial$,
which proves \eqref{swlie:eq:cutdelta}. The purely finite
calculations in Theorem~\ref{swlie:thm:brackets} then give
\eqref{swlie:eq:cutbracket}. The only path of length two
leaving and returning to the cutoff is
$e_L\to e_{L+1}\to e_L$, with weight $a_L^2$.
Thus
\[
 \Pi_LJ^2\Pi_L=J_L^2+\alpha_LP_L^\partial,\qquad
 \Pi_LB^2\Pi_L=B_L^2+\tfrac14\alpha_LP_L^\partial.
\]
Insert these exact identities in \eqref{swlie:eq:W} and
\eqref{swlie:eq:Cas} to obtain the two formulas and their signs.
\end{proof}

Every polynomial has an equally explicit correction.
For $0\leq m,n\leq L$ and integer $r\geq0$,
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:paths}
 (\Pi_LJ^r\Pi_L-J_L^r)_{mn}
 =\sum_{\substack{v_0=n,\ v_r=m,\ v_j\geq0\\
                  |v_{j+1}-v_j|=1,\ \max_jv_j>L}}
              \prod_{j=0}^{r-1}a_{\min(v_j,v_{j+1})}.
\end{equation}
Matrix multiplication expands into exactly these paths;
the finite power retains precisely those staying at most $L$.
The sum is finite for fixed $r$. For
$P(s_0+iu)=\sum_r\gamma_ru^r$, the correction to
$\Pi_LP(s_0+iJ)\Pi_L$ from $P(s_0+iJ_L)$ is
the same sum multiplied by every exact $\gamma_r$,
where $\gamma=T_d\operatorname{coeff}P$ and $d=\deg P$
(with the zero polynomial handled by zero coefficients).
Thus no polynomial is silently evaluated on a finite
Jacobi matrix in place of compression of the full one.

There is also a useful trace check retaining the whole edge:
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:trace}
 \sum_{n=0}^L\delta_n^{(c)}
  =a_L^2-(L+1)(L+2c),\qquad
 \operatorname{tr}\Delta_{c,L}=-(L+1)(L+2c).
\end{equation}
Both follow by telescoping the native recurrence differences.
For $c>0$, the latter is nonzero, so no finite positive
Jacobi cutoff can satisfy all defect-free weight identities.

\section{The actual finite polynomial quotient and its relation defect}

Let $\chi(S)$ be the original full cyclic annihilator of
the sum operator on $E_h^{\otimes k}$, where
$E_h=\swlieC[s]/h$. More explicitly its distinct roots are
$\kappa=\rho_1+\cdots+\rho_k$, with multiplicity
$e_\kappa=\max_{\sum\rho_j=\kappa}(1+\sum_j(m_{\rho_j}-1))$.
This formula follows in the local Chinese-remainder block
$\swlieC[z_1,\ldots,z_k]/(z_1^{m_{\rho_1}},\ldots,z_k^{m_{\rho_k}})$:
the sum operator is $\kappa+\sum_jz_j$,
whose nilpotent part has index exactly
$1+\sum_j(m_{\rho_j}-1)$. Its last nonzero power has
coefficient
$(\sum_j(m_{\rho_j}-1))!/\prod_j(m_{\rho_j}-1)!$
on $\prod_jz_j^{m_{\rho_j}-1}$, which is nonzero.
Taking the maximum over blocks with the same $\kappa$
gives the displayed exponent, with no multiplicity omitted.
Put $q=\deg\chi$, $C_\chi=\swlieC[S]/\chi$, retaining its
original increasing-power remainder coordinates.
The substitution map
$\beta:C_\chi\to E_h^{\otimes k}$,
$[P]\mapsto P(s_1+\cdots+s_k)$, is injective by this
minimal polynomial. The original full physical jet map is
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:unitreceiver}
 \eta=\bigl(j_h(g/h)\bigr)^{\otimes k}\beta.
\end{equation}
The multiplier is the complete original local unit in
each factor, including every Taylor coefficient and
root difference. Explicitly, write $H_\rho(s)=h(s)/(s-\rho)^{m_\rho}$
and $u_{\rho,r}=[z^r](g/h)(\rho+z)$ for $0\leq r<m_\rho$.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:unitcoefficients}
 \begin{split}
 u_{\rho,r}
 =\sum_{a=0}^r\frac{g^{(m_\rho+a)}(\rho)}{(m_\rho+a)!}
 \sum_{\substack{\ell_\sigma\geq0\ (\sigma\ne\rho)\\
                 \sum_{\sigma\ne\rho}\ell_\sigma=r-a}}
 \prod_{\sigma\ne\rho}
 \frac{(-1)^{\ell_\sigma}
       \binom{m_\sigma+\ell_\sigma-1}{\ell_\sigma}}
      {(\rho-\sigma)^{m_\sigma+\ell_\sigma}} .
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
This is the product of the Taylor expansion of
$g(\rho+z)/z^{m_\rho}$ with the negative-binomial
expansions of $1/H_\rho(\rho+z)$.
The empty inner product is one when its required sum is
zero and contributes zero otherwise.
In particular
$u_{\rho,0}=g^{(m_\rho)}(\rho)/
 (m_\rho!\prod_{\sigma\ne\rho}(\rho-\sigma)^{m_\sigma})\ne0$,
so multiplication by these complete jets is invertible.
For every original multi-index $0\leq j_a<m_{\rho_a}$ the
full physical map has the explicit entry
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:etajets}
 (\eta[P])_{\boldsymbol\rho,\boldsymbol j}
 =\sum_{0\leq\nu_a\leq j_a}
     \left(\prod_{a=1}^k u_{\rho_a,j_a-\nu_a}\right)
          \frac{P^{(|\boldsymbol\nu|)}(\kappa)}
               {\prod_{a=1}^k\nu_a!}.
\end{equation}
The formula follows by expanding
$P(\kappa+z_1+\cdots+z_k)$ with the multinomial theorem.
The full jet of \eqref{swlie:eq:physical}
is exactly $\eta[P]$. In particular the algebraic
relation ideal is exactly $\chi\swlieC[S]$, not a
Hilbert orthogonal complement.

\begin{swlietheorem}[Exact failure of grading descent]\label{swlie:thm:descent}
For every nonconstant $\chi$, the native polynomial grading
$\mathcal N$ does not preserve $\chi\swlieC[S]$ and hence
does not descend to $C_\chi$. Its first explicit obstruction
is as follows. Expand the original monic polynomial
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:chiexpand}
 \chi=p_q+\sum_{j<q}\gamma_jp_j,\qquad
 \gamma_j=\frac{\int_\swlieR\overline{p_j(s_0+iu)}
                \chi(s_0+iu)m(u)\,du}{\omega_j}.
\end{equation}
If at least one $\gamma_j\ne0$, then
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:relationdefect1}
 [\mathcal N\chi]_\chi
       =\sum_{j<q}(j-q)\gamma_j[p_j]_\chi\ne0.
\end{equation}
If every $\gamma_j=0$, so $\chi=p_q$, then instead
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:relationdefect2}
 [\mathcal N((S-s_0)\chi)]_\chi
       =2\frac{\omega_q}{\omega_{q-1}}[p_{q-1}]_\chi\ne0.
\end{equation}
The displayed relation is present by degree $L=q$ in the
first case and by degree $L=q+1$ in the second.
At $L=q-1$ there are no nonzero polynomial relations.
Consequently $\mathcal W_\lambda$ in
\eqref{swlie:eq:Wphysical} descends precisely for $\lambda=0$
among real $\lambda$.
\end{swlietheorem}
\begin{proof}
The $p_j$ for $j<q$ give a basis modulo $\chi$ by their
distinct monic degrees. Applying $\mathcal N-q$ to
\eqref{swlie:eq:chiexpand} proves \eqref{swlie:eq:relationdefect1};
its nonzero coefficients cannot cancel in that basis.
If $\chi=p_q$, the phase-retaining recurrence
\eqref{swlie:eq:physical-polys}--\eqref{swlie:eq:recurrence} is
\[
 (S-s_0)p_q=p_{q+1}
                 -\frac{\omega_q}{\omega_{q-1}}p_{q-1}.
\]
Apply $\mathcal N-(q+1)$ to it. Its right side is
$2(\omega_q/\omega_{q-1})p_{q-1}$, which is nonzero
and has degree below $q$. The subtracted multiple
of $(S-s_0)\chi$ is a relation, proving
\eqref{swlie:eq:relationdefect2}.
All multiplication and scalar terms of
\eqref{swlie:eq:Wphysical} preserve the ideal. The remaining
term is $8\pi\lambda^2\mathcal N$, so for $\lambda\ne0$
the same exact obstruction survives, whereas at zero
the operator is multiplication by $(S-s_0)^2-1/4$.
\end{proof}

For the actual reflection-stable $\chi$ the parity
involution does descend: $\chi(k-S)=(-1)^q\chi(S)$.
Thus $P(S)\mapsto P(k-S)$ preserves the ideal and is
an involution of its quotient. This is compatible
with $p_n(k-S)=(-1)^np_n(S)$.
The even/odd decomposition therefore survives exactly,
although the complete integer grading does not.

\section{Attained compression and both independent boundary terms}

Fix $L\geq q-1$. Write $b_n=[p_n]_\chi$ in the original
remainder coordinates. The native orthonormal reduction
matrix and the original attained form are
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:finitequotient}
 \begin{gathered}
 E_L=\left[\frac{i^{-n}b_n}{\sqrt{\omega_n}}\right]_{n=0}^L,
 \qquad K_L^\chi=E_LE_L^*
           =\sum_{n=0}^L\frac{b_nb_n^*}{\omega_n},
 \qquad G_L=(K_L^\chi)^{-1},\\
 R_L^\chi=E_L^*G_L,\qquad
 P_L^\chi=R_L^\chi E_L,\qquad Z_L^\chi=I-P_L^\chi.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The first $q$ monic columns prove surjectivity.
Hence $R_L^\chi$ is a right inverse, $P_L^\chi$
is the orthogonal projection to $(\ker E_L)^\perp$,
and $\ker E_L$ is precisely the unchanged relation
space expressed in native coordinates.
Also $(R_L^\chi)^*R_L^\chi=G_L$.
To see it is the attained original metric, any lift
of $v$ is $R_L^\chi v+z$ with $E_Lz=0$.
The two terms are orthogonal, and therefore its
squared norm is $v^*G_Lv+\swlienorm z^2$.
This recovers exactly the original full affine minimum.
The phases $i^{-n}$ cancel only in the displayed
diagonal sum for $K_L^\chi$; they remain present
in every off-diagonal operator calculation.

For any polynomial-domain action $A:\swliePP_L\to\swliePP_{L+r}$
let $A_{L+r,L}$ be its exact rectangular matrix.
Define its attained finite quotient receiver and relation
defect by
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:generalreceiver}
 \widehat A_L=E_{L+r}A_{L+r,L}R_L^\chi,\qquad
 \mathfrak d_{A,L}=E_{L+r}A_{L+r,L}Z_L^\chi.
\end{equation}
Then, for every native coefficient vector $x$,
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:relationidentity}
 E_{L+r}A_{L+r,L}x
        =\widehat A_LE_Lx+\mathfrak d_{A,L}x.
\end{equation}
This is the identity $I=P_L^\chi+Z_L^\chi$ with
all maps typed. Thus the non-descent of
Theorem~\ref{swlie:thm:descent} has an explicit finite receiver,
not merely a nonidentity.

For a square Hermitian cutoff matrix $A_L$, write
$\overline A_L=E_LA_LR_L^\chi$. Its $G_L$-weighted
matrix is
$G_L\overline A_L=(R_L^\chi)^*A_LR_L^\chi$, so it is
self-adjoint in the attained metric. In particular
$0\leq G_L\overline{\mathsf N}_L\leq L G_L$.
There is no assertion that the eigenvalues of this
compression are integers.
If $A_L,B_L$ are any two square cutoff matrices,
direct multiplication gives the full correction
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:compressedbracket}
 [\overline A_L,\overline B_L]
  =E_L[A_L,B_L]R_L^\chi
    -E_LA_LZ_L^\chi B_LR_L^\chi
    +E_LB_LZ_L^\chi A_LR_L^\chi.
\end{equation}
Substitution of \eqref{swlie:eq:cutbracket} in this formula
retains the native diagonal discrepancy, the rank-one
cutoff defect, and these two relation terms separately.
They must not be replaced by a rank count.
Writing $E=E_L$, $R=R_L^\chi$, $Z=Z_L^\chi$,
the individual Lie brackets are, explicitly,
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:quotientlie}
 \begin{aligned}
 [\overline H_L,\overline E_{\pm,L}]
 ={}&\pm2\overline E_{\pm,L}
   +\tfrac i2E(\Pi_L\Delta_c\Pi_L)R
   -\tfrac i2a_L^2EP_L^\partial R\\
 &-EH_LZE_{\pm,L}R+EE_{\pm,L}ZH_LR,\\
 [\overline E_{+,L},\overline E_{-,L}]
 ={}&\overline H_L/4
       -EE_{+,L}ZE_{-,L}R+EE_{-,L}ZE_{+,L}R .
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}

The exact compressed prolate matrix obeys the particularly
useful identity
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:compressedprolate}
 \begin{split}
 \overline W_{\lambda,L}
 ={}&-\overline J_L^{\,2}
       +8\pi\lambda^2(\overline{\mathsf N}_L+1/4)-1/4\\
 &-E_LJ_LZ_L^\chi J_LR_L^\chi
       -a_L^2E_LP_L^\partial R_L^\chi .
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
To prove it, insert $I=P_L^\chi+Z_L^\chi$
between the two $J_L$ factors of \eqref{swlie:eq:cutprolate}
and use $E_LR_L^\chi=I$.
Each of the two subtracted terms is positive
semidefinite in the $G_L$ metric: after multiplying
by $G_L$ they are respectively
$(Z_L^\chi J_LR_L^\chi)^*(Z_L^\chi J_LR_L^\chi)$
and $a_L^2(P_L^\partial R_L^\chi)^*
                           (P_L^\partial R_L^\chi)$.
This fixes both signs and proves an actual attained
quadratic comparison.

For clarity, the uncompressed action of multiplication
by $u=(S-s_0)/i$ does descend, with quotient matrix $M_u$.
The exact rectangular multiplication satisfies
$E_{L+1}J_{L+1,L}=M_uE_L$. Removing its outgoing
edge gives the explicit difference
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:Jquotient}
 \overline J_L=M_u-a_L
       \frac{i^{-(L+1)}b_{L+1}}{\sqrt{\omega_{L+1}}}
                                  e_L^*R_L^\chi.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, with every phase multiplied out,
$\overline J_L=M_u+(i/\omega_L)b_{L+1}b_L^*G_L$.
Thus the attained compression of multiplication need
not equal its algebraic quotient action. The outgoing
column, including its phase and mass, is exactly known.

The full rectangular receiver $\widehat W_{\lambda,L}$
from \eqref{swlie:eq:generalreceiver} and the square compressed
$\overline W_{\lambda,L}$ are different specified maps.
Their exact bridge is
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:fullcompbridge}
 \begin{aligned}
 \widehat W_{\lambda,L}
   &=-M_u^2+8\pi\lambda^2(\overline{\mathsf N}_L+1/4)-1/4,\\
 \overline W_{\lambda,L}
   &=\widehat W_{\lambda,L}
     -\left(\frac{b_{L+1}b_{L-1}^*}{\omega_{L-1}}
             +\frac{b_{L+2}b_L^*}{\omega_L}\right)G_L .
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The first fraction is omitted when $L=0$.
Indeed full multiplication by $u^2$ descends exactly to
$M_u^2$. The only outgoing two-step columns of
$W_\lambda:\swliePP_L\to\swliePP_{L+2}$ are
$-a_{L-1}a_Le_{L+1}e_{L-1}^*$
and $-a_La_{L+1}e_{L+2}e_L^*$.
Their $i^{-n}$ phase products change both signs to plus,
and their norm coefficients are $1/\omega_{L-1}$ and
$1/\omega_L$. Subtracting those columns proves the
second formula. This bridge retains outgoing quotient
data as well as the returning-edge correction in
\eqref{swlie:eq:compressedprolate}.

All maps can be pushed through the original physical
jet injection \eqref{swlie:eq:unitreceiver}: replace $E_L$
by $\eta E_L$ with codomain $\eta(C_\chi)$ and transport
the form by $\eta^{-1}$. This retains the complete
physical unit, not the Euclidean form on a substitute
jet space. For any fixed original observation $\Lambda$
one instead uses the onto map $\Lambda\eta E_L$
to its actual image and repeats the same attained
right-inverse construction there. The identities
\eqref{swlie:eq:generalreceiver}--\eqref{swlie:eq:compressedbracket}
remain exact, with that observation's actual relation
space; no automatic descent assertion is made for it.

\section{All original phases in explicit compressed matrices}

Set $b_n=[p_n]_\chi$ as above. Direct substitution of
the columns in \eqref{swlie:eq:finitequotient} gives
\begin{equation}\label{swlie:eq:explicitcompressed}
 \begin{aligned}
 \overline H_L
 &=\left[\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}
       \frac{b_jb_{j+1}^*-b_{j+1}b_j^*}{\omega_j}\right]G_L,\\
 \overline B_L
 &=\left[\frac12\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}
       \frac{b_jb_{j+1}^*+b_{j+1}b_j^*}{\omega_j}\right]G_L,\\
 \overline K_L
 &=\left[\sum_{j=0}^L
                  \frac{(j+c)b_jb_j^*}{\omega_j}\right]G_L,\\
 \overline W_{\lambda,L}
 &=\left[\sum_{j=0}^L\frac{w_jb_jb_j^*}{\omega_j}
      +\sum_{j=0}^{L-2}
             \frac{b_jb_{j+2}^*+b_{j+2}b_j^*}{\omega_j}\right]G_L.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Every sum with upper endpoint below its lower endpoint
is zero. For example the $j,j+1$ entry of $H$ is
$-ia_j$ and the corresponding phase product is
$i^{-j}i^{j+1}=i$. Their product is $+a_j$,
and $a_j/\sqrt{\omega_j\omega_{j+1}}=1/\omega_j$.
The reversed pair has the negative sign. For $B$
both signs are positive with factor $1/2$.
For the two-step prolate pair the phase product is
$-1$, changing $-a_ja_{j+1}$ to a positive coefficient;
$a_ja_{j+1}/\sqrt{\omega_j\omega_{j+2}}=1/\omega_j$.
These computations prove the formulas while retaining
all actual complex $b_j$ and their cross terms.

\section{Receiver into the existing interpolation-volume determinants}

The original cyclic-volume calculation \cite{swlie:CV}, CV.1--CV.5,

\section{Human source and exact original conventions}

sonintensor and groundstatedual; Theorem isosonin]{swdual:CCM} construct two
different semilocal transfers and prove their dual-pairing identity.
We give the complete receiving calculation below on the original
positive-real Hilbert space, then on the original theta complex and
the literal tensor packet. The factors of two, prime powers, Mellin
signs and actual arithmetic mass are not discarded.

Put $\mathcal H=L^2((0,\infty),dx)$, with inner product
$\langle f,g\rangle=\int_0^\infty\overline f g\,dx$.
The cosine transform is
\[
 (\swdualF f)(x)=2\int_0^\infty\cos(2\pi xt)f(t)\,dt.
                                                               \tag{SD1}
\]
It is a unitary involution on $\mathcal H$ by the ordinary Fourier
Plancherel theorem applied to even extensions; each even extension
has squared norm twice the positive-real norm. The original generator
is $D=-x\partial_x$. Its centered Hilbert realization is
$(D-1/2)/i$, not a replacement for $D$.
Use the left Mellin transform and its unitary critical-line restriction
\[
 \swdualM f(s)=\int_0^\infty f(x)x^{s-1}\,dx,\qquad
 Uf(t)=\frac{\swdualM f(1/2+it)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}.                       \tag{SD2}
\]
Indeed $x=e^y$ sends $f$ isometrically to $e^{y/2}f(e^y)$ in
$L^2(dy)$, and SD2 is its Fourier transform with the positive
exponential and factor $(2\pi)^{-1/2}$. Thus all subsequent scalar
products use $dt$, not an unrecorded multiple of it.

Let $\mathcal P$ be a fixed finite set of finite primes. For $p\in
\mathcal P$ define the unitary dilation
\[
 (V_pf)(x)=p^{1/2}f(px),\quad
 (V_p^*f)(x)=p^{-1/2}f(x/p),\quad r_p=p^{-1/2}.
                                                               \tag{SD3}
\]
Change of variables proves both the adjoint and unitarity. On their
natural domains these dilations commute with $D$, and all $V_p$ and
$V_q^*$ commute. Directly from SD1, $\swdualF V_p=V_p^*\swdualF$.

\section{Two bounded transfers and the preserved cross-pairing}
Set
\[
 E_{\mathcal P}=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}(I-r_pV_p)^{-1},\qquad
 T_{\mathcal P}=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}(I-r_pV_p^*)
                 =(E_{\mathcal P}^*)^{-1}.                     \tag{SD4}
\]
All inverse factors exist by norm-convergent geometric series since
$r_p<1$. This proves the full Hilbert-space domains and bounded
inverses, without an infinite Euler-product limit.
For one prime the formulas on the dense test space are
\[
 E_pf(x)=\sum_{j=0}^\infty f(p^jx),\qquad
 T_pf(x)=f(x)-p^{-1}f(x/p).                                    \tag{SD5}
\]
The first series is also the Hilbert norm limit because its $j$th
term has norm $p^{-j/2}\|f\|$.
Writing $s=1/2+it$, SD2 gives the exact multipliers
\[
 UE_{\mathcal P}f(t)=e_{\mathcal P}(s)Uf(t),\quad
 e_{\mathcal P}(s)=\prod_p(1-p^{-s})^{-1},
\]
\[
 UT_{\mathcal P}f(t)=b_{\mathcal P}(s)Uf(t),\quad
 b_{\mathcal P}(s)=\prod_p(1-p^{s-1}),\qquad
 \overline{b_{\mathcal P}(s)}e_{\mathcal P}(s)=1.
                                                               \tag{SD6}
\]
The plus sign in SD2 is why the two spectral arguments here are the
opposites of the paper's negative-exponential variable. No scalar
factor is inferred from the paper's other Hilbert conventions.
Equation SD4, or directly SD6 and Plancherel, proves
\[
 \boxed{\langle T_{\mathcal P}f,E_{\mathcal P}g\rangle
 =\langle f,g\rangle=\langle E_{\mathcal P}f,T_{\mathcal P}g\rangle.}
                                                               \tag{SD7}
\]
This keeps the complete complex pairing, rather than its real part.
Let
\[
 b_- =\prod_p(1-r_p),\qquad b_+=\prod_p(1+r_p).
\]
The triangle and reverse triangle inequalities in SD6 give
\[
 b_-\|f\|\le\|T_{\mathcal P}f\|\le b_+\|f\|,
 \qquad b_+^{-1}\|f\|\le\|E_{\mathcal P}f\|\le b_-^{-1}\|f\|.
                                                               \tag{SD8}
\]
These are explicit finite-set constants, not uniform assertions as
the prime set grows.

\section{The exact Sonin-space receiver}
The maps in SD4 are not interchangeable. Define the target Fourier
involution on the same Hilbert coordinate space by
\[
 \mathcal F_{+,\mathcal P}=E_{\mathcal P}\swdualF E_{\mathcal P}^{-1}
   =E_{\mathcal P}(E_{\mathcal P}^*)^{-1}\swdualF.                    \tag{SD9}
\]
The second equality follows by commuting $\swdualF$ past each dilation
factor. The operator $E_{\mathcal P}$ is normal, since all factors
commute with their adjoints. Consequently
$E_{\mathcal P}(E_{\mathcal P}^*)^{-1}$ is unitary; hence so is
$\mathcal F_{+,\mathcal P}$. Similarity in the first expression proves its
square is one, and it is therefore selfadjoint. The exact
intertwining equations are
\[
 \mathcal F_{+,\mathcal P}E_{\mathcal P}=E_{\mathcal P}\swdualF,
 \qquad\mathcal F_{+,\mathcal P}T_{\mathcal P}=T_{\mathcal P}\swdualF.
                                                               \tag{SD10}
\]
For the second, $\swdualF(E^*)^{-1}=E^{-1}\swdualF$ and commutativity give
$E(E^*)^{-1}\swdualF(E^*)^{-1}=(E^*)^{-1}\swdualF$.

Let $P_\lambda$ denote the subspace supported in $[0,\lambda]$,
and put
\[
 \mathcal S_\lambda=(P_\lambda+\swdualF P_\lambda)^\perp,
 \qquad
 \mathcal S_{\lambda,\mathcal P}
      =(P_\lambda+\mathcal F_{+,\mathcal P}P_\lambda)^\perp.              \tag{SD11}
\]
Both $f$ and its indicated Fourier transform vanish below $\lambda$
in the respective space. Formula SD5 shows
$E_{\mathcal P}P_\lambda\subset P_\lambda$ and shows that
$T_{\mathcal P}$ preserves a lower support gap $x<\lambda$.
Thus SD10 gives $T\mathcal S_\lambda\subset
\mathcal S_{\lambda,\mathcal P}$.
Conversely, for $h$ in the latter space write $h=Tf$, using the
bounded inverse of $T$. For $g\in P_\lambda$, SD7 gives
$\langle f,g\rangle=\langle h,Eg\rangle=0$.
Also $E\swdualF g=\mathcal F_{+,\mathcal P}Eg$ lies in
$\mathcal F_{+,\mathcal P}P_\lambda$, so
$\langle f,\swdualF g\rangle=\langle h,E\swdualF g\rangle=0$.
This proves the complete correspondence
\[
 \boxed{T_{\mathcal P}:\mathcal S_\lambda
       \longrightarrow\mathcal S_{\lambda,\mathcal P}
       \text{ is a bounded isomorphism with bounded inverse}.}  \tag{SD12}
\]
It is the explicit Hilbert-coordinate receiver of the semilocal
Sonin stability theorem of \cite{swdual:CCM}. It is not claimed to be an
isometry: the exact metric factors are SD8.

zero-divisor polynomial, denote that seed by $H$:
\begin{equation}\tag{PT2}
 \begin{split}
 H(x)&=(\pi^2x^4-\tfrac32\pi x^2)e^{-\pi x^2},\\
 \phi_*(x)&=(4\pi^2x^4-6\pi x^2)e^{-\pi x^2}=4H(x),\\
 \Theta\phi(x)&=2\sum_{n\geq1}\phi(nx),\qquad
 f_0=\Theta\phi_*=8\sum_{n\geq1}H(nx),\\
 K(u)&=\swthetaE(H)(u)=u^{1/2}\sum_{n\geq1}H(nu)
                   =\tfrac18u^{1/2}f_0(u).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The original scaling generator is $D=-x\partial_x$.
The two transforms, with all measures and signs, are
\begin{equation}\tag{PT3}
 \swthetaM F(s)=\int_0^\infty F(u)u^{s-1}\,du,\qquad
 \widehat K(z)=\int_0^\infty K(u)u^{-iz}\,\frac{du}{u}.
\end{equation}

\begin{swthetatheorem}[Exact seed and transform factors]
The displayed seed gives
\begin{equation}\tag{PT4}
 \swthetaM H(s)=\frac{s(s-1)}8\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2),\quad
 \swthetaM f_0(s)=g(s),\quad
 \widehat K(z)=\frac18g(1/2-iz)=\frac14\Xi(z).
\end{equation}
The last equality uses the functional equation with its unchanged
centre. In particular the literal $H$ in PT2 has transform
$\Xi/4$, not $\Xi$; multiplying $H$ by four gives the latter
transform. Both versions and their comparison map remain explicit.
\end{swthetatheorem}
\begin{proof}
For $\Re s>0$, the Gaussian integral gives
\[
 \int_0^\infty x^{s+r-1}e^{-\pi x^2}\,dx
  =\tfrac12\pi^{-(s+r)/2}\Gamma((s+r)/2).
\]
Consequently the coefficient of
$\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)$ in $\swthetaM H$ is
\[
 \tfrac12(s/2)(s/2+1)-\tfrac34(s/2)=s(s-1)/8.
\]
On $\Re s>1$ absolute convergence permits summing the Mellin
integrals of $H(nx)$, giving $\zeta(s)\swthetaM H(s)$.

For endpoint continuation, use the explicitly normalized Hermite
functions from \cite{swtheta:CCM}, equation \texttt{xih}:
\[
 h_0(x)=2^{1/4}e^{-\pi x^2},\qquad
 h_4(x)=\frac{16\pi^2x^4-24\pi x^2+3}{2\,2^{1/4}\sqrt3}
                                  e^{-\pi x^2}.
\]
Their stated coefficients give exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{PT5}
 H=\frac{\sqrt3}{2^{11/4}}h_4-\frac3{2^{17/4}}h_0,
 \qquad \|H\|_{L^2(\swthetaR,dx)}^2=\frac{33}{2^{17/2}}.
\end{equation}
The norm follows either by direct Gaussian integration or by the
orthonormality of these two displayed Hermite functions.
The Fourier transform with kernel $e^{2\pi ixy}$ fixes both:
differentiate the Gaussian transform four times to verify the
displayed $h_4$ polynomial, and use the Gaussian transform for $h_0$.
Also $H(0)=0$, hence $\int_\swthetaR H=0$ by Fourier invariance.
Poisson summation of this Schwartz function therefore gives
\[
 2\sum_{n\geq1}H(nu)=u^{-1}2\sum_{n\geq1}H(n/u),
 \quad f_0(u)=u^{-1}f_0(1/u),\quad K(u)=K(1/u).
\]
Gaussian decay at infinity and reflection give rapid decay at
both endpoints after every scaling derivative. All Mellin
integrals for $f_0$ and multiplicative Fourier integrals for $K$
are consequently entire. Their equalities on a right half-plane
extend everywhere. Finally $\xi(1-s)=\xi(s)$, equivalently the
reflection identity just proved, identifies the two signs of $z$.
\end{proof}

This scalar correction does not change zeros, but it changes
source masses and Gram matrices and therefore cannot be dropped.
The exact unitary between the original Hilbert observations is
\begin{equation}\tag{PT6}
 U:L^2(\swthetaR_+,du)\longrightarrow L^2(\swthetaR_+,du/u),\qquad
 (UF)(u)=u^{1/2}F(u),\quad K=Uf_0/8.
\end{equation}
Its inverse is multiplication by $u^{-1/2}$, and direct integration
proves unitarity. Thus $\|K\|^2=\|f_0\|^2/64$.
On the original smooth rapid source a direct derivative gives
\begin{equation}\tag{PT7}
 UDU^{-1}=\tfrac12-u\partial_u,
 \qquad -iu\partial_u=i(UDU^{-1}-\tfrac12).
\end{equation}
This proves the generator comparison, including the centre and phase.
The weighted trace here is also exactly
$\swthetaE(\phi_*)=U\Theta\phi_*/2=4K$, with the factor-two trace
convention established by Connes--Consani \cite{swtheta:Hochschild}.

\section{A full finite truncation bound}
Fix $\lambda\geq1$. Let $A_\lambda$ be an actual bounded measurable function
on $[-\lambda,\lambda]$, extended by zero outside that interval;
the source's compactly supported prolate combination is one such
input. Define its actual discrepancy and received finite theta sum:
\begin{equation}\tag{PT8}
 \begin{split}
 d_\lambda&=\sup_{|x|\leq\lambda}|A_\lambda(x)-H(x)|,\\
 K_\lambda(u)&=\mathbf1_{[\lambda^{-1},\lambda]}(u)\sqrt u
                 \sum_{1\leq n\leq\lambda/u}A_\lambda(nu).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The discrepancy is a defined norm, not an assumed decay rate.
We bound every part of the difference for every such input.
Put, for $v\geq1$,
\begin{equation}\tag{PT9}
 \begin{split}
 B(v)&=(\pi^2v^4+\tfrac32\pi v^2)e^{-\pi v^2},\\
 T(v)&=\int_v^\infty B(x)\,dx
   =e^{-\pi v^2}(\tfrac\pi2v^3+\tfrac32v)
                      +\tfrac34\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt\pi v),\\
 J_\nu(v)&=\int_v^\infty x^\nu e^{-\pi x^2}\,dx
     =\frac{\Gamma((\nu+1)/2,\pi v^2)}{2\pi^{(\nu+1)/2}},\\
 I_a(\lambda)&=\frac{\lambda^{1/2+a}-\lambda^{-1/2-a}}{1/2+a}
                                     \quad(0\leq a<1/2),\\
 R_a(\lambda)&=2\left[\pi^2J_{a+7/2}(\lambda)
       +\tfrac32\pi J_{a+3/2}(\lambda)
       +\frac{\lambda^{a-1/2}T(\lambda)}{1/2-a}\right].
 \end{split}
\end{equation}

\begin{swthetatheorem}[Interior discrepancy, omitted lattice tail, and endpoint tail]
For every $0\leq a<1/2$,
\begin{equation}\tag{PT10}
 \sup_{|\Im z|\leq a}
 \left|\widehat K_\lambda(z)-\tfrac14\Xi(z)\right|
 \leq \lambda d_\lambda I_a(\lambda)
       +[B(\lambda)+T(\lambda)]I_a(\lambda)+R_a(\lambda).
\end{equation}
All three terms are retained. The first is the actual interior
approximation error. The second records the omitted nonzero
Gaussian summands beyond the prolate support. The third records
the discarded endpoints of the multiplicative Fourier integral.
\end{swthetatheorem}
\begin{proof}
For $u\in[\lambda^{-1},\lambda]$ the exact difference is
\begin{equation}\tag{PT11}
 \begin{split}
 K_\lambda(u)-K(u)
   ={}&\sqrt u\sum_{n\leq\lambda/u}
                       [A_\lambda(nu)-H(nu)]\\
     &-\sqrt u\sum_{n>\lambda/u}H(nu).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
There are at most $\lambda/u$ terms in the first sum.
Also $B$ is decreasing on $[1,\infty)$, since
\[
 B'(v)=\pi v e^{-\pi v^2}(-2\pi^2v^4+\pi v^2+3)<0.
\]
Taking the first omitted lattice point separately and then
using the integral comparison for a decreasing positive function gives
\begin{equation}\tag{PT12}
 \sum_{n>\lambda/u}|H(nu)|\leq B(\lambda)+T(\lambda)/u.
\end{equation}
Indeed if $n_0=\lfloor\lambda/u\rfloor+1$, its first value is
at most $B(\lambda)$ and
$\sum_{n>n_0}B(nu)\leq u^{-1}\int_{n_0u}^\infty B$.
For $\alpha=\Im z$ the Fourier weight is $u^\alpha$.
The two necessary integrals satisfy
\[
 \int_{\lambda^{-1}}^\lambda u^{\alpha-3/2}\,du
 =\frac{\lambda^{1/2-\alpha}-\lambda^{\alpha-1/2}}{1/2-\alpha}
 \leq I_a(\lambda),
 \qquad
 \int_{\lambda^{-1}}^\lambda u^{\alpha-1/2}\,du
 \leq I_a(\lambda).
\]
For the first inequality write the expression as
$2\sinh(b\log\lambda)/b$, $b=1/2-\alpha\in[1/2-a,1/2+a]$.
It is increasing in $b>0$, since
$t\cosh t-\sinh t\geq0$ for $t\geq0$ by differentiation.
The second inequality follows by $u\mapsto1/u$ and
$\alpha\mapsto-\alpha$. Thus PT11 contributes the first two
terms of PT10.

For $u\geq\lambda\geq1$, the same decreasing-sum estimate gives
$|K(u)|\leq\sqrt u B(u)+u^{-1/2}T(u)$.
The reflection $K(u)=K(1/u)$ sends the missing lower endpoint
integral to the upper endpoint with exponent $-\alpha$.
Their sum is bounded by
\[
 2\int_\lambda^\infty
          [u^{a-1/2}B(u)+u^{a-3/2}T(u)]\,du.
\]
The first integral is the sum of the two $J$ terms in PT9.
For the second, positive Fubini gives
\[
 \int_\lambda^\infty u^{a-3/2}T(u)\,du
 =\int_\lambda^\infty B(v)
       \frac{\lambda^{a-1/2}-v^{a-1/2}}{1/2-a}\,dv
 \leq\frac{\lambda^{a-1/2}T(\lambda)}{1/2-a}.
\]
This proves the third term. Integration by parts twice proves
the explicit $T$ formula; $t=\pi x^2$ proves the $J$ formula.
Every bound is independent of $\Re z$, proving the stated
closed-substrip supremum.
\end{proof}

In particular the concrete truncated Hermite input
$A_\lambda=H|_{[-\lambda,\lambda]}$ has $d_\lambda=0$ and
PT10 proves its transform convergence with an explicit Gaussian
bound. This input is not relabelled as the prolate eigenfunction.
For that different input, PT10 retains its actual $d_\lambda$.
The estimate $d_\lambda=O(\lambda^{-2})$ is the estimate stated
and derived in \cite{swtheta:CCM}, Lemma \texttt{meixnerlem}, by using
Meixner--Sch\"afke and Fuchs. Their original books/papers have not
been read here. Substituting that explicitly attributed estimate
in the first term gives $O(\lambda^{-1/2+a})$; this observation
does not supply the still-unproved approximation to the minimum
eigenvector of the Weil form. PT11 supplies a Gaussian remainder
absent from the literal displayed first bound in
\cite{swtheta:CCM}, proof of Lemma \texttt{hermfact1}.

\section{Divided jets in the actual arithmetic packet}
Let the programme's actual monic zero divisor be
\[
 h(s)=\prod_{\rho\in Z}(s-\rho)^{m_\rho},\qquad d=\deg h,
\]
with its original complete multiplicities and unit
$\upsilon_h=j_h(g/h)$. No hypothetical zero is selected here.
For any entire $F$ define the literal interpolation polynomial
\begin{equation}\tag{PT13}
 \begin{split}
 b_\rho(s)&=h(s)/(s-\rho)^{m_\rho},\\
 \mathcal I_hF(s)&=\sum_{\rho\in Z}b_\rho(s)
   \sum_{j=0}^{m_\rho-1}\frac{1}{j!}
     \left(\frac{d^j}{ds^j}\frac{F(s)}{b_\rho(s)}\right)_{s=\rho}
                      (s-\rho)^j.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
It has degree less than $d$. Its divided jets of orders below
$m_\rho$ agree with $F$ at every $\rho$: all other summands
have the full zero there, and its own summand is Taylor inversion
of the nonzero local factor $b_\rho$. This proves the claim
including all factorials and differences $\rho-\rho'$.
Uniqueness follows because a polynomial of degree less than $d$
with all those zeros is zero. Therefore
\begin{equation}\tag{PT14}
 \mathcal D_hF=(F-\mathcal I_hF)/h
\end{equation}
is entire, with its removable values defined by the same divided
Taylor expansion. For the exact source, $\mathcal I_hg=0$
and $\mathcal D_hg=g/h$. This is an exact complex-linear map,
not an identification of an approximant with the arithmetic class.

Define, with the original sign convention,
\begin{equation}\tag{PT15}
 G_\lambda(s)=8\widehat K_\lambda(i(s-1/2))
       =8\int_{\lambda^{-1}}^\lambda K_\lambda(u)
                                         u^{s-1/2}\,\frac{du}{u}.
\end{equation}
For any $a<1/2$, PT10 bounds $G_\lambda-g$ by eight times
its right side on $|\Re s-1/2|\leq a$. On a circle of radius
$r>0$ lying in that strip, Cauchy's formula gives for each
divided derivative
\begin{equation}\tag{PT16}
 \frac{|(G_\lambda-g)^{(j)}(s_*)|}{j!}
       \leq r^{-j}\sup_{|s-s_*|=r}|G_\lambda(s)-g(s)|.
\end{equation}
All selected nontrivial zeros lie in the open strip $0<\Re s<1$;
for a fixed finite packet, circles for every selected zero can
thus be chosen inside a larger closed substrip. PT13--16
explicitly transport the finite transform error to every original
jet and to the entire divisor quotient. To see convergence on a
compact subset of $0<\Re s<1$ containing divisor roots, surround
it and the finite packet by a bounded contour inside that open strip
and avoiding the roots. On that contour $\min|h|>0$,
PT13 bounds the interpolation error from PT16, and PT14 bounds
the divided error. The maximum principle extends that bound
to its interior. The exact physical unit remains $j_h(g/h)$;
the approximating unit is the explicitly different
$j_h(\mathcal D_hG_\lambda)$ and converges when the stated
transform error tends to zero.
This proves compact convergence of the quotient in the open strip,
not uniform convergence on arbitrary compact subsets of the plane.

The higher derivatives needed by the physical unit can also be
written without hiding an interpolation loss. Write
$f_{\rho,n}=F^{(n)}(\rho)/n!$,
$p_{\rho,n}=(\mathcal I_hF)^{(n)}(\rho)/n!$ and
$\beta_{\rho,a}=[t^a]b_\rho(\rho+t)^{-1}$. Direct Taylor
multiplication gives
\begin{equation}\tag{PT17}
 \frac{(\mathcal D_hF)^{(\ell)}(\rho)}{\ell!}
   =\sum_{a=0}^{\ell}\beta_{\rho,a}
           (f_{\rho,m_\rho+\ell-a}-p_{\rho,m_\rho+\ell-a}).
\end{equation}
Indeed $F-\mathcal I_hF$ vanishes to order $m_\rho$ at
$\rho$; division by $t^{m_\rho}$ shifts the Taylor index, and
division by the remaining nonzero factor gives the displayed
convolution. Expanding each factor
$(\rho-\sigma+t)^{-m_\sigma}$ gives the full coefficients
\begin{equation}\tag{PT18}
 \beta_{\rho,a}=(-1)^a
   \prod_{\sigma\ne\rho}(\rho-\sigma)^{-m_\sigma}
   \sum_{\substack{k_\sigma\ge0\\\sum_{\sigma\ne\rho}k_\sigma=a}}
     \prod_{\sigma\ne\rho}
       \binom{m_\sigma+k_\sigma-1}{k_\sigma}
                      (\rho-\sigma)^{-k_\sigma}.
\end{equation}
For an empty product, the sum is one at $a=0$ and zero otherwise.
Thus receiving the full original quotient jet $\ell<m_\rho$
requires input derivatives through order $2m_\rho-1$, not only
the interpolation derivatives below $m_\rho$. PT16 controls all
these higher derivatives with their explicit $r^{-j}$ factors.
For $F=g$ the interpolation polynomial vanishes identically, and
PT17 gives exactly the original unit $j_h(g/h)$.

These are entire-function and jet statements. The sharp interval
cutoff in PT8 can produce endpoint jumps; its inverse Mellin
source is not silently placed in the original rapid smooth space.
No convergence in its strong seminorms, no convergence of native
moment matrices of increasing degree, and no real-zero claim for
$G_\lambda$ follows from PT10 or PT16. The finite rank-one
self-adjoint compression and its relation correction must still
be calculated in the unchanged quotient metric. Thus the present
map retains the actual missing data instead of discarding it.

\section{The complete closed-substrip estimate}
Let $\lambda\ge1$ and let $A_\lambda$ be bounded and measurable
on $[-\lambda,\lambda]$, extended by zero outside that interval.
Set
\[
 d_\lambda=\sup_{|x|\le\lambda}|A_\lambda(x)-H(x)|,\qquad
 K_\lambda(u)=\mathbf1_{[\lambda^{-1},\lambda]}(u)\sqrt u
       \sum_{1\le n\le\lambda/u}A_\lambda(nu).
\]
This transform is entire because its defining measurable integrand
has compact support away from $0$.
For $u$ in that support the exact error is
\begin{equation}\tag{IC6}
 K_\lambda(u)-K(u)
 =\sqrt u\sum_{n\le\lambda/u}(A_\lambda(nu)-H(nu))
   -\sqrt u\sum_{n>\lambda/u}H(nu).
\end{equation}
The second sum does not vanish: for example $H(n)>0$ for each
integer $n\ge1$. Consequently taking $A_\lambda=H$ on its interval
does not justify dropping this term.

For $v\ge1$ put
\[
 B(v)=(\pi^2v^4+\tfrac32\pi v^2)e^{-\pi v^2},\quad
 T(v)=\int_v^\infty B(x)\,dx.
\]
Here $|H(v)|\le B(v)$ and
\[
 B'(v)=\pi v e^{-\pi v^2}(-2\pi^2v^4+\pi v^2+3)<0.
\]
Indeed $-2y^2+y+3<0$ for $y\ge\pi>3/2$.
Separating the first omitted lattice point and bounding subsequent
points by the integral of this decreasing function gives
\begin{equation}\tag{IC7}
 \sum_{n>\lambda/u}|H(nu)|\le B(\lambda)+T(\lambda)/u.
\end{equation}
Integration by parts, or differentiation followed by the value at
infinity, gives exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{IC8}
 T(v)=e^{-\pi v^2}(\tfrac\pi2v^3+\tfrac32v)
       +\tfrac34\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt\pi v),\qquad
 J_\nu(v)=\int_v^\infty x^\nu e^{-\pi x^2}\,dx
 =\frac{\Gamma((\nu+1)/2,\pi v^2)}{2\pi^{(\nu+1)/2}}.
\end{equation}

Let $0\le a<1/2$ and $\alpha=\Im z$ with $|\alpha|\le a$.
The two integrals required by IC6--7 are
\[
 \int_{1/\lambda}^{\lambda}u^{\alpha-3/2}\,du
 =\frac{\lambda^{1/2-\alpha}-\lambda^{\alpha-1/2}}{1/2-\alpha},
 \quad
 \int_{1/\lambda}^{\lambda}u^{\alpha-1/2}\,du
 =\frac{\lambda^{1/2+\alpha}-\lambda^{-1/2-\alpha}}{1/2+\alpha}.
\]
Each is at most
$I_a=(\lambda^{1/2+a}-\lambda^{-1/2-a})/(1/2+a)$.
To see this, use the increasing function
$b\mapsto2\sinh(b\log\lambda)/b$ for $b>0$; its derivative
has the sign of $t\cosh t-\sinh t$, nonnegative by differentiation
from $t=0$.

For $u\ge\lambda$, $|K(u)|\le\sqrt u B(u)+u^{-1/2}T(u)$.
Reflection IC3 turns the missing lower endpoint into an upper
endpoint with exponent $-\alpha$. Both endpoint errors are thus
at most
\[
 2\int_\lambda^\infty
     \big[u^{a-1/2}B(u)+u^{a-3/2}T(u)\big]\,du.
\]
Positive Fubini evaluates the latter $T$ integral as
\[
 \int_\lambda^\infty B(v)
       \frac{\lambda^{a-1/2}-v^{a-1/2}}{1/2-a}\,dv
 \le\frac{\lambda^{a-1/2}T(\lambda)}{1/2-a}.
\]
Combining these equalities proves the complete bound
\begin{equation}\tag{IC9}
 \begin{split}
 \sup_{|\Im z|\le a}|\widehat K_\lambda(z)-\Xi(z)/4|
 \le{}&\lambda d_\lambda I_a+[B(\lambda)+T(\lambda)]I_a\\
 &+2\left[\pi^2J_{a+7/2}(\lambda)
 +\tfrac32\pi J_{a+3/2}(\lambda)
 +\frac{\lambda^{a-1/2}T(\lambda)}{1/2-a}\right].
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
All constants and exponents match PT9--12. There is no dependence
on $\Re z$. Substitution of the CCM-attributed prolate estimate
$d_\lambda=O(\lambda^{-2})$ makes its first term
$O(\lambda^{-1/2+a})$. The two Gaussian terms also tend to zero.
The actual discrepancy is retained; no approximation to a minimum
Weil eigenvector has been assumed.

\section{The exact divided-quotient jets}
Let $h(s)=\prod_\rho(s-\rho)^{m_\rho}$ for the actual finite
packet of distinct roots and complete positive multiplicities,
and $d=\sum m_\rho$. Put $b_\rho=h/(s-\rho)^{m_\rho}$ and define
the divided derivatives and local reciprocal coefficients by
\[
 f_{\rho,n}=F^{(n)}(\rho)/n!,\quad
 \beta_{\rho,a}=[t^a]b_\rho(\rho+t)^{-1},\quad
 c_{\rho,j}=\sum_{a=0}^j\beta_{\rho,a}f_{\rho,j-a}.
\]
Taylor multiplication gives
\begin{equation}\tag{IC10}
 P=\mathcal I_hF=\sum_\rho b_\rho(s)
                   \sum_{j=0}^{m_\rho-1}c_{\rho,j}(s-\rho)^j.
\end{equation}
At $\rho$ the other summands vanish to order $m_\rho$, while
the local summand agrees with $F$ to that order. Every summand
has degree less than $d$. A difference of two such polynomials
would be divisible by $h$ and have degree less than $d$, hence
would vanish. This proves interpolation and uniqueness.
Therefore $\mathcal D_hF=(F-P)/h$ is entire for entire $F$.

Write $p_{\rho,n}=P^{(n)}(\rho)/n!$, zero for $n\ge d$.
Taylor division now gives the complete quotient-jet formula
\begin{equation}\tag{IC11}
 \frac{(\mathcal D_hF)^{(\ell)}(\rho)}{\ell!}
 =\sum_{a=0}^{\ell}\beta_{\rho,a}
        (f_{\rho,m_\rho+\ell-a}-p_{\rho,m_\rho+\ell-a}).
\end{equation}
Indeed the numerator has Taylor coefficients zero below
$m_\rho$, and division by $(s-\rho)^{m_\rho}$ shifts those
indices by precisely $m_\rho$. Multiplication by $1/b_\rho$
then yields IC11.

For a formula containing only input divided jets, the identity
$P/h=\sum_\sigma\sum_{j<m_\sigma}c_{\sigma,j}
(s-\sigma)^{j-m_\sigma}$ yields
\begin{equation}\tag{IC12}
 \begin{split}
 \frac{(\mathcal D_hF)^{(\ell)}(\rho)}{\ell!}
 ={}&\sum_{a=0}^{m_\rho+\ell}\beta_{\rho,a}f_{\rho,m_\rho+\ell-a}\\
 &-(-1)^\ell\sum_{\sigma\ne\rho}\sum_{j=0}^{m_\sigma-1}
 c_{\sigma,j}\binom{m_\sigma-j+\ell-1}{\ell}
 (\rho-\sigma)^{-(m_\sigma-j+\ell)}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The coefficient follows by expanding each negative integral
power at $\rho$. The reciprocal coefficients themselves are
the following finite expressions, retaining every root difference:
\begin{equation}\tag{IC13}
 \beta_{\rho,a}=(-1)^a\prod_{\sigma\ne\rho}(\rho-\sigma)^{-m_\sigma}
 \sum_{\substack{k_\sigma\ge0\\\sum k_\sigma=a}}
 \prod_{\sigma\ne\rho}\binom{m_\sigma+k_\sigma-1}{k_\sigma}
                    (\rho-\sigma)^{-k_\sigma}.
\end{equation}
This is the coefficient of $t^a$ in the product of the binomial
series $(1+t/(\rho-\sigma))^{-m_\sigma}$, each multiplied by
its unchanged constant $(\rho-\sigma)^{-m_\sigma}$.
For a single root the empty product is $1$ at $a=0$ and the
empty constrained sum is zero at $a>0$.

IC11--12 show that the quotient jet of order $\ell$ requires
input derivatives through $m_\rho+\ell$ at $\rho$ and only
the interpolation jets at other roots. The entire original quotient
jet at $\rho$, of orders $\ell<m_\rho$, therefore uses derivatives
through $2m_\rho-1$. This order is necessary in the unrestricted
holomorphic class: for $F=h(s)(s-\rho)^\ell$, every interpolation
jet is zero, yet $\mathcal D_hF=(s-\rho)^\ell$ has divided jet
$1$ at order $\ell$. Every derivative of $F$ at $\rho$ below
$m_\rho+\ell$ is zero, and the next is
$(m_\rho+\ell)!b_\rho(\rho)\ne0$.

For a finite quantitative bound choose a circle of radius $r_\sigma$
at every root and put
\[
 S_\sigma=\sup_{|s-\sigma|=r_\sigma}|F(s)|,
 \quad A_{\sigma,n}=\sum_{a=0}^n|\beta_{\sigma,a}|r_\sigma^{-(n-a)}.
\]
Cauchy's integral formula gives
$|f_{\sigma,j}|\le S_\sigma r_\sigma^{-j}$ and
$|c_{\sigma,n}|\le S_\sigma A_{\sigma,n}$. Consequently IC12
proves the explicit finite estimate
\begin{equation}\tag{IC14}
 \begin{split}
 \left|\frac{(\mathcal D_hF)^{(\ell)}(\rho)}{\ell!}\right|
 \le{}&S_\rho A_{\rho,m_\rho+\ell}\\
 &+\sum_{\sigma\ne\rho}S_\sigma\sum_{j<m_\sigma}
 A_{\sigma,j}\binom{m_\sigma-j+\ell-1}{\ell}
 |\rho-\sigma|^{-(m_\sigma-j+\ell)}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
These circles need only lie in the domain of $F$; the reciprocal
coefficients are exact local algebraic constants, so another root
inside one of the circles does not invalidate this estimate.

Finally set $G_\lambda(s)=8\widehat K_\lambda(i(s-1/2))$.
Since $-i\,i(s-1/2)=s-1/2$, IC4 gives the exact target $g(s)$.
Also $\Im(i(s-1/2))=\Re s-1/2$. Thus IC9 bounds
$G_\lambda-g$ by eight times its right side on every closed
substrip $|\Re s-1/2|\le a<1/2$. Apply IC14 to this error.
The circles for a fixed packet can all be chosen inside such
a larger closed substrip, proving convergence of every original
quotient jet whenever the displayed transform error tends to zero.
For a compact subset of $0<\Re s<1$, take a bounded contour
inside that strip enclosing both the compact set and packet,
and avoiding the roots. On the contour $|h|$ has a positive
minimum, IC10 controls the interpolant of the error, and division
by $h$ controls its divided error. The maximum principle extends
the bound to the enclosed compact set.

The exact physical unit is still $j_h(g/h)$; its approximant is
$j_h(\mathcal D_hG_\lambda)$ with the higher derivatives specified
above. The sharp interval cutoff may create endpoint jumps.
None of these entire-function, strip, or fixed-packet statements
places that inverse Mellin source in the original strong smooth
space, proves an increasing-degree moment estimate, or proves
that the approximants have only real spectral zeros.

\section{Exact finite verification}

signs; the full tail derivatives and incomplete-gamma constants;
the two interior power integrals; and IC10--13 on four explicitly
artificial repeated-root packets, including nonreal roots.
Those packets are illustrative polynomial data, not zeta zeros.
