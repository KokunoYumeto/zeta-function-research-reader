\section{The actual original conductor on the complete collision quotient}
ECC23--43 gives both original receiving metrics and every raw observation,
jet and trace cost. Here we evaluate the induced original conductor itself
between those two quotient metrics. This retains its original moments,
Gamma masses, order, phase and centres \cite{WCF}; the four marked signed
states are the ones in ECC31, with the original ES marking \cite{ES488}.
The resulting estimate is a direct application to the original
inverse-exterior quantity, rather than to a different multiplication map.

Let $\mathcal U=U\oplus U$, $\mathcal W=W\oplus W$ have their unchanged
original Hilbert metrics and let $\mathcal T=T_A\oplus T_A$. Write
$\mathcal F_b=F_b\oplus F_b$ for $b=U,W$, so
$\mathcal T\mathcal F_U=\mathcal F_W$. Retain the literal local
evaluation matrix $E_t$ and the two positive covariance matrices from ECC39:
\[
 L_t=\diag(E_t,E_t),\qquad
 J_{b,t}=E_tH_b^{-1}E_t^*,\qquad G_{b,t}=J_{b,t}^{-1},\qquad
 q_{b,t}=L_t\mathcal F_b^{-1}.
 \tag{OCQ1}
\]
The matrix $E_t$ has rank two including at $t=0$: its rows are evaluation
at $P$ and the exact divided difference, with the latter equal to the
derivative at zero. On the polynomials $1,r$ those rows form
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&P\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
Thus each $q_{b,t}$ is onto $\mathbb C^4$ and has its complete
four-dimensional kernel. Exact multiplication proves
\[
 q_{W,t}\mathcal T=q_{U,t},\qquad
 \mathcal T\ker q_{U,t}=\ker q_{W,t}.
 \tag{OCQ2}
\]
The reverse kernel inclusion follows by applying $\mathcal T^{-1}$;
there is no assertion based only on matching dimensions.
Equip the quotient by each kernel with its attained minimum norm.
ECC39 proves that its coordinate Gram is
$\mathcal G_{b,t}=\diag(G_{b,t},G_{b,t})$. Therefore the actual induced map is
\[
 \overline{\mathcal T}_t:
 (\mathbb C^4,\mathcal G_{U,t})\longrightarrow
 (\mathbb C^4,\mathcal G_{W,t}),\qquad z\longmapsto z.
 \tag{OCQ3}
\]
Identity coefficients do not equate the two metrics. OCQ2 proves
that OCQ3 is the quotient of the original conductor, with both kernels
and both norms retained, rather than an introduced operator.

\subsection{Every finite singular value and inverse exterior norm}
Define the exact scalar functions
\[
 a_t=\operatorname{tr}(J_{W,t}J_{U,t}^{-1}),\qquad
 b_t=\frac{\det J_{W,t}}{\det J_{U,t}},\qquad
 \kappa_\pm(t)=\frac{a_t\pm\sqrt{a_t^2-4b_t}}2.
 \tag{OCQ4}
\]
All matrices in this formula are the explicit finite original-moment
matrices ECC24--28 and ECC39; the two-by-two inverse uses its adjugate
and positive determinant. No coefficient or metric is replaced.
For an entirely expanded two-by-two formula, put
$J_b=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha_b&\beta_b\\
 \bar\beta_b&\delta_b\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and
$d_b=\alpha_b\delta_b-|\beta_b|^2$. Then
\[
 a_t=\frac{\alpha_W\delta_U+\delta_W\alpha_U
                -2\operatorname{Re}(\beta_W\bar\beta_U)}{d_U},
 \qquad b_t=d_W/d_U.
 \tag{OCQ5}
\]
Positive definiteness shows that $J_WJ_U^{-1}$ is similar to the
positive Hermitian matrix $J_U^{-1/2}J_WJ_U^{-1/2}$.
Its eigenvalues are therefore the positive numbers in OCQ4, including
the repeated case $a_t^2=4b_t$. Isometric coordinates for the inverse
of OCQ3 give the squared singular matrix
$\mathcal G_{W,t}^{-1/2}\mathcal G_{U,t}
 \mathcal G_{W,t}^{-1/2}$, whose two parity blocks have these same
eigenvalues. Consequently all four inverse singular values are
\[
 \sqrt{\kappa_+},\ \sqrt{\kappa_+},\
 \sqrt{\kappa_-},\ \sqrt{\kappa_-}.
 \tag{OCQ6}
\]
Taking their ordered products proves every inverse exterior norm:
\[
 \begin{array}{c|cccc}
 j&1&2&3&4\\\hline
 \|\wedge^j\overline{\mathcal T}_t^{-1}\|&
 \sqrt{\kappa_+}&\kappa_+&\kappa_+\sqrt{\kappa_-}&b_t.
 \end{array}\tag{OCQ7}
\]
Both $J_{b,t}$ tend to their positive evaluation--derivative Grams
$J_{b,0}$, proved in ECC34. Substitution of those same matrices in
OCQ4--7 gives every finite nonzero limiting constant at $t=0$.
In particular no power of $|t|^{-1}$ occurs in this induced original
conductor inverse, although ECC37 and ECC43 compute such powers for
the raw-value and trace inverses separately.

\subsection{Exact cancellation in the original signed observations}
For $t\ne0$ the raw signed observation is $O_t$ from ECC31 and its
original covariance is $C_{b,t}=O_t\diag(H_b^{-1},H_b^{-1})O_t^*$.
The same unchanged $Z_t$ from ECC38 acts at both receivers. ECC39 is
the full congruence
\[
 Z_tC_{b,t}Z_t^*=\diag(J_{b,t},J_{b,t}),\qquad
 C_{b,t}^{-1}=Z_t^*\mathcal G_{b,t}Z_t\quad(b=U,W).
 \tag{OCQ8}
\]
Thus $Z_t$ is an isometry from each raw-value quotient metric to its
own jet quotient metric, simultaneously. The induced conductor on raw
values is also the coordinate identity by OCQ2 with $O_t$ in place
of $L_t$. Conjugation by the same $Z_t$ proves that its entire inverse
spectrum is OCQ6, not just that a leading determinant happens to cancel.
For example the exact top exterior cancellation is
\[
 \sqrt{\frac{\det C_{W,t}}{\det C_{U,t}}}
 =\frac{\det J_{W,t}}{\det J_{U,t}}=b_t.
 \tag{OCQ9}
\]
The common factors from $|s_0s_t|$ and the divided difference are
identical in numerator and denominator. Both odd signs and both even
directions survive this identity. The raw change is singular at zero;
the four-dimensional jet quotient OCQ1--3 extends through zero and
provides its specified, positive limiting metric.

\subsection{A bound by the unchanged original inverse-exterior quantity}
Let $\pi_{U,t}:\mathcal U\to\mathcal U/\ker q_{U,t}$ be the
quotient map, and $\iota_{W,t}:\mathcal W/\ker q_{W,t}\to\mathcal W$
the unique minimum-norm representative. The first is a contraction;
the second is an isometry by definition of the quotient norm. OCQ2
proves the exact inverse factorization
\[
 \overline{\mathcal T}_t^{-1}
   =\pi_{U,t}\mathcal T^{-1}\iota_{W,t}.
 \tag{OCQ10}
\]
To verify it, lift a target class, apply $\mathcal T^{-1}$ and take
its source class; OCQ2 gives that class the same four coordinates.
Changing its target representative changes the source by its full
kernel, so this verification is independent of the chosen lift.

Let $\gamma_1\ge\cdots\ge\gamma_8>0$ be the actual eight singular
values of $(T_A\oplus T_A)^{-1}$ in its original two metrics, as in
ET33--39. For any rank-less-than-$j$ operator $B$ approximating
$\mathcal T^{-1}$, the operator $\pi_U B\iota_W$ has rank less than
$j$ and approximation error at most $\|\mathcal T^{-1}-B\|$.
The least rank-less-than-$j$ error equals the $j$th singular value:
the singular-value decomposition attains it by retaining the first
$j-1$ singular summands, and any rank-less-than-$j$ map kills a vector
in the span of the first $j$ right singular vectors, giving the reverse
inequality. Applying this fact to OCQ10 proves
\[
 s_j(\overline{\mathcal T}_t^{-1})\le\gamma_j\quad(1\le j\le4),
 \qquad
 \boxed{\|\wedge^j\overline{\mathcal T}_t^{-1}\|
       \le\prod_{k=1}^j\gamma_k
       =\|\wedge^j\mathcal T^{-1}\|.}
 \tag{OCQ11}
\]
This holds at every admitted parameter, including the collision itself.
It applies directly to the actual original inverse-exterior estimate,
with the original source and target norms. It proves that this
particular collision quotient creates no additional inverse-exterior
loss. The conclusion is confined to the exact four-state quotient
and the fixed original conductor used throughout this calculation;
it does not assert an arithmetic Frobenius identification or an RH
endpoint.
