

The finite signed-root algebra and its exact trace-to-residue
multiplier are those of RD1--29 and the received continuation
RE1--30 \cite{Fable037,ReceivedRE}. We derive the complete inverse singular
constants for every retained positive coefficient Gram, then
evaluate that Gram through the full original conductor inverse.
The original fixed conductor, its moments and both Gamma centers
are retained. The Gamma polynomial dictionary is the one credited
to the human authors of DLMF Chapter 18 in WCF1--6
\cite{WCF,DLMF18Source}; its application to the original conductor is
FC20--24 and NH26--34 \cite{Fable037}. The new calculation here is the
arbitrary-Gram singular decomposition and its exact original-source
specialization, rather than a new orthogonality result.

\section{The full signed multiplier with its complex phase}
Fix the original reference period and its conductor throughout.
Independently vary the real collision parameter $w=\delta^2>0$,
keeping $g=\gamma^2>0$ fixed. In the unchanged coordinate
$x=r-1/2$, set
\begin{equation}
 h_w(x)=-\tfrac12\bigl(x^4+2(g-w)x^2+(g+w)^2\bigr),\qquad
 S_w=\C[x,t]/(h_w,t^2-h_w'),\qquad
 \mathbf e=(1,x,x^2,x^3,t,tx,tx^2,tx^3).
 \tag{RS1}
\end{equation}
The factor $-1/2$ is unchanged: after replacing $x$ by $r-1/2$
the coefficient of $r^3$ is precisely one. The matrix of
multiplication by $x$ in each parity summand and the derivative
multiplier are
\begin{equation}
 X_w=\begin{pmatrix}
 0&0&0&-(g+w)^2\\1&0&0&0\\0&1&0&-2(g-w)\\0&0&1&0
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 H_w=-2X_w\bigl(X_w^2+(g-w)I\bigr).
 \tag{RS2}
\end{equation}
These follow by reducing $x^4$ in the exact algebra RS1 and
differentiating its unchanged $h_w$. In particular,
\begin{equation}
 H_w^2=-16gwX_w^2,\qquad
 \mathsf J_w=M_{2t^3}=2\begin{pmatrix}0&H_w^2\\H_w&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad c=\frac{1+i}{\sqrt2},\qquad \Theta_w=\mathsf J_w/c.
 \tag{RS3}
\end{equation}
To verify the first identity, the relation RS1 gives
$(X_w^2+(g-w)I)^2=-4gwI$; multiplying by $4X_w^2$
gives the asserted expression. The second follows by cubing
$M_t=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&H_w\\I&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
The phase $c$ is the reflected-residue convention RE10--12:
$x^\sharp=-x$, $t^\sharp=it$, with conjugation of coefficients.
It respects $t^2=h_w'(x)$ and has square one. In that convention
the reflected trace is exactly the reflected residue composed
with $\Theta_w$, not with a multiplier in which $c$ is omitted.
We retain this $c$ in every operator identity below.

Since $\det X_w=(g+w)^2$, RS3 gives for $w>0$
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf J_w^{-1}
 =\frac12\begin{pmatrix}0&H_w^{-1}\\H_w^{-2}&0\end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}
 0&\dfrac{X_w^2+(g-w)I}{16gwX_w}\\[4pt]
 -\dfrac{X_w^{-2}}{32gw}&0
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \Theta_w^{-1}=c\mathsf J_w^{-1}.
 \tag{RS4}
\end{equation}
For the upper-right equality multiply by $H_w$ and use
$(X_w^2+(g-w)I)^2=-4gwI$. The lower-left equality is the
inverse of the first identity RS3. These checks prove both
inverse products and preserve every complex phase.

At $w=0$ define $X_0=X_w|_{w=0}$ and
\begin{equation}
 N=X_0^2+gI=
 \begin{pmatrix}g&0&-g^2&0\\0&g&0&-g^2\\1&0&-g&0\\0&1&0&-g\end{pmatrix},
 \quad H_0=-2X_0N,\quad
 N^2=0,\quad\rank N=\rank H_0=2.
 \tag{RS5}
\end{equation}
The first and third columns of each parity block are proportional,
and the first two columns are independent, so $N$ has rank two.
Because $X_0$ is invertible, $H_0$ has rank two as well.
Since $X_0^2=-gI+N$ and $N^2=0$, its inverse square is
$X_0^{-2}=-(gI+N)/g^2$. Therefore RS4 gives the full matrix limit
\begin{equation}
 w\Theta_w^{-1}\longrightarrow
 c\begin{pmatrix}0&R_1\\R_2&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 R_1=\frac{H_0}{32g^2},\qquad R_2=\frac{gI+N}{32g^3}.
 \tag{RS6}
\end{equation}
For the first block, $H_0X_0^{-2}=-H_0/g$, since $H_0N=0$.
For the second block use the displayed inverse square. This also
proves that the residue of the inverse has rank six.

\section{Every singular constant for an arbitrary retained Gram}
Let $G$ be any fixed positive definite Hermitian four-by-four
matrix in the exact increasing powers $(1,x,x^2,x^3)$.
No parity condition is imposed. Endow $S_w$ with
$G_8=\diag(G,G)$ in the order RS1. For a four-dimensional
operator $A$, put
\begin{equation}
 K_A=G^{-1}A^*GA,\qquad
 a_A=\tr K_A,\qquad
 b_A=\frac{(\tr K_A)^2-\tr(K_A^2)}2.
 \tag{RS7}
\end{equation}
These expressions are computed from the original Gram.
$K_A$ is similar to the positive semidefinite Hermitian matrix
$(G^{1/2}AG^{-1/2})^*(G^{1/2}AG^{-1/2})$, so its eigenvalues
are the squared singular values in that Gram. For $A=N,H_0$
exactly two are positive. Thus the following are all of their
positive singular values, in decreasing order:
\begin{equation}
 \chi_{1,2}=
 \sqrt{\frac{a_N\pm\sqrt{a_N^2-4b_N}}2},\qquad
 \eta_{1,2}=
 \sqrt{\frac{a_{H_0}\pm\sqrt{a_{H_0}^2-4b_{H_0}}}2}.
 \tag{RS8}
\end{equation}
Both inner discriminants are nonnegative: for two positive
eigenvalues $\lambda_1,\lambda_2$ they equal
$(\lambda_1-\lambda_2)^2$. Their products $b_A$ are strictly
positive. Thus all radicals in RS8 are well defined, including
equality of the two singular values.

There is an exact two-by-two evaluation of every invariant in RS8.
It keeps every entry of $G$ and of $G^{-1}$:
\begin{equation}
 V=\begin{pmatrix}g&0\\0&g\\1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 R=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&-g&0\\0&1&0&-g\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 J_g=\begin{pmatrix}0&-g\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 Q=V^*GV,\quad P=RG^{-1}R^*.
 \tag{RS9}
\end{equation}
Both $P,Q$ are positive definite. Matrix multiplication gives
$N=VR$, $RV=0$, $X_0V=VJ_g$ and $H_0=-2VJ_gR$.
The nonzero eigenvalues of products $AB$ and $BA$ agree, with
algebraic multiplicity: for a nonzero eigenvalue, $B$ maps its
generalized eigenspace injectively to that of $BA$, with inverse
obtained from the finite inverse of $AB$ on that eigenspace.
Applying this to the displayed factorizations, or taking the
coefficient identity $\det(I+zAB)=\det(I+zBA)$, yields
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 a_N&=\tr(PQ),& b_N&=\det P\det Q,\\
 a_{H_0}&=4\tr(PJ_g^*QJ_g),&
 b_{H_0}&=16g^2\det P\det Q,\qquad
 \eta_1\eta_2=4g\chi_1\chi_2.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{RS10}
\end{equation}
For example $\det J_g=g$, so the last determinant factor is
$4^2|\det J_g|^2=16g^2$. These are finite two-by-two formulas,
not unresolved singular-value problems. To make their entry use
explicit, indices $0,1,2,3$ refer to the original monomials and
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 Q_{11}&=g^2G_{00}+g(G_{02}+G_{20})+G_{22},\\
 Q_{12}&=g^2G_{01}+gG_{03}+gG_{21}+G_{23},\\
 Q_{22}&=g^2G_{11}+g(G_{13}+G_{31})+G_{33},\\
 P_{11}&=(G^{-1})_{00}-g((G^{-1})_{02}+(G^{-1})_{20})
                                      +g^2(G^{-1})_{22},\\
 P_{12}&=(G^{-1})_{01}-g(G^{-1})_{03}-g(G^{-1})_{21}
                                      +g^2(G^{-1})_{23},\\
 P_{22}&=(G^{-1})_{11}-g((G^{-1})_{13}+(G^{-1})_{31})
                                      +g^2(G^{-1})_{33},
 \quad P_{21}=\overline{P_{12}},\quad Q_{21}=\overline{Q_{12}}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{RS11}
\end{equation}

We next evaluate all four singular values of $R_2$.
Let $\mathcal L=\im N$. Its dimension is two and $N^2=0$,
so $\mathcal L=\ker N$. The $G$-orthogonal decomposition
$\C^4=\mathcal L\oplus\mathcal L^{\perp_G}$ gives
\begin{equation}
 N=\begin{pmatrix}0&S\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 gI+N=\begin{pmatrix}gI&S\\0&gI\end{pmatrix},
 \tag{RS12}
\end{equation}
where $S:\mathcal L^{\perp_G}\to\mathcal L$ is invertible.
Use orthonormal bases on these two actual metric subspaces.
Diagonalize the positive matrix $S^*S$, then take the normalized
images under $S$ as the basis on $\mathcal L$. This makes $S$
diagonal with positive entries $\chi_1,\chi_2$ by isometries.
It does not alter the original metric. After interleaving the
two pairs, $gI+N$ is the orthogonal direct sum of
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}g&\chi_j\\0&g\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
The squared singular-value sum of that two-by-two matrix is
$2g^2+\chi_j^2$, and its singular-value product is $g^2$.
The two positive numbers
$\bigl(\sqrt{4g^2+\chi_j^2}\pm\chi_j\bigr)/2$
have exactly that sum of squares and product, so they are
its two singular values. Consequently the four constants are
\begin{equation}
 \zeta_{j,\pm}
 =\frac{\sqrt{4g^2+\chi_j^2}\pm\chi_j}{64g^3}>0,
 \qquad j=1,2.
 \tag{RS13}
\end{equation}

Form the six positive numbers and the two finite constants
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal C=
 \left\{\frac{\eta_1}{32g^2},\frac{\eta_2}{32g^2},
 \zeta_{1,+},\zeta_{1,-},\zeta_{2,+},\zeta_{2,-}\right\},
 \qquad d_1=\frac1{2\eta_2},\quad d_2=\frac1{2\eta_1}.
 \tag{RS14}
\end{equation}
Write $c_1\ge\cdots\ge c_6$ for the decreasing ordering of
the displayed six numbers; $d_1\ge d_2$. Every ordered singular
value of $\Theta_w^{-1}$ in $G_8$ is now determined:
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_j(\Theta_w^{-1})=\frac{c_j}{w}(1+o(1)),\quad 1\le j\le6,
 \qquad
 \sigma_7(\Theta_w^{-1})=d_1(1+o(1)),\quad
 \sigma_8(\Theta_w^{-1})=d_2(1+o(1)).
 \tag{RS15}
\end{equation}
Here is a proof covering the finite constants as well as the
divergent ones. In $\diag(G,G)$, the squared singular spectrum
of a block matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&A\\B&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$
is the union of the squared singular spectra of $A$ and $B$,
because its metric adjoint times itself is block diagonal.
For the upper-right block of RS4 the singular values are the
reciprocals of twice the singular values of $H_w$. The two
largest singular values of $H_w$ tend to $\eta_1,\eta_2$,
so two inverse singular values tend to $d_2,d_1$.
Multiplying this block by $w$ gives $R_1$, which has precisely
two positive singular values $\eta_j/(32g^2)$. Thus its other
two singular values diverge with those constants. The lower-left
block times $w$ tends to the invertible $R_2$, whose four
singular values are RS13. Singular values are continuous under
matrix convergence: the minimum-maximum description of the
singular values gives their change bounded by the operator norm
of the difference. Finally $|c|=1$, so its unchanged phase in
RS4 changes none of these singular values. This proves RS15.

\section{Every exterior rank and the exact determinant}
The exterior norm in the metric induced by $G_8$ is the product
of the largest singular values. Indeed orthonormal singular
bases induce orthonormal wedge bases, and the matrix entries
on those bases are all corresponding products. Define
\begin{equation}
 C_0=1,\qquad C_r=\prod_{j=1}^r c_j
 =\max_{\substack{I\subset\{1,\ldots,6\}\\|I|=r}}
                         \prod_{j\in I}\mathcal C_j,
 \quad1\le r\le6.
 \tag{RS16}
\end{equation}
The maximum is a finite explicit expression in the radicals
RS8 and RS13 and makes no assumption about their ordering for
different original moments. For $r=6$ the product evaluates to
\begin{equation}
 \zeta_{j,+}\zeta_{j,-}=\frac1{2^{10}g^4},\qquad
 C_6=\frac{\eta_1\eta_2}{2^{30}g^{12}}
     =\frac{\sqrt{b_{H_0}}}{2^{30}g^{12}},\qquad
 C_6d_1=\frac{\eta_1}{2^{31}g^{12}}.
 \tag{RS17}
\end{equation}
The first equality follows by subtracting the two squares in
the numerator of RS13. Multiplying its two instances and
the first two entries of RS14 proves the others.

The determinant is also exact for every $w>0$:
\begin{equation}
 \det H_w=256g^2w^2(g+w)^2,\qquad
 \det\Theta_w=2^{32}g^6w^6(g+w)^6,\qquad
 \det\Theta_w^{-1}=\frac1{2^{32}g^6w^6(g+w)^6}.
 \tag{RS18}
\end{equation}
For the first formula, the roots of the monic quartic in RS1
are $\varepsilon\sqrt w+i\eta\sqrt g$, with independent signs.
At each root, the factor $x^2+g-w$ equals
$2i\varepsilon\eta\sqrt{gw}$. Their product is $16g^2w^2$;
$\det(-2X_w)=16(g+w)^2$. Multiplication gives the first
identity. The determinant of the two swapped four-by-four
blocks of $\mathsf J_w$ is $2^8(\det H_w)^3$, with positive
block-swap sign. Also $c^8=1$. This proves both remaining
identities, including their phases.

Combining RS15--18 gives every inverse exterior asymptote:
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \|\wedge^0\Theta_w^{-1}\|_{G_8}&=1,\\
 \|\wedge^r\Theta_w^{-1}\|_{G_8}&\sim C_rw^{-r},&&1\le r\le6,\\
 \|\wedge^7\Theta_w^{-1}\|_{G_8}&\sim
                       \frac{\eta_1}{2^{31}g^{12}}w^{-6},\\
 \|\wedge^8\Theta_w^{-1}\|_{G_8}&=
                       \frac1{2^{32}g^6w^6(g+w)^6}.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{RS19}
\end{equation}
The complete leading product is
$C_6d_1d_2=1/(2^{32}g^{12})$, agreeing with RS18.
For the original variable $\delta$, every exponent is
$2\min(r,6)$, with exactly the constants displayed above.

\section{The exact Gram furnished by the original source}
We now evaluate $G$, rather than leave it as an auxiliary
positive matrix. Keep the original conductor $L=T_A$ on
\begin{equation}
 U=\mathcal P_{v+3}/\mathcal P_{v-1},\qquad
 W=\mathcal P_3,\qquad
 c_*=a_{\rm amp}/2-4,\quad c_0=c_*+4,\quad
 b=s/2>0,\quad M=(2\pi)^{s/2}.
 \tag{RS20}
\end{equation}
The source has its minimum quotient norm at center $c_0$;
the target has its original Gamma norm at center $c_*$.
Both use the same fixed original order $s$ in the allowed
programme domain. In target coordinates
$y=(S'-c_*)/i$, $x=S'-c_*=iy$.
Thus the spectral coordinate $x=r-1/2$ is sent by the actual
dictionary $r=S'-\kappa$, $\kappa=c_*-1/2$, to exactly
$S'-c_*$. No center is set to zero in the original spaces.

Write $\mu_n=E_A^{(n)}(0)$ and let $v$ be its actual
vanishing order, so $\mu_j=0$ for $j<v$ and $\mu_v\ne0$.
To distinguish the conductor coefficients from $g=\gamma^2$,
use $\mathfrak g_j$ for the coefficients of its exact FC20 unit:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 z(q)=-i\arctan q,\qquad
 \mathfrak g(q)=q^{-v}e^{-4z(q)}E_A(z(q)),\\
 \nu_{v+j}=\sum_{k=0}^{j}\binom{v+j}{v+k}(-4)^{j-k}\mu_{v+k},\\
 \mathfrak g_0=(-i)^v\mu_v/v!,\qquad
 \mathfrak g_1=(-i)^{v+1}\nu_{v+1}/(v+1)!,\\
 \mathfrak g_2=(-i)^{v+2}\nu_{v+2}/(v+2)!-(v/3)\mathfrak g_0,\\
 \mathfrak g_3=(-i)^{v+3}\nu_{v+3}/(v+3)!-((v+1)/3)\mathfrak g_1,\\
 \xi_0=\mathfrak g_0^{-1},\quad
 \xi_1=-\mathfrak g_1\mathfrak g_0^{-2},\quad
 \xi_2=\mathfrak g_1^2\mathfrak g_0^{-3}-\mathfrak g_2\mathfrak g_0^{-2},\\
 \xi_3=-\mathfrak g_1^3\mathfrak g_0^{-4}
       +2\mathfrak g_1\mathfrak g_2\mathfrak g_0^{-3}
       -\mathfrak g_3\mathfrak g_0^{-2}.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{RS21}
\end{equation}
The finite moment formula for $\nu$ follows by multiplying
$e^{-4z}E_A(z)$ as power series. Since
$z(q)^n=(-i)^nq^n(1-nq^2/3+O(q^4))$, it gives every
$\mathfrak g_j$ displayed through degree three. Multiplying
the two cubic truncations $\sum\mathfrak g_jq^j$ and
$\sum\xi_jq^j$ gives $1$ modulo $q^4$, proving all inverse
coefficients with their original phases.

For completeness the original orthonormal source polynomials
and their minimum quotient representatives are
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 p_0(y)=1,\quad p_1(y)=y,\quad
 p_{n+1}(y)=yp_n(y)-n(n-1+b)p_{n-1}(y),\\
 \phi_n(S)=\frac{p_n((S-c_0)/i)}{\sqrt{Mn!(b)_n}},\qquad
 [\phi_v],\ldots,[\phi_{v+3}]\text{ an orthonormal frame of }U,\\
 \rho_n=\sqrt{n!/(b)_n},\qquad
 k_j=\rho_{v+j}^{-2}=(b)_{v+j}/(v+j)!,\quad0\le j\le3.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{RS22}
\end{equation}
The orthogonality and original measure are the fixed Gamma
dictionary of \cite{WCF,DLMF18Source}; its squared norms include $M$.
Each listed polynomial is orthogonal to $\mathcal P_{v-1}$.
Consequently Pythagoras proves that its linear combinations
are precisely the minimum-norm representatives of their classes.

In the original source and target orthonormal frames FC24 has
entry $\xi_{j-r}\rho_j/\rho_{v+r}$ for $0\le r\le j\le3$.
To evaluate its action on monomials, the target orthonormal
coordinate columns of $(1,y,y^2,y^3)$ are
\begin{equation}
 V_W=\begin{pmatrix}
 \sqrt M&0&b\sqrt M&0\\
 0&\sqrt{Mb}&0&(3b+2)\sqrt{Mb}\\
 0&0&\sqrt{2Mb(b+1)}&0\\
 0&0&0&\sqrt{6Mb(b+1)(b+2)}
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{RS23}
\end{equation}
Indeed $p_2=y^2-b$ and $p_3=y^3-(3b+2)y$ follow
from RS22. Multiplying these columns by the complete FC24
inverse, and using
$\rho_j\sqrt{Mj!(b)_j}=\sqrt M\,j!$, gives
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 F=\begin{pmatrix}
 \xi_0&\xi_1&b\xi_0+2\xi_2&(3b+2)\xi_1+6\xi_3\\
 0&\xi_0&2\xi_1&(3b+2)\xi_0+6\xi_2\\
 0&0&2\xi_0&6\xi_1\\0&0&0&6\xi_0
 \end{pmatrix},\quad D_i=\diag(1,i,-1,-i),\\
 U_x=\sqrt M\diag(\sqrt{k_0},\sqrt{k_1},\sqrt{k_2},\sqrt{k_3})FD_i,\\
 L^{-1}(S'-c_*)^j=\sum_{r=0}^3(U_x)_{rj}[\phi_{v+r}],\qquad
 G_{U,x}=U_x^*U_x=M D_i^*F^*\diag(k_0,k_1,k_2,k_3)FD_i.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{RS24}
\end{equation}
The additional $D_i$ is required because $x^j=i^jy^j$.
Thus $G_{U,x}$ is the full original source Gram in precisely
the $x$ coefficients of RS1. The subscript records this receiver;
the programme Gram $\Phi^*\langle\, ,\,\rangle_U\Phi$ in
original ES coordinates keeps its existing notation.

Its entries and inverse can be evaluated without a numerical
matrix inversion. In addition to
$(G_{U,x})_{pq}=M(-i)^p i^q
\sum_{r=0}^{\min(p,q)}k_r\overline{F_{rp}}F_{rq}$,
the complete inverse of $F$ is
\begin{equation}
 B_F=\begin{pmatrix}
 \mathfrak g_0&\mathfrak g_1&\mathfrak g_2-b\mathfrak g_0/2
                       &\mathfrak g_3-b\mathfrak g_1/2\\
 0&\mathfrak g_0&\mathfrak g_1
                       &\mathfrak g_2-(3b+2)\mathfrak g_0/6\\
 0&0&\mathfrak g_0/2&\mathfrak g_1/2\\
 0&0&0&\mathfrak g_0/6
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 G_{U,x}^{-1}=M^{-1}D_i^*B_F\diag(k_j^{-1})B_F^*D_i.
 \tag{RS25}
\end{equation}
To prove the inverse, write
$F=T_3(\xi)V_0$, where $V_0$ has diagonal $(1,1,2,6)$,
entry $(0,2)$ equal to $b$ and entry $(1,3)$ equal to $3b+2$.
All other off-diagonal entries vanish. The reciprocal identity
in RS21 gives $T_3(\xi)^{-1}=T_3(\mathfrak g)$, and direct
upper-triangular multiplication gives $V_0^{-1}T_3(\mathfrak g)=B_F$.
Thus $FB_F=B_FF=I$, proving RS25. All original cross terms
remain in both products RS24--25. Positivity and invertibility
also follow from the exact determinant
\begin{equation}
 \det G_{U,x}=144M^4 k_0k_1k_2k_3|\xi_0|^8>0.
 \tag{RS26}
\end{equation}
In fact all four leading minors are respectively
$Mk_0|\xi_0|^2$, $M^2k_0k_1|\xi_0|^4$,
$4M^3k_0k_1k_2|\xi_0|^6$ and RS26, by the triangular
diagonal of $F$. The phases in $D_i$ have modulus one.

Substituting RS24--25 into RS9--11 and then RS8, RS13--19
therefore evaluates all eight source singular constants in
finite original moments, Gamma weights and real radicals.
Every matrix used in this substitution has all its entries
displayed. The common $M$ cancels from $G_{U,x}^{-1}A^*G_{U,x}A$
because it occurs on both sides of the same operator norm;
it remains unchanged in the source norms RS24 and RS26.

\section{The exact conductor receiver and the completed estimate}
Define the coefficient receivers, on each of the two parity
summands, by
\begin{equation}
 C_Wp=p(S'-c_*),\qquad C_Up=L^{-1}p(S'-c_*),\qquad
 \mathcal C_W=C_W\oplus C_W,\quad
 \mathcal C_U=C_U\oplus C_U.
 \tag{RS27}
\end{equation}
They are isomorphisms from the coefficient space of $S_w$
to $W^2$ and $U^2$ respectively; the inverse of $C_W$ is
$q(S')\mapsto q(x+c_*)$, and that of $C_U$ is
$u\mapsto (Lu)(x+c_*)$. The full inverse matrix in the original
source frame is supplied by RS24--25. The original relation is
$(L\oplus L)\mathcal C_U=\mathcal C_W$.
With $G=G_{U,x}$ in RS7--19, $\mathcal C_U$ is an isometry
from $\diag(G_{U,x},G_{U,x})$ onto the original quotient direct sum,
by its proved Gram RS24. The transported operation and its
inverse are consequently
\begin{equation}
 \Theta_{U,w}=\mathcal C_U\Theta_w\mathcal C_U^{-1},\quad
 \Theta_{U,w}^{-1}=\mathcal C_U\Theta_w^{-1}\mathcal C_U^{-1},\quad
 (L\oplus L)\Theta_{U,w}=\Theta_{W,w}(L\oplus L),
 \quad\Theta_{W,w}=\mathcal C_W\Theta_w\mathcal C_W^{-1}.
 \tag{RS28}
\end{equation}
Substitution and cancellation of the stated inverse maps prove
all identities. The singular constants of $\Theta_{U,w}^{-1}$
are exactly RS14--15 evaluated at the explicit RS24 Gram,
and every original-source exterior norm is exactly RS19 with
that substitution. No condition-number bound replaces a source
constant. The determinant RS18 is unchanged by this similarity.

As a direct equation-(11) consequence, for every rank $1\le r\le8$
the product
\begin{equation}
 w^{\min(r,6)}\|\wedge^r\Theta_{U,w}^{-1}\|_U
 \longrightarrow
 \begin{cases}
 C_r(G_{U,x}),&1\le r\le6,\\
 \eta_1(G_{U,x})/(2^{31}g^{12}),&r=7,\\
 1/(2^{32}g^{12}),&r=8.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{RS29}
\end{equation}
Every limit is strictly positive by RS8 and RS13. Hence this
specific completed trace-to-residue operation has no uniform
inverse exterior bound as the paired collision is approached.
The locus of this loss is the explicit multiplier RS3, whose
rank at $w=0$ is two. The original $L$ remains the fixed
isomorphism with determinant FC23; its nonzero leading moment
$\mu_v$ is retained in RS21--26. Equations RS27--28 are the exact
map between these two statements and do not change the original
conductor's order.
