\section{The exact original coefficient map and Gram}
Fix $\sigma>1$, let $\omega=\sigma+i\tau$ with $\tau\in\R$,
and retain
\[
 h=2\sigma-1,\qquad d=\sigma-\tfrac12=h/2,\qquad
 b=s/2>0,\qquad M=(2\pi)^{s/2}.
 \tag{NH1}
\]
Here $s$ is the fixed original Gamma parameter. The original measure is
\[
 dm_s(y)=\frac{M2^{s/2}}{4\pi\Gamma(s/2)}
               |\Gamma(s/4+iy/2)|^2dy.
 \tag{NH2}
\]
It has the original moments
\[
 m_0=M,\quad m_2=Mb,\quad m_4=M(3b^2+2b),\quad
 m_6=M(15b^3+30b^2+16b),\qquad m_1=m_3=m_5=0.
 \tag{NH3}
\]
The exact polynomials in the rational receiver are
\[
 N_j(z)=\sqrt h\,(z+\bar\omega-1)^j(\omega-z)^{3-j},
 \qquad 0\le j\le3.
 \tag{NH4}
\]
For the original physical target center $c_*$ put
$\kappa=c_*-1/2$. Define the actual numerator map
\[
 \mathcal M_\omega:\C^4\longrightarrow\mathcal P_3,
 \qquad \mathcal M_\omega c(S')=\sum_{j=0}^3c_jN_j(S'-\kappa).
 \tag{NH5}
\]
The target has its unchanged norm
$\|p\|_\Gamma^2=\int|p(c_*+iy)|^2dm_s(y)$.
In NH5, the domain is the displayed coefficient space with its
ordinary Euclidean norm when singular values are computed below.
This declaration identifies the operator whose singular values are
being evaluated; it changes no original target norm.

At $S'=c_*+iy$, the polynomial in NH4 is
$\sqrt h(d+i(y-\tau))^j(d-i(y-\tau))^{3-j}$.
Let $Q(\tau)$ be its coefficient matrix in $(1,y,y^2,y^3)$ after
factoring out the displayed constant $\sqrt h$ from the formula.
Its complete entries, still retaining all phases, are
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
d^3+3id^2\tau-3d\tau^2-i\tau^3&d^3+id^2\tau+d\tau^2+i\tau^3&
d^3-id^2\tau+d\tau^2-i\tau^3&d^3-3id^2\tau-3d\tau^2+i\tau^3\\
-3id^2+6d\tau+3i\tau^2&-id^2-2d\tau-3i\tau^2&
id^2-2d\tau+3i\tau^2&3id^2+6d\tau-3i\tau^2\\
-3d-3i\tau&d+3i\tau&d-3i\tau&-3d+3i\tau\\
i&-i&i&-i
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{NH6}
\]
The Gram of the displayed $y$-monomials is exactly
\[
 K=M\begin{pmatrix}
 1&0&b&0\\0&b&0&3b^2+2b\\
 b&0&3b^2+2b&0\\0&3b^2+2b&0&15b^3+30b^2+16b
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{NH7}
\]
It is positive definite: every nonzero polynomial has positive
integral of its squared modulus against the strictly positive
density NH2. Direct computation also gives the positive leading
principal determinants
\[
 M,\quad M^2b,\quad2M^3b^2(b+1),\quad
 12M^4b^3(b+1)^2(b+2).
 \tag{NH8}
\]
Consequently the original coefficient Gram is the exact matrix
\[
 H_\omega=hQ(\tau)^*KQ(\tau),\qquad
 (H_\omega)_{ij}=h\int(d+i(y-\tau))^{3+j-i}
                      (d-i(y-\tau))^{3-j+i}dm_s(y).
 \tag{NH9}
\]
The second identity follows by conjugating the $i$th polynomial
before multiplying by the $j$th. It proves the native Toeplitz
formula and keeps every shifted odd moment as well as the even ones.

The determinant of NH6 is $-64d^6$, for every $\tau$.
One proof is the translation factorization in NH11 below and
direct expansion at $\tau=0$. Hence
\[
 \det H_\omega
 =h^4(64d^6)^2\det K
 =12h^{16}M^4b^3(b+1)^2(b+2)>0.
 \tag{NH10}
\]
The coefficient map $\sqrt h Q$ in the $y$-basis has determinant
$-h^8$. The receiver matrix $R_\omega^{(3)}$ in the original
$z$-monomial basis has determinant $+h^8$: substitution
$z=1/2+iy$ has determinant $i^{0+1+2+3}=-1$. Thus NH10
agrees with RW11 with its original sign conventions, not only
with its determinant modulus.

\section{All compound leading matrices}
Use row and column indices $0,1,2,3$, preserving the ordered
coefficient basis. Define the full translation matrix
\[
 T(\tau)_{k\ell}=\begin{cases}
 \binom\ell k(-\tau)^{\ell-k},&k\le\ell,\\0,&k>\ell.
 \end{cases}
 \qquad Q(\tau)=T(\tau)Q(0).
 \tag{NH11}
\]
Indeed substituting $y-\tau$ into each degree-$\ell$ monomial
gives exactly the indicated coefficient column. This identity
proves every entry and keeps the actual shift rather than discarding
it in the measure.

For increasing $r$-subsets $I,J\subset\{0,1,2,3\}$, the
$(I,J)$ entry of $\wedge^rT$ is
\[
 (-\tau)^{\sum J-\sum I}
       \det\bigl(\tbinom j i\bigr)_{i\in I,j\in J},
 \tag{NH12}
\]
whenever the coefficient determinant is nonzero. This follows
by factoring the powers out of each row and column of the
minor. Its maximal possible degree is
$m_r=r(4-r)$, attained only at
$I_r=\{0,\ldots,r-1\}$ and $J_r=\{4-r,\ldots,3\}$.
The binomial minors at those positions are one: in degrees
one and four this is immediate, in degree two the matrix is
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\2&3\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
and in degree three it is
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1&1\\1&2&3\\0&1&3\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
Each determinant is one. All other nonzero minors have strictly
smaller degree, so there is no omitted term of a higher order.

Here are convenient unchanged input vectors:
\[
 v_1=(1,-1,1,-1)^T,\quad v_2=(3,-1,-1,3)^T,\quad
 v_3=(1,1,-1,-1)^T,\quad v_4=(1,3,3,1)^T.
 \tag{NH13}
\]
They have Gram $\diag(4,20,4,20)$ in coefficient Euclidean
coordinates. Multiplying the unique leading row of each compound
in NH12 by $\wedge^rQ(0)$ gives the following full limits:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 r&m_r&\lim_{\tau\to\pm\infty}\tau^{-m_r}\wedge^rQ(\tau)\\\hline
 1&3&-i\,e_0v_1^{\mathsf T}\\
 2&4&id\,e_{01}(v_1\wedge v_2)^{\mathsf T}\\
 3&3&-2d^3\,e_{012}(v_1\wedge v_2\wedge v_3)^{\mathsf T}\\
 4&0&-64d^6
\end{array}.
\tag{NH14}
\]
For direct coefficient verification, the three nonzero row
vectors are respectively
\[
 (-i,i,-i,i),\qquad
 (2id,-4id,6id,2id,-4id,2id),\qquad
 (-8d^3,24d^3,-24d^3,8d^3).
\]
The compound subsets use increasing lexicographic order. These
rows follow by minors of $Q(0)$; all other rows vanish in the
limit by NH12. The limit with $\tau^{-m_r}$ retains the sign
for negative $\tau$; its squared Gram limits use $|\tau|^{2m_r}$
and therefore coincide on both ends.

\section{Full eigenvalue and native singular-value asymptotics}

\[

 \tag{NH15}
\]

NH15 are rank one for $r=1,2,3$. If $w_2=v_1\wedge v_2$
and $w_3=v_1\wedge v_2\wedge v_3$, they are
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\tau|^{-6}H_\omega&\longrightarrow hM v_1v_1^{\mathsf T},\\
 |\tau|^{-8}\wedge^2H_\omega&\longrightarrow
              h^2d^2M^2b\,w_2w_2^{\mathsf T},\\
 |\tau|^{-6}\wedge^3H_\omega&\longrightarrow
              8h^3d^6M^3b^2(b+1)\,w_3w_3^{\mathsf T}.
\end{aligned}\tag{NH16}
\]
Their nonzero eigenvalues are their traces. Orthogonality in
NH13 gives $\|v_1\|^2=4$, $\|w_2\|^2=80$ and
$\|w_3\|^2=320$. Substituting the retained identity $d=h/2$
therefore yields all four largest compound-eigenvalue constants
\[
 C_1=4hM,\qquad C_2=20h^4M^2b,\qquad
 C_3=40h^9M^3b^2(b+1),\qquad
 C_4=12h^{16}M^4b^3(b+1)^2(b+2).
 \tag{NH17}
\]
Here $C_4$ is the exact determinant NH10. Every constant is
strictly positive, with the entire original mass $M$ retained.

Let $\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\lambda_3\ge\lambda_4>0$
be the eigenvalues of the displayed coefficient matrix $H_\omega$
with respect to coefficient Euclidean coordinates. A positive
matrix has an orthonormal eigenbasis, whose coordinate wedges
diagonalize its compounds with all products of eigenvalues.
Consequently $\|\wedge^rH_\omega\|=\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_r$.
Continuity of the norm in NH16 and successive ratios of the
constants NH17 prove the complete spectrum
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \lambda_1&\sim4hM|\tau|^6,\\
 \lambda_2&\sim5h^3Mb|\tau|^2,\\
 \lambda_3&\sim2h^5Mb(b+1)|\tau|^{-2},\\
 \lambda_4&\sim\tfrac3{10}h^7Mb(b+1)(b+2)|\tau|^{-6}.
\end{aligned}}\tag{NH18}

The product of the four constants is precisely NH10. This
also gives the exact coefficient condition-number asymptote
\[
 \frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_4}
 \sim\frac{40}{3h^6b(b+1)(b+2)}|\tau|^{12}.
 \tag{NH19}
\]

Because the Gram of the typed numerator operator NH5 is
$H_\omega$, its singular values are $\sqrt{\lambda_j}$.
Thus they are, in decreasing order,
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}

 s_2(\mathcal M_\omega)&\sim\sqrt{5h^3Mb}|\tau|,\\

 s_4(\mathcal M_\omega)&\sim\sqrt{\tfrac3{10}h^7Mb(b+1)(b+2)}|\tau|^{-3}.
\end{aligned}}\tag{NH20}
\]
Its inverse uses the original native space as domain and the

\[
 \|\wedge^r\mathcal M_\omega^{-1}\|
 \sim\sqrt{\frac{C_{4-r}}{C_4}}\,
            |\tau|^{m_{4-r}},\qquad 0\le r\le4,
 \quad C_0=1, m_0=m_4=0.
 \tag{NH21}

This follows either by complementary products of the four
