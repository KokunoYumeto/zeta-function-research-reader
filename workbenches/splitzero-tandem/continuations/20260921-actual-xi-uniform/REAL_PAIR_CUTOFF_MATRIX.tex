\section{Both inverse singular rates at the original growing cutoffs}
This section uses the exact factorization RLA6 \cite{RLA008} and the full original
Gamma frames of WCF1--6 \cite{WCF,DLMF18Source}. The complete RLA source accompanies
this continuation; WCF has its
\href{https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/zeta-function-research-reader/blob/5992ad50c0f2a06921f1fcaded7b4e38097c0739/workbenches/splitzero-tandem/continuations/20260920-original-kernel-mixed-determinants/providers/WEIGHTED_CONDUCTOR_FORWARD.tex#L28}{pinned original proof}.
The general principal-part rank decomposition and its lower corrections
are already WCF15--18. Here their exact two-root specialization yields
both singular rates and the retained unit contribution.
It calculates a limit in the cutoff with
the actual period and unit held fixed.  All four ES states and the
original unit remain those of RLA1--35.  The local parameter choices
below are points of that proved geometric family; no identification
with an arithmetic zeta quartet is made.

Choose fixed radii $0<r_0<R<1$ and a sufficiently small closed
parameter neighborhood of the certified point such that the full
unit $U(t,p)$ in RLA6 is holomorphic and nonzero for $|t|\le R$,
and both actual roots obey $|\alpha|,|\beta|<r_0$.
Such choices exist by RLA5--6 and compactness after shrinking
the original analytic neighborhoods.  They are independent of $D$.
Throughout the inverse calculation fix an actual parameter in this
neighborhood with $\alpha\beta\ne0$ and put
\[
 F(t)=U(t,p)^{-1},\quad
 r=\min(|\alpha|,|\beta|),\quad
 s_r=\max(|\alpha|,|\beta|),\quad
 M_R=\max_{|t|=R}|F(t)|.
 \tag{RCM1}
\]
The symbol $s_r$ is a root modulus, not the original source order
$s$. The function $F$ here is the reciprocal conductor unit; AUH2's
entire centered Xi function is a different, explicitly defined function.
All constants in the following formulas refer to these exact
functions, not to assigned residues or a changed symbol.

\subsection{Two complete principal parts and the remaining analytic function}
For distinct roots let $L(t)$ be the exact linear interpolation of
$F$ at $\alpha,\beta$; for equal roots let it be its first Taylor
polynomial there.  The identity
\[
 \frac1{g(t,p)}=\frac{L(t)}{(t-\alpha)(t-\beta)}+H(t),\qquad
 H(t)=\frac{F(t)-L(t)}{(t-\alpha)(t-\beta)}
 \tag{RCM2}
\]
retains the entire original unit.  The apparent singularities of
$H$ are removable by the interpolation identities, including the
double-root case.  An exact formula, including that case, is
\[
 H(t)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{|z|=R}
       \frac{F(z)}{(z-\alpha)(z-\beta)(z-t)}\,dz,
 \qquad |t|<R.
 \tag{RCM3}
\]
For distinct roots this follows by adding the residues at
$z=t,\alpha,\beta$: the residue at $t$ is $F(t)/q(t)$;
the other two together are $-L(t)/q(t)$.  For a repeated root
the residue is the derivative at that root and gives its first
Taylor polynomial.  The formula can also be obtained by a limit
of the distinct-root formula, uniformly on a smaller circle.
Taking the Taylor coefficient of the Cauchy kernel proves
\[
 h_n=[t^n]g^{-1}=P_n+e_n,\qquad
 |e_n|\le C_R R^{-n},\quad C_R=\frac{M_R}{(R-r_0)^2},
 \tag{RCM4}
\]
for every integer $n\ge0$ simultaneously.  For example the
coefficient integral has denominator $z^{n+1}$; its circle length
$2\pi R$ gives exactly the displayed bound.

The full principal coefficient is
\[
 P_n=\begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\frac{F(\beta)\beta^{-n-1}-F(\alpha)\alpha^{-n-1}}
                         {\alpha-\beta},&\alpha\ne\beta,\\[6pt]
 \displaystyle (n+1)F(\alpha)\alpha^{-n-2}
                  -F'(\alpha)\alpha^{-n-1},&\alpha=\beta.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{RCM5}
\]
Geometric expansion of the simple pole terms, or of the double
pole and its derivative term, proves this formula.  It also
defines $P_n$ for every negative integer $n$, an algebraic
extension that will be used for a finite matrix identity only.
It does not insert negative Taylor coefficients into $g^{-1}$.

Write $C_0=\max_{|t|\le r_0}|F(t)|$ and
$C_1=\max_{|t|\le r_0}|F'(t)|$.  Cauchy's formula gives
$C_0\le M_R R/(R-r_0)$ and
$C_1\le M_R R/(R-r_0)^2$.
For the full interpolation $L(t)=A_0+A_1t$, integration along
the segment between the roots gives $|A_1|\le C_1$ and
$|A_0|\le C_0+r_0C_1$, also for a repeated root.
Expanding both quadratic factors as geometric series therefore
gives the all-$n$ bound
\[
 |P_n|\le(n+1)(C_0+2r_0C_1)r^{-n-2}\quad(n\ge0).
 \tag{RCM6}
\]
Indeed the reciprocal quadratic coefficient is a sum of $n+1$
terms, each bounded by $r^{-n-2}$; the linear part contributes
at most $nC_1r^{-n-1}$.  These retain both original root moduli;
using their minimum here is an explicitly proved upper bound.

\subsection{The exact finite rank-two matrix and its full correction}
Let $N=D+1$ and $T_D(h)_{ij}=h_{j-i}$ for $j\ge i$, zero
otherwise.  Define $\mathcal P_D=(P_{j-i})_{0\le i,j\le D}$
using the full integer formula RCM5.  Then
\[
 T_D(h)=\mathcal P_D+\mathcal E_D-\mathcal L_D,
 \qquad
 (\mathcal E_D)_{ij}=\boldsymbol1_{j\ge i}e_{j-i},\quad
 (\mathcal L_D)_{ij}=\boldsymbol1_{i>j}P_{j-i}.
 \tag{RCM7}
\]
This is equality of every matrix entry.  In the distinct-root
case $\mathcal P_D$ is the sum of the two outer products
\[
 -\frac{F(\alpha)}{(\alpha-\beta)\alpha}
   (\alpha^i)_i(\alpha^{-j})_j^{\mathsf T}
 +\frac{F(\beta)}{(\alpha-\beta)\beta}
   (\beta^i)_i(\beta^{-j})_j^{\mathsf T}.
 \tag{RCM8}
\]
It has rank at most two.  At coalescence RCM5 is
$\alpha^{i-j-2}((j-i+1)F(\alpha)-\alpha F'(\alpha))$;
grouping the terms constant and linear in $i$ gives two outer
products again.  Thus the same rank bound holds at coalescence
without a singular divided-difference representation.

For $k\ge1$, apply the segment derivative formula to
$F(z)z^{k-1}$ in RCM5.  It proves
\[
 |P_{-k}|\le C_1r_0^{k-1}+
   \boldsymbol1_{k\ge2}C_0(k-1)r_0^{k-2},\quad
 \|\mathcal L_D\|\le
 K_-:=\frac{C_1}{1-r_0}+\frac{C_0}{(1-r_0)^2},\quad
 \|\mathcal E_D\|\le\frac{C_R R^{-D}}{1-R}.
 \tag{RCM9}
\]
For the matrix bounds each shift along a fixed diagonal has norm
at most one; sum its absolute coefficient.  The geometric series
and its derivative give $K_-$, and
$\sum_{n=0}^D R^{-n}\le R^{-D}/(1-R)$ gives the last bound.
Hence every correction to the rank-two expression is explicit,
with constants independent of the cutoff.

\subsection{Both singular growth rates, including phase cancellations}
Two identities obtained by multiplying RCM5 are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \alpha P_n-P_{n-1}&=F(\beta)\beta^{-n-1},\\
 \beta P_n-P_{n-1}&=F(\alpha)\alpha^{-n-1},\qquad n\ge1,\\
 P_nP_{n+2}-P_{n+1}^2
     &=-\frac{F(\alpha)F(\beta)}{(\alpha\beta)^{n+3}}.
 \end{aligned}\tag{RCM10}
\]
Substitution in RCM5 proves the first two identities term by term.
For the last, write $P_n=A\alpha^{-n}+B\beta^{-n}$ as in
RCM8.  The two square terms cancel; the remaining term is
$AB(\alpha^{-1}-\beta^{-1})^2(\alpha\beta)^{-n}$,
which is the stated expression.  Its limit proves the same
identity for a repeated root; direct expansion gives it too.

Since $F$ has no zero, set $m_F=\min(|F(\alpha)|,|F(\beta)|)>0$.
Choose the second or first identity in RCM10 according to which
root has modulus $r$.  RCM4 gives, for every $n\ge1$,
\[
 (1+r_0)\max(|h_n|,|h_{n-1}|)
 \ge m_Fr^{-n-1}-C_R(r_0+R)R^{-n}.
 \tag{RCM11}
\]
The error-to-main ratio is at most
$C_R(r_0+R)r_0(r_0/R)^n/m_F$, which tends to zero independently
of $D$.  In particular half the first term remains for every
$n$ beyond the explicit threshold
\[
 n_0=\max\left\{1,
 \left\lceil
  \frac{\max\{0,\log(2C_R(r_0+R)r_0/m_F)\}}
       {\log(R/r_0)}
 \right\rceil\right\}.
 \tag{RCM12}
\]
Thus cancellations of a single coefficient cannot remove the
exponential growth at arbitrarily large cutoffs.  Every coefficient
appears as an entry in the upper triangular matrix.  RCM6 and
RCM4 give an upper bound with only a quadratic factor in $D$
times $r^{-D-2}$.  RCM11 with $n=D$ gives the matching lower
exponential rate, so for its largest singular value
\[
 \lim_{D\to\infty}\sigma_1(T_D(h))^{1/D}=r^{-1}.
 \tag{RCM13}
\]

For the second singular value, the corner minor with rows $0,1$
and columns $D-1,D$ is $h_{D-1}^2-h_Dh_{D-2}$.
RCM10 and the remainder bounds show
\[
 h_{D-1}^2-h_Dh_{D-2}
 =\frac{F(\alpha)F(\beta)}{(\alpha\beta)^{D+1}}(1+o(1))
 \quad(D\to\infty).
 \tag{RCM14}
\]
Here the point is fixed, including its nonzero root moduli.
To verify the relative error, each cross term is bounded using
RCM6 and RCM4 by a fixed constant times
$(D+1)r^{-D-2}R^{-D+2}$; the two remainder products have
bound $2C_R^2R^{-2D+2}$.  Dividing by the nonzero principal
term makes these bounds at most fixed constants times
$(D+1)(s_r/R)^D$ and $(r s_r/R^2)^D$, respectively.
Both tend to zero because $s_r<R$.  The constants may depend
on the fixed roots; no uniform claim at $r=0$ is inferred.
More explicitly, put $A=C_0+2r_0C_1$ and
$J=|F(\alpha)F(\beta)|$. The relative error is bounded by
\[
 \frac{4AC_RR^2s_r}{rJ}(D+1)(s_r/R)^D
 +\frac{2C_R^2R^2rs_r}{J}(rs_r/R^2)^D.
\]
Indeed the three mixed terms are bounded by
$AC_Rr^{-D-2}R^{-D}\{2DrR+(D+1)R^2+(D-1)r^2\}$;
use $r<R$ and divide by $J/(rs_r)^{D+1}$.
The two remaining products have sum at most $2C_R^2R^{-2D+2}$.

The modulus of this minor is at most
$\sigma_1(T_D(h))\sigma_2(T_D(h))$, since it is an entry
of the second exterior matrix.  RCM13--14 therefore prove
$\liminf\sigma_2(T_D(h))^{1/D}\ge s_r^{-1}$.
For the upper bound, if $r<s_r$, subtract the rank-one term
in RCM8 belonging to the smaller root.  The other term has
norm bounded by a constant times $(D+1)s_r^{-D}$, and RCM9
has strictly smaller exponential rate $R^{-1}<s_r^{-1}$.
The minimum--maximum characterization of singular values bounds
$\sigma_2$ by the norm of that residual: restrict to the kernel
of the subtracted rank-one map.  When $r=s_r$, simply use
$\sigma_2\le\sigma_1$ and RCM13.  This also covers coalescence.
We have proved
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \lim_{D\to\infty}\sigma_2(T_D(h))^{1/D}=s_r^{-1},\\
 \lim_{D\to\infty}\|\bigwedge^2T_D(h)\|^{1/D}
          =|\alpha\beta|^{-1},\\
 \limsup_{D\to\infty}\sigma_3(T_D(h))^{1/D}\le R^{-1}.
 \end{gathered}\tag{RCM15}
\]
For the third bound, restrict to the kernel of the rank-at-most-two
matrix in RCM7 and use RCM9.  This proof applies for $D\ge2$,
which suffices for the stated limit.

\subsection{The original amplification and the retained unit contribution}
The original inverse in its original orthonormal Gamma frames is
$B_{D,s}^{-1}=D_\rho^{-1}T_D(h)D_\rho$.
The full source and target masses and centers remain those of
RLA21; both coefficient frames are orthonormal for those measures.
Define $\kappa_{D,s}=\max_{j\le D}\rho_j/\min_{j\le D}\rho_j$.
For every singular index, multiplication by this invertible
diagonal matrix gives
\[
 \kappa_{D,s}^{-1}\sigma_j(T_D(h))\le
 \sigma_j(B_{D,s}^{-1})\le
 \kappa_{D,s}\sigma_j(T_D(h)),\qquad
 \begin{cases}
 \log\kappa_{D,1}\le\tfrac14\log(4D+1),\\
 \log\kappa_{D,s}\le\tfrac12(s/2-1)H_D,&s\ge2.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{RCM16}
\]
Here $H_D=\sum_{j=1}^D1/j$, unrelated to RCM2's analytic
function $H(t)$.  The singular inequalities follow from the
minimum--maximum principle applied to both diagonal factors
and then to their inverses.  For $s=1$, the original weight
induction in RQT15 gives the first bound.  For $s\ge2$,
$\rho_j$ decreases, and
$\kappa_{D,s}^2=\prod_{j=1}^D(1+(s/2-1)/j)$;
use $\log(1+x)\le x$ termwise.  No weight is deleted.

Consequently all three singular-rate statements RCM13 and RCM15
hold for the actual weighted inverse whenever $s=1$, fixed $s$,
or $s=s_D\ge2$ with $s_D\log(D+1)/D\to0$.
In particular they hold for the original amplification
$s_D=a_{{\rm amp},D}=4\ell_D+1\ge9$ on every sequence of the
original cutoffs with $a_{{\rm amp},D}=O(\sqrt D)$.
In particular WCF1 and WCF25 give $q=(a_{\rm amp}+1)^2$ and
$D_i=(q-1-v,q-v,2q-1-v,2q-v)_i$; here the old invertible
family has $v=0$. Each of these four original endpoint sequences
has $D_i=\Theta(a_{\rm amp}^2)$, so it satisfies the stated condition.
The proof is that $\kappa_{D,s_D}^{1/D}\to1$ in RCM16;
for the second exterior power the multiplicative factor is
at most $\kappa_{D,s_D}^2$, with the same conclusion.
This proves uniformity in the growing matrix size at each fixed
admitted geometric parameter, rather than applying a fixed-$D$
local Taylor expansion at changing cutoffs.

There is also an exact evaluation of the remaining total logarithm.
Let the inverse singular values be $\tau_{1,D}\ge\cdots\ge
\tau_{D+1,D}>0$ in any of the original source sequences just
specified.  Triangularity and the exact factorization give
\[
 \prod_{j=1}^{D+1}\tau_{j,D}=|g_0|^{-D-1},\qquad
 g_0=U(0,p)\alpha\beta,\qquad
 \lim_{D\to\infty}\frac1D\sum_{j=3}^{D+1}\log\tau_{j,D}
             =-\log|U(0,p)|.
 \tag{RCM17}
\]
Take logarithms of the first identity, subtract the first two
singular logarithms and use RCM13 and RCM15.  The root factors
cancel exactly, leaving the original analytic unit.  Its value
is not set to one, and its contribution is extensive in the
dimension.  This identity does not evaluate arbitrary intermediate
exterior ranks growing with $D$; it evaluates precisely the two
leading rates and the complete remaining logarithmic sum.

On the actual real parameter slice, RLA10 gives
$\beta=-\overline\alpha$, so both leading inverse singular
rates are $|\alpha|^{-1}$ and the second exterior rate is
$|\alpha|^{-2}$.  These statements include every phase
cancellation and the repeated-root line away from zero.
They concern $D\to\infty$ at a fixed invertible parameter.
RPD and RQT20--22 concern approach to the singular parameter
at a fixed cutoff and retain their different full constants;
no interchange of these limits has been made.
