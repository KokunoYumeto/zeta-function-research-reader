\section{A certified moving singularity in the original unit phase}
\label{sec:unit-phase-persistence}
The finite point FPZ4 extends to a nonconstant holomorphic curve of
simple zeros of the complete original factor. This section supplies an
explicit phase disk, the full-series bounds proving that statement,
and its exact effect on the original weighted conductor. It also
proves a singularity by varying the actual unit while keeping the
certified period fixed. The quartet remains the geometric member
$\delta=1/4$, $\gamma=3$ of FPZ1. This calculation does not identify
that quartet with arithmetic zeta zeros. Original period and weighted
coordinates are those of \cite{PCLOriginal,WCF}; the four ES marks
and their human sources remain \cite{ES488} and FSR1--24.

\subsection{The exact two-parameter family}
Use the exact rational centre $c$ in FPZ4, and put
\[
 r=10^{-9},\qquad \varepsilon=10^{-10},\qquad
 a=\alpha(1+ix),\quad \alpha\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\},
 \quad J=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,1),\quad W(x)=I+ixJ.
 \tag{UPP1}
\]
For real $x$, the original matrix is
$U=\alpha W=\operatorname{diag}(a,\bar a,\bar a,a)$.
Its magnitude is $|\alpha|\sqrt{1+x^2}$ and has not been assigned
a new value. The scalar $\alpha$ cancels with its exact inverse in
the original conjugation. For $|x|<1$ define its holomorphic extension by
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 W^{-1}&=(I-ixJ)/(1+x^2),\\
 M_j(z,x)&=W^{-1}V R(z)^{-1}D_jR(z)V^{-1}W,\\
 f_j(\xi,z,x)&=Q_0(M_j(z,x)(e^{\rho_a\xi})_a),\quad
 d_j(z,x)=f_j(0,z,x),\\
 E_A(\xi,z,x)&=\prod_{j=1}^4 f_j(\xi,z,x),\quad
 \mu_n(z,x)=\partial_\xi^n E_A(0,z,x).
 \end{aligned}\tag{UPP2}
\]
Here $R,V,D_j,\rho_a$ are exactly FPZ1--3. Complex $x$ is used
for analytic continuation; the two entries $\alpha(1\pm ix)$ are
the actual conjugate unit entries precisely on the real phase axis.
No branch factor or Gamma ray factor in the original $H$ is changed.
The identity with $H^{-1}D_jH$ follows directly from PCL7--9.

There is a unique holomorphic function $z(x)$ on $|x|<\varepsilon$
with a continuous extension to its closed disk such that
\[
 |z(x)-c|<r,\quad d_1(z(x),x)=0,\quad z(0)=z_*.
 \tag{UPP3}
\]
All four factors are retained. On this entire closed phase disk,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 m(x)&=\mu_1(z(x),x)
     =(f_1)_\xi(0,z(x),x)\prod_{j=2}^4d_j(z(x),x),
       &|m(x)|&>14.01,\\
 b(x)&=(\mu_0)_z(z(x),x)
     =(d_1)_z(z(x),x)\prod_{j=2}^4d_j(z(x),x),
       &|b(x)|&>1516,\\
 e(x)&=(\mu_0)_x(z(x),x)
     =(d_1)_x(z(x),x)\prod_{j=2}^4d_j(z(x),x),
       &|e(x)|&>53.65.
 \end{aligned}\tag{UPP4}
\]
The full enclosing proof of these numerical assertions follows.

\subsection{Every derivative and every infinite tail used in the certificate}
Put $A=R^{-1}$ and retain the indicated noncommuting matrix orders.
Twice differentiating $AR=I$ gives
$A_z=-AR_zA$ and $A_{zz}=2AR_zAR_zA-AR_{zz}A$. Therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 C_j&=AD_jR,\quad (C_j)_z=AD_jR_z-AR_zAD_jR,\\
 (C_j)_{zz}&=AD_jR_{zz}-2AR_zAD_jR_z
       +2AR_zAR_zAD_jR-AR_{zz}AD_jR,\\
 (M_j)_{z^h}&=W^{-1}V(C_j)_{z^h}V^{-1}W\quad(h=0,1,2),\\
 (M_j)_x&=i[M_j,J]/(1+x^2),\quad
 (M_j)_{zx}=i[(M_j)_z,J]/(1+x^2),\\
 (M_j)_{xx}&=-\{[[M_j,J],J]+2ix[M_j,J]\}/(1+x^2)^2.
 \end{aligned}\tag{UPP5}
\]
The phase formulas follow by differentiating $W^{-1}NW$:
$W^{-1}W_x=(xI+iJ)/(1+x^2)$, whose scalar summand commutes with
$M_j$. A second differentiation gives the last formula.
Define the complex bilinear form
$\mathfrak b(v,w)=v_1w_4+v_4w_1-v_2w_3-v_3w_2$.
For $y=M_j\mathbf1$, $h=M_j(\rho_a)_a$, the complete derivatives are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 d_j&=\mathfrak b(y,y)/2,&(d_j)_z&=\mathfrak b(y,y_z),\\
 (d_j)_{zz}&=\mathfrak b(y_z,y_z)+\mathfrak b(y,y_{zz}),&
 (d_j)_x&=\mathfrak b(y,y_x),\\
 (d_j)_{zx}&=\mathfrak b(y_x,y_z)+\mathfrak b(y,y_{zx}),&
 (d_j)_{xx}&=\mathfrak b(y_x,y_x)+\mathfrak b(y,y_{xx}),\\
 (f_j)_\xi&=\mathfrak b(y,h),&
 (f_j)_{\xi z}&=\mathfrak b(y_z,h)+\mathfrak b(y,h_z),\\
 (f_j)_{\xi x}&=\mathfrak b(y_x,h)+\mathfrak b(y,h_x).
 \end{aligned}\tag{UPP6}
\]
These identities are product rules for the original quadratic,
not Hermitian replacements. Every $\rho_a$ retains its original $1/2$.

The coefficient bound FPZ6 is used with $N=400$, $K=11/2$,
$C_R=4K^3$, $T_R=6K^5/5$. Let $\varrho$ be an outward modulus
bound on the closed rectangle
$|\Re(z-c)|,|\Im(z-c)|\le r$, and put $w=T_R\varrho$.
Besides FPZ7's $\tau_0,\tau_1$, the omitted second derivative has bound
\[
 \tau_2=
 \frac{C_R(2N+4)N(N+1)T_R^2w^{N-1}}
 {(N+1)!\{1-w(N+3)/(N(N+2))\}}.
 \tag{UPP7}
\]
Indeed its $n$th majorant is
$C_R(2n+2)T_R^2w^{n-2}/(n-2)!$; the ratio of successive terms is
$w(n+2)/((n+1)(n-1))$, bounded by $w(N+3)/(N(N+2))$ for
$n\ge N+1$. The computed denominator is positive. Geometric
summation gives UPP7, including every omitted coefficient.
All three polynomial derivatives are evaluated by simultaneous Horner
recursion $P_2\leftarrow zP_2+2P_1$,
$P_1\leftarrow zP_1+P$, $P\leftarrow zP+R_n$, in that order,
and their respective tails are added entrywise.

The certificate improves the matrix enclosure by an exact Taylor
integral, retaining the same full functions. On a rectangle centred
at $c$, let $h$ bound $|z-c|$ and let $L_{ar}$ bound $|R''_{ar}|$

\[
 \begin{aligned}

 |R'_{ar}(z)-R'_{ar}(c)|&\le L_{ar}h.
 \end{aligned}\tag{UPP8}
\]
Every segment from $c$ stays inside this rectangle; this restriction

\subsection{The graph moves, and a fixed period gives a phase singularity}
Differentiating the exact zero identity gives
\[
 z'(x)=-\frac{(d_1)_x}{(d_1)_z}(z(x),x)
       =-\frac{e(x)}{b(x)},\qquad
 u'(x)=\frac{(d_1)_x}{z(x)^2(d_1)_z},\quad u(x)=1/z(x).
 \tag{UPP14}
\]
The disk excludes zero. UPP10--12 give $|z'(x)|<1.145$ and hence
$|z(x)-z(0)|<1.145|x|$. A second run of the same full-series
certificate, with period radius $10^{-14}$ and phase radius

\[

       +i[.011074723404\pm7.11\cdot10^{-13}],\\
 e(0)&\in[21.49053615\pm2.97\cdot10^{-9}]
       +i[-49.20852959\pm2.47\cdot10^{-9}],\qquad |e(0)|>53.696.
 \end{aligned}\tag{UPP15}
\]
Both derivatives are nonzero; neither statement is a numerical
guess at an unenclosed root. Thus $\mu_0(z_*,x)=e(0)x+O(x^2)$

Here is also an exact two-parameter description of the singularity.
Within the period disk the identity
\[
 \begin{aligned}

Every derivative in the integral differs from $A_c$ by less than

\subsection{The full original weighted return on both transverse paths}

\[
 \det B_{D,s}(z,x)=\mu_0(z,x)^{D+1}.
 \tag{UPP17}
\]

 \begin{aligned}
