\paragraph{Objects and provenance.}
This derivation retains the original FC15a marked matrix, FC5 quartet,
FC21 conductor, FC26--35 interpolation, and EQR receiver construction
\cite{FC}. The exact four-point coordinates are those attributed by
the programme to its canonical 488-page ES reader; the initial
three-dimensional polynomial example is attributed there to Levent
Alp\"oge with credit to Fable and Terence Tao's exposition. No new
attribution for those coordinates is inferred here. The original
Gamma norms remain those of WCF2--6 \cite{WCF}. OFM1--17, independently
proved in IR1--27 \cite{OFMReview}, give the actual real-period facts
$0\le v\le32$, $\mu_v>0$, and $\mu_n\in\R$ for all $n$.
The present calculation proves that these actual moments cannot
satisfy the receiver's two complex zero-jet equations at any real
evaluation point. It does not assert an RH endpoint or a conductor
order jump.

\section{The actual coefficient map and its exact inverse}
Let $0<d<1/2$ and $g>2$ denote the retained $\delta,\gamma$.
Set $\rho=(1/2+d+ig,1/2+d-ig,1/2-d+ig,1/2-d-ig)$ and retain
\[
 K=\begin{pmatrix}
 0&i&1/\sqrt2&1/\sqrt2\\
 0&-1&-1-\sqrt2 i&1-\sqrt2 i\\
 i/2&-3i&2\sqrt2+6i&-2\sqrt2+6i\\
 0&13&-34-19\sqrt2 i&34-19\sqrt2 i
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad \det K=77\sqrt2 i/2.
 \tag{RJR1}
\]
Writing $k=K^{-1}e_1$, form the complete complex polynomial
\[
 b(X)=\sum_{a=1}^4k_a
 \frac{\prod_{c\ne a}(X-\rho_c)}{\prod_{c\ne a}(\rho_a-\rho_c)}
 =\sum_{q=0}^3b_qX^q.
 \tag{RJR2}
\]
The coefficients are therefore specified by finite sums of the
unchanged entries of $K$ and the unchanged quartet. All denominators
are nonzero. Evaluation gives $b(\rho_a)=k_a$; uniqueness of a cubic
with four prescribed values proves that its coefficient vector is
$(V_\rho^{\mathsf T})^{-1}K^{-1}e_1$ exactly.

Let $x=P+\kappa$ be the original real output translation, including
the unchanged $\kappa$. For $q=0,1,2,3$ define
\[
 \eta_q(x)=\sum_{r=0}^q\binom{v+q}{r}\mu_{v+q-r}x^r,
 \quad \eta_0=\mu_v>0,\quad
 \eta'_q=(v+q)\eta_{q-1}\quad(q\ge1).
 \tag{RJR3}
\]
The derivative follows from
$r\binom{v+q}{r}=(v+q)\binom{v+q-1}{r-1}$.
The complete inverse transformation is
\[
 \mu_{v+q}=\sum_{r=0}^q\binom{v+q}{r}(-x)^r\eta_{q-r}(x).
 \tag{RJR4}
\]
Indeed substitution of RJR3 on the right gives, for $0\le k\le q$,
the coefficient of $\mu_{v+q-k}$ equal to
\[
 x^k\sum_{r=0}^k(-1)^r
 \binom{v+q}{r}\binom{v+q-r}{k-r}
 =x^k\binom{v+q}{k}\sum_{r=0}^k(-1)^r\binom kr.
\]
This equals one for $k=0$ and zero for $k>0$. Thus no moment has
been deleted in passing to the real vector $\eta$.

The literal monomial action of the conductor is
$T_AS^{v+q}=\sum_{r=0}^q\binom{v+q}{r}\mu_{v+q-r}S'^r$.
Consequently the actual first marked output column and its derivative
at $P$ are
\[
 m_1(P)=\sum_{q=0}^3b_q\eta_q(x),\qquad
 m'_1(P)=\sum_{q=1}^3(v+q)b_q\eta_{q-1}(x).
 \tag{RJR5}
\]
All $\eta_q$ are real here. Taking real and imaginary parts of both
equalities therefore yields
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}\Re m_1\\\Im m_1\\\Re m'_1\\\Im m'_1\end{pmatrix}
 =C_v\eta,\qquad
 C_v=\begin{pmatrix}
 \Re b_0&\Re b_1&\Re b_2&\Re b_3\\
 \Im b_0&\Im b_1&\Im b_2&\Im b_3\\
 (v+1)\Re b_1&(v+2)\Re b_2&(v+3)\Re b_3&0\\
 (v+1)\Im b_1&(v+2)\Im b_2&(v+3)\Im b_3&0
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{RJR6}
\]
The reality restriction is essential to this particular four-real-
equation map. It has not been extended to nonreal original periods
by an unsupported identification.

\section{A determinant with an exact rational sign certificate}
Expanding RJR2 and the determinant in RJR6 gives
\[
 \det C_v=\frac{F(d,g,v)}{367313408d^4g^4(d^2+g^2)^2},
 \tag{RJR7}
\]
where every term of the numerator is
\[
\begin{aligned}
F={}&-15859712d^4v-31719424d^4
 -88996864\sqrt2d^3gv^2-254510080\sqrt2d^3gv
 -153032704\sqrt2d^3g\\
&-182632752d^2g^2v^2-1014291124d^2g^2v
 -588913440d^2g^2-53025280\sqrt2d^2gv-3964928d^2v\\
&+23775460\sqrt2dg^3v^2-71596940\sqrt2dg^3v
 -238295720\sqrt2dg^3+318415300dg^2v
 +23809280\sqrt2dgv\\
&+285948100g^4v+571896200g^4-71487025g^2v.
\end{aligned}\tag{RJR8}
\]
One finite way to verify the determinant is to form each $b_q$ from
RJR2, multiply $C_v$ by $4928dg(d^2+g^2)$, and evaluate its
determinant over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)[d,g,v]$. Dividing by the fourth
power of this exact denominator gives RJR7--8. The accompanying
independent checker performs the interpolation from $K$, verifies
its four prescribed values, constructs all sixteen real matrix
entries, and evaluates this determinant exactly.

Set $T=2d$, $Z=v/32$, and $H=g-2$. These are invertible parameter
maps with $d=T/2$, $v=32Z$, and $g=H+2$. Their ranges are
$0<T<1$, $0\le Z\le1$, $H>0$ in the actual domain. Put
\[
 p_{ijk}=[T^iZ^jH^k]F(T/2,H+2,32Z),\qquad
 B_{abk}=\sum_{i=0}^a\sum_{j=0}^b p_{ijk}
 \frac{\binom ai}{\binom4i}\frac{\binom bj}{\binom2j}.
 \tag{RJR9}
\]
Here $0\le a\le4$, $0\le b\le2$, $0\le k\le4$. Since
\[
 T^i=\sum_{a=i}^4\frac{\binom ai}{\binom4i}
      \binom4aT^a(1-T)^{4-a},
\]
and the identical degree-two formula holds for $Z^j$, coefficient
substitution proves the entire identity
\[
 F(d,g,v)=\sum_{a=0}^4\sum_{b=0}^2\sum_{k=0}^4 B_{abk}
 \binom4aT^a(1-T)^{4-a}\binom2bZ^b(1-Z)^{2-b}H^k.
 \tag{RJR10}
\]
The binomial identity follows by taking out $T^i$ and applying
the binomial theorem to the remaining power $4-i$.

Each coefficient from the complete finite formula RJR9 is uniquely
$A_{abk}+D_{abk}\sqrt2$ with rational $A_{abk},D_{abk}$.
Use the rational certificate
\[
 L_{abk}=\begin{cases}
 A_{abk}+7D_{abk}/5,&D_{abk}\ge0,\\
 A_{abk}+10D_{abk}/7,&D_{abk}<0.
 \end{cases}\qquad B_{abk}\ge L_{abk}.
 \tag{RJR11}
\]
The last inequality uses $7/5<\sqrt2<10/7$, proved by squaring
these positive rationals. Substitution of RJR8 into RJR9 and RJR11
gives the following exact minima over the five values $k=0,\ldots,4$:
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrr}
 a\backslash b&0&1&2\\\hline
 0&571896200&5147065800&9722235400\\
 1&571896200&5147065800&9722235400\\
 2&571896200&5147065800&9722235400\\
 3&571896200&5147065800&9722235400\\
 4&571896200&5147065800&9722235400
\end{array}\tag{RJR12}
\]
This table is a compressed display of a completely specified finite
arithmetic certificate, not a sampled sign test. The machine-readable
companion gives each of the 75 triples $(A,D,L)$ and verifies all of
them exactly, as well as reconstructing the full polynomial RJR10.
RJR8--11 specify every table entry without an uncomputed optimization.

Each Bernstein family is nonnegative and sums to one. Thus already
the least entry in RJR12 proves
\[
 F(d,g,v)\ge571896200\sum_{k=0}^4(g-2)^k>0,
 \qquad\det C_v>0.
 \tag{RJR13}
\]
In fact the dependence on $b$ in RJR12 is affine, so the same
certificate gives the stronger retained-order bound
\[
 F(d,g,v)\ge(571896200+285948100v)
              \sum_{k=0}^4(g-2)^k.
 \tag{RJR14}
\]
Indeed the three displayed minima are
$571896200+4575169600b$. For the degree-two Bernstein family
$\sum_b b\binom2bZ^b(1-Z)^{2-b}=2Z=v/16$. Substitution into
RJR10 proves RJR14. Both bounds hold on the entire real interval
$0\le v\le32$, not only at even or integer values.

\section{The nonzero jet and its return to both original metrics}
The real vector $\eta$ is nonzero by $\eta_0=\mu_v>0$. Therefore
RJR6 and RJR13 imply
\[
 U_1(P):=(m_1(P),m'_1(P))^{\mathsf T}\ne0
 \qquad(P\in\R).
 \tag{RJR15}
\]
This says the actual complex cubic has no repeated real root.
For its complete quantitative version, let $\|C_v\|_F$ be the
Frobenius norm. If $\sigma_1\ge\cdots\ge\sigma_4>0$ are the
singular values, then
$\sigma_4=\det C_v/(\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3)
\ge\det C_v/\|C_v\|_F^3$. Hence
\[
 \|U_1(P)\|_2=\|C_v\eta\|_2
 \ge\frac{\det C_v}{\|C_v\|_F^3}\|\eta\|_2
 \ge\frac{\det C_v}{\|C_v\|_F^3}\mu_v>0.
 \tag{RJR16}
\]
The equality of the two Euclidean norms in the first expression
retains both real and imaginary components of both complex values.
It is a coefficient estimate, not a change in the programme metric.

For the actual collision path with real $P\ne0$, retain both root
labels $\epsilon\in\{0,1\}$ and both square-root choices
$\lambda^2=(2\epsilon-1)3P/22$. EQR2 and EQR6 give
$wY\to U_1(P)/\lambda$ with $t=w^2$. The complete original
quotient metric $G_{b,t}$ for $b=U,W$ converges to the positive
$G_{b,0}$ from EQR4. Taking its continuous quadratic form yields
\[
 \begin{split}
 |t|^{1/2}\|Y\|_{G_{b,t}}
 &\longrightarrow\frac{\sqrt{U_1(P)^*G_{b,0}U_1(P)}}{|\lambda|}\\
 &\ge\frac{\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}(G_{b,0})}}{|\lambda|}
       \frac{\det C_v}{\|C_v\|_F^3}\mu_v>0.
 \end{split}\tag{RJR17}
\]
Thus every one of the four labelled choices has the stated nonzero
escape coefficient in each original quotient norm. This excludes
the bounded-jet cases EQR7--10 for real admitted periods and real
$P\ne0$. It leaves their general complex-period classification
intact, and it does not alter any complementary coordinate in EQR3--4
or the invertible original conductor in OFM15--16.
