\paragraph{The actual singular period and the scope of this derivation.}
The companion finite-period certificate \cite{FinitePeriod} uses
the original geometric family at $\delta=1/4$, $\gamma=3$ and
the actual real-unit phase. Its unique root is in the stated disk
of radius $10^{-14}$ about
$0.011136978231486808477778740306233906621055400363647396688725562781
+0.0078780133477760021239711966284294386302928222474756316845213852718i$
in the inverse period. The certified moment facts there are
$\mu_0=0$ and $\mu_1\ne0$, so the actual symbol order is one.
The interval proof belongs to that companion calculation; this note
independently derives the exact algebraic ES return at those coefficients.
In every formula the original $\mu_1,\mu_2,\mu_3$ and the quartet
remain intact. The proof also records the full parameter formulas
so none of their geometry is hidden by the numerical values.
The certificate concerns the geometric family; it does not identify
this quartet with zeta zeros or prove the arithmetic unit's phase.

The polynomial and marking below were checked directly against
the canonical version-69 ES reader, theorem
\texttt{explicit-four-time-Jacobian-collision} \cite{ES488}.
The original conductor and norms are FC20--32 and WCF \cite{FC,WCF}.
This is a calculation in the fixed old spaces
$\mathcal P_3\longrightarrow\mathcal P_3$, whose image is
$\mathcal P_2$ at this period, followed by the exact actual-order
return. It does not silently identify those two presentations.

\section{Literal polynomial, marking and kernel}
Write $q=(a,y,z,w)$ in the original ES coordinates and retain
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_0(q)&=a^3z+2a^2y-ia,\\
 P_2(q)&=-a^3y^2z-2ia^2yz+a^2w-2a^2y^3-10iay^2+3az+y,\\
 P_3(q)&=2a^3y^3z+6ia^2y^2z+2a^2wy+4a^2y^4
          +2iaw-4iay^3-2ayz+2iz+7y^2,\\
 P_4(q)&=2a^3y^4z+8ia^2y^3z+a^2wy^2+4a^2y^5
          +2iawy+7iay^4-10ay^2z-4iyz-w-3y^3,
 \qquad P=(P_0,P_2,P_3,P_4)^{\mathsf T}.
\end{aligned}\tag{FSR1}
\]
The original ordered columns are
\[
 K=\begin{pmatrix}
 0&i&1/\sqrt2&1/\sqrt2\\
 0&-1&-1-\sqrt2i&1-\sqrt2i\\
 i/2&-3i&2\sqrt2+6i&-2\sqrt2+6i\\
 0&13&-34-19\sqrt2i&34-19\sqrt2i
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 q_{\mathrm M}=Ke_{\mathrm M},\quad q_{\mathrm N}=Ke_{\mathrm N},
 \quad q_{\mathrm T}=Ke_{\mathrm T},\quad q_{\mathrm E}=Ke_{\mathrm E}.
 \tag{FSR2}
\]
Direct polynomial calculation, with $i^2=-1$ and the positive
$\sqrt2$, gives
\[
 \det K=77\sqrt2i/2,\qquad \det DP(q)=-2,\qquad
 P(q_j)=h_0=(0,0,-1,0)^{\mathsf T}=2iq_{\mathrm M}
       \quad(j=\mathrm M,\mathrm N,\mathrm T,\mathrm E).
 \tag{FSR3}
\]
For the first identity, expansion in the first column reduces
the determinant to $i/2$ times $30\sqrt2+94/\sqrt2=77\sqrt2$.
For the polynomial identity the companion checker differentiates
all sixteen entries of FSR1 and expands the determinant over
$\mathbb Q(i)$, giving exactly $-2$ with every nonconstant
coefficient zero. It separately substitutes all four complete
columns of FSR2. These are literal algebraic identities, not
numerical determinant evaluations.

Retain
$\rho=(1/2+\delta+i\gamma,1/2+\delta-i\gamma,
1/2-\delta+i\gamma,1/2-\delta-i\gamma)$ and
$(V_\rho)_{rj}=\rho_j^r$, $0\le r\le3$. Define the exact original
coefficient isomorphism
\[
 \Phi q=(V_\rho^{\mathsf T})^{-1}K^{-1}q,
 \qquad (\Phi^{-1}f)=K(f(\rho_j))_j.
 \tag{FSR4}
\]
The inverse follows by evaluating the interpolating cubic. The
quartet is distinct and
$\det V_\rho=-64\delta^2\gamma^2(\delta^2+\gamma^2)\ne0$.
The literal old conductor at the certified period is
\[
 T_A(c_0+c_1S+c_2S^2+c_3S^3)
  =\mu_1c_1+\mu_2c_2+\mu_3c_3
        +(2\mu_1c_2+3\mu_2c_3)S'+3\mu_1c_3S'^2.
 \tag{FSR5}
\]
This is the original Taylor formula, with its proved $\mu_0=0$;
it retains every surviving moment. Its kernel is exactly the
constants, its rank is three, and its image is all of
$\mathcal P_2$: successive leading coefficients are
$\mu_1,2\mu_1,3\mu_1$, all nonzero.

In the unchanged ES coordinates its complete kernel is therefore
\[
 \ker(T_A\Phi)=\C n,\qquad
 n=K\mathbf1=
 \begin{pmatrix}\sqrt2+i\\-1-2\sqrt2i\\19i/2\\13-38\sqrt2i\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \Phi n=1.
 \tag{FSR6}
\]
Indeed $K^{-1}n=\mathbf1$, which evaluates the constant polynomial.
The four labelled target vectors $y_j=T_A\Phi q_j=T_A\ell_j$
satisfy exactly one relation,
\[
 y_{\mathrm M}+y_{\mathrm N}+y_{\mathrm T}+y_{\mathrm E}=0.
 \tag{FSR7}
\]
If $\sum_jc_jy_j=0$, FSR5 makes $\sum_jc_j\ell_j$ constant;
evaluation gives $c_{\mathrm M}=c_{\mathrm N}=c_{\mathrm T}=c_{\mathrm E}$.
In particular all four target vectors remain distinct. Thus the
rank loss preserves the four labels and identifies exactly their
constant label direction.

\section{The full obstruction to descent, on every kernel line}
Use the complete four-label projection and mean
\[
 E=I_4-\tfrac14\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 \pi(q)=EK^{-1}q,\qquad
 \tau(q)=\tfrac14\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}K^{-1}q.
 \tag{FSR8}
\]
Their exact inverse is $q=K(\pi(q)+\tau(q)\mathbf1)$.
No label has been removed: $\pi(q)$ lies in the full labelled
hyperplane $\mathbf1^\perp$. In particular
$\pi(q_j)=e_j-\mathbf1/4$ and $\tau(q_j)=1/4$ for all four marks.

For completeness write $P_j(q)=\sum_\alpha p_{j,\alpha}q^\alpha$
with precisely the displayed monomials of FSR1. The whole
polynomial obstruction is
\[
\begin{split}
 \mathcal O(q,\lambda)&=EK^{-1}\bigl(P(q+\lambda n)-P(q)\bigr)
                  =\sum_{r=1}^8\lambda^r EK^{-1}A_r(q),\\
 (A_r(q))_j&=\sum_\alpha p_{j,\alpha}
       \sum_{\substack{\theta\le\alpha\\|\theta|=r}}
           \binom\alpha\theta q^{\alpha-\theta}n^\theta .
\end{split}\tag{FSR9}
\]
The multi-index formula is the binomial theorem applied to each
original monomial, and specifies every coefficient with its sign.
The machine-readable companion retains all eight complete vector
coefficients as exact polynomials. Descent of $P$ to the quotient
would require this entire obstruction to be zero.

Its leading coefficient can be evaluated without any omitted term.
The only degree-eight monomial of FSR1 is $2a^3y^4z$ in the last
component. Thus, for every $q$,
\[
 A_8(q)=\Lambda\,e_w,\qquad
 \Lambda=2n_a^3n_y^4n_z\ne0,
 \qquad
 EK^{-1}e_w=\frac1{308}
 \begin{pmatrix}17+i\sqrt2\\-3+i\sqrt2\\-13-i\sqrt2\\-1-i\sqrt2\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{FSR10}
\]
Here $e_w=(0,0,0,1)^{\mathsf T}$ is the fourth ambient ES
coordinate vector; it is not the marked point $Ke_{\mathrm E}$.
Every factor of $\Lambda$ is nonzero by FSR6 and the displayed
four-label vector is nonzero. Consequently $\mathcal O(q,\lambda)$
has degree exactly eight in $\lambda$ for every $q$. The nonlinear
map does not descend through any entire old conductor fibre.
Equivalently $\Phi P(q+\lambda n)-\Phi P(q)$ is not always
constant. This proves the obstruction for the exact original map.

The obstruction also has a nonzero component in the original conductor
image. Let
$b^{(w)}(S)=\Phi e_w$. Exact interpolation from FSR2 gives
\[
 K^{-1}e_w=
 (6/77,1/77,-(3+i\sqrt2)/154,(3-i\sqrt2)/154)^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad
 [S^3]b^{(w)}(S)=
 -\frac{7\gamma+i(2\delta+\sqrt2\gamma)}
          {616\delta\gamma(\delta^2+\gamma^2)}\ne0.
 \tag{FSR11}
\]
To check the coefficient, the centered quartic is
$w^4+2(\gamma^2-\delta^2)w^2+(\delta^2+\gamma^2)^2$.
Its derivatives at the four roots are $\pm8i\delta\gamma w$.
The interpolation leading coefficient is the sum of each prescribed
value divided by that derivative, yielding FSR11; shifting $w=S-1/2$
does not change a cubic's leading coefficient. Therefore
\[
 [\lambda^8S'^2]\,T_A\Phi\bigl(P(q+\lambda n)-P(q)\bigr)
       =3\mu_1\Lambda\,[S^3]b^{(w)}\ne0.
 \tag{FSR12}
\]
The exact actual $\mu_1$ appears in this component. No change of
norm or arbitrary conductor coefficient is used to detect it.

\section{The exact total return with the one lost scalar restored}
Put $J=T_A\Phi:\C^4\to\mathcal P_2$. We construct the entire
inverse of the augmented old quotient
\[
 \Theta(q)=(Jq,\tau(q))\in\mathcal P_2\oplus\C.
 \tag{FSR13}
\]
Let $Y(S')=Y_0+Y_1S'+Y_2S'^2$. Define
\[
\begin{split}
 c_3&=Y_2/(3\mu_1),\qquad
 c_2=(Y_1-3\mu_2c_3)/(2\mu_1),\qquad
 c_1=(Y_0-\mu_2c_2-\mu_3c_3)/\mu_1,\\
 \sigma_1&=1/2,\quad
 \sigma_2=1/4+\delta^2-\gamma^2,\quad
 \sigma_3=1/8+\tfrac32(\delta^2-\gamma^2),\\
 c_0&=-\sigma_1c_1-\sigma_2c_2-\sigma_3c_3,
 \qquad p_Y(S)=\sum_{j=0}^3c_jS^j,\\
 \Theta^{-1}(Y,t)&=K\bigl(p_Y(\rho_j)+t\bigr)_j.
\end{split}\tag{FSR14}
\]
The values $\sigma_j$ are the exact quartet averages
$\tfrac14\sum_a\rho_a^j$. FSR5 proves $T_Ap_Y=Y$, and the
chosen $c_0$ proves $\tfrac14\sum_ap_Y(\rho_a)=0$.
Thus $\Theta\Theta^{-1}=I$. Conversely $\Phi q-p_{Jq}$ is
constant by FSR5 and its average is $\tau(q)$, proving the other
composition. Every old coordinate has therefore been recovered.

The strongest total polynomial correspondence on these fixed spaces
is now the explicit conjugate
\[
 \widehat P(Y,t)=
 \left(JP\bigl(K(p_Y(\rho_j)+t)_j\bigr),\quad
       \tau P\bigl(K(p_Y(\rho_j)+t)_j\bigr)\right),
 \qquad \Theta P=\widehat P\Theta,
 \qquad\det D\widehat P=-2.
 \tag{FSR15}
\]
All entries are the finite formulas FSR1--2 and FSR14. The last
identity follows from conjugation by the same invertible linear
$\Theta$, so its two determinants cancel exactly. The first
component genuinely depends on $t$ to degree eight by FSR12;
the omitted scalar cannot be suppressed in this correspondence.

There is nevertheless an exact three-dimensional slice containing
all four marked points. Define
\[
 F_{1/4}(Y)=JP\bigl(\Theta^{-1}(Y,1/4)\bigr).
 \tag{FSR16}
\]
All four $y_j$ from FSR7 belong to this same affine slice, and
\[
 \Theta(q_j)=(y_j,1/4),\qquad
 \widehat P(y_j,1/4)=(2iy_{\mathrm M},i/2),\qquad
 F_{1/4}(y_j)=2iy_{\mathrm M}.
 \tag{FSR17}
\]
This is a specified slice map, with its missing output scalar
retained in FSR15. It is not an assertion of descent along the
old kernel fibres.

Its derivative at every marked point is also computable exactly.
Let $\ell=\tfrac14\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}K^{-1}$. The cofactor
identity in the coordinates FSR13 gives
\[
 \det DF_{1/4}(y_j)=-2\ell\,(DP(q_j))^{-1}n.
 \tag{FSR18}
\]
Indeed the desired determinant is the upper-left three-by-three
minor of $\Theta DP(q_j)\Theta^{-1}$; its complementary inverse
entry is $\ell(DP(q_j))^{-1}n$, and its full determinant is $-2$.
Evaluation gives the following complete table:
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
 j&616\det DF_{1/4}(y_j)\\\hline
 \mathrm M&3580+8830i-4\sqrt2(549+1003i)\\
 \mathrm N&-80720-12413i-8\sqrt2(1247+746i)\\
 \mathrm T&-39134+21326i+\sqrt2(3107-124144i)\\
 \mathrm E&-108226+40740i+\sqrt2(-2587+216544i)
\end{array}\tag{FSR19}
\]
Each entry is nonzero since its imaginary part has a nonzero rational
coefficient in the basis $1,\sqrt2$. In particular these are four
distinct regular inputs with one common output. The first two
determinants differ, so this three-dimensional slice does not have
the constant Jacobian of the complete four-dimensional polynomial.
Every assertion here is an exact value at a named marked point;
no sampled value has been used as a global determinant proof.

\section{The complete original quotient metric and actual-order return}
The section $p_Y$ in FSR14 was chosen by the label mean; its metric
correction is retained explicitly. Use the original source centre
$c=a_{\rm amp}/2$, source order $s\in\{1,a_{\rm amp}\}$,
$b=s/2$ and mass $M=(2\pi)^{s/2}$. The original Gamma moments give
\[
 \frac{\langle1,p_Y\rangle_U}{M}
  =c_0+cc_1+(c^2-b)c_2+(c^3-3cb)c_3=:\chi(Y),
 \quad
 \|p_Y+t\|_U^2=
 \|p_Y\|_U^2-M|\chi(Y)|^2+M|t+\chi(Y)|^2.
 \tag{FSR20}
\]
The convention is $\langle f,g\rangle=\int\bar f g\,dm_s$.
For the first formula substitute $S=c+iy$; the unchanged measure
has mass $M$, zero odd moments and $\int y^2dm_s=Mb$ by WCF2.
The second follows by expanding the two squares. It retains all
cross terms between the lost constant coordinate and the chosen
section. The attained quotient norm is therefore exactly
$\|p_Y\|_U^2-M|\chi(Y)|^2$, with minimizing scalar $t=-\chi(Y)$.
The target is the inherited original $\mathcal P_2$ norm at centre
$a_{\rm amp}/2-4$, with its same mass and order. Thus the nonlinear
obstruction was not obtained by imposing a new metric on the quotient.

Finally the actual order-one four-column receiver has an exact
relation to this singular old receiver. Define
\[
 \Phi_1q=[S\Phi q]\in\mathcal P_4/\mathcal P_0,
 \qquad \Psi_1q=T_A(S\Phi q)\in\mathcal P_3.
 \tag{FSR21}
\]
Multiplication by $S$ is the displayed coefficient isomorphism
from $\mathcal P_3$ to these classes; division of their unique
representatives with zero constant coefficient gives its inverse.
The original $T_A$ in these corrected spaces is an isomorphism:
its four successive leading coefficients are
$\mu_1,2\mu_1,3\mu_1,4\mu_1$. In particular
\[
 [T_A]_{S,S^2,S^3,S^4}^{1,S',S'^2,S'^3}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \mu_1&\mu_2&\mu_3&\mu_4\\
 0&2\mu_1&3\mu_2&4\mu_3\\
 0&0&3\mu_1&6\mu_2\\
 0&0&0&4\mu_1
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \det\Psi_1=\frac{24\mu_1^4}{(\det V_\rho)(\det K)}\ne0,
 \qquad \Phi_1n=[S],\qquad \Psi_1n=\mu_1.
 \tag{FSR22}
\]
These determinants use the coefficient basis $S,S^2,S^3,S^4$
in the source and $1,S',S'^2,S'^3$ in the target, exactly as stated;
the original quotient and target Gamma norms remain unchanged.

The exact correction between the two receiving maps is
\[
 \Psi_1q=S'Jq+T_{E_A'}\Phi q,\qquad
 T_{E_A'}f(S')=\sum_\nu a_\nu\beta_\nu f(S'+\beta_\nu).
 \tag{FSR23}
\]
Here $E_A(\xi)=\sum_\nu a_\nu e^{\beta_\nu\xi}$ is the entire
original collected conductor with all its amplitudes and exponents.
Expanding $(S'+\beta_\nu)f(S'+\beta_\nu)$ proves FSR23 term
by term. The correction has moments $\mu_{j+1}$ and nonzero
constant diagonal $\mu_1$, so it restores the lost direction
explicitly rather than hiding it. On the actual order-one spaces,
\[
 P_U^{(1)}=\Phi_1P\Phi_1^{-1},\qquad
 P_W^{(1)}=\Psi_1P\Psi_1^{-1},\qquad
 T_AP_U^{(1)}=P_W^{(1)}T_A.
 \tag{FSR24}
\]
Both compositions are legitimate isomorphism conjugations, and
their four marked inputs remain distinct. Equations FSR9--12,
FSR13--20 and FSR21--24 together give the complete relation:
the nonlinear polynomial fails to descend through the singular
old kernel, its old quotient has an exact return with one scalar
restored, and its actual-order return is intertwined by the same
original conductor with the full correction FSR23.
