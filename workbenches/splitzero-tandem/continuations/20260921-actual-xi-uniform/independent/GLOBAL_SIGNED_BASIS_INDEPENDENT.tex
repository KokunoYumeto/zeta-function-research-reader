The proposed eight sections were supplied in the received
ramification continuation, Section 2 \cite{Intake}. Their
even basis and odd coefficient module have established human
provenance: specialize Melanie Matchett Wood's equations
\texttt{E:basis} and \texttt{E:Isbasis} to
$(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4)=(A,1,B,C,D)$ \cite{Wood}.
Wood credits the even basis to Birch--Merriman and its
multiplication to Nakagawa; her equation \texttt{E:mult}
is their explicit ring law. The form here is primitive
because $f_1=1$, and Wood's proposition \texttt{P:Iinvert}
identifies its odd coefficient module with $I_f^{-1}$.
We now prove directly that the proposed sections are a
global basis of the actual signed extension from FS2--10,
including its original sign convention, and calculate its
entire multiplication tensor and trace. No theorem about
a different choice of sign coordinate is substituted.

\section{Regular sections in both original root charts}
Let $\mathfrak B=\C[A,B,C,D]$ and retain
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 h(r)=Ar^4+r^3+Br^2+Cr+D,\qquad
 k(v)=A+v+Bv^2+Cv^3+Dv^4,\\
 h(r)=0,\quad t^2=h'(r),\qquad
 k(v)=0,\quad\theta^2=-k'(v),\\
 v=1/r,\quad\theta=-t/r,\qquad
 e=(1,Ar,Ar^2+r,Ar^3+r^2+Br),\\
 o=t(A,Ar+1,Ar^2+r+B,Ar^3+r^2+Br+C).
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{GB1}
\end{equation}
The completed cover $X$ is obtained by these two charts
and exact inverses $r=1/v$, $t=-\theta/v$.
This is the finite cover constructed in FS2--10
\cite{FS}; in particular the reciprocal original factor
is exactly $t=1/a$. The gluing identity follows by
differentiating $k(v)=v^4h(1/v)$ at a root.

On the infinity chart the same sections are the polynomials
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 e_0=1,\qquad e_1=-1-Bv-Cv^2-Dv^3,\qquad
 e_2=-B-Cv-Dv^2,\qquad e_3=-C-Dv,\\
 (o_0,o_1,o_2,o_3)=(-e_1,-e_2,-e_3,D)\theta.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{GB2}
\end{equation}
For instance $A/v=-1-Bv-Cv^2-Dv^3$ follows by dividing
the root equation on the overlap; its right side is regular
on the whole infinity chart. The same relation gives
$(A+v)/v^2=-B-Cv-Dv^2$ and
$(A+v+Bv^2)/v^3=-C-Dv$.
Multiplying these expressions by $t=-\theta/v$ proves
the odd formulas, including the last value $D\theta$.
They agree on the overlap and so define all eight global
regular sections. The minus signs in GB2 are necessary
for the retained $\theta=-t/r$ convention; the incoming
continuation used the opposite infinity coordinate.

On the open $A\ne0$, coefficient columns in the basis
$(1,r,r^2,r^3,t,tr,tr^2,tr^3)$ are given by
\begin{equation}
 E=\begin{pmatrix}
 1&0&0&0\\0&A&1&B\\0&0&A&1\\0&0&0&A
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 Q=\begin{pmatrix}
 A&1&B&C\\0&A&1&B\\0&0&A&1\\0&0&0&A
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 M_{\mathrm{fin}}=\diag(E,Q),\quad
 \det M_{\mathrm{fin}}=A^7.
 \tag{GB3}
\end{equation}
The triangular diagonals prove $\det E=A^3$ and
$\det Q=A^4$, so they give a basis there. Complete
inverses are the following coefficient recurrences:
\[
 1=e_0,\quad r=e_1/A,\quad
 r^2=(e_2-r)/A,\quad r^3=(e_3-r^2-Br)/A,
\]
\[
 t=o_0/A,\quad tr=(o_1-t)/A,\quad
 tr^2=(o_2-tr-Bt)/A,\quad
 tr^3=(o_3-tr^2-Btr-Ct)/A.
\]
Every term on each right side was specified earlier in its
line. Thus these are finite explicit inverse columns, with
all their denominators admitted on the stated open.

\section{Every other chart and its complete inverse}
For $p\in\{0,1,-1,2\}$ work on $a_p=h(p)\ne0$, and set
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 R=(p-r)^{-1},\quad T=t/(p-r),\qquad
 R=-v/(1-pv),\quad T=\theta/(1-pv),\\
 r=p-R^{-1},\quad t=T/R,\qquad
 v=R/(pR-1),\quad\theta=T/(1-pR),\\
 h_p(R)=R^4h(p-R^{-1})
        =a_pR^4+b_pR^3+c_pR^2+d_pR+A,\\
 a_p=Ap^4+p^3+Bp^2+Cp+D,\quad
 b_p=-4Ap^3-3p^2-2Bp-C,\\
 c_p=6Ap^2+3p+B,\qquad d_p=-4Ap-1,\qquad
 T^2=h_p'(R).
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{GB4}
\end{equation}
These are the original FS7 maps. At a root,
$h_p'(R)=R^2h'(p-R^{-1})$, proving the sign relation.
Every displayed inverse is used on its explicit nonzero
denominator; the inverse opens $R\ne0$ and $pR-1\ne0$
cover the new chart. On it the original finite cover has
the free monomial basis $(1,R,R^2,R^3,T,TR,TR^2,TR^3)$.
The derivative remains $h_p'$, including its leading
coefficient; it is not replaced by the derivative of a
polynomial divided by $a_p$.

Put
$k_1=Ap+d_p$,
$k_2=Ap^2+p-c_p-2pd_p$,
$k_3=Ap^3+p^2+Bp+b_p+pc_p+p^2d_p$.
The complete even coefficient matrix of the global sections
in this chart is
\begin{equation}
 E_p=\begin{pmatrix}
 1&k_1&k_2&k_3\\
 0&c_p&-b_p-2pc_p&a_p+pb_p+p^2c_p\\
 0&b_p&-a_p-2pb_p&pa_p+p^2b_p\\
 0&a_p&-2pa_p&p^2a_p
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{GB5}
\end{equation}
The complete odd coefficient matrix, whose rows refer to
$(T,TR,TR^2,TR^3)$, is
\begin{equation}
 Q_p=\begin{pmatrix}
 -d_p&c_p+3pd_p&-b_p-2pc_p-3p^2d_p&
                        a_p+pb_p+p^2c_p+p^3d_p\\
 -c_p&b_p+3pc_p&-a_p-2pb_p-3p^2c_p&
                        pa_p+p^2b_p+p^3c_p\\
 -b_p&a_p+3pb_p&-2pa_p-3p^2b_p&
                        p^2a_p+p^3b_p\\
 -a_p&3pa_p&-3p^2a_p&p^3a_p
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{GB6}
\end{equation}
These identities can be derived without a denominator $A$.
Substitute $r=p-1/R$, $t=T/R$ into GB1, and successively use
\[
 A/R=-a_pR^3-b_pR^2-c_pR-d_p
\]
and its products by $R^{-1}$ to remove the apparent negative
powers. The coefficients of $1/R$ that occur next are
integer multiples of $Ap,Ap^2,Ap^3$ and are eliminated by
the same equation. For example this gives
$e_1=k_1+c_pR+b_pR^2+a_pR^3$ and
$o_0/T=-d_p-c_pR-b_pR^2-a_pR^3$.
Repeating for the remaining columns gives precisely GB5--6.
All resulting entries are polynomials in the original
coefficients and $p$. Equality on $R\ne0$ proves their equality
on the whole completed chart: FS4--5 prove that $X$ is
integral and its finite-root open is dense, so multiplication
by this nonzero coordinate has no torsion. Equivalently the
identities follow by the explicit finite and infinity
substitutions in GB2 and GB4 on their covering opens.

Column additions followed by expansion give
\begin{equation}
 \det E_p=a_p^3,\qquad \det Q_p=a_p^4,\qquad
 M_p=\diag(E_p,Q_p),\qquad \det M_p=a_p^7.
 \tag{GB7}
\end{equation}
The following inverse expressions also verify these matrices
fully. Write $q_1=3Ap+1$, $q_2=3Ap^2+2p+B$ and
$q_3=Ap^3+p^2+Bp+C$. Then, as elements of the global
even basis,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 R&=(q_3e_0+p^2e_1+pe_2+e_3)/a_p,\\
 R^2&=-(q_2e_0+2pe_1+e_2+b_pR)/a_p,\\
 R^3&=(q_1e_0+e_1-c_pR-b_pR^2)/a_p .
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{GB8}
\end{equation}
The even inverse also has its first column $1=e_0$.
For the odd inverse every column is explicitly
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 T&=(p^3o_0+p^2o_1+po_2+o_3)/a_p,\\
 TR&=-(3p^2o_0+2po_1+o_2+b_pT)/a_p,\\
 TR^2&=(3po_0+o_1-c_pT-b_pTR)/a_p,\\
 TR^3&=-(o_0+d_pT+c_pTR+b_pTR^2)/a_p .
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{GB9}
\end{equation}
Each is a four-step finite formula: substituting the
previously defined columns removes every occurrence of
$R,R^2,T,TR,TR^2$ from the coefficient expressions.
Multiplication by GB5--6 then gives respectively the
four monomial columns, proving both inverse products.
Thus these inverses use only the admitted denominator
$a_p$ and remain valid at $R=0$.

The five base opens really cover all coefficients, because
\[
 1=\tfrac12h(0)-\tfrac12h(1)-\tfrac16h(-1)
                         +\tfrac16h(2)-2A.
\]
The eight global sections are a basis on each of them by
GB3 and GB7. The map from the free module $\mathfrak B^8$
to $\pi_*\mathcal O_X$ that sends its columns to those
sections is therefore an isomorphism on this covering and
hence an isomorphism globally. Explicitly, both its kernel
and cokernel vanish after localization at each of the five
elements; the unit-ideal identity makes any element of either
module vanish, by taking a sufficiently large power of that
identity. Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)
 =\mathfrak B e_0\oplus\mathfrak B e_1\oplus
  \mathfrak B e_2\oplus\mathfrak B e_3
 \oplus\mathfrak B o_0\oplus\mathfrak B o_1\oplus
  \mathfrak B o_2\oplus\mathfrak B o_3
 \quad\text{is free of rank eight.}
 \tag{GB10}
\end{equation}
This proves the assertion at $A=0$ and at every collision
without dividing by the discriminant.

\section{The complete multiplication tensor}
For reproducibility, the coefficient rule underlying every
entry below is explicit. On $A\ne0$ let
\begin{equation}
 Z=\begin{pmatrix}
 0&0&0&-D/A\\1&0&0&-C/A\\0&1&0&-B/A\\0&0&1&-1/A
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 L=4AZ^3+3Z^2+2BZ+CI.
 \tag{GB11}
\end{equation}
Let $f_i(r)=e_i$ and $q_j(r)=o_j/t$ be precisely GB1.
In the global ordered eight-section basis, the eight
multiplication matrices are given by the full block formulas
\begin{equation}
 M_{e_i}=\diag(E^{-1}f_i(Z)E,Q^{-1}f_i(Z)Q),\qquad
 M_{o_j}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&E^{-1}Lq_j(Z)Q\\Q^{-1}q_j(Z)E&0
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{GB12}
\end{equation}
GB3 gives every inverse coefficient, so these are complete
finite expressions. The following tables evaluate them to
polynomial coefficients with no denominator. The column of
$M_a$ indexed by a basis vector $b$ is exactly the coefficient
column on the right of the displayed product $ab$.
Thus the tables specify all entries of all eight matrices;
$M_{e_0}=I_8$ supplies the remaining unit products.

To prove the entries, multiply the displayed polynomials in
GB1. Reduce $r^4$ by $Ar^4=-r^3-Br^2-Cr-D$ and use
$t^2=h'(r)$ for an odd--odd product. Applying GB3 produces
exactly GB12 and the tables. The exact checker performs every
such product and verifies all identities
$M_aM_b=\sum_k[ab]_kM_k$.
Because every coefficient in the evaluated tables is a
polynomial, the identities extend from $A\ne0$ to the full
free module GB10: that module has no $A$-torsion.
This proves the entire algebra law on the actual completion,
including all multiple-root fibres.

\section{Trace determinants without a missing leading coefficient}
Let $\Delta=\Disc(h)$ mean the binary quartic discriminant:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \Delta={}&256A^3D^3-192A^2CD^2-128A^2B^2D^2
 +144A^2BC^2D-27A^2C^4\\
 &+144ABD^2-6AC^2D-80AB^2CD+18ABC^3
 +16AB^4D-4AB^3C^2\\
 &-27D^2+18BCD-4C^3-4B^3D+B^2C^2 .
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{GB18}
\end{equation}
The trace blocks above are computed by the ordinary
multiplication trace in this rank-eight algebra. The single
element trace vector is
$(8,-2,-4B,-6C,0,0,0,0)$, obtained by taking the diagonals
of GB12. In particular every displayed block entry can also
be obtained by applying this vector to its product table.
Parity makes each mixed multiplication matrix off-diagonal,
so its trace is zero.

For $A\ne0$ and distinct roots let $V_r=(r_j^i)_{j,i=0}^3$.
The root-algebra trace Gram in monomials is $V_r^{\mathsf T}V_r$,
whose determinant is $\Delta/A^6$. Its signed extension
doubles that trace on the even summand. On the odd summand
it gives twice the root trace after multiplication by $h'$.
The determinant of multiplication by $h'$ is
$\prod_jh'(r_j)=\Delta/A^2$; the six unordered root-pair
signs have product $(-1)^6=1$. Using GB3 therefore gives
\begin{equation}
 \det T_{ee}=2^4(A^3)^2\frac{\Delta}{A^6}=16\Delta,\qquad
 \det T_{oo}=2^4(A^4)^2
           \frac{\Delta}{A^6}\frac{\Delta}{A^2}=16\Delta^2,
 \qquad \det T=256\Delta^3.
 \tag{GB19}
\end{equation}
Both sides are polynomial coefficient identities by the
full multiplication calculation, so they hold at $A=0$
and on $\Delta=0$ as well. Degeneration of this trace
form does not remove any vector from the free basis GB10.

\section{The original seven-point target with its eighth completed state}
At $(A,B,C,D)=(0,0,-1,0)$ choose $p=2$.
Then $(a_p,b_p,c_p,d_p)=(6,-11,6,-1)$ and
$h_p(R)=R(R-1)(2R-1)(3R-1)$. The transition matrices are
\begin{equation}
 E_2=\begin{pmatrix}
 1&-1&0&1\\0&6&-13&8\\0&-11&38&-32\\0&6&-24&24
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 Q_2=\begin{pmatrix}
 1&0&-1&0\\-6&25&-34&16\\11&-60&108&-64\\-6&36&-72&48
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \det E_2=6^3,\quad\det Q_2=6^4.
 \tag{GB20}
\end{equation}
Their complete inverses are GB8--9 with these exact coefficients.
All eight vectors remain independent. The discriminant is
$\Delta=4$, so the trace determinants are $64$, $256$ and
$16384$ for the even, odd and full blocks respectively.
Every entry at this target is
\begin{equation}
 T_{ee}=\begin{pmatrix}
 8&-2&0&6\\-2&2&0&-2\\0&0&4&0\\6&-2&0&6
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 T_{oo}=\begin{pmatrix}
 -2&0&2&0\\0&6&0&2\\2&0&6&0\\0&2&0&-2
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad T_{eo}=0.
 \tag{GB21}
\end{equation}
The six finite states and the two infinity states have the
following complete evaluation columns:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
 \text{selected root}&\text{sign coordinate}&(e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3)&
                    (o_0,o_1,o_2,o_3)\\\hline
 r=-1&t=\pm\sqrt2&(1,0,-1,1)&(0,t,-t,0)\\
 r=0&t=\pm i&(1,0,0,0)&(0,t,0,-t)\\
 r=1&t=\pm\sqrt2&(1,0,1,1)&(0,t,t,0)\\
 v=0&\theta=\pm i&(1,-1,0,1)&(\theta,0,-\theta,0)
 \end{array}
 \tag{GB22}
\end{equation}
Each row is obtained from GB1 or GB2; the two values of its
sign coordinate are distinct states. At infinity $T=\theta$
by GB4. The original affine source has $b=i=1/\theta$, hence
$\theta=-i$ is its included state and $\theta=i$ is the
omitted state restored by the completion. The original seven
affine states and all eight completed states are therefore
represented with their unchanged sign orientation.
