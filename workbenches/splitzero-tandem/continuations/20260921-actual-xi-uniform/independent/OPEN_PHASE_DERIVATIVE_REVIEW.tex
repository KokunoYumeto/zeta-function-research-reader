This note supplies the complete differentiation and enclosing formulas
for continuing the certified finite original-period zero in the actual
unit phase. It uses the full PCL coefficient family and the original
four factors, with the same coordinates and signs as FPZ1--14.
The numerical phase interval is computed in the receiving calculation;
no unevaluated interval is asserted here to have been certified.

\section{The actual unit and the full conjugation}
Keep the original ordered quartet, $V$, $D_j$ and $R(z)=\mathcal R(z)$.
Write an actual nonzero unit whose real part is nonzero as
\[
 a=\alpha(1+ix),\qquad \alpha\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\},
 \quad x\in\mathbb R,\qquad
 J=\diag(1,-1,-1,1),\quad W(x)=I+ixJ,\quad U=\alpha W.
 \tag{OPD1}
\]
Thus the full original $U$ has entries $(a,\bar a,\bar a,a)$.
Its magnitude is $|a|=|\alpha|\sqrt{1+x^2}$, not a newly assigned
unit magnitude. The identity $J^2=I$ gives
\[
 W^{-1}=\frac{I-ixJ}{1+x^2},\qquad
 M_j(z,x)=U^{-1}VR^{-1}D_jRV^{-1}U
         =W^{-1}VR^{-1}D_jRV^{-1}W.
 \tag{OPD2}
\]
The common $\alpha$ cancels with its exact inverse. PCL7--9 already
proves the separate cancellation of the original scalar, Gamma ray
factors and branch powers in this conjugation; they remain in the
original period matrix $H$ and in its physical-coordinate metrics.
For real $x$, OPD1 is exactly the actual conjugate-unit pattern.
For complex $x$ near a real point, OPD2 defines its holomorphic
extension; the two entries $\alpha(1\pm ix)$ are not asserted to
be complex conjugates off the real phase axis.

\section{Both period derivatives, with every matrix order retained}
Put $A=R^{-1}$. Differentiating $AR=I$ once and twice proves
\[
 A_z=-AR_zA,\qquad
 A_{zz}=2AR_zAR_zA-AR_{zz}A.
 \tag{OPD3}
\]
Here $U,V,D_j$ are fixed with respect to $z$. Therefore
\[
 \begin{split}
 (M_j)_z={}&U^{-1}V
       (AD_jR_z-AR_zAD_jR)V^{-1}U,\\
 (M_j)_{zz}={}&U^{-1}V\bigl(
       AD_jR_{zz}-2AR_zAD_jR_z
       +2AR_zAR_zAD_jR-AR_{zz}AD_jR
                         \bigr)V^{-1}U.
 \end{split}\tag{OPD4}
\]
The first line is the product rule applied to $AD_jR$.
For the second line apply it twice, obtaining
$A_{zz}D_jR+2A_zD_jR_z+AD_jR_{zz}$, and substitute OPD3.
This derives both the coefficient two and every noncommuting order.

An equivalent internal check is useful. Let $C_j=AD_jR$,
$L=AR_z$ and $K=AR_{zz}$. With $[B,C]=BC-CB$,
\[
 (C_j)_z=[C_j,L],\qquad L_z=K-L^2,\qquad
 (C_j)_{zz}=[[C_j,L],L]+[C_j,K-L^2]
 =[C_j,K]-2LC_jL+2L^2C_j.
 \tag{OPD5}
\]
Expanding the commutators gives OPD4, since $RA=I$.

\section{The full first phase and mixed derivatives}
Since $W_x=iJ$ and $J$ commutes with $W$, put
\[
 Q(x)=W^{-1}W_x=\frac{xI+iJ}{1+x^2}.
 \tag{OPD6}
\]
Differentiating $W^{-1}NW$ for the $x$-independent
$N=VR^{-1}D_jRV^{-1}$ gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 (M_j)_x&=[M_j,Q]=\frac{i}{1+x^2}[M_j,J],\\
 (M_j)_{zx}&=[(M_j)_z,Q]
                =\frac{i}{1+x^2}[(M_j)_z,J],\\
 (M_j)_{zzx}&=\frac{i}{1+x^2}[(M_j)_{zz},J].
 \end{split}\tag{OPD7}
\]
The scalar $xI/(1+x^2)$ contributes zero to the commutator,
an exact cancellation. No transpose or adjoint is used here.
For completeness a second phase derivative is
\[
 (M_j)_{xx}=
 -\frac{[[M_j,J],J]+2ix[M_j,J]}{(1+x^2)^2}.
 \tag{OPD8}
\]
This follows by differentiating OPD7 and using OPD7 a second time.
These formulas are well-defined for every real $x$, including
negative $\alpha$, and analytically near each such phase point.

\section{Exact derivatives of the original quadratic factors}
Retain $\rho_a=1/2+w_a$ and $t(\xi)=(e^{\rho_a\xi})_{a=1}^4$.
Define the symmetric bilinear form
\[
 \mathfrak b(v,w)=v_1w_4+v_4w_1-v_2w_3-v_3w_2,
 \qquad Q_0(v)=\tfrac12\mathfrak b(v,v).
 \tag{OPD9}
\]
All products are complex bilinear; complex conjugation would change
the original symbol. Set $y=M_jt(\xi)$ and use subscripts for
partial derivatives, for example $y_z=(M_j)_zt(\xi)$ and
$y_{zx}=(M_j)_{zx}t(\xi)$. The full factor
$f_j(\xi,z,x)=Q_0(y)$ satisfies
\[
 \begin{split}
 f_j&=\tfrac12\mathfrak b(y,y),\qquad
 (f_j)_z=\mathfrak b(y,y_z),\\
 (f_j)_{zz}&=\mathfrak b(y_z,y_z)+\mathfrak b(y,y_{zz}),\\
 (f_j)_x&=\mathfrak b(y,y_x),\qquad
 (f_j)_{zx}=\mathfrak b(y_x,y_z)+\mathfrak b(y,y_{zx}),\\
 (f_j)_{xx}&=\mathfrak b(y_x,y_x)+\mathfrak b(y,y_{xx}).
 \end{split}\tag{OPD10}
\]
The ordinary product rule proves each line. At $\xi=0$,
$t(0)=\mathbf1$, so OPD10 gives $d_j,(d_j)_z,(d_j)_{zz},
(d_j)_x,(d_j)_{zx}$ for $d_j(z,x)=f_j(0,z,x)$ directly.
To retain the original first symbol derivative set
$t_\xi(\xi)=\diag(\rho_a)t(\xi)$ and $y_\xi=M_jt_\xi$.
Then
\[
 (f_j)_\xi=\mathfrak b(y,y_\xi),\quad
 (f_j)_{\xi z}=\mathfrak b(y_z,y_\xi)+\mathfrak b(y,y_{\xi z}),
 \quad
 (f_j)_{\xi x}=\mathfrak b(y_x,y_\xi)+\mathfrak b(y,y_{\xi x}).
 \tag{OPD11}
\]
In particular $y_\xi(0)=M_j(\rho_a)_a$. The original $1/2$
in each $\rho_a$ is retained in all these expressions.

\section{A complete second-derivative series tail}
FPZ5--8 supplies the exact coefficient estimate
$|(R_n)_{ar}|\le C_R(2n+2)T_R^n/n!$. Let $N\ge1$,
$|z|\le\varrho$, $w=T_R\varrho$, and
\[
 q_2=\frac{w(N+3)}{N(N+2)}.
 \tag{OPD12}
\]
When the computed $q_2$ is strictly below one, every entry of
the omitted second derivative is bounded by the explicit number
\[
 \left|R_{zz}(z)-\sum_{n=2}^N n(n-1)R_nz^{n-2}\right|_{ar}
 \le \tau_2:={
 C_R(2N+4)N(N+1)T_R^2w^{N-1}
 \over (N+1)!(1-q_2)}.
 \tag{OPD13}
\]
Indeed the majorant term for $n\ge N+1$ is
$C_R(2n+2)T_R^2w^{n-2}/(n-2)!$.
The successive ratio is
$w(n+2)/((n+1)(n-1))$. Its two factors satisfy
$(n+2)/(n+1)\le(N+3)/(N+2)$ and
$1/(n-1)\le1/N$, proving the upper ratio $q_2$.
The first term and geometric summation give OPD13 exactly.
If $w=0$, the same formula is interpreted by its continuous limit;
the receiving finite disk has $w>0$.

All three derivative polynomials can be evaluated in one Horner pass.
Initialize $P=R_N$, $P_1=P_2=0$ and, for each descending coefficient
$R_{N-1},\ldots,R_0$, use the updates in the stated order
\[
 P_2\leftarrow zP_2+2P_1,\qquad
 P_1\leftarrow zP_1+P,\qquad P\leftarrow zP+R_n.
 \tag{OPD14}
\]
The old values are thereby used on each right-hand side. The
identity $(zP+R_n)''=2P'+zP''$ proves the second update rule,
and $(zP+R_n)'=P+zP'$ proves the first-derivative one.
Adding the full entrywise tails $\tau_0,\tau_1,\tau_2$ to the
three resulting ball matrices encloses the entire original functions.

\section{A two-parameter zero count with quantitative margins}
Let $c$ be the exact FPZ4 centre and put $A_c=(d_1)_z(c,0)$.
For a chosen radius $r>0$ and phase half-width $\varepsilon>0$,
use the full product set
\[
 \mathcal K=\{(z,x):|z-c|\le r,\ -\varepsilon\le x\le\varepsilon\}.
 \tag{OPD15}
\]
A rectangular complex-period enclosure containing the disk can be
used throughout; all segments occurring below remain in that rectangle.
The actual enclosing calculation supplies numbers with the following
meanings:
\[
 \begin{split}
 e_0&\ge|d_1(c,0)|,\qquad m\le|A_c|,\\
 L_x&\ge\sup_{\mathcal K}|(d_1)_x|,\quad
 L_{zx}\ge\sup_{\mathcal K}|(d_1)_{zx}|,\quad
 L_{zz}\ge\sup_{\mathcal K}|(d_1)_{zz}|.
 \end{split}\tag{OPD16}
\]
Integration on the real phase segment gives
$|d_1(c,x)|\le e_0+L_x|x|$ and
$|(d_1)_z(c,x)-A_c|\le L_{zx}|x|$.
Taylor's integral remainder along the complex period segment gives
\[
 |d_1(z,x)-d_1(c,x)-(d_1)_z(c,x)(z-c)|
 \le \tfrac12L_{zz}|z-c|^2.
 \tag{OPD17}
\]
The coefficient $1/2$ is the integral of $1-t$ from zero to one.
Combining the last three inequalities on $|z-c|=r$ proves
\[
 |d_1(z,x)-A_c(z-c)|
 \le e_0+L_x\varepsilon+L_{zx}\varepsilon r
                  +\tfrac12L_{zz}r^2.
 \tag{OPD18}
\]
Thus the arithmetic test to evaluate is precisely
\[
 e_0+L_x\varepsilon+L_{zx}\varepsilon r+\tfrac12L_{zz}r^2<mr.
 \tag{OPD19}
\]
Whenever this strict inequality has been verified by the full
enclosure, the homotopy to the nonzero linear function $A_c(z-c)$
has no boundary zero for any retained real phase. Rouch\'e's
zero count gives exactly one zero, counted with multiplicity, for
each such phase. It is therefore simple. This proves the content
of the test, rather than substituting a presumed zero count.

For choosing a width, bounds may first be evaluated on a larger
trial half-width $\varepsilon_0$. Write
$H=mr-e_0-L_{zz}r^2/2$. Once the evaluated $H$ is positive,
any positive width satisfying
$\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0$ and
$(L_x+rL_{zx})\varepsilon\le H/2$ has a strict zero-count margin
of at least $H/2$. If $L_x+rL_{zx}=0$, no restriction is imposed
by that product. This is an explicit rule for the receiving
arithmetic computation; the chosen numerical width and its
receipt must accompany an asserted phase interval.

A separate useful positive margin is
\[
 \kappa=m-L_{zz}r-L_{zx}\varepsilon.
 \tag{OPD20}
\]
The path $(c,0)\to(c,x)\to(z,x)$ and OPD16 show
$|(d_1)_z(z,x)-A_c|\le L_{zx}\varepsilon+L_{zz}r$,
so $|(d_1)_z(z,x)|\ge\kappa$ throughout the disk whenever
the computed $\kappa$ is positive. That margin also proves
injectivity in $z$ on the disk: integrate along the segment
between any two points and compare with $A_c(z_1-z_2)$.
The count OPD19 already gives simplicity at the unique zero;
OPD20 provides a quantitative derivative bound in addition.

\section{Exact motion of the zero and the unchanged conductor return}
Denote the unique root established by the evaluated OPD19 by $z(x)$.
The simplicity just proved gives a holomorphic implicit root near
each real phase, and uniqueness makes these local roots agree on
their overlaps. Thus the retained real interval has one real-analytic
root curve through the original $z(0)=z_*$. Differentiating the
exact identity $d_1(z(x),x)=0$ gives
\[
 z'(x)=-\frac{(d_1)_x(z(x),x)}{(d_1)_z(z(x),x)}
      =-\frac{\mathfrak b(y,y_x)}{\mathfrak b(y,y_z)},\qquad
 u'(x)=\frac{(d_1)_x(z(x),x)}{z(x)^2(d_1)_z(z(x),x)},
 \quad u(x)=z(x)^{-1}.
 \tag{OPD21}
\]
The reciprocal expression uses a disk excluding zero, as does
the original FPZ4 disk. These derivatives retain the exact actual
unit phase; they are not derivatives of a phase-averaged symbol.
With a positive verified OPD20 margin, one obtains
$|z'(x)|\le L_x/\kappa$ and
$|z(x)-z(0)|\le (L_x/\kappa)|x|$.

The same actual box computation tests the other three $d_j$ and
$(f_1)_\xi$ using OPD10--11. On its verified nonzero region,
the entire first moment and transverse period derivative are
\[
 \begin{split}
 m(x)&=\mu_1(z(x),x)
 =(f_1)_\xi(0,z(x),x)\prod_{j=2}^4d_j(z(x),x)\ne0,\\
 b(x)&=(\mu_0)_z(z(x),x)
 =(d_1)_z(z(x),x)\prod_{j=2}^4d_j(z(x),x)\ne0,\\
 (\mu_0)_x(z(x),x)&=(d_1)_x(z(x),x)
                        \prod_{j=2}^4d_j(z(x),x),\qquad
 z'(x)=-\frac{(\mu_0)_x}{(\mu_0)_z}.
 \end{split}\tag{OPD22}
\]
The product rule proves the identities because the first factor is
exactly zero there. They prove $\ord_{\xi=0}E_A(\xi,z(x),x)=1$.
The prime-five-orbit argument FPZ14 applies without change: any
association among the five quadrics would make $f_1$ identically
zero in $\xi$, contrary to its verified first derivative.

Finally retain the original $s\in\{1,a_{\rm amp}\}$, the two
centres, the Gamma measure and $\rho_n=\sqrt{n!/(s/2)_n}$.
The fixed old $v=0$ matrix in degree $D$ is
\[
 B_{D,s}(z,x)=\diag(\rho_0,\ldots,\rho_D)^{-1}
 T_D\bigl(e^{-4\omega(t)}E_A(\omega(t),z,x)\bigr)
 \diag(\rho_0,\ldots,\rho_D),\qquad\omega=-i\arctan t.
 \tag{OPD23}
\]
At the root its first two scalar coefficients are $0,-im(x)$.
The first column and last row vanish, and the remaining square
block has diagonal $(-im(x))\rho_j/\rho_{j-1}$.
Hence its rank is $D$, its kernel is the original constant
polynomial, and its determinant near the curve is
$\mu_0(z,x)^{D+1}$ with
$\mu_0(z,x)=b(x)(z-z(x))+O((z-z(x))^2)$.
For each retained phase and every exterior rank $1\le r\le D+1$,
the complete original-metric consequence is
\[
 \lim_{z\to z(x)}|z-z(x)|^{D+1}
   \|\bigwedge^r B_{D,s}(z,x)^{-1}\|
 =\frac{\|\bigwedge^{D+1-r}B_{D,s}(z(x),x)\|}
        {|b(x)|^{D+1}}>0.
 \tag{OPD24}
\]
This follows directly from the product of the forward singular
values and the determinant, as in FPZ19--21. There is one inverse
singular value of order $D+1$ and $D$ with finite positive limits.
The actual order-one quotient
$\mathcal P_{D+1}/\mathcal P_0\to\mathcal P_D$ remains invertible
with diagonal $(-im(x))\rho_{j+1}/\rho_j$. Both domains are retained.
The numerical persistence computation must establish the margins
used above before OPD22--24 are asserted for its phase interval.
