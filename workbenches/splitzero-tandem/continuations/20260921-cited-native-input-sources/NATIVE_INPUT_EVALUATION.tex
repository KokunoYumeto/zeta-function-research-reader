The integral construction applies to every real
\(0<\delta<1/2,\gamma>2\) and integer \(m\ge1\). Restricting it to a

\section{A holomorphic integrand with the exact original values}
Put
\begin{equation}\tag{NI1}
 Q(x)=x^2+2(\delta^2-\gamma^2)x+(\delta^2+\gamma^2)^2,\quad
 P(x)=Q(x)^{2m},\quad
 e_n=\int_\R\frac{y^{2n}|g(1/2+iy)|^2}{2\pi P(y^2)}\,dy.
\end{equation}
Pairing the two horizontal factors at each height gives
\(h(1/2+iy)=Q(y^2)^m\), with no sign remaining.
For real \(y\), \(g(1/2-iy)=\overline{g(1/2+iy)}\).
Consequently
\begin{equation}\tag{NI2}
 e_n=\int_0^\infty F_n(y)\,dy,\qquad
 F_n(z)=\frac{z^{2n}g(1/2+iz)g(1/2-iz)}{\pi Q(z^2)^{2m}}.
\end{equation}
The two physical halves account for \(1/\pi\). The product in NI2,
rather than a complex absolute value, is the analytic continuation
used for quadrature. Its denominator zeros are
\(z=\pm\gamma\pm i\delta\). Hence it is holomorphic in
\(|\operatorname{Im}z|<\delta\). The executed callback conservatively
requires \(|\operatorname{Im}z|<\delta/2\) whenever analyticity is
requested. The factorization
\[
 g(s)=s(s-1)\exp[-s\log(\pi)/2]\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)
\]
uses the real constant \(\log\pi\), so introduces no variable
logarithm branch. In that narrower strip \(0<\operatorname{Re}s<1\);
the Gamma and zeta factors have no poles. If a complex ball is not
certified inside this strip, the analytic callback returns a nonfinite
ball, causing subdivision rather than an unsupported analytic bound.
For the undivided moments
\(\beta_n=\int_\R y^{2n}|g(1/2+iy)|^2dy/(2\pi)\),
delete the denominator in NI2 and use the strip
\(|\operatorname{Im}z|<1/4\).

\section{Both omitted tails, with all constants}
The previously proved original Gamma envelope \cite{NP} is
\begin{equation}\tag{NI3}
 \frac{|\Gamma(1/4+iy/2)|^2}{2\pi}
 \le C_\Gamma e^{-\alpha |y|}(1+|y|)^{-1/2},\quad
 C_\Gamma=\sqrt{2\sqrt5}e^{2/3},\quad \alpha=\pi/2.
\end{equation}
The fractional-part formula
\[
 \zeta(s)=\frac{s}{s-1}-s\int_1^\infty\{x\}x^{-s-1}\,dx
 \qquad(\operatorname{Re}s>0,\ s\ne1)
\]
follows by integrating the step function \(\lfloor x\rfloor\)
for \(\operatorname{Re}s>1\) and continuing the absolutely
convergent fractional-part integral to the displayed domain.
On \(s=1/2+iy\), the first term has modulus one and the integral
has modulus at most two. Thus
\(|\zeta(s)|\le1+2|s|\le2(1+|y|)\).
Using \(|s(s-1)|^2=(y^2+1/4)^2\) gives
\begin{equation}\tag{NI4}
 \frac{|g(1/2+iy)|^2}{2\pi}
 \le D e^{-\alpha |y|}(1+|y|)^6,\qquad
 D=\frac{4C_\Gamma}{\sqrt\pi}.
\end{equation}
Here the exponent \(11/2\) from NI3 was bounded by \(6\), increasing
the explicit bound; the actual integrand in NI2 is unchanged.

For any real \(v\), \(Y\ge1\), and
\(b=\alpha-\max(v,0)/Y>0\), logarithmic differentiation gives
\[
 y^v e^{-\alpha y}
 \le Y^v e^{-\alpha Y}e^{-b(y-Y)}\quad(y\ge Y).
\]
Integration proves
\begin{equation}\tag{NI5}
 \int_Y^\infty y^v e^{-\alpha y}\,dy
 \le \frac{Y^v e^{-\alpha Y}}{\alpha-\max(v,0)/Y}.
\end{equation}
Each of the four factors of \(h(1/2+iy)\) has modulus at least
\(\delta\), so \(P(y^2)\ge\delta^{8m}\).
Equations NI4--NI5 and \((1+y)^6\le64y^6\) therefore yield
\begin{equation}\tag{NI6}
 0\le \int_{|y|>Y}\frac{y^{2n}|g(1/2+iy)|^2}{2\pi P(y^2)}\,dy
 \le T_{n,Y}^{\rm all}
 =\frac{128D\delta^{-8m}Y^{2n+6}e^{-\alpha Y}}
 {\alpha-(2n+6)/Y}.
\end{equation}
This guard requires \(Y>(2n+6)/\alpha\), with no requirement
that the cutoff exceed \(\gamma\). The entire resonant region
beyond the cutoff, if present, is included in this bound.
For the undivided measure remove the factor \(\delta^{-8m}\).

A second useful bound applies when
\(Y\ge2R_0,\ R_0=\sqrt{\gamma^2+\delta^2}\).
Every original root difference then has modulus at least \(y/2\).
Put \(v=2n+6-8m\). The same calculation gives
\begin{equation}\tag{NI7}
 T_{n,Y}^{\rm far}=
 \frac{128D\,2^{8m}Y^{v}e^{-\alpha Y}}
 {\alpha-\max(v,0)/Y}.
\end{equation}
Whenever both guards hold, the smaller upper bound may be used.
All comparisons and constants in the implementation use outward
ball arithmetic. No numerical Gamma asymptotic replaces NI3.

\section{The executable certificate and its finite input map}
Johansson's original author source \cite{Johansson}, sections 2--3,
describes analytic ball quadrature, its analytic callback, and the
need to add an explicit improper-integral tail. Its requested
tolerances are guidelines; the returned radius is decisive.
The program performs the following finite operations.
First it doubles an integer cutoff from
\(\max(16,2n+8)\) until the upper tail is below one eighth of the
requested radius. It partitions the finite interval into unit
segments, also splitting at \(\gamma-\delta,\gamma,\gamma+\delta\)
when strictly inside a segment. It integrates NI2 by analytic ball
quadrature on every segment. Each integral includes endpoint and
function-evaluation rounding. It checks finiteness and that zero
belongs to the imaginary enclosure. Finally it adds the real
interval \([0,T_{n,Y}]\) and checks the resulting radius.
\begin{equation}\tag{NI8}
 I_{n,Y}\ni\int_0^YF_n(y)\,dy
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{Re}I_{n,Y}+[0,T_{n,Y}]\ni e_n .
\end{equation}
This implication is the certificate. It follows by NI2, the
nonnegative omitted integrand, and NI6--NI7. An unsuccessful
radius check raises an error and writes no success receipt.
Resource limits are explicit; no claim that every configured
run succeeds is made. Increasing precision, subdivision or
work limits changes the computation, never the mathematical object.

The finite seed theorem gives, with \(D_0=4m\),
\begin{equation}\tag{NI9}
 x^n=P(x)q_n(x)+r_n(x),\quad \deg r_n<D_0,\qquad
 e_n=\sum_{j<D_0}r_{n,j}e_j+\sum_{t\le n-D_0}q_{n,t}\beta_t.
\end{equation}
All coefficients, repeated-pole alternatives and proofs are QS7--QS30.
The exact worst-case input-radius multiplier is
\(\kappa_n=\sum_j|r_{n,j}|+\sum_t|q_{n,t}|\).
For a requested finite list, set
\(\kappa=\max_n\kappa_n\).
Providing each seed and undivided input to radius
\(\tau/(2\kappa)\), and the final arithmetic to radius
\(\tau/2\), gives every output radius at most \(\tau\).
In particular, QS22 proves
\begin{equation}\tag{NI10}
 \sum_{j<D_0}|r_{n,j}|
 \ge \frac{\binom{n}{2m-1}}{\binom{4m-1}{2m-1}}
       (\gamma^2+\delta^2)^{n-4m+1}\quad(n\ge4m-1).
\end{equation}
Thus four seeds for a simple quartet remove repeated integration,
but their required accuracy still increases with output degree.
Parameter enclosures additionally use QS23; their uncertainty is
never erased by assigning exact sample parameters.

For \(m_k=w_h^{*k}\), the exact original sum coordinate gives
\begin{equation}\tag{NI11}
 \zeta_j=j![z^j]\left(\sum_{n=0}^L\frac{e_nz^{2n}}{(2n)!}\right)^k
 \quad(0\le j\le2L),\qquad \zeta_0=e_0^k.
\end{equation}
The complete finite input errors are QS32 or MI17. In particular,
a common divided-moment radius
\(\tau\le\varepsilon/[3k(B+1)^{k-1}(2N)!]\), with
\(\tau\le1/3\) and the original exponential-moment cap \(B\),
supplies the MI21 current and MI22 determinant receivers.
Both sources, their onto maps and their original mass remain fixed.

\section{Residue duality receives the original positive moments}
This section proves the incoming Track III identities \cite{Intake}.
Let \(d\rho\) be the original positive parity measure or its positive
tilt, with all required moments finite and its polynomial Grams
positive definite. Let \(p_n\) be its actual monic orthogonal polynomial,
\(H=[\mu_{i+j}]_{0\le i,j<n}\), and \(C_p\) multiplication by \(z\)
in the basis \(1,z,\ldots,z^{n-1}\) of \(\C[z]/(p_n)\).
Define
\[
 \lambda_p(f)=[z^{n-1}]\operatorname{rem}_{p_n}f,\quad
 J_{ij}=\lambda_p(z^{i+j}),\quad
 q(z)=\int\frac{p_n(z)-p_n(x)}{z-x}\,d\rho(x).
\]
The residue Gram has zero entries for \(i+j<n-1\) and ones on
its anti-diagonal. Thus \(\det J=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}\).
The leading coefficient of \(q\) is the actual mass \(\mu_0\).

The roots \(t_j\) of \(p_n\) are simple and real in the convex hull
of the support: otherwise the product of its sign-changing factors
there has degree less than \(n\), and its product with \(p_n\)
has fixed nonzero sign on a set of positive measure, contrary to
orthogonality. Let
\(L_j(z)=p_n(z)/[(z-t_j)p_n'(t_j)]\).
Lagrange interpolation and the definition of \(q\) give
\(\lambda_p(qf)=\int f\,d\rho\) for \(\deg f<n\).
For \(f=z^{i+j}\), divide by \(p_n\); the quotient degree is at
most \(n-2\), so its integral times \(p_n\) vanishes. Consequently
\begin{equation}\tag{NI12}
 H=Jq(C_p),\qquad
 w_j=\frac{q(t_j)}{p_n'(t_j)}
     =\int L_j^2\,d\rho>0.
\end{equation}
For the second identity, \(L_j^2-L_j\) is divisible by \(p_n\)
with quotient degree at most \(n-2\), and orthogonality applies again.
Both \(H\) and \(J\) are invertible, proving that \(q(C_p)\) is a unit.
This is the exact conversion from a bilinear residue pairing to
the real matrix defining the positive Hermitian coefficient metric.
It preserves the full mass. It gives the classical Gauss resolvent
\(\sum_jw_j/(a+t_j)=-q(-a)/p_n(-a)\) by partial fractions.
The quadrature context and finite-dimensional matrix interpretation
are human results; see \cite{ZDS}, section 2.

For an arbitrary monic real \(h\) of degree \(n\), form its perfect
residue Gram \(J_h\), companion \(C_h\), and define
\([k]=J_h^{-1}(\mu_0,\ldots,\mu_{n-1})^T\).
The defining system proves
\(\lambda_h(kf)=\int f\,d\rho\) for \(\deg f<n\).
Literal polynomial division therefore proves, without simple-root
or discriminant assumptions,
\begin{equation}\tag{NI13}
 H_{ij}=[J_hk(C_h)]_{ij}
       +\int h(x)\operatorname{quo}_h(x^{i+j})\,d\rho(x).
\end{equation}
The entire second term is a finite combination of the same moments.
For \(h=p_n\) it vanishes by orthogonality. An ES quartic receives
its actual nonzero relation term whenever present.
Finally the improved MI floor \(H\succeq h_*I\) gives
\begin{equation}\tag{NI14}
 \norm{q(C_p)^{-1}}_2=\norm{H^{-1}J}_2
 \le h_*^{-1}\norm J_2 .
\end{equation}
This strengthens Track III's interval-only inverse bound by the
already proved moving-interval floor.

\section{One finite uncertainty family, two original minima}
We now prove Track V's receiving formulas and extend their exact
parameter map to raw moments. Let
\(H=H_0+V\Theta V^*>0\), \(H_0>0\),
\(\Theta=\operatorname{diag}(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_d)\) real,
and \(A:\C^n\to\C^r\) the original onto map. Put
\[
 P_0=H_0^{-1},\quad G_0=(AP_0A^*)^{-1},\quad
 L=V^*P_0V,\quad Z=G_0AP_0V,\quad
 K=L-Z^*G_0^{-1}Z,
\]
\[
 B=(P_0-P_0A^*G_0AP_0)V .
\]
The middle matrix in \(B\) is positive semidefinite: writing
\(U=AP_0^{1/2}\), it is
\(P_0^{1/2}[I-U^*(UU^*)^{-1}U]P_0^{1/2}\).
The bracket is the orthogonal projection onto \(\ker U\).
This proves \(K\succeq0\) and \(AB=0\).

The identity obtained by multiplying out \(HH^{-1}=I\) is
\(H^{-1}=P_0-P_0V\Theta(I+L\Theta)^{-1}V^*P_0\).
The determinant \(\det(I+L\Theta)=\det H/\det H_0\) is positive.
Likewise, compression of
\(I+P_0^{1/2}V\Theta V^*P_0^{1/2}>0\) to \(\ker U\)
and the determinant lemma prove \(\det(I+K\Theta)>0\).
Thus every inverse below exists even when a parameter is zero.
Put \(T=\Theta(I+K\Theta)^{-1}=(I+\Theta K)^{-1}\Theta\).
It is Hermitian, and direct substitution gives
\begin{equation}\tag{NI15}
 G=(AH^{-1}A^*)^{-1}=G_0+ZTZ^*,\quad
 X=P_0A^*G_0-BTZ^*,\quad
 \frac{\det G}{\det G_0}=\frac{\det(I+L\Theta)}{\det(I+K\Theta)}.
\end{equation}
For an explicit check of the minimizing identity,
\(HX=A^*(G_0+ZTZ^*)\): expand, use
\(H P_0A^*G_0=A^*G_0+V\Theta Z^*\),
\(HB=V-A^*Z+V\Theta K\), and
\((I+\Theta K)T=\Theta\).
Since \(AX=I\), every \(Hv\)-orthogonality equation against
\(\ker A\) holds, giving the unique minimum.
The determinant ratio follows from the determinant lemma applied to
\(G_0+ZTZ^*\), using
\(L=K+Z^*G_0^{-1}Z\).
For an original onto observation \(\Lambda\), use this same
\(\Theta\) with \(\Lambda A\). Direct substitution yields
\[
 G_B=(\Lambda G_E^{-1}\Lambda^*)^{-1}.
\]
This is the exact nested minimum, with no new independent error
introduced at the second quotient.

Let \(W\) be the fixed original three-column matrix and put
\(F=W^*G_EW\), \(C=W^*\Lambda^*G_B\Lambda W\), \(R=F-C\).
The complete mixed current is
\begin{equation}\tag{NI16}
 I_\times=2\operatorname{Re}
  (\overline{R_{13}}C_{23}+\overline{C_{13}}R_{23}),\qquad
 I_R+I_C+I_\times=2\operatorname{Re}(\overline{F_{13}}F_{23}),
\end{equation}
where \(I_R=2\operatorname{Re}(\overline{R_{13}}R_{23})\)
and \(I_C=2\operatorname{Re}(\overline{C_{13}}C_{23})\).
Expansion of \(F=R+C\) proves the identity with both complex products.

Write \(\Delta_E=\det(I+K_E\Theta)\) and \(G_E=N_E/\Delta_E\),
and similarly for \(B\). In NI15,
\(N_E=G_{0,E}\Delta_E+Z_E\Theta\operatorname{adj}(I+K_E\Theta)Z_E^*\).
Each column of \(I+K_E\Theta\) is affine in its own parameter.
The cofactor multiplying \(\theta_i\) omits that column; hence each
entry numerator and denominator is multiaffine.
Putting the currents over their positive common denominators
gives degrees at most two per parameter for \(I_C\), and at most
four for \(I_R,I_\times\). These bounds apply to fixed \(W\).

If a native monic column varies, let \(I\) insert lower-degree
coefficients, \(q_0\) be its original monic minimum, and put
\(M_0=I^*H_0I\), \(B_0=IM_0^{-1}I^*V\),
\(K_0=V^*IM_0^{-1}I^*V\), \(b=V^*q_0\).
Direct multiplication of the lower-degree normal equations gives
\begin{equation}\tag{NI17}
 q_\theta=q_0-B_0\Theta(I+K_0\Theta)^{-1}b,\quad
 h_\theta=h_0+b^*\Theta(I+K_0\Theta)^{-1}b.
\end{equation}
The correction has zero leading coefficient. Its normal equations
give the minimum; substituting it in the quadratic form proves the
second formula by completing the square. Unit-norm columns also
retain \(h_\theta^{-1/2}\). The fixed-column degree estimate is
not reused after this substitution.

\section{The exact raw-moment parameter map}
For \(H_y=[\zeta_{i+j}]_{0\le i,j<n}\), an error \(\epsilon_\ell\)
in \(\zeta_\ell\) gives
\(\Delta H_y=\sum_{\ell=0}^{2n-2}\epsilon_\ell E_\ell\),
where \((E_\ell)_{ij}=1_{i+j=\ell}\).
With the original coefficient vectors \(e_i\),
\begin{equation}\tag{NI18}
 E_\ell=
 \sum_{\substack{i<j\\i+j=\ell}}
 \frac{(e_i+e_j)(e_i+e_j)^*-(e_i-e_j)(e_i-e_j)^*}{2}
 +\sum_{2i=\ell}e_ie_i^* .
\end{equation}
Expanding each pair proves the formula entry by entry.
Thus NI15 applies using precisely the tied parameters
\(+\epsilon_\ell/2,-\epsilon_\ell/2\), and
\(\epsilon_\ell\) for the diagonal term.
They are images of the same raw-moment variable, not separate errors.
For the original fixed coefficient transport \(T_0\),
\(H_S=T_0^*H_yT_0\); replace every displayed vector by
\(T_0^*e_i\), including the sums and differences.
This proves the exact morphism into the original source coordinates.
Odd moments remain identically zero for the even source.

There are
\[
 r_\ell=\min(\ell+1,\,2n-1-\ell,\,n)
\]
active coordinates in \(E_\ell\); on them it is a reversal permutation.
Therefore \(\operatorname{rank}E_\ell=r_\ell\) and
\(\norm{E_\ell}_2=1\). NI18 uses exactly \(r_\ell\) rank-one columns.
After tying their parameters, the quotient numerator and denominator
have degree at most \(r_\ell\) in \(\epsilon_\ell\).
Thus the fixed-frame observed current has degree at most
\(2r_\ell\), and the kernel and mixed currents at most \(4r_\ell\),
in that raw-moment variable. This follows by substituting the same
variable in the multiaffine formula, not by asserting that a raw
moment changes only one matrix entry.

If \(|\epsilon_\ell|\le\varepsilon\), every row of \(\Delta H_y\)
has absolute sum at most \(n\varepsilon\), and the same holds for
columns. Hence \(\norm{\Delta H_y}_2\le n\varepsilon\).
For an enclosure around a midpoint Gram with verified floor \(h_0>0\),
\(\varepsilon\le h_0/(2n)\) proves positivity of the entire box.
Alternatively, if the actual Gram has the MI floor \(h_*>0\) and
the midpoint is within \(n\varepsilon\), that midpoint has floor
\(h_*-n\varepsilon\); every point of the box is within
\(2n\varepsilon\) of the actual Gram.
The explicit choice \(\varepsilon\le h_*/(4n)\) therefore proves
a floor \(h_*/2\) throughout the box. Congruence by the original
invertible coefficient map preserves this positivity.

Finally a rational-box current numerator can be certified without
discarding its dependence. Map the box explicitly to \([0,1]^d\).
For \(P(t)=\sum_\beta a_\beta t^\beta\) and degree bounds \(d_i\),
its Bernstein coefficients are
\begin{equation}\tag{NI19}
 b_\alpha=\sum_{\beta\le\alpha}a_\beta
 \prod_i\frac{\binom{\alpha_i}{\beta_i}}{\binom{d_i}{\beta_i}}.
\end{equation}
This follows by expanding the identity
\(t^j=\sum_{\ell=j}^d\binom{\ell}{j}\binom d j^{-1}
\binom d\ell t^\ell(1-t)^{d-\ell}\) in each coordinate.
The basis is nonnegative and sums to one, so its coefficient minimum
and maximum enclose the polynomial on the entire box.
Rational subdivision preserves exactness.
A negative point of an enclosing box need not be a feasible point
of the original moment family. The actual current sign requires
a certificate on a box containing its actual inputs.

\section{What the computation establishes}
The undivided mass is checked against the existing original-theta
certificate \cite{AM}. Parameter-family divided seeds are separately
labelled and are checked through the exact identity
\(\beta_0=\sum_{j=0}^{4m}p_je_j\), using a directly integrated extra
moment as well as the seed values. These are tests of the implemented
maps and numerical certificates, not actual zero data.
No absolute arithmetic allocation or individual projected sign is
assigned by this calculation.
The completed contribution is the original integral evaluator,
its complete tails, the finite seed/precision theorem, and the exact
route from those inputs into both native minima and residue conversion.
The programme's remaining values continue to use those original inputs.
