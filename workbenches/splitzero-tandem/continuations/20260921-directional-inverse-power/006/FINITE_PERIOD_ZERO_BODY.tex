\section{A certified finite geometric period with an order-one conductor symbol}
\label{sec:finite-period-zero}

This section constructs a finite complex period of the complete PCL
coefficient family \cite{PCLOriginal} at which the original symbol changes order.
It uses the original four factors, the original exponential variable and
the original Gamma metrics \cite{WCF}. The quartet parameters in this
construction are specified geometric parameters. They are not asserted
to be zeros of the Riemann zeta function. The statement concerns this
coefficient family and its exact ES return; it is not an arithmetic RH
conclusion. The ES marking and human provenance remain FC1--15a and
\cite{ES488}.

\subsection{The full convergent functions and the exact point}
Fix
\[
 \delta=\frac14,\quad\gamma=3,\quad
 \beta=\frac{143}{8},\quad\eta=\frac{21025}{256},\quad
 w=(\delta+i\gamma,\delta-i\gamma,-\delta+i\gamma,-\delta-i\gamma),
 \quad\rho_a=\frac12+w_a.
 \tag{FPZ1}
\]
The unit is on its nonzero real phase $a=\bar a\ne0$. Its magnitude is
unchanged: $U=aI$ cancels with $U^{-1}$ in PCL8. Put
$V_{ar}=w_a^r$ for $0\le r\le3$, $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/5}$ and
$D_j=\operatorname{diag}(\zeta^{-j},\zeta^{-2j},\zeta^{-3j},\zeta^{-4j})$.
Retain every coefficient of the original period:
\[
 \mathcal R(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}R_nz^n,\qquad
 (R_n)_{ar}=\sum_{\substack{p,h\ge0\\2p+4h=5n+r+1-a}}
 \frac{(\beta/3)^p\eta^h}{p!h!}(-5)^{p+h-n}(a/5)_{p+h-n}.
 \tag{FPZ2}
\]
This is precisely PCL2, with row index $1\le a\le4$. The exact
conjugation PCL7--9, including all branch powers and Gamma ray factors,
gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 M_j(z)&=V\mathcal R(z)^{-1}D_j\mathcal R(z)V^{-1},\\
 t(\xi)&=(e^{\rho_a\xi})_{a=1}^4,\quad Q_0(t)=t_1t_4-t_2t_3,\\
 f_j(\xi,z)&=Q_0(M_j(z)t(\xi)),\quad d_j(z)=f_j(0,z),\\
 E_A(\xi,z)&=\prod_{j=1}^4f_j(\xi,z),\quad
 \mu_n(z)=\partial_\xi^n E_A(0,z).
 \end{split}\tag{FPZ3}
\]
No coefficient of $A_z$, including its off-diagonal coefficients, is
discarded by this identity.

The following two terminating decimals specify exact rational numbers:
\[
 \begin{split}
 c_R&=0.011136978231486808477778740306233906621055400363647396688725562781,\\
 c_I&=0.0078780133477760021239711966284294386302928222474756316845213852718,\\
 c&=c_R+ic_I,\qquad r=10^{-14},\qquad
 \mathbb D=\{z:|z-c|<r\}.
 \end{split}\tag{FPZ4}
\]
There is exactly one zero $z_*$ of $d_1$ in $\mathbb D$, counted with
multiplicity. It is simple. Equations FPZ2--4 and this unique-root
condition define the point exactly, rather than specifying it by an
unverified floating-point approximation. The proof follows below.

\subsection{An explicit bound for every omitted series coefficient}
Set $K=11/2$, $C_R=4K^3$ and $T_R=6K^5/5$. Then
$K^2\ge5\beta/3$, $K^4\ge5\eta$ and $K\ge1$. For each term in
FPZ2 let $m=p+h$ and $L=m-n\ge0$. Since $0<a/5<1$,
$(a/5)_L\le L!$. Also
\[
 \frac{n!L!}{p!h!}=\frac{\binom mp}{\binom mn}\le2^m,
 \qquad 2m\le5n+3,\qquad 2^m\le4\,6^n.
 \tag{FPZ5}
\]
The defining constraint gives $K^{2p+4h}\le K^{5n+3}$, and there
are at most $2n+2$ contributing pairs. Multiplying these inequalities
with $5^L=5^{m-n}$ proves the uniform entrywise estimate
\[
 |(R_n)_{ar}|\le C_R(2n+2)\frac{T_R^n}{n!}.
 \tag{FPZ6}
\]
It proves absolute convergence on every bounded disk, including that
of the derivative series; it also bounds every tail actually used here.
Let $N=400$, and let $\rho$ be an outward upper bound for the modulus
on the rectangle $|\Re(z-c)|\le r$, $|\Im(z-c)|\le r$.
With $w=T_R\rho$, the entrywise tails satisfy
\[
 \begin{split}
 \left|\mathcal R-\sum_{n=0}^N R_nz^n\right|_{ar}
 &\le\tau_0=
 \frac{C_R(2N+4)w^{N+1}}{(N+1)!(1-2w/(N+2))},\\
 \left|\mathcal R'-\sum_{n=1}^N nR_nz^{n-1}\right|_{ar}
 &\le\tau_1=
 \frac{C_R(2N+4)(N+1)T_Rw^N}
 {(N+1)!(1-3w/(N+1))}.
 \end{split}\tag{FPZ7}
\]
For the first tail the ratio of successive majorant terms is at most
$2w/(N+2)$. For the derivative tail that ratio is
$w(n+2)/(n(n+1))\le3w/(N+1)$ for $n\ge N+1$.
Both denominators are positive. Summing the resulting geometric
majorants proves FPZ7, including the full infinite remainder.

\subsection{The enclosing calculation and the zero count}
The reproducible calculation is
\texttt{certify\_finite\_period\_zero.py}, with its full ball output in
\texttt{FINITE\_PERIOD\_ZERO\_CERTIFICATE.json}.
It uses Python-FLINT 0.9.0, 768-bit precision and one worker.
The inclusion-preserving arithmetic and outward radius operations are
those of Fredrik Johansson's Arb \cite{human:arb2016}, sections
``Features and example applications'', ``Radii and magnitude bounds''
and ``Precision and bounds''. The original author TeX was read at
those loci; the matrix-tail and zero-count proofs here are the present
calculation, not claims attributed to that software paper.

All rational coefficients in FPZ2 are evaluated as enclosing balls.
Simultaneous Horner evaluation encloses $\mathcal R,\mathcal R'$;
the two tails in FPZ7 are then added to every real and imaginary part.
In particular the complete run proves
\[
 \rho<0.013642,\qquad \tau_0<6.44\cdot10^{-98},\qquad
 \tau_1<2.92\cdot10^{-93}.
 \tag{FPZ8}
\]
The inverse is an enclosing matrix inverse. The independent companion
review also verifies $\|I-B\mathcal R(z)\|_\infty<1.73\cdot10^{-9}$
on the entire rectangle, with $B$ the exact dyadic midpoint of the
computed centre inverse. Thus a convergent Neumann series proves local
invertibility independently of PCL5. This is a certificate for the
full matrix, not for the truncated polynomial alone.

The derivative used in the calculation is exactly
\[
 M'_j=V\bigl(\mathcal R^{-1}D_j\mathcal R'
 -\mathcal R^{-1}\mathcal R'\mathcal R^{-1}D_j\mathcal R\bigr)V^{-1}.
 \tag{FPZ9}
\]
For example, if $y=M_j\mathbf1$ and $y'=M'_j\mathbf1$, then
$d'_j=y'_1y_4+y_1y'_4-y'_2y_3-y_2y'_3$.
The original symbol derivative uses $M_j(\rho_a)_a$, retaining
the original $1/2$ in every $\rho_a$.
On the closed rectangle the certified strict bounds are
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 |d_1(c)|<5\cdot10^{-63},\quad |d'_1(c)|>434,\quad
 \sup|d'_1(z)-d'_1(c)|<8,\\
 \Im\partial_\xi f_1(0,z)>3.68,\qquad
 -0.24<\Re d_2(z)<-0.22,\\
 -2.43<\Re d_3(z)<-2.41,\qquad
 -3.36<\Re d_4(z)<-3.34.
 \end{gathered}\tag{FPZ10}
\]
For $|z-c|=r$, integration of the derivative along the straight
segment from $c$ proves
\[
 |d_1(z)-d'_1(c)(z-c)|
 \le |d_1(c)|+8r<434r<|d'_1(c)(z-c)|.
 \tag{FPZ11}
\]
The homotopy between the two holomorphic functions has no boundary
zero. Its contour integral of the logarithmic derivative is continuous
and integer-valued: isolated zeros locally have the form
$(z-z_0)^m h(z)$ with $h(z_0)\ne0$, so each contributes its
integer multiplicity to that integral. The count is therefore constant.
The linear function has one zero, proving the unique simple zero asserted
after FPZ4. For any two distinct points $z_1,z_2$ in the convex
rectangle, the same segment integration gives
\[
 |d_1(z_1)-d_1(z_2)-d'_1(c)(z_1-z_2)|\le8|z_1-z_2|,
 \qquad |d_1(z_1)-d_1(z_2)|>426|z_1-z_2|.
\]
Thus $d_1$ is injective on the entire rectangle. The other three factors
have no zero anywhere in that rectangle by FPZ10. Therefore $\mu_0$
also has exactly one simple zero in the rectangle as well as in the disk.

At that zero define, with every original factor retained,
\[
 m_*:=\mu_1(z_*)=\partial_\xi f_1(0,z_*)\prod_{j=2}^4d_j(z_*),\quad
 \alpha_*:=\mu'_0(z_*)=d'_1(z_*)\prod_{j=2}^4d_j(z_*).
 \tag{FPZ12}
\]
The enclosing calculation proves $|m_*|>13.80$ and
$|\alpha_*|>1524$. Consequently
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_{\xi=0}E_A(\xi,z_*)=1,\qquad
 \mu_0(z)=\alpha_*(z-z_*)+O((z-z_*)^2),\qquad
 u_*:=z_*^{-1}\in
 [59.8455390191\pm8.74\cdot10^{-11}]
 +i[-42.333202544\pm4.75\cdot10^{-10}].
 \tag{FPZ13}
\]
These are enclosures of the exact constructed point, not replacements
for its definition. The finite period and any local logarithm branch
remain those of PCL1 and PCL7.

\subsection{The five distinct original quadrics at this finite period}
Let $H$ be the full original period matrix at $u_*$ and put
$Q_j(X)=Q_0(H^{-1}D^{-j}X)$ for $j\in\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z$.
Every $Q_j$ is an invertible linear pullback of the rank-four quadratic
$Q_0$. Such a quadratic cannot factor into two linear forms, since a
product of two linear forms has symmetric matrix rank at most two.
Hence each $Q_j$ is irreducible.

The action $X\mapsto D^{-1}X$ on their projective classes has order
five. Every orbit length divides five. An association between any two
distinct indexed quadrics would therefore make this orbit have length
one. It would give $Q_1=\lambda Q_0(H^{-1}\,\cdot)$ for a nonzero
scalar $\lambda$. But $Q_0(t(\xi))=0$ for every $\xi$, because
$\rho_1+\rho_4=\rho_2+\rho_3=1$. Substitution would imply
$f_1(\xi,z_*)=0$ identically, contradicting FPZ10. Thus
\[
 Q_0(H^{-1}\,\cdot),Q_1,Q_2,Q_3,Q_4
 \text{ are five pairwise nonassociated irreducible quadrics.}
 \tag{FPZ14}
\]
This proves the required five-orbit property at the actual constructed
point. It does not infer any separately imposed coordinate-section
coprimality or large-period radius from the orbit property.

\subsection{The exact fixed-space rank loss in every original degree}
Fix $a_{\rm amp}=4\ell+1\ge9$ and $s\in\{1,a_{\rm amp}\}$.
Retain $c_0=a_{\rm amp}/2$, $c_1=c_0-4$, the complete measure
$dm_s$ in FC28, and $\rho_n=\sqrt{n!/(s/2)_n}$ in WCF2.
For every $D\ge0$ consider the \emph{fixed} old $v=0$ spaces
$\mathcal P_D\to\mathcal P_D$ with these exact two norms.
Their matrix, also at the singular parameter, is
\[
 B_{D,s}(z)=\operatorname{diag}(\rho_0,\ldots,\rho_D)^{-1}
 T_D(g(z))\operatorname{diag}(\rho_0,\ldots,\rho_D),\quad
 g(t,z)=e^{-4\omega(t)}E_A(\omega(t),z),\quad
 \omega(t)=-i\arctan t.
 \tag{FPZ15}
\]
WCF3--6 prove this identity in the original orthonormal frames.
In particular $g_0=\mu_0$ and at $z_*$,
\[
 g_{0,*}=0,\quad g_{1,*}=-im_*,\quad
 g_{2,*}=4m_*-\mu_2(z_*)/2,\quad
 g_{3,*}=i\bigl(\mu_3(z_*)/6-2\mu_2(z_*)+25m_*/3\bigr).
 \tag{FPZ16}
\]
The Taylor coefficients of $\omega=-it+it^3/3+O(t^5)$ prove all
four formulas. Every higher coefficient is given by the convergent
FPZ2--3 composition in FPZ15. Explicitly, for every $n$ set
$y_{j,k}=M_j(z_*)(\rho_a^k)_a$; then
\[
 f_{j,n}=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk
 (y_{j,k,1}y_{j,n-k,4}-y_{j,k,2}y_{j,n-k,3}),\quad
 \mu_n(z_*)=\sum_{n_1+\cdots+n_4=n}
 \frac{n!}{n_1!\cdots n_4!}\prod_{j=1}^4 f_{j,n_j}.
 \tag{FPZ17}
\]
Here $f_{1,0}=0$ exactly. Thus no moment needed in any degree is
an unspecified independent input.

For $D\ge1$, at $z_*$ the first column and last row of FPZ15
vanish. Removing them leaves an upper triangular $D$ by $D$ matrix
with diagonal $g_{1,*}\rho_j/\rho_{j-1}$, $1\le j\le D$.
Its determinant is $g_{1,*}^D\rho_D\ne0$. Consequently
\[
 \operatorname{rank}B_{D,s}(z_*)=D,\quad
 \ker B_{D,s}(z_*)=\mathbb C\phi_{0,s,c_0},\quad
 \operatorname{im}B_{D,s}(z_*)=\mathcal P_{D-1},\quad
 \det B_{D,s}(z)=\mu_0(z)^{D+1}.
 \tag{FPZ18}
\]
For $D=0$ the rank is zero and the same kernel and determinant
statements hold. The actual new order is $v=1$; its WCF map is instead
$\mathcal P_{D+1}/\mathcal P_0\to\mathcal P_D$ and is invertible,
with diagonal $(-im_*)\rho_{j+1}/\rho_j$. This gives the exact
map between the fixed-space degeneration and the order-adjusted
quotient, retaining both domains.

\subsection{All inverse exterior poles in the exact original metrics}
Let $n=D+1$, $1\le r\le n$, $\tau=z-z_*$, and
$B_*=B_{D,s}(z_*)$. In a sufficiently small punctured disk FPZ11--13
give $v=0$ and $B_{D,s}(z)$ is invertible. In singular-value order,
the identity
\[
 \|\bigwedge^r B_{D,s}(z)^{-1}\|
 =\frac{\|\bigwedge^{n-r}B_{D,s}(z)\|}
 {|\det B_{D,s}(z)|}
 \tag{FPZ19}
\]
follows by cancelling the product of all $n$ singular values; it also
holds for $r=n$ with the zeroth exterior norm equal to one.
All exterior norms in the numerator converge by continuity of their
matrix entries and the operator norm. FPZ18 makes their limits positive.
Thus the complete leading asymptotes are
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \lim_{z\to z_*}|z-z_*|^{D+1}
 \|\bigwedge^r B_{D,s}(z)^{-1}\|
 =\frac{\|\bigwedge^{D+1-r}B_*\|}{|\alpha_*|^{D+1}}>0.}
 \tag{FPZ20}
\]
There is one inverse singular value with pole $D+1$ and $D$ inverse
singular values with finite positive limits. Indeed FPZ18 leaves exactly
$D$ positive forward singular values; their product tends to
$|g_{1,*}|^D\rho_D$, while their product with the remaining singular
value is $|\mu_0|^{D+1}$. In particular
\[
 \lim_{z\to z_*}|z-z_*|^{D+1}\|B_{D,s}(z)^{-1}\|
 =\frac{\rho_D|m_*|^D}{|\alpha_*|^{D+1}}.
 \tag{FPZ21}
\]
No mass or center has changed: the common $M_s$ cancels only when
expressing the map in its two original orthonormal frames.

For the requested four-dimensional return, $D=3$, the three nonzero
forward singular values are exactly those of
\[
 C_s=\begin{pmatrix}
 g_{1,*}\rho_1&g_{2,*}\rho_2&g_{3,*}\rho_3\\
 0&g_{1,*}\rho_2/\rho_1&g_{2,*}\rho_3/\rho_1\\
 0&0&g_{1,*}\rho_3/\rho_2
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{FPZ22}
\]
This matrix, FPZ12, FPZ16--17 and FPZ2--4 specify every constant
without free coefficients. If $\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\lambda_3>0$
are the roots of $\det(\lambda I-C_s^*C_s)$, the four constants in
FPZ20, in exterior ranks $1,2,3,4$, are respectively
\[
 \frac1{|\alpha_*|^4}
 \left(\rho_3|m_*|^3,\sqrt{\lambda_1\lambda_2},
                           \sqrt{\lambda_1},1\right).
 \tag{FPZ23}
\]
The singular values of the inverse have orders $(4,0,0,0)$.
All four exterior ranks have order four. This is the exact finite-period
mechanism entering the inverse-exterior expression of audit equation
(11). It prevents a bound uniform across this geometric parameter
disk on the fixed old spaces. It does not assert a failure of an estimate
whose period is fixed, whose quotient uses the actual order, or whose
arithmetic parameter set has not been mapped to this disk.

Finally $z-z_* =-(u-u_*)/(uu_*)$. Therefore the original-period version
of FPZ20 has the exact constant
\[
 \lim_{u\to u_*}|u-u_*|^{D+1}
 \|\bigwedge^r B_{D,s}(u^{-1})^{-1}\|
 =|u_*|^{2(D+1)}
 \frac{\|\bigwedge^{D+1-r}B_*\|}{|\alpha_*|^{D+1}}.
 \tag{FPZ24}
\]
Both coordinates and their exact conversion are retained. The remaining
arithmetic question is not answered by assigning this geometric point
to the zeta-zero locus; no such assignment is made here.

\subsection{A finite complex zero exists for every original quartet on this phase}
There is also a general existence result, using the new coefficient
calculation FUT24--26 below. For every original
$0<\delta<1/2$, $\gamma>2$ and real nonzero unit, each of the four
functions $d_j$ has a nonzero finite complex zero. Here is a complete
growth argument; it does not assert the five-orbit condition at every
zero obtained by this existence argument.

In FPZ5--6 choose instead
$K=\max\{1,(5\beta/3)^{1/2},(5\eta)^{1/4}\}$, retaining the actual
$\beta,\eta$. The same bound gives
\[
 |\mathcal R_{ar}(z)|\le 2C_R(1+T_R|z|)e^{T_R|z|}.
 \tag{FPZ25}
\]
PCL5 gives $\mathcal R^{-1}=\operatorname{adj}\mathcal R$.
Each adjugate entry is a sum of six triple products. Write
$v_V=\|V\|_\infty$, $v_I=\|V^{-1}\|_\infty$ and
$H(r)=2C_R(1+T_Rr)e^{T_Rr}$. Then
$\|\operatorname{adj}\mathcal R\|_\infty\le24H^3$ and
$\|\mathcal R\|_\infty\le4H$, so FPZ3 gives
$|d_j(z)|\le2(96v_Vv_I)^2H(|z|)^8$. In particular
$|d_j(z)|\le A(1+|z|)^8e^{B|z|}$ with the full constants
$A=2(96v_Vv_I)^2(2C_R)^8\max(1,T_R)^8$ and $B=8T_R$.
PCL parity, or FUT3's direct parity proof, makes $d_j$ even.
The earlier PZX coefficient and FUT24--26 prove that both its
second and fourth Taylor coefficients are nonzero.

If it had no nonzero zero, $F(z)=d_j(z)/z^2$ would be an entire
zero-free function with $F(0)\ne0$. The integral of $F'/F$ on the
simply connected complex plane defines an entire logarithm $h$ with
$F=e^h$. The growth bound implies
$\Re h(z)\le A_0+B|z|+8\log(1+|z|)$, with the compact disk
absorbed into $A_0$. Write $h(z)=\sum h_nz^n$ and let $M_R$ be
this upper bound on $|z|=R$. Fourier integration of the real part
and its mean-value identity gives, for $n\ge1$,
\[
 |h_n|\le\frac{2(M_R-\Re h(0))}{R^n}.
 \tag{FPZ26}
\]
Indeed subtract $M_R$ inside the nonconstant Fourier coefficient;
the integral of $M_R-\Re h$ is $2\pi(M_R-\Re h(0))$ and this
integrand is nonnegative. Letting $R\to\infty$ proves $h_n=0$
for every $n\ge2$. Hence $F=e^{h_0+h_1z}$. Evenness implies
$e^{2h_1z}=1$ for every $z$, whose derivative at zero gives
$h_1=0$. This would make $d_j$ a constant multiple of $z^2$,
contradicting its nonzero fourth coefficient. A nonzero finite zero
therefore exists for each original factor. FPZ4--14 additionally
construct one particular such zero and prove its precise symbol order
and orbit property; those additional conclusions came from the full
certificate, not from the growth argument alone.
