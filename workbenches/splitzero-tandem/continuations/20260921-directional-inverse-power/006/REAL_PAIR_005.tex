\section{Every original cutoff: the complete two-pole law and its exact resonance}
This section calculates every cutoff of the same original conductor

Retain RPCJ5--12's actual constants $c>0$, $d=-iR$, $e<0$,
$c_3,d_2,e_1,f$. For each integer $D\ge1$ set $N=D+1$ and
\[
 B_{D,s}(z)=\operatorname{diag}(\rho_0,\ldots,\rho_D)^{-1}
          T_D(g(\cdot,z))\operatorname{diag}(\rho_0,\ldots,\rho_D),
 \qquad \rho_j=\sqrt{j!/(s/2)_j}.
 \tag{RPAC1}
\]
Here $T_D(g)_{ij}=g_{j-i}$ for $j\ge i$, and is zero otherwise;
$g(t,z)=e^{-4\omega(t)}E_A(\omega(t),z)$ and
$\omega(t)=-i\arctan t$, exactly RPCJ9. Both original values
$s\in\{1,a_{\rm amp}\}$ retain their full mass
$(2\pi)^{s/2}$ and centers $a_{\rm amp}/2$, $a_{\rm amp}/2-4$.
The displayed matrix uses their orthonormal frames.

Put $\tau=z-z_*$. Since $g_0=c\tau^2+O(\tau^3)$, it is
nonzero in a sufficiently small punctured neighborhood of zero.
There the exact inverse is the weighted triangular matrix of the
coefficients $h_n=[t^n]g(t,z)^{-1}$, $0\le n\le D$.
Define the finite expressions
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 H_n&=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
    (-1)^{n-j}\binom{n-j}{j}
           \frac{d^{n-2j}e^j}{c^{n-j+1}},\qquad H_{-1}=0,\\
 K_n&=-[y^n]\frac{c_3+d_2y+e_1y^2+fy^3}{(c+dy+ey^2)^2}.
 \end{aligned}\tag{RPAC2}
\]
Coefficient extraction in the second line is finite: explicitly it is
$-\sum_{j=0}^{\min(3,n)}a_j\sum_{k=0}^{n-j}H_kH_{n-j-k}$,
where $(a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3)=(c_3,d_2,e_1,f)$.
Every constant on the right is an original derivative in RPCJ5--11.
They are not independently prescribed values.

The complete inverse entries have the Laurent expansions
\[
 h_n(\tau)=\tau^{-n-2}\{H_n+\tau K_n+O(\tau^2)\},\qquad
 cH_0=1,\quad cH_n+dH_{n-1}+eH_{n-2}=0\quad(n\ge1),
 \tag{RPAC3}
\]
where $H_{-2}=0$. To prove both claims, substitute $t=\tau y$
as an exact auxiliary coefficient identity, retaining the inverse
substitution and all powers:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 g(\tau y,z_*+\tau)
 &=\tau^2(c+dy+ey^2)
   +\tau^3(c_3+d_2y+e_1y^2+fy^3)+O(\tau^4),\\
 \tau^2g(\tau y,z_*+\tau)^{-1}
 &=\frac1{c+dy+ey^2}
 -\tau\frac{c_3+d_2y+e_1y^2+fy^3}{(c+dy+ey^2)^2}
 +O(\tau^2).
 \end{aligned}\tag{RPAC4}
\]
These identities hold coefficientwise through each fixed degree $D$;
analyticity gives the stated remainder for every such finite set.
Terms $g_jt^j$ with $j\ge4$ first contribute at order at least four

\subsection{Exact resonance and the impossibility of consecutive zeros}
In terms of the actual derivatives $A=G_z$ and $B=G_\xi$, put
\[
 \lambda=\frac{|B|}{|A|}>0,\qquad
 \theta=\frac{\Re(A\bar B)}{|A||B|}\in[-1,1].\tag{RPAC5}
\]
Their magnitudes remain in every formula through $c=|A|^2$,
$d=-2ic\lambda\theta$, $e=-c\lambda^2$.
Define $U_0(X)=1$, $U_1(X)=2X$ and
$U_n(X)=2XU_{n-1}(X)-U_{n-2}(X)$. These are the explicitly
defined polynomials; the familiar Chebyshev notation introduces
no external bound. Induction in RPAC3 proves
\[
 H_n=\frac{(i\lambda)^n}{c}U_n(\theta),\qquad
 H_{n-1}^2-H_nH_{n-2}=\frac{e^{n-1}}{c^{n+1}}
                      \quad(n\ge1).\tag{RPAC6}
\]
For the second identity, its left side at $n=1$ is $c^{-2}$.
Substitution of the recurrence makes the left side at $n+1$
equal to $e/c$ times its value at $n$, proving it for all $n$.
In particular $H_D$ and $H_{D-1}$ cannot vanish together.

The angle addition formula for sine verifies the same recurrence
for $\sin((n+1)\phi)/\sin\phi$, with the same initial terms.
The endpoints give $U_n(1)=n+1$ and
$U_n(-1)=(-1)^n(n+1)$ by the recurrence. It follows that the
complete zero set is
\[
 H_D=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \theta=\cos\frac{k\pi}{D+1}\text{ for some }k=1,\ldots,D.
 \tag{RPAC7}
\]
Indeed these are $D$ distinct roots of the degree-$D$ polynomial,
whose leading coefficient is $2^D$, so there are no other roots.
This characterizes all exceptional cutoffs without discarding any
original derivative or asserting that a finite interval decides
infinitely many equalities.

\subsection{The entire limiting forward block and all exterior constants}
