$\mathcal H_\xi(h)=h-\Lambda^{-1}G(\xi,p_*+h)$, with complex outputs

Differentiating $G(\xi,p(\xi))=0$ twice proves the formula.  Because

\subsection{All resonance rays in the original coordinates}

put $\phi_k=k\pi/(D+1)$ and define the exact real vectors
\[
 \begin{aligned}

 &=\Lambda^{-1}(rB e^{i\varepsilon\phi_k})\\
 &=\frac r\Delta

 \end{aligned}\tag{RPD20}
\]
Their actual values satisfy $A_v=rB e^{i\varepsilon\phi_k}$,
$c_v=r^2|B|^2$, $R_v=2r|B|^2\cos\phi_k$ and
$\lambda_v=1/r$.  No magnitude is discarded: $r$ remains arbitrary
and all its powers remain in RPD9--19.
They give every resonant direction.  In fact for any nonzero $v$
the number $A_v/B$ is nonzero, and the equality
$\Re(A_v/B)/|A_v/B|=\cos\phi_k$ forces
$A_v/B=r e^{i\phi_k}$ or $r e^{-i\phi_k}$ for its actual $r>0$.
Applying the inverse in RPD4 proves exhaustiveness.

For positive rays there are exactly $2D$ distinct choices, since
their image arguments $\pm\phi_k$ are distinct modulo $2\pi$.
The exact duplication upon forgetting orientation is
\[
 v_{D,D+1-k,-\varepsilon}(r)=-v_{D,k,\varepsilon}(r).
 \tag{RPD21}
\]
Thus there are exactly $D$ unoriented real lines.  There is one
line with $\theta=0$ precisely when $D$ is odd; it corresponds to
$k=(D+1)/2$.  All remaining values of $\theta$ are paired with
$-\theta$.  Under any real scaling $\ell\ne0$, direct substitution
in RPD9 and RPD13 gives
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \theta_{\ell v}=\operatorname{sgn}(\ell)\theta_v,\quad
 H_n(\ell v)=\ell^{-n-2}H_n(v),\quad
 K_n(\ell v)=\ell^{-n-1}K_n(v),\\
 c_{\ell v}=\ell^2c_v,\ d_{\ell v}=\ell d_v,\
 c_{3,\ell v}=\ell^3c_{3,v},\ d_{2,\ell v}=\ell^2d_{2,v},\
 e_{1,\ell v}=\ell e_{1,v}.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{RPD22}
\]
The constants $e,f$ stay fixed.  This proves resonance invariance
under every nonzero real scaling, including the signs of $H_n,K_n$.
An inverse pole of exponent $p$ has its norm constant multiplied by
$|\ell|^{-p}$, exactly as follows also from the literal reparameterization
$p_*+\tau\ell v=p_*+(\ell\tau)v$.

These formulas also classify repetitions across all cutoffs.  An
unoriented direction has a unique image argument
$\phi\in[0,\pi)$ for the line $\mathbb R(A_v/B)$.
It is resonant for some cutoff precisely when
$\phi/\pi=m/q$ is rational with $0<m<q$ and $\gcd(m,q)=1$;
its resonant cutoffs are exactly $D=jq-1$, $j\ge1$.
The line $\phi=0$ and every irrational argument are nonresonant at
every cutoff.  Two cutoffs $D,E$ share exactly
$\gcd(D+1,E+1)-1$ resonance lines: the common fractions strictly
between zero and one are the multiples of the reciprocal of that
greatest common divisor.  This follows by reducing the fractions
in RPD20; it is an exact classification, not an interval test for
an infinite list of equalities.

\subsection{Curvature correction and the complete resonance transition}

Let a real path have
$p(\tau)=p_*+\tau v+\tfrac12\tau^2a+O(\tau^3)$, $v\ne0$.
Twice differentiating $G(\xi,p(\tau))$ shows that RPD9--14 still
hold with
\[
 C_v\longmapsto C_v+\Lambda(a),\qquad
 K_n^{\rm path}=K_n(v)+\tfrac12 DH_n(v)[a].\tag{RPD23}
\]
Here $H_v,F,B$ are unchanged to the orders in question.
To verify the second equality directly, the increments are
$\delta c_3=\Re(\Lambda(a)\overline{A_v})$
and $\delta d_2=-i\Re(B\overline{\Lambda(a)})$, with no increment
in $e_1,f$.  They equal $\tfrac12 Dc_v[a]$ and
$\tfrac12 Dd_v[a]$.  Differentiating $q_v^{-1}$ in RPD13 therefore
gives exactly the additional half derivative.  The full resonant
block uses this corrected upper-right coefficient; a tangent
direction alone does not specify that coefficient.

We now prove a uniform transition formula rather than apply a
fixed nonresonant asymptotic at changing directions.  Fix any
resonant nonzero $v$ for cutoff $D$ and any real $w$ transverse to
its line.  The derivative
$\kappa_D=DH_D(v)[w]$ is nonzero.  To see this, use the exact
transverse vector $w_0=\Lambda^{-1}(iA_v)$.
It has $Dc_v[w_0]=0$ and
$D\theta_v[w_0]=-\Im(A_v\bar B)/(|A_v||B|)\ne0$ at each
interior root in RPD15.  Those polynomial roots are simple, so
$DH_D(v)[w_0]\ne0$.  Homogeneity in RPD22 gives
$DH_D(v)[v]=-(D+2)H_D(v)=0$.
Every real $w$ is a unique real combination of $v,w_0$ because
their images are $A_v,iA_v$; transversality means its $w_0$
coefficient is nonzero.  This proves the assertion and also gives
the exact derivative
\[
 DH_D(v)[w_0]=\frac{(i\lambda_v)^D}{c_v}
 U_D'(\theta_v)\frac{-\Im(A_v\bar B)}{|A_v||B|}.\tag{RPD24}
\]

For real $\tau>0$ and $\epsilon\to0$, write
$v_\epsilon=v+\epsilon w$ and evaluate the literal point
$p_*+\tau v_\epsilon$.  On a fixed compact neighborhood of $v$,
$c_{v'}$ is bounded away from zero.  The complete analytic
coefficient expansion proving RPD14 has uniformly bounded
remainders there.  It follows entry by entry that
\[
 \tau^{D+1}B_{D,s}(p_*+\tau v_\epsilon)^{-1}
 =\iota\begin{pmatrix}
 H_{D-1}(v_\epsilon)\rho_{D-1}/\rho_0&
 \{H_D(v_\epsilon)/\tau+K_D(v_\epsilon)\}\rho_D/\rho_0\\
 0&H_{D-1}(v_\epsilon)\rho_D/\rho_1
 \end{pmatrix}+O(\tau).
 \tag{RPD25}
\]
Here $\iota$ inserts the $2\times2$ corner at rows $0,1$ and columns
$D-1,D$.  For $D=1$ it is the identity insertion.  The error is in
the original matrix norm and is uniform for these $v_\epsilon$.
The two diagonal corner errors are $O(\tau)$ by RPD14 at degree
$D-1$; the top-right error is $O(\tau)$ at degree $D$; every other
entry has degree at most $D-2$ and hence is $O(\tau)$ or smaller.
This proves the formula, including its uniformity and all weights.

If $\epsilon(\tau)/\tau\to L\in\mathbb R$, the two divergent
inverse singular values both have pole $D+1$, with constants the
two singular values of
\[
 N_{D,s}(v;L,w)=\begin{pmatrix}
 H_{D-1}(v)\rho_{D-1}/\rho_0&
 \{K_D(v)+L\kappa_D\}\rho_D/\rho_0\\
 0&H_{D-1}(v)\rho_D/\rho_1
 \end{pmatrix}.\tag{RPD26}
\]
Indeed $H_D(v+\epsilon w)=\kappa_D\epsilon+O(\epsilon^2)$,
so RPD25 converges to the displayed invertible block.  Its
determinant modulus is $L_{2,D,s}(v)$ by RPD15--16, independent
of $L$.  All remaining inverse singular values have the same
finite positive limits as before.  If instead
$|\epsilon(\tau)|/\tau\to\infty$ while $\epsilon(\tau)\to0$,
RPD25's single growing entry and the same determinant identity give
\[
 \begin{aligned}

     |\kappa_D|\frac{\rho_D}{\rho_0}
                   |\epsilon(\tau)|\tau^{-D-2},\\
 \sigma_2(B_{D,s}^{-1})&\sim
     \frac{L_{2,D,s}(v)\rho_0}{|\kappa_D|\rho_D}
                   |\epsilon(\tau)|^{-1}\tau^{-D}.
 \end{aligned}\tag{RPD27}
\]
For every $2\le r\le N$ the exterior asymptotic is always
$L_{r,D,s}(v)\tau^{-2N}$, since the direction tends to $v$ and
$g_0/\tau^2\to c_v$.  Thus no divergent crossover coefficient is
hidden inside a falsely uniform fixed-direction estimate.

In particular take $\epsilon(\tau)=q_0\tau^q$, $q_0\ne0$, $q>0$.
For $0<q<1$ the two pole exponents are
$(D+2-q,D+q)$, with constants
