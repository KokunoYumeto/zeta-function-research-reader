\paragraph{Scope and provenance.}
The input is the actual first marked column of the original conductor
in FC5, FC15a, OFM17 and ARR1--12 \cite{FC,ARR}. Its four-point
coordinates retain the programme's attribution to the canonical
488-page ES reader, and that reader's attribution of the initial
polynomial example to Levent Alp\"oge, Fable and Terence Tao.
The present result is an exact extension in the collision centre,
not a replacement of the period-dependent moments. The original
period remains real and admitted. Thus OFM6--11 gives the actual
$v\in\{0,2,\ldots,32\}$, $\mu_n\in\R$, and $\mu_v>0$.
All four original labels, every moment and both original Gamma

by choosing an arbitrary real vector of four moments.

\section{The full coefficient map at an arbitrary complex centre}
Retain $0<\delta<1/2$, $\gamma>2$, the original real $\kappa$, and
\[
 \rho=(1/2+\delta+i\gamma,1/2+\delta-i\gamma,
       1/2-\delta+i\gamma,1/2-\delta-i\gamma),\qquad
 K=\begin{pmatrix}
 0&i&1/\sqrt2&1/\sqrt2\\
 0&-1&-1-\sqrt2i&1-\sqrt2i\\
 i/2&-3i&2\sqrt2+6i&-2\sqrt2+6i\\
 0&13&-34-19\sqrt2i&34-19\sqrt2i
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{CJR1}
\]
The entries have not been rescaled. Write $k=K^{-1}e_1$ and
\[
 b(X)=\sum_{a=1}^4k_a
 \frac{\prod_{c\ne a}(X-\rho_c)}{\prod_{c\ne a}(\rho_a-\rho_c)}
 =\sum_{q=0}^3b_qX^q.
 \tag{CJR2}
\]
The quartet is distinct and $\det K=77\sqrt2i/2\ne0$.
Consequently CJR2 is exactly the coefficient vector
$(V_\rho^{\mathsf T})^{-1}K^{-1}e_1$, with inverse evaluation
$b(\rho_a)=k_a$. This supplies every original coefficient by a
finite formula in the unchanged $\delta,\gamma$ and $K$.

Put $P=\Re P+i\Im P$, $x=\Re P+\kappa$, and $y=\Im P$.
For $0\le q\le3$ define
\[
 \eta_q(x)=\sum_{r=0}^q\binom{v+q}{r}\mu_{v+q-r}x^r,
 \qquad
 \mu_{v+q}=\sum_{r=0}^q\binom{v+q}{r}(-x)^r\eta_{q-r}(x).
 \tag{CJR3}
\]
Both formulas retain all four actual moments. Their compositions
are the identity: the coefficient at shift degree $j$ in either
composition is $\binom{v+q}{j}(x-x)^j$. In particular $\eta$ is
real and $\eta_0=\mu_v>0$.
For the complex displacement the binomial theorem gives
\[
 \eta_q(x+iy)=\sum_{r=0}^q\binom{v+q}{r}(iy)^r\eta_{q-r}(x).
 \tag{CJR4}
\]
For completeness, after inserting CJR3, the coefficient of
$\mu_{v+q-j}$ on the right is
$\binom{v+q}{j}\sum_{r=0}^j\binom jr(iy)^rx^{j-r}
=\binom{v+q}{j}(x+iy)^j$.

The original conductor therefore gives exactly
\[
 \begin{split}
 \beta_j(y)&=\sum_{q=j}^3\binom{v+q}{q-j}(iy)^{q-j}b_q,\\
 m_1(P)&=\sum_{j=0}^3\beta_j(y)\eta_j(x),\\
 m'_1(P)&=\sum_{j=0}^2(v+j+1)\beta_{j+1}(y)\eta_j(x),\\
 C_v(y)&=\begin{pmatrix}
 \Re\beta_0&\Re\beta_1&\Re\beta_2&\Re\beta_3\\
 \Im\beta_0&\Im\beta_1&\Im\beta_2&\Im\beta_3\\
 (v+1)\Re\beta_1&(v+2)\Re\beta_2&(v+3)\Re\beta_3&0\\
 (v+1)\Im\beta_1&(v+2)\Im\beta_2&(v+3)\Im\beta_3&0
 \end{pmatrix},\\
 C_v(y)\eta(x)&=(\Re m_1(P),\Im m_1(P),
                         \Re m'_1(P),\Im m'_1(P))^{\mathsf T}.
 \end{split}\tag{CJR5}
\]
The derivative coefficient follows from
$(v+q)\binom{v+q-1}{q-1-j}=(v+j+1)\binom{v+q}{q-1-j}$.
Thus CJR5 is the full required real four-by-four map, including
all terms through $y^3$ and both components of both complex jets.

\section{An exact quartic determinant and its entire real exceptional set}
Here are finite coefficient expressions making the determinant small
enough to read while retaining an explicit inverse to every coordinate
operation. Direct expansion of CJR2 gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 D_0&=616\delta\gamma(\delta^2+\gamma^2),\\
 u&=355\sqrt2\gamma-214\delta,
 \qquad w=308\sqrt2\delta+210\gamma,\\
 b_3&=(u+iw)/D_0,\qquad Q=u^2+w^2>0,\\
 b_2&=\frac{-(172\delta^2-642\delta+172\gamma^2+
                   1065\sqrt2\gamma)/2
       +7i(44\sqrt2\delta^2-66\sqrt2\delta+
                    44\sqrt2\gamma^2-45\gamma)}{D_0}.
 \end{split}\tag{CJR6}
\]
In particular $w>0$ and $b_3\ne0$. Define the exact coefficient
ratios and order constants
\[
 a=b_2/b_3=a_R+ia_I,\quad b=b_1/b_3=b_R+ib_I,
 \quad c=b_0/b_3=c_R+ic_I,
 \qquad A=v+1,\ B=v+2,\ C=v+3.
 \tag{CJR7}
\]
These names leave $b_3$ intact. Multiplication by $b_3$ is the
inverse operation. More explicitly, if
$M(b_3)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\Re b_3&-\Im b_3\\
\Im b_3&\Re b_3\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, then the exact identity is
\[
 C_v(y)=\operatorname{diag}(M(b_3),M(b_3))\,\widetilde C_v(y),
 \tag{CJR8}
\]
where the full matrix on the right is
\[
\widetilde C_v(y)=
\begin{pmatrix}
 c_R-Ayb_I-\frac{AB}{2}y^2a_R&
 b_R-Bya_I-\frac{BC}{2}y^2&a_R&1\\
 c_I+Ayb_R-\frac{AB}{2}y^2a_I-\frac{ABC}{6}y^3&
 b_I+Bya_R&a_I+Cy&0\\
 A(b_R-Bya_I-\frac{BC}{2}y^2)&Ba_R&C&0\\
 A(b_I+Bya_R)&B(a_I+Cy)&0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{CJR9}
\]
This is an equality of actual matrices, not a change in the original
moments, norms or marked state.

Set
\[
\begin{split}
 h&=a_I+Cy,\qquad D=b_I-\frac{B}{C}a_Ra_I,\\
 E&=c_I-\frac{A}{C}a_Ib_R-\frac{AB}{3C^2}a_I^3,\\
 L&=BE-\frac{AB}{C}a_RD,\qquad
 q=\frac{AB^2}{3C^2}>0,\\
 G(h)&=qh^4+Lh-AD^2.
\end{split}\tag{CJR10}
\]
All symbols here are finite expressions in the original coefficients
CJR2 and the actual order $v$. The complete determinant is
\[
 \boxed{\det C_v(y)=|b_3|^4 C\,
          G(a_I+Cy).}
 \tag{CJR11}
\]
To prove it, subtract $h/C$ times row three of CJR9 from row two.
The latter row becomes
$(E+ABh^3/(3C^2),D,0,0)$, while row four is
$(A(D+Ba_Rh/C),Bh,0,0)$.
Expand the determinant in column four and then column three.
The result is $C$ times
\[
 Bh\left(E+\frac{AB}{3C^2}h^3\right)
     -AD\left(D+\frac{Ba_R}{C}h\right)=G(h).
\]
Finally $\det M(b_3)^2=|b_3|^4$, proving CJR11 with its sign.
This proves the entire quartic, including every coefficient; expanding
the finite definitions CJR2, CJR6--7 and CJR10 recovers a rational
expression in $\delta,\gamma,v,y$ without any unspecified term.

The sign locating all its real roots is particularly useful. CJR6
gives the exact identity
\[
 a_I=\frac{28(8455\gamma-1408\sqrt2\delta)
                   (\delta^2+\gamma^2)}{Q}>0.
 \tag{CJR12}
\]
For the last inequality, $\gamma>2$, $\delta<1/2$ and
$\sqrt2<3/2$ give
$8455\gamma-1408\sqrt2\delta>16910-1056>0$.
ARR5--9 prove $\det C_v(0)>0$ on the entire retained domain;
CJR11 therefore gives $G(a_I)>0$.

If $D\ne0$, $G(0)=-AD^2<0$ and $G(h)\to+\infty$ at both
ends of the real line. Its derivative $4qh^3+L$ is strictly
increasing and has exactly one zero. Hence $G$ decreases strictly
to its unique minimum and increases strictly thereafter. It has
exactly two real simple roots, denoted $h_-<0<h_+$. Since
$G(a_I)>0$ and $a_I>0$, necessarily
\[
 h_-<0<h_+<a_I,\qquad
 y_-=(h_--a_I)/C<-a_I/C<y_+=(h_+-a_I)/C<0.
 \tag{CJR13}
\]
These are exactly the two singular heights of the original matrix.
They are exact real roots of the displayed polynomial, distinguished
by the disjoint half-lines $h<0$ and $h>0$; no sample
or numerical root choice enters their definition.

If $D=0$, factorization gives
\[
 G(h)=h(qh^3+L).
 \tag{CJR14}
\]
For $L\ne0$ the two real simple roots are $0$ and the unique real
$h_c$ satisfying $h_c^3=-L/q$. If $h_c>0$, the same positivity
$G(a_I)>0$ forces $h_c<a_I$; if $h_c<0$, this inequality already
holds. Both singular heights are therefore strictly negative.
For $D=L=0$, the sole root is $h=0$ with multiplicity four,
again giving the strictly negative height $-a_I/C$.
CJR13--14 classify every real height and every degeneration of this
determinant on the original parameter domain.

\section{The entire kernel, including its positive-leading-moment part}
The classification of a singular matrix alone does not assert that
the original moments lie in its kernel. We calculate the kernel and
the inverse moment map explicitly to locate exactly this distinction.
Put
\[
 R_1=b_R-Bya_I-BCy^2/2,\qquad
 R_0=c_R-Ayb_I-ABy^2a_R/2.
 \tag{CJR15}
\]
At every singular height with $h\ne0$, the whole real kernel is
one-dimensional. Its vectors, with real parameter $t$, are exactly
\[
\begin{split}
 \eta_0&=t,\\
 \eta_1&=-\frac{A(D+Ba_Rh/C)}{Bh}\,t,\\
 \eta_2&=-\frac{AR_1\eta_0+Ba_R\eta_1}{C},\\
 \eta_3&=-R_0\eta_0-R_1\eta_1-a_R\eta_2.
\end{split}\tag{CJR16}
\]
Indeed row four in CJR9 fixes $\eta_1$, row three fixes
$\eta_2$, and row one fixes $\eta_3$. The remaining equation is
the determinant equation $G(h)=0$. Conversely these substitutions
satisfy all four rows, proving both necessity and sufficiency.
Nonzero kernel vectors have $t\ne0$, and the part with a positive
leading moment has exactly $t>0$.

The value $h=0$ is singular precisely when $D=0$. At that height
the reduced row two is $(E,0,0,0)$ and row four is zero.
If $L=BE\ne0$, all kernel vectors instead satisfy
\[
 \eta_0=0,\qquad \eta_1=t,\qquad
 \eta_2=-Ba_Rt/C,\qquad
 \eta_3=-R_1t-a_R\eta_2.
 \tag{CJR17}
\]
These cannot be the actual moments because $\mu_v>0$.
There is also an exact quantitative component obstruction at this
singular height. In CJR9, $h=D=0$ makes the second row exactly
$(E,0,0,0)$: its second entry is $D=0$, and its first entry is
$E$ by the already proved row identity. Since $E=L/B\ne0$,
the actual moment vector gives
\[
 \Im\bigl(m_1(P)/b_3\bigr)=E\mu_v,\qquad
 |m_1(P)|\ge |b_3E|\mu_v>0.
 \tag{CJR17a}
\]
The inequality follows from $|z|\ge|\Im z|$ and multiplication
by the unchanged $|b_3|$. Thus this determinant-singular height
has a nonzero actual jet component with its exact constant.
If $D=L=0$, row two is also zero and the whole kernel is
\[
 \eta_0=t_0,\quad \eta_1=t_1,\quad
 \eta_2=-(AR_1t_0+Ba_Rt_1)/C,\quad
 \eta_3=-R_0t_0-R_1t_1-a_R\eta_2.
 \tag{CJR18}
\]
The kernel dimension is exactly two; rows one and three remain
independent because their last two nonzero pivots are $1$ and $C$.
Its positive-leading-moment part has exactly $t_0>0$.

For any of these real vectors, CJR3 supplies the unique associated
four real moment entries at each real $x$. Thus CJR16--18 and
CJR3 are the complete forward and inverse finite correspondence
between exceptional jets and four moment entries. A real admitted
period produces a repeated jet exactly when its own entries
$(\mu_v,\ldots,\mu_{v+3})$ satisfy these displayed equalities.
No assertion that an arbitrary listed vector is produced by a
period has been used or proved.

\section{The excluded region and the original norms}
Every singular height in CJR13--14 is strictly negative. Therefore
the actual real-period receiver obeys
\[
 (m_1(P),m'_1(P))\ne(0,0)
 \qquad\text{for every }P\text{ with }\Im P\ge0.
 \tag{CJR19}
\]
This extends ARR10 to the entire closed upper half-plane.
There is also an exact wider horizontal strip. Let $h_*$ be the
largest real root of $G$. CJR13--14 construct this root in all
cases and prove $h_*<a_I$. Set
\[
 y_*=(h_*-a_I)/C<0.
 \tag{CJR20}
\]
Then $C_v(y)$ is invertible for every $y>y_*$. The conclusion
CJR19 holds on this larger open half-plane as well. At the endpoint
the precise positive-moment possibilities, including the eliminated
$\eta_0=0$ stratum, are exactly CJR16--18.

Away from the finite singular heights, the actual Euclidean jet
has the rigorous lower bound
\[
 \|(m_1(P),m'_1(P))\|_2
 \ge \frac{|\det C_v(y)|}{\|C_v(y)\|_F^3}\,\mu_v>0.
 \tag{CJR21}
\]
The least singular value of an invertible four-by-four matrix is
the absolute determinant divided by its three other singular
values, each bounded by its Frobenius norm. Apply this to the real
vector CJR3, whose norm is at least $\eta_0=\mu_v$.
This proves CJR21 without changing the original receiving norm.

For the actual two-root collision path EQR1--6 \cite{EQR}, retain
all four choices $\epsilon\in\{0,1\}$ and both signs of
$\lambda^2=(2\epsilon-1)3P/22$, with $P\ne0$ and $t=w_0^2$.
At every centre whose height is nonsingular, the complete original
quotient states and each original Gamma metric $G_{b,t}$, $b=U,W$,
satisfy
\[
\begin{split}
 w_0Y&\longrightarrow U_1/\lambda,
       \qquad U_1=(m_1(P),m'_1(P))^{\mathsf T},\\
 |t|^{1/2}\|Y\|_{G_{b,t}}
 &\longrightarrow \frac{\sqrt{U_1^*G_{b,0}U_1}}{|\lambda|}\\
 &\ge \frac{\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}(G_{b,0})}}{|\lambda|}
       \frac{|\det C_v(y)|}{\|C_v(y)\|_F^3}\,\mu_v>0.
\end{split}\tag{CJR22}
\]
EQR2 gives the vector limit; continuity of its positive original
Gram gives the norm limit. Thus all four labelled states escape
in each original quotient metric on the stated region, retaining
the full metric constants. The complete complement remains EQR3--4.
The possible lower-half-plane exceptions have been reduced to the
explicit one- or two-dimensional moment spaces CJR16--18. Their
intersection with the actual period family has not been asserted.
No original conductor order jump or RH endpoint follows from
the present jet classification alone.

\section{The complete finite list of candidates for each actual period}
The real coordinate of each remaining point can also be calculated
from the actual moments. At every exceptional root $h\ne0$, take
$\eta^*$ to be CJR16 with $t=\mu_v$, at its fixed height
$y=(h-a_I)/C$. Define
\[
\begin{split}
 x_h&=\frac{\eta^*_1-\mu_{v+1}}{A\mu_v},
       \qquad P_h=x_h-\kappa+iy,\\
 J_2(h)&=\mu_{v+2}+B\mu_{v+1}x_h+
                      \frac{AB}{2}\mu_vx_h^2-\eta^*_2,\\
 J_3(h)&=\mu_{v+3}+C\mu_{v+2}x_h+
        \frac{BC}{2}\mu_{v+1}x_h^2+
        \frac{ABC}{6}\mu_vx_h^3-\eta^*_3.
\end{split}\tag{CJR23}
\]
There is exactly one candidate at that height, and it is a repeated
jet exactly when the two displayed real residuals vanish. To prove
this, the first two equalities $\eta_0=\mu_v$ and
$\eta_1=\mu_{v+1}+A\mu_vx$ force the displayed $x_h$.
The next two components of CJR3 are precisely the two residuals.
CJR16 then proves both directions, including all four real jet
equations. This formula applies to both roots in CJR13 and to the
nonzero root in CJR14; the $h=0,D=0,L\ne0$ case is already excluded.

In the remaining $D=L=0$ case, put $y_0=-a_I/C$ and evaluate
$R_0,R_1$ at $y_0$. Set
\[
\begin{split}
 Q_2&=ABC\mu_v/2>0,\\
 Q_1&=ABa_R\mu_v+BC\mu_{v+1},\\
 Q_0&=AR_1\mu_v+Ba_R\mu_{v+1}+C\mu_{v+2},\\
 \mathcal X&=\begin{cases}
 \varnothing,&Q_1^2-4Q_2Q_0<0,\\
 \{(-Q_1+\sqrt{Q_1^2-4Q_2Q_0})/(2Q_2),
   (-Q_1-\sqrt{Q_1^2-4Q_2Q_0})/(2Q_2)\},&Q_1^2-4Q_2Q_0\ge0,
 \end{cases}\\
 H(x)&=R_0\mu_v+R_1(\mu_{v+1}+A\mu_vx)
       +a_R(\mu_{v+2}+B\mu_{v+1}x+AB\mu_vx^2/2)\\
 &\hspace{8mm}+\mu_{v+3}+C\mu_{v+2}x+
                 BC\mu_{v+1}x^2/2+ABC\mu_vx^3/6.
\end{split}\tag{CJR24}
\]
The complete repeated-jet set is now
$\{x-\kappa+iy_0:x\in\mathcal X,\ H(x)=0\}$.
Indeed row three of CJR9 on the actual moments is exactly
$Q_2x^2+Q_1x+Q_0$, and row one is $H(x)$.
The other two rows impose no further equation after the proved
elimination because $h=D=E=0$. The quadratic formula and the
remaining explicit cubic therefore enumerate the complete set.

For any one actual period, at most one candidate from CJR23 or
CJR24 can survive. The original polynomial has exact leading term
\[
 m_1(P)=b_3\frac{ABC}{6}\mu_vP^3+
                         \text{terms of degree at most two},
 \qquad b_3\frac{ABC}{6}\mu_v\ne0.
 \tag{CJR25}
\]
Two distinct repeated roots would give a degree-four factor of
this degree-three polynomial, a contradiction. Thus the original
exceptional point has been reduced to a finite explicit test using
its actual four moments and original coefficients. The calculation
does not replace those entries by freely chosen moments. For the
collision application omit $P=0$, as required in EQR5.
