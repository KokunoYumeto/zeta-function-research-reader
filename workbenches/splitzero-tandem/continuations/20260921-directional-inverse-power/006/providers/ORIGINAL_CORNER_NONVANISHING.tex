\subsection{The exact primitive column and both original quadratic brackets}

\[
 \tau=\frac{\gamma^2-\delta^2}{3}>0,\qquad
 d_h=2\delta\gamma>0,\qquad

 \tag{OC53}\label{eq:OC53}

\[

  =w^3+xw^2+(x^2+\beta)w+x(x^2+\beta),\qquad
 V^{-1}e_1=\frac{(x(x^2+\beta),x^2+\beta,x,1)^{\mathsf T}}{h_c'(x)}.

Indeed this polynomial vanishes at the other three roots and has value
$h_c'(x)$ at $x$. Since $\beta=6\tau$, multiplication by the exact
matrix $J$ yields
\[
 JV^{-1}Ue_1=\frac{\mathfrak u}{h_c'(x)}
                 (xB,A,x,1)^{\mathsf T},\qquad h_c'(x)=4id_hx\ne0.
 \tag{OC54}\label{eq:OC54}
\]
This proves the received equation (22) in its original coordinates.

For a vector $Y$, the coefficient polynomial of $J^{-1}Y$ is
$P_Y(w)=(Y_1+2\tau Y_3)+(Y_2+4\tau Y_4)w+Y_3w^2+Y_4w^3$.

 \begin{split}
 \widetilde Q(Y)&:=Q_0(U^{-1}VJ^{-1}Y)\\

 \end{split}\tag{OC55}\label{eq:OC55}

This is NC12 with its complete scalar, not the auxiliary real quadratic

For $1\le j\le4$ set $\omega=\zeta_5^{-j}=e^{i\vartheta}$ and

Its two brackets in (OC55) are exactly

 \begin{split}

 &=\alpha\omega^2\{(B+Ar)^2-r(A+Br)^2\}\\

For the first factorization, subtract the two squares and use

 q_{j,1}(0)=

\subsection{Polynomial identity and a bound uniform in the full unit phase}

Set $\epsilon=d_h/\tau$, $C=\cos^2\vartheta$ and
$S=\sin^2\vartheta=1-C$. The identities
$1+r=2\omega\cos\vartheta$ and
$1-r=-2i\omega\sin\vartheta$ give
\[
 \begin{split}
 Z/\tau^3&=\epsilon^2-3-i\epsilon(5+\epsilon^2),\\
 \frac{|F_\vartheta|^2}{16\tau^6|1-r|^2}
  &=(9+\epsilon^2)
       \{[(1-\epsilon^2)C-1]^2+4\epsilon^2C^2\},\\
 \frac{|G_\vartheta|^2}{16\tau^6|1-r|^2}
  &=S\{(3-\epsilon^2)\sin\vartheta
                   -\epsilon(5+\epsilon^2)\cos\vartheta\}^2.
 \end{split}\tag{OC58}\label{eq:OC58}
\]
In particular the sign of the mixed term is fixed before the polynomial
expansion. Expanding these two expressions, using $S=1-C$, and
subtracting proves the exact received equation (24):
\[
 \begin{split}
 \frac{|F_\vartheta|^2-|G_\vartheta|^2}{16\tau^6|1-r|^2}
 &=2\epsilon(5+\epsilon^2)(3-\epsilon^2)
             S\sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta\\
 &\quad+\epsilon^2(7-21C+50C^2)\\
 &\quad+\epsilon^4(-1-6C+20C^2)
          +\epsilon^6(2C^2-C).
 \end{split}\tag{OC59}\label{eq:OC59}
\]
The accompanying script checks the identity over the rational polynomial
ring, with zero residual, rather than at a list of numerical phases.

For a nonidentity fifth root, the two possible values of $S$ are

 S\ge\tfrac14,\qquad

Here $0\le C\le1$, and

Also $|\alpha|\le4\tau$, $|B|^2\le2\tau^2$ and

\subsection{A closed finite majorant from the complete original coefficients}

For clarity, the row index in the next calculation is $1\le b\le4$
and the centered-polynomial column index is $0\le r\le3$. The
complete retained PCL2 formula is
\[
 \mathcal R_{br}(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}
 \sum_{\substack{p,h\ge0\\2p+4h=5n+r+1-b}}
 \frac{\beta^p\eta^h}{3^pp!h!}
            (-1)^L5^L(b/5)_L z^n,
 \qquad L=p+h-n.
 \tag{OC65}\label{eq:OC65}
\]
The index $r$ in $\mathcal R_{br}$ denotes its column labeled $r$,
so in ordinary one-based matrix notation this is column $r+1$.
Every contributing term has
\[
 n=\frac{2p+4h+b-r-1}{5},\qquad
 L=\frac{r+3p+h+1-b}{5},\qquad
 b/5+L=\frac{r+3p+h+1}{5}>0.
\]
For contributing integer pairs $L\ge0$, since a negative integer $L$
would make $b+5L\le-1$ although $r+3p+h+1\ge1$.
Euler's defining integral for Gamma therefore rewrites the absolute
value of the Pochhammer factor exactly. On $|z|=2$, enlarging the
resulting positive sum by dropping the congruence restriction gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 |\mathcal R_{br}(z)|
 &\le\frac{5^{(r+1-b)/5}2^{(b-r-1)/5}}{\Gamma(b/5)}
 \int_0^\infty s^{(r+1)/5-1}
       \exp\{-s+A_2s^{3/5}+B_2s^{1/5}\}\,ds,\\
 A_2&=\frac{|\beta|}{3}5^{3/5}2^{2/5},\qquad
 B_2=|\eta|5^{1/5}2^{4/5}.
 \end{split}\tag{OC66}\label{eq:OC66}
\]
This step preserves every original coefficient before estimating it.
To justify the summation and integral, first perform it for finite
positive sums, then use monotone convergence. The two unrestricted
exponential series sum respectively to
$\exp(A_2s^{3/5})$ and $\exp(B_2s^{1/5})$; the bound proved next
shows the resulting integral finite. Thus there is no reliance on an
unquantified tail convergence assertion in (OC66).

For $0<p<1$, $a\ge0$ and $s\ge0$, elementary maximization gives
\[
 as^p\le\frac{s}{4}+(1-p)(4p)^{p/(1-p)}a^{1/(1-p)}.
\]
For $a>0$ this follows by differentiating $as^p-s/4$: its maximum
is attained at $s=(4pa)^{1/(1-p)}$. For $a=0$ it is immediate.
Applying this separately with $p=3/5$ and $p=1/5$ proves
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 K_2=\frac25\left(\frac{12}{5}\right)^{3/2}A_2^{5/2}
       +\frac45\left(\frac45\right)^{1/4}B_2^{5/4},\\
 M_{br}=5^{(r+1-b)/5}2^{b/5}
          \frac{\Gamma((r+1)/5)}{\Gamma(b/5)}e^{K_2},
 \qquad M=\max_{1\le b\le4,\,0\le r\le3}M_{br},\\
 |\mathcal R_{br}(z)|\le M_{br}\le M\quad(|z|=2).
 \end{gathered}\tag{OC67}\label{eq:OC67}
\]
Indeed the integrand in (OC66) is at most
$e^{K_2}s^{(r+1)/5-1}e^{-s/2}$, whose integral equals
$e^{K_2}2^{(r+1)/5}\Gamma((r+1)/5)$.
Every quantity in (OC67) is now a finite explicit function of the
original $\beta,\eta$ and positive Gamma constants.

Use the induced maximum-row-sum norm and put

 \|V\|_\infty=1+\rho_0+\rho_0^2+\rho_0^3,
 \qquad \|V^{-1}\|_\infty=\frac{\rho_0^2}{d_h},

To prove the inverse norm formula, the cardinal column at any root $w$

\subsection{Original finite periods and all four corner coefficients}

Let $R_{\rm adm}$ be the already specified original admissible radius.
The original five-orbit requirement remains imposed there. Define
\[
 R_{\rm corner}^{\rm explicit}
   =\max\left\{R_{\rm adm},1,

 \tag{OC71}\label{eq:OC71}

R_{\rm corner}^{\rm explicit}$, (OC64) and (OC70), at the actual
$z=\varpi^{-1}$, prove
\[
 |q_{j,\alpha}(\varpi^{-1})|
 \ge m_{\rm corner}-\frac{\mathcal M_2}{|\varpi|}
 \ge\frac{m_{\rm corner}}2>0
            \qquad(1\le j,\alpha\le4).
 \tag{OC72}\label{eq:OC72}
\]
The received threshold with $2C_{\rm corner}/m_{\rm corner}$ is
valid and may be smaller; (OC71) makes its tail constant quantitatively
explicit from the original period coefficients.

In the original affine Segre coordinates $t(Z,W)=(ZW,Z,W,1)^{\mathsf T}$,
the full biform is
$A_z(Z,W)=\prod_{j=1}^4Q_0(L_j(z)t(Z,W))
 =\sum_{r,s=0}^8a_{rs}(z)Z^rW^s$.

 \begin{gathered}

 \end{gathered}\tag{OC73}\label{eq:OC73}

When the original notation writes $a_{88}(\varpi)$ for the coefficient

coefficient, scalar and unit phase remains in its defining product.

For completeness, on any already admitted domain $|\varpi|\ge R_*>0$
the exceptional inverse periods for simultaneous nonvanishing are
specified by the one original entire product
\[
 F_{\rm corner}(z)=\prod_{j=1}^4\prod_{\alpha=1}^4q_{j,\alpha}(z),
 \qquad
 \mathcal Z_*=
 \{z:|z|\le R_*^{-1},\ F_{\rm corner}(z)=0\}.
 \tag{OC74}\label{eq:OC74}
\]
By (OC64) this product is nonzero at zero. Its zeros are isolated;
an infinite subset of the compact disk would have an accumulation point
in its entire domain, contradicting the identity theorem. Thus
$\mathcal Z_*$ is finite, and all four original corner coefficients
are nonzero outside it. This is the \emph{union} of the sixteen
zero sets, equivalently the zero set of their product. The phrase
``common zero set'' in the incoming paragraph must not be interpreted as
their intersection, since excluding only the intersection would not
establish simultaneous nonvanishing. No lower bound is asserted at an
exceptional period. The explicit large-period result (OC71)--(OC73)
has already excluded every such zero in its domain.

The new conclusion supplied to the original observation calculation is
the exact nonzero original terminal coefficient and its quantitative
lower bound (OC73), with the period fixed while the cardinal degree grows.
This proof does not invert the complete conductor or assign a value to
the independent proper-source target minimum.
