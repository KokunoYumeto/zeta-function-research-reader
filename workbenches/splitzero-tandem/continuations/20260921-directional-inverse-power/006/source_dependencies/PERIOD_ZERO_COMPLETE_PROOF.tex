\section{An evaluated original-period domain with zero conductor vanishing order}
\label{sec:period-zero-value}
Retain the complete simple-quartet data of PCL1--11 and RPC1--26:
\[
 x=\delta+i\gamma,\quad y=\delta-i\gamma,\quad
 0<\delta<\tfrac12,\quad\gamma>2,\quad
 U=\operatorname{diag}(a,\bar a,\bar a,a),\quad a\ne0.
 \tag{PZ1}
\]
Here $a=v_h(1/2+x)$ is the original entire-unit value; its phase is
not chosen. Set $\beta=-(x^2+y^2)=2(\gamma^2-\delta^2)$ and
$\eta=x^2y^2=(\delta^2+\gamma^2)^2$. Write $\tau=u^{-1}$ for the
original inverse period, reserving $w$ for the exponential-symbol variable.
The original matrix is exactly
\[
 \mathsf H(u)=e^{\mathfrak a/u}G_u\mathcal R(\tau)V^{-1}U,
 \quad L_j(\tau)=U^{-1}V\mathcal R(\tau)^{-1}D^{-j}
                         \mathcal R(\tau)V^{-1}U,
 \quad j=1,2,3,4.
 \tag{PZ2}
\]
The cancellation of $e^{\mathfrak a/u}$ and $G_u$ in this conjugation
is the literal identity $L_j=\mathsf H^{-1}D^{-j}\mathsf H$, since
$G_u$ and $D$ commute. Thus no amplitude, period column, contour factor,
or original phase has been replaced. The matrix $\mathcal R$ is the
entire PCL2 series, with determinant one and inverse its adjugate.
Put
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 d_j(\tau)=Q_0(L_j(\tau)\boldsymbol1),\qquad
 Q_0(t)=t_1t_4-t_2t_3,\quad\boldsymbol1=(1,1,1,1)^T,\\
 \mathcal E(\tau)=\prod_{j=1}^4d_j(\tau),\qquad
 E_{A,u}(w)=\sum_{b,c=0}^{8}a_{bc}(\tau)
       e^{[4+(2b-8)\delta+i(2c-8)\gamma]w}.
 \end{gathered}\tag{PZ3}
\]
The complete coefficient product PCL8--10 proves
$E_{A,u}(0)=A_\tau(1,1)=\mathcal E(\tau)$, including all four
nonreduced conductor factors and their actual multiplicities.

\subsection{The exact limiting value, including both exceptional amplitude phases}
Define
\[
 r=\frac a{\bar a},\quad T=\frac{x^2}{y^2},\quad
 A_e=\frac{2T-1}{3(T-1)},\quad B_e=\frac{2-T}{3(T-1)},
 \quad C_e=(r-1)(A_e/r+B_e),\quad
 D_e=(r-1)(A_e/r-B_e).
 \tag{PZ4}
\]
All denominators are nonzero: $a,y\ne0$ and
$x^2-y^2=4i\delta\gamma\ne0$. Neither $A_e$ nor $B_e$ vanishes,
because $|T|=1$, whereas their putative roots are $1/2$ and $2$.
One has $A_e-B_e=1$ and $B_e=-\overline{A_e}$, by direct
substitution of $\bar T=T^{-1}$. Hence
\[
 \begin{split}
 C_e&=1-2\operatorname{Re}(A_e/r)\in\mathbb R,\\
 D_e&=2i\bigl(\operatorname{Im}A_e-
                      \operatorname{Im}(A_e/r)\bigr)\in i\mathbb R.
 \end{split}\tag{PZ5}
\]
For $\rho_j=\zeta^{-j}$ and $h_j=\rho_j^3-\rho_j$, the full value is
\[
 \boxed{\quad d_j(0)=h_jD_e(2\rho_j+h_jC_e).\quad}
 \tag{PZ6}
\]
Here is a direct proof retaining both off-diagonal entries in PCL4.
On even centered coefficients $(p_0,p_2)$, its middle conjugation is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}\rho_j&\beta(\rho_j^3-\rho_j)/3\\0&\rho_j^3\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Evaluation at $(x,y)$ gives the matrix
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\rho_j+h_jA_e&-h_jA_e\\
h_jB_e&\rho_j-h_jB_e\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
Indeed $(x^2+\beta/3)/(x^2-y^2)=A_e$ and
$(y^2+\beta/3)/(x^2-y^2)=B_e$. Conjugation by the original
$\operatorname{diag}(a,\bar a)$ gives
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}\rho_j+h_jA_e&-h_jA_e/r\\
 rh_jB_e&\rho_j-h_jB_e\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The input $\boldsymbol1$ has even evaluation vector $(1,1)^T$ and
zero odd evaluation vector. Its two output values are therefore
$p_j=\rho_j+h_jA_e(1-r^{-1})$ and
$q_j=\rho_j+h_jB_e(r-1)$, each repeated at the opposite root.
The original quadratic is exactly $p_j^2-q_j^2$.
Its difference is $h_jD_e$ and its sum is $2\rho_j+h_jC_e$,
proving PZ6. This calculation is an evaluation through explicit
invertible coefficient and primary maps, not an assignment of the unit.

No factor $2\rho_j+h_jC_e$ vanishes. In fact its vanishing would
imply $2+(\rho_j^2-1)C_e=0$. The imaginary part forces $C_e=0$
because $\rho_j^2$ is nonreal, and the real part would then be $2=0$.
Also $h_j\ne0$. Consequently
\[
 d_j(0)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 r=1\ \hbox{or}\ r=A_e/B_e,
 \qquad j=1,2,3,4.
 \tag{PZ7}
\]
The second ratio is a unit complex number and is distinct from one.
Thus this is an exhaustive two-phase statement about the actual
amplitude, with neither phase omitted.

Multiplying all four PZ6 values gives the exact real expression
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \mathcal E(0)=5D_e^4\bigl(5(C_e-1)^4+10(C_e-1)^2+1\bigr).
 \quad}\tag{PZ8}
\]
To verify the constant, $\prod_j\rho_j=1$ and
$\prod_j(\rho_j^2-1)=5$. The remaining product is
\[
 \prod_{j=1}^4\bigl(2-C_e+C_e\rho_j^2\bigr)
 =\frac{(2-C_e)^5+C_e^5}{2}
 =5(C_e-1)^4+10(C_e-1)^2+1.
\]
The first equality follows by including the missing fifth root one
in $\prod_{\xi^5=1}(s+C_e\xi)=s^5+C_e^5$, then dividing by
$s+C_e=2$, so it remains valid at $C_e=0$. Since $D_e$ is purely
imaginary, its fourth power is positive when it is nonzero. PZ8 is
therefore strictly positive off exactly the two phases in PZ7.
At those phases its value is zero; this inverse-period limit alone
makes no assertion of zero value at a finite original period.

\subsection{A finite original-period domain and the complete zero-order test}
The PCL22 identity gives
$L_j(-\tau)=P_{\rm opp}L_j(\tau)P_{\rm opp}$.
Since $P_{\rm opp}\boldsymbol1=\boldsymbol1$ and
$Q_0(P_{\rm opp}t)=Q_0(t)$, each $d_j$ is even entire. Thus write
\[
 d_j(\tau)=\sum_{n\ge0}d_{j,2n}\tau^{2n},\qquad
 C_j^{(0)}=\sum_{n\ge1}|d_{j,2n}|<\infty.
 \tag{PZ9}
\]
Every coefficient is explicitly obtained by multiplying PCL2 and
its finite adjugate in PZ2 and then the two products in PZ3. Absolute
convergence at radius one proves the displayed finiteness.
For an actual amplitude with $r\notin\{1,A_e/B_e\}$, define
\[
 R_0=\max\left\{R_*,1,
       \max_{1\le j\le4}\sqrt{2C_j^{(0)}/|d_j(0)|}\right\}.
 \tag{PZ10}
\]
Here $R_*$ is exactly RPC21 or RPC23 for this original quartet and
unit, so this domain already lies in the proved five-orbit locus.
For every original logarithm branch and $|u|\ge R_0$,
\[
 |d_j(u^{-1})-d_j(0)|\le C_j^{(0)}|u|^{-2}
                       \le\tfrac12|d_j(0)|,
 \quad
 \frac{\mathcal E(0)}{16}\le|E_{A,u}(0)|
                     \le\frac{81\mathcal E(0)}{16},
 \quad \operatorname{ord}_{w=0}E_{A,u}(w)=0.
 \tag{PZ11}
\]
The error follows by summing the entire series at $|u|^{-1}\le1$;
if $C_j^{(0)}=0$ it is identically zero. Multiplication of the four
lower and upper bounds gives the factors $2^{-4}$ and $(3/2)^4$.
PZ8 supplies $\prod_j|d_j(0)|=\mathcal E(0)>0$. A holomorphic
function with nonzero value has zero vanishing order, proving the
last equality without a genericity hypothesis on the period in this
domain. Single-valuedness follows from the proved conjugation PZ2.

At arbitrary original periods on the five-orbit locus, the full test
is still explicit: expand each actual $(2,2)$ factor
$q_j(\tau;Z,W)=\sum_{b,c=0}^2c_{j,bc}(\tau)Z^bW^c$ and set
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 E_{j,u}(w)=\sum_{b,c=0}^2c_{j,bc}(\tau)
       e^{[1+(2b-2)\delta+i(2c-2)\gamma]w},\\
 v_j=\min\left\{0\le n\le8:
    \sum_{b,c=0}^2c_{j,bc}(\tau)
        [1+(2b-2)\delta+i(2c-2)\gamma]^n\ne0\right\},\\
 v=\sum_{j=1}^4v_j,\qquad
 E_{A,u}=\prod_{j=1}^4E_{j,u},\qquad
 \frac{E_{A,u}^{(v)}(0)}{v!}
      =\prod_{j=1}^4\frac{E_{j,u}^{(v_j)}(0)}{v_j!}\ne0.
 \end{gathered}\tag{PZ12}
\]
The nine exponents in each factor are distinct by their real and
imaginary parts. Their nine-by-nine derivative Vandermonde is
invertible. On the five-orbit locus the factor is nonzero by the
Segre restriction argument in BTK26, so the displayed minimum exists.
This proves the exact retained test, including all multiplicities,
and $v\le32$ on every remaining fibre.

For positive real original $u$, the actual real-period identity PCL15
also proves $v$ is even. Indeed for real $w$ put
$Z=e^{2\delta w}$ and $W=e^{2i\gamma w}$. The full exponential
factorization gives
\[
 E_{A,u}(w)=e^{(4-8\delta-8i\gamma)w}A_\tau(Z,W)
           =e^{(4-8\delta)w}W^{-4}A_\tau(Z,W)\ge0.
 \tag{PZ13}
\]
The last expression is the exact squared modulus in PCL15. The
function is nonzero analytic on the five-orbit locus. Its first
nonzero Taylor term at zero must have even degree and positive real
coefficient, because otherwise its real values would change sign on
the two sides of zero. Hence $v\in\{0,2,4,\ldots,32\}$ on that
positive-real-period locus. This retains the actual curve in PCL15,
with one real coordinate and one unit-circle coordinate.

On the evaluated domain PZ11 the low-coordinate space $\mathbb C^v$
in ROG26 is zero, $Q_{N,s}$ in ROG23 is the zero map, and the full
ROG27 shear is exactly the identity for both original source orders
$s=1,k$. Its determinant and every exterior power have norm one;
the ROG32 graph-shear error is therefore exactly zero. The polynomial

No connecting-rank conclusion has been inferred from this zero-order
calculation; the exact target relation is the unchanged RDS8 sequence
with $\mathscr N_0=\mathscr E_k$ and dimension $q-q'$.

\section{The two exceptional unit phases have exact second inverse-period order}
Retain the original roots
\[
 x=\delta+i\gamma,\quad y=\delta-i\gamma,\quad
 X=x^2,\quad Y=y^2,\quad
 \beta=-(X+Y),\quad\eta=XY,
 \qquad 0<\delta<\tfrac12,\quad\gamma>2.
\]
The original ordered evaluation roots are $(x,y,-y,-x)$.
Let $V$ have rows $(1,w,w^2,w^3)$ at those four roots, let
$U=\operatorname{diag}(a,\bar a,\bar a,a)$ with $a\ne0$, and
put $r=a/\bar a$.  This notation retains the exact scalar map
$U=\bar a\,\operatorname{diag}(r,1,1,r)$; the scalar $\bar a$
cancels between $U^{-1}$ and $U$ in the original conjugation.
For $j=1,2,3,4$, put $\rho=\zeta^{-j}$ and
$D_\rho=\operatorname{diag}(\rho,\rho^2,\rho^3,\rho^4)$.
The original PCL2 coefficient matrix is denoted by
$R(z)=J+R_1z+R_2z^2+\cdots$, and the original expression is
\[
 q_\rho(z)=Q_0\left(U^{-1}VR(z)^{-1}D_\rho R(z)V^{-1}U\mathbf1\right),
 \qquad Q_0(t)=t_1t_4-t_2t_3.
\]
Every map in this expression has its original domain, codomain and order.
In particular $V^{-1}U\mathbf1=\bar a\,c_r$, where
\[
 c_r=\left(\frac{X-rY}{X-Y},0,\frac{r-1}{X-Y},0\right)^{\mathsf T}.

Evaluation of the even polynomial represented by $c_r$ at
$x,y,-y,-x$ gives $(r,1,1,r)$, proving this identity directly.

\subsection{One finite radius for every actual amplitude and the original receiving maps}
Use the complete coefficient calculation PZX1--PZX6, which proves that
both exceptional phases in PZ7 have nonzero inverse-period coefficient
$d_{j,2}$ for every $j$. Define, from the actual value $a$,
\[
 \ell_A=\begin{cases}0,&a/\bar a\notin\{1,A_e/B_e\},\\
                      2,&a/\bar a\in\{1,A_e/B_e\},\end{cases}
 \quad b_j=d_{j,\ell_A}\ne0,\quad
 C_j=\sum_{n\ge1}|d_{j,\ell_A+2n}|<\infty.
 \tag{PZU1}
\]
The symbol $\ell_A$ describes vanishing at the inverse-period limit;
it is not the symbol-variable vanishing order $v$. The leading
$b_j$ is exactly PZ6 or the applicable PZX formula, retaining the
original amplitude phase and every constant. Put
\[
 R_A=\max\left\{R_*,1,
           \max_{1\le j\le4}\sqrt{2C_j/|b_j|}\right\}.
 \tag{PZU2}
\]
The Taylor series of $\tau^{-\ell_A}d_j(\tau)$ is entire after
its removable value at zero is included, proving convergence of $C_j$.

\[
 \left|u^{\ell_A}d_j(u^{-1})-b_j\right|
        \le C_j|u|^{-2}\le\tfrac12|b_j|.
 \tag{PZU3}

Consequently the complete, original zero-symbol value has the bounds
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \frac{\prod_j|b_j|}{16|u|^{4\ell_A}}
 \le |E_{A,u}(0)|
 \le \frac{81\prod_j|b_j|}{16|u|^{4\ell_A}},
 \qquad v=\operatorname{ord}_{w=0}E_{A,u}(w)=0.

This proves an original-period domain for every original nonzero

 \mathcal Z_A^{\ge R_*}
