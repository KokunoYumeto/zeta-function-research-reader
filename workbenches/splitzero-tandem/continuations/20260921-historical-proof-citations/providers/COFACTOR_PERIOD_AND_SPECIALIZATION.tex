This proof integrates the user-supplied notes
\emph{04 One-step proper-kernel period locus} and
\emph{05 Good coefficient specialization}, retaining the original
cofactor products, invariant images and conductor multiplicities.
It supplies the complete sixteen-cofactor recovery used in those notes
and proves the actual specialization maps. It is a coefficient-module
calculation. Its arithmetic action and metric receivers are specified
below; a geometric Frobenius realization of the arithmetic action is
not supplied by changing the coefficient field.

\section{The original conductor, including the projective vertex}
Let $S=\C[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4]$ with
$g x_i=\zeta^i x_i$, where $\zeta$ is the original primitive fifth
root. Replacing all characters by their negatives changes no argument
below and corresponds to the inverse-action convention in NC5.
Retain the original invertible period matrix $H$, quadric
$Q=Q_0(H^{-1}x)$, $Q_0(t)=t_1t_4-t_2t_3$, and five pairwise
nonassociate quadrics $Q_a=g^aQ$, $0\le a\le4$. Put
\[
 P=\prod_{a=0}^4Q_a,\quad A=\prod_{a=1}^4Q_a,\quad
 R=S/(Q),\quad B=\im(S^{\langle g\rangle}\to R).
                                                                    \tag{CP1}
\]
Every $Q_a$ is irreducible: an invertible change reduces it to the
Segre determinant, which is primitive and linear in $t_1$, with
coprime coefficients $t_4,-t_2t_3$. The product $P$ is invariant,
and prime factorization shows
$B=S^{\langle g\rangle}/P S^{\langle g\rangle}$.

We recall and prove the full original conductor identity used here:
\[
 \{f\in R:fR\subset B\}=AR.                                      \tag{CP2}
\]
For any homogeneous lift $h$, the invariant sum
$\sum_{a=0}^4g^a(Ah)$ restricts to $Ah$ on $Q=0$: every other
term contains $Q$. This proves $AR\subset B$, without a factor
of five. For the reverse inclusion localize at $x_i^5$. Each
character in $S[x_i^{-1}]$ is an invariant times a unique one
of $1,x_i,\ldots,x_i^4$, since $i$ is nonzero modulo five.
Thus this ring is free of rank five over its invariant ring,
with relation $T^5-x_i^5$ and invertible derivative $5T^4$.
After this faithfully flat change the invariant inclusion becomes
\[
 S/(P)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{a=0}^4 S/(Q_a)                    \tag{CP3}
\]
on that chart. A tuple supported on component $a$ is in the image
precisely when it is the restriction of a polynomial divisible by
each $Q_b$, $b\ne a$. In the polynomial UFD that intersection
is their product. Every ideal of the direct sum splits componentwise;
therefore its conductor is the product ideal on each component.
Faithful flatness descends this equality to the chart.

The nonzero coordinate charts cover the projective smooth quadric.
In its original Segre presentation the degree-$d$ part of $R$ is
the biform space $T_{d,d}$. Chartwise membership in $AR$ says
that $f/A$ is regular as a section of
$\mathcal O(d-8,d-8)$ on all four charts. On the ordinary Segre
charts these regularity conditions say that it is a biform of that
bidegree; if $d<8$ it is zero. Alternatively each irreducible
biform factor of $A$ defines a projective divisor, and its full
valuation in $f$ is at least its valuation in $A$; UFD divisibility
gives the same conclusion. This proves CP2 with no residual
vertex-supported term.

\section{Sixteen actual cofactor maps recover every conductor class}
In degree $d$ write $J_d=B_d/(AR)_d$ and define
\[
 X_{{\rm full},d}:J_d\longrightarrow
       \bigoplus_{i=1}^4\bigoplus_{r=1}^4(R/B)_{d+4r},\qquad
 [f]\longmapsto([x_i^{4r}f])_{i,r}.                                \tag{CP4}
\]
This is well-defined since $AR$ is an ideal of $R$ contained in $B$.
It is injective at every degree on the original five-orbit domain.
Indeed, if $f\in B$ and all sixteen products are in $B$, localize
at $x_i^5$. The five powers $1,x_i^4,x_i^8,x_i^{12},x_i^{16}$
span the character components over the invariants on this chart:
their characters $0,4i,3i,2i,i$ are all distinct, and powers differing
by multiples of five differ by an invariant unit. Hence $fR\subset B$
on this chart. This holds for every $i$, so the complete proof of
CP2 gives $f\in AR$. This proves injectivity and gives an explicit
local module receiver: express each monomial as one of those five
powers times an invariant, and apply its known cofactor component.
No iteration of the four original one-step products is presumed.

The same proof holds over any algebraically closed field of
characteristic different from five, retaining an actual order-five
$\zeta$ and smooth five-orbit quadric. All uses of division above
are by invariant units or by five. Polynomial UFD and Segre-chart
divisibility hold in these characteristics, including characteristic two.

\section{The four one-step products and all divisorial failures}
For the original one-step proper-source kernel use the actual low-jet
subspace $J_{v,d}\subset J_d$ and the four tests $[x_i^4f]\in J_{d+4}$.
For an invariant lift of such a class, the tests are literal
$x_i^4f\in B$, since $AR\subset B$. Consequently
\[
 f\mathcal T\subset B,\qquad
 \mathcal T=B+x_1^4B+x_2^4B+x_3^4B+x_4^4B.                         \tag{CP5}
\]
The module $R$ is finite over $B$, being a quotient of the finite
invariant extension $S/S^{\langle g\rangle}$. On any nonzero-coordinate
chart a geometric fibre is a reduced subset of the five orbit points:
base change the etale extension $T^5-x_i^5$, which splits into
five distinct points, and impose $Q$. Suppose it has $r$ points
and its representative has $l$ active coordinates. The evaluations
of $1$ and the active $x_i^4$ are nonzero column multiples of the
Fourier-five matrix, restricted to $r$ rows and $l+1$ distinct
characters. Every square Fourier-five minor is nonzero. Size-two
minors are nonzero root multiples of $1-\zeta^a$, $a\not\equiv0$;
size-one minors are units; complementary minors in the inverse
$5^{-1}(\zeta^{-ij})_{i,j=0}^4$ prove sizes three and four, and the full
Vandermonde proves size five. Thus $r\le l+1$ implies that
$\mathcal T$ spans this fibre. The finite cokernel then vanishes
locally: write a generating vector $v=Mv$ with all entries of $M$
in the local maximal ideal; the adjugate of $I-M$, whose determinant
is a unit, gives $v=0$. This proves the needed local generation.

At a projective divisor $l=4$ always meets this criterion. For
$l=3$ failure requires all five orbit points to lie in $Q$. The
six quadratic monomials on that coordinate plane have five distinct
characters, with exactly one repeated character. Fourier inversion
then forces every singleton-character coefficient to vanish; their
monomials are nonzero on the active-coordinate locus. The plane
restriction must therefore be a single-character conic. For $l=2$
failure requires at least four orbit points in $Q$. The three binary
monomials have distinct characters, and any three rows have full
rank by the proved Fourier minors. Every coefficient must vanish,
so the coordinate line is contained in $Q$. The locus $l=1$ consists
of isolated projective points and cannot contain a divisor of the
smooth quadric. These exhaust all possibilities.

Exclude the six coordinate-line containments and, on each coordinate
plane, retain one nonzero singleton-character coefficient. Its monomial
is nonzero at every point of the active three-coordinate stratum, so
Fourier inversion excludes a five-point fibre there. Then CP5 and the just-proved
generation imply that $f$ belongs to the conductor at every
projective divisor. The last divisibility argument in CP2 retains
every restricted-factor multiplicity and gives $f\in AR$. Therefore
the original one-step proper-source kernel is zero on this locus,
without requiring coprimality between different orbit restrictions.

\section{Evaluation of the period gates, with the full original unit}
Use the actual PCL coefficient family \cite{PCL} and write
\[
 H(u)=e^{\mathfrak a/u}G_u\mathcal R(u^{-1})V^{-1}U,
 \quad U=\diag(a,\bar a,\bar a,a),\quad a\ne0,\quad z=u^{-1}.
\]
The invertible diagonal map $X=G_u^{-1}x$ commutes with the cyclic
action. Multiplying the quadric by the retained nonzero scalar
$e^{2\mathfrak a/u}$ yields exactly
\[
 \widetilde Q_z(X)=Q_0(U^{-1}V\mathcal R(z)^{-1}X).                 \tag{CP6}
\]
The products $x_i^{4r}$ acquire their actual nonzero diagonal
factors, so all the kernel assertions transport in both directions.
No invariance of a Hermitian metric under this diagonal map is asserted.

Retain the original $0<\delta<1/2$ and $\gamma>2$.
Put $r=\delta+i\gamma$, $R_0=r^2$, $T_0=\bar r^2$,
$\beta=-(R_0+T_0)=2(\gamma^2-\delta^2)>0$,
$\eta=R_0T_0=(\delta^2+\gamma^2)^2>0$, $p=a^{-2}$,
$b=\bar a^{-2}$, $u_0=(2R_0-T_0)/3$, $v_0=(2T_0-R_0)/3$.
The exact PCL value is $J=\mathcal R(0)=I-\beta E_{13}/3-2\beta E_{24}/3$.
Substitution into CP6 gives
\[
 \widetilde Q_0=p(X_1+u_0X_3)^2-b(X_1+v_0X_3)^2
       -pR_0(X_2-v_0X_4)^2+bT_0(X_2-u_0X_4)^2.                    \tag{CP7}
\]
Denote its even and odd symmetric blocks by
\[
 \begin{array}{lll}
 e_0=p-b,&e_1=pu_0-bv_0,&e_2=pu_0^2-bv_0^2,\\
 o_0=-pR_0+bT_0,&o_1=pR_0v_0-bT_0u_0,
     &o_2=-pR_0v_0^2+bT_0u_0^2.
 \end{array}
\]
Expansion proves
\[
 e_0e_2-e_1^2=-pb(R_0-T_0)^2\ne0,\quad
 o_0o_2-o_1^2=-pbR_0T_0(R_0-T_0)^2\ne0.                           \tag{CP8}
\]
On the plane omitting $X_1$, the repeated-character terms are
$X_3^2,X_2X_4$; single-character failure would require $o_0=o_2=0$.
This implies $u_0^2=v_0^2$, whereas
$(u_0-v_0)(u_0+v_0)=(R_0-T_0)(R_0+T_0)/3\ne0$.
The plane omitting $X_4$ analogously requires $e_0=e_2=0$ and
has the same contradiction. On the plane omitting $X_2$, the
off-character coefficients include $e_1,e_2$, not both zero by
CP8. On the plane omitting $X_3$ they include $o_0,o_1$, also
not both zero. Thus all four plane gates have a nonzero constant
coefficient.

The lines $(1,3)$ and $(2,4)$ are excluded by CP8. The line
$(1,2)$ requires $e_0=o_0=0$, hence $R_0=T_0$, impossible.
The lines $(1,4)$ and $(2,3)$ would force respectively the vanishing
of the same nonzero expression
\[
 T_0u_0^2-R_0v_0^2
 =-(R_0-T_0)(R_0^2-7R_0T_0+T_0^2)/9.                             \tag{CP9}
\]
Indeed $(R_0^2+T_0^2)/(R_0T_0)\in[-2,2]$, so its second factor
divided by $R_0T_0$ is at most $-5$. Only the line $(3,4)$
can remain at $z=0$. There $e_2=o_2=0$, CP8 gives $e_1o_1\ne0$,
and substitution gives $(o_1/e_1)^2=\eta$ and
$T_0u_0^4=R_0v_0^4$.

The complete PCL2 coefficient formula is
\[
 R_{ar,n}=\sum_{2p+4h=5n+r+1-a}
 \frac{\beta^p\eta^h}{3^pp!h!}(-1)^L5^L(a/5)_L,
 \quad L=p+h-n,\quad1\le a\le4,\quad0\le r\le3.                  \tag{CP10}
\]
For $n=1$, its four needed entries are exactly
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 \mathcal R'_{21}=\eta-\beta^2/9,
 &\mathcal R'_{23}=7\beta^3/81-2\beta\eta/3,\\
 \mathcal R'_{12}=\beta^3/27-\beta\eta/3,
 &\mathcal R'_{14}=-11\beta^4/324+\beta^2\eta/3-\eta^2/2.
 \end{array}                                                       \tag{CP11}
\]
These follow by the finite sums in CP10, so none is inferred from
a chosen asymptotic fit. Put $K=\mathcal R'(0)J^{-1}$. Multiplication
gives
\[
 K_{23}=\beta(4\beta^2-27\eta)/81<0,\qquad
 K_{14}=-(\beta^4-12\beta^2\eta+54\eta^2)/108<0.                   \tag{CP12}
\]
The first sign uses $0<\beta/\sqrt\eta<2$; the second uses
$(\beta^2/\eta-6)^2+18>0$. Differentiating
$\widetilde Q_z=\widetilde Q_0(J\mathcal R(z)^{-1}X)$ gives the
matrix derivative $-K^T\widetilde Q_0-\widetilde Q_0K$.
On $e_2=o_2=0$ its $(3,4)$ entry is
\[
 \widetilde Q'_{34}(0)=-e_1[K_{14}+(o_1/e_1)K_{23}].                \tag{CP13}
\]
To evaluate the possible cancellation put $b_0=\beta/\sqrt\eta$.
Substituting $u_0,v_0$ into $T_0u_0^4=R_0v_0^4$, factoring
$R_0-T_0\ne0$, and using $R_0+T_0=-\beta$, $R_0T_0=\eta$
gives $b_0^4-27b_0^2+81=0$. Its sole root in $(0,2)$ is
$b_0=3(\sqrt5-1)/2$, which satisfies $b_0^2+3b_0-9=0$.
The plus sign $o_1/e_1=\sqrt\eta$ makes the bracket negative.
For the minus sign reduction by this quadratic gives
\[
 K_{14}-\sqrt\eta K_{23}=\eta^2(108-45b_0)/324>0.                 \tag{CP14}
\]
The possible limiting coordinate line therefore has a nonzero first
variation for every original unit phase. The original phase $a$
has remained in $p,b,e_1,o_1$ throughout the calculation.

Choose one nonzero coefficient of each of the six line restrictions,
and one nonzero singleton-character coefficient of each of the four
plane restrictions. These singleton coefficients exclude the five-point
fibre on the active plane stratum; this is the exact property used in
the divisorial proof. CP7--14 specifies ten entire functions $g_\nu(z)$.
Their first nonzero orders $r_\nu$ are zero, except that the
$(3,4)$ line may have order one, with coefficient given by twice
CP13 if ordinary polynomial coefficients rather than symmetric
matrix entries are used. Retain that factor of two in this convention.
Let $c_\nu=[z^{r_\nu}]g_\nu\ne0$ and
\[
 C_\nu=1+\sum_{n>r_\nu}|[z^n]g_\nu|,\quad
 \epsilon_\nu=\min(1,|c_\nu|/(2C_\nu)),\quad
 R_{\rm prop}=\max(R_*,(\min_\nu\epsilon_\nu)^{-1}).              \tag{CP15}
\]
The entire PCL matrix and its entire inverse imply convergence of
these sums. For $0<|z|\le\epsilon_\nu$ the complete tail is at most
$C_\nu|z|^{r_\nu+1}$, so $|g_\nu(z)|\ge|c_\nu||z|^{r_\nu}/2$.
Here $R_*$ is the original admitted rank/five-orbit lower bound.
Thus every fixed original $|u|\ge R_{\rm prop}$ has zero original
proper-source kernel for every original rank-valid degree.
More generally its possible exceptional set is the fixed finite set
\[
 \mathcal X_{\rm prop}=\{u:|u|\ge R_*,\ \prod_\nu g_\nu(1/u)=0\}.
                                                                    \tag{CP16}
\]
After dividing the product by its stated zero order at zero, it is
entire and nonzero at zero. Its zeros in the compact disk
$|z|\le R_*^{-1}$ are finite, proving the assertion. A selected
gate zero does not assert actual kernel failure there; CP4 remains
injective even at such a point. No degree-uniform period drift is used.

\section{Coefficient reduction and the exact Frobenius pullback}
Let $\mathcal O$ be the subring of the original complex coefficient
field generated over $\mathbb Z[1/5]$ by the finitely many entries
of $H,H^{-1}$ and $\zeta$, and the coefficients of all five quadrics.
Invert a nonzero leading coefficient of each quadric in one fixed
monomial order, and one nonzero coefficient in each of two distinct
character components of $Q$. Those components exist on the five-orbit
locus. These are finitely many nonzero original coefficients, independent
of degree. The relation $\Phi_5(\zeta)=0$ and $5$ invertible make
$\zeta$ have order exactly five in every geometric residue field.
The matrix $H$ remains invertible, the Segre quadric remains smooth,
and its retained two different characters ensure the five quadrics
remain nonassociate. No growing grid Vandermonde has been inverted.

Put $S_{\mathcal O}=\mathcal O[x_1,\ldots,x_4]$ and form $R,B,J$
over this ring by CP1/CP4. The leading monomials are monic after the
specified localizations, so division by $Q$ or $P$ gives a basis of
normal monomials degree by degree. For $B$, division by invariant
$P$ stays in character zero: its leading monomial also has character
zero, and subtraction preserves it. The differences $1-\zeta^j$ are
units since their product is five, so the character-zero monomials
are exactly the invariant module. Thus $R_d$ and $B_d$ are finite free.
The restriction $B_d\to R_d$ is injective over the fraction field
by prime factorization, hence over $\mathcal O$ by freeness; it remains
injective over every geometric residue field by the same argument.

A finite matrix with independent columns over a residue field has
an invertible maximal minor on a neighbourhood of that point. On
that neighbourhood elementary elimination using its literal inverse
gives a left inverse and a free complementary block. Applying this
to $B_d\to R_d$ proves that $(R/B)_d$ is locally free and its
formation commutes with residue-field change. Multiplication
$A:R_{d-8}\to B_d$ remains injective in every residue field, because
$R$ there is a domain and $A\ne0$. Its cokernel $J_d$ is therefore
locally free by the same argument; likewise $(R/AR)_d$.

CP4 is now a matrix of finite locally free modules over $\mathcal O$.
Its every geometric residue fibre is injective by the complete
character/chart proof already given, and field extension is faithful
on vector spaces. Local inverse minors again give a locally split
injection with locally free cokernel. Therefore
\[
 \ker(X_{{\rm full},d}\otimes_{\mathcal O}\kappa)=0                \tag{CP17}
\]
for every residue field $\kappa$, with exactly the original graded
source and target images. It also splits as a module map over this
affine coefficient ring: choose finitely many principal open sets
with the local left inverses; multiplying each by a sufficiently
large power of its defining element clears denominators in the
finite matrices. These powers still generate the unit ideal.
A linear combination equal to one of the multiplied local left
inverse identities supplies a global left inverse. This does not
supply a degree-uniform chosen inverse or a Hermitian isometry.
In particular tensoring this literal left inverse with any
$\mathcal O$-algebra $T$ gives
\[
 \ker(X_{{\rm full},d}\otimes_{\mathcal O}T)=0.\tag{CP17a}
\]
Thus the proved injection survives arbitrary coefficient base change,
including nonreduced algebras. This follows from the displayed splitting,
without an added flatness hypothesis on $T$.

The finite coefficient maps also have an explicit Frobenius relation.
For a residue field of characteristic $p\ne5$ and a matrix $X$
for CP4 in any of these local bases, entrywise Frobenius satisfies
\[
 F(Xv)=X^{(p)}F(v).                                               \tag{CP18}
\]
This follows entry by entry from $(a+b)^p=a^p+b^p$ and
$(ab)^p=a^pb^p$. The source and target on the right are their
Frobenius pullbacks, with coefficient matrix $X^{(p)}$.
This exact semilinear map does not require identifying it with
$\exp((\log Q_F)M_S)$.

For the independent original sum-polynomial presentation the
evaluation map is still literally
\[
 V_k[(a,b),r]=\lambda_{ab}^{r},\quad0\le r<(k+1)^2.              \tag{CP19}
\]
If residue-field reduction makes two grid values equal, their rows
are equal and this square matrix has a nonzero kernel. The retained
cofactor maps act on the biform module, so do not remove this
explicit kernel of the preceding interpolation map. For any
composable injection $X_{{\rm full},d}$ its pullback on a preceding
map $F$ has exactly $\ker(X_{{\rm full},d}F)=\ker F$, by CP17.
This gives the exact kernel receiver across coefficient specialization.
Original derivative coordinates additionally require their factorials
to be invertible; Hasse coefficients use the full product identity
without such a division. A low-jet subspace is restricted only through
its actual matrix, not identified with its reduction without its
rank calculation.

\section{Return to the original proper-source metric}
On the complement of CP16 the original proper-source kernel $E$ is
zero as an equality of its defined coefficient spaces. In its retained
value frame $Z_R$, the original source and target therefore read
\[
 \begin{split}
 Q_N^p&=Z_R^*J_0^*D_{k,N-v}^{(s_0)}J_0Z_R,\\
 H_N^p&=F_R^*D_{\rm target}F_R
  -F_R^*D_{\rm target}J_*
   (J_*^*D_{\rm target}J_*)^{-1}J_*^*D_{\rm target}F_R.
 \end{split}                                                       \tag{CP20}
\]
These are the original maps from NC25--27, not a new choice of
source order or target direction space. The source-$E$ determinant
correction is exactly one. The complete target-$J_*$ subtraction
in CP20 persists. Thus the finite-field cofactor recovery and the
enlarged period locus give exact receivers into that remaining
metric calculation, without assigning it a value from a rank count.
