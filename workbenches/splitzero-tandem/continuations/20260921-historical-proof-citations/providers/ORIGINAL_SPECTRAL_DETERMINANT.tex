\section{Original objects, adjoints and the parity map}
Use exactly the packet and measure in NG1:
\begin{equation}\tag{SD1}
\begin{gathered}
0<\delta<\tfrac12,\quad\gamma>2,\quad m_0\ge1,\quad
k\ge5,\quad k\equiv1\pmod4,\\
e=1+k(m_0-1),\quad q=e(k+1)^2,\quad c=k/2,\\
Q(y)=\prod_{a,b=0}^k
\bigl[y-(2b-k)\gamma+i(2a-k)\delta\bigr]^e,\\
\dd\mu_k(y)=w_h^{*k}(y)\dd y,\qquad
w_h(y)=|(2\xi/h)(1/2+iy)|^2/(2\pi).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The stipulated full quartet divisor $h$, its arithmetic unit and all
multiplicities remain in this measure. The original coordinate is
$S=c+iy$; the map $\C[S]/(\chi)\to E=\C[y]/(Q)$ substitutes this exact
coordinate, with $Q=i^{-q}\chi(c+iy)$. The even degree $q$ is divisible
by four. The complete root multiset is closed under negation and
conjugation, has no real root, and lies in $|y|\le R_k$, where
$R_k=k\sqrt{\delta^2+\gamma^2}$. In particular $Q$ is positive on $\R$.

For each $q-1\le N\le2q-1$, let $J_N:\PP_{\le N}\to E$ be the
actual remainder map and $L_N:E\to\PP_{\le N}$ its attained minimum
section in $L^2(\mu_k)$. Its image $\mathcal V_N$ is the orthogonal
complement of $Q\PP_{<d}$, $d=N+1-q$. The quotient metric $G_N$ makes
$L_N$ an isometry. Let $M$ be multiplication by $y$ on $E$ and let
$C_N=L_N^{-1}P_{\mathcal V_N}yL_N$. Throughout, $\dagger$ means the
adjoint in this particular $G_N$, not a coefficient-space adjoint.
If $p_{N+1}$ is the real monic source orthogonal polynomial, NG21 gives
\begin{equation}\tag{SD2}
M=C_N+r_N\ell_N,\qquad r_N=[p_{N+1}],\qquad
\ell_N(v)=[y^N]L_Nv,\qquad C_N^\dagger=C_N.
\end{equation}
Indeed, subtract $\ell_N(v)p_{N+1}$ from $yL_Nv$ before its degree-$N$
projection, then take its remainder. This proves both the sign and the
map in SD2 in the original metric.

The even original measure makes $\Pi[P(y)]=[P(-y)]$ a unitary involution.
It preserves the remainder map, its minimum section, and the relation
space. Thus
\begin{equation}\tag{SD3}
\Pi C_N\Pi=-C_N,\qquad
\Pi r_N=(-1)^{N+1}r_N,\qquad
\ell_N\Pi=(-1)^N\ell_N,\qquad \ell_Nr_N=0.
\end{equation}
Write $v_N=\ell_N^\dagger$ and
$\kappa_N=\norm{r_N}_{G_N}\norm{v_N}_{G_N}=\norm{r_N\ell_N}_{G_N}$.
Both factors are nonzero. A zero remainder of $p_j$ would make the
nonreal roots of $Q$ roots of a source orthogonal polynomial, whose
roots are real. Moreover, the remainder of $p_N$ is nonzero and its
minimum section has a nonzero degree-$N$ coefficient: the identity
$\langle[p_N],x\rangle_{G_N}=\omega_N[y^N]L_Nx$ proves this directly.
The real-root property follows from the elementary orthogonality
argument: the product of the real sign-changing factors, if of degree
less than $j$, would have a nonzero integral against $p_j$. This is the
standard orthogonal-polynomial fact recorded by Koornwinder and
coauthors \cite{DLMF}; its hypotheses hold for the full measure here.

\section{An exact determinant with the full roots and boundary vectors}
For $a>0$ define the following actual positive operators and scalars:
\begin{equation}\tag{SD4}
D_a=C_N^2+a^2I,\qquad
A_a=r_N^\dagger D_a^{-1}r_N,\qquad
B_a=\ell_ND_a^{-1}\ell_N^\dagger,\qquad
p_N^C(z)=\det(zI-C_N).
\end{equation}
The symbol $p_N^C$ is a characteristic polynomial of degree $q$, and
is distinct from the source polynomial $p_{N+1}$.

\begin{theorem}[Original regularized determinant]
For every cutoff and every $a>0$,
\begin{equation}\tag{SD5}
\boxed{\det(M^\dagger M+a^2I)
=|Q(ia)|^2+a^2|p_N^C(ia)|^2 A_aB_a.}
\end{equation}
Every term is computed in the original attained metric and every root
and repeated factor of $Q$ remains in the first term.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For this proof write $C=C_N$, $r=r_N$, $v=\ell_N^\dagger$ and
$h=v^\dagger D_a^{-1}Cr$. The difference between $M^\dagger M+a^2I$
and $D_a$ is
$Crv^\dagger+vr^\dagger C+\norm r^2vv^\dagger$.
The determinant identity $\det(I+UV)=\det(I+VU)$ follows by eliminating
either diagonal block of $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&U\\-V&I\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
Apply it with columns $Cr,v$ and the two-by-two coefficient matrix
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&\norm r^2\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
Expanding that two-by-two determinant gives
\[
\frac{\det(M^\dagger M+a^2I)}{\det D_a}
=|1+h|^2+
\bigl(\norm r^2-r^\dagger CD_a^{-1}Cr\bigr)B_a
=|1+h|^2+a^2A_aB_a.
\]
There has been no estimate or discarded term in this expansion.
Parity gives $v^\dagger D_a^{-1}r=0$, because $D_a$ preserves the two
opposite parity spaces in SD3. The rank-one determinant formula,
proved by the same block elimination, therefore gives
\[
\frac{Q(ia)}{p_N^C(ia)}
=1-v^\dagger(iaI-C)^{-1}r
=1+v^\dagger(C+iaI)D_a^{-1}r=1+h.
\]
Finally $\det D_a=|p_N^C(ia)|^2$, since $C$ is selfadjoint. Substitute
these two equalities in the preceding expansion to prove SD5.
\end{proof}

This formula also identifies the contribution at zero exactly:
\begin{equation}\tag{SD6}
\det(M^\dagger M)=|Q(0)|^2
=\prod_{a,b=0}^k\bigl[(2b-k)^2\gamma^2+(2a-k)^2\delta^2\bigr]^e.
\end{equation}
Here one can take determinants of $M$ directly; no invertibility of
$C_N$ at zero is assumed.

\section{The finite logarithmic error and the action receiver}
Put $a=q\sqrt t$, for an arbitrary fixed $t>0$, and define
\begin{equation}\tag{SD7}
\begin{gathered}
F_N(t)=\log\det(I+M^\dagger M/(tq^2)),\qquad
L_N^{\rm sp}(t)=\log\det(I+C_N^2/(tq^2)),\\
\alpha_N(t)=q^2 A_a/\norm{r_N}^2,\quad
\beta_N(t)=q^2 B_a/\norm{\ell_N^\dagger}^2,\qquad
\zeta_N(t)=\frac{|Q(ia)/p_N^C(ia)|^2}{a^2A_aB_a}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
These definitions do not rescale the original metric. They specify the
arguments of dimensionless spectral tests, just as in NG23.
SD5 gives the complete finite equality
\begin{equation}\tag{SD8}
F_N(t)=L_N^{\rm sp}(t)+2\log\kappa_N-2\log q
+\log(t\alpha_N(t)\beta_N(t))+\log(1+\zeta_N(t)).
\end{equation}
Let $B=5\max\{1,C_{\rm mult}(h)\}$, where the original multiplication
constant is CAI22/NG3. The degree of $L_Nv$ is at most $2q-1$, so that
NG3 proves $\norm{C_N}\le Bq$ throughout this window. Consequently
\begin{equation}\tag{SD9}
\frac1{t+B^2}\le\alpha_N(t),\beta_N(t)\le\frac1t,
\qquad
0\le\zeta_N(t)\le
\frac{q^2}{\kappa_N^2}\frac{(t+B^2)^2}{t}
\exp\!\left(\frac{R_k^2}{tq}\right).
\end{equation}
For the last inequality, pair opposite roots of $Q$ to obtain
$|Q(ia)|\le a^q(1+R_k^2/a^2)^{q/2}$. The paired real eigenvalues of
$C_N$ give $|p_N^C(ia)|\ge a^q$, even if a zero eigenvalue occurs.
Finally $A_aB_a\ge\kappa_N^2/[q^4(t+B^2)^2]$. These prove SD9 with
the complete root polynomial, without a missing region or a root limit.

We now prove the uniform lower bound needed to evaluate this error.
The trace of SD2 squared, using $(r_N\ell_N)^2=0$, is
\begin{equation}\tag{SD10}
\Tr M^2=\Tr C_N^2+2\ell_N C_Nr_N
=\frac{qk(k+2)}3(\gamma^2-\delta^2).
\end{equation}
The plus sign here follows from SD2. Changing the convention to
$M=C_N-\mathsf R_N$ changes the named rank-one vector accordingly.
The last equality sums the squared full roots in SD1; the mixed term
vanishes and $(k+1)^{-1}\sum_{a=0}^k(2a-k)^2=k(k+2)/3$.

NG20 and GM18--20 prove, if $d/q\to s\in(0,1]$,
\[
q^{-3}\Tr C_N^2\longrightarrow\mathfrak m(s)>1/24.
\]
The same limit at $s=0$ holds even when $d\to\infty$ and $d/q\to0$.
Indeed the source compression has dimension $q+d$ and uniformly
bounded spectrum after division by $q$. Its relation compression has
dimension $d$, so its divided second moment is $O(d/q)$. The rank-two
off-diagonal comparison in NG19--20 has divided error $O(1/q)$ for
the squared test on that fixed compact interval. The source moment
therefore tends to $\mathfrak m(0)=2/3$. The explicit GM18 formula
is continuous at zero and on $[0,1]$. A contradiction subsequence,
then a convergent subsequence of $d/q$, proves uniform convergence to
these moments over the full finite window. In particular, eventually
\[
\Tr C_N^2\ge q^3/48,\qquad |\Tr M^2|\le q^3/96
\]
for every cutoff. By SD10 and $|\ell_N C_Nr_N|\le Bq\kappa_N$,
\begin{equation}\tag{SD11}
\boxed{\kappa_N\ge q^2/(192B)}
\end{equation}
eventually uniformly in $N$. This uses the proved spectral limit, and
does not claim an effective numerical threshold in $k$.

The minimum--maximum characterization of singular values gives
\begin{equation}\tag{SD12}
|s_1(M)-\kappa_N|\le Bq,\qquad s_j(M)\le Bq\quad(j\ge2).
\end{equation}
The first statement is the two triangle inequalities for the operator
norm. For the second restrict the domain to $\ker\ell_N$ (codimension
one), where $M=C_N$, and use the singular-value variational formula.
Thus one singular value exceeds the scale $q$ by a factor tending to
infinity, and all the others are bounded by $Bq$. In particular
$\log s_1(M)^2=2\log\kappa_N+O_h(q^{-1})$ uniformly.

\section{The full root product at zero}
Here the repeated roots can be evaluated without using a metric limit.
Define
\begin{equation}\tag{SD15}
\mathfrak g(\delta,\gamma)=
\tfrac12\log(\delta^2+\gamma^2)-\tfrac32
+\tfrac12\left[\frac\delta\gamma\arctan\frac\gamma\delta
+\frac\gamma\delta\arctan\frac\delta\gamma\right].
\end{equation}
At every fixed original multiplicity,
\begin{equation}\tag{SD16}
\boxed{\log|Q(0)|=q\log k+q\mathfrak g(\delta,\gamma)
+O_h(q\log k/k).}
\end{equation}
To prove it, divide each root in SD1 by $k$. Its logarithmic modulus is
$f(x,y)=\tfrac12\log(\delta^2x^2+\gamma^2y^2)$ on the grid
$(x,y)=((2a-k)/k,(2b-k)/k)$. The empirical average of $f$ tends to
one quarter of its integral over $[-1,1]^2$, with error
$O_h(\log k/k)$. Here are bounds at its singularity. Voronoi cells
of this grid have diameter at most $3/k$. Outside the cells within
$6/k$ of the origin, $|\nabla f|\le C_h/(|x|+|y|)$; integration
of this bound against a cell diameter gives $O_h(1/k)$, since this
radial inverse is integrable in two dimensions. The finitely many
remaining cells have total error $O_h(\log k/k^2)$, by scaling them
by $k$ and integrating $|\log\sqrt{x^2+y^2}|$ on a bounded square.
No grid point is the origin, because $k$ is odd. The Voronoi weights
differ from the uniform weights $1/(k+1)^2$ by total variation
$O(1/k)$; all sampled absolute values are $O_h(\log k)$. This proves
the claimed error for the empirical average, including boundary cells.
Multiplication by the full $q=e(k+1)^2$ proves the error in SD16.

For the exact integral, evenness reduces it to $I=\int_0^1\int_0^1f$.
Homogeneity gives $xf_x+yf_y=1$ away from zero. Integrate
$\partial_x(xf)+\partial_y(yf)=2f+1$ on the square with a small
quarter disk removed. The new boundary term tends to zero as its
radius does, since it is $O(r^2|\log r|)$. Hence
$2I+1=\int_0^1f(1,y)\dd y+\int_0^1f(x,1)\dd x$.
Direct integration of the two logarithms gives SD15.

\section{The necessary small singular values in the original metric}
Write $\lambda_{j,N}=s_j(M)^2/q^2$ in decreasing order. Set
\[
\log^+x=\max\{\log x,0\},\qquad \log^-x=\max\{-\log x,0\}.
\]
Define the actual logarithmic loss
$\mathcal D_N=\sum_{j=1}^q\log^-\lambda_{j,N}$.
By SD6,
\begin{equation}\tag{SD17}
\sum_{j=1}^q\log\lambda_{j,N}=2\log|Q(0)|-2q\log q.
\end{equation}
The perturbation between $M^\dagger M$ and $C_N^2$ has rank at most
two: its image lies in the span of $C_Nr_N$ and $v_N$.
For Hermitian matrices differing by rank $r$, the eigenvalue counts
below any threshold differ by at most $r$: intersect the spectral
subspace with the kernel of their difference and apply the variational
principle, then interchange the matrices. This proves the count bound
here, including multiplicities and endpoint conventions.

After removing the one large singular value in SD12, all remaining
$\lambda_{j,N}$ lie in $[0,B^2]$. Apply the count bound to
$\min\{\log^+x,\log^+(B^2)\}$, whose variation is finite. Removing
the top singular value costs at most the constant $\log^+(B^2)$.
NG20, including the $s=0$
extension proved above, gives uniformly for $d/q\in[0,1]$
\begin{equation}\tag{SD18}
\frac1q\sum_{j=2}^q\log^+\lambda_{j,N}
=b(d/q)+o_h(1),\qquad
b(s)=\int\log^+(y^2)\dd\nu_s(y).
\end{equation}
The full density is the proved NG20/GM16 probability
\[
\dd\nu_s(y)=\left[h_0\left(\frac y{1+s}\right)
-s|y|\rho_s(y^2)\right]\dd y,\quad
h_0(y)=\frac1{2\pi}\operatorname{arcosh}(2/|y|)1_{0<|y|<2}.
\]
The exact $\rho_s$ and its elliptic endpoints are given and proved in
the complete NG/GM sources accompanying this note. The integral in
SD18 is finite; it is an explicit one-dimensional quadrature of that
proved density. Uniformity follows by the same subsequence argument
as SD11 and continuity of these compactly supported measures.

Use $\log^-x=\log^+x-\log x$ in SD17 and the top singular value in
SD12. This yields the evaluated logarithmic loss
\begin{equation}\tag{SD19}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\mathcal D_N={}&2\log\kappa_N+2(q-1)\log q-2q\log k
-2q\mathfrak g(\delta,\gamma)+qb(d/q)+o_h(q).
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}
The error is uniform over the original window. Unlike a weak spectral
limit applied to $\log x$ at zero, this calculation uses the exact
full determinant at zero, and uses the spectral limit only on the
bounded positive part. There is no assumption of uniform integrability
of the negative logarithm.

There is also an entirely finite bound for the smallest singular value.
For $0<\eta<1$, let
\begin{equation}\tag{SD20}
 m_{N,\eta}=\min\{q,\,2+\#\{j:|\lambda_j(C_N)|<q\sqrt\eta\}\}.
\end{equation}
If $q>R_k/\sqrt\eta$, the exact finite formula is
\begin{equation}\tag{SD21}
\boxed{\frac{s_q(M)^2}{q^2}
\le\eta\exp\!\left(
\frac{2\log|Q(0)|-2q\log q-q\log\eta}{m_{N,\eta}}
\right).}
\end{equation}
This displayed guard is a direct check on specified finite data, not
an additional unproved analytic estimate. To verify the formula, put
$n=\#\{j:\lambda_{j,N}<\eta\}$. The determinant and the guard give
$\sum\log\lambda_{j,N}<q\log\eta$, so $n\ge1$. The count bound
gives $n\le m_{N,\eta}$. Since all remaining values are at least
$\eta$, SD17 is at least $n\log\lambda_{q,N}+(q-n)\log\eta$.
Solving for $\log\lambda_{q,N}$ and replacing $n$ by its upper bound
in the resulting negative fraction proves SD21.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform decay below every inverse power]
For every fixed $A>0$, the original attained metrics satisfy
\begin{equation}\tag{SD22}
\boxed{\max_{q-1\le N\le2q-1}s_q(M;G_N)/q\le q^{-A}}
\end{equation}
for all sufficiently large admissible $k$, at every fixed $m_0$.
Every algebraic eigenvalue of this same $M$ has modulus at least
$\sqrt{\delta^2+\gamma^2}>2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The last statement follows directly from SD1: $2a-k$ and $2b-k$ are
nonzero odd integers. For the first statement the positivity proof
GM17 gives $\nu_s(y)\le h_0(y/(1+s))\le h_0(y/2)$ for $0\le s\le1$.
Consequently
\begin{equation}\tag{SD23}
\nu_s\{|y|<\sqrt\eta\}
\le\frac{\sqrt\eta}{\pi}
\left(1+\log\frac8{\sqrt\eta}\right)=:\mathfrak a(\eta)
\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
This uses $\operatorname{arcosh}u\le\log(2u)$ and integrates the
logarithm explicitly. The measures have no atoms. NG20 and the
subsequence argument therefore give $m_{N,\eta}/q\le
\mathfrak a(\eta)+o_h(1)$ uniformly in $N$ for each fixed $\eta$.
Meanwhile $\log(q/R_k)/\log q\to1/2$ if $m_0=1$ and $2/3$ if
$m_0>1$, and $\log|Q(0)|\le q\log R_k$. Choose $\eta$ with
$\mathfrak a(\eta)<1/(8A+8)$. Eventually $m_{N,\eta}/q<1/(4A+4)$,
the guard in SD21 holds, and $\log(q/R_k)>\tfrac13\log q$.
In SD21 the numerator is at most
$-\tfrac23q\log q+q|\log\eta|$.
Its ratio to $m_{N,\eta}$ is eventually smaller than $-2A\log q$,
because $\tfrac23(4A+4)>2A$. Exponentiating and taking square roots
proves SD22, with the harmless fixed prefactor absorbed by the strict
inequality in that coefficient. No effective value of the threshold
is inferred from the limiting spectral theorem.
\end{proof}

\section{The new metric handoff and the determinant it controls}

Fix the same multiplication $M$ and two positive metrics $G_0,G_1$.
Put $K=\log(G_0^{-1/2}G_1G_0^{-1/2})$ and
\[
G_s=G_0^{1/2}e^{sK}G_0^{1/2},\quad
S_s=e^{sK/2}G_0^{1/2},\quad T_s=S_sMS_s^{-1},\quad
X_s=T_s^*T_s,\quad Y_s=T_sT_s^*.
\]
The map $S_s:(E,G_s)\to\C^q$ is an exact isometry, since
$S_s^*S_s=G_s$. It does not replace either original metric.
Differentiation gives $T_s'=[K,T_s]/2$ and
$X_s'=T_s^*KT_s-(KX_s+X_sK)/2$. Cyclicity of the trace, or direct
termwise differentiation of the absolutely convergent exponential
series, then gives
\begin{equation}\tag{SD26}
\begin{aligned}
\partial_s\Tr e^{-\tau X_s}
&=\Tr K\bigl[\tau X_se^{-\tau X_s}-\tau Y_se^{-\tau Y_s}\bigr],\\
\partial_s\log\det(aI+X_s)
&=\Tr K\bigl[Y_s(aI+Y_s)^{-1}-X_s(aI+X_s)^{-1}\bigr],\\
\partial_s\Tr(aI+X_s)^{-1}
&=\Tr K\bigl[X_s(aI+X_s)^{-2}-Y_s(aI+Y_s)^{-2}\bigr].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For the second formula use $d\log\det D=\Tr D^{-1}dD$, obtained by
expanding the determinant to first order; for the third differentiate
$D^{-1}D=I$. The equality
$T_s f(T_s^*T_s)T_s^*=Y_sf(Y_s)$ follows on singular vectors, and
also follows by polynomial approximation on their finite spectra.
This proves all three displayed formulas.

In each bracket the two matrices have the same trace, since $X_s,Y_s$
have the same eigenvalues with multiplicities. Their spectra lie,
respectively, in $[0,1/\exp(1)]$, $[0,1]$, and $[0,1/(4a)]$.
The difference of two Hermitian operators between $0$ and $cI$ has
norm at most $c$. Subtract an arbitrary scalar $bI$ from $K$ and use
$|\Tr UV|\le\norm U_1\norm V$. Integration over $0\le s\le1$ yields
\begin{equation}\tag{SD27}
\begin{gathered}
\mathfrak d(G_0,G_1)=\min_{b\in\R}\norm{K-bI}_1,\\
|P_\tau(M;G_1)-P_\tau(M;G_0)|\le\mathfrak d/\exp(1),\\
|\log\det(aI+M^{\dagger_{G_1}}M)
-\log\det(aI+M^{\dagger_{G_0}}M)|\le\mathfrak d,\\
|\Tr(aI+M^{\dagger_{G_1}}M)^{-1}
-\Tr(aI+M^{\dagger_{G_0}}M)^{-1}|\le\mathfrak d/(4a).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Here $P_\tau(M;G)=\Tr e^{-\tau M^{\dagger_G}M}$ and all parameters
are positive. Scalar changes of a metric leave its adjoint unchanged;
this proves the scalar invariance used in $\mathfrak d$. No source
mass has been removed from any original quotient or action.
The heat constant is sharp: for
$M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and
$G_s=\operatorname{diag}(e^s,1)$ one has
$P_1=1+\exp(-e^{-s})$, whose derivative at zero is $1/\exp(1)$,
while $\mathfrak d(I,G_s)=|s|$.
The logarithmic constant is also sharp as a supremum: the derivative
of $\log\det(aI+M^{\dagger_{G_s}}M)$ at zero is $-1/(a+1)$,
whose absolute value tends to one as $a\downarrow0$.

For the original source bracket $H^-\preceq H\preceq H^+$ and the
same full onto map $\mathsf A$, minimization in each unchanged fibre
gives $G^-\preceq G\preceq G^+$, where
$G^\pm=(\mathsf A(H^\pm)^{-1}\mathsf A^*)^{-1}$. Let
$\rho_1\ge\cdots\ge\rho_q\ge1$ be the eigenvalues of
$(G^-)^{-1/2}G^+(G^-)^{-1/2}$ and $m=\lfloor q/2\rfloor$. Then
\begin{equation}\tag{SD28}
\boxed{\mathfrak d(G^-,G)\le D_*:=\sum_{j=1}^m\log\rho_j.}
\end{equation}
Indeed, the minimum sum of absolute deviations of $q$ real numbers
is their upper $m$ sum minus their lower $m$ sum: any median is a
minimizer, as is seen by the left and right slopes of this convex
piecewise linear function. Apply this to the logarithms of the
generalized eigenvalues $r_j$ of $G$ relative to $G^-$. Their lower
sum is nonnegative and $r_j\le\rho_j$ by the variational principle.
The bound is attained over the whole matrix bracket by choosing the
top $m$ eigenvalues to be $\rho_j$ and the rest one in the eigenbasis
of its upper endpoint. This need not be an attained member of a
particular correlated source family, but it is a valid uniform bound
for every such member. The correlated source family remains intact.

With $a=tq^2$, SD27--28 give a certified error $D_*$ for the actual
$F_N(t)$ in SD7. They can therefore be applied directly to the two
terms and the boundary correction in SD13, while retaining their
common metric. In particular a spectral-radius estimate has not been
substituted for the original metric-dependent singular values.
The endpoint discrepancy $\mathfrak d(G^-,G^+)$ cannot replace $D_*$:
if $G^+=\kappa G^-$ it is zero, whereas intermediate metrics can be
anisotropic. For $M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
the intermediate metrics $\operatorname{diag}(\kappa,1)$ and
$\operatorname{diag}(1,\kappa)$ give heat traces $1+e^{-\kappa\tau}$
and $1+e^{-\tau/\kappa}$; the two endpoints both give $1+e^{-\tau}$.
This checks the handoff's correction to endpoint-only heat bracketing.
