\subsection{1.2 Finite lower and upper constants}\label{finite-lower-and-upper-constants}

For an integer \(n\ge1\), define \[
\begin{gathered}

M_n=4(R+14)^5,\qquad L_n=\log(2M_n),\\

C_h=\max\{2^{24},\,[(2R_h)^2+1](2R_h+6)^{10}\},\qquad

\end{gathered} \tag{P1}
\] Then, on the complete polynomial space of degree at most \(n\), \[
\boxed{

 \le b_{h,n}\|P\|_\sigma^2 .
} \tag{P2}
\] In particular, \[
-\log a_{h,n}=O_h(\log^2(n+2)),\qquad
\log b_{h,n}=O_h(\log(n+2)). \tag{P3}
\] The constants in (P1) are deliberately conservative. No assertion of pointwise nonvanishing of zeta on the critical line enters (P2).

\subsubsection{Proof: the Gamma polynomial cannot put substantial mass on the excluded set}\label{proof-the-gamma-polynomial-cannot-put-substantial-mass-on-the-excluded-set}

The digamma series gives, for \(a>0,t\ge0\), \[
\operatorname{Im}\psi(a+it)
=\frac{t}{a^2+t^2}+\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{t}{(j+a)^2+t^2}.
\] At \(a=1/4\), the first term is at most \(2\), and comparison of the decreasing summands with their integral bounds the sum by \(\pi/2\). Consequently \[
|(\log\sigma)'(y)|<4.
\] For the interval \([y-1,y+1]\), expansion in its orthonormal Legendre basis bounds point evaluation by \((n+1)^2/2\) times its unweighted squared norm. The preceding density comparison therefore proves \[
 |P(y)|^2\sigma(y)\le B_n\|P\|_\sigma^2. \tag{P4}
\] This is a full complex-polynomial bound.

The Gamma Jacobi coefficients are \(\sqrt{j(j-1/2)}\); hence \(\|yP\|_\sigma\le2(n+1)\|P\|_\sigma\). For \(R\) in (P1), \[
 \int_{|y|>R}|P(y)|^2\sigma(y)\,dy
 \le\frac1{16}\|P\|_\sigma^2. \tag{P5}
\]

Put \(g(s)=(s-1)\zeta(s)\). The Euler--Maclaurin identity, with its even Bernoulli remainder retained, is \[
\zeta(s)=\frac1{s-1}+\frac12+\frac{s}{12}
-\frac{s(s+1)(s+2)}{720}
-\frac{(s)_4}{24}\int_1^\infty
 \widetilde B_4(x)x^{-s-4}\,dx,\qquad \Re s>-3.
\] It follows by integrating the periodic Bernoulli remainder once in the usual DLMF formula. Since \(\sup|\widetilde B_4|\le1/30\), on \(\Re s\ge-2\) it gives the conservative bound \[
 |g(s)|\le(|s|+4)^5. \tag{P6}
\] At \(s=2+ij\), the Euler product gives \(|1/\zeta(2+ij)|\le\zeta(2)<2\), whence \(|g(2+ij)|>1/2\).

For each integer \(-R\le j\le R\), consider \(g(2+ij+z)\) in \(|z|\le4\). It is bounded by \(M_n\). Jensen's formula, or its direct factorization and mean-log proof, bounds the number of its zeros in \(|z|<3\), with multiplicity, by \(K_n\). Divide by the disk-radius-three Blaschke product of exactly those zeros. The quotient \(F\) is nonvanishing in \(|z|<3\), is bounded above by \(M_n\), and satisfies \(|F(0)|\ge1/2\). The positive harmonic function \(\log(M_n/|F|)\), using the radius-three Poisson kernel on \(|z|\le2\), is at most \(5L_n\). Thus \[
 |F(z)|\ge M_ne^{-5L_n}=\frac1{32M_n^4},\qquad |z|\le2.
\] Each removed factor has modulus \[
 \left|\frac{3(z-a)}{9-\bar a z}\right|\ge|z-a|/5
 \quad(|a|<3,\ |z|\le2).
\] On the critical-line cell \(j-1/2\le y\le j+1/2\), the relative coordinate \(z=-3/2+i(y-j)\) lies in \(|z|<2\). Except within distance \(\epsilon_n\) of one of the listed zeros, it follows that \[
 |g(1/2+iy)|\ge c_n. \tag{P7}
\] The total length excluded from \([-R,R]\) is at most \(2m_nK_n\epsilon_n=1/(32B_n)\). By (P4) its polynomial Gamma mass is at most \(1/32\) of the full norm. Together with (P5), the retained set therefore carries at least \(29/32\), in particular at least \(1/2\), of that norm. Zeros have been accounted for in an estimate; they have not been removed from the original source.

The exact original density ratio is \[
 \frac{w_h(y)}{\sigma(y)}
 =\frac{|1/2+iy|^2|g(1/2+iy)|^2}
 {\sqrt\pi\,|h(1/2+iy)|^2}. \tag{P8}
\] On the retained set, \(|1/2+iy|^2\ge1/4\) and \(|h(1/2+iy)|\le(R+R_h)^d\). Combining (P7), (P8), and the retained half-mass proves the lower half of (P2).

\subsubsection{Proof: upper bound, including removable critical-line factors}\label{proof-upper-bound-including-removable-critical-line-factors}

Because \(h\) is a divisor of the actual zeta zero divisor, \(g/h\) is entire. All its prescribed denominator roots satisfy \(0<\Re\rho<1\). On the boundary of the rectangle \[
 -1\le\Re s\le2,\qquad |\Im s|\le2R_h,
\] every factor of \(h\) has modulus at least one. Formula (P6) and the maximum principle therefore give \[
 |g(s)/h(s)|\le(2R_h+6)^5
\] throughout that rectangle. This explicitly uses removable values; it does not divide by a zero on the original line.

On \(|y|\ge2R_h\), instead use \(|h(1/2+iy)|\ge(|y|/2)^d\), (P6), and \(|1/2+iy|\le2|y|\). Together with the preceding rectangle bound, these estimates give \[
 w_h(y)\le C_h(1+y^2)^6\sigma(y). \tag{P9}
\] The factor \((1+y^2)^6\) is exactly \(|(y+i)^6|^2\). Six applications of the Gamma multiplication bound, at the increasing degrees \(n,n+1,\ldots,n+5\), give the upper half of (P2), with \(b_{h,n}\) as declared. Finally \(K_n=O(\log(n+2))\) and \(-\log\epsilon_n=O(\log(n+2))\), proving (P3).

\subsection{1.3 An explicit bound for all original divided jets}\label{an-explicit-bound-for-all-original-divided-jets}

Set \[
 Z_h=\max_\rho|\rho-1/2|,\quad
 u_n=\tfrac12\log(2n+1),\quad M_\sigma=\sqrt{2\pi},
\] \[

\] Then \[
\boxed{

 \log\Lambda_{h,n}=O_h(\log^2(n+2)).
} \tag{P11}
\]

To see every factor, use the original generating function \[
 \sum_{m\ge0}p_m(y)z^m/m!=(1+z^2)^{-1/4}e^{y\arctan z},
 \qquad \|p_m\|_\sigma^2=M_\sigma m!(1/2)_m.

 |\arctan z|\le u_n,\quad
 |1+z^2|^{-1/4}\le(n+1)^{1/4},\quad |z|^{-m}\le e
