\paragraph{Scope and provenance.}
This note independently proves a finite-dimensional comparison for the original
metric and rank-one family specified below. It receives the fixed matrices used
in the programme's quadratic metric calculation; it does not replace that metric
or its quotient, and does not identify singular values with arithmetic zeros.
Every identity and estimate used in the comparison is proved here by matrix
algebra and absolutely convergent power series. No infinite-dimensional heat
expansion, Riemann-hypothesis assertion, or uninspected literature result is used.
The human context is Connes's Gaussian heat test function \cite{ConnesHeat}
and Connes--van Suijlekom's rank-one spectral-action calculation
\cite{ConnesSuijlekom}. The original author sources were inspected by the parent
heat reader and the preceding spectral-action reader, respectively, not
independently inspected for this comparison. Their bibliographic identities
and source locators are retained below with that reading boundary explicit.
This comparison independently proves its finite formulas; it invokes none of
those sources' infinite-dimensional theorems and does not attribute their
historical constructions to the programme.

\section{The retained data and the exact comparison map}

Let \(E=\mathbb C^d\), with its specified positive Hermitian matrix \(G\).
For columns \(x,y\) and endomorphisms \(X\), set
\begin{equation}
 \langle x,y\rangle_G=x^*Gy,\qquad
 X^\adj=G^{-1}X^*G,\qquad
 \|X\|_{\mathrm{HS},G}^2=\tr(X^\adj X),\qquad
 \|X\|_G=\sup_{x\ne0}\frac{\|Xx\|_G}{\|x\|_G}.
 \tag{HM1}\label{HM1}
\end{equation}
Fix the original \(G\)-selfadjoint \(A\), column \(r\), and row \(\ell\), with
\begin{equation}
 A^\adj=A,\qquad Q=r\ell,\qquad \ell r=0,\qquad
 T(t)=A-tQ,\quad t\in\mathbb R,\qquad
 \epsilon^2=\tr(Q^\adj Q).
 \tag{HM2}\label{HM2}
\end{equation}
In particular \(Q^2=r(\ell r)\ell=0\), including the case \(Q=0\).
The contextual rank-one perturbation method occurs in
\cite[original-source Section \texttt{sect:sa}]{ConnesSuijlekom};
the metric and all finite formulas below are explicitly specified and proved here.
The finite heat traces compared below are
\begin{equation}
 Z_{\mathrm{hol}}(s,t)=\tr\exp(-sT(t)^2),\qquad
 Z_{\mathrm{met}}(s,t)=\tr\exp(-sT(t)^\adj T(t)),\qquad s>0.
 \tag{HM3}\label{HM3}
\end{equation}
The Gaussian heat choice is the finite counterpart of the test-function
calculation in \cite[original-source formula \texttt{ft}]{ConnesHeat};
this citation supplies context, not an infinite trace identity used in the proof.
The first uses the original endomorphism; the second uses that same
endomorphism and its original metric adjoint. Both exponentials are defined
by their everywhere convergent matrix power series. Our exact scalar comparison is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta(s,t)=\Rea Z_{\mathrm{hol}}(s,t)-Z_{\mathrm{met}}(s,t).
 \tag{HM4}\label{HM4}
\end{equation}
The second term is real, because \(T^\adj T\) is \(G\)-selfadjoint and positive.

For clarity about norms, the map \(U:E_G\longrightarrow E_{I_d}\),
\(Ux=G^{1/2}x\), is an isometry, with inverse \(G^{-1/2}\).
It carries \(X\) to \(UXU^{-1}\), and carries \(X^\adj\) to
\((UXU^{-1})^*\). Traces are preserved by this conjugation, as follows
directly by summing matrix entries in \(\tr XY=\tr YX\).
This proves the positivity just used and permits a proof of norm inequalities
in these isometric coordinates; all statements below remain in the fixed \(G\).

The vector \(v=G^{-1}\ell^*\) satisfies \(\ell x=\langle v,x\rangle_G\).
Writing \(a=r^*Gr\) and \(b=v^*Gv=\ell G^{-1}\ell^*\), multiplication gives
\begin{equation}
 Q^\adj=vr^*G,\qquad Q^\adj Q=a\,vv^*G,\qquad
 \epsilon^2=ab,\qquad
 \|Q\|_G=\|Q\|_{\mathrm{HS},G}=\epsilon.
 \tag{HM5}\label{HM5}
\end{equation}
Indeed \(Qx=r\langle v,x\rangle_G\), so Cauchy--Schwarz bounds its squared
norm by \(ab\|x\|_G^2\); if \(a,b>0\), equality holds at \(x=v\).
If either is zero, \(Q=0\). The finite-dimensional Cauchy--Schwarz inequality
itself follows by minimizing \(\|x-\alpha y\|_G^2\) over complex \(\alpha\).

\section{A dimension-independent coefficient estimate}

Fix \(t\). In this section \(T=T(t)\) and \(B=\|T\|_G\).
The following interpolation is an exact comparison path inside the original
endomorphism space, not a substitute for the original family:
\begin{equation}
 H=\frac{T+T^\adj}{2},\qquad K=\frac{T-T^\adj}{2i},\qquad
 Z(u)=H+iuK=\frac{1+u}{2}T+\frac{1-u}{2}T^\adj,
 \quad 0\le u\le1.
 \tag{HM6}\label{HM6}
\end{equation}
Both \(H\) and \(K\) are \(G\)-selfadjoint. The operator norm of an adjoint
equals that of the operator: in the isometric coordinates above this follows
from \(\|Xx\|=\sup_{\|y\|=1}|y^*Xx|\).
Thus the convex combination in \eqref{HM6} proves
\begin{equation}
 \|Z(u)\|_G\le B,\qquad \|Z(u)^\adj\|_G\le B.
 \tag{HM7}\label{HM7}
\end{equation}
Since \(Q^2=(Q^\adj)^2=0\), direct expansion yields
\begin{equation}
 K=\frac{t(Q^\adj-Q)}{2i},\qquad
 \|K\|_{\mathrm{HS},G}^2=\tr K^2=\frac{t^2\epsilon^2}{2}.
 \tag{HM8}\label{HM8}
\end{equation}

We will use the following explicit trace estimate. A cyclic word containing
two specified factors \(K\) and otherwise factors of operator norm at most
\(B\), of total length \(m\), has absolute trace at most
\(\|K\|_{\mathrm{HS},G}^2 B^{m-2}\).
To prove this, cyclically write the word as \(K U K V\). Matrix-entry
Cauchy--Schwarz in the isometric coordinates gives
\(|\tr XY|\le\|X\|_{\mathrm{HS},G}\|Y\|_{\mathrm{HS},G}\).
Summing squared column norms proves
\(\|XY\|_{\mathrm{HS},G}\le\|X\|_G\|Y\|_{\mathrm{HS},G}\);
taking adjoints proves the corresponding right-multiplication inequality.
Applying these to \(KU\) and \(KV\), and using submultiplicativity of the
operator norm, proves the stated estimate. Empty products have norm one.

\begin{lemma}
For every integer \(n\ge1\), put
\begin{equation}
 D_n(t)=\tr\bigl((T^\adj T)^n\bigr)-\Rea\tr(T^{2n}).
 \tag{HM9}\label{HM9}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 D_1(t)=t^2\epsilon^2,\qquad
 |D_n(t)|\le n(2n-1)t^2\epsilon^2 B^{2n-2}.
 \tag{HM10}\label{HM10}
\end{equation}
For \(n=1\), the power \(B^0\) equals one even when \(B=0\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Define
\(f_n(u)=\tr((Z(u)^\adj Z(u))^n)-\Rea\tr Z(u)^{2n}\).
At \(u=0\), both words are \(H^{2n}\), so \(f_n(0)=0\).
For the alternating word, differentiation at zero produces \(n\) copies
of \(+iK\) and \(n\) copies of \(-iK\). Cyclicity makes their traces equal
apart from these signs, and they cancel. For the holomorphic word, its trace
derivative is \(2ni\tr(H^{2n-1}K)\), whose real part vanishes:
\(\tr(H^{2n-1}K)\) is real because its conjugate is
\(\tr(KH^{2n-1})\). Consequently \(f_n'(0)=0\).

A second derivative of a word of length \(m=2n\) has \(m(m-1)\) ordered
terms, each obtained by differentiating two distinct factors. Each term
has two factors \(K\), multiplied by powers of \(i\) of modulus one.
By \eqref{HM7} and the trace estimate above, the absolute value of each
term is at most \(\|K\|_{\mathrm{HS},G}^2B^{m-2}\).
There are two words in \(f_n\), whence
\[
 |f_n''(u)|\le2m(m-1)\|K\|_{\mathrm{HS},G}^2B^{m-2}.
\]
Twice integrating the polynomial \(f_n\) gives
\(f_n(1)=\int_0^1(1-u)f_n''(u)\,du\).
Its absolute value is therefore at most
\(m(m-1)\|K\|_{\mathrm{HS},G}^2B^{m-2}\), which becomes the bound
in \eqref{HM10} after \eqref{HM8}.
Finally,
\(\tr(T^\adj T)-\Rea\tr(T^2)=2\tr K^2=t^2\epsilon^2\)
by expanding \(T=H+iK\); this proves its exact first coefficient.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact heat series, explicit error, and recovery of the allowance}

The defining power series and \eqref{HM9} give the exact identity
\begin{equation}
 \Delta(s,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
       \frac{(-1)^{n+1}s^n}{n!}D_n(t).
 \tag{HM11}\label{HM11}
\end{equation}
There is no limiting dimension in this formula. Its absolute convergence
follows either from the finite matrix exponential or directly from
\eqref{HM10}. In particular, the dimension cancels before any estimate.

\begin{theorem}
Put \(x=sB^2\) and \(\mathcal E(x)=(1+2x)e^x-1\). Then
\begin{equation}
 \left|\Delta(s,t)-s t^2\epsilon^2\right|
 \le s t^2\epsilon^2\mathcal E(x).
 \tag{HM12}\label{HM12}
\end{equation}
For \(0\le x<1\), the further completely explicit bound is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal E(x)\le\frac{3x}{1-x}.
 \tag{HM13}\label{HM13}
\end{equation}
All bounds remain valid with \(B\) replaced by any known upper bound, in
particular \(\|A\|_G+|t|\epsilon\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For \(B>0\), summing \eqref{HM10} for \(n\ge2\) proves the first bound,
because differentiating the absolutely convergent scalar exponential gives
\[
 \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n(2n-1)x^n}{n!}
   =x(1+2x)e^x.
\]
Subtract its \(n=1\) term and divide by \(B^2\); the result is
\(s[(1+2x)e^x-1]\), exactly \eqref{HM12}.
When \(B=0\), \(T=0\), so \(K=0\) and \eqref{HM8} gives
\(t^2\epsilon^2=0\); both sides of \eqref{HM12} vanish.
The coefficientwise inequality \(e^x\le\sum_{j\ge0}x^j=1/(1-x)\)
for \(0\le x<1\) gives \eqref{HM13}. Monotonicity follows directly from
the nonnegative coefficients of \(\mathcal E\). The stated upper bound
for \(B\) follows from the triangle inequality and \eqref{HM5}.
\end{proof}

For example, \(sB^2\le1/7\) implies
\begin{equation}
 \frac12\,s t^2\epsilon^2\le\Delta(s,t)
       \le\frac32\,s t^2\epsilon^2.
 \tag{HM14}\label{HM14}
\end{equation}
More generally, for any \(0<\eta<1\), the sufficient restriction
\(sB^2\le\eta/(3+\eta)\) gives relative error at most \(\eta\).
For each fixed \(t\ne0\), the exact recovery formula is
\begin{equation}
 \epsilon^2=\lim_{s\downarrow0}\frac{\Delta(s,t)}{s t^2}.
 \tag{HM15}\label{HM15}
\end{equation}
The convergence rate is the displayed error \(\mathcal E(sB^2)\), not an
unquantified remainder. At \(t=0\), both heat traces agree for every \(s\),
and no division by \(t\) is made. If \(\mathcal E(x)<1\) and \(t\ne0\),
\eqref{HM12} also gives the finite interval
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\Delta(s,t)}{s t^2[1+\mathcal E(x)]}
 \le\epsilon^2\le
 \frac{\Delta(s,t)}{s t^2[1-\mathcal E(x)]}.
 \tag{HM16}\label{HM16}
\end{equation}
This is an estimate using the same \(\epsilon\), \(G\), and \(T\); it is
not an independent claim that the allowance tends to zero in an arithmetic
limit. Evaluating the two heat traces and an admissible norm bound remains
a calculation on those actual supplied matrices.

\section{The next coefficient retains its full cubic term}

Retain the possibly complex number \(\gamma=\ell Ar=\tr AQ\).
The following exact formula sharpens \eqref{HM12}:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 D_2(t)={}&2t^2\tr\bigl(A^2(QQ^\adj+Q^\adj Q)\bigr)
       -4t^3\epsilon^2\Rea\gamma+t^4\epsilon^4,\\
 \Delta(s,t)={}&s t^2\epsilon^2-\frac{s^2}{2}D_2(t)+R_3(s,t),\\
 |R_3(s,t)|\le{}&s t^2\epsilon^2
       \bigl[(1+2x)e^x-1-3x\bigr].
 \end{split}
 \tag{HM17}\label{HM17}
\end{equation}
To prove the coefficient, first use \(Q^2=0\) and trace cyclicity in the
six words containing two \(Q\)'s in \((A-tQ)^4\). Four have zero trace;
the other two give \(\tr(AQAQ)=\gamma^2\). Every word with three or four
\(Q\)'s has zero trace. Thus
\[
 \Rea\tr T^4=\tr A^4-4t\Rea\tr A^3Q+2t^2\Rea\gamma^2.
\]
Writing \(C=AQ+Q^\adj A\) and \(R=Q^\adj Q\), one has
\(T^\adj T=A^2-tC+t^2R\). Expansion and cyclicity give
\[
 \tr(T^\adj T)^2=\tr A^4-2t\tr A^2C
  +t^2[\tr C^2+2\tr A^2R]-2t^3\tr CR+t^4\tr R^2.
\]
Here direct multiplication proves
\[
 \tr A^2C=2\Rea\tr A^3Q,\quad
 \tr C^2=2\Rea\gamma^2+2\tr A^2QQ^\adj,
 \quad \tr CR=2\epsilon^2\Rea\gamma,\quad \tr R^2=\epsilon^4.
\]
For the third and fourth identities, \(QQ^\adj Q=\epsilon^2Q\) and
\((Q^\adj Q)^2=\epsilon^2Q^\adj Q\) follow at once from \eqref{HM5}.
Subtracting proves the first line of \eqref{HM17}. Its remainder follows
from the same absolutely convergent coefficient estimate as before, now
subtracting the \(n=2\) bound, whose contribution is \(3x\).
In particular, no imaginary part is silently discarded in the holomorphic
trace: its \(\Rea\gamma^2\) term cancels exactly against the metric trace's
corresponding term. The surviving cubic term is explicitly
\(-4t^3\epsilon^2\Rea\gamma\).

\section{Exact two-dimensional example and limitations of sign claims}

Take the original finite data
\begin{equation}
 G=\begin{pmatrix}3&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
 A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1/3\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 Q=\begin{pmatrix}0&4/3\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 \epsilon^2=16/3.
 \tag{HM18}\label{HM18}
\end{equation}
Then \(A^\adj=A\), \(Q^2=0\), and, writing \(a=(1-4t)/3\),
\[
 T=\begin{pmatrix}0&a\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 T^2=aI_2,\qquad T^\adj T=\operatorname{diag}(1/3,3a^2).
\]
Consequently the exact heat comparison is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta(s,t)=2e^{-sa}-e^{-s/3}-e^{-3sa^2},\qquad
 B^2=\max\{1/3,3a^2\}.
 \tag{HM19}\label{HM19}
\end{equation}
Its first coefficient is
\(1/3+3a^2-2a=16t^2/3\), retaining the mass-dependent metric \(G\).
This is an explicitly identified finite example, not a constructed packet
of zeta zeros. Positivity in \eqref{HM14} is a small-\(s\) statement, not
a claim for every heat time. For example, another allowed family is
\(G=I_2\), \(A=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\),
\(Q=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), \(t=1\).
It gives \(T=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&2\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\),
so \(\Delta(s,1)=2e^{-2s}-e^{-s}-e^{-4s}<0\) whenever \(s\ge\log 2\),
because \(2e^{-2s}\le e^{-s}\) and \(e^{-4s}>0\).
The retained metric allowance is nevertheless exactly recovered by
\eqref{HM15}.

\paragraph{Verification scope.}
The accompanying exact checker verifies the finite coefficient identities,
including nonidentity metrics and complex \(\gamma\), and deliberate
sign, metric, and cubic-term failure controls. Those finite checks are
not the proof of the uniform estimate: its proof is the interpolation,
word count, and convergent scalar sum above.
