This calculation uses the original observation $\Lambda_k$, the
original upper corner idempotents, the full original output Gram,
and the actual four conductor corner coefficients. The coefficient
test and conductor lift are the explicit maps in
\cite[O1--3 and O10]{Observe}. The four labelled Fable source
columns are those of \cite{ES488}, and their original-conductor
receivers are retained from \cite{Bridge}. This note does not
replace the upper corner classes by their minimum-norm lifts.
It proves the restriction and its iterated recovery even when
some actual observation columns vanish.

\section{The actual invariant corner subspace}
Fix an original admitted simple quartet
\[
 (\rho_{\mathrm M},\rho_{\mathrm N},\rho_{\mathrm T},\rho_{\mathrm E})
 =\left(\tfrac12+\delta+i\gamma,\tfrac12+\delta-i\gamma,
        \tfrac12-\delta+i\gamma,\tfrac12-\delta-i\gamma\right),
 \quad 0<\delta<\tfrac12,\quad\gamma>2.
\]
At the original degree $k\ge9$ let $e_l\in E_k$ be the four
upper-grid root idempotents with indices
$(k,k),(k,0),(0,k),(0,0)$ in this order. Put
\begin{equation}
 J_+:\C^4\longrightarrow E_k,\quad J_+c=\sum_lc_le_l,
 \qquad D_k=\diag(\lambda_l),\quad\lambda_l=k\rho_l.
 \tag{AO1}
\end{equation}
The original arithmetic action satisfies exactly
$A_kJ_+=J_+D_k$, since each $e_l$ is its actual root idempotent.
All four $\lambda_l$ are distinct. Hence the four-dimensional
image of $J_+$ is invariant under that action.

Let $\mathcal B_k$ be the original output space, with its
complete positive Hermitian Gram matrix $Q$. Set
\begin{equation}
 b_l=\Lambda_k e_l,\qquad
 B=(b_{\mathrm M}\ b_{\mathrm N}\ b_{\mathrm T}\ b_{\mathrm E})
       =\Lambda_kJ_+,
 \qquad I=\{l:b_l\ne0\},\quad m=|I|.
 \tag{AO2}
\end{equation}
The letters $B$ and $Q$ in this note always denote these
actual observation data. No column is normalized. Inner
products are conjugate linear in the first argument.
On the corner subspace, for every integer $n\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda_k A_k^nJ_+c=B D_k^n c.\tag{AO3}
\end{equation}
This is obtained by iterating AO1. It does not require the
one-step kernel of $\Lambda_k$ in the entire $E_k$ to be invariant,
and makes no assertion that the whole observation quotient
inherits $A_k$.

\section{Full stacked Gram and exact recovery with at most four observations}
For $p\ge1$ retain the unscaled stack
\begin{equation}
 Z_p c=(BD_k^t c)_{t=0}^{p-1}\in\mathcal B_k^{\oplus p},
 \qquad Q_p=I_p\otimes Q.
 \tag{AO4}
\end{equation}
Its full original Gram has entries
\begin{equation}
 (K_p)_{lr}=(Z_p^*Q_pZ_p)_{lr}
   =b_l^*Qb_r\sum_{t=0}^{p-1}\overline{\lambda_l}^{\,t}\lambda_r^t.
 \tag{AO5}
\end{equation}
These are exact finite sums with all original root scales and
off-diagonal output correlations retained. If $l\notin I$,
the corresponding row and column are zero.

Suppose first $m>0$. For each $l\in I$ define the actual spectral
separating polynomial and actual output functional
\begin{equation}
 p_l(S)=\prod_{r\in I\setminus\{l\}}
              \frac{S-\lambda_r}{\lambda_l-\lambda_r}
        =\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}p_{l,t}S^t,
 \qquad
 \vartheta_l(y)=\frac{b_l^*Qy}{b_l^*Qb_l}.
 \tag{AO6}
\end{equation}
All denominators are nonzero because the original roots are
distinct and $Q>0$. The exact inverse on the active coordinates is
\begin{equation}
 c_l=\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}p_{l,t}\vartheta_l(y_t),
 \qquad (y_t)_{t=0}^{m-1}=Z_m c,\quad l\in I.
 \tag{AO7}
\end{equation}
To prove it, substitute $y_t=\sum_rb_r\lambda_r^tc_r$.
The sum over $t$ becomes
$\sum_{r\in I}p_l(\lambda_r)\vartheta_l(b_r)c_r=c_l$,
since $p_l(\lambda_r)=\delta_{lr}$ and
$\vartheta_l(b_l)=1$. Thus
\begin{equation}
 \ker Z_m=\operatorname{span}\{e_l^{\rm label}:l\notin I\},
 \qquad K_{m,I}>0.
 \tag{AO8}
\end{equation}
Here $e_l^{\rm label}$ is a coordinate vector of $\C^4$, and
$K_{m,I}$ is the active principal submatrix of AO5.
In particular even linearly dependent or collinear nonzero
$b_l$ are recovered by their original, distinct arithmetic
eigenvalues. At most $m\le4$ observations are needed.
If $m=0$, every stack is identically zero, and AO8 is to be
read as the full four-dimensional kernel; no inverse is assigned.

The polynomial
\begin{equation}
 \chi_I(S)=\prod_{l\in I}(S-\lambda_l)
       =S^m+\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}c_tS^t\tag{AO9}
\end{equation}
is the minimal polynomial of the actual active corner action.
Indeed an annihilating polynomial must vanish at each of its
$m$ distinct eigenvalues; divisibility by their product proves
minimality. The degree-$m-1$ separation in AO6 uses precisely
these roots, without the other $(k+1)^2-4$ grid roots.
For the particular columns the least number of observations is
exactly
\[
 p_{\min}=\min\{1\le p\le m:\rank K_{p,I}=m\}.
\]
This is a finite criterion using the full original matrices.
The upper bound $m$ is sharp in general: when all active columns
are nonzero multiples of one fixed vector, a stack of $p<m$
observations has rank at most $p$, so cannot recover $m$
coordinates. This last example states sharpness of the proved
general bound, not a replacement of the programme's columns.

\section{Exact finite action on the recovered observation space}
Let $\mathcal R_I=\operatorname{im}Z_m$ for $m>0$. Define on the
ambient stack the literal block companion operator
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal S(y_0,\ldots,y_{m-1})
    =(y_1,\ldots,y_{m-1},-\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}c_ty_t),
 \tag{AO10}
\end{equation}
with the $m=1$ formula interpreted as $y_0\mapsto-c_0y_0$.
For actual corner data, AO9 gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal S Z_m=Z_mD_k.\tag{AO11}
\end{equation}
The first $m-1$ components are shifts of the original observed
iterates; the last follows from
$\lambda_l^m+\sum_tc_t\lambda_l^t=0$ for every active label.
Consequently $\mathcal R_I$ is invariant, and the restriction
of $\mathcal S$ to it is exactly conjugate to the active
diagonal action $D_{k,I}$ by AO7. Its exponential satisfies
$e^{z\mathcal S}Z_m=Z_m e^{zD_k}$ for every complex $z$ by
the convergent finite-dimensional matrix series. This is a
constructed action on the actual stacked data, not an action
postulated on the whole one-step quotient.

The original inherited stack metric on active coordinates is
$K_{m,I}$. Its complete infinitesimal Hermitian defect is
\begin{equation}
 D_{k,I}^*K_{m,I}+K_{m,I}D_{k,I},\qquad
 [D_{k,I}^*K_{m,I}+K_{m,I}D_{k,I}]_{lr}
       =(\overline{\lambda_l}+\lambda_r)(K_m)_{lr}.
 \tag{AO12}
\end{equation}
Subtracting the original central term $kK_{m,I}$ leaves
$(\overline{\lambda_l}+\lambda_r-k)(K_m)_{lr}$.
On the single active eigenvector of label $l$ its value is
$2k\varepsilon_l\delta\,(K_m)_{ll}$, where
$\varepsilon_l$ is the original real-part sign. This proves
both signs whenever the active set contains both real-part
signs, with the entire original Gram still present.

For completeness, if $H=J_+^*G_{E_k}J_+>0$ is the original
corner Gram of a specified physical source metric, its quotient
by the invisible coordinate subspace has the exact Schur Gram
\begin{equation}
 H_{\rm quot}=H_{II}-H_{IZ}H_{ZZ}^{-1}H_{ZI},
 \qquad Z=I^c.
 \tag{AO13}
\end{equation}
With $Z$ empty this means $H_{II}$; with $I$ empty no inverse
is asserted. Complete the square in
$(c_I,c_Z)^*H(c_I,c_Z)$: its minimum occurs at
$c_Z=-H_{ZZ}^{-1}H_{ZI}c_I$ and gives AO13. This minimum
represents only the norm of the recovered corner quotient;
the receiver AO1 still consists of the original upper
idempotents. The exact squared inverse norm from the actual
stack to this quotient is
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{c_I\ne0}\frac{c_I^*H_{\rm quot}c_I}
                         {c_I^*K_{m,I}c_I}
 =\lambda_{\max}
     (K_{m,I}^{-1/2}H_{\rm quot}K_{m,I}^{-1/2}).\tag{AO14}
\end{equation}
This equality follows by substituting $K_{m,I}^{1/2}c_I$ in
the Rayleigh quotient. It retains both original metrics and
does not assign a small or degree-uniform value to this
finite expression.

\section{What the four actual conductor coefficients prove}
Set $j=k-8$. Let $J_-:\C^4\to E_j$ insert the four lower
corner idempotents in their same marked order. The original
conductor coefficient map has the exact corner restriction
\begin{equation}
 C J_+=J_-D_{\rm edge},\qquad
 D_{\rm edge}=\diag(d_{\mathrm M},d_{\mathrm N},d_{\mathrm T},d_{\mathrm E})
      =\diag(a_{88},a_{80},a_{08},a_{00}).\tag{AO15}
\end{equation}
This is the corner term of the original finite convolution,
with no other shift omitted: an extreme upper index can be
formed only from the corresponding extreme lower index
and the extreme degree-eight coefficient. Equivalently the
four entries equal the original point evaluations
$A_H(He_l)$, proved in FC16a of \cite{Bridge}.

Each nonzero $d_l$ certifies that $b_l\ne0$. To prove this
directly from the original observation, use the conductor
lift \cite[O3]{Observe}. Multiply the actual degree-eight
conductor biform by the lower corner monomial with index
$(j,j),(j,0),(0,j)$ or $(0,0)$. Its coefficient at the matching
upper corner is exactly $d_l$; there is no other contribution
to that extreme coefficient. The original five-term invariant
lift in O3 realizes this biform in $\Theta_k(S_k^G)$.
The original coefficient criterion O1 then gives
\begin{equation}
 I_{\rm edge}:=\{l:d_l\ne0\}\ \subseteq\ I.\tag{AO16}
\end{equation}
If $d_l=0$, this particular coefficient test is zero, but the
full column vanishes only if every original invariant
coefficient test in O1 is zero. Thus $I$ in AO2 is the exact
active set; $I_{\rm edge}$ is a proved subset, not an assumed
equality. On any locus where all four actual coefficients
are nonzero, $I$ consists of all four labels.

The arithmetic shift across AO15 is also exact:
\begin{equation}
 C A_kJ_+-A_j C J_+
       =J_-D_{\rm edge}\diag(8\rho_l).\tag{AO17}
\end{equation}
Indeed use AO1 and its lower counterpart
$A_jJ_-=J_-\diag(j\rho_l)$; the two diagonal matrices commute,
and $k-j=8$. This retains the original additive shift and
does not identify the upper and lower arithmetic actions.

\section{The labelled Fable receiver and all previously computed escapes}
Let the four original Fable collision columns be $q_l=Ke_l^{\rm label}$,
where
\[
 K=\begin{pmatrix}
 0&i&1/\sqrt2&1/\sqrt2\\
 0&-1&-1-\sqrt2i&1-\sqrt2i\\
 i/2&-3i&2\sqrt2+6i&-2\sqrt2+6i\\
 0&13&-34-19\sqrt2i&34-19\sqrt2i
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad \det K=77\sqrt2i/2.
\]
The label-preserving actual upper observation and its stack are
\begin{equation}
 q\longmapsto BK^{-1}q,\qquad
 \mathcal F_m(q)=Z_mK^{-1}q.\tag{AO18}
\end{equation}
At the four marked points these give respectively $b_l$ and
$(\lambda_l^t b_l)_{t=0}^{m-1}$. These formulas retain each
marked source identity; the different raw-coordinate insertion
$q\mapsto Bq$ does not have that property and is not substituted.
Their exact action satisfies
$\mathcal S\mathcal F_m=\mathcal F_m K D_kK^{-1}$ by AO11.

For an arbitrary original direction $v\in\C^4$, AO8 gives
the complete visibility test and its exact norm:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal F_m(v)=0\iff (K^{-1}v)_l=0\text{ for every }l\in I,
 \qquad
 \|\mathcal F_m(v)\|_{Q_m}^2
    =(K^{-1}v)_I^*K_{m,I}(K^{-1}v)_I.
 \tag{AO19}
\end{equation}
In contrast a one-step observation can cancel nonzero columns;
AO19 is proved for the complete finite stack. The exact
one-step visibility test is simply $BK^{-1}v\ne0$, with
its full vector sum retained.

The two leading directions of the established Fable escapes
have particularly useful coefficients. With the original raw
coordinate vectors $e_a=(1,0,0,0)^{\mathsf T}$ and
$e_w=(0,0,0,1)^{\mathsf T}$, direct multiplication by $K$ gives
\begin{align}
 c_a:=K^{-1}e_a
 &=\left(-4\sqrt2-\frac{180i}{77},\ -\frac{30i}{77},\
          \frac{47\sqrt2-64i}{154},\
          \frac{47\sqrt2+64i}{154}\right)^{\mathsf T},\nonumber\\
 c_w:=K^{-1}e_w
 &=\left(\frac6{77},\frac1{77},\
          \frac{-3-\sqrt2i}{154},\
          \frac{3-\sqrt2i}{154}\right)^{\mathsf T}.
 \tag{AO20}
\end{align}
Every one of these eight entries is nonzero. Therefore either
direction is visible in the actual $m$-stack as soon as even
one actual corner column is nonzero. Its exact positive norm
is given by AO19. If $I$ is empty, both and every other
corner direction are invisible to all iterates.

In particular for the original sign-partner path
\[
 \widehat q_\epsilon=(-\epsilon,2i/\epsilon,
                     6i/\epsilon^2-i/2,40i/\epsilon^3),
 \qquad\epsilon>0,
\]
the literal vector limit $\epsilon^3\widehat q_\epsilon\to40ie_w$
and the fixed linearity of AO18 prove
\begin{equation}
 \epsilon^3\|\mathcal F_m(\widehat q_\epsilon)\|_{Q_m}
 \longrightarrow40\sqrt{(c_w)_I^*K_{m,I}(c_w)_I}>0
 \quad(I\ne\varnothing).
 \tag{AO21}
\end{equation}
For all eight auxiliary quartet-limit branches of \cite[GF25]{Global},
with the original receiver held fixed, the established coordinate
limit is $\sqrt\tau\,q_{\varepsilon,\eta,\pm}(\tau)
\to\kappa_{\varepsilon,\pm}e_a$, where
$|\kappa_{\varepsilon,\pm}|=1/(2\gamma_{\rm aux})$.
Here $\tau$ is the auxiliary quartet separation, not a change
in the fixed original arithmetic $\delta$ of AO1. Their exact
observed escape constants are
\begin{equation}
 \sqrt\tau\,\|\mathcal F_m(q_{\varepsilon,\eta,\pm}(\tau))\|_{Q_m}
 \longrightarrow\frac{\sqrt{(c_a)_I^*K_{m,I}(c_a)_I}}
                         {2\gamma_{\rm aux}}>0.
 \tag{AO22}
\end{equation}
The selected-root collision trajectories of \cite[CA13]{Bridge}
have $(t-t_*)^{1/4}q(t)\to\kappa e_a$ on the retained branch;
the identical argument gives constant
$|\kappa|\sqrt{(c_a)_I^*K_{m,I}(c_a)_I}$ with the original
quarter-power rate. Every limit here follows from the actual
source coordinates and a fixed finite linear map, so no
estimate of an unproved one-step quotient action is involved.

These conclusions also have a direct conductor counterpart.
For any prescribed original positive lower-corner Gram
$H_->0$, the marked conductor image is
$J_-D_{\rm edge}K^{-1}q$. Its exact squared norm in a raw
direction $v$ is
\begin{equation}
 (K^{-1}v)^*D_{\rm edge}^*H_-D_{\rm edge}(K^{-1}v).
 \tag{AO23}
\end{equation}
For $v=e_a$ or $e_w$ this is strictly positive if and only
if at least one of the four explicit original $d_l$ is
nonzero, because AO20 has no zero entry and $H_->0$.
If all four $d_l$ vanish, the entire marked corner conductor
image is zero; the actual observation may still be nonzero,
and its definitive test remains AO2 and AO19. If even one
$d_l$ is nonzero, AO16 certifies that both Fable escape
directions survive the original finite observation stack.
On the explicit domain where all four $d_l$ are nonzero,
all four marked coordinates are recovered, with the exact
inverse AO7 and full original norm cost AO14.
