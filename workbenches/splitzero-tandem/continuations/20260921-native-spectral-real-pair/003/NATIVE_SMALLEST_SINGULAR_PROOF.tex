\section{The unique small singular direction of the native arithmetic quotient}\label{the-unique-small-singular-direction-of-the-native-arithmetic-quotient}

\subsection{The theorem}\label{the-theorem}

Use the original simple-quartet domain: fixed \texttt{h}, \texttt{0\textless{}delta\textless{}1/2}, \texttt{gamma\textgreater{}2}, odd \texttt{k=1\ mod\ 4}, and \texttt{q=(k+1)\^{}2}. All metrics below are the original least-source-norm metrics for \texttt{mu\_k=w\_h\^{}\{*k\}}, not a constructed jet metric. Write

\[
 Q_k(y)=\prod_{a,b=0}^{k}\bigl(y-(2b-k)\gamma+i(2a-k)\delta\bigr),\qquad
 E_k=\mathbb C[y]/(Q_k),\qquad M_k[P]=[yP].
\]

Let \texttt{G\_N} be its original metric at degree \texttt{N}, and order the original-metric singular values as \(s_{1,N}\ge\cdots\ge s_{q,N}>0\).

The imported analytic inputs are explicitly limited to:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  NG2--NG3: the full-polynomial Gamma comparison and multiplication bound;
\item
  NG10/NG15--NG16: the zero law of the \textbf{full} reference relation measure \texttt{Q\_k\^{}2\ sigma}, with the specified equilibrium probability \texttt{rho\_s};
\item
  H4--H5 of the supplied \emph{Original-metric heat transitions and complete mixed-family control}: the simple-quartet monic-norm/outgoing profile, including \texttt{N=2q}.
\end{itemize}

No numerical xi moment is presumed known. The finite assertions proved below do not use a spectral limit. The asymptotic theorem uses the three named analytic inputs, not a claim that a hypothetical off-critical quartet has been exhibited.

Define \[
 I(\delta,\gamma)=\log(\delta^2+\gamma^2)-3
 +\frac\delta\gamma\arctan\frac\gamma\delta
 +\frac\gamma\delta\arctan\frac\delta\gamma,                         \tag{S1}
\] \[
 J(s)=(1+s)\bigl(\log(1+s)-1\bigr)
       -\frac s2\int\log x\,\rho_s(x)\,dx,\quad J(0)=-1,             \tag{S2}
\] and retain the input's \textbf{same} profile \[
 \psi(0)=\log(4/\pi),\qquad
 \psi(s)=s\log\frac{\kappa_s}{E(\kappa_s)}
          +\log\frac{1+\sqrt{1-\kappa_s^2}}{E(\kappa_s)},
 \quad 1+s=\frac{E(\kappa_s)}{\sqrt{1-\kappa_s^2}K(\kappa_s)}.       \tag{S3}
\] For any odd cutoff sequence \texttt{N} in \texttt{{[}q-1,2q-1{]}} with \texttt{(N+1-q)/q\ -\textgreater{}\ s}, the same conclusions hold at both \texttt{N} and \texttt{N+1}: \[
 \boxed{\log s_{q,N}
 =-q\log(q/k)+q\{I(\delta,\gamma)/2-\psi(s)-J(s)\}+o_h(q).}         \tag{S4}
\] There is an explicitly given \nolinkurl{D_{k,N}=exp(O_h(k+log q))} such that \[
 \boxed{s_{q-1,N}\ge D_{k,N}^{-1}}                                  \tag{S5}
\] at \textbf{every} cutoff \texttt{q-1\textless{}=N\textless{}=2q}. Hence only one singular direction has the scale in S4. Simultaneously, \[
 \log s_{1,N}=q\psi(s)+o_h(q),                                      \tag{S6}
\] \[
 \boxed{\sum_{j=2}^{q-1}\log s_{j,N}
             =q\log q+qJ(s)+o_h(q).}                               \tag{S7}
\] In S4--S7, using the exact \texttt{s\_N} instead of its limit gives the equivalent sequential statement; at an adjacent even cutoff one can use the preceding odd cutoff's \texttt{s\_N}.

For the four original signs \texttt{+,+,-,-} at \texttt{q-1,q,2q-1,2q}, \[
 \boxed{\lim\frac1q\mathcal R\log s_{q,N}^{\,2}
 =4\{\psi(1)+J(1)-\psi(0)+1\}=:\mathfrak c_{\min}.}                 \tag{S8}
\] The reproducible interval calculation below proves \[
 -\frac{1354751}{10^6}<\mathfrak c_{\min}
 < -\frac{1353893}{10^6}.                                          \tag{S9}
\] The large and middle singular contributions have the limits \[
 \mathfrak c_{\max}=4(\psi(0)-\psi(1)),\qquad
 \mathfrak c_{\mathrm{mid}}=-4(J(1)+1),\qquad
 \mathfrak c_{\min}+\mathfrak c_{\max}+\mathfrak c_{\mathrm{mid}}=0.   \tag{S10}
\] The last cancellation is required by the unchanged algebraic determinant, not by independently choosing favorable errors.

\subsection{1. The inverse itself has one unbounded direction}\label{the-inverse-itself-has-one-unbounded-direction}

Let \texttt{J\_N} and \texttt{L\_N} be the original onto remainder map and its attained minimum section. Thus \texttt{J\_N} is a contraction from the original polynomial source, and \texttt{L\_N} is an isometry onto its full minimum space. Define the actual polynomial division operator \[
 \mathscr D P=\frac{P-P(0)}y,
 \quad u_N=J_N\left[\frac{Q_k-Q_k(0)}y\right],\quad
 \varphi_N(v)=(L_Nv)(0).
\] Both displayed quotients are polynomials. Direct multiplication by \texttt{y} modulo the \textbf{full} \texttt{Q\_k} gives \[
 \boxed{M_k^{-1}=J_N\mathscr D L_N-\frac{u_N\varphi_N}{Q_k(0)}.}     \tag{S11}
\] The functional \texttt{varphi\_N} is evaluation of the specified minimum representative; it is not a purported evaluation at zero on the quotient algebra. This distinguishes the two maps.

Here is a quantitative norm bound for the first term. For the original Gamma measure \[
 d\sigma(y)=|\Gamma(1/4+iy/2)|^2\,dy/(2\pi),
\] its orthonormal Jacobi coefficients are \nolinkurl{a_j=sqrt(j(j-1/2))}. The parity block of its \texttt{2n}-dimensional Jacobi matrix is upper bidiagonal, with diagonal \texttt{a\_(2j+1)} and superdiagonal \texttt{a\_(2j+2)}. Every squared inverse entry is at most \texttt{2}: in a fixed row the next squared entry is obtained by multiplying by \[
 a_{2j+2}^2/a_{2j+3}^2<1,
\] and a diagonal inverse entry is at most \texttt{a\_1\^{}\{-2\}=2}. The full inverse has the same operator norm as this parity block; bounding that block by its Frobenius norm gives \[
 \|J_{2n}^{-1}\|^2\le n(n+1).                                     \tag{S12}
\] Embed any polynomial of degree at most \texttt{L} in the least even Jacobi section of dimension at least \texttt{L+2}. With \[
 n_L=\lceil(L+2)/2\rceil,
\] full multiplication has no omitted outgoing coefficient, and S12 gives \[
 \|yP\|_\sigma^2\ge\frac{\|P\|_\sigma^2}{n_L(n_L+1)}.             \tag{S13}
\]

The monic Gamma recurrence gives exactly \[
 p_{2j}^{\sigma}(0)=(-1)^j4^j(1/2)_j(1/4)_j,\quad
 \omega_n^\sigma=\mathfrak m_\sigma n!(1/2)_n,
 \quad\mathfrak m_\sigma=\sqrt{2\pi}.
\] Consequently \[
 \mathfrak m_\sigma\,\mathsf K_L^{\sigma}(0,0)
 =\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor L/2\rfloor}
 \frac{(1/2)_j(1/4)_j}{j!(3/4)_j}\le L+1.                          \tag{S14}
\] Apply S13 to \texttt{mathscr\ D\ P} and S14 to \texttt{P(0)}: \[
 \|\mathscr D P\|_\sigma
 \le\sqrt{n_L(n_L+1)}(1+\sqrt{L+1})\|P\|_\sigma.                  \tag{S15}
\] Let the \textbf{original} NG2 comparison on degree at least \texttt{L} be \[
 \ell_{k,L}\|P\|_\sigma^2\le\|P\|_{\mu_k}^2\le u_k\|P\|_\sigma^2.
\] Then the usable finite constant is \[
 \boxed{D_{k,N}
 =\sqrt{u_k/\ell_{k,N}}\sqrt{n_N(n_N+1)}(1+\sqrt{N+1}).}           \tag{S16}
\] Equations S11 and S16 show that all but the largest singular value of \texttt{M\_k\^{}\{-1\}} are at most \texttt{D\_\{k,N\}}. This proves S5. Its logarithm is \texttt{O\_h(k+log\ q)} by NG2. The source mass was retained in S14 and canceled only in the displayed evaluation inequality.

\subsection{2. Parity gives a determinant identity at zero}\label{parity-gives-a-determinant-identity-at-zero}

Write \texttt{q=2n}. The original source parity is an isometry, with two quotient subspaces of dimension \texttt{n}. In the ordered even/odd remainder coordinates, \[
 G_N=\operatorname{diag}(G_{e,N},G_{o,N}),\qquad
 M_k=\begin{pmatrix}0&X\\I&0\end{pmatrix},                         \tag{S17}
\] where \texttt{X} is multiplication by \texttt{x=y\^{}2} modulo \texttt{Q\_k(sqrt\ x)}.

The original selfadjoint compression \texttt{C\_N} is odd under parity and differs from \texttt{M\_k} by rank one. Its rank is even. Since \texttt{M\_k} is invertible, its rank is at least \texttt{q-1}; therefore \[
 \boxed{C_N\text{ is invertible at every one of these cutoffs.}}   \tag{S18}
\] This does not give it a uniform spectral gap at even cutoffs.

If \texttt{N} is odd, the top omitted monomial is even. The lower-left block is untouched. Selfadjointness in S17 therefore gives \[
 C_N=\begin{pmatrix}0&G_{e,N}^{-1}G_{o,N}\\I&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad |\det C_N|=\frac{\det G_{o,N}}{\det G_{e,N}}.               \tag{S19}
\] Let \texttt{p\_j\^{}mu} be the source monic polynomials and \texttt{U\_j} the monic polynomials for the \textbf{complete relation measure} \texttt{Q\_k\^{}2\ mu\_k}. Put \texttt{d=N+1-q}, an even integer. Schur complementing the entire relation spaces in each parity gives \[
 \boxed{|\det C_N|=\frac{|p_{N+1}^{\mu_k}(0)|}{|U_d(0)|}.}         \tag{S20}
\] To see every factor, push the even measure forward by \texttt{x=y\^{}2}. The ratio of the odd and even source Grams is \(\det H[x\lambda]/\det H[\lambda]=|p_{(N+1)/2}^{\lambda}(0)|\). The same ratio for the relation Grams is \(|p_{d/2}^{Q^2\lambda}(0)|\). The monic changes of polynomial basis have determinant one; source and relation masses are not changed. Their quotient is S20.

The smallest modulus of a \texttt{C\_N} eigenvalue at an odd cutoff has a finite lower bound. Indeed the two parity metrics minimize in the same polynomial fibres, in measures \texttt{lambda} and \texttt{x\ lambda}. Apply S13 and the original comparison on the entire lifted polynomials to obtain \[
 \|C_N^{-1}\|_{G_N}
 \le e^{\Lambda_k/2}\sqrt{n_* (n_*+1)},                            \tag{S21}
\] where \nolinkurl{Lambda_k=log(u_k/ell_(k,3q+4))} and \nolinkurl{n_*=ceil((3q+6)/2)} is a safe degree guard. Any sharper original multiplication bound may also be used. The logarithm of S21 is \texttt{O\_h(k+log\ q)}.

\subsection{3. The logarithmic reference limit is justified at zero}\label{the-logarithmic-reference-limit-is-justified-at-zero}

Weak convergence alone cannot be applied to a singular logarithm. Two finite estimates resolve this issue here.

\subsubsection{3.1 A hard gap for the reference relation measure}\label{a-hard-gap-for-the-reference-relation-measure}

Suppose \texttt{q\textgreater{}=16} and every root of the monic \texttt{Q\_k} has modulus at most \texttt{q/512}. For every polynomial \texttt{P} of degree at most \texttt{q}, \[
 \boxed{\int y^2|Q_kP|^2d\sigma
 \ge\frac{q^2}{524288}\int|Q_kP|^2d\sigma.}                        \tag{S22}
\] Here is a proof with numerical constants. Put \texttt{c=1/512} and \nolinkurl{c_sigma=Gamma(1/4)^2/(2pi)}. Euler's product gives \[
 \frac{c_\sigma e^{-\pi|y|/2}}{1+4y^2}\le\sigma(y)\le c_\sigma.   \tag{S23}
\] For the lower bound, the decreasing product-log summand is bounded by its first value plus its integral, and \(\int_0^\infty\log(1+a^2/t^2)dt=\pi a\).

On \texttt{\textbar{}y\textbar{}\textless{}=cq}, \nolinkurl{|Q_k(y)|<=(2cq)^q}; on \texttt{{[}q,2q{]}}, \nolinkurl{|Q_k(y)|>=(q/2)^q}. On the scaled interval \texttt{{[}1,2{]}}, the Legendre reproducing kernel bounds every degree-\texttt{q} polynomial at \texttt{\textbar{}t\textbar{}\textless{}=c} by \[
 |P(qt)|^2\le(q+1)^2 100^q\int_1^2|P(qu)|^2du.                   \tag{S24}
\] This follows from the orthogonal polynomials \nolinkurl{sqrt(2j+1)P_j(2t-3)} and the elementary bound \texttt{\textbar{}P\_j(x)\textbar{}\textless{}=10\^{}j} for \texttt{\textbar{}x\textbar{}\textless{}=5}, obtained from the Legendre integral representation.

Divide the mass on \texttt{\textbar{}y\textbar{}\textless{}=cq} by the mass on \texttt{{[}q,2q{]}}. S23--S24 bound that ratio by \[
 2c(1+16q^2)(q+1)^2(1600e^\pi c^2)^q
 <\frac{(1+16q^2)(q+1)^2}{256\,2^q}\le1.                         \tag{S25}
\] The exponential bound uses only \texttt{pi\textless{}4}, \texttt{e\textless{}3}. The last inequality holds at \texttt{q=16} and its successive ratio is below one thereafter. At least half the mass is consequently outside \texttt{\textbar{}y\textbar{}\textless{}=cq}, proving S22.

All squared nodes of every relevant reference relation compression therefore lie in \nolinkurl{[1/524288,B_sigma]} after division by \texttt{q\^{}2}. The root-radius condition is eventually satisfied because \texttt{k/q\ -\textgreater{}\ 0}; its displayed constant is not suppressed. NG10 now legitimately applies to \texttt{log\ x} on a fixed compact interval separated from zero.

\subsubsection{3.2 Moving the negative characteristic-value comparison to zero}\label{moving-the-negative-characteristic-value-comparison-to-zero}

The NG6 characteristic-value argument is valid for \textbf{every} \texttt{a\textgreater{}0}, not only the source's convenient statement \texttt{a\textgreater{}=1}. For a fixed positive \texttt{a}, all negative powers of \texttt{x+a} remain bounded at zero. Its proof gives \[
 |\log|p_n^\lambda(-a)|-\log|p_n^\eta(-a)||
 \le\tfrac12\Lambda+\log(1+B_\lambda/a)
                   +\tfrac52\log(1+B_\eta/a).                    \tag{S26}
\] For clarity, the determinant slope \texttt{f(1)-f(0)} is \texttt{log\textbar{}p\_n(-a)\textbar{}}. Convexity traps it between half the \texttt{(-2,0)} and \texttt{(0,2)} increments. The two polynomial spaces \texttt{P\_\textless{}n} and \texttt{(x+a)P\_\textless{}n} share a codimension-one intersection. Cancel its full Gram; the two quotient minima change by at most \texttt{Lambda}. The rational spaces \texttt{P\_\textless{}n} and \texttt{P\_\textless{}n/(x+a)} have the same codimension-one cancellation, with comparison cost \(\Lambda+2\log((a+B_\lambda)(a+B_\eta)/a^2)\). Successive Christoffel transforms interlace, bounding the three reference slope differences by \texttt{log(1+B\_eta/a)}. Combining the two directions yields S26. This establishes its explicit \texttt{a\textgreater{}0} domain without ever evaluating a rational Gram at \texttt{a=0}.

For source and relation measures alike, S13 on the full lifted polynomials supplies the common squared-node floor \[
 \delta_k=\frac{e^{-\Lambda_k}}{q^2 n_*(n_*+1)}.
\] Choose \texttt{a=delta\_k/(n+1)}. Since \[
 0\le\log|p_n(-a)|-\log|p_n(0)|
 =\sum_j\log(1+a/x_j)\le n a/\delta_k<1,
\] S26 gives \[
 \boxed{|\log|p_n^\lambda(0)|-\log|p_n^\eta(0)||
 \le2+\tfrac12\Lambda_k+\log(1+B_\lambda/a)
                         +\tfrac52\log(1+B_\eta/a).}             \tag{S27}
\] The right side is \texttt{O\_h(k+log\ q)}, because the original all-degree multiplication bounds make \nolinkurl{B_lambda,B_eta=O_h(1)} on these scaled spaces and \nolinkurl{log(1/a)=Lambda_k+O(log q)}.

For source monics, the exact Gamma value and Stirling's formula give \[
 \log|p_{N+1}^{\mu_k}(0)|
 =(N+1)\log(N+1)-(N+1)+O_h(k+\log q).                             \tag{S28}
\] For relation monics with \texttt{d/q\ -\textgreater{}\ s\textgreater{}0}, S22, NG10 and S27 give \[
 \log|U_d(0)|=d\log q+\frac{sq}{2}\int\log x\rho_s(x)dx+o_h(q).    \tag{S29}
\] At \texttt{s=0} the log of the scaled relation characteristic value is \nolinkurl{O(d)+O_h(k+log q)=o(q)} by the same fixed compact node bounds. Subtracting S29 from S28 in S20 proves \[
 \boxed{\log|\det C_N|=q\log q+qJ(s)+o_h(q)}                      \tag{S30}
\] for odd cutoffs. At \texttt{N=q-1} its error is \texttt{O\_h(k+log\ q)}; no rate for the reference zero-law convergence at \texttt{s\textgreater{}0} is invented.

\subsection{4. Deriving the unique smallest singular value}\label{deriving-the-unique-smallest-singular-value}

For odd \texttt{N}, put \texttt{C=C\_N}, \texttt{r=r\_N}, \texttt{v=ell\_N\^{}dagger}. Define \[
 z=1+\ell_NC^{-1}r=\frac{\det M_k}{\det C},\quad
 a=C^{-1}r,\quad b=C^{-1}v.
\] The complete Sherman--Morrison identity is \[
 M_k^{-1}=C^{-1}-\frac{ab^\dagger}{z}.                            \tag{S31}
\] Its second term is square zero: \texttt{ell\_N\ C\^{}(-2)r=0} by opposite parity. Its norm is \[
 \epsilon_N^-=
 \epsilon_N\frac{|\det C|}{|\det M_k|}
 \frac{\|C^{-1}r\|}{\|r\|}\frac{\|C^{-1}v\|}{\|v\|}.             \tag{S32}
\] If \texttt{b\_C\textless{}=s\_min(C)} and \texttt{B\_C\textgreater{}=\textbar{}\textbar{}C\textbar{}\textbar{}}, then the actual finite bracket is \[
 \left(\frac{\epsilon_N|\det C|}{B_C^2|\det M_k|}-b_C^{-1}\right)_+
 \le\|M_k^{-1}\|
 \le b_C^{-1}+\frac{\epsilon_N|\det C|}{b_C^2|\det M_k|}.           \tag{S33}
\] All norms in S31--S33 use \texttt{G\_N}. Equations S21 and NG3 make the logs of the two resolvent-vector ratios only \texttt{O\_h(k+log\ q)}.

The unchanged root product is \[
 \log|\det M_k|=q\log k+\frac q2 I(\delta,\gamma)+o_h(q).           \tag{S34}
\] To evaluate \texttt{I}, integrate \nolinkurl{log(delta^2 u^2+gamma^2 v^2)} over \texttt{{[}0,1{]}\^{}2} and use the divergence of \texttt{(u,v)} times that function. The boundary integrals give S1. The midpoint Riemann sum converges despite the integrable logarithmic origin. An explicit bound, with \texttt{n=(k+1)/2}, is \[
 \left|I_k-I-2\log(1+1/k)\right|
 \le\frac{c_0+\sqrt2(\gamma/\delta)^2[2(n-1)+H_{n-1}]}{n^2},       \tag{S35}
\] where \nolinkurl{c_0=|log((delta^2+gamma^2)/4)-I|}. The origin cell is integrated exactly by homogeneity. Group the remaining cells by their largest index and use \nolinkurl{||grad log(delta^2u^2+gamma^2v^2)||<=2(gamma/delta)^2/sqrt(u^2+v^2)}.

Insert S30, S34 and the supplied H5, \nolinkurl{log epsilon_N=q psi(s)+O_h(k log^2(q+2))}, into S32. The correction dominates \texttt{C\^{}\{-1\}} by a factor tending to infinity on the scale \texttt{q\ log(q/k)}. Taking the log of S31 proves S4 at odd cutoffs. At \texttt{N=q-1}, S35 and the explicit source value improve the remainder to \nolinkurl{O_h(k log^2(q+2))}.

\subsubsection{The adjacent even cutoff is not omitted}\label{the-adjacent-even-cutoff-is-not-omitted}

Let \nolinkurl{omega=omega_(N+1)} and \[
 d=\frac{\omega}{\omega+\|r\|_{G_N}^2}.

\subsection{5. Evaluating the remaining endpoint constant}\label{evaluating-the-remaining-endpoint-constant}

Let \texttt{u=u\_s}, \texttt{v=v\_s} be the original equilibrium endpoints, \texttt{0\textless{}u\textless{}v}, with \[
 uK(\kappa_s)=2,\quad vE(\kappa_s)=2(1+s),\quad

 m=(u^2+v^2)/2,\quad d=(v^2-u^2)/2,\quad c=uv,\quad

\] \[
 r_t=\frac{d}{m+t^2+\sqrt{(u^2+t^2)(v^2+t^2)}}.
\] Then the log moment in S2 has the explicit convergent one-dimensional expression \[
 \boxed{\int\log x\rho_s(x)dx
 =\log A_+-\frac2s\log(1-r^2)
       +\frac1s\int_0^\infty\log(1-r r_t)dt.}                     \tag{S39}
\] Here the integral is absolutely convergent and negative. No original Gamma normalization or density factor is removed.

For a derivation, use \texttt{x=m+d\ cos\ theta}. The Fourier expansion of \texttt{log\ x} has constant term \texttt{log\ A\_+} and cosine coefficients \nolinkurl{2(-1)^(j+1)r^j/j}.  \frac1\pi\int_{u^2}^{v^2}

\] Insert this into the original equilibrium density representation GM7, with \texttt{alpha=2/s}, \texttt{beta=pi/s}, and use the retained normalization \(\alpha/c=(\beta/(2\pi))\int_0^\infty dw/(\sqrt w R_w)\). The constant terms cancel exactly; then \texttt{w=t\^{}2} gives S39. The cancellation involves convergent integrals. It is not a subtraction of separately divergent quantities.

\subsection{6. An explicit late-time heat return}\label{an-explicit-late-time-heat-return}

For fixed real \texttt{b}, set \[
 \tau_k(b)=\exp\{2q[\log(q/k)+b]\},\qquad
 B(s)=\psi(s)+J(s)-I(\delta,\gamma)/2.
\] S4 and S5 imply \[
 \boxed{\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\tau_k(b)M_k^{\dagger_{G_N}}M_k}
 \longrightarrow \mathbf1_{\{b<B(s)\}},\quad b\ne B(s).}           \tag{S42}
\] There is no division by \texttt{q}: all middle and large directions are extinguished, and the unique smallest direction either survives or is extinguished. Specifically, their total contribution is at most \((q-1)\exp[-\tau_k(b)/D_{k,N}^2]\to0\).

The endpoint enclosures prove \[
