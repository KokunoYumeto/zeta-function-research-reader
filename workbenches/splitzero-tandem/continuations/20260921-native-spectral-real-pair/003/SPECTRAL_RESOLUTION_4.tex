\section{IV. The ES trace-rigidity theorem as two exact heat jets, with the full mixed return}\label{iv.-the-es-trace-rigidity-theorem-as-two-exact-heat-jets-with-the-full-mixed-return}

\subsection{IV.1 What the newer ES source has proved}\label{iv.1-what-the-newer-es-source-has-proved}

The ES repository at commit \nolinkurl{5787b359a73edd62130caf76f573a40d607463c1} contains \emph{Global trace integrality and the original ES source}, TR1--TR11. The following arithmetic theorem is \textbf{an inherited result}, not a new occupancy argument:

For \(x,y,z\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\) satisfying \(1/x+1/y+1/z=4/p\), the two power sums \[
 s_1=x+y+z,\qquad s_2=x^2+y^2+z^2
\] are integers if and only if all three denominators are integers. Rationality is essential; the same source supplies algebraic irrational counterfamilies to its omission.

Here is the length-three argument, recording the exact assumptions used by our receiver. Let \(e_1=s_1\), \(e_2=(s_1^2-s_2)/2\). At a prime where a root has negative valuation \(-a\), if precisely one root has minimal valuation then its term uniquely minimizes \(e_1\), contradicting integrality. If precisely two do, their product uniquely minimizes \(e_2\) with valuation \(-2a\), contradicting \(v_\ell(e_2)\ge-v_\ell(2)\ge-1\). Thus all three have the same reduced denominator at every denominator prime, giving a common denominator \(d\). It is odd, because three reduced odd numerators over an even denominator would have nonintegral first sum. The reciprocal identity \(e_2/e_3=4/p\), together with \(2e_2\in\mathbb Z\), gives \(d^3\mid2m\), where \(m\) is the reduced reciprocal numerator and divides four. An odd positive integer whose cube divides eight is one. Conversely, integer denominators give integer power sums. This is the TR1--TR4 proof specialized to three roots.

\subsection{IV.2 Two centres recover both arithmetic traces}\label{iv.2-two-centres-recover-both-arithmetic-traces}

Let \(T\) be multiplication by the literal root coordinate on the four-root algebra with roots \((p,x,y,z)\), counted with their literal algebraic multiplicities. No Hermitian adjoint occurs in this definition. Put \(\nu=1\) for the four-dimensional root algebra and \(\nu=2\) for its eight-dimensional signed completion. The latter is free of rank two over the root algebra, so multiplication traces are doubled even at a repeated-root fibre.

For a fixed real centre \(c\ne0\), define the genuine \textbf{holomorphic} heat trace \[
 H_c(t)=\operatorname{Tr}e^{-t(T-cI)^2}.
 \tag{E1}
\] Its trace depends only on the roots with algebraic multiplicity, not on whether \(T\) has Jordan blocks. Expanding each triangular primary block gives \[
 H_c(0)=4\nu,\qquad
 H_c'(0)=-\nu\left[(p-c)^2+(x-c)^2+(y-c)^2+(z-c)^2\right].
\] It follows exactly that \[
 \boxed{s_2=-H_0'(0)/\nu-p^2,\qquad
 s_1=\frac{H_c'(0)-H_0'(0)+4\nu c^2}{2\nu c}-p.}
 \tag{E2}
\] The reciprocal equation itself is the separate multiplication trace \[
 \operatorname{Tr}T^{-1}=5\nu/p.
 \tag{E3}
\] Thus on the specified positive \textbf{rational-root} source, the two quantities in E2 are integers exactly when the denominators are integral. The newer ES theorem is the arithmetic implication; E1--E2 are the exact heat receiver developed here. We have not proved that an integer pair of heat jets occurs at every prescribed prime.

If \(L\) is an original invertible conductor onto its receiving image, \(T'=LTL^{-1}\) satisfies \(H_c^{T'}=H_c^T\) by the entire power series and trace cyclicity. This is a holomorphic-action transport, not an identification of the positive singular-value heat trace. The latter would require the transported metric \(L^{-*}GL^{-1}\) or the exact pullback correction when the target metric is separately prescribed.

\subsection{IV.3 Finite samples give rigorous jet intervals}\label{iv.3-finite-samples-give-rigorous-jet-intervals}

Suppose the literal roots are real and their centred magnitudes satisfy \(|t_j-c|\le R_c\), including the distinguished root \(p\). For \(t>0\), set \[
 \widehat h_c(t)=\frac{H_c(t)-4\nu}{t}.
\] For \(v\ge0\), the Taylor integral remainder gives \(0\le e^{-v}-1+v\le v^2/2\). Applying this to the four roots proves \[
 \boxed{0\le\widehat h_c(t)-H_c'(0)\le2\nu tR_c^4.}
 \tag{E4}
\] If the computed \(H_c(t)\) has a certified absolute error at most \(\epsilon_c\), the jet radius is at most \(2\nu tR_c^4+\epsilon_c/t\). The error in the reconstructed first sum is consequently at most \[
 \boxed{\frac{t(R_c^4+R_0^4)}{|c|}
       +\frac{\epsilon_c+\epsilon_0}{2\nu|c|t},}
 \tag{E5}
\] and the error in the reconstructed second sum is at most \[
 \boxed{2tR_0^4+\frac{\epsilon_0}{\nu t}.}
 \tag{E6}
\] These are outward estimates at finite \(t\), not a numerical differentiation assertion. The root bound justifies the holomorphic trace estimate because its trace is the exact root sum; it is not substituted for a native operator norm in a nonnormal norm inequality.

If a known common reduced denominator \(d\) of \(x,y,z\) is at most \(D\), then a noninteger \(s_1\) has distance at least \(1/D\) from every integer, and a noninteger \(s_2\) has distance at least \(1/D^2\). Therefore an interval for \(s_1\) contained strictly within \((n-1/D,n+1/D)\) and an interval for \(s_2\) contained strictly within \((m-1/D^2,m+1/D^2)\) certify exact integrality. Their centres need not themselves equal integers. Without a denominator bound, arbitrarily close rational nonintegers prevent this inference.

For example, \(p=13\), \((x,y,z)=(4,156/5,39)\) is a positive rational ES triple obtained from the raw gate below, but its literal first trace is \(436/5\), not an integer. Taking smaller heat time alone cannot turn it into an integral witness.

\subsection{IV.4 The complete mixed minimum retains the missing second-trace term}\label{iv.4-the-complete-mixed-minimum-retains-the-missing-second-trace-term}

Use the supplied original-metric observation frame. Write the fixed operator as \[
 T=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix},
\] and the minimum-section change of coordinates as \[
 S_t=\begin{pmatrix}I&X_t\\0&I\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 X_t=tV(I+tW)^{-1}.
\] The input's M16 supplies the exact similarity \[
 S_t^{-1}TS_t=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 A_K&B_R\\c&A_B
 \end{pmatrix},
\] \[
 A_K=a-X_tc,\quad A_B=d+cX_t,
 \quad B_R=b+aX_t-X_td-X_tcX_t.
 \tag{E7}
\] Thus \[
 \boxed{\operatorname{Tr}T=\operatorname{Tr}A_K+\operatorname{Tr}A_B,}
\] \[
 \boxed{\operatorname{Tr}T^2
 =\operatorname{Tr}A_K^2+\operatorname{Tr}A_B^2
       +2\operatorname{Tr}(B_Rc).}
 \tag{E8}
\] Both follow by block multiplication and cyclicity of rectangular traces. The reverse block is quadratic in \(X_t\) and cannot be omitted. The two heat jets E2 can therefore be reconstructed from the full subquotient data E8; the observed block alone is not the original root trace.

\textbf{An actual ES spectrum in an explicit nontrivial frame.} Take \((p;x,y,z)=(13;4,18,468)\) and conjugate the diagonal root matrix by the rational orthogonal rotation with first block \[
 O=\begin{pmatrix}3/5&-4/5\\4/5&3/5\end{pmatrix}.
\] The corresponding first block of \(T\) is \[
 \begin{pmatrix}181/25&108/25\\108/25&244/25\end{pmatrix},
\] with remaining diagonal entries \(18,468\). Let the first coordinate be the kernel and take a covariance supported by the all-ones positive block on the first two coordinates. Then \(X_t\) has first entry \(t/(1+t)\) and its other entries zero. At every \(t\ge0\), E8 returns the exact full trace \(503\) and full squared trace \(219533\). At \(t=0\), omitting the reverse coupling loses precisely \(2(108/25)^2\) from the squared trace. This example changes coordinates and introduces a declared covariance; it is not presented as a measured native period or a computed xi packet.

There is an exact finite expression beyond the jets. On the open spectral domain where the two inverses exist, \[
 \det(zI-T)=\det(zI-A_K)\det(zI-A_B)
 \det\bigl(I-(zI-A_B)^{-1}c(zI-A_K)^{-1}B_R\bigr).
 \tag{E9}
\] It follows from the complete block Schur complement. Clearing denominators gives a polynomial identity, so apparent factor cancellations are retained by continuation. Taking the logarithmic derivative and applying the finite Cauchy formula transports any entire test, including E1, through these same blocks with the complete correction. The formula does not claim that only \(\dim K\) eigenvalues move.

\subsection{IV.5 The raw gate has an exact finite spacing certificate}\label{iv.5-the-raw-gate-has-an-exact-finite-spacing-certificate}

The ES source's TR7--TR11 fixes \(p\equiv1\pmod4\), \(p/4<a<p/2\), \(R=4a-p\), and an integer \(U>0\) with \(R\mid a+U\). The original rational return is \[
 x=a,\quad y=\frac{pa(U+a)}{RU},\quad z=\frac{p(a+U)}R.
\] The literal trace satisfies \[
 \operatorname{den}(p+x+y+z)=\frac{U}{\gcd(U,pa^2)}.
 \tag{E10}
\] This is the inherited exact divisor test: integral trace is equivalent to \(U\mid pa^2\), and its defining-prime bit selects the original M or E channel. Our finite heat/trace interval can certify it: if a rigorous interval centred at \(s\) with radius \(r\) satisfies \(|s-n|+r<1/U\) for an integer \(n\), the exact trace must be integral. This uses the known denominator spacing, not an assumption that approximate integrality implies integrality.

For \(p=13,a=4,R=3\), \(U=5\) gives the nonintegral trace \(436/5\), whereas \(U=104\) returns the actual witness \((4,18,468)\) and trace \(503\). The formulas recover their different statuses without changing the raw gate. They provide an exact receiver for the newer trace-rigidity result; finding an occupied raw gate at every hard prime remains a separate arithmetic problem.
