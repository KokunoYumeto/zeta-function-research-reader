This note proves the finite completion proposed in EA1--28 of
\cite{ExceptionalAtlas}, retaining the original reciprocal factor
$t=1/a$, both infinity signs and all derivative constants.
The original polynomial and marked states remain attributed
to \cite{ES488}; the ordinary-fibre calculations and existing
nonfinite factor completion are recorded in \cite{Fable029}.
The finite completion below is a different, explicitly related
object. All schemes and algebras in this note are over $\C$.

\section{The original map and two completed root charts}
The original source coordinates are $(a,y,z,w)$. Define
\begin{align}
b&=i+ay,&c&=-i+2ay+a^2z,\nonumber\\
d&=-iy-a(iz+2y^2)-a^2yz,&e&=2z-7iy^2+aw,\nonumber\\
f&=iw+3iy^3-4yz+a(6iy^2z+wy+4y^4)+2a^2y^3z,\nonumber\\
P&=(ac,ae+bd,af+be,bf)=(A,B,C,D).
\tag{FS1}
\end{align}
Direct multiplication gives $ad+bc=1$ and
$a^3f-a^2be+ab^2d-b^3c=1$. Thus
$(aU+bV)(cU^3+dU^2V+eUV^2+fV^3)=H_u(U,V)$, where
\[
H_u(U,V)=AU^4+U^3V+BU^2V^2+CUV^3+DV^4,\quad
h_u(r)=Ar^4+r^3+Br^2+Cr+D,\quad
k_u(v)=A+v+Bv^2+Cv^3+Dv^4 .
\]
The coefficient of $U^3V$ remains one throughout.
For $a\ne0$, division by the linear factor gives
$r=-b/a=-y-i/a$ and $a^2h_u'(r)=1$.
Keeping the reciprocal factor as the actual new coordinate,
define
\begin{equation}
X_f=\Spec\frac{\C[A,B,C,D,r,t]}{(h_u(r),\,t^2-h_u'(r))},
\qquad
X_\infty=\Spec\frac{\C[A,B,C,D,v,\theta]}
 {(k_u(v),\,\theta^2+k_u'(v))}.
\tag{FS2}
\end{equation}
On $r\ne0$ and $v\ne0$ the exact gluing maps and inverses are
\begin{equation}
v=r^{-1},\quad\theta=-t/r,\qquad
r=v^{-1},\quad t=-\theta/v .
\tag{FS3}
\end{equation}
At a root, differentiating $k(v)=v^4h(1/v)$ gives
$k'(v)=-v^2h'(1/v)$. Hence FS3 preserves both equations
and signs, and direct substitution proves the inverse
compositions. Glue these charts to form $X$ with projection
$\pi:X\to\mathbb A^4_u$. No coordinate $t$ or $\theta$ is
inverted in defining the completed charts.

The equations on the finite chart solve as
\begin{equation}
C=t^2-4Ar^3-3r^2-2Br,\qquad
D=3Ar^4+2r^3+Br^2-rt^2.
\tag{FS4}
\end{equation}
Substitution in both directions proves
$X_f=\mathbb A^4_{t,r,A,B}$, including $A=0$ and $t=0$.
At $v=0$, the other chart has exactly
\begin{equation}
A=0,\quad\theta=\pm i,\qquad
D_\pm=\{v=0,\ \theta=\mp i\}\simeq\mathbb A^3_{B,C,D}.
\tag{FS5}
\end{equation}
Thus $D_+$ is the included original sign $\theta=-i$ and
$D_-$ is the missing original sign $\theta=i$.
Eliminating $A$ on $X_\infty$ leaves the equation
$1+\theta^2+2Bv+3Cv^2+4Dv^3=0$.
At $v=0$ its derivative in $\theta$ is $2\theta\ne0$.
Therefore $X$ is smooth there, and it is smooth elsewhere
by FS3--4.

\section{Five explicit finite free descriptions over the target}
For $p\in\{0,1,-1,2\}$ write $c_p=H_u(p,1)=h_u(p)$.
The following identity is literal coefficient arithmetic:
\begin{equation}
1=\tfrac12h_u(0)-\tfrac12h_u(1)-\tfrac16h_u(-1)
                       +\tfrac16h_u(2)-2A .
\tag{FS6}
\end{equation}
It is verified by comparing each coefficient of $A,B,C,D$
and the fixed cubic term. Consequently the five principal
opens $D(A),D(c_0),D(c_1),D(c_{-1}),D(c_2)$ cover the
entire characteristic-zero target.

On $D(c_p)$ use the determinant-one substitution
$(U,V)=(pU'-V',U')$ and put
$h_p(R)=H_u(pR-1,R)$. Its leading coefficient is precisely
the unit $c_p$. The complete root/sign transitions are
\begin{align}
R&=(p-r)^{-1},&T&=t/(p-r)
                    &&\text{on the finite overlap},\nonumber\\
R&=-v/(1-pv),&T&=\theta/(1-pv)
                    &&\text{on the infinity overlap},\nonumber\\
r&=p-R^{-1},&t&=T/R&& (R\ne0),\nonumber\\
v&=R/(pR-1),&\theta&=T/(1-pR)&& (pR-1\ne0).
\tag{FS7}
\end{align}
Every denominator is an explicitly stated unit on the relevant
overlap. On $D(c_p)$ the marked root cannot equal $p$;
thus $p-r$ is a unit on its finite root scheme. More
algebraically $h_u(p)-h_u(r)$ is divisible by $p-r$,
and $h_u(r)=0$, so the unit $c_p$ proves this assertion.
Likewise the infinity denominator $1-pv$ cannot vanish
there. The inverse opens cover the new chart because $R$
and $pR-1$ generate the unit ideal.

The derivative identity is
$h_p'(R)=R^2h_u'(p-R^{-1})$ at a root, obtained by
differentiating $h_p(R)=R^4h_u(p-R^{-1})$.
Thus $T^2=h_p'(R)$ on both overlaps; at $v=0$, $R=0$
and $T=\theta$, so the infinity sign is preserved exactly.
FS7 and its inverse formulas give an isomorphism of the
whole preimage over $D(c_p)$ with
\begin{equation}
\Spec\,\mathcal O_{D(c_p)}[R,T]/
                 (h_p(R),\,T^2-h_p'(R)).
\tag{FS8}
\end{equation}
The same description on $D(A)$ is the original finite
chart FS2 with the additional unit $A$; all marked roots
there are finite. In either case, writing $\lambda$ for
the retained leading coefficient, division by the unit
$\lambda$ makes only the root relation monic.
The sign relation is still exactly $T^2=h_p'(R)$,
not the derivative of the divided polynomial.
The successive monic relations give the free module basis
\begin{equation}
1,R,R^2,R^3,\ T,TR,TR^2,TR^3 .
\tag{FS9}
\end{equation}
Indeed the root quotient is free on the first four monomials;
the monic quadratic relation in $T$ makes its extension
free on $1,T$. This proves on all five opens, without
any restriction on their discriminants, that
\begin{equation}
\pi:X\longrightarrow\mathbb A^4_u
\text{ is finite locally free of rank eight.}
\tag{FS10}
\end{equation}

The finite chart is irreducible. It is dense in $X$:
on the infinity chart the remaining equation is monic
in $\theta$, so its coordinate algebra is free over
$\C[v,B,C,D]$ and multiplication by $v$ is injective.
Thus no irreducible component is supported on $v=0$;
every such point is also smooth by FS5.
It follows that $X$ is irreducible and smooth, hence normal.
Since FS10 has rank eight and is dominant, its function
field is a degree-eight extension of $\C(A,B,C,D)$.
Finally, a finite normal integral scheme over an affine
base is the integral closure of that base in its function
field: any element integral over the base is also integral
over its finite coordinate ring, so normality includes it.
This proves that $X$ is the finite normalization in the
actual original degree-eight function-field extension.

\section{The original open immersion, including its infinity sheet}
The full resultant-one factor space is
$\mathcal F=\{ad+bc=1,\ a^3f-a^2be+ab^2d-b^3c=1\}$.
Its exact identification with the unramified part of $X$
is given on the finite chart by
\begin{align}
a&=t^{-1},&b&=-r/t,&c&=At,&d&=(1+Ar)t,\nonumber\\
e&=(B+r+Ar^2)t,&f&=(C+Br+r^2+Ar^3)t&& (t\ne0),
\tag{FS11}
\end{align}
and on the infinity chart by
\begin{align}
a&=-v/\theta,&b&=\theta^{-1},&
c&=\theta(1+Bv+Cv^2+Dv^3),\nonumber\\
d&=\theta(B+Cv+Dv^2),&
e&=\theta(C+Dv),&f&=\theta D
\qquad(\theta\ne0).
\tag{FS12}
\end{align}
Multiplication of the factors gives $H_u$ in either formula.
The resultant is $t^{-2}h'_u(r)=1$ in FS11 and
$-\theta^{-2}k'_u(v)=1$ in FS12.
Their inverse is $(r,t)=(-b/a,1/a)$ when $a\ne0$ and
$(v,\theta)=(-a/b,1/b)$ when $b\ne0$.
Since $ad+bc=1$, these inverse charts cover $\mathcal F$.
Direct substitution and FS3 prove every inverse composition.
The excluded locus is the reduced divisor
$R_0=\{t=0\}\simeq\mathbb A^3_{r,A,B}$; on the infinity
chart it is $\theta=0$, which never meets $v=0$.
Thus $\mathcal F\simeq X\setminus R_0$ exactly.

The original source maps to $X$ via FS1 and these inverse
factor charts. For $a\ne0$ its complete inverse is
\begin{equation}
q(t,r,A,B)=\left(t^{-1},-r-it,\,
At^3+2rt+3it^2,\,
7ir^2t+(B-17r+Ar^2)t^2-13it^3-2At^4\right).
\tag{FS13}
\end{equation}
All terms come from the successive original factor equations;
substitution verifies $Pq=\pi$.
At $a=0$ its factors have $b=i$, hence $\theta=-i$.
It has exactly the original state
$q_\infty=(0,B,i(7B^2-C)/2,11B^3-2BC-D)$ over
every $A=0$ target.

For an explicit inverse on an open neighborhood of that
entire infinity sheet, use $b(b+i)\ne0$ in $\mathcal F$
and set
\begin{align}
Y&=\frac{a^2f-abe+2b^2d}{b+i},\nonumber\\
Z&=\frac{af-be+7Y^2-10iaY^3-4a^2Y^4}{2b^3},\nonumber\\
W&=\frac{a^2eY^3+7ia^2Y^5+3iaeY^2-17aY^4
                       -2eY-f-11iY^3}{b^3}.
\tag{FS14}
\end{align}
No factor $a$ is divided out in FS14. The original two
factor equations give
$b^2+1=a(a^2f-abe+2b^2d)$, so $b-i=aY$.
Substitution of FS1 gives the three polynomial identities
\[
a^2f-abe+2b^2d=(b+i)y,\qquad
af-be+7y^2-10iay^3-4a^2y^4=2b^3z,
\]
\[
a^2ey^3+7ia^2y^5+3iaey^2-17ay^4-2ey-f-11iy^3=b^3w.
\]
These identities follow by expanding the displayed factor
polynomials and prove that FS14 is the inverse on the
dense subopen $a\ne0$. Both it and FS1 are regular on
the whole stated open set, which is reduced as an open
subset of the smooth irreducible $X$. Equality of the
compositions there follows from equality on that dense
open. This proves the inverse also at $a=0$.
The opens $a\ne0$ and $b(b+i)\ne0$ cover precisely the
original image in $\mathcal F$: at $a=0$ the resultant
forces $b=\pm i$, and the omitted case is $b=-i$.
We have therefore proved, with complete coordinate maps,
\begin{equation}
\mathbb A^4_q\simeq X\setminus(R_0\cup D_-),\qquad
R_0\cap D_-=\varnothing,\qquad
R_0\simeq D_-\simeq\mathbb A^3 .
\tag{FS15}
\end{equation}
It is an open immersion, rather than an assertion that
$t=1/a$ is an invertible change at its new boundary.

\section{The different, branch divisor and full trace discriminant}
In the original column order $(t,r,A,B)$ and target order
$(A,B,C,D)$, differentiating FS4 and FS13 gives
\begin{equation}
\det D\pi=-2t^3,\qquad
\det Dq=t^3\quad(t\ne0),\qquad
\det DP=-2.
\tag{FS16}
\end{equation}
For the first determinant, the remaining $(t,r)$ minor
after the $(A,B)$ rows is
$\begin{psmallmatrix}2t&-\kappa\\-2rt&r\kappa-t^2\end{psmallmatrix}$,
where $\kappa=12Ar^2+6r+2B$; it has determinant $-2t^3$.
For the second, the triangular pivots of the source
parameterization are $-t^{-2},-1,t^3,t^2$, whose product
is $t^3$. The chain rule proves the last identity where
$a\ne0$, and polynomial equality extends it to all original
source points.

The reduced ramification support is $R_0$; the actual
different (or ramification Cartier divisor) is
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Fitt}_0\Omega_{X/\mathbb A^4}
=(t^3)\quad\hbox{on }X_f,\qquad
\operatorname{Ram}(\pi)=3R_0 .
\tag{FS17}
\end{equation}
Indeed both source and target are smooth of dimension four,
so their differential presentation has determinant FS16.
Alternatively the relations in FS2 have relative Jacobian
in variables $(r,t)$ equal to
$\begin{psmallmatrix}h'(r)&0\\-h''(r)&2t\end{psmallmatrix}$,
whose determinant is $2t^3$ on $X_f$.
At an infinity point, the relative derivative matrix of
$(k(v),\theta^2+k'(v))$ in $(v,\theta)$ has determinant
$2\theta k'(0)=2\theta\ne0$.
Thus no ramification component is hidden at infinity.
The changes FS7 transport the determinant by units and
give FS17 globally.

Define the exact binary quartic discriminant $\mathfrak D$
by its polynomial extension from
$\mathfrak D=A^6\prod_{i<j}(r_j-r_i)^2$ for $A\ne0$.
At $A=0$ its specialization is exactly
\begin{equation}
\mathfrak D(0,B,C,D)
=B^2C^2-4C^3-4B^3D+18BCD-27D^2.
\tag{FS18}
\end{equation}
One way to verify both the polynomial extension and this
constant is the determinant of the Sylvester matrix of
$h,h'$ divided by $A$ on $A\ne0$, then expansion and
evaluation at zero. FS18 is the ordinary discriminant of
the monic cubic; the projective infinity root is simple.
Thus the reduced branch locus is exactly
$\{\mathfrak D=0\}$, the image of $R_0$.
This also identifies the locus where every root chart of
the completed cover is etale; no $A=0$ component is added.

In a free root/sign chart with polynomial leading coefficient
$\lambda$ and basis FS9, let $M_R$ be multiplication by $R$
in the degree-four root algebra. Define
$T_{ij}=\Tr(M_R^{i+j})$ and
$T'_{ij}=\Tr(M_{h_p'}M_R^{i+j})$, $0\le i,j\le3$.
The full bilinear trace Gram in the unaltered basis is
\begin{equation}
\left(\Tr_{X/u}(e_ie_j)\right)_{i,j=1}^{8}
=\begin{pmatrix}2T&0\\0&2T'\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\det\left(\Tr_{X/u}(e_ie_j)\right)
=256\,\frac{\mathfrak D^3}{\lambda^{14}} .
\tag{FS19}
\end{equation}
The algebra is the direct sum of its root module and $T$
times that module. Multiplication by a root-algebra element
acts identically on these two summands, so its trace is twice
the root trace. Odd multiplication has trace zero (also by
conjugating with the sign involution), and the product of
two odd factors is exactly $h_p'$ times their root product.
This proves the block formula even on nonreduced fibres.

For distinct roots, write
$\Delta=\prod_{i<j}(R_j-R_i)$ and
$V=(R_j^i)_{0\le i\le3,1\le j\le4}$.
Then $T=VV^{\mathsf T}$ and
$T'=V\diag(h_p'(R_j))V^{\mathsf T}$, so
$\det T=\Delta^2$, $\det T'=\lambda^4\Delta^4$:
the six pair-sign reversals give
$\prod_j h_p'(R_j)=\lambda^4\Delta^2$.
The determinant of the full blocks is consequently
$2^8\lambda^4\Delta^6
=256(\lambda^6\Delta^2)^3/\lambda^{14}$, as asserted.
Both sides are regular coefficient functions on this free
chart; equality on the dense distinct-root locus proves
equality on every fibre.

The binary discriminant remains unchanged under the
determinant-one substitutions used above. Explicitly,
$c_p=A\prod_j(p-r_j)$ and
$R_j-R_i=(r_j-r_i)/[(p-r_j)(p-r_i)]$, so the factors
in $c_p^6\prod_{i<j}(R_j-R_i)^2$ cancel exactly to
$A^6\prod_{i<j}(r_j-r_i)^2$. The identity extends to all
coefficient points. Thus FS19 on $D(A)$ has denominator
$A^{14}$, and on $D(c_p)$ it has denominator $c_p^{14}$.
Since those denominators are units only on their explicitly
stated opens, the complete global discriminant ideal is
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{disc}(\pi)=(\mathfrak D^3).
\tag{FS20}
\end{equation}
In particular $A^{-14}$ in the first basis is not an
additional ramification divisor. At $A=0$ choose any
$p\in\{0,1,-1,2\}$ with $c_p\ne0$, which FS6 guarantees;
FS19 then evaluates the exact trace determinant with its
full constant $256$ and no singular denominator.

\section{Every nonreduced fibre in the original reciprocal coordinate}
Fix a target $u$. At a finite root $r_0$ of exact multiplicity
$m$ put $\epsilon=r-r_0$ and factor the actual polynomial as
$h_u(r_0+\epsilon)=\epsilon^m g(\epsilon)$ with $g(0)\ne0$.
The root algebra local at $r_0$ is exactly
$\C[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^m)$: all other root factors are
units there. In this algebra
$h'_u(r_0+\epsilon)=m g(0)\epsilon^{m-1}$.
Thus the completed local factor, with its original $t$,
is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal A_{m,c}
=\C[\epsilon,t]/(\epsilon^m,\ t^2-c\epsilon^{m-1}),
\qquad c=m g(0)\ne0 .
\tag{FS21}
\end{equation}
No leading coefficient has been absorbed into $t$.
The two successive monic relations give the exact basis
and multiplication
\begin{align}
\mathcal B_m&=(1,\epsilon,\ldots,\epsilon^{m-1},
              t,t\epsilon,\ldots,t\epsilon^{m-1}),\nonumber\\
\epsilon^i\epsilon^j&=\epsilon^{i+j},&
\epsilon^i(t\epsilon^j)&=t\epsilon^{i+j},&
(t\epsilon^i)(t\epsilon^j)&=c\epsilon^{m-1+i+j},
\tag{FS22}
\end{align}
with every exponent at least $m$ interpreted as zero.
These formulas specify every structure constant and prove
the length $2m$, including all nilpotents.
For $m=1$ the algebra is
$\C[t]/(t^2-c)=\C\oplus\C$, its two points being
$t=\pm\sqrt c$; these are the two original factor signs.
For $m=2$, the exact isomorphism without changing $t$ is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal A_{2,c}\simeq\C[t]/(t^4),\qquad
\epsilon\longmapsto t^2/c,\qquad
t\longmapsto t .
\tag{FS23}
\end{equation}
The inverse sends the class of $t$ to the actual reciprocal
factor. Its square and fourth power satisfy the two original
relations, proving both compositions.
For $m=3$ and $m=4$ the full original algebras remain
$\C[\epsilon,t]/(\epsilon^3,t^2-c\epsilon^2)$ and
$\C[\epsilon,t]/(\epsilon^4,t^2-c\epsilon^3)$.
If one wishes only to compare their abstract isomorphism
types, choose an actual square root $\lambda^2=c$ and send
the new symbol $s$ to $t/\lambda$; the inverse sends $t$
to $\lambda s$. This records the precise comparison to
the coefficient-one presentation and both choices of
$\lambda$; it does not replace the retained coordinate.

For completeness the local maximal-ideal filtrations are
also exact. For $m=2$ they are those of $(t)$ in
$\C[t]/t^4$, with dimensions $4,3,2,1,0$.
For $m=3,4$, put $\mathfrak n=(\epsilon,t)$. Direct
multiplication using FS22 gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
m&(\dim\mathcal A,\dim\mathfrak n,\dim\mathfrak n^2,\ldots)
 &\text{associated graded dimensions}&\operatorname{Soc}(\mathcal A)\\\hline
2&(4,3,2,1,0)&(1,1,1,1)&\C\,t\epsilon\\
3&(6,5,3,1,0)&(1,2,2,1)&\C\,t\epsilon^2\\
4&(8,7,5,3,1,0)&(1,2,2,2,1)&\C\,t\epsilon^3
\end{array}
\tag{FS24}
\end{equation}
For example at $m=4$ the bases of the successive nonzero
powers from the second onward are
$(\epsilon^2,\epsilon^3,t\epsilon,t\epsilon^2,t\epsilon^3)$,
$(\epsilon^3,t\epsilon^2,t\epsilon^3)$ and $(t\epsilon^3)$.
At $m=3$ they are $(\epsilon^2,t\epsilon,t\epsilon^2)$
and $(t\epsilon^2)$. The socle statements follow by
annihilating an arbitrary FS22 basis combination first by
$\epsilon$ and then by $t$.
Since multiplication by a nilpotent has trace zero,
for $m\ge2$ the local trace pairing is exactly
\begin{equation}
\Tr_{\mathcal A_{m,c}}(uv)=2m\,u(0,0)v(0,0),\qquad
\operatorname{rad}\Tr=\mathfrak n,\qquad
\operatorname{rank}\Tr=1 .
\tag{FS25}
\end{equation}
To see the trace identity directly, write a local element
as its constant plus a nilpotent in $\mathfrak n$; its
multiplication operator has that constant on all $2m$
diagonal entries after ordering any compatible filtration.
The reduced $m=1$ factor has its two nondegenerate scalar
trace pairings. Thus FS25 also specifies the complete
degenerate trace forms, not just their vanishing determinants.

The constants in FS21 have the following original-coordinate
expressions, with no unspecified unit:
\begin{equation}
c=\frac{h_u^{(m)}(r_0)}{(m-1)!},\qquad
c_2=12Ar_0^2+6r_0+2B,\quad
c_3=12Ar_0+3,\quad c_4=4A .
\tag{FS26}
\end{equation}
The expression for $m=1$ is just $c_1=h_u'(r_0)$.
Exact multiplicity means the stated constant is nonzero.
For example a cubic triple root has $c_3=3$.
The quadruple-root algebra is not $\C[t]/t^8$:
FS24 shows its embedding dimension is two, and FS22 gives
$t^4=c_4^2\epsilon^6=0$. Its length eight is retained by
the two generators and their full multiplication, not by
declaring the reciprocal factor to have nilpotency order eight.

The entire fibre is the product of these local factors,
by the Chinese remainder theorem in its root algebra.
When $A=0$ the finite-root part has total length six,
and the two infinity points FS5 contribute two reduced
copies of $\C$. The complete list is therefore
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
\text{degree}&\text{root multiplicities}&\text{full completed algebra}
 &\#\text{original points}&\operatorname{rank}\Tr\\\hline
4&1+1+1+1&\C^8&8&8\\
4&2+1+1&\mathcal A_{2,c}\times\C^4&4&5\\
4&2+2&\mathcal A_{2,c}\times\mathcal A_{2,c'}&0&2\\
4&3+1&\mathcal A_{3,c}\times\C^2&2&3\\
4&4&\mathcal A_{4,c}&0&1\\\hline
3&1+1+1&\C^6\times\C^2_\infty&7&8\\
3&2+1&\mathcal A_{2,c}\times\C^2\times\C^2_\infty&3&5\\
3&3&\mathcal A_{3,3}\times\C^2_\infty&1&3
\end{array}
\tag{FS27}
\end{equation}
Every algebra has length eight. Each displayed local
coefficient is the actual FS26 coefficient at that root;
in the $2+2$ case $c$ and $c'$ refer to its two different
roots and are both retained. The original open removes
the entire local factor of each multiple root, because
its $t$ is nilpotent and its principal open $D(t)$ is
empty. Of the two infinity factors it removes only
$\theta=i$. This proves every original count in FS27.
The trace ranks follow by summing FS25 and the simple
scalar trace ranks. Thus the table records both the
geometric points and their nonreduced local schemes.

\section{The omitted surface, ramification parametrization and deck map}
A quartic has no simple root precisely in the two
patterns $2+2$ and $4$. Its leading coefficient is
nonzero. With the unchanged cubic coefficient one,
being a scalar times a quadratic square is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
4AB-1-8A^2C=0,\qquad D-AC^2=0,\qquad
h_u(s)=A\left(s^2+\frac{s}{2A}+C\right)^2 .
\tag{FS28}
\end{equation}
Indeed write the monic quadratic as $s^2+ps+q$;
comparison of its cubic and linear coefficients gives
$p=1/(2A)$, $q=C$, and the other two give FS28.
The maps
$(A,C)\mapsto(A,(4A)^{-1}+2AC,C,AC^2)$ and
$(A,B,C,D)\mapsto(A,C)$ are inverse, with $A\ne0$.
This proves that the original omitted image is exactly
the smooth surface $\C^*\times\C$.
Its quadratic has a double root exactly when
$C=1/(16A^2)$. Thus its full quadruple curve is
\begin{equation}
B=\frac3{8A},\qquad C=\frac1{16A^2},\qquad
D=\frac1{256A^3},\qquad r_0=-\frac1{4A},\qquad A\ne0 .
\tag{FS29}
\end{equation}
Outside this curve FS28 has two length-four completed
points; on the curve it has the full two-generator
length-eight algebra in FS27.

On $R_0$ the projection is exactly FS4 at $t=0$.
Its differential in coordinates $(A,B,r)$ has its first
two rows equal to $(1,0,0)$ and $(0,1,0)$; the third
column in full target coordinates is
$(0,0,-\kappa,r\kappa)^{\mathsf T}$,
$\kappa=12Ar^2+6r+2B$.
It therefore has rank three exactly at roots of
multiplicity two, and rank two at roots of multiplicity
at least three. This proves the precise rank drop,
including cubic triples, without substituting the name
of the discriminant for its parametrization.

Both signs extend on the whole completed algebra as the
regular deck involution
\begin{equation}
(r,t)\mapsto(r,-t),\qquad
(v,\theta)\mapsto(v,-\theta).
