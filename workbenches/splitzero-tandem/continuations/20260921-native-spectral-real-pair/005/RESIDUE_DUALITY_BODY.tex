\section{Residue duality on the complete signed-root algebra}
The finite completion FS1--25 retains nilpotents at a multiple
root. Here a nilpotent means a nonzero algebra element whose
positive power is zero. We compute a bilinear pairing that
detects every such element, the exact map from it to ordinary
trace, and both maps in the original conductor spaces.
The general relation between a coefficient-residue functional,
its Hankel matrix and the inverse Bezout matrix is classical;
we use Jouve and Rodriguez-Villegas \cite[Section 2, especially
Lemma \texttt{Q-lemma} and Theorem \texttt{thBsharp}]{JouveRV}.
All matrices and constants for the original quartic, its signed
extension and its conductor return are proved below.

Let $u=(A,B,C,D)$, with $A\ne0$, and retain
\begin{equation}
 h=Ar^4+r^3+Br^2+Cr+D,\quad
 E_u=\C[r]/(h),\quad
 S_u=E_u[t]/(t^2-h'),\quad
 \mathbf e=(1,r,r^2,r^3,t,tr,tr^2,tr^3).
 \tag{RD1}
\end{equation}
Division by $h$ has a unique remainder of degree at most three
because its leading coefficient is a unit. Thus $E_u$ has the
first four displayed basis vectors, and division by the monic
polynomial in $t$ proves that every element of $S_u$ has a
unique expression $R+tS$, with $R,S\in E_u$. Define
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_u(F)=\sum_{h(r_0)=0}\operatorname{Res}_{r=r_0}
                 \frac{F(r)\,dr}{h(r)}
     =\frac{[r^3](F\bmod h)}{A},\qquad
 \Lambda_u(R+tS)=\lambda_u(S),\qquad
 \mathcal B_u(F,G)=\Lambda_u(FG).
 \tag{RD2}
\end{equation}
The residue formula includes every multiple root. Subtracting
the remainder adds a polynomial differential with no finite
residue. For the remainder, its coefficient of $r^{-1}$ at
infinity is its cubic coefficient divided by $A$; the sum
of the finite residues is that coefficient. This proves RD2
directly and also proves that the definition descends to $E_u$.
The functional is complex-linear; the pairing is complex
bilinear and symmetric. No conjugation is used in RD2.

In the displayed order the two full Gram matrices and inverse are
\begin{align}
 H_u&=\begin{pmatrix}
 0&0&0&A^{-1}\\
 0&0&A^{-1}&-A^{-2}\\
 0&A^{-1}&-A^{-2}&A^{-3}-BA^{-2}\\
 A^{-1}&-A^{-2}&A^{-3}-BA^{-2}&-A^{-4}+2BA^{-3}-CA^{-2}
 \end{pmatrix},\nonumber\\
 J_u&=\begin{pmatrix}C&B&1&A\\B&1&A&0\\1&A&0&0\\A&0&0&0\end{pmatrix},
 &\mathcal B_u&\longleftrightarrow
 B_u=\begin{pmatrix}0&H_u\\H_u&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 B_u^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&J_u\\J_u&0\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{RD3}
\end{align}
To prove every entry, put $l_j=\lambda_u(r^j)$. RD2 gives
$l_0=l_1=l_2=0$, $l_3=A^{-1}$; multiplying $h=0$ by
$1,r,r^2$ gives successively
$l_4=-A^{-2}$, $l_5=A^{-3}-BA^{-2}$ and
$l_6=-A^{-4}+2BA^{-3}-CA^{-2}$.
Consequently $(H_u)_{ij}=l_{i+j}$ for $0\le i,j\le3$.
The product $(R+tS)(P+tQ)$ has odd coefficient $RQ+SP$,
which proves the block matrix. Direct row multiplication
gives $H_uJ_u=J_uH_u=I_4$. Equivalently, $J_u$ is the
coefficient matrix of $(h(x)-h(y))/(x-y)$; in the sign
convention of \cite[Section 2.3]{JouveRV} it is $B(h,1)$.
Expansion along the reversed diagonal gives
\begin{equation}
 \det H_u=A^{-4},\qquad \det B_u=A^{-8}.
 \tag{RD4}
\end{equation}
In particular $B_u$ is invertible on the entire $A\ne0$
chart, including its discriminant. Explicit dual vectors
are $t\sum_j(J_u)_{ji}r^j$ for $r^i$, and
$\sum_j(J_u)_{ji}r^j$ for $tr^i$. Pairing against those
eight vectors recovers the eight original coefficients.

\section{The exact passage from residue to trace}
For $F\in S_u$ let $M_F$ denote multiplication by $F$.
Define $L=M_{h'}$ on $E_u$. Multiplication by $t$ and by
$2t^3$ in the order RD1 are
\begin{equation}
 M_t=\begin{pmatrix}0&L\\I_4&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 M_{2t^3}=2\begin{pmatrix}0&L^2\\L&0\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{RD5}
\end{equation}
Every trace, including on a nonreduced fibre, satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Tr}_{S_u}(M_F)=\Lambda_u(2t^3F),\qquad
 T_u:=\bigl(\operatorname{Tr}M_{e_i e_j}\bigr)_{ij}
    =B_uM_{2t^3}
    =2\begin{pmatrix}H_uL&0\\0&H_uL^2\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{RD6}
\end{equation}
Here is a proof valid on each fibre without a distinct-root
assumption. On the length-$m$ algebra at $r_0$, multiplication
by $R$ is triangular in powers of $r-r_0$, and its diagonal
is $R(r_0)$ repeated $m$ times. Its trace is $mR(r_0)$.
Writing $h=(r-r_0)^m g$ shows that the residue of $h'R/h$
is also $mR(r_0)$. Addition over the roots gives
$\operatorname{Tr}_{E_u}M_R=\lambda_u(h'R)$.
On $E_u\oplus tE_u$ the diagonal blocks of $M_{R+tS}$
are both $M_R$, so its trace is $2\lambda_u(h'R)$.
The odd coefficient of $2t^3(R+tS)$ is $2h'R$.
This proves the functional identity and then the Gram identity.

The matrix of $M_r$ on $E_u$ is completely explicit:
\begin{equation}
 Z=\begin{pmatrix}
 0&0&0&-D/A\\1&0&0&-C/A\\0&1&0&-B/A\\0&0&1&-1/A
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 L=4AZ^3+3Z^2+2BZ+CI_4 .
 \tag{RD7}
\end{equation}
Thus RD5--6 contain no unevaluated quotient multiplication.
With the usual quartic discriminant retaining the factor $A^6$,
$\det L=\prod h'(r_j)=\operatorname{Disc}(h)/A^2$:
at four distinct roots each unordered difference contributes
its square and the six sign changes have positive product.
Both sides are polynomials in the coefficients with powers of
$A$ inverted, so this identity holds at repeated roots too.
Taking determinants in RD5--6 now proves
\begin{equation}
 \det M_{2t^3}=2^8(\det L)^3,\qquad
 \det T_u=\frac{256\operatorname{Disc}(h)^3}{A^{14}}.
 \tag{RD8}
\end{equation}
The determinant loss is exactly multiplication by $2t^3$.
The residue identification $S_u\to S_u^*$ in RD3 is an
isomorphism; its composition with $M_{2t^3}$ is the trace
identification. This specifies the actual morphism and its
kernel, rather than merely comparing the names of two forms.

\section{Every local nilpotent and every lost trace direction}
At a root of multiplicity $m\in\{2,3,4\}$ put
$\epsilon=r-r_0$ and write the complete polynomial
$h(r_0+\epsilon)=\epsilon^m g(\epsilon)$, $g_0=g(0)\ne0$.
The local signed algebra and its residue functional are
\begin{equation}
 S_{u,r_0}=\C[\epsilon,t]/
   (\epsilon^m,\ t^2-mg_0\epsilon^{m-1}),\qquad
 \Lambda_{r_0}(R+tS)=[\epsilon^{m-1}]\frac{S}{g(\epsilon)}.
 \tag{RD9}
\end{equation}
The first relation gives the local root algebra by the
polynomial Chinese remainder construction; differentiating
$\epsilon^m g$ and reducing modulo $\epsilon^m$ gives
the second relation with its exact coefficient $mg_0$.
The last equation is the definition of the residue at $r_0$.
All coefficients of $g$ needed in the truncated inverse
remain in that functional.

Write $g^{-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}b_j\epsilon^j$ modulo
$\epsilon^m$. These coefficients are finite exact expressions:
$b_0=g_0^{-1}$ and
$b_j=-g_0^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^j g_k b_{j-k}$.
In the order $(1,\epsilon,\ldots,\epsilon^{m-1},
t,t\epsilon,\ldots,t\epsilon^{m-1})$ the Gram matrix is
\begin{equation}
 B_{r_0}=\begin{pmatrix}0&H_{r_0}\\H_{r_0}&0\end{pmatrix},
 \quad (H_{r_0})_{ij}=
 \begin{cases}b_{m-1-i-j}&i+j\le m-1,\\0&i+j>m-1,\end{cases}
 \quad \det B_{r_0}=(-1)^m g_0^{-2m}.
 \tag{RD10}
\end{equation}
The determinant follows from the anti-triangular matrix:
$\det H_{r_0}=(-1)^{m(m-1)/2}g_0^{-m}$; swapping its two
blocks adds $(-1)^m$. A more explicit proof of invertibility
gives every coefficient of $F=R+tS$:
\begin{equation}
 R_j=\Lambda_{r_0}(F\,t\epsilon^{m-1-j}g),\qquad
 S_j=\Lambda_{r_0}(F\,\epsilon^{m-1-j}g),
 \quad 0\le j<m .
 \tag{RD11}
\end{equation}
Indeed the first odd coefficient is $R\epsilon^{m-1-j}g$,
and the second is $S\epsilon^{m-1-j}g$. Dividing by the
unchanged $g$ in RD9 extracts precisely the stated coefficient.
Thus every nonzero nilpotent has an explicitly specified
test vector with nonzero pairing.

On the other hand $t^4=0$ and
\begin{equation}
 2t^3F=2mg_0 R_0\,t\epsilon^{m-1},\qquad
 \operatorname{Tr}M_F=2mR_0,\qquad
 \ker M_{2t^3}=(\epsilon,t),\quad
 \operatorname{im}M_{2t^3}=\C\,t\epsilon^{m-1}.
 \tag{RD12}
\end{equation}
Here $t^4=m^2g_0^2\epsilon^{2m-2}=0$ because $m\ge2$.
Multiplication by $t^3=mg_0t\epsilon^{m-1}$ kills all
terms except $R_0$. The image vector has
$\Lambda_{r_0}(t\epsilon^{m-1})=g_0^{-1}$, proving
the trace formula and both displayed spaces. The trace
pairing is exactly $2mR_0P_0$ and has rank one on this
length-$2m$ algebra. For a simple root $h'(r_0)\ne0$,
the two signs are distinct, $t$ is a unit, and the trace
pairing has rank two. Therefore for a fibre with $n_1$
simple roots and $n_{\ge2}$ multiple roots the full trace
rank is $2n_1+n_{\ge2}$, while residue rank is always eight.

\section{The paired critical limit in the original quartet}
Retain $\gamma>0$, $\eta\in\{+1,-1\}$ and the original
factor $-1/2$. At $\delta=0$ put
\begin{equation}
 h_0(r)=-\tfrac12\bigl((r-\tfrac12)^2+\gamma^2\bigr)^2,
 \quad r_\eta=\tfrac12+i\eta\gamma,\quad
 g_\eta(\epsilon)=-\tfrac12(\epsilon+2i\eta\gamma)^2 .
 \tag{RD13}
\end{equation}
At each of the two roots the exact relations are
$g_0=2\gamma^2$, $g_1=-2i\eta\gamma$,
$t^2=4\gamma^2\epsilon$. Hence the two explicit mutually
inverse local homomorphisms identify the algebra with
$\C[t]/(t^4)$ by
$\epsilon\mapsto t^2/(4\gamma^2)$ and $t\mapsto t$.
This is the double-root case only; the triple and quadruple
root algebras remain the two-generator algebras RD9.
In the unchanged powers $(1,t,t^2,t^3)$ one obtains
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda_\eta(1)=\Lambda_\eta(t^2)=0,\quad
 \Lambda_\eta(t)=\frac{i\eta}{2\gamma^3},\quad
 \Lambda_\eta(t^3)=2,\qquad
 B_\eta=\begin{pmatrix}
 0&i\eta/(2\gamma^3)&0&2\\
 i\eta/(2\gamma^3)&0&2&0\\
 0&2&0&0\\2&0&0&0
 \end{pmatrix},\quad\det B_\eta=16.
 \tag{RD14}
\end{equation}
For example $\Lambda(t)=-g_1/g_0^2$ by RD9, and
$\Lambda(t^3)=4\gamma^2\Lambda(t\epsilon)=2$.
These evaluations prove every entry and the determinant.
Ordinary trace in this basis is $4$ times the product of
constant coefficients, so its rank is one in each cluster.

Set $X(\gamma)=\xi(1/2+i\gamma)$. The entire completion
and its reflection law are those of AR10--12, with the
human sources cited there. Reflection gives
$\xi(r_\eta)=X(\gamma)$ and differentiation gives
$\xi'(r_\eta)=-i\eta X'(\gamma)$. Thus its exact class is
\begin{equation}
 j_\eta\xi=X(\gamma)-\frac{i\eta X'(\gamma)}{4\gamma^2}t^2,
 \quad
 \Lambda_\eta((j_\eta\xi)t^3)=2X(\gamma),\quad
 \Lambda_\eta((j_\eta\xi)t)=
 \frac{i\eta X(\gamma)}{2\gamma^3}
       -\frac{i\eta X'(\gamma)}{2\gamma^2}.
 \tag{RD15}
\end{equation}
Every equality follows by reducing the Taylor series modulo
$\epsilon^2$ and using RD14. These two measurements recover
both $X$ and $X'$ without division by either:
\begin{equation}
 X=\tfrac12\Lambda_\eta((j_\eta\xi)t^3),\quad
 X'=2i\eta\gamma^2\left[
 \Lambda_\eta((j_\eta\xi)t)
 -\frac{i\eta}{4\gamma^3}\Lambda_\eta((j_\eta\xi)t^3)
 \right].
 \tag{RD16}
\end{equation}
The coefficient of $t^2$ is therefore not erased by passing
to the completed fibre. This statement makes no assumption
about whether a particular $X$ or $X'$ is zero. For any
entire $F$ of order $\nu$ at a root of multiplicity $m$,
its multiplication rank on RD9 is exactly
$2\max(m-\nu,0)$: multiplication by its unit Taylor factor
is invertible, and multiplication by $\epsilon^\nu$ has
rank $m-\nu$ on each of the two free parity summands when
$\nu<m$, and rank zero otherwise.

Both local measurements are literal measurements on the
whole eight-dimensional fibre. Put $d_\eta=2i\eta\gamma$,
$\epsilon_\eta=r-r_\eta$ and define its root projector by
\begin{equation}
 e_\eta(r)=\frac{(r-r_{-\eta})^2}{d_\eta^2}
                \left(1-\frac{2\epsilon_\eta}{d_\eta}\right)
                  \pmod{h_0}.
 \tag{RD17}
\end{equation}
It is $1$ modulo $\epsilon_\eta^2$ and $0$ modulo
$(r-r_{-\eta})^2$. The two ideals are coprime since
$d_\eta\ne0$, so $e_++e_-=1$, $e_\eta^2=e_\eta$ and
$e_+e_-=0$. These identities prove the direct product
decomposition, with the original signs retained.
For every $F$, $\Lambda_\eta(F)=\Lambda_u(e_\eta F)$.
Thus RD15 uses the global test vectors $e_\eta t^3$ and
$e_\eta t$, with all products reduced in the exact RD1 basis.

\section{Every dual vector in the two original conductor spaces}
Fix the original period and its four distinct labelled reference
roots, as in FC26--32. The reference quartet is held fixed while
$u$ varies in RD1; it is not replaced by the colliding roots.
Write $\mathcal U=\mathcal P_{v+3}/\mathcal P_{v-1}$,
$\mathcal W=\mathcal P_3$, and keep the exact maps
$X,Y,\Phi,\Psi,E,O$ of FC32 and SF2--3. In particular
$Y=T_AX$, $\Psi=T_A\Phi$, and $O$ is the original invertible
odd frame. In the actual target variable $S'$ the center is
$c_*=a_{\rm amp}/2-4$; retain $\kappa=c_*-1/2$.
The spectral coefficient map is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal R_u:\mathcal W\oplus\mathcal W\longrightarrow S_u,
 \quad(p,q)\longmapsto p(r+\kappa)+tq(r+\kappa),\qquad
 \mathcal R_u^{-1}(R+tS)=(R(S'-\kappa),S(S'-\kappa)).
 \tag{RD18}
\end{equation}
The remainders $R,S$ have degree at most three, so the
two substitution maps compose to the identity in both
directions. Their coefficient matrix is
$D_\kappa=\operatorname{diag}(C_\kappa,C_\kappa)$ with
the full binomial matrix RW5; its determinant is one.

For the original eight signed coordinates $z=(\alpha,\beta)$ set
\begin{align}
 \mathcal J_W z&=(Y\alpha,\Psi(E\alpha+O\beta)),\nonumber\\
 \mathcal J_U z&=(X\alpha,\Phi(E\alpha+O\beta)),\qquad
 \mathcal J_W=(T_A\oplus T_A)\mathcal J_U,\nonumber\\
 Q_u&=D_\kappa
 \begin{pmatrix}Y&0\\\Psi E&\Psi O\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \mathcal Q_u=\mathcal R_u\mathcal J_W
      =\mathcal R_u(T_A\oplus T_A)\mathcal J_U .
 \tag{RD19}
\end{align}
The entries of $Y,\Psi$ in this matrix are in the original
target monomial basis. Its complete inverse on a class
$R+tS$ is
\begin{equation}
 \alpha=Y^{-1}C_{-\kappa}[R],\qquad
 \beta=O^{-1}\left(\Psi^{-1}C_{-\kappa}[S]-E\alpha\right).
 \tag{RD20}
\end{equation}
Square brackets here mean the cubic coefficient column.
Substitution in RD19 proves both inverse identities. Its
actual source state is, without changing its quotient norm,
$(T_A^{-1}R(S'-\kappa),T_A^{-1}S(S'-\kappa))$.
Every coefficient of this inverse is the finite recurrence
FC24 with its original moments and weights. Thus RD17 and
RD20 explicitly return every critical derivative test to
the original source, including its retained label component.

The residue and trace matrices in the signed coordinates are
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde B_u=Q_u^{\mathsf T}B_uQ_u,\quad
 \widetilde B_u^{-1}=Q_u^{-1}B_u^{-1}Q_u^{-\mathsf T},\quad
 \widetilde T_u=\widetilde B_u\,\widetilde D_u,\quad
 \widetilde D_u=Q_u^{-1}M_{2t^3}Q_u,\quad
 \det\widetilde B_u=\frac{(\det Y\det\Psi\det O)^2}{A^8}.
 \tag{RD21}
\end{equation}
These are transpose congruences because residue is bilinear.
All inverses exist for every $A\ne0$, including $\delta=0$
in RD13. In contrast, the exact kernel of the trace matrix
is $Q_u^{-1}\ker M_{2t^3}$, with every local component
given by RD12. No kernel of the original $T_A$ has been
introduced by this composition.

The original positive norms are retained separately. Set
$L_0=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&0\\E&O\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
With the original full Gram matrices of RW14 and RW19,
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb H_W=L_0^*\operatorname{diag}(G_Y,G)L_0,
 \quad
 \mathbb H_U=L_0^*\operatorname{diag}(G_X,G_U)L_0,
 \quad
 \|\ell\|_{W^*}^2=\ell\mathbb H_W^{-1}\ell^*,\quad
 \|\ell\|_{U^*}^2=\ell\mathbb H_U^{-1}\ell^* .
 \tag{RD22}
\end{equation}
Here $\ell$ is any one of the exact linear rows
$f^{\mathsf T}\widetilde B_u$ furnished by RD11 or RD17--21.
The dual norm follows by writing a positive Gram as $H^{1/2}H^{1/2}$
and applying Cauchy--Schwarz to $\ell H^{-1/2}$, with equality
on its adjoint column. This derivation changes no input norm.
It includes all Gamma masses, original centers and moments
already evaluated in FC28--30 and RW19--20.

The existing full Weil form also has an exact expression
through this nondegenerate residue pairing. For its actual
Hermitian matrix $\mathbb W$ from RW14, define the
conjugate-linear map
\begin{equation}
 \mathscr A_u(z)=\widetilde B_u^{-1}\mathbb W^{\mathsf T}\bar z,
 \qquad
 \mathcal B_u(\mathcal Q_u\mathscr A_u(z),\mathcal Q_u w)
       =z^*\mathbb Ww.
 \tag{RD23}
\end{equation}
Transpose the displayed formula and use the symmetry of
$\widetilde B_u$ to prove the equality. Since the latter
matrix is invertible and $\mathbb W$ is Hermitian,
$\ker\mathscr A_u=\ker\mathbb W$; its exact kernel is
RW17, and its rank equals that of $\mathbb W$.
Equation RD23 is a proved correspondence, not a claim that
bilinear nondegeneracy implies Hermitian positivity.

\section{The remaining root charts and the seven-point fibre}
No zero of $A$ is discarded from the finite completion.
On the open $h(p)\ne0$, $p\in\{0,1,-1,2\}$, use the
actual determinant-one chart FS7:
\begin{equation}
 R=(p-r)^{-1},\quad T=t/(p-r),\quad
 h_p(R)=R^4h(p-R^{-1})=aR^4+bR^3+cR^2+dR+e .
 \tag{RD24}
\end{equation}
Its leading coefficient is $a=h(p)\ne0$ and its signed
equation remains $T^2=h_p'(R)$. Define
$\Lambda_p(U+TV)=[R^3](V\bmod h_p)/a$.
The complete matrices in this chart are
\begin{equation}
 H_p=\begin{pmatrix}
 0&0&0&a^{-1}\\0&0&a^{-1}&-ba^{-2}\\
 0&a^{-1}&-ba^{-2}&b^2a^{-3}-ca^{-2}\\
 a^{-1}&-ba^{-2}&b^2a^{-3}-ca^{-2}&-b^3a^{-4}+2bca^{-3}-da^{-2}
 \end{pmatrix},\quad
 H_p^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}d&c&b&a\\c&b&a&0\\b&a&0&0\\a&0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{RD25}
\end{equation}
The same three remainder recurrences prove every entry and
their product is the identity. The signed Gram is
$B_p=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&H_p\\H_p&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
$\det B_p=a^{-8}$. The trace map is again exactly $M_{2T^3}$.
On the overlap with $A\ne0$, let $\iota$ substitute
$r=p-1/R$, $t=T/R$. The derivative identity in FS7 proves
that this is an algebra isomorphism, with the displayed
inverse, and the residue change of variable gives
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda_u(F)=\Lambda_p(R^3\iota(F)).
 \tag{RD26}
\end{equation}
Indeed $dr/h=R^2dR/h_p$, while the odd coefficient of
$\iota(F)$ is $S(p-1/R)/R$. Multiplication by $R^3$
therefore gives precisely the transformed residue. On this
overlap $R$ is a unit, so the transition is invertible.
This identifies the two dualities with the exact factor
$R^3$ retained.

There is also a full physical coefficient receiver on this
chart, including $R=0$. For $f\in\mathcal P_3$ define
\begin{equation}
 (Z_pf)(R)=R^3 f(p+\kappa-R^{-1}),\qquad
 f(S')=(p+\kappa-S')^3
       (Z_pf)((p+\kappa-S')^{-1}).
 \tag{RD27}
\end{equation}
Each expression is a polynomial of degree at most three;
the formulas compose to the identity by substitution.
For every monomial its forward image is exactly
$R^{3-j}((p+\kappa)R-1)^j$, $0\le j\le3$.
Reversing the column order gives diagonal $(-1)^j$;
both resulting signs are $(-1)^6=1$, so $\det Z_p=1$.
Thus $(f,g)\mapsto Z_pf+T Z_pg$ is an isomorphism
$\mathcal W^2\to S_{u,p}$ even at the infinity root.
Composing with $\mathcal J_W$ and its exact inverse RD20,
using $Z_p$ in place of $C_\kappa$, proves every analogue
of RD19--22 with $B_p$ and determinant denominator $a^8$.

This coefficient receiver is not silently identified with
the algebra change of variable. On their overlap its two
parity parts are $R^3\iota(F_{\mathrm{even}})$ and
$R^4\iota(F_{\mathrm{odd}})$. Therefore for two physical inputs
with finite-chart images $F,G$, the two pulled-back pairings
are related by the explicit invertible multiplier
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda_p(F_pG_p)
       =\Lambda_u((p-r)^{-4}FG).
 \tag{RD28}
\end{equation}
The odd product acquires $R^7$, and RD26 removes $R^3$,
leaving $R^4=(p-r)^{-4}$, which proves this identity.
The five opens cover the entire target by FS6. Hence these
formulas supply receivers and their exact comparisons on
every fibre of the original finite completion.

At the original target $(A,B,C,D)=(0,0,-1,0)$ take $p=2$.
Then the full polynomial, roots and residue determinant are
\begin{equation}
 h_2(R)=6R^4-11R^3+6R^2-R,\quad
 R\in\{0,\tfrac13,\tfrac12,1\},\quad
 \det B_2=6^{-8},\quad
 \det T_2=256\cdot4^3/6^{14}.
 \tag{RD29}
\end{equation}
Expansion of $(2R-1)^3R-(2R-1)R^3$ proves the polynomial.
Its factorization $R(R-1)(2R-1)(3R-1)$ proves the roots;
their difference product gives discriminant $4$.
At $R=0$, $T^2=h_2'(0)=-1$ gives both $T=\pm i$.
FS7 sends these to the two original infinity signs;
$T=-i$ is the original affine state and $T=i$ is the
omitted state. Thus all eight completed states, the seven
original affine states and the exact missing sign are
present in the same invertible residue calculation.
