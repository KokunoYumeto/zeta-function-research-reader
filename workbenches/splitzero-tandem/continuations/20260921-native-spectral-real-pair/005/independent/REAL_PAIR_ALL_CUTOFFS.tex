\section{Every original cutoff: the complete two-pole law and its exact resonance}
This section calculates every cutoff of the same original conductor
at the real pair of RPZ1--19. It also identifies the entire resonance
locus of the RPCJ simple real-pair family. No moment, original weight
or higher coefficient is removed. The full geometric family and
Gamma dictionary retain \cite{PCLOriginal,WCF,DLMF18Source}.

Retain RPCJ5--12's actual constants $c>0$, $d=-iR$, $e<0$,
$c_3,d_2,e_1,f$. For each integer $D\ge1$ set $N=D+1$ and
\[
 B_{D,s}(z)=\operatorname{diag}(\rho_0,\ldots,\rho_D)^{-1}
          T_D(g(\cdot,z))\operatorname{diag}(\rho_0,\ldots,\rho_D),
 \qquad \rho_j=\sqrt{j!/(s/2)_j}.
 \tag{RPAC1}
\]
Here $T_D(g)_{ij}=g_{j-i}$ for $j\ge i$, and is zero otherwise;
$g(t,z)=e^{-4\omega(t)}E_A(\omega(t),z)$ and
$\omega(t)=-i\arctan t$, exactly RPCJ9. Both original values
$s\in\{1,a_{\rm amp}\}$ retain their full mass
$(2\pi)^{s/2}$ and centers $a_{\rm amp}/2$, $a_{\rm amp}/2-4$.
The displayed matrix uses their orthonormal frames.

Put $\tau=z-z_*$. Since $g_0=c\tau^2+O(\tau^3)$, it is
nonzero in a sufficiently small punctured neighborhood of zero.
There the exact inverse is the weighted triangular matrix of the
coefficients $h_n=[t^n]g(t,z)^{-1}$, $0\le n\le D$.
Define the finite expressions
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 H_n&=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
    (-1)^{n-j}\binom{n-j}{j}
           \frac{d^{n-2j}e^j}{c^{n-j+1}},\qquad H_{-1}=0,\\
 K_n&=-[y^n]\frac{c_3+d_2y+e_1y^2+fy^3}{(c+dy+ey^2)^2}.
 \end{aligned}\tag{RPAC2}
\]
Coefficient extraction in the second line is finite: explicitly it is
$-\sum_{j=0}^{\min(3,n)}a_j\sum_{k=0}^{n-j}H_kH_{n-j-k}$,
where $(a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3)=(c_3,d_2,e_1,f)$.
Every constant on the right is an original derivative in RPCJ5--11.
They are not independently prescribed values.

The complete inverse entries have the Laurent expansions
\[
 h_n(\tau)=\tau^{-n-2}\{H_n+\tau K_n+O(\tau^2)\},\qquad
 cH_0=1,\quad cH_n+dH_{n-1}+eH_{n-2}=0\quad(n\ge1),
 \tag{RPAC3}
\]
where $H_{-2}=0$. To prove both claims, substitute $t=\tau y$
as an exact auxiliary coefficient identity, retaining the inverse
substitution and all powers:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 g(\tau y,z_*+\tau)
 &=\tau^2(c+dy+ey^2)
   +\tau^3(c_3+d_2y+e_1y^2+fy^3)+O(\tau^4),\\
 \tau^2g(\tau y,z_*+\tau)^{-1}
 &=\frac1{c+dy+ey^2}
 -\tau\frac{c_3+d_2y+e_1y^2+fy^3}{(c+dy+ey^2)^2}
 +O(\tau^2).
 \end{aligned}\tag{RPAC4}
\]
These identities hold coefficientwise through each fixed degree $D$;
analyticity gives the stated remainder for every such finite set.
Terms $g_jt^j$ with $j\ge4$ first contribute at order at least four
in the first line. They are retained in the remainder and in the
exact inverse. Taking $[y^n]$ in the second line multiplies the
original $h_n$ by exactly $\tau^{n+2}$, proving RPAC3.
Expanding the reciprocal geometric series proves RPAC2, and
multiplying it by $c+dy+ey^2$ proves the recurrence.

\subsection{Exact resonance and the impossibility of consecutive zeros}
In terms of the actual derivatives $A=G_z$ and $B=G_\xi$, put
\[
 \lambda=\frac{|B|}{|A|}>0,\qquad
 \theta=\frac{\Re(A\bar B)}{|A||B|}\in[-1,1].\tag{RPAC5}
\]
Their magnitudes remain in every formula through $c=|A|^2$,
$d=-2ic\lambda\theta$, $e=-c\lambda^2$.
Define $U_0(X)=1$, $U_1(X)=2X$ and
$U_n(X)=2XU_{n-1}(X)-U_{n-2}(X)$. These are the explicitly
defined polynomials; the familiar Chebyshev notation introduces
no external bound. Induction in RPAC3 proves
\[
 H_n=\frac{(i\lambda)^n}{c}U_n(\theta),\qquad
 H_{n-1}^2-H_nH_{n-2}=\frac{e^{n-1}}{c^{n+1}}
                      \quad(n\ge1).\tag{RPAC6}
\]
For the second identity, its left side at $n=1$ is $c^{-2}$.
Substitution of the recurrence makes the left side at $n+1$
equal to $e/c$ times its value at $n$, proving it for all $n$.
In particular $H_D$ and $H_{D-1}$ cannot vanish together.

The angle addition formula for sine verifies the same recurrence
for $\sin((n+1)\phi)/\sin\phi$, with the same initial terms.
The endpoints give $U_n(1)=n+1$ and
$U_n(-1)=(-1)^n(n+1)$ by the recurrence. It follows that the
complete zero set is
\[
 H_D=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \theta=\cos\frac{k\pi}{D+1}\text{ for some }k=1,\ldots,D.
 \tag{RPAC7}
\]
Indeed these are $D$ distinct roots of the degree-$D$ polynomial,
whose leading coefficient is $2^D$, so there are no other roots.
This characterizes all exceptional cutoffs without discarding any
original derivative or asserting that a finite interval decides
infinitely many equalities.

\subsection{The entire limiting forward block and all exterior constants}
For $D\ge2$ the limiting matrix has nonzero block $C_{D,s}$
with rows $0,\ldots,D-2$ and columns $2,\ldots,D$:
\[
 (C_{D,s})_{ij}=
 \begin{cases}g_{j+2-i}(z_*)\rho_{j+2}/\rho_i,&j\ge i,\\
 0,&j<i,
 \end{cases}\quad 0\le i,j\le D-2 .\tag{RPAC8}
\]
Its diagonal is $e\rho_{i+2}/\rho_i$, so it is invertible.
All higher coefficients $g_3,\ldots,g_D$, with their actual
moments and center corrections, remain in this block.
For $D=1$ use the empty block, whose zeroth exterior norm and
determinant are one. The limiting forward rank is exactly $N-2$.
For $2\le r\le N$ set
\[
 L_{r,D,s}=\frac{\|\bigwedge^{N-r}C_{D,s}\|}{c^N}>0.
 \tag{RPAC9}
\]
The norms in RPAC9 are completely specified finite matrix norms
of RPAC8 in the original orthonormal frames. In particular
\[
 L_{2,D,s}=\frac{|e|^{D-1}\rho_{D-1}\rho_D}
                    {c^{D+1}\rho_0\rho_1},\qquad
 L_{N,D,s}=c^{-N}.\tag{RPAC10}
\]
The first formula follows by multiplying the diagonal of RPAC8;
its weight product telescopes to the displayed original ratios.
It also gives $c^{-2}$ at $D=1$.

For every original cutoff and every $2\le r\le N$ one has
\[
 \lim_{z\to z_*}|z-z_*|^{2N}
       \|\bigwedge^rB_{D,s}(z)^{-1}\|=L_{r,D,s}.
 \tag{RPAC11}
\]
To prove this identity, let the forward singular values of an
invertible $N$-dimensional matrix be
$\sigma_1\ge\cdots\ge\sigma_N>0$. The product of the largest
$r$ inverse singular values is
$\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_{N-r}/|\det B|$.
Thus its $r$th inverse exterior norm equals
$\|\bigwedge^{N-r}B\|/|\det B|$.
Here $\det B=g_0^N$ and $g_0=c\tau^2+O(\tau^3)$.
The limiting numerator is the strictly positive numerator in
RPAC9. Continuity gives RPAC11, for complex as well as real
approaches. This proves the exponent $2N$ at every exterior rank
from two through $N$, without a resonance restriction.

\subsection{Both complete inverse spectra}
When $H_D\ne0$, RPAC3 shows that the only entry of pole order
$D+2$ is the top-right entry, with coefficient
$H_D\rho_D/\rho_0$. All other entries have lower pole order.
Consequently, with
$A_{D,s}=|H_D|\rho_D/\rho_0>0$, the two divergent inverse
singular values have exact exponents and leading constants
\[
 (D+2,D),\qquad (A_{D,s},L_{2,D,s}/A_{D,s}).\tag{RPAC12}
\]
The first constant follows by taking the norm after multiplication
by $\tau^{D+2}$. The second follows by dividing RPAC11 at rank
two by the first singular asymptotic. Every other inverse singular
value has a finite positive limit, the reciprocal of the nonzero
forward singular values of $C_{D,s}$ in reverse order.

When $H_D=0$, RPAC6 gives $H_{D-1}\ne0$. The entire leading
inverse matrix at order $D+1$ has the block
\[
 N_{D,s}=\begin{pmatrix}
 H_{D-1}\rho_{D-1}/\rho_0&K_D\rho_D/\rho_0\\
 0&H_{D-1}\rho_D/\rho_1
 \end{pmatrix}.\tag{RPAC13}
\]
It occupies rows $0,1$ and columns $D-1,D$.
All other entries vanish after multiplication by $\tau^{D+1}$.
For $D=1$ this is the full matrix, with diagonal entries
$H_0=1/c$; hence the statement also covers the endpoint.
The block is invertible. Its two positive singular values are
the exact leading constants of the two divergent inverse
singular values, both of exponent $D+1$.
For an explicit finite formula write its entries as
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\0&d_0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
set $S=|a|^2+|b|^2+|d_0|^2$ and $Q=|ad_0|^2$, and obtain
\[
 \sigma_\pm(N_{D,s})=
   \left(\frac{S\pm\sqrt{S^2-4Q}}2\right)^{1/2}.
 \tag{RPAC14}
\]
This follows from the trace and determinant of $N_{D,s}^*N_{D,s}$.
The full $K_D$ in RPAC2 retains every next-jet correction.
Furthermore RPAC6 at $n=D$ gives
$|H_{D-1}|^2=|e|^{D-1}/c^{D+1}$, so
$|\det N_{D,s}|=L_{2,D,s}$, consistently with RPAC11.

These calculations exhaust the alternatives. The inverse exterior
exponents are
\[
 \begin{cases}
 (D+2,2N,\ldots,2N),&H_D\ne0,\\
 (D+1,2N,\ldots,2N),&H_D=0.
 \end{cases}
\]
The first exterior constants are
$A_{D,s}$ and $\sigma_+(N_{D,s})$, respectively; all remaining
constants are RPAC9. At cutoff $D=0$ the single matrix entry is
$g_0$, so its sole inverse pole has exponent two and constant
$c^{-1}$. In the original coordinate $u$, every constant at pole
exponent $p$ is multiplied by $|u_*|^{2p}$, by the exact identity
$z-z_*=-(u-u_*)/(uu_*)$.

\subsection{An actual finite range on the certified original pair}
The companion \nolinkurl{check_real_pair_all_cutoffs.py} first
checks the finite coefficient and reciprocal identities exactly,
then propagates the complete RPZ certificate's outward enclosures
for $c$, $|B|^2$ and $R$ to the actual
$\theta=R/(2\sqrt{c|B|^2})$. For every $D=1,\ldots,100$ it
encloses each of the $D$ values $\cos(k\pi/(D+1))$ and verifies
strict separation from that same interval for $\theta$.
Every one of these 5050 comparisons is with an enclosing interval;
no decimal equality test is used. The full receipt records the
minimum gap and the source-certificate hash. It verifies
\[
 \left|\theta-\cos\frac{k\pi}{D+1}\right|>1.95\,10^{-5}
 \quad(1\le k\le D\le100).\tag{RPAC15}
\]
This strict bound proves $H_D\ne0$ throughout those one hundred
cutoffs. Therefore the actual original pair has the singular poles
$(D+2,D,0,\ldots,0)$ and exterior poles
$(D+2,2D+2,\ldots,2D+2)$ for every $1\le D\le100$,
with every constant given by RPAC9--12. For larger cutoffs,
RPAC2 and RPAC7--14 remain the exact complete formulas; a finite
interval check does not assert nonresonance at every integer cutoff.
The arithmetic underlying the outward comparisons is Johansson's
Arb \cite{human:arb2016}; the polynomial recurrence, full pole law
and its original-metric constants have been proved above.
