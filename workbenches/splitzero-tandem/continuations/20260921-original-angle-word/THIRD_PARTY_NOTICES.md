The inherited proof container retains the unchanged **Homological Algebra** chapter by **The Stacks Project Authors**, version [a04446e57ec1fbc252a871afcec7752fb2807b14](https://github.com/stacks/stacks-project/blob/a04446e57ec1fbc252a871afcec7752fb2807b14/homology.tex). Its GNU Free Documentation License applies separately from the surrounding files; the unchanged [licence copy](STACKS_COPYING.txt) and [pinned upstream licence](https://github.com/stacks/stacks-project/blob/a04446e57ec1fbc252a871afcec7752fb2807b14/COPYING) accompany it. No additional restriction is imposed. Building that complete chapter requires its upstream dependencies; retention is not a claim of exhaustive reading.

Original NIST DLMF equation sources and credits remain in the retained source bank. Gamma formulas credit **R. A. Askey and R. Roy**; orthogonal-polynomial formulas credit **T. H. Koornwinder, R. Wong, R. Koekoek and R. F. Swarttouw**, with **W. P. Reinhardt**. Exact formula locations and actual reading coverage are recorded in `SOURCE_READING_USE.md`.

The zero-count estimate retains historical attribution to **J. L. W. V. Jensen**, [Sur un nouvel et important théorème de la théorie des fonctions](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02417878), 1899. The ledger states that the original article was not read in this task; every finite zero-count and harmonic estimate used is proved locally. The selected-column calculation credits **Ming Gu and Stanley C. Eisenstat**, [Efficient Algorithms for Computing a Strong Rank-Revealing QR Factorization](https://doi.org/10.1137/0917055), 1996, as context. Original-author TeX for that paper was not obtained; the edition proves its finite exchange statements directly rather than importing an unread theorem.

Earlier human credits remain unchanged, including **Christian Krattenthaler**, [Advanced Determinant Calculus](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9902004v3), and **Geneviève Dusson, Israel Michael Sigal and Benjamin Stamm**, [arXiv:2105.02058v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.02058v1). No full original paper is newly redistributed. A programme derivation is not represented as an original theorem of its cited sources.
