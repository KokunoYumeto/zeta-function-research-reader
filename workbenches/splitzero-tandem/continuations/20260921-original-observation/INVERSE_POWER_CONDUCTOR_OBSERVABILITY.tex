The late-heat class in LS1 is $-Q_k(0)[y^{-1}]$.
LS13--17 detects that single class. Here we prove that the unchanged
conductor detects an explicitly growing space of inverse powers.
We give a left inverse using the original lower roots, then carry its
bound through the actual minimum metrics. Every conductor coefficient,
root difference and physical factor $i$ remains.

\section{Original objects and the exact rational response}
Keep the simple-quartet domain and complete conductor of KF1--10
and OR1--4, reproduced here where used. Fix odd $k\ge9$, $k\equiv1\bmod4$,
$0<\delta<1/2$, $\gamma>2$ and the unchanged original period.
Set
\[
c=k/2,\quad c'=c-4,\quad q=(k+1)^2,\quad q'=(k-7)^2,\quad
D_0=\sqrt{\delta^2+\gamma^2}.
\]
The roots in the original $S$ coordinate are
\[
\beta_{ab}=c+(2a-k)\delta+i(2b-k)\gamma,\quad 0\le a,b\le k,
\]
and the lower roots are
\[
\eta_{ab}=c'+(2a-k+8)\delta+i(2b-k+8)\gamma,
\quad 0\le a,b\le k-8.
\tag{IK1}
\]
The monic polynomials $\chi,\chi'$ have precisely these roots;
the prime on $\chi'$ denotes the lower polynomial.
On $E=\C[S]/(\chi)$ put $y=(S-c)/i$.
Every upper $y$-root is nonzero, so multiplication by $y$ is invertible.
Let $\mathcal J$ consist of the nonzero actual conductor coefficients
$a_j=a_{rs}$, and put
\[
J=|\mathcal J|,\quad b_j=4+(2r-8)\delta+i(2s-8)\gamma,\quad
E_A(z)=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}a_je^{b_jz},\quad
v=\operatorname{ord}_0E_A,\quad \mu_v=E_A^{(v)}(0).
\tag{IK2}
\]
The shifts are distinct, $\mu_v\ne0$, and $v\le J-1$.
Indeed if the first $J$ moments vanished, the Vandermonde matrix
on the distinct $b_j$ would kill the nonzero vector $(a_j)$.

Define the original conductor value map
\[
C_A:E\longrightarrow\C^{q'},\qquad
(C_A[p])_\eta=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}a_jp(\eta+b_j).
\tag{IK3}
\]
Root addition in IK1--2 puts each $\eta+b_j$ among the upper roots;
therefore IK3 is independent of the representative modulo $\chi$.
The complete original kernel satisfies $K\subset\ker C_A$;
its further invariant columns remain as in KF2 and MR1--10.

For an integer $s\ge1$, put $U_s d=\sum_{n=1}^s d_n[y^{-n}]$,
where $d\in\C^s$. The exact response is
\[
(C_AU_s d)_\eta
=R_d(\eta-c),\qquad
R_d(z)=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\sum_{n=1}^s
 \frac{a_j i^n d_n}{(z+b_j)^n}.
\tag{IK4}
\]
The factor $i^n$ comes from $y^{-n}=i^n(S-c)^{-n}$.
No quotient polynomial or coordinate has been replaced.

\section{Degree bound and injectivity on the inverse-power space}
Let $B_A(z)=\prod_j(z+b_j)$. The function $P_d=B_A^sR_d$
is a polynomial. If $d\ne0$, write
$n_0=\min\{n:d_n\ne0\}$ and $t=\max\{n:d_n\ne0\}$.
At every $z=-b_j$, the rational function $R_d$ has a pole
of exactly order $t$, whose highest coefficient is $a_ji^td_t\ne0$.
At infinity the binomial expansion gives
\[
R_d(z)=i^{n_0}d_{n_0}(-1)^v
 \binom{n_0+v-1}{v}\mu_v z^{-n_0-v}
 +O(z^{-n_0-v-1}).
\tag{IK5}
\]
To verify the leading term, use
$(z+b)^{-n}=\sum_{r\ge0}(-1)^r\binom{n+r-1}{r}b^rz^{-n-r}$.
All moments with $r<v$ vanish. Among terms with exponent
$n_0+v$, each $n>n_0$ has $r<v$ and vanishes too.
The displayed coefficient is nonzero. Hence
\[
\deg P_d=Js-v-n_0\le D_s:=Js-v-1.
\tag{IK6}
\]
In particular the degree estimate retains every shift.

Assume the explicit finite guard
\[
Js-v\le q'.
\tag{IK7}
\]
There are $q'$ distinct lower evaluation points $z_\eta=\eta-c$,
and no $B_A(z_\eta)$ vanishes. If $C_AU_sd=0$, then $P_d$
vanishes at all these points. Its degree is at most $D_s<q'$,
so $P_d=0$. The nonzero pole in IK5 then forces $d=0$.
We have proved
\[
C_AU_s\ \hbox{is injective},\qquad
U_s\C^s\cap K=\{0\},\qquad
1\le s\le\left\lfloor\frac{q'+v}{J}\right\rfloor.
\tag{IK8}
\]
This also proves independence of these actual inverse powers.
It does not replace the full observation kernel by the conductor kernel.

\section{A left inverse in original root differences}
Select exactly $D_s+1=Js-v$ distinct lower roots in lexicographic
$(a,b)$ order, and write their centered values as $z_0,\ldots,z_{D_s}$.
Let $C_s$ be the $(D_s+1)\times s$ matrix in IK4 restricted
to these rows. Define
\[
L_\alpha(z)=\prod_{\ell\ne\alpha}
 \frac{z-z_\ell}{z_\alpha-z_\ell},\qquad
P_w(z)=\sum_{\alpha=0}^{D_s}B_A(z_\alpha)^s
 w_\alpha L_\alpha(z).
\tag{IK9}
\]
This is Lagrange polynomial interpolation; see Temme \cite{Temme}, with the exact nodal differences in IK9. For $w=C_sd$, the polynomial $P_w$ equals $P_d$, by the
degree bound and its values at all the chosen points.

Fix any $j_*\in\mathcal J$ and put
$D_*(w)=\prod_{j\ne j_*}(b_j-b_{j_*}+w)^s$.
This polynomial is nonzero at zero. In the following coefficient extraction use the scalar indeterminate $t$, so the input vector $w$ remains distinct. The exact left inverse is
\[
(W_sw)_n=
\frac1{a_{j_*}i^n}
[t^{s-n}]\frac{P_w(-b_{j_*}+t)}{D_*(t)},
\quad 1\le n\le s,\qquad
W_sC_s=I_s .
\tag{IK10}
\]
To check it without any limit, divide $P_d=B_A^sR_d$ by $D_*$.
At $z=-b_{j_*}+w$ the resulting expression is $w^sR_d$.
Its terms of degree below $s$ are exactly
$\sum_n a_{j_*}i^n d_nw^{s-n}$.
All other summands in IK4 are analytic at this pole and become
divisible by $w^s$. This proves every row of IK10.

For an entirely finite size bound put $R=kD_0$ and
$M=s(J-1)$. Define
\[
h_r=\begin{cases}
\binom{M+r-1}{r}(2\delta)^{-M-r},&M>0,\\
1,&M=0,\ r=0,\\0,&M=0,\ r>0.
\end{cases}
\]
The coefficient bound on $D_*^{-1}$ is $|[w^r]D_*^{-1}|\le h_r$:
expand each $(b_j-b_{j_*}+w)^{-s}$ and use
$|b_j-b_{j_*}|\ge2\delta$.
Every $|z_\alpha+b_j|\le R$ is an actual upper-root modulus,
and every $|z_\alpha-z_\ell|\ge2\delta$. Consequently
\[
|[w^t]L_\alpha(-b_{j_*}+w)|
 \le\binom{D_s}{t}R^{D_s-t}(2\delta)^{-D_s}.
\]
Define the explicit nonnegative number
\[
\mathcal W_s=
\frac{\sqrt{s(D_s+1)}\,R^{Js}}
 {|a_{j_*}|(2\delta)^{D_s}}
 \max_{1\le n\le s}\;
 \sum_{t=0}^{\min(D_s,s-n)}
 \binom{D_s}{t}R^{D_s-t}h_{s-n-t}.
\tag{IK11}
\]
The preceding bounds in IK9--10 give $\|W_s\|_2\le\mathcal W_s$,
by first bounding each entry and then its Frobenius norm.
Thus $\|C_sd\|_2\ge\|d\|_2/\mathcal W_s$.
This is a bound in the specified coefficient and literal value coordinates;
the next step carries it to the original metrics.

\section{Return to the actual attained observation}
At any original cutoff $N$, retain its positive attained metric $G_N$
on $E$, complete observation $\Lambda:E\to B$ with kernel $K$,
\[
Q_N=(\Lambda G_N^{-1}\Lambda^*)^{-1},\qquad
\mathcal L_N=G_N^{-1}\Lambda^*Q_N,\quad
P_B=\mathcal L_N\Lambda .
\]
Let $C_{\rm sel}:E\to\C^{D_s+1}$ consist of the selected conductor rows.
It has full row rank. Indeed the complete $C_A$ has rank $q'$:
$\mathcal T_A$ lowers polynomial degree by exactly $v$ with nonzero
leading coefficient, so it surjects from degree below $q$ onto degree
below $q-v$; this contains the degree-below-$q'$ interpolation space
because $q-q'=16k-48\ge96>v$. Evaluation at the distinct lower roots is onto.
Thus any selected subset of its rows is independent.

Put
\[
\Gamma_{\rm sel}=C_{\rm sel}G_N^{-1}C_{\rm sel}^*,\quad
H_s=U_s^*G_NU_s,\quad
R_N=C_{\rm sel}\mathcal L_N .
\]
Both Grams are positive definite. Since $C_{\rm sel}$ kills $K$,
$C_{\rm sel}=R_N\Lambda$, and direct multiplication gives
$R_NQ_N^{-1}R_N^*=\Gamma_{\rm sel}$.
The minimum over $R_Nb=w$ of $b^*Q_Nb$ is
$w^*\Gamma_{\rm sel}^{-1}w$:
its section is $Q_N^{-1}R_N^*\Gamma_{\rm sel}^{-1}$,
and every other lift has an orthogonal nonnegative correction.
It follows, in the unchanged coefficient space $\C^s$, that
\[
\begin{gathered}
U_s^*\Lambda^*Q_N\Lambda U_s
 \succeq C_s^*\Gamma_{\rm sel}^{-1}C_s
 \succeq\frac{I_s}
 {\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma_{\rm sel})\,\mathcal W_s^2},\\
\boxed{\frac{\|P_BU_sd\|_{G_N}^2}{\|U_sd\|_{G_N}^2}
 \ge
 \frac1{\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma_{\rm sel})\,
          \mathcal W_s^2\,\lambda_{\max}(H_s)}
 >0\qquad(d\ne0).}
\end{gathered}\tag{IK12}
\]
Every matrix here is the original attained matrix or its explicitly
proved restriction or quotient. IK12 is a quantitative finite
observability statement on a space whose permitted dimension grows
as $q'/J$. It is not a uniform positive limiting fraction:
the complete metric factors in its denominator remain.
For $s=1$ it applies to LS1's late-heat line, up to its exact
nonzero scalar $-Q_k(0)$. No individual complex phase or $kq$
allocation is assigned by this positive detection theorem.


For a version exposing its coordinate factors, let
$G_N^{\rm val}=V^{-*}G_NV^{-1}$ be the literal upper-root value metric,
$\kappa_N^{\rm val}=\lambda_{\max}(G_N^{\rm val})/
\lambda_{\min}(G_N^{\rm val})$ and $A_1=\sum_j|a_j|$.
Each conductor row has Euclidean norm at most $A_1$,
so the selected matrix has norm at most $\sqrt{D_s+1}A_1$.
Each inverse-power value has modulus at most $\delta^{-n}$,
because every upper root has centered real part an odd nonzero
multiple of $\delta$. Thus
\[
\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma_{\rm sel})
 \le\frac{(D_s+1)A_1^2}{\lambda_{\min}(G_N^{\rm val})},
\qquad
\lambda_{\max}(H_s)
 \le\lambda_{\max}(G_N^{\rm val})q\sum_{n=1}^s\delta^{-2n}.
\]
Substitution into IK12 gives the explicit original-coordinate bound
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\|P_BU_sd\|_{G_N}^2}{\|U_sd\|_{G_N}^2}
\ge
\left[
 \kappa_N^{\rm val}\,q(D_s+1)A_1^2\mathcal W_s^2
 \sum_{n=1}^s\delta^{-2n}
\right]^{-1}>0.}
\tag{IK13}
\]
For the actual Gamma comparison in KF33--35 and its original
full-source transfer, $\log\kappa_N^{\rm val}=O_h(q\log(q+2))$.
Uniformly over every integer $s$ in IK8, the other factors on the
right of IK13 have logarithms at most $O_A(q\log(q+2))$.
Indeed $Js\le q'+v\le q+80$, $D_s<q'$, and $M=s(J-1)\le q+80$.
All binomial indices in IK11 are at most $2q+160$, so each
binomial is bounded by $2^{2q+160}$; there are at most $q+81$
summands. The powers of $R=kD_0$ have total exponent $O(q)$,
while the powers of the fixed $2\delta$, $|a_{j_*}|^{-1}$,
and the geometric sum contribute at most $O_A(q)$ in logarithm.
This proves a uniform lower bound $\exp[-O_{A,h}(q\log(q+2))]$
on the entire allowed inverse-power space, at all four original cutoffs.
It supplies a finite quantitative receiver for growing inverse powers;
its lower bound is allowed to tend to zero.
