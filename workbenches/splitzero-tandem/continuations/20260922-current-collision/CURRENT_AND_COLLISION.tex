\subsection{1. The original objects and the retained phase}\label{part1-the-original-objects-and-the-retained-phase}

At one literal original cutoff \(N\), retain the complete source metric \(G_N\), observation \(\Lambda\), minimum section \(L_N\), and isometry

\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle Q_N=(\Lambda G_N^{-1}\Lambda^*)^{-1},\qquad

\end{equation*}
 The original multiplication operator and boundary vectors obey 
\begin{equation*}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle \begin{gathered}M=C+\epsilon fe^\dagger,\quad C=C^\dagger,\quad e^\dagger f=0,\quad \|e\|=\|f\|=1,\\ \epsilon>0.\end{gathered}$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 These are the exact \href{https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/zeta-function-research-reader/blob/5c69161ca70ee187f41df0bfe786e8b6eebed422/workbenches/splitzero-tandem/continuations/20260922-current-effective-cutoff/OBSERVED_CURRENT_PLANE.tex\#L90}{OCP5--7} source classes. \begin{equation*}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle \begin{gathered}u=J^\dagger e,\quad v=J^\dagger f,\quad a=\|u\|^2,\quad d=\|v\|^2,\quad r=u^*v=x_r+ib,\\ D=ad-|r|^2>0.\end{gathered}$\end{adjustbox}

\begin{equation*}
\tag{PO1}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle g=u/\sqrt a,\qquad

 The strict positivity is the original complete two-column area certificate; it is not inferred from \(a>0\) alone. The vectors \(g,h\) are orthonormal in the actual observed metric coordinates. The current vanishes on their orthogonal complement and has matrix

\tag{PO2}

For the original observed terminal unit vector \(\widehat y\), or a probe being compared under the same finite constraints, write

 The actual terminal \(\eta=\eta_N\) is the bound in \href{https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/zeta-function-research-reader/blob/c9df443d9e1df000dfbb083943f97197e746eb08/workbenches/splitzero-tandem/branches/identity-absorber-square/OBSERVED_SUPPORT_PROPAGATION.md\#L315}{OSP38--43}, carried from TR10 without changing its domain. \subsection{2. An exact joint phase/overlap region}\label{part1-an-exact-joint-phaseoverlap-region}

Use the orthonormal basis of the same current plane 
\begin{equation*}
\tag{PO4}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle v_0=v/\sqrt d=\frac{r g+\sqrt D h}{\sqrt{ad}},\qquad
n_0=\frac{\sqrt D g-\bar r h}{\sqrt{ad}}.$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 Direct inner products prove orthogonality and unit norms, including every complex conjugation. Put 
\begin{equation*}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle \begin{gathered}A=v_0^*\widehat y,\quad B=n_0^*\widehat y,\quad |A|^2=\rho,\quad |B|^2=\mu-\rho,\\ \overline A B=X+iY.\end{gathered}$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 Multiplying PO2 by the two columns in PO4 gives 
\begin{equation*}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle \begin{gathered}W|_{v_0,n_0}=\\ \begin{pmatrix}-2\epsilon b&i\epsilon\sqrt D\\
-i\epsilon\sqrt D&0\end{pmatrix}.\end{gathered}$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 Consequently 
\begin{equation*}
\tag{PO5}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle j=-2\epsilon b\rho-2\epsilon\sqrt D\,Y,\qquad
X^2+Y^2=\rho(\mu-\rho).$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 This is the exact shared phase, not only a Cauchy bound.

For any proposed real pair \((j,\rho)\), define 
\begin{equation*}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle \begin{gathered}Y(j,\rho)=-\frac{j+2\epsilon b\rho}{2\epsilon\sqrt D},\\ R(j,\rho)=\rho(\mu-\rho)-Y(j,\rho)^2,\end{gathered}$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 
\begin{equation*}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle Z_{\rm mid}(j,\rho)
=\frac{D\mu+(x_r^2-b^2-D)\rho-(b/\epsilon)j}{ad},$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 
\begin{equation*}
\tag{PO6}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle \begin{gathered}Z_{\min}(j,\rho)\quad =Z_{\rm mid}(j,\rho)-\frac{2\sqrt D\,|x_r|}{ad}\sqrt{R(j,\rho)}\\ \hbox{when }R(j,\rho)\ge0.\end{gathered}$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 Then the complete feasible region for the mass and coordinate constraints is 
\begin{equation*}
\tag{PO7}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle \boxed{
0\le\rho\le\mu,\qquad
R(j,\rho)\ge0,\qquad Z_{\min}(j,\rho)\le\eta^2.
}$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 All three conditions are necessary and sufficient.

Here is the full reconstruction proof. Invert PO4 on its plane to obtain \(c_g=(rA+\sqrt D B)/\sqrt{ad}\). Therefore 
\begin{equation*}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle \begin{gathered}|c_g|^2=\quad \frac{D\mu+(|r|^2-D)\rho+2\sqrt D(x_rX+bY)}{ad}\\ =Z_{\rm mid}+\frac{2\sqrt D x_r}{ad}X.\end{gathered}$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 PO5 requires \(X=\pm\sqrt R\); these two possibilities give exactly the two values \(Z_{\rm mid}\pm2\sqrt D|x_r|\sqrt R/(ad)\). If PO7 holds and \(\rho>0\), choose \(A=\sqrt\rho\) and \(B=(X+iY)/\sqrt\rho\), using the sign of \(X\) which gives \(Z_{\min}\). They have the required norms and current, and \(|c_g|^2=Z_{\min}\). The reconstructed value automatically lies between zero and \(\mu\), since PO4 is unitary. Thus no additional lower bound for \(Z_{\min}\) has been silently dropped.

If \(\rho=0\), PO7 forces \(Y=j=0\) and \(R=0\); choose \(A=0\), \(B=\sqrt\mu\). If \(\rho=\mu\), take \(B=0\), \(A=\sqrt\mu\); the current is then \(j=-2\epsilon b\mu\). If \(\mu=0\), the only feasible pair is \((0,0)\). These choices prove sufficiency at every endpoint as well.

To obtain an observed unit vector when \(\mu<1\), add a vector of norm \(\sqrt{1-\mu}\) in the orthogonal complement of the current plane. This complement is nonzero in the actual programme: \href{https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/zeta-function-research-reader/blob/6cf5b3aa8ff395eba6b5512b28d117334da5767b/workbenches/splitzero-tandem/continuations/20260922-cutoff-profile/RETAINED_COMPLETE_PROOF_SOURCES.tex\#L11073}{OCS32's five-orbit rank bound} gives \(\dim B\ge\lfloor q/5\rfloor+\mathbf1_{5\mid k}>2\). In a separate two-dimensional model unit vectors require \(\mu=1\); the phase formulas still hold for vectors of squared norm \(\mu\).

Two useful degenerate geometries follow without dividing by \(x_r\): 
\begin{equation*}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle x_r=0:\quad |c_g|^2=Z_{\rm mid}\quad
\hbox{for both allowed signs of }X;$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 
\begin{equation*}
\tag{PO8}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle r=0:\quad
\boxed{\max\{0,\mu-\eta^2\}\le\rho\le\mu,\qquad
j^2\le4\epsilon^2D\rho(\mu-\rho).}$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 For general \(r\), dropping only the coordinate cap gives the exact filled ellipse \((j+2\epsilon b\rho)^2\le4\epsilon^2D\rho(\mu-\rho)\). With the cap, PO7 retains the necessary extra restriction. It does not replace the actual region by an independently chosen interval for each variable.

\subsection{3. Sharp current-plus-overlap envelopes}\label{part1-sharp-current-plus-overlap-envelopes}

For any real coefficient \(\theta\), the same phase-dependent expression is 
\begin{equation*}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle j+\theta\rho=\widehat y^*(W+\theta P_v)\widehat y,\qquad
P_v=vv^*/d .$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 In the original \(g,h\) frame write 
\begin{equation*}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle W+\theta P_v=
\begin{pmatrix}A_\theta&H_\theta\\\bar H_\theta&B_\theta\end{pmatrix},$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 
\begin{equation*}
\tag{PO9}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle \begin{gathered}A_\theta=-2\epsilon b+\frac{\theta|r|^2}{ad},\quad B_\theta=\frac{\theta D}{ad},\\ H_\theta=\sqrt D\left(\frac{\theta r}{ad}-i\epsilon\right),\\ \delta_\theta=A_\theta-B_\theta.\end{gathered}$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 All phases remain. Exact expansion gives 
\begin{equation*}
\tag{PO10}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle \begin{gathered}\det(W+\theta P_v)=-\epsilon^2D,\quad \Gamma_\theta\quad =\sqrt{\delta_\theta^2+4|H_\theta|^2}\\ =\sqrt{(\theta-2\epsilon b)^2+4\epsilon^2D}>0.\end{gathered}$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 Thus this entire auxiliary Hermitian pencil remains indefinite on the current plane, even though its restriction to a narrow coordinate cap can have one sign. The determinant cancellation follows because \(A_\theta B_\theta\) and \(|H_\theta|^2\) have identical quadratic and mixed \(\theta\) terms.

For \(\sigma\in\{+1,-1\}\), define 
\begin{equation*}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle \begin{gathered}z_\sigma(\theta,\mu)\\ =\min\left\{\eta^2,\frac{\mu}{2}
\left(1+\frac{\sigma\delta_\theta}{\Gamma_\theta}\right)\right\},\end{gathered}$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 
\begin{equation*}
\tag{PO11}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle \begin{gathered}\mathcal M_\sigma(\theta,\mu)\quad =\sigma B_\theta\mu+\sigma\delta_\theta z_\sigma\\ +2|H_\theta|\sqrt{z_\sigma(\mu-z_\sigma)}.\end{gathered}$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 The minimum is nonnegative and at most \(\mu\). The exact sharp bounds are 
\begin{equation*}
\tag{PO12}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle \boxed{
-\mathcal M_-(\theta,\mu)\le j+\theta\rho
\le\mathcal M_+(\theta,\mu).
}$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 The numbers \(\mathcal M_\sigma\) need not be positive under a severe coordinate cap; they are the actual extrema of \(\sigma(j+\theta\rho)\), with their signs retained.

To prove PO12 put \(z=|c_g|^2\), so \(0\le z\le\min(\eta^2,\mu)\). For this fixed \(z\), the maximum of \(\sigma(j+\theta\rho)\) is exactly 
\begin{equation*}
\tag{PO13}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle \sigma B_\theta\mu+\sigma\delta_\theta z
+2|H_\theta|\sqrt{z(\mu-z)}.$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 If \(H_\theta\ne0\), equality is achieved by 
\begin{equation*}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle c_g=\sqrt z,\qquad
c_h=\sigma\,\frac{\overline H_\theta}{|H_\theta|}
\sqrt{\mu-z}.$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 For \(\mu>0\), PO13 is strictly concave on \(0<z<\mu\), and its derivative vanishes uniquely at \(\mu(1+\sigma\delta_\theta/\Gamma_\theta)/2\). Indeed the derivative is \(\sigma\delta_\theta+|H_\theta|(\mu-2z)/\sqrt{z(\mu-z)}\); substitution gives zero, with the sign fixed before squaring. Truncating this maximizer by the cap proves PO11.

If \(H_\theta=0\), PO13 is affine in \(z\). By PO10, \(\delta_\theta\ne0\); hence its maximum is at \(z=0\) or the cap endpoint according to the sign of \(\sigma\delta_\theta\), exactly as PO11 states. This degeneracy can occur only when \(x_r=0\), \(b\ne0\), and \(\theta=\epsilon ad/b\). If \(\mu=0\), every expression is zero. If the chosen \(z\) is zero or \(\mu\), the vanishing amplitude makes its relative phase immaterial. These cases cover every equality and degeneracy scope.

At \(\theta=0\), one has \(\delta_0=-2\epsilon b\), \(|H_0|=\epsilon\sqrt D\), \(\Gamma_0=2\epsilon\sqrt{b^2+D}\). PO11--12 therefore reduce exactly to \href{https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/zeta-function-research-reader/blob/c9df443d9e1df000dfbb083943f97197e746eb08/workbenches/splitzero-tandem/branches/identity-absorber-square/OBSERVED_SUPPORT_PROPAGATION.md\#L402}{OSP48--50}, preserving the signed rank-one correction. For \(\theta\ne0\), their common phase is optimized in PO13; adding independent optimal bounds for \(j\) and \(\rho\) generally cannot attain this value.

\subsection{4. Complete measured-mass interval receiver}\label{part1-complete-measured-mass-interval-receiver}

The original measured-support estimate supplies \(m_-\le\mu\le m_+\), with 
\begin{equation*}
\tag{PO14}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle m_-=\max\{0,(\mathfrak s-E^{\rm supp})/d\},\qquad
m_+=\min\{1,(\mathfrak s+E^{\rm supp})/d\}.$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 The proof and original errors are \href{https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/zeta-function-research-reader/blob/c9df443d9e1df000dfbb083943f97197e746eb08/workbenches/splitzero-tandem/branches/identity-absorber-square/OBSERVED_SUPPORT_PROPAGATION.md\#L325}{OSP39--41}. For a general \(\theta\), replacing \(\mu\) by \(m_+\) in PO12 is not always valid: the maximum can decrease with mass on a narrow coordinate cap.

There is nevertheless an exact finite calculation using at most three candidates. Put \(h_\theta=|H_\theta|\) and \(Z=\eta^2\). For each sign \(\sigma\), start with the two candidates \(\mathcal M_\sigma(\theta,m_-)\) and \(\mathcal M_\sigma(\theta,m_+)\). When \(0<Z\le m_+\), put 
\begin{equation*}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle v_{\min}=\max\{0,m_--Z\},\qquad v_{\max}=m_+-Z.$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 On the active cap \(z=Z\), define 
\begin{equation*}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle f_{\sigma,Z}(v)=\sigma A_\theta Z+\sigma B_\theta v
+2h_\theta\sqrt{Zv}.$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 If \(\sigma B_\theta<0\) and \(h_\theta>0\), set 
\begin{equation*}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle v_*=\min\{v_{\max},\max\{v_{\min},
h_\theta^2Z/B_\theta^2\}\}.$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 Otherwise take the endpoint of \([v_{\min},v_{\max}]\) maximizing this displayed scalar function. This latter case is monotone when \(h_\theta>0\) and affine when \(h_\theta=0\), so its endpoint evaluation is exact. Include \(f_{\sigma,Z}(v_*)\) as the third candidate. Let \(\mathcal U_\sigma(\theta)\) be the largest included candidate. Then 
\begin{equation*}
\tag{PO15}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle \boxed{\begin{gathered}\sup_{\substack{m_-\le\mu\le m_+\\|c_g|^2\le\eta^2}}\\ \sigma(j+\theta\rho)=\mathcal U_\sigma(\theta).\end{gathered}}$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 The supremum is over the precise mass/coordinate information; the remaining observed norm is supplied as in PO7.

Here is a proof that no interior optimizer is missed. After optimizing the phase, put \(z=|c_g|^2\), \(v=|c_h|^2\). The feasible set is \(z,v\ge0\), \(z\le Z\), \(m_-\le z+v\le m_+\). The objective is \(\sigma A_\theta z+\sigma B_\theta v+2h_\theta\sqrt{zv}\). At a differentiable interior stationary point its two partial derivatives would give \(A_\theta B_\theta=h_\theta^2\), contradicting the strict negative determinant PO10. Thus a maximum lies on a boundary. The two mass boundaries give PO11. The boundary \(z=Z\) gives the scalar calculation above. At \(z=0\) or \(v=0\) the function is linear in the other coordinate, so an endpoint lies on one of those previously listed boundaries. If \(Z=0\), the two mass endpoints already suffice. If \(Z>m_+\), that cap cannot bind. If \(h_\theta=0\), the entire objective is affine and its vertices are among the same candidates. Compactness proves attainment.

This prevents an unsupported monotonicity step when the source trace correction has either sign.

\subsection{1. Original objects and source identities}\label{part2-original-objects-and-source-identities}

At a literal original cutoff \(N\in\{q-1,q,2q-1,2q\}\), retain the positive source metric \(G_N\), onto observation \(\Lambda\), and minimum isometry 
\begin{equation*}
\tag{CRX1}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle Q_B=(\Lambda G_N^{-1}\Lambda^*)^{-1},\qquad
L=G_N^{-1}\Lambda^*Q_B,\qquad J=LQ_B^{-1/2}.$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 A dagger on the source uses \(G_N\); a star on the observed space uses the Euclidean coordinates given by this exact isometry. In the original decomposition \(M=C+\epsilon fe^\dagger\), retain \(C=C^\dagger\), the orthonormal source vectors \(e,f\), and \(\epsilon>0\). Put 
\begin{equation*}
\tag{CRX2}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle \begin{gathered}u=J^\dagger e,\quad v=J^\dagger f,\quad a=u^*u,\quad d=v^*v,\quad r=u^*v,\\ D=ad-|r|^2\ge\Theta_N>0,\quad \alpha=1-a.\end{gathered}$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 These are the original \href{https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/zeta-function-research-reader/blob/4ba9285b66af58a6d58fc502a494c1fb45ca5b79/workbenches/splitzero-tandem/continuations/20260922-support-transport/SUPPORT_SPECTRUM_AND_TERMINAL_MASS.md\#L44}{ST5--6 and ST13--20} data, with their finite native guards. The actual terminal-cardinal estimates retain the five-orbit and admitted-period domain specified there. The finite matrix proof below uses the displayed positive-area data and applies throughout that established domain.

Write 
\begin{equation*}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle C_B=J^\dagger CJ=C_B^*,\qquad
M_B(s)=C_B+(\epsilon v+su)u^*,\quad
s_*=-\epsilon r/a,$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 
\begin{equation*}
\tag{CRX3}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle \begin{gathered}b=\epsilon\sqrt D>0,\quad c=\frac{|r|}{\sqrt D},\quad \beta_r=\Im r,\\ M_\theta=M_B(\theta s_*),\quad 0\le\theta\le1.\end{gathered}$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 The symbol \(\beta_r\) denotes the imaginary part of the retained cross-Gram entry. It is not the source leverage called \(\beta\) in NV. The complex parameter \(s\), straight-segment parameter \(\theta\), and spectral coordinate \(\zeta\) have separate roles. The underlying metric is fixed at this particular cutoff throughout the \(s\)-deformation.

The exact collision and rank-one determinant/inverse identities are \href{https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/zeta-function-research-reader/blob/c9df443d9e1df000dfbb083943f97197e746eb08/workbenches/splitzero-tandem/branches/identity-absorber-square/OBSERVED_COLLISION.md\#L128}{OC1--17}. \subsection{2. A singular-value bound from the actual current}\label{part2-a-singular-value-bound-from-the-actual-current}

In the original orthonormal observed frame \(g=u/\sqrt a\), \(h=(v-(r/a)u)/\sqrt{D/a}\), direct multiplication gives 
\begin{equation*}
\tag{CRX4}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle \begin{gathered}W_\theta=i(M_\theta-M_\theta^*)\\ =\begin{pmatrix}-2\epsilon(1-\theta)\beta_r&-ib\\ib&0\end{pmatrix}\\ \hbox{on }\operatorname{span}\{g,h\},\end{gathered}$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 and zero on its orthogonal complement. The same \(C_B\) remains in every full \(M_\theta\); its contribution to this displayed difference cancels by selfadjointness. The two eigenvalues of CRX4 are \(-x_\theta\pm\sqrt{b^2+x_\theta^2}\), where \(x_\theta=\epsilon(1-\theta)\beta_r\). Thus 
\begin{equation*}
\tag{CRX5}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle \begin{gathered}h_\theta:=\tfrac12\|W_\theta\|\quad =\tfrac12\left(\sqrt{b^2+x_\theta^2}+|x_\theta|\right)\\ \le b/2+\epsilon|\beta_r|\le b(1/2+c),\quad h_1=b/2.\end{gathered}$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 The first inequality follows from \(\sqrt{b^2+x^2}\le b+|x|\), proved by squaring the nonnegative sides.

For every unit observed vector \(x\), 
\begin{equation*}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle \Im\langle x,(\zeta I-M_\theta)x\rangle
=\Im\zeta+\tfrac12\langle x,W_\theta x\rangle.$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 Cauchy--Schwarz and the triangle inequality imply 
\begin{equation*}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle \| (\zeta I-M_\theta)x\|
\ge |\Im\zeta|-h_\theta.$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 If this right side is positive, the square finite matrix is injective and therefore invertible. Taking the infimum over all unit \(x\) proves 
\begin{equation*}
\tag{CRX6}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle \boxed{\|G_\theta(\zeta)\|\le\frac1{|\Im\zeta|-h_\theta},
\qquad G_\theta=(\zeta I-M_\theta)^{-1}.}$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 The estimate holds for every real part of \(\zeta\). It uses neither normality nor a norm bound on \(C_B\). In particular a large or noncommuting \(C_B\) has not been deleted from the inverse.

\subsection{3. Evaluated collision error and marked resolvent receivers}\label{part2-evaluated-collision-error-and-marked-resolvent-receivers}

Retain a positive exterior margin \(\varrho>0\) in the original spectral units: 
\begin{equation*}
\tag{CRX7}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle |\Im\zeta|\ge(1/2+\varrho)b,\qquad
c\le\varrho/4.$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 Then CRX6 applies to the whole segment. The exact inverse identity, obtained by multiplying both inverses into their defining matrices, is 
\begin{equation*}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle G_1-G_0=s_*G_1uu^*G_0.$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 Since \(|s_*|\|uu^*\|=\epsilon|r|=bc\), CRX5--7 give 
\begin{equation*}
\tag{CRX8}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle \boxed{
b\|G_1-G_0\|
\le\frac{c}{\varrho(\varrho-c)}
\le\frac{4c}{3\varrho^2}.
}$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 The collision inverse has denominator at least \(b\varrho\); the native inverse has denominator at least \(b(\varrho-c)\). Both denominators, and the physical spectral coordinate, remain in the first bound. For any two original marked vectors \(x,y\), CRX8 gives the explicit receiver 
\begin{equation*}
\tag{CRX9}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle |x^*(G_1-G_0)y|
\le\|x\|\,\|y\|\frac{c}{b\varrho(\varrho-c)}.$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 There is no identification of these two vector phases. The sharper exact ordered-pair identity in OC15 remains applicable when the two actual scalar factors are available.

Let \(p_\theta(\zeta)=\det(\zeta I-M_\theta)\), and put \(m_0=u^*G_0u\). Multilinearity in the columns gives \(p_\theta/p_0=1-\theta s_*m_0\): all terms taking two rank-one perturbation columns vanish. The actual scalar satisfies 
\begin{equation*}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle |s_*m_0|\le\epsilon|r|\|G_0\|
\le\frac{c}{\varrho-c}\le\frac13.$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 The logarithm based at \(\theta=0\) is therefore the absolutely convergent series \(-\sum_{j\ge1}(\theta s_*m_0)^j/j\) along this entire original deformation segment. It has no winding ambiguity or determinant zero there. Summing absolute values and using \(1/j\le1\) proves 
\begin{equation*}
\tag{CRX10}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle \boxed{\begin{gathered}\left|\operatorname{Log}\frac{p_1(\zeta)}{p_0(\zeta)}\right|\\ \le\frac{c}{\varrho-2c}\le\frac{2c}{\varrho}.\end{gathered}}$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 Both the real logarithmic-volume error and the continuously followed determinant-phase error obey CRX10. This is a quantitative evaluation of the collision transfer error; it is not an evaluation of either determinant separately.

\subsection{4. Native source bounds and all four cutoffs}\label{part2-native-source-bounds-and-all-four-cutoffs}

The contraction \(0\prec U^*U\preceq I_2\) implies \(|r|^2\le(1-a)(1-d)\le\alpha\), by the determinant of its positive semidefinite complement. Consequently 
\begin{equation*}
\tag{CRX11}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle \boxed{c_N\le\sqrt{\alpha_N/\Theta_N}.}$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 This gives a finite sufficient check for CRX7: require \(\sqrt{\alpha_N/\Theta_N}\le\varrho_N/4\), using the already defined original finite quantities. The retained native estimates \(\alpha_N\le\exp[-2q\psi((N+1-q)/q)+O_{h,\varpi}(k\log(q+2))]\) and \(-\log\Theta_N=O_{h,\varpi}(k\log(q+2))\) yield the evaluated transfer rate \(c_N\le\exp[-q\psi((N+1-q)/q)+O_{h,\varpi}(k\log(q+2))]\). The effective bounds are CRX8--11 themselves; this asymptotic does not replace any finite guard.

At the four actual cutoffs choose exterior coordinates \(\zeta_N\) satisfying CRX7 separately, or use a single physical \(\zeta\) whose imaginary part meets all four inequalities. Define the original signed return on these scalar logarithmic transfers by \(\mathcal R f=f_{q-1}+f_q-f_{2q-1}-f_{2q}\). Then 
\begin{equation*}
\tag{CRX12}
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}$\displaystyle \boxed{\begin{gathered}\left|\mathcal R\operatorname{Log}\frac{p_{*,N}(\zeta_N)}{p_{0,N}(\zeta_N)}\right|\\ \le\sum_{N\in\{q-1,q,2q-1,2q\}}\quad \frac{c_N}{\varrho_N-2c_N}\\ \le\sum_N\frac{2}{\varrho_N}\sqrt{\frac{\alpha_N}{\Theta_N}}.\end{gathered}}$\end{adjustbox}
\end{equation*}
 This follows by applying the four signs and then the triangle inequality. No pair of cutoff metrics, source columns, or source functions has been identified. Each \(p_{*,N}\) uses its own collision \(s_{*,N}\) and keeps its entire original \(C_{B,N}\).

The guard in CRX7 specifies exactly the exterior region proved here. Frequencies failing that guard remain a separate part of the original phase calculation. In particular CRX12 is not integrated over an unbounded interval with a frequency-independent error, and the original interior frequency contribution is not discarded. The calculation establishes an exponentially controlled full-operator collision transfer on its stated exterior region. The actual native marked phase remains an arithmetic datum.
