\begin{abstract}
The boundary direction of the split-zero calculation remains substantive after withdrawing the implication from supported-zero invertibility to a Riemann-hypothesis contradiction. We distinguish inverse-monoid regular inversion from support localization, and calculate the exact forcing term introduced when regular reciprocals are substituted into the finite zero dynamics. For absolutely continuous labelled trajectories, this forcing measures collision residence; it vanishes for an instantaneous collision but detects every persistent repeated-root cluster. A finite Duhamel formula returns these trajectories to the unchanged coefficient heat equation.

For a genuine analytic backward-heat collision of multiplicity $m$, we independently calculate the pole and finite part of the complete local inverse-gap energy, with the surrounding analytic unit retained. The pole is universal; the finite part depends on the second logarithmic derivative of that unit. In the original Rodgers--Tao normalization this produces an explicit completed-zeta derivative expression, without asserting that such a collision occurs at time zero. The collision forcing is mapped into the actual cotangent trace radical when its multiplicity is at least three, with a nonzero perfect-residue value. We also determine the minimum residue-jet order needed to recover the energy finite part: a double collision requires one more infinitesimal order than its own perfect local residue pairing. The new repository's nonzero filtered holonomy residue, normalization/Tor sequence, class-field character and complete endpoint-averaging correction are then compared at their actual maps. A trace-zero residue is retained as a nonzero algebra map; the positive averaged endpoint is not substituted for the full Weil form.
\end{abstract}

\section{Regular inversion is an internal operation with a changing local identity}
Let $L$ be a bounded distributive lattice, and use the original full-support carrier
\[
 G_L(\C)=\{(0,a):a\in L\}\cup\{(z,1):z\in\C\},
 \quad \tau=(0,0),\quad e=(0,1).
\]
Addition takes the amplitude sum and support join; multiplication takes the amplitude product and support meet. The unit is $(1,1)$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:inverse}
The multiplicative monoid has the unique regular inverse
\[
 \rinv{(z,1)}=(z^{-1},1)\quad(z\ne0),
 \qquad \rinv{(0,a)}=(0,a).
\]
It satisfies $xx^{\langle-1\rangle}x=x$ and
$x^{\langle-1\rangle}xx^{\langle-1\rangle}=x^{\langle-1\rangle}$.
For the supported zero, $e\rinv e=e$, not $1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a nonzero amplitude the two equations force the ordinary reciprocal and top support. For $x=(0,a)$ and a zero-label candidate $(0,b)$, the equations give $a\wedge b=a$ and $a\wedge b=b$, hence $a=b$. A nonzero-amplitude candidate cannot satisfy its second equation because the product through $x$ has zero amplitude. Existence follows immediately from the displayed formulas. This also proves uniqueness and includes $\tau$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The original zero object and its two adjunctions}
The source's singlet algebra is $A=\{\tau,e\}$, with its own multiplicative unit $1_A=e$. Thus $e^{-1}=e$ is already an ordinary unit identity in $A$. Its inclusion into $G(\C)$ is not unital with respect to $1_{G(\C)}$, whereas the retraction $r(x)=ex$ is unital as a map into $A$. This is ZH1--3, not a new localization convention \citep{ZH}.

For an arbitrary $G(\C)$-semimodule $M$, the complete zero semilattice is $L_M=eM$. The maps
\[
 p_M:M\longrightarrow L_M,\quad m\longmapsto em,
 \qquad i_M:L_M\hookrightarrow M
\]
satisfy $p_Mi_M=I$, $i_Mp_M=e\cdot(-)$. If $J$ embeds bottomed join-semilattices as modules with every supported scalar acting identically, then
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_S(M,J L)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\vee,\bot}(eM,L),
 \quad
 \operatorname{Hom}_S(J L,M)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\vee,\bot}(L,eM).
\]
For the first identity, $f(m)=ef(m)=f(em)$; for the second, $h(\ell)=h(e\ell)=eh(\ell)$. These equations construct both inverse factorizations and both natural maps. Every support label remains. The earlier support-localization argument addresses a different operation; it does not exhaust these internal zero-object and inverse-monoid structures.

This operation requires no localization. The different localization $G_L(\C)[e^{-1}]\cong L$ identifies every supported amplitude with the local unit. Its arithmetic ring base change is zero. The present calculation instead uses the regular inverse on the unchanged carrier and retains the defect of its local identity. On top-supported real amplitudes define
\[
 d(a)=1-a\rinv a.
\]
Here subtraction is the ring subtraction in that supported fibre, so its zero is $e$. Under arithmetic reflection, $d(a)$ is the indicator of $a=0$. It is not the indicator of external absence.

\subsection{The exact Rodgers--Tao potential discrepancy}
For positive $a$, the potential in \citet[\S7, Eq.~(62)]{RT} is
\[
 V(a)=a^{-2}+2a-3=\frac{(a-1)^2(2a+1)}{a^2}.
\]
Their use has a nonzero gap and does not assign a finite value at $a=0$. Set
\[
 V_{\rm exp}(a)=(\rinv a)^2+2a-3,
 \qquad V_{\rm fact}(a)=(a-1)^2(2a+1)(\rinv a)^2.
\]
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:potential}
On the unchanged top-supported carrier, including $a=e$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pot}
 V_{\rm exp}-V_{\rm fact}=(2a-3)d(a)=-3d(a).
\end{equation}
Its arithmetic values at $a=0$ are $-3$, $0$, and $-3$, respectively.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $b=\rinv a$. The identities $a^2b=a$, $ab^2=b$ give
$a^2b^2=ab$ and $a^3b^2=a$. Expanding the factored expression gives
$2a-3ab+b^2$. Its difference from $b^2+2a-3$ is $-3(1-ab)$.
Also $a(1-ab)=0$ in the supported amplitude ring. This proves every formula including the supported-zero specialization.
\end{proof}

The same algebraic rational function on the punctured amplitude line has two different regular-inverse extensions. Their discrepancy is the explicit boundary indicator, not an omitted numerical error. Neither extension is the lower-semicontinuous extended value of the original positive potential, which is $+\infty$ at zero. Point assignment alone also does not change an almost-everywhere differential equation; the next section states precisely when dynamics do change.

\section{The complete contact forcing in zero dynamics}
For a real or complex scalar, write
\[
 \iota(a)=\begin{cases}a^{-1},&a\ne0,\\0,&a=0.\end{cases}
\]
This is the arithmetic amplitude of the preceding regular inverse. Let $x_i(t)$ be absolutely continuous labelled functions on a real time interval. Suppose that almost everywhere
\begin{equation}\label{eq:totalode}
 \dot x_i=2\sum_{j\ne i}\iota(x_i-x_j).
\end{equation}
This definition retains each label even at coincidence. Set
\[
 P(t,z)=\prod_{i=1}^n(z-x_i(t)),\qquad
 P_{ij}(t,z)=\prod_{k\notin\{i,j\}}(z-x_k(t)).
\]
\begin{theorem}[Complete coefficient defect]\label{thm:contact}
The coefficients of $P$ are absolutely continuous and obey
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contact}
 (\partial_t+\partial_z^2)P
 =\mathcal D(t,z):=
 2\sum_{i<j}\mathbf1_{\{x_i(t)=x_j(t)\}}P_{ij}(t,z)
\end{equation}
almost everywhere, hence also as distributions in time. At each time, if the distinct cluster centres are $c_1,\ldots,c_r$ with multiplicities $m_1,\ldots,m_r$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clusters}
 \mathcal D=
 \sum_{\ell:m_\ell\ge2}
 m_\ell(m_\ell-1)(z-c_\ell)^{m_\ell-2}
 \prod_{j\ne\ell}(z-c_j)^{m_j}.
\end{equation}
This polynomial vanishes if and only if every multiplicity is one.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Products of finitely many bounded absolutely continuous functions on a compact time interval are absolutely continuous. With $P_i=\prod_{j\ne i}(z-x_j)$, their chain rule gives
\[
 P_t=-2\sum_{i<j}\iota(x_i-x_j)(P_i-P_j).
\]
The exact polynomial identity $P_i-P_j=(x_i-x_j)P_{ij}$ changes this to
$-2\sum_{i<j}(1-\mathbf1_{x_i=x_j})P_{ij}$. On the other hand,
$P_{zz}=2\sum_{i<j}P_{ij}$, proving \eqref{eq:contact}. Grouping the coincident pairs proves \eqref{eq:clusters}.

To see that the cluster summands cannot cancel, fix a centre with $m_\ell\ge2$ and take the coefficient of order $m_\ell-2$ at $z=c_\ell$. Its own summand is
\[
 m_\ell(m_\ell-1)\prod_{j\ne\ell}(c_\ell-c_j)^{m_j}\ne0,
\]
while every other summand vanishes to order at least $m_\ell$ there. The converse is immediate when there are no repeated centres.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Residence, rather than an assigned value at one time]\label{cor:residence}
A trajectory satisfying \eqref{eq:totalode} obeys the original coefficient heat equation if and only if its collision times have Lebesgue measure zero. An isolated collision is therefore different from a positive-time interval of coincident roots.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:contact} and the pointwise noncancellation just proved show that its distributional defect is zero exactly when there is no cluster almost everywhere. Continuity of $x_i$ makes every collision set measurable. Since there are finitely many coefficients, vanishing of their locally integrable distributions is exactly almost-everywhere vanishing.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Every delay is a different totalized solution]\label{ex:delay}
On $t\ge0$, for any $\theta\in[0,\infty)$, the two labelled functions
\[
 x_\pm(t)=\pm\sqrt{2(t-\theta)_+}
\]
are absolutely continuous, have the same double-zero initial data, and satisfy \eqref{eq:totalode} almost everywhere. The everywhere-stationary pair is another such solution. Their polynomial for finite $\theta$ is
\[
 P_\theta(t,z)=z^2-2(t-\theta)_+,
 \qquad (\partial_t+\partial_z^2)P_\theta
 =2\mathbf1_{[0,\theta)}(t).
\]
No Dirac mass is present in this first derivative because $P_\theta$ is continuous. The original heat solution is $P_0=z^2-2t$; its square-root derivative is integrable at zero even though it is not finite there. The totalized equation alone loses uniqueness, whereas requiring the displayed defect to vanish selects immediate heat separation.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[Exact return to the original coefficient flow]\label{prop:duhamel}
On polynomials of degree at most $n$, set $E_t=e^{-t\partial_z^2}$, a finite sum. Every trajectory in Theorem~\ref{thm:contact} satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:duhamel}
 P(t)=E_{t-t_0}P(t_0)+
 \int_{t_0}^t E_{t-s}\mathcal D(s)\,ds.
\end{equation}
Consequently subtracting the full integral returns the actual heat solution. For Example~\ref{ex:delay}, the removed term is precisely $2\min(t,\theta)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Differentiate $E_{-(t-t_0)}P(t)$ coefficientwise almost everywhere and apply \eqref{eq:contact}. All matrices and integrals are finite dimensional, and their coefficients are locally integrable. Integrate and multiply by $E_{t-t_0}$. For the example, $E_t$ fixes constants, so the integral is $2\min(t,\theta)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The contact forcing has a precise cotangent receiver}
At one of the repeated cluster centres in \eqref{eq:clusters}, use the original local coordinate $x=z-c$ and put
\[
 P=x^mU(x),\qquad U(0)\ne0,\qquad
 A_c=\C[x]/(x^m),\qquad
 q_c=m(m-1)x^{m-2}U(x)\pmod{x^m}.
\]
Every other cluster summand vanishes modulo $x^m$, so $q_c$ is the actual localization of the full forcing polynomial. Give $A_c$ its perfect residue functional
\[
 \lambda_U(f)=\Res_{x=0}\frac{f(x)}{x^mU(x)}\,dx.
\]
The unit $U$ is not replaced by its constant coefficient.
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:contactcotangent}
The localized contact forcing is nonzero and has the exact value
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contactresidue}
 \lambda_U(xq_c)=m(m-1).
\end{equation}
For $m\ge3$ it belongs to
\[
 \operatorname{Ann}_{A_c}(P')=H^{-1}(L_{A_c/\C})=(x),
\]
so its regular multiplication trace, and its trace pairing with every element of $A_c$, vanish. It nevertheless has the nonzero pairing \eqref{eq:contactresidue} with the class of $x$ in $H^0(L_{A_c/\C})$.
For $m=2$ it is a unit multiple of $2$ and its regular trace is $4U(0)$; it does not lie in that radical.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The coefficient of $x^{m-2}$ in $q_c$ is $m(m-1)U(0)\ne0$. In its residue with $x$, the whole $U$ cancels exactly and leaves $m(m-1)\Res dx/x$, proving \eqref{eq:contactresidue}. Modulo $x^m$, the derivative is $P'=mx^{m-1}U(0)$, so its annihilator is $(x)$ in characteristic zero. The hypersurface cotangent complex is $[A_c\xrightarrow{P'}A_c\,dx]$, in degrees $-1,0$; its kernel and cokernel have the residue pairing induced by $\lambda_U$, as in RD27--28a. If $m\ge3$, $q_c\in(x)$, and the trace of multiplication by any element of $(x)$ is zero in the complete triangular $m$-dimensional basis. The cokernel class of $x$ is nonzero for $m\ge3$, and the residue computation proves its stated value. For $m=2$, $q_c(0)=2U(0)$, so its multiplication trace is twice that value. All conclusions follow on the full local algebra, not on its reduced quotient.
\end{proof}

This gives an explicit bridge between the new heat forcing and the newly published cotangent/trace result. It does not say that all collision defects have zero arithmetic trace: the double-root case is explicitly different.

The dynamics in this section is a finite comparison, not a truncation of the infinite Rodgers--Tao principal-value sum. For a genuine finite cluster in the entire function, its remaining roots enter through the full analytic unit, retained next.

\section{Genuine analytic collisions: pole, finite part, and environment}
Let $F(t,z)$ be jointly holomorphic near $(0,x_0)$, of real type for real $t,z$, satisfying $F_t=-F_{zz}$ and
\[
 F(0,x_0+x)=x^m u(x),\qquad m\ge2,\quad u(0)\ne0.
\]
Write
\[
 a=\frac{u'(0)}{u(0)},\quad b=\frac{u''(0)}{2u(0)},\quad
 \kappa=(\log u)''(0)=2b-a^2.
\]
The logarithmic derivatives are branch-independent and retain the actual unit. The same argument works after shifting any real collision time to zero.

Define the monic heat-Hermite polynomials
\[
 \HH_m(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}
 \frac{(-1)^j m!}{j!(m-2j)!}X^{m-2j}.
\]
They obey $\HH_m'=m\HH_{m-1}$,
$\HH_{m+1}=X\HH_m-2m\HH_{m-1}$ and
$2\HH_m''-X\HH_m'+m\HH_m=0$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:hermite}
The roots $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_m$ of $\HH_m$ are real and simple, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hermitesum}
 \sum_{i\ne j}\frac1{(\lambda_i-\lambda_j)^2}
 =\frac{m(m-1)}8.
\end{equation}
Their full discriminant is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hermitedisc}
 \prod_{i<j}(\lambda_i-\lambda_j)^2
 =2^{m(m-1)/2}\prod_{j=1}^m j^j.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Rodrigues' formula $\HH_m=(-2)^m e^{X^2/4}\partial_X^m e^{-X^2/4}$ follows by differentiating, or by its generating function $e^{XT-T^2}$. Integration by parts proves orthogonality to every lower-degree polynomial for the positive Gaussian weight. If it had fewer than $m$ sign-changing real roots, multiplying the linear factors at its real sign changes would produce a lower-degree polynomial whose product with $\HH_m$ had one strict sign, contradicting orthogonality. Thus all roots are real and simple.

At a root, logarithmic differentiation of the product and the differential equation give
$S_i:=\sum_{j\ne i}(\lambda_i-\lambda_j)^{-1}=\lambda_i/4$.
In $\sum_iS_i^2$, the cross terms on every unordered triple cancel by
\[
 \frac1{(x-y)(x-z)}+\frac1{(y-x)(y-z)}
 +\frac1{(z-x)(z-y)}=0.
\]
The $X^{m-2}$ coefficient of $\HH_m$ gives
$\sum_i\lambda_i^2=2m(m-1)$. This proves \eqref{eq:Hermitesum}.
For the discriminant, use $\HH_m'=m\HH_{m-1}$ and evaluate the recurrence at the roots of $\HH_{m-1}$. Successive resultants give $m^m\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}(-2j)^j$; the discriminant sign cancels $(-1)^{m(m-1)/2}$, giving \eqref{eq:Hermitedisc}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Complete local-energy expansion]\label{thm:finitepart}
For sufficiently small $t>0$, the $m$ roots in the isolated collision cluster are real and simple. They can be labelled so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:roots}
 x_i(t)=x_0+\lambda_i\sqrt t+2at+
 \kappa\lambda_i t^{3/2}+O(t^2).
\end{equation}
The ordered-pair cluster energy has the expansion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy}
 \mathcal E_{\rm cl}(t):=
 \sum_{i\ne j}\frac1{(x_i(t)-x_j(t))^2}
 =\frac{m(m-1)}{8t}
 -\frac{m(m-1)}4\kappa+O(t).
\end{equation}
Its discriminant satisfies, with $\nu=m(m-1)/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:disc}
 \mathcal D_{\rm cl}(t)=
 \left(2^\nu\prod_{j=1}^m j^j\right)t^\nu
 \bigl(1+2\nu\kappa t+O(t^2)\bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $t=\epsilon^2$, $z=x_0+\epsilon X$. The PDE Taylor coefficients are determined by
$\partial_t^jF=(-1)^j\partial_z^{2j}F$. The vanished first $m$ spatial coefficients therefore ensure that the following expression is holomorphic in $\epsilon$ and $X$ near each bounded $X$-set:
\[
 \frac{F(\epsilon^2,x_0+\epsilon X)}{u(0)\epsilon^m}
 =\HH_m(X)+a\epsilon\HH_{m+1}(X)
 +b\epsilon^2\HH_{m+2}(X)+O(\epsilon^3).
\]
This is a local expansion of the original analytic solution; its remainder follows from joint holomorphy, not from applying an infinite differential series outside its domain.

At each simple $\lambda_i$, the implicit function theorem gives a unique real-analytic root $X_i(\epsilon)$. Use
\[
 \HH_{m+1}(\lambda_i)=-2\HH_m'(\lambda_i),\quad
 \HH_{m+1}'(\lambda_i)=0,\quad
 \HH_{m+2}(\lambda_i)=-2\lambda_i\HH_m'(\lambda_i),\quad
 \frac{\HH_m''(\lambda_i)}{\HH_m'(\lambda_i)}=\frac{\lambda_i}{2}.
\]
The first two implicit coefficients are $2a$ and $(2b-a^2)\lambda_i$, proving \eqref{eq:roots}. It also proves reality and simplicity for positive $t$.

Every difference is
$(\lambda_i-\lambda_j)\sqrt t(1+\kappa t)+O(t^2)$.
Squaring and inverting, then using \eqref{eq:Hermitesum}, proves the pole and constant term in \eqref{eq:energy}. The preliminary error is $O(\sqrt t)$; it improves to $O(t)$ because the energy is a symmetric rational function of the roots and hence a meromorphic function of the analytic Weierstrass coefficients. It has at most a simple pole by the already proved estimate. Similarly the discriminant is analytic in $t$; its product expansion and \eqref{eq:Hermitedisc} give \eqref{eq:disc} and the displayed integer-power remainder.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The complete environment term]\label{prop:environment}
Factor the original solution on the cluster disk as
$F(t,z)=U(t,z)\prod_i(z-x_i(t))$, where $U$ is the nonvanishing analytic Weierstrass unit. Put $g(t,z)=\partial_z\log U(t,z)$. Then
\begin{align}
 \dot x_i&=2\sum_{j\ne i}\frac1{x_i-x_j}+2g(t,x_i),\label{eq:envode}\\
 \partial_t\log\mathcal D_{\rm cl}
 &=4\mathcal E_{\rm cl}
 +4\sum_{i<j}\frac{g(t,x_i)-g(t,x_j)}{x_i-x_j}.\label{eq:env}
\end{align}
The second term in \eqref{eq:env} tends to $4\nu\kappa$. It is essential to the finite part in \eqref{eq:energy}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At a simple root, $\dot x_i=F_{zz}/F_z$. Differentiate the displayed factorization to obtain \eqref{eq:envode}. Also
$\partial_{x_i}\log\mathcal D_{\rm cl}=2\sum_{j\ne i}(x_i-x_j)^{-1}$.
The triple cancellation in Lemma~\ref{lem:hermite} gives the first term of \eqref{eq:env}, and symmetrizing $\sum_i g(t,x_i)\sum_{j\ne i}(x_i-x_j)^{-1}$ gives the second. Finally the divided differences tend to $\partial_zg(0,x_0)=\kappa$. All contributions from outside the chosen cluster are in $U$; none were omitted to obtain a finite subsystem.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The finite part is not determined by the collision algebra}
For any real $b$, take the exact polynomial heat solution
\[
 F_b(t,z)=z^2-2t+b(z^4-12tz^2+12t^2).
\]
Its initial local algebra at zero is $\C[z]/(z^2)$ for every $b$, and its two cluster roots have the same leading $\pm\sqrt{2t}$ and the same local interchange. Yet $u(z)=1+bz^2$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oppositefp}
 \FP_{t=0}\mathcal E_{{\rm cl},b}(t)=-b.
\end{equation}
In particular the two signs $b=1,-1$ have identical collision lengths, leading discriminant residue and local root monodromy, but opposite finite parts. They are finite exact controls, not xi evaluations. This identifies the additional data to retain: the actual local unit and not only its support or reduced zero algebra.

\subsection{The completed-zeta derivative receiver}
If the actual $H_0$ in \eqref{eq:dbn} has a real zero $x_0$ of multiplicity $m\ge2$, put
$\rho_0=1/2+ix_0/2$ and $v(s)=\xi(s)/(s-\rho_0)^m$. The local unit is
\[
 u(x)=\frac{(i/2)^m}{8}v(\rho_0+ix/2),
 \qquad \kappa=-\frac14(\log v)''(\rho_0).
\]
Consequently the exact finite-part receiver is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xifp}
 \FP_{t=0}\mathcal E_{\rm cl}(t)
 =\frac{m(m-1)}{16}
 \left[
 \frac{2\xi^{(m+2)}(\rho_0)}{(m+1)(m+2)\xi^{(m)}(\rho_0)}
 -\left(\frac{\xi^{(m+1)}(\rho_0)}{(m+1)\xi^{(m)}(\rho_0)}\right)^2
 \right].
\end{equation}
No such multiple zero at time zero is asserted. This formula states which original arithmetic derivatives, including the full unit, decide the finite part if the collision exists. A common scalar multiplying $\xi$ cancels in these ratios; the affine factor $i/2$ is retained and produces the coefficient $1/16$.

For an entire function of genus at most one with real zeros, after removing a zero of multiplicity $m$ at $x_0$, the Hadamard product gives
\[
 (\log u)''(0)=-\sum_{\lambda\ne x_0}
 \frac{m_\lambda}{(x_0-\lambda)^2}\le0.
\]
The linear exponential factor has zero second logarithmic derivative and the series converges absolutely. This applies to the usual order-one xi product under RH. Hence a strictly negative value in \eqref{eq:xifp}, if established at an actual such collision, would detect arithmetic information not present in the collision algebra alone. We have not assigned that value a sign or presumed the collision. The source's general $\Lambda_{\rm dBN}\ge0$ theorem is unchanged.

\subsection{How many perfect-residue jets determine the finite part?}
The current RD duality theorem provides more than the multiplication trace. Its exact information content for the collision energy can also be calculated. In the same original spatial coordinate, define, for each $n\ge1$,
\[
 A_n=\C[x]/(x^n),\qquad
 \lambda_n(f)=\Res_{x=0}\frac{f(x)}{x^nu(x)}\,dx.
\]
For $n=m$ this is the raw local residue of the actual collision germ $x^mu(x)$, before any Jacobian multiplier. The pairing $\lambda_n(fg)$ is perfect: its matrix is anti-triangular with nonzero anti-diagonal $1/u(0)$.
\begin{theorem}[A three-coefficient residue receiver]\label{thm:residuejet}
For every $n\ge3$, put
\[
 L_0=\lambda_n(x^{n-1}),\quad
 L_1=\lambda_n(x^{n-2}),\quad
 L_2=\lambda_n(x^{n-3}).
\]
Then $L_0\ne0$ and the actual multiplicity-$m$ collision finite part is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residueFP}
 \FP\mathcal E_{\rm cl}
 =\frac{m(m-1)}4
 \left[\frac{2L_2}{L_0}-\left(\frac{L_1}{L_0}\right)^2\right].
\end{equation}
For $m\ge3$, choose $n=m$: the original full residue pairing suffices. For a double collision, its original $A_2$ and even its complete perfect residue pairing need not determine the finite part. The first larger residue object $A_3$ does determine it, with its actual quotient and kernel retained.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The reciprocal unit has the exact expansion
\[
 u(x)^{-1}=u(0)^{-1}\bigl[1-ax+(a^2-b)x^2+O(x^3)\bigr].
\]
Hence $L_0=1/u(0)$, $L_1=-a/u(0)$ and $L_2=(a^2-b)/u(0)$. Substitution in \eqref{eq:energy} proves \eqref{eq:residueFP}.

For $m=2$, the examples $u_b=1+bx^2$ have the same functional on $A_2$: $\lambda_2(1)=0$, $\lambda_2(x)=1$. Their whole residue Gram is therefore the same matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, but \eqref{eq:oppositefp} gives every value $-b$. Thus the full perfect pairing, not just its regular trace, is insufficient at that order. On $A_3$ the additional coefficient is $\lambda_3(1)=-b$, while $\lambda_3(x)=0$ and $\lambda_3(x^2)=1$, giving the correct value from \eqref{eq:residueFP}.
\end{proof}

The comparison is the explicit sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow\C x^2\longrightarrow A_3
 \xrightarrow{\pi}A_2\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \lambda_2(f)=\lambda_3(x\widetilde f).
\]
It is independent of the lift $\widetilde f$, since $x\ker\pi=0$ in $A_3$. The missing value $\lambda_3(1)$ is retained rather than inferred from $\lambda_2$. For a genuine double zeta zero, $A_3$ here is an explicitly added analytic thickening; it is not asserted to be an extra actual zero multiplicity or the unchanged theta packet. This locates the first additional local data required by the energy receiver.
