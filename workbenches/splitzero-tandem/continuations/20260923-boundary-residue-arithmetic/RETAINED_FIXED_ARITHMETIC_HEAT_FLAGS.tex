arithmetic Cauchy tests. It evaluates the complete next trace
coefficient, the exact change of basis and local determinant,
and the compensating matrix retained in the full arithmetic formula.
All original units, coordinates, support labels and pole locations
are kept. The complete preceding proofs HC, HH, HD, HF and HL are
included as the two retained LaTeX sources in this package.
HC and HH are already published in
\href{https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/zeta-function-research-reader/tree/3c022a0adde0a6aa3d8fc8e43ef42795d88e44b0/workbenches/splitzero-tandem/continuations/20260923-supported-zero-collision-holonomy}{the pinned collision-holonomy edition}.

\section{Fixed arithmetic tests on every original heat flag}
\label{sec:fixed-arithmetic-heat-flags}

The preceding flag calculation uses specified coefficient lifts in the
original algebra. Here the tests are fixed functions of the original
arithmetic variable. Their higher Taylor coefficients are retained.
This constructs the exact map from those tests into the entire original
collision algebra, and evaluates its determinant and the next trace
coefficient. The heat convention is Brad Rodgers and Terence Tao,
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.05914v5}{\emph{The de Bruijn--Newman
constant is non-negative}}, author equations \texttt{hoz},
\texttt{htdef}, and \texttt{sas}. The full reflected arithmetic trace
and support coordinates are those of
\href{https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/zeta-function-research-reader/blob/5a89872598df0902b7c1393cf8e4692ca3010a95/workbenches/splitzero-tandem/branches/tau-zero-prime-positivity/SUPPORTED_ZERO_PRIME_WEIL_DERIVATION.md}{SZW19--38},
which proves the explicit formula with both endpoints using Alain
Connes, \emph{Trace formula in noncommutative geometry and the zeros
of the Riemann zeta function}, Appendix II, Theorem 6.
The complete finite calculations below do not require moving an
infinite zero sum through a derivative.

\paragraph{HR1. Original objects and fixed tests.}
Fix an actual real zero $x_0$ of the original $H_0$, with its actual
order $m$. Put $\rho=1/2+ix_0/2$, $s=\rho+i\xi/2$, $t=u^2$.
Retain $H_t(x_0+\xi)=U(t,\xi)W(t,\xi)$, the original unit $U$,
and every root $\theta_\nu(u)$ of $W$. The complete proofs HC1--14
give
\[
 \theta_\nu=r_\nu u+2cu^2+b r_\nu u^3+O(u^4),\qquad
 c=a_{m+1}/a_m,\quad d=a_{m+2}/a_m,\quad b=2d-c^2,
 \tag{HR1}
\]
where $r_\nu$ are all roots of $P_m$ in HF2. Write
$p_n=\sum_\nu r_\nu^n$, so $p_0=m$, $p_{2j+1}=0$,
and all even moments are evaluated by HF7.
For fixed holomorphic functions $F,G$ near $\rho$ and their
reflected neighbourhoods, put
\[
 F^\#(s)=\overline{F(1-\bar s)},\quad
 f_n=F^{(n)}(\rho)/n!,\quad g_n=G^{(n)}(\rho)/n!,
 \quad
 Z_{\rho,t}(F^\#G)=\sum_{\nu=1}^m
   (F^\#G)(\rho+i\theta_\nu/2).
 \tag{HR2}
\]
Reflection fixes $\rho$, and sends the local variable
$\xi$ to $\bar\xi$. Therefore the pullbacks of $F,G$ have
coefficients $\alpha_n=(i/2)^n f_n$ and
$\beta_n=(i/2)^n g_n$, and the reflected product has coefficient
$\sum_{a+b=n}\bar\alpha_a\beta_b$ at $\xi^n$.
The sum HR2 is analytic in $t$: it is invariant under $u\mapsto-u$,
and each summand is holomorphic in $u$. It also equals the regular
multiplication trace in the original algebra, by evaluation for
nonzero $t$ and analytic continuation to zero. This proves the
test-to-algebra map and its reflected trace compatibility.

\paragraph{HR2. The next coefficient for all fixed vanishing tests.}
Suppose the actual Taylor coefficients of $F,G$ vanish in degrees
less than $j$, where $0\le j<m$. Then
\[
 Z_{\rho,t}(F^\#G)=\frac{t^j}{4^j}
       \left[p_{2j}\bar f_j g_j+t\mathcal C_j(F,G)+O(t^2)\right],
 \tag{HR3}
\]
where the full coefficient, without dropping any test jets, is
\[
\begin{split}
 \mathcal C_j(F,G)={}&
 \left(2jb\,p_{2j}+4j(2j-1)c^2p_{2j-2}\right)\bar f_j g_j\\
 &+i(2j+1)c p_{2j}
       (\bar f_j g_{j+1}-\bar f_{j+1}g_j)\\
 &+\frac{p_{2j+2}}4
       (\bar f_{j+1}g_{j+1}-\bar f_jg_{j+2}-\bar f_{j+2}g_j).
\end{split}
 \tag{HR4}
\]
For $j=0$ the first line is zero; no negative-index moment is used.
To prove this, besides HF8 use the direct expansion of HR1:
\[
 s_{2j+1}(t)=2(2j+1)c p_{2j}t^{j+1}+O(t^{j+2}),\quad
 s_{2j+2}(t)=p_{2j+2}t^{j+1}+O(t^{j+2}).
 \tag{HR5}
\]
All further powers contribute $O(t^{j+2})$, including their
convergent analytic tail: on the finite branches $|\theta_\nu|\le C|u|$,
the tail is $O(u^{2j+3})$, and its branch sum is even in $u$.
The three required product coefficients are exactly
\[
 \frac{\bar f_jg_j}{4^j},\qquad
 \frac{i(\bar f_jg_{j+1}-\bar f_{j+1}g_j)}{2\,4^j},\qquad
 \frac{\bar f_{j+1}g_{j+1}-\bar f_jg_{j+2}-\bar f_{j+2}g_j}{4^{j+1}}.
 \tag{HR6}
\]
Substitution proves HR3--4. When $j=0$ these formulas give the
complete first derivative, including the derivative of the analytic
unit. When $j=1$ the leading response is
$m(m-1)\bar F'(\rho)G'(\rho)/2$, agreeing with HF6 under the exact
coordinate $s-\rho=i\xi/2$. At every $j$ the first nonzero
coefficient is $p_{2j}\bar f_jg_j/4^j$; it is not a value assigned
to the degenerate endpoint trace.

\paragraph{HR3. A complete original Cauchy-test frame.}
Take any $m$ distinct poles $z_1,\ldots,z_m$ with $\Re z_l>1$,
and set $F_l(s)=1/(s-z_l)$. Let $C_\xi(t)$ be the matrix of
multiplication by the original $\xi$ in the ordered basis
$1,\xi,\ldots,\xi^{m-1}$ of $A=\mathbb C\{t\}[\xi]/W$.
The exact coefficient column of the fixed test is
\[
 R_l(t)=\left((\rho-z_l)I+\frac i2 C_\xi(t)\right)^{-1}e_0,
 \qquad R(t)=(R_1(t),\ldots,R_m(t)).
 \tag{HR7}
\]
Here $e_0$ is the first coordinate vector, not supported zero.
At $t=0$, $C_\xi$ is nilpotent and $\rho-z_l\ne0$, so all
inverses and their analytic continuations exist. Formula HR7
follows by multiplying by the denominator in $A$; it retains all
coefficients of $W$ at every time. In particular,
\[
 R_{nl}(0)=\frac{(-i/2)^n}{(\rho-z_l)^{n+1}},\qquad
 \det R(0)=\frac{(-i/2)^M\prod_{a<b}(z_b-z_a)}
                       {\prod_l(\rho-z_l)^m},\quad
 M=m(m-1)/2.
 \tag{HR8}
\]
Indeed the first formula is the finite geometric inverse; the
second is the Vandermonde determinant in $1/(\rho-z_l)$.
Its nonzero value proves that the fixed tests give an analytic
basis of the entire original coefficient algebra as a module.
This is a module isomorphism, with the algebra multiplication
transported by HR7 when needed.

There is also an explicit fixed rational test realizing each
arithmetic endpoint jet. Put $D(s)=\prod_l(s-z_l)$, and let
$[D(\rho+v)]_{<m-j}$ mean its Taylor polynomial of degree at most
$m-j-1$. Then
\[
 B_j(s)=\frac{(s-\rho)^j[D(\rho+v)]_{<m-j}|_{v=s-\rho}}{D(s)}
       =(s-\rho)^j+O((s-\rho)^m),\quad 0\le j<m.
 \tag{HR9}
\]
The numerator has degree at most $m-1$, so
$B_j=\sum_l N_j(z_l)F_l/D'(z_l)$, by subtraction of its
simple-pole residues and decay at infinity. The Taylor remainder
in the numerator is $O(v^m)$ and $D(\rho)\ne0$, proving HR9.
Thus every endpoint jet is reached by actual fixed Cauchy tests.
Their coefficients at degrees $m$ and above remain those of HR9
and must be included in HR4; they are not set to zero.

\paragraph{HR4. Exact Gram determinant and its arithmetic correction.}
Write $K(t)=(s_{a+b}(t))_{0\le a,b<m}$ for the full original
reflected trace matrix. For real $t$ the local test matrix is
\[
 C_\rho(t)_{al}=Z_{\rho,t}(F_a^\#F_l),\qquad
 C_\rho(t)=R(t)^*K(t)R(t).
 \tag{HR10}
\]
This follows from HR2 and HR7 even at negative time: reflection
permutes the nonreal roots, and remains the original coefficient
conjugation. There is no replacement by a Euclidean norm there.
For nonzero $t$, evaluation of the columns gives the Cauchy matrix
$(1/(\rho+i\theta_\nu/2-z_l))_{\nu l}$. Its determinant is
the following exact formula:
\[
 \det R(t)=
 \frac{(-i/2)^M\prod_{a<b}(z_b-z_a)}
      {\prod_{\nu,l}(\rho+i\theta_\nu/2-z_l)}.
 \tag{HR11}
\]
For completeness, multiplying the Cauchy determinant by its common
denominator produces an alternating polynomial separately in
the row points and in the poles. Its degrees force it to be a
constant times the two Vandermonde products. Comparing one
successive pole residue, starting with size one, gives the
constant $(-1)^M$. The row Vandermonde is $(i/2)^M$ times
the Vandermonde in $\theta_\nu$; cancelling the latter with the
evaluation determinant proves HR11. Both sides extend analytically
to zero. Therefore the identity also follows there without a
division by a vanishing Vandermonde.

The exact determinant and its first relative correction are
\[
 \det C_\rho(t)=\operatorname{disc}_\xi W(t,\xi)\,
 \frac{4^{-M}|\prod_{a<b}(z_b-z_a)|^2}
      {|\prod_{\nu,l}(\rho+i\theta_\nu/2-z_l)|^2},
 \tag{HR12}
\]
\[
 \det C_\rho(t)=
 \frac{D_m\,4^{-M}|\prod_{a<b}(z_b-z_a)|^2}
      {\prod_l|\rho-z_l|^{2m}}
 t^M\left(1+\mathcal E_\rho t+O(t^2)\right),
 \quad D_m=2^M\prod_{a=1}^m a^a,
 \tag{HR13}
\]
\[
 \mathcal E_\rho=m(m-1)b
 -2mc\Re\left(i\sum_l\frac1{\rho-z_l}\right)
 -\frac{m(m-1)}2\Re\sum_l\frac1{(\rho-z_l)^2}.
 \tag{HR14}
\]
Indeed $\det K=\operatorname{disc}W$ by the Vandermonde square.
HC14 evaluates it as $D_mt^M(1+m(m-1)bt+O(t^2))$.
For $a_l=\rho-z_l$, HR1 gives
\[
 \sum_\nu\log(a_l+i\theta_\nu/2)
 =m\log a_l+t\left(\frac{imc}{a_l}
                  +\frac{m(m-1)}{4a_l^2}\right)+O(t^2).
 \tag{HR15}
\]
Choose any local logarithm at each nonzero $a_l$; its derivative
and the real part are independent of that choice. Equations
HR12--15 prove the claim. The full original unit enters through
$b=2d-c^2$ and $c$; no model values are inserted.

\paragraph{HR5. Complete support and the arithmetic complement.}
For the complete support lattice $L$, lift the module isomorphism
HR7 by $(x,1_L)\mapsto(R(t)x,1_L)$ and
$(0,\lambda)\mapsto(0,\lambda)$ at every lower support label.
Its inverse is the identical construction using $R(t)^{-1}$.
They preserve addition, scalar action, and all zero supports
directly. The reflected pairing retains the output support as
well; a zero scalar value remains supported zero at the specified
label. This explicitly relates the fixed Cauchy tests to the
original full supported module, including its endpoint nilpotents.

To identify the exact place of the local matrix inside the whole
arithmetic expression, use the original $g_t(s)=16H_t(-2i(s-1/2))$
and $L_t=g_t'/g_t$. At zero,
\[
 L_0(s)=\frac1s+\frac1{s-1}-\frac12\log\pi
        +\frac12\psi(s/2)-\sum_{n\ge2}\Lambda(n)n^{-s},
       \qquad\Re s>1.
 \tag{HR16}
\]
This follows by the logarithmic derivative of
$g_0=s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$; all series are
absolutely convergent on the displayed domain. The complete
Cauchy-test arithmetic matrix from SZW is
\[
 C_{\rm full}(0)_{al}
  =\frac{L_0(z_l)+\overline{L_0(z_a)}}{z_l+\bar z_a-1},
 \qquad C_{\rm ext}(0)=C_{\rm full}(0)-C_\rho(0),
 \quad C_\rho(0)_{al}=
 \frac{m}{\overline{\rho-z_a}(\rho-z_l)}.
 \tag{HR17}
\]
For clarity, the first formula follows by subtracting the two
genus-one logarithmic derivatives at $z_l$ and $1-\bar z_a$.
Their constants and the regularizing $1/\rho'$ terms cancel,
and each zero $\rho'$ contributes
$-1/((\rho'-(1-\bar z_a))(\rho'-z_l))$, with its multiplicity.
This is exactly $F_a^\#(\rho')F_l(\rho')$.
The series converges absolutely since its terms are
$O((1+|\rho'|)^{-2})$ and the zero count is
$O(R\log(2+R))$, as proved in SZW25. Thus the subtraction does
not discard any zero or any regularizing term. Reflection of the
original completed zeta function gives
$L_0(1-\bar z_a)=-\overline{L_0(z_a)}$, which proves HR17.
Every endpoint, Gamma term and prime power in HR16 is retained
in $C_{\rm ext}$ by this exact subtraction. The local time
derivative HR3 is likewise an actual part of the full derivative:
the heat equation gives
\[
 J_0(s)=\left.\partial_tL_t(s)\right|_0
       =\frac14\partial_s(L_0'(s)+L_0(s)^2),\qquad
 \dot C_{\rm ext}(0)_{al}=
 \frac{J_0(z_l)+\overline{J_0(z_a)}}{z_l+\bar z_a-1}
       -\dot C_\rho(0)_{al}.
 \tag{HR18}
\]
The coordinate gives $\partial_tg=g''/4$, proving HR18 by
differentiating the quotient at fixed poles, where $g_0$ is
nonzero. The whole moving-zero interpretation follows from the
complete companion proof \texttt{ACTUAL\_HEAT\_ZERO\_DISTRIBUTION\_VARIATION.md},
AG1--11, included unchanged in this package. Its actual heat integral
gives uniform growth $|g_t(s)|\le\exp(C_T(R+1)\log(R+2))$
on $|s|\le R,|t|\le T$. Jensen's formula and $g_t(0)\ne0$
near zero give $N_t(R)=O(R\log(R+2))$ and the uniform
$|\rho'|^{-2}$ tail. The genus-one logarithmic derivative then
gives, for every rational $A=O(s^{-2})$ with its poles away from
the zeros, the finite identity
$Z_t(A)=-\sum_p\operatorname{Res}_p(A L_t)$.
Its right side is holomorphic in $t$, so the derivative in HR18
is the derivative of the actual whole trace. All growth estimates,
pole exclusions, residue cancellations and the extension from
original compact Mellin tests are proved in AG1--16; no derivative
of an unrestricted infinite sum is assumed here.
The finite local derivative is HR4 at $j=0$.
These formulas specify the full compensating matrix, rather than
assigning its sign from the positive local flag coefficients.
The lower support entries in SZW33--34 are fixed endpoint values;
the present maps preserve them and their zero time derivative.

The classical factorization input in AG7 is Jacques Hadamard's
finite-order factorization theorem; historical source:
\href{https://www.numdam.org/item/JMPA_1893_4_9__171_0/}{\emph{\'Etude
sur les propri\'et\'es des fonctions enti\`eres et en particulier
d'une fonction consid\'er\'ee par Riemann}}, Journal de
Math\'ematiques Pures et Appliqu\'ees (4) 9 (1893), 171--215.
The zero-count input is Johan Ludwig William Valdemar Jensen,
\href{https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02417878}{\emph{Sur un nouvel et
important th\'eor\`eme de la th\'eorie des fonctions}}, Acta
Mathematica 22 (1899), 359--364. The historical metadata are
attribution, not a claim to have read original-author TeX for
