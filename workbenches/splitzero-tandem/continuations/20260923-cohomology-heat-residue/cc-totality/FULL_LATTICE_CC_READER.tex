The controlling object and its two exact coordinates are
\[
G_L(R)=\{(0,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}\cup(R\times\{1_L\}),
\qquad(q,\sigma)(r,\lambda)=(r,\lambda).
\]
Throughout, $L$ is the entire bounded distributive lattice. Every
label, both distinguished zeros, each join and meet, and the original
nonzero-amplitude constraint are retained. The finite-lattice prime
classification states its finite hypothesis separately; the coefficient
and sheaf constructions apply to arbitrary nontrivial $L$.

The human sources are Alain Connes and Caterina Consani's
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.05585v2}{\emph{Absolute algebra and
Segal's $\Gamma$-sets}},
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00456v1}{\emph{Riemann--Roch for the ring
$\mathbb Z$}},
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.05580v1}{\emph{Geometry of the Arithmetic Site}},
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06604v1}{\emph{On the Absolute Geometry of
Spec Z and the Fargues--Fontaine curve}}, and
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00299v1}{\emph{The Absolute Twistor Line and
the Geometry of $\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z}$}}.
The exact original TeX versions are retained with archive receipts.
Every application below includes its proof locators. The full lattice
foundation is The Clankers,
\href{https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/zeta-function-research-reader/blob/a2851f9eb352e7e4376bc629de86fa4ed9c1c434/workbenches/splitzero-tandem/foundations/split-support-geometry-v11/split_support_geometry_arithmetic_curve_v11.tex#L1226}{\emph{Split Support Geometry}, v11, Definition \texttt{def:lattice-split}}.
The user attributes the earlier scalar supported-zero prime chain to
Gemini 2.5 Pro; the present text does not claim its rediscovery.

This cumulative component contains the complete derivations currently
proved: coefficient functors and cancellation, bounded sections,
divisor multiplication and morphisms, tropical base change and primes,
absolute-curve characters and sheaf comparisons, and the original
spectral receiver with its tensor and nilpotent sectors. It is not a
claim to have completed the entire corpus synthesis, a Deligne purity
comparison, or the Riemann hypothesis.
