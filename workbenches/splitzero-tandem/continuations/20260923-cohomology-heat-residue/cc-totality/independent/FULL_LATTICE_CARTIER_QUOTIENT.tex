\subsection{The genuine unit quotient}

Let $K$ be a field and
$v:K^\times\to\Gamma$ a surjective valuation to an ordered
abelian group, written additively, with $v(0)=+\infty$.
Write
\begin{equation}\tag{FCQ1}
\begin{gathered}
\mathcal O_v=\{x:v(x)\ge0\},\qquad
\mathfrak m_v=\{x:v(x)>0\},\\
\kappa_v=\mathcal O_v/\mathfrak m_v,\qquad
U_v=\mathcal O_v^\times=\{u:v(u)=0\}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The trivial valuation is permitted. Fix the original nontrivial
bounded distributive lattice $L$ and retain
\begin{equation}\tag{FCQ2}
\begin{gathered}
S=G_L(K)=\{z_\ell=(0,\ell):\ell\in L\}
 \cup\{\widehat x=(x,1_L):x\in K^\times\},\\
\tau=z_{0_L},\qquad e=z_{1_L},\\
(x,\ell)+(y,m)=(x+y,\ell\vee m),\qquad
(x,\ell)(y,m)=(xy,\ell\wedge m).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The notation for a zero-amplitude pair in the last line always
means its displayed $z$-label. The actual multiplicative units
of $G_L(\mathcal O_v)$ are precisely $\widehat u$ with
$u\in U_v$: an invertible element must have nonzero amplitude,
and its amplitude and inverse must both belong to $\mathcal O_v$.
Conversely $\widehat u^{-1}=\widehat{u^{-1}}$ verifies the assertion.

These units act by multiplication on $S$. Their action is
\begin{equation}\tag{FCQ3}
\widehat u\,\widehat x=\widehat{ux},\qquad
\widehat u\,z_\ell=z_\ell .
\end{equation}
It fixes every labelled zero. Multiplication by a unit is an
additive automorphism; it is not being called a unital semiring
automorphism, since it need not fix the multiplicative unit.
Let $\mathscr Q_L(K,v)$ be its orbit set. Nonzero amplitudes
have the same orbit exactly when their valuations agree:
$v(x)=v(y)$ is equivalent to $x/y\in U_v$.
Thus the exact orbit set is
\begin{equation}\tag{FCQ4}
\mathscr Q_L(K,v)=\{z_\ell:\ell\in L\}
 \sqcup\{\mathbf a:a\in\Gamma\},\qquad
\mathbf a=[\widehat x]\ \Longleftrightarrow\ a=-v(x).
\end{equation}
In particular the symbol $\mathbf0$ is the nonzero-amplitude
unit orbit. It is neither $\tau$ nor $e$.

Define orbit multiplication and multivalued addition by
\begin{equation}\tag{FCQ5}
[x][y]=[xy],\qquad
[x]\boxplus[y]=\{[\widehat u\,x+\widehat w\,y]:
                       u,w\in U_v\}.
\end{equation}
Changing either representative changes neither set: absorb its
unit into $u$ or $w$. Multiplication is well defined because
the product of two such unit factors is a unit. The operations
on arbitrary subsets below mean union over their elements.

\subsection{The complete addition table and the residue-field correction}

The operations retaining every lattice element are
\begin{equation}\tag{FCQ6}
\begin{gathered}
z_\ell z_m=z_{\ell\wedge m},\qquad
z_\ell\mathbf a=z_\ell,\qquad
\mathbf a\mathbf b=\mathbf{a+b},\\
z_\ell\boxplus z_m=\{z_{\ell\vee m}\},\qquad
z_\ell\boxplus\mathbf a=\{\mathbf a\},\\
\mathbf a\boxplus\mathbf b=\{\mathbf{\max(a,b)}\}
                    \quad(a\ne b).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
For the equal-value case, the exact formula is
\begin{equation}\tag{FCQ7}
\mathbf a\boxplus\mathbf a=
\begin{cases}
\{e\}\cup\{\mathbf b:b<a\},&|\kappa_v|=2,\\
\{e\}\cup\{\mathbf b:b\le a\},&|\kappa_v|>2.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The set $b<a$ may be empty for the trivial valuation.

\begin{proof}
The first two lines of FCQ6 follow by the original split laws.
For different valuations the nonarchimedean equality
$v(x+y)=\min(v(x),v(y))$ gives its last line for every choice
of unit representatives.

For equal values, divide both representatives by an element
of valuation $-a$. The possible sums reduce to sums of two
units. Their valuations are nonnegative, or $+\infty$.
Every strictly positive value $\gamma\in\Gamma$ occurs: choose
$t\in K^\times$ with $v(t)=\gamma$, and write
$t=1+(t-1)$. Both $1$ and $t-1$ are units. Exact cancellation
is $1+(-1)=0$. In the split carrier, this cancellation has
support $1_L\vee1_L=1_L$, and hence gives $e$, never $\tau$.

A sum of two units has valuation zero exactly when the sum
of their two nonzero residues is nonzero. Over $\mathbf F_2$
the only two nonzero residues are both $1$, whose sum is zero,
so valuation zero cannot occur. If $|\kappa_v|>2$, choose a
nonzero residue different from $-1$ and lift it to a unit $u$.
Then $1+u$ has nonzero residue and valuation zero. Multiplying
all these constructions by the chosen element of valuation
$-a$ proves both directions of FCQ7.
\end{proof}

This corrects the generality of Lemma \texttt{hyper} in the
source. For $K=\mathbb Q$ at the prime $2$, with its required
scale $v(2)=2-1=1$, the units have odd numerator and odd
denominator in reduced form. The sum of two units has even
numerator and odd denominator and cannot be a unit.
Consequently
\begin{equation}\tag{FCQ8}
\mathbf0\boxplus\mathbf0
 =\{e,\mathbf{-1},\mathbf{-2},\ldots\},
 \qquad
\mathbf0\notin\mathbf0\boxplus\mathbf0.
\end{equation}
Every displayed negative value occurs by
$1+(2^j-1)=2^j$, $j\ge1$, and $e$ occurs by cancellation.
Replacing $e$ by the ordinary zero gives the same counterexample
to the source's claimed equal-value interval including its
upper endpoint. The correction concerns that finite-stage
lemma, not an assertion that the cyclotomic endpoint fails.

\subsection{The exact multivalued algebra and its projections}

The orbit object is a commutative associative multivalued
additive monoid with identity $\tau$. Its multiplication is
a commutative monoid with identity $\mathbf0$ and absorber
$\tau$, and it distributes over the multivalued addition
with equality of sets:
\begin{equation}\tag{FCQ9}
c(a\boxplus b)=ca\boxplus cb.
\end{equation}
Here these axioms specify the resulting multivalued algebra;
no additive-inverse axiom is tacitly included.

\begin{proof}
Commutativity follows from FCQ5. An iterated three-term orbit
sum on either parenthesization is exactly
\[
\{[\widehat u\,x+\widehat w\,y+\widehat t\,z]:
                      u,w,t\in U_v\}.
\]
To verify this assertion, a representative of an intermediate
orbit differs from its defining two-term sum by a unit, which
multiplies both corresponding coefficients. Conversely one
may take that extra unit to be $1$. This proves associativity
including every possible zero-amplitude intermediate sum.
The identity equation follows because a unit multiple of
$\tau$ is $\tau$ and addition by $\tau$ is the original identity.
The multiplicative monoid laws descend from FCQ2. For
distributivity, fixing a representative $c$, both sides of
FCQ9 are exactly
\[
\{[c(\widehat u\,x+\widehat w\,y)]:u,w\in U_v\}
 =\{[\widehat u\,cx+\widehat w\,cy]:u,w\in U_v\}.
\]
This uses original distributivity and proves equality, not
merely one inclusion.
\end{proof}

It is not an ordinary hyperring with zero $\tau$. Indeed
$\tau\in a\boxplus b$ forces both amplitudes to vanish and
their support join to be $0_L$, so $a=b=\tau$.
Thus no other element has an additive inverse to $\tau$.
The zero-amplitude part
$Z_L=\{z_\ell:\ell\in L\}$ is closed under addition and
absorbs multiplication into itself. Its addition and
multiplication are exactly join and meet.

There is nevertheless an exact ordinary hyperfield inside
this object, with its different additive zero:
\begin{equation}\tag{FCQ10}
\mathscr H(K,v)=\{e\}\sqcup\{\mathbf a:a\in\Gamma\}
       \cong K/U_v.
\end{equation}
On this subset, $e$ is the additive identity and the
multiplicative absorber. Every $\mathbf a$ has
multiplicative inverse $\mathbf{-a}$. Since $-1\in U_v$,
each is its own additive opposite, with $e$ in its self-sum;
FCQ6 shows that no other value can be its opposite.
For completeness, reversibility of this hyperaddition
follows directly from a field equation. The assertion
$[c]\in[a]\boxplus[b]$ in $K/U_v$ means
$wc=ua+tb$ for some $u,t,w\in U_v$. Solving for $a$ gives
$a=u^{-1}wc-u^{-1}tb$, which is precisely
$[a]\in[c]\boxplus[-b]$. Solving back gives the converse.
Associativity and distributivity were already proved.
This verifies all the hyperfield axioms of FCQ10 without
assuming the incorrect residue-independent addition formula.

Write $0_{\mathscr H}$ for $e$ when it is regarded as the
zero of FCQ10. There are exact projections
\begin{equation}\tag{FCQ11}
\begin{aligned}
\pi:\mathscr Q_L(K,v)&\longrightarrow\mathscr H(K,v),
&\pi(z_\ell)&=0_{\mathscr H},
&\pi(\mathbf a)&=\mathbf a,\\
\sigma:\mathscr Q_L(K,v)&\longrightarrow L,
&\sigma(z_\ell)&=\ell,
&\sigma(\mathbf a)&=1_L .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Both preserve multiplication and their respective units
and zeros. For addition they preserve the complete image
sets:
$\pi(a\boxplus b)=\pi(a)\boxplus\pi(b)$ and
$\sigma(a\boxplus b)=\{\sigma(a)\vee\sigma(b)\}$.
These statements follow in each case from FCQ6--7.
The second formula retains the support of cancellation.

The two maps together are injective and have precisely the
following image:
\begin{equation}\tag{FCQ12}
\mathscr Q_L(K,v)\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
\{(h,\ell)\in\mathscr H(K,v)\times L:
                  h\ne0_{\mathscr H}\Rightarrow\ell=1_L\}.
\end{equation}
The inverse sends $(0_{\mathscr H},\ell)$ to $z_\ell$
and $(\mathbf a,1_L)$ to $\mathbf a$.
Under this bijection the sum is
\[
(h,\ell)\boxplus(h',m)
 =\{(t,\ell\vee m):t\in h\boxplus h'\},
\]
and the product is $(hh',\ell\wedge m)$.
These sets obey the displayed admissibility condition:
if a sum has nonzero amplitude at least one input has
nonzero amplitude and hence top support. A product of
nonzero amplitudes has both supports top. Thus FCQ12
is an exact construction of the full object from its
ordinary Cartier hyperfield and its complete support lattice,
not an identification of their distinct zero elements.

The lattice inclusion $\ell\mapsto z_\ell$ preserves join,
meet and the actual zero, but sends the lattice unit to
$e$, not to $\mathbf0$. The inclusion of FCQ10 preserves
its full operations and its multiplicative unit, but sends
its additive zero to $e$, not to $\tau$. Their composites
with the corresponding projections are identities.
The original orbit map $q:G_L(K)\to\mathscr Q_L(K,v)$
preserves products exactly and satisfies
$q(x+y)\in q(x)\boxplus q(y)$; its unit-saturated image
of sums is exactly FCQ5. The commutative square
\begin{equation}\tag{FCQ13}
\begin{aligned}
G_L(K)&\longrightarrow\mathscr Q_L(K,v)\\
\big\downarrow{\scriptstyle\mathrm{amplitude}}
&\hspace{2.7em}\big\downarrow{\scriptstyle\pi}\\
K&\longrightarrow K/U_v
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
uses the ordinary field quotient on the bottom row.

Every bounded lattice homomorphism $\epsilon:L\to L'$
induces the exact operation-preserving map
$z_\ell\mapsto z_{\epsilon(\ell)}$,
$\mathbf a\mapsto\mathbf a$.
It commutes with both projections in FCQ11 and with
FCQ13. In particular this gives all Boolean-branch maps,
without replacing the original $L$ by a chosen branch.

\subsection{The actual valuation scale and the divisor modules}

At a prime $\wp$ of a number field $K$ above the rational
prime $p$, retain the source's scale
$v_\wp(p)=p-1$. If $e_\wp$ is its ramification index and
$\operatorname{ord}_\wp$ takes value one at a uniformizer,
then
\begin{equation}\tag{FCQ14}
v_\wp=\alpha_\wp\operatorname{ord}_\wp,\qquad
\alpha_\wp=\frac{p-1}{e_\wp},\qquad
\Gamma_\wp=\alpha_\wp\mathbb Z.
\end{equation}
This follows because
$\operatorname{ord}_\wp(p)=e_\wp$ and the required scale
fixes the multiplier. If the residue degree is $f_\wp$,
the residue field has $p^{f_\wp}$ elements. The second
line of FCQ7 applies except exactly when
$p=2$ and $f_\wp=1$; in that case its first line applies.
All intervals in the source's negative-valuation coordinate
are therefore intervals in the actual group
$\alpha_\wp\mathbb Z$, with the specified endpoint rule.

For a discrete valuation, let $\varpi$ be a uniformizer
and $\alpha=v(\varpi)>0$. The original fractional ideal
$\varpi^{-n}\mathcal O_v$ and its full split lift have
the following exact orbit set under $U_v$:
\begin{equation}\tag{FCQ15}
G_L(\varpi^{-n}\mathcal O_v)/U_v
 =\{z_\ell:\ell\in L\}
      \sqcup\{\mathbf a:a\in\Gamma,\ a\le n\alpha\}.
\end{equation}
Indeed membership of a nonzero amplitude is
$v(x)\ge-n\alpha$, which is exactly the displayed
inequality for $a=-v(x)$. Every such value occurs by
the definition of the value group. The whole zero lattice
occurs and is fixed by the unit action. The addition table
FCQ6--7 preserves this bound, and multiplication by the
coefficient quotient, whose nonzero values satisfy $a\le0$,
preserves it as well.

For the finite stalk at a rational prime in SDM4,
$I_D=p^{-n_p}\mathbb Z_{(p)}$, the formula is
\begin{equation}\tag{FCQ16}
G_L(I_D)/\mathbb Z_{(p)}^\times
 =\{z_\ell:\ell\in L\}
   \sqcup\{\mathbf a:a\in(p-1)\mathbb Z,\
                              a\le(p-1)n_p\}.
\end{equation}
The coefficient $n_p$ has not been replaced: the multiplier
$p-1$ records the source's valuation scale.
For the principal change $D'=D+(q)$, the actual map
$x\mapsto q^{-1}x$ in SDM13 sends
\[
\mathbf a\longmapsto\mathbf{a+v_p(q)},\qquad
z_\ell\longmapsto z_\ell,
\qquad v_p(q)=(p-1)\operatorname{ord}_p(q).
\]
Its target bound is
$(p-1)n'_p=(p-1)n_p+v_p(q)$, exactly the translated
source bound. This proves the local Cartier receiver of
the original divisor-module isomorphism, with all signs
and all support labels retained.

\subsection{Why the cyclotomic stalk has the full closed interval}

Let $K^{\rm cyc}=\mathbb Q^{\rm cyc}$ be the union of the
cyclotomic number fields, and let $v$ extend the $p$-adic
valuation with $v(p)=p-1$. Let $\mathcal O_v$ now be its
valuation ring in this full field. Its residue field is
\begin{equation}\tag{FCQ17}
\kappa_v=\overline{\mathbb F}_p.
\end{equation}
Here the equality means an isomorphism inside a fixed
algebraic residue-field closure.

\begin{proof}
Every residue of an algebraic element of $K^{\rm cyc}$
is algebraic over $\mathbb F_p$, so the residue field is
an algebraic extension of $\mathbb F_p$. For every $f\ge1$
the field contains all roots of unity of order
$n=p^f-1$. Their reductions are distinct. To prove this,
if a nontrivial $n$-th root $\zeta$ reduced to $1$,
then the equation
$1+\zeta+\cdots+\zeta^{n-1}=0$ would reduce to
$n=0$ in characteristic $p$, contrary to $p\nmid n$.
Applying this to the ratio of any two roots proves
distinctness. Their $n$ distinct nonzero reductions are
all the roots of $X^n-1$; together with zero they are
the roots of $X^{p^f}-X$. In characteristic $p$ this
root set is closed under addition, subtraction,
multiplication and inversion of nonzero elements,
because the $p^f$-power map is a field homomorphism.
It is therefore a copy of $\mathbb F_{p^f}$ inside
the residue field. Taking every $f$ proves FCQ17.
\end{proof}

In particular this residue field always has more than two
elements. At $p=2$, the correction is already visible after
adjoining a primitive cube root of unity: its residue lies
in $\mathbb F_4\setminus\mathbb F_2$, and its sum with $1$
is nonzero. This supplies the equal-value endpoint missing
over the finite field $\mathbb F_2$.

The source's cyclotomic value-group calculation, with its
scale unchanged, is
\begin{equation}\tag{FCQ18}
v((K^{\rm cyc})^\times)=H(p)=\mathbb Z[1/p].
\end{equation}
Its local-field input, explicitly used in the proof of
Proposition \texttt{sheavesonspz}, is that the local
cyclotomic extension is obtained by adjoining the unramified
roots of unity of order prime to $p$ and the totally ramified
$p$-power roots of unity. The source cites Jean-Pierre Serre,
\emph{Corps locaux}, second edition (1968), for this
local-field statement. We retain that cited input rather
than assert an unreferenced new ramification theorem.
Its consequences for the value group can be checked
with the exact constants as follows.

For a primitive $p^m$-th root $\zeta$,
$\Phi_{p^m}(1+T)$ is Eisenstein at $p$: its constant
term is $p$, and its reduction modulo $p$ is
$T^{(p-1)p^{m-1}}$. The latter identity follows from
$\Phi_{p^m}(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}X^{jp^{m-1}}$
by reducing in characteristic $p$ and substituting
$X=1+T$. The valuation of $\pi=\zeta-1$ is positive,
since a $p$-power root of unity reduces to $1$ in
residue characteristic $p$. In its Eisenstein equation,
all intermediate terms then have value strictly greater
than $v(p)$ if the leading term has value $v(p)$;
more directly, equality of the minimal values in that
equation forces
\[
((p-1)p^{m-1})\,v(\zeta-1)=v(p)=p-1.
\]
Indeed, if the leading value were smaller than $v(p)$,
it would be the unique minimum, since an intermediate
coefficient has value at least $v(p)$; if it were larger,
the constant term would be the unique minimum.
Thus $v(\zeta-1)=p^{-(m-1)}$.
The unramified extensions do not enlarge the value group;
the finite totally ramified cyclotomic stage has index
$(p-1)p^{m-1}$, so its group is exactly
$p^{-(m-1)}\mathbb Z$. Taking their union proves FCQ18.
This is also why replacing $v(p)$ by $1$ without recording
the multiplier would give the wrong displayed group.

Combining FCQ7, FCQ17 and FCQ18 proves the full cyclotomic
stalk, rather than importing an assumed tropical addition:
\begin{equation}\tag{FCQ19}
\begin{gathered}
\mathscr Q_L(K^{\rm cyc},v)
 =\{z_\ell:\ell\in L\}
       \sqcup\{\mathbf a:a\in\mathbb Z[1/p]\},\\
\mathbf a\boxplus\mathbf a
 =\{e\}\cup\{\mathbf b:b\in\mathbb Z[1/p],\ b\le a\}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Collapsing the full zero lattice by $\pi$ gives precisely
the source's $H(p)_{\rm hmax}$, with its ordinary zero
written $-\infty$. The same closed interval is not valid
at every finite residue-$\mathbf F_2$ stage, as FCQ8 shows.

For an extension of valued fields with the same stated scale,
the induced map of quotient orbit sets is injective:
the nonzero orbits are determined by the unchanged valuation
and the zero labels are fixed. Its additive image is contained
in the target hyperaddition, but equality need not hold.
New value-group elements or a larger residue field can add
new target sums. At each cyclotomic stalk, every element,
unit witness and finite sum relation occurs in a finite
cyclotomic subfield, after adjoining the finitely many
elements involved. Thus the filtered union of these exact
finite-stage quotient relations is the relation FCQ19.
This proves how the corrected finite-stage rule yields the
source's final cyclotomic hyperfield.

\subsection{The quotient sheaf over the original arithmetic scheme}

Let $A$ be an integral domain with fraction field $K$, and
$X=\operatorname{Spec}A$. On this irreducible space define
the split meromorphic sheaf $G_L(\underline K)$ by the
constant full carrier on each nonempty open, with its
coordinatewise operations. The ordinary unit sheaf
$\mathcal O_X^\times$ acts by FCQ3. Let
\begin{equation}\tag{FCQ20}
\mathscr C_L(X)
 =\bigl(G_L(\underline K)/\mathcal O_X^\times\bigr)^\#,
\qquad
\mathscr C(X)
 =\bigl(\underline K/\mathcal O_X^\times\bigr)^\#.
\end{equation}
The superscript means sheafification of the orbit presheaf.
The second sheaf is the source's Cartier hyperring sheaf.
In the first, the relation of multivalued addition is the
sheafification of the orbit-sum relation FCQ5; equivalently,
membership is locally witnessed by unit representatives.
The multiplication and the support and amplitude maps
are compatible with restriction, so they give sheaf maps.

At every point $x\in X$, the exact stalk is
\begin{equation}\tag{FCQ21}
\mathscr C_L(X)_x
 =G_L(K)/\mathcal O_{X,x}^\times.
\end{equation}
To prove the orbit assertion, an equality of two stalk
orbits is witnessed by one unit germ, and that unit and
its inverse are represented on some common neighborhood.
After shrinking, their product is the identity, so the
germ is represented by an actual section of the unit
sheaf there. The converse follows by taking germs.
The same argument with the finitely many representatives
and unit witnesses in FCQ5 proves the stalk formula for
hyperaddition. Sheafification leaves these stalks
unchanged. Thus no relation is introduced merely by
asserting a stalk calculation.

When $\mathcal O_{X,x}$ is a valuation ring, FCQ21 has
the full table FCQ6--7. At the generic point its unit
group is $K^\times$, and the quotient has just one
nonzero-amplitude orbit, together with the full $L$:
\begin{equation}\tag{FCQ22}
\mathscr C_L(X)_\eta=\{z_\ell:\ell\in L\}\sqcup\{\mathbf0\},
\quad
\mathbf0\boxplus\mathbf0=
\begin{cases}
\{e\},&K=\mathbf F_2,\\
\{e,\mathbf0\},&|K|>2.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
This follows either from the same residue argument for
the trivial valuation or directly by summing two nonzero
field elements. For the number fields and cyclotomic
field here, the second case applies and the collapsed
generic stalk is the Krasner hyperfield. The map from a
valuation stalk to the generic stalk sends every
$\mathbf a$ to $\mathbf0$ and fixes every $z_\ell$.
If the valuation is nontrivial, its group has an element
less than any given $a$, which supplies the nonzero
generic self-sum even in the residue-$\mathbf F_2$ case.
If it is trivial, FCQ22 already gives the exact rule.
This arithmetic generization preserves hyperaddition in the
inclusion sense, and is not in general strong. For distinct
$a,b\in\Gamma$, the image of
$\mathbf a\boxplus\mathbf b$ is the singleton
$\{\mathbf0\}$, whereas the generic sum
$\mathbf0\boxplus\mathbf0$ is $\{e,\mathbf0\}$ for
the fields in this application. The larger generic
unit group has added a cancellation. Thus the sheaf
relations and their restriction maps retain this
distinction; no equality of these two sets is assumed.

There is an exact sheaf version of FCQ12:
\begin{equation}\tag{FCQ23}
\mathscr C_L(X)\ \cong\
\{(h,\ell)\in\mathscr C(X)\times\underline L:
                         h\ne0\Longrightarrow\ell=1_L\}.
\end{equation}
The condition is understood locally, or equivalently on
germs, and the operations are the componentwise formulas
following FCQ12. The natural map sends an orbit to its
ordinary Cartier orbit and its retained support. It is
bijective on every stalk by FCQ21 and FCQ12, so it is
an isomorphism of sheaves; the stalk calculation also
proves exact preservation of their multivalued relations.
This is the explicit sheaf map to the original Cartier
construction, retaining all of its zero-fibre data.

For $A=\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q^{\rm cyc}}$, a prime above $p$
has local ring equal to the valuation ring used in FCQ19.
Indeed both are the union of the compatible finite
localizations at that prime: every element and its
denominator occur in a finite cyclotomic subfield.
The ordinary collapsed stalks in FCQ23 are consequently
$H(p)_{\rm hmax}$ at closed primes and the Krasner
hyperfield at the generic point, exactly as in the
source's Proposition \texttt{sheavesonspz}, now with
the corrected finite-stage addition. Applying FCQ23 to
that source comparison gives the full supported sheaf
over its arithmetic-site pullback. The finite-support
valuation sections and the normalization remain those
constructed in that proposition; no numerical value
has been assigned to a lattice label.

\subsection{Extension to the actual spectrum of the split carrier}

The arithmetic scheme $X$ in FCQ20 is not the whole
prime spectrum of $S_A=G_L(A)$. To retain that additional
geometry, put $Y=\operatorname{Spec}S_A$, with its
local-fraction structure sheaf $\mathcal O_Y$.
The original prime classification and localization give
\begin{equation}\tag{FCQ24}
\begin{gathered}
Q_{\mathfrak q}=\{z_\ell:\ell\in L\}
       \cup\{\widehat a:a\in\mathfrak q,\ a\ne0\},
\qquad
P_{\mathfrak p}=\{z_\ell:\ell\in\mathfrak p\},\\
(\mathcal O_Y)_{Q_{\mathfrak q}}=G_L(A_{\mathfrak q}),
\qquad
(\mathcal O_Y)_{P_{\mathfrak p}}
       =L[(L\setminus\mathfrak p)^{-1}],\\
V_Y(e)\cong X,\qquad D_Y(e)\cong\operatorname{SpecLat}L.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathfrak q$ is an ordinary prime of $A$ and
$\mathfrak p$ is a lattice prime. All of these primes
are present.

For explicitness, at an arithmetic prime every denominator
has nonzero amplitude and top support, so fraction equality
is exactly the amplitude fraction equality with each
$z_\ell$ retained; this proves the first localization.
At a support prime, $e$ is invertible. As a multiplicative
idempotent it becomes the unit; since
$e\widehat a=e$ for every supported amplitude, all such
amplitudes become that unit. The remaining lattice
localization is the second displayed stalk. Writing
$F=L\setminus\mathfrak p$, its elements have the exact
equality relation
\begin{equation}\tag{FCQ25}
\ell\sim_F m
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists\nu\in F:\ \nu\wedge\ell=\nu\wedge m.
\end{equation}
The relation is just localization equality after clearing
one denominator. Finite distributivity proves that it is
a lattice congruence. Elements of $F$ become top, and
bottom does not become top: a witness to that equality
would force a member of $F$ to be bottom. This proves
the relevant support stalks without replacing them by
Boolean residues.

Let $\mathcal M_Y$ be the meromorphic $S_A$-algebra sheaf
obtained by inverting all supported nonzero amplitudes
$T=\{\widehat a:0\ne a\in A\}$. Concretely it is the
sheaf of local fractions of $T^{-1}S_A=G_L(K)$ as an
$S_A$-algebra: on each principal-open local chart one
also inverts that chart's denominators. These local
fractions and their agreement relations define the
sheaf, and give
\begin{equation}\tag{FCQ26}
(\mathcal M_Y)_{Q_{\mathfrak q}}=G_L(K),\qquad
(\mathcal M_Y)_{P_{\mathfrak p}}
       =L[(L\setminus\mathfrak p)^{-1}].
\end{equation}
The second equality holds because those additional
supported denominators are already the unit in the
support stalk, by FCQ24.

Define the full quotient on the actual split spectrum by
\begin{equation}\tag{FCQ27}
\widetilde{\mathscr C}_L(Y)
       =(\mathcal M_Y/\mathcal O_Y^\times)^\# .
\end{equation}
The orbit and addition relations are sheafified as in
FCQ20. At an arithmetic stalk its unit group is
$\widehat{A_{\mathfrak q}^\times}$, and at a support
stalk the only multiplicative unit of the lattice
semiring is its top: an invertible idempotent equals
the unit. Hence its exact two kinds of stalks are
\begin{equation}\tag{FCQ28}
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde{\mathscr C}_L(Y)_{Q_{\mathfrak q}}
   &=G_L(K)/A_{\mathfrak q}^\times,\\
\widetilde{\mathscr C}_L(Y)_{P_{\mathfrak p}}
   &=L[(L\setminus\mathfrak p)^{-1}].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The quotient--stalk commutation is proved by the same
finite-unit-witness argument as FCQ21. In the second
line the orbit action is trivial, and addition and
multiplication are the single-valued join and meet.
Thus both the arithmetic Cartier data and the support
open are calculated.

On the closed arithmetic subspace
$i:X\cong V_Y(e)\hookrightarrow Y$, restriction of
FCQ27 is canonically FCQ20. On a basic open
$D_Y(\widehat a)$ its arithmetic part is $D_X(a)$;
the fraction maps restrict to the identical split
amplitudes and unit sections there. These charts
give the map, and FCQ21 and FCQ28 prove it an
isomorphism on every arithmetic stalk. On the open
$D_Y(e)$, the restriction of FCQ27 is the ordinary
local-fraction sheaf of the lattice semiring $L$,
because inverting $e$ identifies both
$\mathcal O_Y$ and $\mathcal M_Y$ with that sheaf
and its unit quotient is trivial.

The generization map from an arithmetic stalk to a
support stalk has the exact formula
\begin{equation}\tag{FCQ29}
G_L(K)/A_{\mathfrak q}^\times
       \longrightarrow L[(L\setminus\mathfrak p)^{-1}],
\qquad
z_\ell\longmapsto[\ell]_F,\qquad
[\widehat x]\longmapsto[1_L]_F .
\end{equation}
It is well defined because every ordinary unit and
every nonzero amplitude becomes top there. It sends
each multivalued sum to the singleton join of its
images, including cancellation, since cancellation
has support $1_L$. Multiplication also commutes.
This is the localized support projection, and is
the actual connecting map between the two strata,
not an assertion that the support stratum is absent.

Equations FCQ23 and FCQ27--29 supply both requested
Cartier bridges. Over the ordinary arithmetic
subspace they retain the original unit-orbit
Cartier hyperfield together with every $z_\ell$.
Over the full split spectrum they additionally
retain the localized support sheaf and its exact
generization maps. The independent additive zero
$\tau$ and cancellation zero $e$ remain distinct
at arithmetic stalks; their behavior on the support
open is the explicitly calculated localization
$e=1$. None of these constructions assumes a
Frobenius weight, positivity theorem or purity.
