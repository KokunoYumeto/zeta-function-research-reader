\subsection{The exact matrix extension criterion}

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional complex vector space and
$A\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(V)$. Give $\operatorname{GL}(V)$
its usual complex topology. Fix a rational prime $p$ and put
\[
\rho_A(h)=\exp(hA),\qquad h\in H_p=\mathbb Z[1/p].
\tag{OLR1}
\]
This is an additive-group homomorphism into $\operatorname{GL}(V)$:
the absolutely convergent exponential series for the commuting
matrices $hA,tA$ gives $\rho_A(h+t)=\rho_A(h)\rho_A(t)$,
and $\rho_A(-h)$ is its inverse.

The following three statements are equivalent:
\[
\begin{gathered}
\rho_A\text{ extends continuously to }\mathbb Q_p;\qquad
\exp(p^r A)=I_V\text{ for some integer }r\ge0;\\
A\text{ is semisimple and every eigenvalue belongs to }
                          2\pi i\mathbb Z[1/p].
\end{gathered}
\tag{OLR2}
\]
Here semisimple means diagonalizable over $\mathbb C$; it is not a
claim that the original metric makes the eigenbasis orthogonal.
The same statements are equivalent to continuity of OLR1 on $H_p$
for its $p$-adic topology. The extension is unique.

Here is an elementary proof of necessity that retains nilpotents.
For any induced matrix norm, the neighbourhood
$\mathcal U=\{T:\|T-I_V\|<1/2\}$ contains no nontrivial subgroup.
Indeed, if a subgroup lies in $\mathcal U$, all positive and negative
integer powers of any one of its elements $T$ lie there.
For every eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $T$ this implies
$|\lambda^m-1|<1/2$ for all integers $m$.
Positive or negative powers exclude $|\lambda|\ne1$.
For $|\lambda|=1$, the same bound implies
$\Re\lambda^m>7/8$. If $\lambda\ne1$, the geometric sum gives
$n^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^n\lambda^m\to0$, contradicting that inequality.
Thus every eigenvalue is one, and $T=I_V+D$ with $D$ nilpotent.
If $D\ne0$, choose the largest $j$ with $D^j\ne0$. Some entry of
$T^m=\sum_{a=0}^j\binom maD^a$ is a polynomial of degree $j$
with nonzero leading coefficient, so its powers are unbounded.
This again contradicts membership in $\mathcal U$. Hence $T=I_V$.

Continuity at zero of an extension provides an integer $r\ge0$ with
$\rho_A(p^r\mathbb Z_p)\subset\mathcal U$; this image is a subgroup,
so it is trivial. In particular $\exp(p^rA)=I_V$.
The identical argument applied to the subgroup $p^r\mathbb Z$
inside $H_p$ proves this conclusion from continuity on $H_p$ alone.

On a Jordan primary space write $A=\alpha I+D$, $D^s=0$.
The equality $\exp(p^rA)=I$ first implies
$\exp(p^r\alpha)=1$, so $\alpha=2\pi i a/p^r$ for an integer $a$.
It then requires $\exp(p^rD)=I$. But
\[
\exp(p^rD)-I
=p^rD\left(\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\frac{(p^rD)^j}{(j+1)!}\right).
\tag{OLR3}
\]
The factor in parentheses is $I$ plus a nilpotent and is invertible,
with inverse given by a finite geometric sum. Thus $D=0$.
Conversely finitely many eigenvalues in $2\pi i\mathbb Z[1/p]$
have a common denominator $p^r$, and diagonalization proves
$\exp(p^rA)=I$.

Sufficiency and uniqueness can be proved directly without assuming
completeness of a particular matrix norm on the open set
$\operatorname{GL}(V)$. The inclusion induces a group isomorphism
\[
H_p/p^r\mathbb Z\ \xrightarrow{\ \simeq\ }\
                    \mathbb Q_p/p^r\mathbb Z_p.
\tag{OLR4}
\]
Its kernel is $H_p\cap p^r\mathbb Z_p=p^r\mathbb Z$.
It is onto because truncating the $p$-adic digits of any $x$ just
before exponent $r$ gives a finite element $h_x\in H_p$ with
$x-h_x\in p^r\mathbb Z_p$. Define
$\widetilde\rho_A(x)=\exp(h_xA)$.
Changing $h_x$ changes it by an integer multiple of $p^r$, so this
definition is independent of the choice. OLR4 proves the group law.
It is constant on the open cosets of $p^r\mathbb Z_p$, hence
continuous. Truncated digit expansions also prove density of $H_p$
in $\mathbb Q_p$, and agreement on a dense subset of a Hausdorff
target proves uniqueness. This completes OLR2.

Using the original ACF24 convention
$\psi_p(x)=\exp(2\pi i\{x\}_p)$, the explicit spectral formula is
\[
A=\sum_j2\pi i b_jP_j\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\widetilde\rho_A(x)=\sum_j\psi_p(b_jx)P_j,
\qquad b_j\in\mathbb Z[1/p].
\tag{OLR5}
\]
For $h\in H_p$, $b_jh-\{b_jh\}_p$ is an integer, proving agreement
with the original exponential. No phases or eigenspace projectors
have been changed.
For comparison, every $A$, including every Jordan nilpotent, gives
the continuous archimedean representation $t\mapsto\exp(tA)$ on
$\mathbb R$. Thus OLR2 describes the intersection of that matrix
family with the $p$-adic local family; it does not exclude the
archimedean local points admitted in the Connes--Consani source.

\subsection{The maximal admissible subspace and its exact defect}

For arbitrary $A$ define $D_r(A)=\exp(p^rA)-I$. Then
\[
\begin{split}
V_{p\text{-loc}}(A)
&=\bigcup_{r\ge0}\ker D_r(A)\\
&=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\operatorname{Spec}(A)\cap2\pi iH_p}
                             \ker(A-\alpha I).
\end{split}
\tag{OLR6}
\]
The kernels increase because $T^p-I=(T-I)\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}T^j$.
On an $\alpha$-primary space, if $e^{p^r\alpha}\ne1$, then
$D_r(A)$ is a nonzero scalar times $I$ plus a nilpotent, so is
invertible. If $e^{p^r\alpha}=1$, OLR3 proves that its kernel is
exactly the kernel of the primary nilpotent, the actual eigenspace.
This proves OLR6. Finitely many allowable $\alpha$ have a common
denominator, so the union is already $\ker D_{r_0}(A)$ for one
$r_0\ge0$ and remains that kernel for all $r\ge r_0$.
The restriction to this subspace satisfies OLR2. Conversely every
$A$-invariant subspace on which the restriction is $p$-adic local is
contained in OLR6 by OLR2. Moreover every linear subspace invariant
under $\rho_A(H_p)$ is $A$-invariant: for its vector $v$ the vectors
$p^n(\rho_A(p^{-n})-I)v$ belong to that subspace and converge to
$Av$ by the exponential series; finite-dimensional subspaces are
closed. Thus maximality applies to the original group action itself.

The defect is the original quotient, with its exact connecting map:
\[
\begin{gathered}
0\longrightarrow V_{p\text{-loc}}(A)\longrightarrow V
\xrightarrow{\pi}\mathfrak D_p(A):=V/V_{p\text{-loc}}(A)
\longrightarrow0,\\
\overline D_r:\mathfrak D_p(A)\xrightarrow{\ \simeq\ }
       \operatorname{im}D_r(A),\qquad
\overline D_r([v])=D_r(A)v\quad(r\ge r_0).
\end{gathered}
\tag{OLR7}
\]
It is well-defined and injective because its kernel before taking
the quotient is exactly OLR6; it is onto its displayed image.
All maps intertwine $A$ and $\exp(hA)$ because $D_r(A)$ commutes
with them. A good-eigenvalue Jordan block can have a local quotient
even though the entire block is not local. OLR7 retains that extension
through the original nilpotent; it does not claim a decomposition
into a local summand and a complementary invariant summand.

\subsection{Application to every label and cutoff of the original receiver}

Retain exactly
\[
\begin{gathered}
k\ge17,\quad k\equiv1\pmod4,\quad q=(k+1)^2,
\quad\delta,\gamma>0,\quad q-1\le N\le2q,\\
\lambda_{ab}=(2b-k)\gamma-i(2a-k)\delta,\quad
Q_k(y)=\prod_{a,b=0}^k(y-\lambda_{ab}),\\
E=\mathbb C[y]/(Q_k),\quad M[v]=[yv],\quad
A_p=i(\log p)M,\quad F_p=e^{A_p},\quad
\langle v,w\rangle_N=v^*G_Nw.
\end{gathered}
\tag{OLR8}
\]
The four cutoffs $q-1,q,2q-1,2q$ remain individual members of this
range. No replacement of $G_N$ is used.
The roots are distinct, since equality forces equality of real and
imaginary parts and then of both integer labels.
Let $\ell_{ab}$ be the original Lagrange polynomial with value one
at $\lambda_{ab}$ and zero at the other roots, and let $P_{ab}$ be
the projector onto its coefficient. Evaluation proves that these
vectors form a basis and $M\ell_{ab}=\lambda_{ab}\ell_{ab}$.
Thus the complete exponent and its locality are
\[
\begin{gathered}
A_p\ell_{ab}
=\bigl((2a-k)\delta\log p+i(2b-k)\gamma\log p\bigr)\ell_{ab},\\
E_{p\text{-loc}}(A_p)=0,\qquad
\mathfrak D_p(A_p)=E,\\
D_r(A_p)=\sum_{a,b}(\omega_{ab}^{p^r}-1)P_{ab},\qquad
D_r(A_p)^{-1}=\sum_{a,b}(\omega_{ab}^{p^r}-1)^{-1}P_{ab},\\
\omega_{ab}=p^{(2a-k)\delta}e^{i(2b-k)\gamma\log p}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{OLR9}
\]
Indeed $k$ is odd, so $2a-k\ne0$. Every exponent in OLR9 has
nonzero real part, excluding every label from OLR6. Every
$\omega_{ab}^{p^r}-1$ is nonzero for the same reason, proving the
displayed inverse. This is the entire original defect, not only
an observation of a selected eigenvalue.

For $v=\sum_i c_i\ell_i$ and $w=\sum_jd_j\ell_j$, the complete
original Gram form of the defect is
\[
\langle D_rv,D_rw\rangle_N
=\sum_{i,j}\overline c_i d_j
 \overline{(\omega_i^{p^r}-1)}(\omega_j^{p^r}-1)
                   \langle\ell_i,\ell_j\rangle_N.
\tag{OLR10}
\]
All off-diagonal Gram entries survive. On one original vector its
literal squared factor is
\[
\begin{split}
\|D_r\ell_{ab}\|_N^2
={}&\bigl[p^{2p^r(2a-k)\delta}
-2p^{p^r(2a-k)\delta}
 \cos\bigl(p^r(2b-k)\gamma\log p\bigr)+1\bigr]
                          \|\ell_{ab}\|_N^2.
\end{split}
\tag{OLR11}
\]
The topology failure therefore keeps the full phase as well as the
radial exponent. More precisely $D_rv\not\to0$ for any nonzero $v$.
If a coefficient with $2a-k>0$ is nonzero, applying its fixed
bounded projector to $D_rv$ proves divergence of that component.
Otherwise every occupied label has $2a-k<0$, and the finite
spectral sum gives $D_rv\to-v\ne0$.

The uncentred action is also retained:
\[
\widetilde A_p=A_p+\frac{k\log p}{2}I,\qquad
\widetilde F_p=e^{\widetilde A_p}=p^{k/2}F_p.
\tag{OLR12}
\]
Its admissible subspace is exactly the sum of those original
$\mathbb C\ell_{ab}$ for which
$k/2+(2a-k)\delta=0$ and
$(2b-k)\gamma\log p/(2\pi)\in H_p$.
This follows directly from OLR6 and keeps the mass factor.
For the stated general hypothesis $\delta>0$ one must retain both
conditions; discarding $k/2$ would be a different calculation.

\subsection{The original nilpotent and observation-kernel return}

Retain the programme vectors $e,f$ and the literal coefficient
$\epsilon>0$ with $e^\dagger f=0$ and
$\|e\|_N=\|f\|_N=1$. For $R=\epsilon fe^\dagger$, $R^2=0$,
and the complete locality calculation is
\[
E_{p\text{-loc}}(R)=\ker R=e^\perp,\qquad
D_r(R)=p^r\epsilon fe^\dagger,\qquad
\|D_r(R)v\|_N^2=p^{2r}\epsilon^2|e^\dagger v|^2.
\tag{OLR13}
\]
The exponential is exactly $I+hR$, proving the first two statements
by OLR6, and the original unit norm of $f$ proves the third.
Thus the nonzero nilpotent is not removed by the fact that its sole
eigenvalue is zero. This calculation concerns this specified $R$;
it does not replace $M+R$ by $M$ when the two fail to commute.
For any such sum, OLR3--OLR7 apply to its actual primary spaces.

For the original isometric section $J:B\to E$, retain
$P_B=JJ^\dagger$, $Q=I-P_B$, and $K=QE=\ker\Lambda$.
Put $U_r=J^\dagger F_p^{p^r}J$.
The complete visible and kernel defect is
\[
\begin{split}
D_r(A_p)Jb&=J(U_r-I)b+QF_p^{p^r}Jb,\\
\|D_r(A_p)Jb\|_N^2
&=\|(U_r-I)b\|_B^2+\|QF_p^{p^r}Jb\|_N^2.
\end{split}
\tag{OLR14}
\]
Insert $I=P_B+Q$ and use $QJ=0$ for the first identity.
The two terms are orthogonal in the original metric for the second.
The combined map on the right is injective: its vanishing gives
$D_r(A_p)Jb=0$, then OLR9 gives $Jb=0$, hence $b=0$.
No such conclusion about the visible term alone is asserted.
For arbitrary $h,t$ the original compression has the exact product
defect
\[
J^\dagger\rho_{A_p}(h+t)J
-J^\dagger\rho_{A_p}(h)J J^\dagger\rho_{A_p}(t)J
=J^\dagger\rho_{A_p}(h)Q\rho_{A_p}(t)J.
\tag{OLR15}
\]
Insertion of $I=P_B+Q$ proves it. Thus treating compression as a
group representation would remove precisely the displayed return.

\subsection{Full lattice support and the exact lifted correspondence}

For the full bounded distributive lattice $L$, keep
$V_L^\uparrow=\{(0,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}
\cup\{(v,1_L):v\in V\}$ over $G_L(\mathbb C)$, and
$T^\uparrow(v,\lambda)=(Tv,\lambda)$ for every linear $T$.
The component laws give
$(TU)^\uparrow=T^\uparrow U^\uparrow$ and
$(T+U)^\uparrow=T^\uparrow+U^\uparrow$ directly.
In particular the zero linear map lifts to
$\varepsilon_V(v,\lambda)=(0,\lambda)$, not to the map constant
at $\tau=(0,0_L)$.
The exact support-retaining version of OLR7 is
\[
\begin{gathered}
D_r(A)^\uparrow
=\overline D_r^{\,\uparrow}\pi^\uparrow,\qquad
(D_r(A)^\uparrow)^{-1}(Z_L)
=V_{p\text{-loc}}(A)_L^\uparrow\quad(r\ge r_0),\\
Z_L=\{(0,\lambda):\lambda\in L\},\qquad
\pi^\uparrow(v,\lambda)=([v],\lambda).
\end{gathered}
\tag{OLR16}
\]
Equality is checked on each $(v,\lambda)$; membership in the
inverse image is exactly $D_r(A)v=0$ with its support unchanged.
The fibre of $\pi^\uparrow$ over $(0,\lambda)$ for $\lambda<1_L$
is the singleton $(0,\lambda)$; over $(0,1_L)$ it is
$V_{p\text{-loc}}(A)\times\{1_L\}$.
For the original $A_p$, OLR9 says that the inverse image in OLR16
is precisely $Z_L$, and the defect lift is an automorphism on the
whole synchronized carrier. In particular no support label is erased.

With $L$ discrete and the carrier a subspace of $V\times L$,
the lifted representation is continuous whenever its matrix
representation is continuous: its action is
$(h,(v,\lambda))\mapsto(\rho_A(h)v,\lambda)$.
Every zero state is fixed. This does not turn a zero-valued monoid
character $T^h\mapsto z_\lambda$ into a group-valued local point:
the fixed vector of a representation and the value of a character
are different specified maps, and $z_\lambda$ is not a unit.

The original free-coordinate support information has its exact
correspondence as well for a retained basis of positive dimension.
In that basis write a free vector as
$(v,\boldsymbol\mu)$, where $v_i\ne0$ forces $\mu_i=1_L$.
The idempotent synchronization is
\[
P_{\rm syn}(v,\boldsymbol\mu)=(v,\bigvee_i\mu_i),\qquad
P_{\rm syn}^{-1}(v,\lambda)
=\{(v,\boldsymbol\mu):\bigvee_i\mu_i=\lambda,
                         \ v_i\ne0\Rightarrow\mu_i=1_L\}.
\tag{OLR17}
\]
This is the matrix pair $(I,\mathbf1)$ on free coordinates:
its amplitude is $I$ and its support sends every output coordinate
to the full finite join. Its image is exactly the synchronized
carrier, and its square is itself, proving OLR17.
Taking the inverse image of OLR16 retains all masks with
$v\in V_{p\text{-loc}}(A)$. In the original case these are all
zero-amplitude masks, with arbitrary $\boldsymbol\mu$.
No group action with the free-module identity has been inferred
from an action on this retract.
For dimension zero the free zero-coordinate semimodule has one
element, with empty support join $0_L$, whereas the synchronized
carrier of the zero complex vector space still contains every
$(0,\lambda)$, $\lambda\in L$. Thus OLR17 is not a surjection
onto that latter carrier in dimension zero unless $L$ has one
element. The original receiver and its positive tensor powers have
dimensions $q>0$ and $q^t>0$, so the stated correspondence applies
to them, including their zero-amplitude fibres.

\subsection{Frobenius, the complete twistor return, and the boundary}

Source Frobenius acts by precomposition $h\mapsto mh$, so its
operator parameter is exactly $A\mapsto mA$.
For $m=p^j$, $j\in\mathbb Z$, OLR6 gives
$V_{p\text{-loc}}(p^jA)=V_{p\text{-loc}}(A)$, because multiplication
by $p^j$ preserves $H_p$ and the eigenspace kernels.
OLR5 gives the corresponding $p$-adic parameters $b_j\mapsto p^j b_j$.
For a general positive integer $m$ the exact condition is
$m\alpha\in2\pi iH_p$; primes dividing $m$ other than $p$ can
enlarge the admissible subspace. For $m=0$ the action is identity
on the whole space. These statements retain every source Frobenius.

Keep every positive odd $d$, $\chi_d=(-1)^{(d-1)/2}$, and the
original receiver
\[
C_d(v_0,\ldots,v_{d-1})
=(\chi_d F_p^{\chi_d}v_{d-1},v_0,\ldots,v_{d-2}),\qquad
G_{N,d}=\operatorname{diag}(G_N,\ldots,G_N).
\tag{OLR18}
\]
Let $\iota_s,\pi_s$ be the literal block inclusion and projection.
The identity $C_d^d=\operatorname{diag}(\chi_dF_p^{\chi_d},\ldots)$
proves the exact recovery of every locality defect:
\[
\begin{split}
D_r(A_p)
&=\chi_d^{p^r}\pi_s C_d^{\chi_d d p^r}\iota_s-I_E\\
&=\chi_d^{p^r}p^{-k\chi_d d p^r/2}
       \pi_s(p^{k/2}C_d)^{\chi_d d p^r}\iota_s-I_E.
\end{split}
\tag{OLR19}
\]
Negative powers in the $\chi_d=-1$ case are legitimate because the
receiver is invertible. The powers of the sign multiply to one,
and the scalar mass in the second equality is explicitly cancelled
by its displayed inverse factor; it has not been omitted.
Thus the original nonzero defect has an exact return through every
odd receiver and every original block.

The explicit logarithm on the full TOB16 eigenbasis has eigenvalues
\[
\nu_{ab,t}=
\frac{\chi_di(\log p)\lambda_{ab}
       +i\pi(1-\chi_d)/2+2\pi it}{d},\qquad0\le t<d.
\tag{OLR20}
\]
Its exponential is $C_d$. The eigenvectors
$(\ell_{ab},e^{-\nu_{ab,t}}\ell_{ab},\ldots,
 e^{-(d-1)\nu_{ab,t}}\ell_{ab})$ form a basis: within each label
the $d$ distinct roots give an invertible Vandermonde matrix.
Every real part is $\chi_d(2a-k)\delta\log p/d\ne0$.
OLR6 therefore gives zero admissible subspace for this entire
$dq$-dimensional exponential family, despite its contracted real
parts. The complete Gram entries remain TOB17, not a diagonal
substitute for $G_{N,d}$.

The same return is visible in the complete original boundary.
Put $T_d=\chi_dF_p^{\chi_d}$ and keep the TOB26--TOB28 maps
$\mathcal B_{d,H}$ and $L_{d,H}$, for $H\ge0$. Their exact norms are
\[
\begin{split}
\|\mathcal B_{d,H}v\|^2
&=p^k\sum_{s=0}^{d-1}
       (\|T_d^{-H}v_s\|_N^2+\|T_d^{H+1}v_s\|_N^2),\\
\|L_{d,H}v\|^2
&=\sum_{j=-H}^H\sum_{s=0}^{d-1}\|T_d^{-j}v_s\|_N^2.
\end{split}
\tag{OLR21}
\]
For completeness these maps use the original orthonormal annuli
\[
h_j(\mathbf x)=p^{kj/2}(1-p^{-1})^{-k/2}
\mathbf1_{(p^j\mathbb Z_p^\times)^k}(\mathbf x).
\]
The complete finite receiver has coordinates
$x_{d\ell+s}=T_d^{-\ell}v_s$.
The boundary at $dH+s$ consists exactly of
$p^{k/2}(h_{dH+s+1}\otimes x_{dH+s}
 -h_{-dH-s}\otimes x_{-dH-s-1})$.
Its two annuli are disjoint; summing over $s$ gives the first line.
The interval $[-dH,dH+d-1]$ gives the second line by the same
orthogonality. Invertibility of $T_d$ makes the joint boundary
injective. On blocks in $\mathbb C\ell_{ab}$, finite geometric
summation gives
\[
\frac{\|\mathcal B_{d,H}v\|^2}{\|L_{d,H}v\|^2}
\longrightarrow p^k\left|p^{2\chi_d(2a-k)\delta}-1\right|>0.
\tag{OLR22}
\]
Indeed the ratio is
$p^k(R^{-2H}+R^{2H+2})/\sum_{j=-H}^HR^{-2j}$,
$R=p^{\chi_d(2a-k)\delta}\ne1$; divide by the largest
geometric term to obtain the limit. OLR19 and OLR21 are the
explicit maps linking this complete boundary with locality's
unchanged original prime return.

\subsection{All tensor labels and the nonzero local tensor sector}

For $t\ge1$, retain the entire original tensor object
$E^{\otimes t}$ with metric $G_N^{\otimes t}$ and generator
$A_p^{[t]}=\sum_{j=1}^tI^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes A_p\otimes
I^{\otimes(t-j)}$.
The commuting summands give
$e^{hA_p^{[t]}}=\rho_{A_p}(h)^{\otimes t}$.
On the original vector
$\ell_{a_1b_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\ell_{a_tb_t}$ its exponent is
\[
\bigl(2\textstyle\sum_j a_j-tk\bigr)\delta\log p
+i\bigl(2\textstyle\sum_j b_j-tk\bigr)\gamma\log p.
\tag{OLR23}
\]
The tensor of the original Lagrange bases is a basis, so OLR6
proves that the exact admissible projector is
\[
\Pi_{p,t}=\sum_{\substack{\sum a_j=tk/2\\
(2\sum b_j-tk)\gamma\log p/(2\pi)\in H_p}}
       P_{a_1b_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes P_{a_tb_t}.
\tag{OLR24}
\]
An empty index set means the zero projector. These spectral
projectors need not be orthogonal for $G_N^{\otimes t}$;
that metric remains unchanged, with entries the products of
the original Gram entries.
The entire complementary defect also has an explicit receiver:
$[v]\mapsto(I-\Pi_{p,t})v$ is an isomorphism from
$E^{\otimes t}/\operatorname{im}\Pi_{p,t}$ to
$\ker\Pi_{p,t}$, with inverse sending a vector to its quotient
class. Its kernel before passing to the quotient is exactly the
admissible range; $\Pi_{p,t}$ commutes with the tensor generator,
so both maps intertwine the complete exponential action.
This is a spectral direct sum, not an orthogonal replacement of
the metric. OLR7 and OLR16 apply to all its retained components.
Since $k$ is odd, the admissible space is zero for odd $t$.
For even $t$, define
$n_{t,j}=[x^j](1+x+\cdots+x^k)^t$.
Counting the independent $a$- and $b$-tuples gives its exact dimension
\[
\dim\operatorname{im}\Pi_{p,t}
=n_{t,tk/2}\sum_{j=0}^{tk}n_{t,j}\,
 \mathbf1_{(2j-tk)\gamma\log p/(2\pi)\in H_p}.
\tag{OLR25}
\]
Each coefficient counts precisely the tuples with the indicated
sum by expansion of the polynomial product, proving the count.

In particular at $t=2$ the original $q$ independent vectors
\[
\ell_{ab}\otimes\ell_{k-a,k-b},\qquad0\le a,b\le k,
\tag{OLR26}
\]
have exponent zero and hence the trivial local character.
There can be additional labels in OLR24 with nonzero allowed
phase, and OLR25 retains them exactly. Thus the local tensor
sector is nonzero although the individual receiver has no
nonzero local amplitude. This calculation cannot be replaced by
tensoring the zero admissible subspace of the individual factor.
The involution $R_0\ell_{ab}=\ell_{k-a,k-b}$ satisfies
$R_0MR_0=-M$ by evaluation on each basis vector, so
$R_0F_pR_0=F_p^{-1}$. It identifies explicitly the reverse label
in every vector OLR26. Their Gram entry at labels $i,j$ is
$\langle\ell_i,\ell_j\rangle_N
 \langle R_0\ell_i,R_0\ell_j\rangle_N$; no unproved isometry
of $R_0$ in the original metric is assumed.

All mass factors also tensor. For the uncentred generator
\[
\widetilde A_p^{[t]}=A_p^{[t]}+\frac{tk\log p}{2}I,
\tag{OLR27}
\]
the first condition in OLR24 becomes
$tk/2+(2\sum a_j-tk)\delta=0$; its phase condition is unchanged.
In particular the vectors OLR26 have uncentred exponent
$k\log p$, so their centred trivial characters do not make
the uncentred characters $p$-adic local.

For a general primary tensor block, the same calculation retains
all nilpotents. If its factor generators are $\alpha_jI+D_j$,
the tensor generator is $(\sum_j\alpha_j)I+D_{\rm tot}$ with
$D_{\rm tot}=\sum_jI\otimes D_j\otimes I$ nilpotent.
OLR3 proves that its admissible space is exactly $\ker D_{\rm tot}$
when $\sum_j\alpha_j\in2\pi iH_p$, and zero otherwise.
For the original rank-one $R$, its tensor generator
$R^{[t]}=\sum_jI\otimes R\otimes I$ satisfies
\[
e^{hR^{[t]}}=(I+hR)^{\otimes t},\qquad
(R^{[t]})^{t+1}=0,\qquad
(R^{[t]})^t=t!R^{\otimes t}\ne0,
\quad E_{p\text{-loc}}(R^{[t]})=\ker R^{[t]}.
\tag{OLR28}
\]
Commutativity of the factor operators gives the exponential;
every repeated slot in a product vanishes since $R^2=0$, and
the $t!$ orders using every slot give the last nonzero power.
OLR3 gives the final kernel. All these tensor maps admit the
support-preserving lifts OLR16--OLR17 on their actual full carriers.

Its kernel dimension can be calculated exactly, with the original
rank-two support projector $\Pi=ee^\dagger+ff^\dagger$:
\[
\boxed{
\dim\ker R^{[t]}
=\sum_{j=0}^t\binom tj(q-2)^{t-j}
                         \binom{j}{\lfloor j/2\rfloor}.}
\tag{OLR29}
\]
Here $R$ and $\epsilon$ are precisely those in OLR13.
To prove the formula, let $W=\operatorname{im}\Pi=\mathbb Ce\oplus
\mathbb Cf$ and $U=\ker\Pi$, an orthogonal decomposition for
the unchanged $G_N$. The original vectors $e,f$ are orthonormal;
$Re=\epsilon f$, $Rf=0$, and $R|_U=0$.
Tensoring the exact decomposition $E=W\oplus U$ gives a direct
orthogonal sum indexed by the set of slots occupied by $W$.
A sector with $j$ such slots has operator $X_j$ on $W^{\otimes j}$,
the sum of the original $R$ over these slots, tensored with the
identity on the remaining $U$ factors.

Grade $W^{\otimes j}$ by the number $r$ of factors equal to $f$.
Its grade-$r$ subspace $V_r$ has dimension $\binom jr$.
The operator $X_j$ maps $V_r$ to $V_{r+1}$, with coefficient
$\epsilon$ on every replacement of one $e$ by $f$.
Its original-metric adjoint $Y_j=X_j^\dagger$ reverses those
replacements with the same real coefficient $\epsilon$.
Terms in different slots commute. In one slot
$(R^\dagger R-RR^\dagger)e=\epsilon^2e$ and
$(R^\dagger R-RR^\dagger)f=-\epsilon^2f$.
Consequently
\[
(Y_jX_j-X_jY_j)|_{V_r}=\epsilon^2(j-2r)I_{V_r},\qquad
\|X_jv\|^2-\|Y_jv\|^2
=\epsilon^2(j-2r)\|v\|^2.
\tag{OLR30}
\]
Thus $X_j:V_r\to V_{r+1}$ is injective when $r<j/2$.
The isometry interchanging $e$ and $f$ in each of the $j$ slots
interchanges $X_j$ and $Y_j$ and sends grade $r+1$ to grade
$j-r-1$. For $r\ge j/2$ that latter grade is less than $j/2$,
so $Y_j:V_{r+1}\to V_r$ is injective by the result just proved.
Its adjoint $X_j:V_r\to V_{r+1}$ is then surjective.
The total kernel dimension therefore telescopes:
\[
\dim\ker X_j
=\sum_{r=\lceil j/2\rceil}^j
      \left(\binom jr-\binom j{r+1}\right)
=\binom j{\lfloor j/2\rfloor}.
\tag{OLR31}
\]
For odd $j$, the two middle grades have equal dimension and the
injective intervening map is an isomorphism, consistent with this
sum. For $j=0$, $W^{\otimes0}=\mathbb C$ and $X_0=0$, so the
same formula gives one. Each $j$-slot sector has the further
multiplicity $(q-2)^{t-j}$ and there are $\binom tj$ such sectors.
Their direct sum proves OLR29. No coefficient $\epsilon$, tensor
slot, or original metric term was removed.

The original single receiver, its nilpotent channels, its complete
observation return, its odd twistor receivers, and its tensor
local sector have thus been placed in the specified local-point
framework by exact maps. The calculation selects no locality
condition that Connes--Consani impose on all points: their
archimedean points still include every original exponential above.
It therefore neither proves RH by imposing $p$-locality nor
produces an RH counterexample by exhibiting a nonlocal receiver.
