This working paper reconstructs specified parts of the original
arithmetic programme over its actual lattice coefficient object
$G_L(\mathbb C)$. The previously used $G_{\mathbb B}(\mathbb C)$ is a
Boolean special case; its spectrum is not a description of the full
support spectrum. The foundational lattice results below are credited
to the retained Version 11 source. The new calculations construct
the exact recovered carriers, maps, original metric, mixed kernels,
and the map from prime-support defects to Weil's local distribution.

The central algebraic recovery is the path closure of the original
companion operator. Its support graph forces a synchronized carrier
which retains the entire lattice $L$. The original linear maps and
prime group action lift on that carrier, and all preimages of its
projection from the full free semimodule are calculated. Supported
zero maps remain distinct from actual absence. The calculation also
exhibits a higher mixed kernel invisible to individual branch
linearizations and computes an exact inverse recovering all joint
support moments. An explicit compact test detects any prescribed
prime-support defect, with both Weil moment conditions satisfied.

The recovery also supplies a pointed category whose zero morphisms
are the retained supported zeros. Its exact kernels, cokernels and
cohomology recover the original complex. For the actual companion
prime matrices, an invertible lift to the entire free semimodule is
impossible; a proved fibre groupoid and its prime correspondences
retain every mask with their precise composition laws. The full
additive support jet and all extensions of the earlier scalar action
are calculated. Joint tensor observations then recover every finite
mixed support coefficient and connect the support-addition operators
to the diagonal trace insertions through an explicit incidence algebra.

These are recovery and defect calculations, not a completed proof or
disproof of the Riemann hypothesis. No sign for the full-place Weil
form is assumed. In particular a counterexample to a restricted
place functional is explicitly identified as such.

\section{The full support lattice: stalks, residues, and exact branch recovery}

\paragraph{Original source and correction of scope.}
The source is The Clankers, \emph{Split Support Geometry: Universal
Zero Fibres, Arithmetic Curves, and Frobenius-Perfect Quantization},
June 2026, pre-publication draft Version 11, file
\texttt{split\_support\_geometry\_arithmetic\_curve\_v11.tex},
SHA-256 \texttt{5fa6a55aa9d1a2e57599e206087d3d2466b25dd99fa1a90d5f86367aafab1b4d}.
Its Definition \texttt{def:lattice-split} and the existing theorems
on prime classification, the spectral ordinal sum, support
localization, Birkhoff support coordinates, and the branch-selected
spectrum already construct and prove
the full lattice model, its spectrum, finite support coordinates,
and branch selection. Those are the source's results, not new
discoveries of this continuation. Its bibliography credits
Garrett Birkhoff, \emph{Lattice Theory}, third edition (1967),
for the lattice-theoretic background. The local calculations
and explicit infinite-point distinctions below apply that
source without replacing its lattice by one Boolean branch.

Let $R$ be a nonzero commutative unital ring and $L$ a nontrivial
bounded distributive lattice. The original object is
\[
A=G_L(R)=\{z_\lambda=(0,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}
\cup\{(r,1_L):r\in R\},
\]
\[
(r,\lambda)+(s,\mu)=(r+s,\lambda\vee\mu),
\quad (r,\lambda)(s,\mu)=(rs,\lambda\wedge\mu).
\tag{FLS1}
\]
Write $Z_L=\{z_\lambda:\lambda\in L\}$,
$\tau_L=z_{0_L}$ and $e_L=z_{1_L}$. The source's prime and
topology theorem gives
\[
P_{\mathfrak p}=\{z_\lambda:\lambda\in\mathfrak p\}
\quad(\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{SpecLat}L),\qquad
Q_{\mathfrak q}=Z_L\cup\{(r,1_L):r\in\mathfrak q\}
\quad(\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Spec}R),
\tag{FLS2}
\]
with every $P_{\mathfrak p}$ strictly below every
$Q_{\mathfrak q}$. Its principal opens are
\[
D(z_\lambda)=\{P_{\mathfrak p}:\lambda\notin\mathfrak p\},
\qquad
D((r,1_L))=\operatorname{SpecLat}L\cup D_R(r).
\tag{FLS3}
\]
In the following, stalk means the prime localization of the
usual sheaf of local fractions for this semiring-prime
spectrum. None of these local computations substitutes the
underlying multiplicative-monoid spectrum or the twisted
prime-congruence spectrum for (FLS2).

\subsection{Support stalks are localized lattices, not generally Boolean}

Fix $\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{SpecLat}L$ and set
$F=L\setminus\mathfrak p$. This is the complementary prime
filter. Since $e_L\notin P_{\mathfrak p}$, first invert
$e_L$. The source's support-localization theorem gives
$A[e_L^{-1}]\cong L$, and a supported amplitude maps to
$1_L$. The remaining denominators are exactly the elements
of $F$. Hence the full support stalk is
\[
A_{P_{\mathfrak p}}\cong L[F^{-1}].
\tag{FLS4}
\]
This can be written without unspecified fractions. An
idempotent which becomes invertible becomes the unit, so
every element of $F$ becomes $1$. Every fraction is therefore
represented by an element of $L$. The exact equality relation is
\[
\lambda\sim_F\mu
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists\nu\in F:\ \nu\wedge\lambda=\nu\wedge\mu,
\qquad L[F^{-1}]=L/{\sim_F}.
\tag{FLS5}
\]
The equivalence follows directly from multiplicative fraction
equality. It is also a lattice congruence: reflexivity uses
$1_L\in F$; symmetry is immediate; transitivity uses the
meet of two witnesses, which remains in $F$. Meeting a witness
with a common factor proves compatibility with meets, and
distributivity proves compatibility with joins. All joins,
meets, zero and unit in this stalk are therefore explicit.

The elements outside $\mathfrak p$ all have class $1$.
An element $\lambda\in\mathfrak p$ cannot have class $1$:
a witness would give $\nu\wedge\lambda=\nu$ with
$\nu\in F$, hence $\nu\leq\lambda$ and $\nu\in\mathfrak p$,
a contradiction. Thus the stalk has the unique maximal ideal
\[
\mathfrak m_{\mathfrak p}
=\{[\lambda]_F:\lambda\in\mathfrak p\},
\qquad
L[F^{-1}]/_{\rm Bourne}\mathfrak m_{\mathfrak p}
\cong\mathbb B.
\tag{FLS6}
\]
For maximality, the only multiplicative unit of any lattice
semiring is its top: an invertible idempotent is the unit.
All other classes here lie in $\mathfrak m_{\mathfrak p}$,
which is an ideal. For the quotient statement, the Bourne
congruence identifies every element of this ideal with zero,
while all elements outside it are already $1$. It cannot
identify $1$ with zero: an equality $1\vee i=j$ with both
$i,j\in\mathfrak m_{\mathfrak p}$ would put $1$ in the ideal.
This proves the two-element residue. It does not prove that
the stalk before quotienting has two elements.

For finite $L$, the source's finite Birkhoff theorem identifies
$F$ with the principal filter $\uparrow j$ of a unique
nonzero join-irreducible element $j$. Then
\[
L[F^{-1}]\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\downarrow j,
\qquad[\lambda]_F\longmapsto\lambda\wedge j,
\qquad \downarrow j=\{\mu:\mu\leq j\},
\tag{FLS7}
\]
where the unit on the right is $j$. A witness in (FLS5)
implies equality after meeting with $j$, since $j$ is below
every witness. Conversely equality after meeting with $j$
uses $j$ itself as witness. Surjectivity follows by taking
$\lambda=\mu\leq j$. Distributivity proves that this
bijection preserves both operations. Thus it is the claimed
semiring isomorphism, with every support below $j$ retained.

For the source's chain example $L=\{0<a<1\}$ this gives
\[
A_{P_{\{0\}}}\cong\{0,a\}\cong\mathbb B,
\qquad
A_{P_{\{0,a\}}}\cong\{0,a,1\}.
\tag{FLS8}
\]
The second stalk is not Boolean, although its residue (FLS6)
is Boolean. This is an explicit counterexample to the
assertion that every support stalk is $\mathbb B$.

If $L$ is any Boolean algebra, finite or infinite, every
support stalk is instead $\mathbb B$. Indeed, for
$\lambda\in\mathfrak p$, its Boolean complement
$\neg\lambda$ is in $F$, and
$(\neg\lambda)\wedge\lambda=0$. Formula (FLS5) therefore
makes $[\lambda]_F=0$. Elements in $F$ have class $1$,
so the exact isomorphism is the branch itself:
\[
L[F^{-1}]\cong\mathbb B,
\qquad[\lambda]_F\longmapsto
\epsilon_{\mathfrak p}(\lambda)
=\mathbf1_{\lambda\notin\mathfrak p}
\quad(L\text{ Boolean}).
\tag{FLS9}
\]

\subsection{Arithmetic stalks retain the entire lattice}

Fix $\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Spec}R$.
The complement of $Q_{\mathfrak q}$ consists precisely of
the supported amplitudes $(s,1_L)$ with $s\notin\mathfrak q$.
The complete localization is
\[
A_{Q_{\mathfrak q}}\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }G_L(R_{\mathfrak q}),
\qquad
\frac{(r,1_L)}{(s,1_L)}\longmapsto(r/s,1_L),
\qquad
\frac{z_\lambda}{(s,1_L)}\longmapsto z_\lambda.
\tag{FLS10}
\]
The fraction operations verify the map. Equality between
supported fractions is exactly ring fraction equality;
when their amplitude becomes zero the image is $z_{1_L}$.
Multiplication by any supported denominator leaves each
$z_\lambda$ unchanged, so two such lower-support fractions
are equal precisely when their lattice labels are equal.
A supported fraction cannot become $z_\lambda$ for
$\lambda<1_L$, because every fraction-equality witness
preserves the support $1_L$ on its supported side. These
facts prove injectivity; all displayed target elements
have the given preimages, proving surjectivity.

The supported and amplitude residue maps consequently are
\[
G_L(R_{\mathfrak q})\twoheadrightarrow
G_L(\kappa(\mathfrak q))\xrightarrow{p_L}\kappa(\mathfrak q).
\tag{FLS11}
\]
The first identifies represented amplitudes with the same
residue but keeps all $z_\lambda$ distinct. The composite
is the Bourne quotient by the maximal amplitude ideal,
which identifies the entire zero lattice with the field
zero. This proves the different residue fibres exactly.
For $R=\mathbb Z$, both the generic arithmetic stalk
$G_L(\mathbb Q)$ and every finite-place stalk
$G_L(\mathbb Z_{(p)})$ still contain the full lattice $L$.

\subsection{A single branch and all branches have different exact fibres}

Let $X=\operatorname{SpecLat}L$ and write
$\epsilon_x:L\to\mathbb B$ for its branch at $x$.
The source's branch quotient is
$\beta_x:G_L(R)\to G_{\mathbb B}(R)$, acting as identity
on the represented amplitudes and as $\epsilon_x$ on the
zero lattice. Its spectral inclusion selects exactly
$\{x\}\blacktriangleright\operatorname{Spec}R$.
The maps on its stalks, calculated above, are
\[
L[(L\setminus x)^{-1}]\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb B
\quad\text{at the selected support point},
\]
\[
G_L(R_{\mathfrak q})\twoheadrightarrow G_{\mathbb B}(R_{\mathfrak q})
\quad\text{at an arithmetic point}.
\tag{FLS12}
\]
The first is the residue map (FLS6); the second applies
$\epsilon_x$ to the entire remaining zero lattice. These
are the exact maps by which choosing one branch can lose
support even when the amplitude is unchanged.

For finite $L$, the source already proves that all branches
recover $L$: their coordinate patterns are the order ideals
of its join-irreducible poset $J(L)$. We retain that result
and extend its concrete comparison to arbitrary bounded
distributive $L$. The needed prime-separation argument is
included. If $\lambda\nleq\mu$, the lattice ideal
$\downarrow\mu$ and filter $\uparrow\lambda$ are disjoint.
By the maximal principle choose an ideal $I$ maximal among
those containing the first and avoiding the second. It is
prime. Indeed if $a,b\notin I$, the ideals obtained by
adjoining $a$ and $b$ each meet the filter. There are
$f,g$ in the filter and $i,j\in I$ such that
$f\leq i\vee a$ and $g\leq j\vee b$. Distributivity gives
\[
f\wedge g\leq(i\vee a)\wedge(j\vee b)
\leq i\vee j\vee(a\wedge b).
\tag{FLS13}
\]
If $a\wedge b\in I$, the right-hand side belongs to $I$,
so the filter would meet $I$, a contradiction. Thus $I$
is prime, contains $\mu$, and omits $\lambda$. This is
the classical distributive-lattice prime-separation
argument, used here with its choice principle explicit.

It follows that the simultaneous branch map is injective:
\[
\boldsymbol\beta:G_L(R)\hookrightarrow
\prod_{x\in X}G_{\mathbb B}(R),
\qquad a\longmapsto(\beta_x(a))_{x\in X}.
\tag{FLS14}
\]
Distinct nonzero amplitudes, or a nonzero and a zero
amplitude, are separated by any coordinate. Two elements
of the zero lattice are separated by (FLS13). Its image
is exactly the families with a common amplitude $r$ such
that, when $r=0$, the set of coordinates having supported
zero is $D_L(\lambda)$ for one $\lambda\in L$:
\[
\operatorname{im}\boldsymbol\beta
=\{((r,1))_{x\in X}:r\in R\}
\cup\{((0,\epsilon_x(\lambda)))_{x\in X}:\lambda\in L\}.
\tag{FLS15}
\]
This gives the exact consistency restriction on a family
of branches; arbitrary independent branch values are not
automatically a full-support element.

The allowable support patterns in (FLS15) are exactly
the compact opens of $X$. The identities
$D_L(\lambda\vee\mu)=D_L(\lambda)\cup D_L(\mu)$ and
$D_L(\lambda\wedge\mu)=D_L(\lambda)\cap D_L(\mu)$ follow
from the prime-ideal axioms. To prove compactness, suppose
$D_L(\lambda)\subseteq\bigcup_iD_L(\mu_i)$ has no finite
subcover. Then $\lambda$ is not below any finite join of
the $\mu_i$, by the separation argument. The ideal
generated by all the $\mu_i$ is disjoint from
$\uparrow\lambda$; applying the same maximal-ideal proof
produces a prime containing every $\mu_i$ and omitting
$\lambda$, contradicting the cover. Every compact open
has a finite cover by basic opens, so it is itself one
basic open. We have proved the exact recovery map
\[
L\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\mathcal K\mathcal O(X),
\qquad \lambda\longmapsto D_L(\lambda),
\tag{FLS16}
\]
where $\mathcal K\mathcal O(X)$ is the lattice of compact
open subsets with union and intersection. This is the
classical spectral representation applied to the original
zero lattice, not a replacement of that lattice by a
single stalk. A chosen branch alone records only whether
$x\in D_L(\lambda)$, identifying all labels on each of
the two sides of that membership test.

For finite $L$ the lost-support objects can be given still
more explicitly using the source's Birkhoff coordinates.
Let $J=J(L)$, retain only a subset $T\subseteq J$ of the
branches, and suppose the observed positive coordinates
are $U\subseteq T$. The entire zero-support fibre is
\[
\{I\in\operatorname{Idl}(J):I\cap T=U\}
=\left[\downarrow U,\ J\setminus\uparrow(T\setminus U)\right]
\subseteq\operatorname{Idl}(J),
\tag{FLS23}
\]
where the interval is empty when its lower bound is not
contained in its upper bound. To prove this, containing
$U$ forces the order ideal $I$ to contain $\downarrow U$.
Omitting every point of $T\setminus U$ forces it to omit
their upward closure. Conversely every order ideal between
these two bounds contains the required positive coordinates
and none of the negative coordinates. The lower and upper
bounds are themselves order ideals, so this is the exact
interval of omitted information, with its inherited joins
and meets and its inclusion into $L$ through Birkhoff's
map. For a single branch $j$ the two fibres are therefore
\[
\epsilon_j^{-1}(0)\cong
[\varnothing,J\setminus\uparrow j],
\qquad
\epsilon_j^{-1}(1)\cong[\downarrow j,J].
\tag{FLS24}
\]
Every branch is necessary for full reconstruction when $L$
is finite: if $j$ is omitted, the two order ideals
$\downarrow j$ and $(\downarrow j)\setminus\{j\}$ have
identical values at every retained branch and are distinct.
This proves the failure of that observation by constructing
its exact fibre, rather than replacing the lost states by
an unspecified error.

\subsection{Infinite Boolean branches and complete-frame points}

For $L=\mathcal P(I)$, bounded lattice homomorphisms
$L\to\mathbb B$ are exactly ultrafilters $U$ on $I$,
with $\epsilon_U(A)=\mathbf1_{A\in U}$. This follows
because finite meets and joins give a prime filter,
and complementation gives exactly one of $A,I\setminus A$
in it. Conversely that ultrafilter rule preserves the
two finite operations. All these branches belong to
the semiring-prime spectrum used in (FLS2), including
nonprincipal ultrafilters when they exist. A point of
the \emph{complete frame}, however, must preserve
arbitrary joins. Such a branch is exactly evaluation
at a single $i\in I$:
\[
\epsilon_i(A)=\mathbf1_{i\in A},\qquad
\epsilon_U\left(\bigcup_{i\in I}\{i\}\right)=1
\ne\bigvee_{i\in I}\epsilon_U(\{i\})=0
\quad(U\text{ nonprincipal}).
\tag{FLS17}
\]
The displayed equation proves failure of complete-join
preservation for a nonprincipal branch. Conversely a
complete-join preserving branch applied to
$I=\bigcup_i\{i\}$ must send some singleton to $1$;
the filter laws then make it evaluation at that element.
Evaluation branches alone already recover $\mathcal P(I)$
by all its membership coordinates, but they are not all
the bounded-lattice prime points when $I$ is infinite.

More generally, for a complete Boolean algebra $L$,
complete-frame points are exactly its atoms. A bounded
lattice branch preserves complements. If it also preserves
arbitrary joins, De Morgan's law shows it preserves
arbitrary meets. For its filter $F$, put
$a=\bigwedge_{f\in F}f$. Its value at $a$ is $1$, so
$a\ne0$ and $a\in F$. If $0<b<a$, either $b\in F$,
contradicting minimality of $a$, or $\neg b\in F$,
contradicting $a\leq\neg b$. Thus $a$ is an atom and
the branch is $\lambda\mapsto\mathbf1_{a\leq\lambda}$.
Conversely an atom supplies a completely join-preserving
branch: finite meet with that atom distributes over any
join, and each resulting meet is either zero or the atom.
Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{\rm Frm}(L,\mathbb B)
\cong\operatorname{Atoms}(L),
\quad
\operatorname{Hom}_{\rm DLat}(L,\mathbb B)
\cong\operatorname{Ultrafilters}(L)
\quad(L\text{ complete Boolean}).
\tag{FLS18}
\]
An atomless nontrivial complete Boolean algebra consequently
has no complete-frame points, while its bounded-lattice
branches still separate elements by (FLS13). Empty frame
point data cannot reconstruct that algebra. This is a
change of the permitted morphisms, not disappearance of
the original lattice or its semiring-prime spectrum.

\subsection{Exact corrections to the Boolean-only calculation}

The earlier TPW calculation concerns $G_{\mathbb B}(R)$.
It remains a calculation of one Boolean quotient, and
must be so labelled. In the full source,
\[
G_L(0)=L,\qquad G_L(R)[e_L^{-1}]=L,\qquad
Q_{(0)}=Z_L\quad(R\text{ a domain}).
\tag{FLS19}
\]
The first is immediate from (FLS1), the second is the
source's localization theorem, and the third is (FLS2).
In particular the arithmetic zero prime contains every
mixed support label, not merely $\tau_L$ and $e_L$.
Moreover absence by itself need not be prime. For
$L=\mathcal P(\{1,2\})$,
\[
z_{\{1\}}z_{\{2\}}=\tau_L,
\qquad z_{\{1\}}\ne\tau_L\ne z_{\{2\}},
\tag{FLS20}
\]
so $\{\tau_L\}$ is not prime. The support stratum has
both coordinate branches, with neither a substitute for
the other. The unchanged arithmetic residue amplitude
alone therefore does not describe the changed source
spectrum or prove a statement about every original
mixed-support operator.

The compact-carrier test algebra constructed in TPW is
a different specified support object: its carrier
multiplication is set product on the multiplicative
test line. The original zero lattice has meet as its
multiplication. Their exact respective products are
\[
e_S*e_T=e_{ST}\quad\text{in the TPW carrier algebra},
\qquad z_\lambda z_\mu=z_{\lambda\wedge\mu}
\quad\text{in }G_L(R).
\tag{FLS21}
\]
TPW supplies a map to the source's single-fibre Boolean
model, not an identification with the entire lattice
model. Full original-lattice convolution or trace claims
must retain $L$ and calculate their branch maps, including
which finite or infinite joins those maps preserve.
In particular finite sums are always detected by all
branches, whereas a chosen infinite-join observation
cannot be moved through a nonprincipal branch without
checking it; (FLS17) is an explicit failure calculation.

The surviving exact comparison, both before and after
arithmetic localization, is
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
G_L(R)&\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow&\displaystyle\prod_{x\in X}G_{\mathbb B}(R)\\
\downarrow&&\downarrow\\
G_L(R_{\mathfrak q})&\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow&
\displaystyle\prod_{x\in X}G_{\mathbb B}(R_{\mathfrak q}).
\end{array}
\tag{FLS22}
\]
Every map is the explicit amplitude localization or
branch map proved above, so the square commutes on
$(r,1_L)$ and every $z_\lambda$, hence on all elements.
Injectivity of each horizontal map follows from prime
separation. This retains the full lattice through all
its compatible branches and specifies exactly the data
lost when only one branch is used.

\section{Exact recovery of the amplitude constructions over the full lattice}
\label{sec:full-lattice-recovery}

\paragraph{Source and scope.}
The coefficient object in this section is the actual $G_L(\mathbb C)$ of
The Clankers, \emph{Split Support Geometry}, Version 11, Definition
\texttt{def:lattice-split}, lines 1226--1249 of the retained original TeX.
The complete source is retained as
\texttt{sources/FULL\_LATTICE\_FOUNDATION\_V11.tex}.
Its existing prime, localization and branch theorems are antecedents,
not results first discovered in this continuation. We now construct
the maps that recover the existing amplitude linear algebra over this
coefficient object. This construction gives a specified subobject of
the full free semimodule; it does not identify those two objects.

Let $L$ be a nontrivial bounded distributive lattice and put
$S_L=G_L(\mathbb C)$. Write $z_\lambda=(0,\lambda)$,
$\tau_L=z_0$ and $e_L=z_1$. For a complex vector space $V$ define
\[
V_L^\uparrow
 =\{(0_V,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}
   \cup\{(v,1_L):v\in V\}\subset V\times L.
\tag{FLR1}
\]
Its operations, with every zero retained, are
\[
(v,\lambda)+(w,\mu)=(v+w,\lambda\vee\mu),\qquad
(c,\alpha)(v,\lambda)=(cv,\alpha\wedge\lambda).
\tag{FLR2}
\]
These are closed operations: a nonzero sum has a nonzero summand and
therefore top support; a nonzero scalar product has both factors of
top support. Associativity, units and the two distributive laws follow
from the vector-space laws and distributivity in $L$.
The actual zero is $(0_V,0_L)$. Thus FLR1 is an $S_L$-semimodule.
Its amplitude projection $a_V(v,\lambda)=v$ is equivariant for
the ring reflection $p_L:S_L\to\mathbb C$.

For each original linear map $T:V\to W$, define
\[
T_L^\uparrow(v,\lambda)=(Tv,\lambda).
\tag{FLR3}
\]
When $Tv=0$, the output keeps its original support. The formulas FLR2
prove additivity and $S_L$-linearity of FLR3. They also prove
\[
(UT)_L^\uparrow=U_L^\uparrow T_L^\uparrow,\quad
(T+T')_L^\uparrow=T_L^\uparrow+T_L^{\prime\uparrow},\quad
(\mathrm{id}_V)_L^\uparrow=\mathrm{id}_{V_L^\uparrow}.
\tag{FLR4}
\]
For the sum, both output supports are $\lambda$, so their join is
$\lambda$. The lifted zero linear map is
\[
\varepsilon_V=(0_{V,V})_L^\uparrow,
\qquad\varepsilon_V(v,\lambda)=(0_V,\lambda)=e_L(v,\lambda).
\tag{FLR5}
\]
It is distinct from the categorical zero map, which has constant value
$(0_V,0_L)$. The lift is faithful, since evaluation on $(v,1_L)$
recovers $Tv$. Thus FLR4 proves actual composition identities without
silently erasing the distinction in FLR5.

The lift is also compatible with every Boolean branch
$\epsilon:L\to\mathbb B$. The map
\[
b_{\epsilon,V}:V_L^\uparrow\longrightarrow V_{\mathbb B}^\uparrow,
\quad(v,\lambda)\longmapsto(v,\epsilon(\lambda))
\tag{FLR6}
\]
is equivariant for the source's coefficient map $\beta_\epsilon$,
and $b_{\epsilon,W}T_L^\uparrow=T_{\mathbb B}^\uparrow b_{\epsilon,V}$.
This follows by substituting FLR2 and using preservation of join and
meet. It specifies exactly how every branch calculation receives the
full-lattice calculation; one such square does not recover all of $L$.

\subsection{The original metric, section and full supported kernel}
Take an original Hermitian vector space $(V,h_V)$, with the convention
that the first variable is conjugate-linear. Its lifted pairing is
\[
H_{V,L}((v,\lambda),(w,\mu))
  =(h_V(v,w),\lambda\wedge\mu)\in S_L.
\tag{FLR7}
\]
If its amplitude is nonzero, both vectors have top support, so the
displayed pair lies in $S_L$. The Hermitian symmetry, additivity and
sesquilinearity follow respectively from those of $h_V$, distributivity
in $L$, and FLR2. In particular
\[
H_{W,L}(T_L^\uparrow x,y)
 =H_{V,L}(x,(T^\dagger)_L^\uparrow y).
\tag{FLR8}
\]

\[
\begin{split}
W_p(f)&=(\log p)\sum_{m\ge1}p^{-m/2}
                  (f(p^m)+f(p^{-m})),\\
W_{\rm arch}(f)&=(\log(4\pi)+\gamma)f(1)\\
&\quad+\int_1^\infty
 [f(x)+f(x^{-1})-2x^{-1/2}f(1)]
 \frac{x^{1/2}}{x-x^{-1}}\,\frac{dx}{x}.
\end{split}
\tag{FLC14}
\]
Here $W_{\rm arch}$ is Connes's $W_{\mathbb R}$; his notation
$W_\infty$ in the subsequent positivity discussion has the opposite
sign. These local distributions are quoted with their human attribution,
not claimed as new. The integral converges at $1$ because the bracket
vanishes there to at least first order, and at infinity because the
test terms have compact support and the subtracted term decays.

The place-support lattice has the following explicit valuation into
this collection of local distributions:
\[
\mathcal W_A(f)=\mathbf1_A(\infty)W_{\rm arch}(f)
                         +\sum_{p\in A}W_p(f).
\tag{FLC15}
\]
There are finitely many nonzero summands for each $f$: if
$\operatorname{supp}f\subset[e^{-T},e^T]$, they require
$m\log p\le T$. Its exact support law is
\[
\mathcal W_{A\cup B}+\mathcal W_{A\cap B}
                 =\mathcal W_A+\mathcal W_B.
\tag{FLC16}
\]
This follows term by term from the indicator identity and does not
identify meet with convolution. For a bounded weight $c$ define
$\mathcal W_c=c_\infty W_{\rm arch}+\sum_pc_pW_p$ in the same way.
For $g\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R_+^\times)$ let
$g^*(x)=\overline{g(x^{-1})}$ and $f=g*g^*$, with Haar measure $dx/x$.
Substitution $x=e^u$ gives the exact correlation
\[
H_g(a)=f(e^a)=\int_{\mathbb R}
 g(e^u)\overline{g(e^{u-a})}\,du,
\quad H_g(-a)=\overline{H_g(a)}.
\tag{FLC17}
\]
Consequently the entire changed prime term is
\[
\boxed{\mathcal W_c(g*g^*)-
             c_\infty\mathcal W_{\mathbf1}(g*g^*)
 =2\sum_{p,m\ge1}(c_p-c_\infty)(\log p)p^{-m/2}
                              \Re H_g(m\log p).}
\tag{FLC18}
\]
No archimedean term is omitted: it cancels because its two coefficients
are $c_\infty$. Every finite term is displayed. Combining FLC13 at
$a=p$ gives the exact relation to the foundational support defect
\[
\mathcal W_c(g*g^*)-c_\infty\mathcal W_{\mathbf1}(g*g^*)
 =-2\sum_{p,m\ge1}\mathscr D(c)(p)\,p^{-m/2}
                              \Re H_g(m\log p).
\tag{FLC19}
\]
This is an exact morphism from the existing prime-support defect
to the change in this defined place-weighted arithmetic functional.
It does not assert that the choice of this functional is forced by
an analytic trace of the full lattice geometry.
The Weil criterion concerns the full-place distribution, with its
stated Mellin vanishing conditions. Formula FLC19 calculates the
change produced by each place-support weight before such a criterion
can be applied to a restricted or mixed expression. In the convention
where the desired Weil quadratic form is nonnegative, put
$\mathcal Q_c(g)=-\mathcal W_c(g*g^*)$. Its defect is the negative
of FLC19, so the coefficient is $+2$. The classical criterion in the
quoted source is $\mathcal Q_{\mathbf1}(g)\ge0$ for all its compact
tests with $\widehat g(\pm i/2)=0$; no such sign is assumed here.

\subsection{The exact fate of the mixed branch kernel in this map}
For a finite set $I$ of labelled places, extend the place valuation
linearly from support states by
\[
\mathcal V:\mathbb C[\mathcal P(I)]\longrightarrow
 \mathcal D'(\mathbb R_+^\times),\qquad
\delta_A\longmapsto\sum_{v\in A}W_v,
\tag{FLC20}
\]
where the archimedean label means $W_{\rm arch}$.
Its exact factorization is
\[
\mathcal V\!\left(\sum_Ac_A\delta_A\right)
 =\sum_{v\in I}\left(\sum_{A\ni v}c_A\right)W_v.
\tag{FLC21}
\]
This follows by exchanging two finite sums. Hence
$\ker\mathcal B\subseteq\ker\mathcal V$: every higher mixed vector
FLC9 with $|T|\ge2$ is killed by this specific local-distribution map.
In particular the four-state vector FLC10 has image zero, since
$\mathcal W_\varnothing-\mathcal W_{\{1\}}-
\mathcal W_{\{2\}}+\mathcal W_{\{1,2\}}=0$.
The same finite-sum argument proves that the foundational defect map
$\delta_A\mapsto\Delta(A)$ kills this mixed kernel as well.
Thus FLC19 calculates first place moments exactly. It does not
silently calculate the higher joint moments in FLC11.
The vector $h$ has zero image under FLC20 even though its invariant
line has the trace described after FLC10. These are two different
specified maps: evaluating a distribution on a vector is not taking
the trace on its invariant span. Their proved relationship is the
kernel factorization FLC21. A trace realization retaining joint
support must carry that kernel explicitly.

\section{A compact Weil-admissible test detecting each prime-support defect}

\paragraph{Sources and fixed conventions.}
The support defect is the original place-support construction of
The Clankers, \emph{Split Support Geometry}, Version 11,
\texttt{def:adelic-support-defect}. Its rational-coordinate
calculation is FLC12--13 in the present continuation.
The local explicit-formula contributions and test-function
convention are Alain Connes's original TeX, \emph{The Riemann
Hypothesis: Past, Present and a Letter Through Time},
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04022}{arXiv:2602.04022},
section \texttt{sectweilpos}, equations \texttt{wp} and
\texttt{arch}, together with the immediately following
statement of Andr\'e Weil's criterion. The exact formula
below uses $W_{\rm arch}=W_{\mathbb R}$ from that source;
it does not use the opposite-sign quantity which the source
subsequently names $W_\infty$.

Let $\Omega=\{\infty\}\sqcup\mathbb P$, let
$c\in\ell^\infty(\Omega;\mathbb R)$, and retain
\[
d_p=\mathscr D(c)(p)=(c_\infty-c_p)\log p,
\qquad
\mathcal W_c=c_\infty W_{\rm arch}+\sum_q c_qW_q,
\qquad Q_c(g)=-\mathcal W_c(g*g^*),
\tag{PDD1}
\]
where
\[
g^*(x)=\overline{g(x^{-1})},\quad
(g*g^*)(x)=\int_0^\infty g(y)\overline{g(y/x)}\,\frac{dy}{y},
\quad
W_q(f)=(\log q)\sum_{m\geq1}q^{-m/2}
\bigl(f(q^m)+f(q^{-m})\bigr).
\tag{PDD2}
\]
All these prime sums are finite for each compactly supported
test function. The admissibility conditions in this
convention are
\[
\widehat g(i/2)=\int_0^\infty g(x)x^{1/2}\,\frac{dx}{x}=0,
\qquad
\widehat g(-i/2)=\int_0^\infty g(x)x^{-1/2}\,\frac{dx}{x}=0.
\tag{PDD3}
\]
Weil's nonnegative quadratic form is $Q_{\mathbf1}$ on
these tests. The following computations prove the exact
support correction; they do not assume a sign for
$Q_{\mathbf1}$.

\subsection{The completely specified compact test}

Fix any rational prime $p$ and set
\[
a_p=\log p,\qquad
\varepsilon_p=\frac18\log\frac{p+1}{p},\qquad
\]
\[
u_p(t)=
\begin{cases}
\exp\!\left(-\dfrac{1}{1-(t/\varepsilon_p)^2}\right),
   &|t|<\varepsilon_p,\\
0,&|t|\geq\varepsilon_p,
\end{cases}
\qquad v_p(t)=u_p''(t)-\frac14u_p(t).
\tag{PDD4}
\]
The function $u_p$ is real, smooth, nonzero and compactly
supported. For smoothness at each endpoint, every one-sided
derivative is a finite sum of powers of
$[1-(t/\varepsilon_p)^2]^{-1}$ and polynomials in $t$
times the exponential in PDD4. The exponential tends to
zero faster than each such power, so every derivative
extends with value zero. At $|t|<\varepsilon_p$, writing
$b(t)=1-(t/\varepsilon_p)^2$ gives the explicit original
derivative and coefficient
\[
v_p(t)=u_p(t)
\left(
\frac{4t^2}{\varepsilon_p^4 b(t)^4}
-\frac{8t^2}{\varepsilon_p^4 b(t)^3}
-\frac{2}{\varepsilon_p^2 b(t)^2}-\frac14
\right).
\tag{PDD5}
\]
Both $v_p$ and every derivative vanish at the support
endpoints. Its nonzero amplitude is proved without an
assumption about a numerical integral: integration by
parts gives
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}v_p(t)u_p(t)\,dt
=-\int_{\mathbb R}|u_p'(t)|^2\,dt
 -\frac14\int_{\mathbb R}|u_p(t)|^2\,dt<0.
\quad
N_p:=\int_{-\varepsilon_p}^{\varepsilon_p}|v_p(t)|^2\,dt>0.
\tag{PDD6}
\]
The rightmost strict inequality follows because $v_p$
cannot be the zero function in view of the preceding
strictly negative integral. $N_p$ is kept as this exact
integral of the explicitly specified function; no change
of its scale has been made.

For $\sigma\in\{1,-1\}$ define the exact multiplicative
test function
\[
g_{p,\sigma}(x)=v_p(\log x)
                   +\sigma v_p(\log x-a_p),
\qquad f_{p,\sigma}=g_{p,\sigma}*g_{p,\sigma}^*.
\tag{PDD7}
\]
It is real, smooth, and supported in the union
$[e^{-\varepsilon_p},e^{\varepsilon_p}]
\cup[pe^{-\varepsilon_p},pe^{\varepsilon_p}]$.
For either $b=1/2$ or $b=-1/2$, two integrations by parts
with the vanishing endpoint derivatives give
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}v_p(t)e^{bt}\,dt
=(b^2-1/4)\int_{\mathbb R}u_p(t)e^{bt}\,dt=0.
\tag{PDD8}
\]
The integral for $v_p(t-a_p)$ is $e^{ba_p}$ times this
zero. Substitution $x=e^t$ now proves both conditions
PDD3 for every $g_{p,\sigma}$.

\subsection{Exact isolation from every other prime power}

Put $C_p(b)=\int_{\mathbb R}v_p(t)v_p(t-b)\,dt$.
It is real and even, $C_p(0)=N_p$, and
$C_p(b)=0$ when $|b|>2\varepsilon_p$, because the two
compact supports then do not meet. Expansion of PDD7
gives the full correlation, including both mixed terms:
\[
f_{p,\sigma}(e^b)
=2C_p(b)+\sigma C_p(b-a_p)+\sigma C_p(b+a_p).
\tag{PDD9}
\]
There are no suppressed phases or translated pieces in
this identity.

For any integer $n\geq2$ with $n\ne p$,
\[
|\log n-a_p|\geq\log\frac{p+1}{p}
                 =8\varepsilon_p.
\tag{PDD10}
\]
For $n\geq p+1$ this is monotonicity of the logarithm.
For $n\leq p-1$ it follows from
$p/(p-1)>(p+1)/p$, which is equivalent to
$p^2>p^2-1$; when $p=2$ there is no such integer
$n\geq2$ on the lower side. Also
$2\varepsilon_p<\log2\leq\log n$ and
$2\varepsilon_p<a_p$ because
$(p+1)/p\leq3/2<2^4$. Formula PDD9 therefore implies
\[
f_{p,\sigma}(q^m)=f_{p,\sigma}(q^{-m})
=\begin{cases}
\sigma N_p,&q=p,\ m=1,\\
0,&\text{every other prime }q\text{ and integer }m\geq1.
\end{cases}
\tag{PDD11}
\]
Indeed the $2C_p(\log(q^m))$ term vanishes by
$\log(q^m)\geq\log2$; the positive-shift argument
$\log(q^m)+a_p$ is also outside the support. The
remaining argument $\log(q^m)-a_p$ is outside it by
PDD10 unless $q^m=p$. Unique prime factorization gives
$q=p,m=1$ in the latter case. Its contribution is
$\sigma C_p(0)$. Evenness proves the reciprocal
evaluation as well.

\subsection{Detection, inversion, and the exact sign}

Every local prime contribution is now calculated:
\[
W_q(f_{p,\sigma})
=\begin{cases}
2\sigma(\log p)p^{-1/2}N_p,&q=p,\\
0,&q\ne p.
\end{cases}
\tag{PDD12}
\]
The archimedean contribution cancels in the difference
with coefficient $c_\infty$. Thus
\[
\begin{split}
\mathcal W_c(f_{p,\sigma})
-c_\infty\mathcal W_{\mathbf1}(f_{p,\sigma})
&=-2\sigma d_p p^{-1/2}N_p,\\
Q_c(g_{p,\sigma})-c_\infty Q_{\mathbf1}(g_{p,\sigma})
&=2\sigma d_p p^{-1/2}N_p.
\end{split}
\tag{PDD13}
\]
These identities follow either directly from PDD12 or
from FLC19 with the positive Weil-form sign accounted
for. They include the archimedean term through its
exact cancellation, rather than omitting it from a
formula for $Q_{\mathbf1}$.

In particular, the map from the original support defect
$\mathscr D(c)$ to its quadratic-form correction on
Weil-admissible compact tests is injective. Each prime
coordinate has the explicit recovery formula
\[
d_p=\frac{\sqrt p}{2N_p}
\bigl(Q_c(g_{p,1})-c_\infty Q_{\mathbf1}(g_{p,1})\bigr).
\tag{PDD14}
\]
If all corrections vanish, every $d_p$ is zero. Conversely
their vanishing makes the correction identically zero
by FLC19. For rational $a\ne0$ the remaining defect is
then recovered as the original finite sum
$\mathscr D(c)(a)=\sum_p\nu_p(a)d_p$.
This is an exact inverse on the image of the defect map,
not just a necessary bound.

There is also a definite sign calculation for the
archimedean-free subspace. If $c_\infty=0$ and
$c_p\ne0$ for some prime, PDD13 gives
\[
Q_c(g_{p,\sigma})
=-2\sigma c_p(\log p)p^{-1/2}N_p.
\tag{PDD15}
\]
Choosing $\sigma=\operatorname{sgn}(c_p)$ makes this
strictly negative, and the opposite sign makes it
strictly positive. Consequently no nonzero real weight
with $c_\infty=0$ gives a nonnegative quadratic form on
all the admissible tests. For a nonempty place support
$A$ omitting $\infty$, take any $p\in A$: its indicator
weight has the explicit negative value
$Q_{\mathbf1_A}(g_{p,1})=-2(\log p)p^{-1/2}N_p$.
This is a counterexample to positivity of that restricted
place functional, not a counterexample to RH or an
evaluation of the sign of the full-place form.

\paragraph{The higher mixed-support kernel is still retained.}
The injectivity in PDD14 starts with the place defect
$\mathscr D(c)$, not with arbitrary formal linear
combinations of support states. In a finite Boolean
channel model, a formal vector $\sum_A t_A\delta_A$
maps to the place weights
$c_i=\sum_{A\ni i}t_A$. Hence all the higher mixed
vectors in FLC7--10 map to zero under both the defect
and the local-place functional. For example
$\delta_\varnothing-\delta_{\{1\}}-\delta_{\{2\}}
+\delta_{\{1,2\}}$ has both place weights zero.
The exact mixed kernel and its nonzero formal fixed
trace are therefore not detected by PDD14. The maps
in FLC20--21 retain that kernel explicitly; detecting
every prime coordinate of their image does not erase it.

\section{Exact recovery of joint support by local distributions}
\label{sec:joint-support-distribution}

\paragraph{Sources and the constructed maps.}
The ring-null support lattice is the object in The Clankers,
\emph{Split Support Geometry}, Version 11,
\texttt{def:lattice-split} and \texttt{thm:lattice-normal-form}.
The source's adelic support valuation and its linear extension are
\texttt{def:adelic-support-defect} and
\texttt{thm:adelic-defect-linearization}.
The local distributions used here are Alain Connes's
\emph{The Riemann Hypothesis: Past, Present and a Letter Through Time},
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04022}{arXiv:2602.04022},
section \texttt{sectweilpos}, equations \texttt{wp} and
\texttt{arch}; the retained original author TeX has these at
lines 854--867. We use $W_{\rm arch}=W_{\mathbb R}$ there,
not the opposite-sign quantity later named $W_\infty$.
The local distributions, their human attribution, and the original
support states are retained without alteration.

The map $\delta_A\mapsto\sum_{v\in A}W_v$ is the specified
distribution-valued place valuation in FLC20. This section constructs
its joint observations and their exact relation to traces on the
formal zero-support representation. The displayed insertion of the
Connes distributions is part of the definition of the observable;
it is not assumed to be an analytic trace forced by the base.

Let $G=\mathbb R_+^\times$ with Haar measure $dx/x$.
Let $I$ be a finite set of $n\ge1$ distinct labelled places of
$\mathbb Q$, and put $V=\mathbb C[\mathcal P(I)]$, with its
original basis $\delta_A$, $A\subseteq I$.
For a vector $c=\sum_A c_A\delta_A$ define
$J_T(c)=\sum_{A\supseteq T}c_A$ for every $T\subseteq I$.
Here $J_\varnothing(c)$ is its total coefficient sum.
The simultaneous individual-branch map has kernel
$K=\{c:J_\varnothing(c)=0,\ J_{\{i\}}(c)=0\text{ for all }i\in I\}$,
as computed in FLC6--FLC10.

\subsection{The exact joint coordinates of the mixed kernel}

For each $S\subseteq I$, retain the vector
$v_S=\sum_{A\subseteq I}(-1)^{|A\cap S|}\delta_A$.
Its joint coordinates are
\[
J_T(v_S)=
\begin{cases}
(-1)^{|S|}2^{n-|T|},&S\subseteq T,\\
0,&S\not\subseteq T.
\end{cases}
\tag{JMD1}
\]
To prove this, write $A=T\cup B$ with $B\subseteq I\setminus T$.
If $S\setminus T$ is nonempty, toggle any coordinate in that set;
the paired signs cancel. If $S\subseteq T$, the sign is constantly
$(-1)^{|S|}$ over all $2^{n-|T|}$ choices of $B$.
The $v_S$ form a basis: their squared norms in the original orthonormal
support-state basis are $2^n$, and for $S\ne U$ the inner product
vanishes by toggling a coordinate in their symmetric difference.
In particular the $v_S$ with $|S|\ge2$ are a basis of $K$.
Indeed they belong to $K$ by JMD1, and the conditions defining $K$
have rank $n+1$: a linear relation among the functions $1$ and
$\mathbf1_{i\in A}$ vanishes first on $A=\varnothing$ and then
on every singleton only when all its coefficients vanish.
Thus $\dim K=2^n-n-1$, exactly the number of those basis vectors.

\subsection{Literal compact tests for each local distribution}

Put $\eta=(\log2)/16$ and define
\[
\begin{split}
w_\eta(t)&=
\begin{cases}
\exp\!\left(-\dfrac{1}{1-(t/\eta)^2}\right),&|t|<\eta,\\
0,&|t|\ge\eta,
\end{cases}\\
\psi_\infty(e^t)&=w_\eta(t-4\eta)+w_\eta(t+4\eta).
\end{split}
\tag{JMD2}
\]
Every one-sided derivative of the bump is a polynomial in $t$ and
inverse powers of $1-(t/\eta)^2$, multiplied by the displayed
exponential. At either endpoint the exponential tends to zero faster
than each such inverse power. Therefore $w_\eta$, and hence
$\psi_\infty$, is smooth with compact support.
The latter is real, nonnegative and invariant under $x\mapsto x^{-1}$.
Its logarithmic support satisfies
$3\eta\le|t|\le5\eta<\log2$, and it vanishes at $x=1$.
Every $W_p(\psi_\infty)$ is consequently zero.
The exact archimedean value is
\[
\beta:=W_{\rm arch}(\psi_\infty)
=2\int_0^\infty
\psi_\infty(e^t)\frac{e^{-t/2}}{1-e^{-2t}}\,dt>0.
\tag{JMD3}
\]
This follows from FLC14, with its constant term and subtraction both
zero because $\psi_\infty(1)=0$.
The integral is finite since its support avoids zero and infinity.
It is strictly positive since its integrand is positive for
$3\eta<t<5\eta$.

For each rational prime $p$ retain the exact PDD4--PDD7 functions:
$a_p=\log p$, $\varepsilon_p=\frac18\log((p+1)/p)$,
$u_p(t)=\exp(-1/(1-(t/\varepsilon_p)^2))$ for
$|t|<\varepsilon_p$ and zero elsewhere,
$v_p=u_p''-u_p/4$,
$g_{p,1}(e^t)=v_p(t)+v_p(t-a_p)$, and
$f_{p,1}=g_{p,1}*g_{p,1}^*$.
Keep $N_p=\int|v_p(t)|^2dt>0$ at its original scale.
Define
\[
\begin{split}
\alpha_p&=2(\log p)p^{-1/2}N_p>0,\qquad
A_p=W_{\rm arch}(f_{p,1}),\\
\psi_p&=\beta f_{p,1}-A_p\psi_\infty,\qquad
b_\infty=\beta,\quad b_p=\beta\alpha_p.
\end{split}
\tag{JMD4}
\]
All coefficients here are the displayed integrals and factors;
none of the original functions or support states has had its scale
changed. The new function $\psi_p$ is a specified linear combination
used for decoding distributions.

For completeness the prime evaluations used in this construction
follow directly from the original bump.
Integration by parts gives
$\int v_pu_p=-\int|u_p'|^2-\frac14\int u_p^2<0$, proving $N_p>0$.
Let $C_p(t)=\int v_p(s)v_p(s-t)ds$.
It vanishes outside $[-2\varepsilon_p,2\varepsilon_p]$, is even,
and has value $N_p$ at zero. Expanding the convolution yields
$f_{p,1}(e^t)=2C_p(t)+C_p(t-a_p)+C_p(t+a_p)$.
For every integer $m\ge2$, $m\ne p$,
$|\log m-\log p|\ge\log((p+1)/p)=8\varepsilon_p$.
For $m\ge p+1$ this is monotonicity of the logarithm; for
$m\le p-1$ it follows from $p/(p-1)>(p+1)/p$.
Also $2\varepsilon_p<\log2$.
Thus among all prime powers $q^k$, $k\ge1$, only $q^k=p$ yields
a nonzero evaluation, and that evaluation and its reciprocal both
equal $N_p$. Therefore
$W_q(f_{p,1})=\alpha_p\mathbf1_{q=p}$.
Together with JMD3 this proves the full duality
\[
\boxed{W_v(\psi_u)=b_u\mathbf1_{v=u},\qquad b_u>0,}
\quad v,u\in\{\infty\}\sqcup\mathbb P,
\tag{JMD5}
\]
where the label $\infty$ on $W_v$ means $W_{\rm arch}$.
For $u=p$, its archimedean value is
$\beta A_p-A_p\beta=0$; its only nonzero finite value is
$\beta\alpha_p$ at $p$. For $u=\infty$, the assertions are JMD3
and the support calculation following JMD2.

These decoding functions are not asserted to be convolution squares
or to satisfy Weil's two vanishing conditions.
The original $g_{p,1}$ does satisfy them: for $b=\pm1/2$,
two integrations by parts give
$\int v_p(t)e^{bt}dt=(b^2-1/4)\int u_p(t)e^{bt}dt=0$,
and translation by $a_p$ multiplies this zero by $e^{ba_p}$.
The distinction preserves the domains of both constructions.

\subsection{The original support diagonal and the complete recovery}

Retain $\mathcal V:V\to\mathcal D'(G)$ from FLC20,
$\mathcal V\delta_A=\sum_{v\in A}W_v$.
For $r\ge1$ define the linearized set diagonal and its observation
\[
\Delta^{(r)}:V\longrightarrow V^{\otimes r},\qquad
\Delta^{(r)}\delta_A=\delta_A^{\otimes r},\qquad
\mathcal V_r=\mathcal V^{\otimes r}\circ\Delta^{(r)}.
\tag{JMD6}
\]
The set map copies each original support state before its
observations are taken; its extension is linear.
In particular
\[
\mathcal V_r(c)
=\sum_{A\subseteq I}c_A
       \left(\sum_{v\in A}W_v\right)^{\otimes r}.
\tag{JMD7}
\]
Each term is a tensor product of distributions in separate variables,
and the sum is finite, so the target is $\mathcal D'(G^r)$.
Equivalently the expression acts as a continuous multilinear
functional on $r$ compact smooth functions.
No product of singular distributions in one variable is used.
The expression is linear in $c$; it is not
$\mathcal V(c)^{\otimes r}$, which has products of different
coefficients $c_Ac_B$ and loses the stated diagonal construction.

Given a tuple $(i_1,\ldots,i_r)$ of labels from $I$, let $T$ be its
set of distinct entries. JMD5 gives the exact joint decoder
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal V_r(c)(\psi_{i_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\psi_{i_r})
=\left(\prod_{j=1}^r b_{i_j}\right)J_T(c).}
\tag{JMD8}
\]
Indeed the contribution of $\delta_A$ is
$\prod_j\sum_{v\in A}W_v(\psi_{i_j})
=\prod_j b_{i_j}\mathbf1_{i_j\in A}$.
The product of indicators is exactly $\mathbf1_{T\subseteq A}$.
Summing with the original coefficients $c_A$ proves the identity.
Every $b_{i_j}$ is strictly positive, so division by the displayed
literal factor recovers the joint coordinate without an ambiguity.

Put $\mathcal V_0(c)=J_\varnothing(c)$.
For every $0\le r\le n$ the full observation through degree $r$
has exact kernel
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\bigcap_{s=0}^r\ker\mathcal V_s
&=\{c:J_T(c)=0\text{ for all }|T|\le r\}\\
&=\operatorname{span}\{v_S:|S|>r\}.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{JMD9}
\]

$Y(z)=\iota_K^\dagger e^{zM}J$. Differentiating the entire exponential
series and applying TDP5 gives
\begin{equation}\tag{TDP6}
\begin{gathered}
U'=HU+B_{01}Y,\qquad Y'=B_{10}U+D_KY,\qquad U(0)=I_B,\quad Y(0)=0,\\
Y(z)=\int_0^z e^{(z-t)D_K}B_{10}U(t)\,dt,\\
U'(z)=HU(z)+\int_0^z\mathcal K(z-t)U(t)\,dt,
\qquad \mathcal K(t)=B_{01}e^{tD_K}B_{10}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Integrals are along the straight segment, with the usual complex
orientation. The second equation follows by differentiating
$e^{-zD_K}Y(z)$ and integrating; substitution proves the third.
Thus TDP6 is an identity of entire functions, including purely imaginary
prime arguments. A second variation-of-constants calculation gives
\begin{equation}\tag{TDP7}
U(z)-e^{zH}=
\int_0^z\!\int_0^t
 e^{(z-t)H}B_{01}e^{(t-v)D_K}B_{10}U(v)\,dv\,dt.
\end{equation}
The difference is the full original-kernel return, including its complex
signs. No positive sign for this operator is asserted.

Insertion of $I_E=P+Q$ in $e^{(z+w)M}$ proves the exact product defect
\begin{equation}\tag{TDP8}
\begin{aligned}
U(z+w)-U(z)U(w)
 &=J^\dagger e^{zM}Qe^{wM}J,\\
U(mz)-U(z)^m
 &=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}U(z)^{m-1-j}
   J^\dagger e^{zM}Qe^{jzM}J\qquad(m\ge1).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For the second identity the sum is empty at $m=1$. If the assertion is
known for $m$, write
$U((m+1)z)-U(z)^{m+1}=U(z)(U(mz)-U(z)^m)
+U((m+1)z)-U(z)U(mz)$ and use the first identity. This proves it for
all integers $m$. Specializing $z=i\log p$ gives the exact correction
between the observed prime-power action and powers of the observed
one-prime action. For the uncentred action both sides acquire precisely
$p^{mk/2}$, with no change to any of the kernel factors.

The same defect determines the local determinant. Write
$W^\dagger F_pW=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A_p&B_p\\C_p&D_p\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
Over formal power series in $u$, every matrix with constant term $I$ is
invertible. Block Gaussian elimination then gives
\begin{equation}\tag{TDP9}
\begin{gathered}
\sum_{m\ge0}\Phi(F_p^m)u^m
 =\bigl[I_B-uA_p-u^2B_p(I_K-uD_p)^{-1}C_p\bigr]^{-1},\\
\det(I_E-uF_p)
 =\det(I_K-uD_p)\det(I_B-uA_p)\,\mathcal E_p(u),\\
\mathcal E_p(u)=
\det\!\left[I_B-u^2(I_B-uA_p)^{-1}
 B_p(I_K-uD_p)^{-1}C_p\right].
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
To verify the first identity, expand $(I_E-uF_p)^{-1}$ as its geometric
series and take its upper-left block. Eliminate the lower-left block
using the invertible lower-right block $I_K-uD_p$; its Schur complement
is the displayed inverse. Taking determinants proves the second
identity, and factoring $I_B-uA_p$ proves the third. Since both sides are
rational functions, the identities also hold wherever the displayed
inverses exist, and extend across removable singularities after clearing
denominators. They retain every pole cancellation carried by $K$.

The formal logarithmic derivative, or the identity
$-\log\det(I-uT)=\sum_{m\ge1}\operatorname{tr}(T^m)u^m/m$, yields
\begin{equation}\tag{TDP10}
\begin{aligned}
-\log\mathcal E_p(u)
 &=\sum_{m\ge1}
 \frac{\operatorname{tr}(F_p^m)-\operatorname{tr}(A_p^m)
 -\operatorname{tr}(D_p^m)}{m}u^m,\\
[u^2](-\log\mathcal E_p(u))&=\operatorname{tr}(B_pC_p).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Indeed the diagonal blocks of $F_p^2$ have traces
$\operatorname{tr}(A_p^2)+\operatorname{tr}(B_pC_p)$ and
$\operatorname{tr}(D_p^2)+\operatorname{tr}(C_pB_p)$; cyclicity of the
finite trace proves the coefficient claim. These traces are generally
complex. Positivity of $\Phi$ on positive operators does not change this
calculation into a nonnegative Euler coefficient.

\subsection{The support algebra exhibits the cancellation explicitly}

The original support representation and its boundary operator are
\begin{equation}\tag{TDP11}
\begin{gathered}
\rho(c_0,c_1+c_2\eta)=c_0(I-\Pi)+c_1\Pi+c_2R,\qquad
\Pi=ee^\dagger+ff^\dagger,\qquad R=\epsilon fe^\dagger,\\
e^\dagger f=0,\quad \|e\|=\|f\|=1,\quad \epsilon>0,\quad R^2=0,\\
[\tau]\longmapsto I_E,\qquad [n]\longmapsto\Pi+nR.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The symbol multiplication in the last line represents addition in the
original monoid $\mathbb Z\sqcup\{\tau\}$. In particular $[p]^m=[mp]$
there, not $[p^m]$. The original $\tau$ action cannot be identified with
the multiplicative prime-power law by replacing one of these indices.
The exact one-parameter group in the same algebra is
$\rho(1,1+z\eta)=I_E+zR=e^{zR}$; at integral $z=n$ this is the image
of $[\tau]+[n]-[0]$. Its multiplication law follows from $R^2=0$.

Retain the measured columns and all their phases:
\begin{equation}\tag{TDP12}
\begin{gathered}
u=J^\dagger e,\quad v=J^\dagger f,\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}a&r\\\bar r&d\end{pmatrix}
 =[u,v]^*[u,v],\quad \alpha=1-a,\quad \mathfrak d=ad-|r|^2,\\
T=\Phi(R)=\epsilon vu^*,\qquad \kappa=\epsilon r,\qquad
T^2=\kappa T,\\
e_K=\iota_K^\dagger e,\quad f_K=\iota_K^\dagger f,\quad
\|e_K\|^2=\alpha,\quad\|f_K\|^2=1-d,\quad e_K^*f_K=-r.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The identity $T^2=\kappa T$ is direct multiplication using $u^*v=r$.
The last line follows by inserting $P+Q$ in the inner products of $e,f$.
The support map preserves the original nilpotent on $E$, but compression
can give it the nonzero eigenvalue $\kappa$. Since $\mathfrak d>0$ in
the original finite area guards, neither $u$ nor $v$ is zero, so $T\ne0$.

The complete $R$ blocks, its memory and its two exponentials are
\begin{equation}\tag{TDP13}
\begin{gathered}
W^\dagger RW=
\begin{pmatrix}T&\epsilon ve_K^*\\
 \epsilon f_Ku^*&\epsilon f_Ke_K^*\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\mathcal K_R(z)=-\kappa e^{-\kappa z}T,\\
\Phi(e^{zR})=I_B+zT,\qquad
e^{zT}=I_B+f_\kappa(z)T,\qquad
f_\kappa(z)=\begin{cases}(e^{\kappa z}-1)/\kappa,&\kappa\ne0,\\
z,&\kappa=0.
\end{cases}
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
For the memory identity, the lower-right block sends $f_K$ to
$-\kappa f_K$. Thus its exponential sends $f_K$ to
$e^{-\kappa z}f_K$, and multiplication by the two off-diagonal blocks
gives $-\kappa e^{-\kappa z}T$. The first exponential truncates because
$R^2=0$. For the second, induction gives $T^j=\kappa^{j-1}T$ for $j\ge1$;
substitution in the entire exponential series proves the formula,
including its removable value at $\kappa=0$.

For direct verification that the memory cancels the extra exponential,
substitute $U_R(z)=I_B+zT$ in TDP6 with the blocks in TDP13. The sum of
the two terms on its right is
$T+z\kappa T-\kappa T\int_0^z e^{-\kappa(z-t)}(1+t\kappa)dt=T$,
since the integrand is the derivative in $t$ of
$t e^{-\kappa(z-t)}$. This is precisely $U_R'(z)$.

Put $c=i\log p$, $n_E=\dim E=q$ and $b_B=\dim B$. The exact support
prime action $I_E+cR$ and all its prime powers satisfy
\begin{equation}\tag{TDP14}
\begin{gathered}
\Phi((I_E+cR)^m)=I_B+mcT,\\
(I_B+cT)^m=I_B+
\begin{cases}((1+c\kappa)^m-1)T/\kappa,&\kappa\ne0,\\
mcT,&\kappa=0,
\end{cases}\\
\det(I_E-w(I_E+cR))=(1-w)^{n_E}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The first line is the binomial theorem with $R^2=0$. For the second line
expand by the binomial theorem and use $T^j=\kappa^{j-1}T$; the scalar
binomial formula supplies the coefficient. All eigenvalues of $R$ are
zero, so all eigenvalues of $I_E+cR$ equal one; this proves the last line.
In contrast, the observed block has a candidate eigenvalue
$1+c\kappa$. This is a compression effect, not a moved eigenvalue of
the full support action.

When $K\ne0$, its original block is $I_K+c\epsilon f_Ke_K^*$ and has
the candidate eigenvalue $1-c\kappa$. Its exact factors in TDP9 are
\begin{equation}\tag{TDP15}
\begin{aligned}
\det(I_B-w(I_B+cT))
 &=(1-w)^{b_B-1}(1-w-wc\kappa),\\
\det(I_K-w(I_K+c\epsilon f_Ke_K^*))
 &=(1-w)^{n_E-b_B-1}(1-w+wc\kappa),\\
\mathcal E_{p,R}(w)
 &=\frac{(1-w)^2}{(1-w)^2-w^2c^2\kappa^2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
These identities first hold formally at $w=0$. The first two follow
from the rank-one determinant identity
$\det(I+xy^*)=1+y^*x$, which is proved by extending $x$ to a basis when
$x\ne0$; the case $x=0$ is immediate. Their product, the last line and
TDP14 multiply to an identity. Alternatively substituting the blocks
of TDP13 into TDP9 gives the last line directly. In the inverse
determinant, its factor $\mathcal E_{p,R}(w)^{-1}$ cancels the two
candidate poles at $1/(1+c\kappa)$ and $1/(1-c\kappa)$. If $\kappa=0$
this factor is identically one. If $K=0$, then $r=0$, $\kappa=0$ and the
kernel factor is absent; TDP14 remains valid. This is a complete
pole-cancellation calculation on the actual support receiver.

The original estimates do not authorize deleting $\kappa$:
\begin{equation}\tag{TDP16}
\begin{gathered}
|\kappa|^2=\epsilon^2|r|^2
 \le\epsilon^2\alpha(1-d),\qquad
\|T\|=\epsilon\sqrt{ad},\\
\alpha\le e^{-2q\psi(t_N)+O_{h,\varpi}(k\log(q+2))},\quad
\log\epsilon=q\psi(t_N)+O_{h,\varpi}(k\log(q+2)),\\

\qquad t_N=(N+1-q)/q.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The first inequality is the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality on $K$ using

The full $M=C+R$ in ST3 must still be used. For $\lambda$ outside the
spectrum of the original self-adjoint $C$, rank-one elimination gives

\det(\lambda I_E-M)=\det(\lambda I_E-C)
 \bigl[1-\epsilon e^\dagger(\lambda I_E-C)^{-1}f\bigr].
\end{equation}
Factor $\lambda I-C$ on the left and use the rank-one determinant

\subsection{Tensor products retain the same defect, with exact mixed sectors}

Let $\nu\ge1$ be an independent tensor count and retain the product
metric $G_N^{\otimes\nu}$. Define
\begin{equation}\tag{TDP18}
\begin{gathered}
J_\nu=J^{\otimes\nu},\quad P_\nu=P^{\otimes\nu},\quad
Q_\nu=I-P_\nu
 =\sum_{\varnothing\ne S\subset\{1,\ldots,\nu\}}
 \bigotimes_{j=1}^{\nu}\begin{cases}Q,&j\in S,\\P,&j\notin S,
 \end{cases}\\
M_\nu=\sum_{j=1}^\nu I^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes M\otimes
 I^{\otimes(\nu-j)},\qquad H_\nu=\sum_{j=1}^\nu
 I^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes H\otimes I^{\otimes(\nu-j)},\\
J_\nu^\dagger e^{zM_\nu}J_\nu=U(z)^{\otimes\nu},\qquad
e^{zH_\nu}=(e^{zH})^{\otimes\nu}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The projections in the first line have mutually orthogonal ranges:
two distinct subsets place $PQ=0$ in at least one factor. Expansion of
$(P+Q)^{\otimes\nu}$ proves their sum. The summands of $M_\nu$ commute,
so multiplication of their convergent exponential series proves the
last line. Tensoring therefore preserves the full source action, and
also preserves the difference caused by observation:
\begin{equation}\tag{TDP19}
U(z)^{\otimes\nu}-(e^{zH})^{\otimes\nu}
 =\sum_{j=1}^\nu U(z)^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes
 \bigl(U(z)-e^{zH}\bigr)\otimes(e^{zH})^{\otimes(\nu-j)}.
\end{equation}
The right side telescopes after inserting and subtracting tensors with
successively one further factor $U(z)$. No mixed-kernel subset is
discarded by TDP18 or TDP19.

For the original support put $A=\Phi(\Pi)=[u,v][u,v]^*$, with its two
strictly positive eigenvalues $\vartheta_+,\vartheta_-$ from ST7. Let
$A_\nu=A^{\otimes\nu}$, which is the exact compression of
$\Pi^{\otimes\nu}$. Its eigenvalues and defect are
\begin{equation}\tag{TDP20}
\begin{gathered}
\vartheta_+^{\nu-j}\vartheta_-^j
 \quad\hbox{with multiplicity }{\nu\choose j},\quad 0\le j\le\nu,
 \quad\hbox{and }0\hbox{ on the remaining }b_B^\nu-2^\nu\hbox{ dimensions},\\
A_\nu-A_\nu^2
 =\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}(A^2)^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes(A-A^2)
 \otimes A^{\otimes(\nu-j)}
 =(Q_\nu\Pi^{\otimes\nu}J_\nu)^\dagger
  (Q_\nu\Pi^{\otimes\nu}J_\nu).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Tensor products of orthonormal eigenvectors prove the first line,
including labelled multiplicities when values coincide. A telescoping
expansion proves the first equality in the second line. Since
$\Pi^{\otimes\nu}$ is an orthogonal projection, insertion of
$P_\nu+Q_\nu$ proves the last equality. Each summand of the middle
expression is positive semidefinite. Positivity of this defect does
not make it zero.

There are exact amplification values, not merely an unspecified error:
\begin{equation}\tag{TDP21}
\begin{gathered}
\det{}^+A_\nu=\mathfrak d^{\,\nu2^{\nu-1}},\qquad
\frac{\log\det{}^+A_\nu}{\nu2^\nu}=\tfrac12\log\mathfrak d,\\
\left|\frac{\log\det{}^+A_\nu}{\nu2^\nu}
 -\tfrac12\log d\right|\le\frac{\alpha}{2\Theta_N},\qquad
0\le1-\vartheta_+^\nu\le\nu\alpha,\\
\frac1\nu\log(\vartheta_+^{\nu-j}\vartheta_-^j)
 =(1-j/\nu)\log\vartheta_++(j/\nu)\log\vartheta_-.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Each of $\vartheta_+$ and $\vartheta_-$ occurs $\nu2^{\nu-1}$ times in
the product of the $2^\nu$ nonzero eigenvalues, proving the determinant
identity. ST11 then proves its estimate. The identity
$1-x^\nu=(1-x)\sum_{j=0}^{\nu-1}x^j$ for $0\le x\le1$, together with
ST8, proves the bound on the first tensor branch. The final line is the
logarithm of the exact eigenvalue. In particular a non-unit second
support eigenvalue contributes a loss proportional to the tensor
count. Taking a $\nu$th root does not remove that value. This is the
precise contrast with a fixed exponent error divided by the tensor
count in Deligne's amplification.

The support representation survives observation as a homomorphism
exactly when both original support vectors are retained:
\begin{equation}\tag{TDP22}
\Phi\circ\rho\text{ is multiplicative}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
a=d=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad e,f\in K^\perp.
\end{equation}
For necessity, multiplicativity makes $A^2=A$. Its two nonzero
eigenvalues must then be one. Thus $a+d=\operatorname{tr}A=2$ and
$0\le a,d\le1$ imply $a=d=1$; Cauchy--Schwarz on $K$ gives $r=0$.
The norm identities in TDP12 imply $e,f\in K^\perp$. Conversely, in
that case both $\Pi$ and $R$ preserve $K^\perp$ and are zero on $K$.
Their compressions obey the identical multiplication table
$A^2=A$, $AT=TA=T$, $T^2=0$. The complement $I_B-A$ has zero products
with $A,T$, so the algebra formula in TDP11 is multiplicative.
The defect space determined by failure is the actual range
$\operatorname{im}(Q\Pi J)\subset K$, with Gram operator $A-A^2$,
and its tensor analogues are the explicitly decomposed ranges in TDP20.

The support nilpotent also tensors without dropping mixed terms. Put

\begin{gathered}

\quad (0\le j\le\nu),\qquad

In expanding the $j$th power, only words using $j$ distinct positions

Multiplication by the block matrix in TDP5 proves the last isomorphism.

Solving the second equation of TDP24 proves the first line, and taking

\subsection{What this changes in a Deligne-style amplification}

Deligne's two relevant mechanisms have different exact inputs. In
1.4.3--1.5.2 the cohomological denominator has a bounded weight shift;
the trace formula identifies it with the Euler product; nonnegative
coefficients for the even tensor powers prevent a local pole from
being hidden inside the product. The finite tensor calculation

\begin{equation}\tag{TDP27}
2w(\alpha)<2+2^{-j}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
w(\alpha)<1+2^{-j-1}.
\end{equation}
This line is just division by two. Attribution of the full surrounding
proof remains to Deligne; the local abbreviated English source is not
used to supply omitted hypotheses or maps.

For the present programme, TDP3 gives the actual prime eigenvalues,
TDP24 gives their exact observation map, TDP18 gives the full tensor

The kernel contribution is the concrete rational function
$\mathcal E_p$, not an unnamed missing term. Its support-only value in

All formulas apply separately at $N=q-1,q,2q-1,2q$. Any signed return
uses the original signs $+,+,-,-$; for scalar absolute error estimates
the triangle inequality gives the sum of the four respective errors.
No cancellation between these cutoffs has been assumed. No equivalence
between the original conductor and Deligne's full cohomological trace

positivity assertion. The calculation instead supplies the exact
prime-power, determinant and tensor defect objects that the original
programme now has to evaluate, with their connecting maps proved.

There is an exact positive receiver for every finite original prime
combination, with the adjoint in the original metric. Let $\mathcal I$
be any finite set of prime-power indices $(p,m)$, let $c_{p,m}\in\mathbb C$
be arbitrary, and put $X=\sum_{(p,m)\in\mathcal I}c_{p,m}F_p^m$. Then
\begin{equation}\tag{TDP28}
\begin{aligned}
\Phi(X^\dagger X)-\Phi(X)^\dagger\Phi(X)
 &=(QXJ)^\dagger(QXJ)\\
 &=\sum_{(p,m),(r,n)\in\mathcal I}
 \overline{c_{p,m}}c_{r,n}\,
 J^\dagger(F_p^m)^\dagger QF_r^nJ\succeq0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Insertion of $I_E=P+Q$ between $X^\dagger$ and $X$ proves the first

the second identity, with all phases retained. Evaluation on $b\in B$

prime-action Gram identity. Equating it to Weil's scalar positivity
functional would require an additional proved map; no such equality is
asserted here. The positive adjoint-product defect and the Euler

factors: the latter contains $B_p(I-uD_p)^{-1}C_p$, without an adjoint.
