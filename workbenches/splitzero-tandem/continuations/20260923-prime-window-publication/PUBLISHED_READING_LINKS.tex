\subsection{Human source and exact coordinate
comparison}\label{human-source-and-exact-coordinate-comparison}

The compact density, Fourier product and zero multiplicities are
classical. The point-of-use attribution cites Juan Arias de Reyna,
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.05442v1}{\emph{An infinitely
differentiable function with compact support: Definition and
properties}, arXiv:1702.05442v1}, the author's English translation of
his 1982 paper. Theorem 1 and equations (1), (4), (9) give the source
construction. In the retained programme coordinates, the density is
b\_r(v) = r\^{}(-1) phi(v/r), and its entire transform is G\_r(z) =
phi-hat(rz/(2 pi i)), with the original r = 1/64. These equalities,
their Jacobian, the Fourier sign, index shift and resulting zero
multiplicities are proved beside UP's construction.

Arias de Reyna credits Jessen and Wintner's 1935 construction; that
historical attribution is identified as coming through his paper, not as
a new reading of their original. The author TeX was read through the
main argument and bibliography. The
\href{https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/zeta-function-research-reader/blob/84edd11df011c3e161a57dad9f083276a3c0bb95/workbenches/splitzero-tandem/branches/tau-zero-prime-positivity/CITATION_REVISION.json}{citation-revision
record} records the publication-copy changes against the unchanged owner
snapshot. Complete original proofs and displayed equations remain. The
source author's archive stays private; the publication supplies
citations and programme derivations rather than copying the archive.
