\section{All-prime tensor coinvariants of the actual scalar boundary}

This calculation uses the original maps in
\texttt{06\_adelic\_boundary.tex}, AB1--5 and AB20--24, and
\texttt{11\_mixed\_extension\_prime\_trace.tex}, ME1--7.
Their complete public proof is retained in
\href{https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/zeta-function-research-reader/blob/3c78cfbd9e194d35117b33277c421c6564ce3dbf/workbenches/splitzero-tandem/continuations/20260923-full-prime-support/031/01_REBUILT_MATHEMATICS.tex}{the pinned full prime-support source, AB1--5, AB20--24 and ME1--7}.
All tensor products are algebraic over \(\mathbb C\).
The group is the original \(\Gamma=\mathbb Q^\times\), with
\(|p|_p=p^{-1}\), the unchanged Archimedean absolute value, and the
unchanged self-dual additive Haar measures. In particular no factor
from the reference functions or the diagonal action is removed.

\subsection{The original direct limit as a function space}

Let \(\mathcal P\) denote all positive rational primes,
\(I=\{\infty\}\sqcup\mathcal P\), and
\(\Omega=\{0,1\}^{I}\) with its compact product topology.
For finite \(F\subset\mathcal P\), write
\[
X_F=\{0,1\}^{\{\infty\}\sqcup F},\qquad
B_F=\mathbb C^{X_F}.
\]
The original face vector \(e_A\) is identified with the coordinate
function \(\mathbf1_{\{\epsilon_v=\mathbf1_{v\in A}\}}\) on \(X_F\).
It is not the supported scalar zero \(e\).
Under this identification AB20 and ME1 become the exact pullback
along coordinate forgetting:
\[
j_{FF'}c=c\otimes\bigotimes_{p\in F'\setminus F}(1,1),
\qquad
(j_{FF'}c)(x)=c(x|_{\{\infty\}\sqcup F}).
\tag{TB1}
\]
The two entries of every added factor are both one, as supplied by
the actual function \(\mathbf1_{\mathbb Z_p}\). There is no
averaging coefficient.

Let \(\operatorname{LC}(X,\mathbb C)\) mean the complex-valued
locally constant functions on a compact space \(X\).
Every locally constant function on \(\Omega\) factors through a
finite \(X_F\). Indeed, choose a cylinder neighborhood on which it
is constant about each point. Compactness gives a finite subcover;
the union of those neighborhoods' finite coordinate sets is a
single finite determining set. Enlarging that set to contain
\(\infty\) and its occurring primes gives \(F\).
Conversely a function pulled back from \(X_F\) is locally constant.
Thus the original direct limit is exactly
\[
B=\varinjlim_F B_F
   =\operatorname{LC}(\Omega,\mathbb C).
\tag{TB2}
\]
This is also an isomorphism for coefficientwise multiplication,
because TB1 preserves that multiplication and its unit.
The arithmetic operators below are linear multiplication
operators, not unital algebra automorphisms. The original
Schwartz boundary maps are likewise used as linear maps.

Fix an integer \(r\ge1\), and put \(A_r=B^{\otimes r}\).
The exact tensor identification is
\[
A_r\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{LC}(\Omega^r,\mathbb C),
\qquad
f_1\otimes\cdots\otimes f_r\longmapsto
 \bigl((\epsilon^{(1)},\ldots,\epsilon^{(r)})
          \mapsto\prod_{i=1}^r f_i(\epsilon^{(i)})\bigr).
\tag{TB3}
\]
At each finite stage, the coordinate delta functions give a basis
and the displayed map identifies its tensor basis with the delta
basis of \(X_F^r\). Any finite collection of tensor factors is
represented at one common \(F\). Every locally constant function
on \(\Omega^r\) also depends on one such finite \(F\), by the same
compactness argument. These facts prove both injectivity and
surjectivity of TB3.

At a point of \(\Omega^r\), define the actual subsets and their
cardinalities
\[
S_v=\{i\in[r]:\epsilon_v^{(i)}=1\},\qquad
m_v=|S_v|,\qquad v\in I.
\]
For \(\gamma=\pm\prod_p p^{n_p}\), with only finitely many
\(n_p\ne0\), the tensor of the original AB5 action is
\[
(D_\gamma f)(x)=\chi_\gamma(x)f(x),\qquad
\chi_\gamma(x)=
 \prod_{p\in\mathcal P}p^{\,n_p(m_\infty(x)-m_p(x))}.
\tag{TB4}
\]
This follows by multiplying all \(r\) original characters.
The sign \(-1\) acts trivially. The product in TB4 is finite and
retains every prime exponent. Each \(\chi_\gamma\) is an invertible
locally constant function.

\subsection{The full quotient by all rational relations}

Define the closed subset
\[
Z_r=\{x\in\Omega^r:m_p(x)=m_\infty(x)\text{ for every }p\}.
\tag{TB5}
\]
For finite \(F\), write \(X_{r,F}=X_F^r\), and let
\(Z_{r,F}\subset X_{r,F}\) impose precisely the conditions for
\(p\in F\). The projection \(Z_r\to Z_{r,F}\) is onto: given its
finite subset data, at each missing prime choose any subset of
\([r]\) of size \(|S_\infty|\). The same argument shows that
each map \(Z_{r,F'}\to Z_{r,F}\) is onto.

For \(d=-r,-r+1,\ldots,r\), set
\(\delta_p=m_\infty-m_p\), and define the exact polynomial projector
\[
\Pi_{p,0}=
\prod_{\substack{-r\le d\le r\\d\ne0}}
              \frac{D_p-p^d I}{1-p^d},\qquad
\Pi_F=\prod_{p\in F}\Pi_{p,0}.
\tag{TB6}
\]
Every denominator is nonzero. Since \(D_p\) multiplies by
\(p^{\delta_p}\), the first operator is multiplication by
\(\mathbf1_{\{\delta_p=0\}}\), and the second by the pullback of
\(\mathbf1_{Z_{r,F}}\). This proves TB6 on all of \(A_r\).

Let the actual coinvariant relation space be
\[
\mathcal I_r=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}
       \{(D_\gamma-I)f:\gamma\in\Gamma,\ f\in A_r\}.
\]
It is an ideal for coefficientwise multiplication:
\(h(D_\gamma-I)f=(D_\gamma-I)(hf)\).
Moreover it is exactly the ideal generated by
\(\chi_p-1\), \(p\in\mathcal P\).
For inverses use
\(\chi_p^{-1}-1=-\chi_p^{-1}(\chi_p-1)\);
for products use
\(uv-1=(u-1)v+(v-1)\).
These identities prove both inclusions with only finite sums.

The restriction map has the exact kernel and quotient
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal I_r
 =\{f\in A_r:f|_{Z_r}=0\},\qquad
(A_r)_\Gamma\xrightarrow{\sim}
 Q_r:=\operatorname{LC}(Z_r,\mathbb C),\quad [f]\mapsto f|_{Z_r}.
}
\tag{TB7}
\]
Here is a complete finite-stage proof. Each relation vanishes on
\(Z_r\), by TB4--5. Conversely, represent a function \(f\) vanishing
on \(Z_r\) at a finite \(F\). Surjectivity of
\(Z_r\to Z_{r,F}\) implies \(f|_{Z_{r,F}}=0\), hence
\(\Pi_Ff=0\).
For each \(p\), the polynomial
\[
R_p(t)=\frac{1-
 \prod_{d\ne0,\,-r\le d\le r}(t-p^d)/(1-p^d)}{t-1}
\]
is a polynomial, because its numerator vanishes at \(t=1\).
Ordering \(F=\{p_1,\ldots,p_N\}\), the telescoping product identity
then gives the explicit relation decomposition
\[
f=(I-\Pi_F)f
 =\sum_{j=1}^N(D_{p_j}-I)R_{p_j}(D_{p_j})
                \left(\prod_{i<j}\Pi_{p_i,0}\right)f.
\tag{TB8}
\]
If \(F\) is empty, vanishing on \(Z_r\) already forces \(f=0\).
This proves the kernel statement.

For surjectivity, every locally constant function on the compact
closed subspace \(Z_r\) factors through a finite \(Z_{r,F}\).
To see this, choose relative cylinder neighborhoods of constancy,
take a finite subcover, and take their common finite coordinate set.
Points with those same coordinates then have the same value.
Extend the resulting function on the finite set \(Z_{r,F}\)
arbitrarily to \(X_{r,F}\), for example by zero on its complement.
Its pullback lies in \(A_r\) and has the prescribed restriction.
This proves all of TB7, including its algebra structure.
Equivalently, compactness supplies a finite subcover of the support
of a function vanishing on \(Z_r\) by the clopen sets
\(\{\chi_p\ne1\}\), and division there by \(\chi_p-1\) gives the
same ideal. No completed function space is used.

The invariant vectors, in contrast, are
\[
\boxed{A_r^\Gamma=0\quad(r\ge1).}\tag{TB9}
\]
An invariant function can be nonzero only on \(Z_r\), since
\((p^{\delta_p}-1)f=0\) at every point. But \(Z_r\) has empty
interior in \(\Omega^r\). Every nonempty cylinder constrains only
finitely many primes; choose a new prime and prescribe a subset
at it whose cardinality differs from the already chosen
\(m_\infty\). Such a cardinality always exists for \(r\ge1\).
The resulting point leaves \(Z_r\) without leaving the cylinder.
A nonzero locally constant function has a nonempty open nonzero
set, so it cannot be supported on \(Z_r\).

In particular no \(\Gamma\)-equivariant section of TB7 exists:
its target is nonzero and trivial as a \(\Gamma\)-module, whereas
an equivariant section would have invariant image.
There is no all-prime projector on \(A_r\) obtained by silently
forming the infinite product in TB6. Applied to the constant
function one, that putative pointwise product is
\(\mathbf1_{Z_r}\), which is not locally constant.
Thus the quotient TB7 is not an invariant subspace of \(A_r\).

\subsection{Balanced subset space and the endpoint tensor map}

Let \(\mathcal C_m=\{S\subset[r]:|S|=m\}\).
The definitions give the exact homeomorphism
\[
Z_r=
\bigsqcup_{m=0}^r
 \left(\mathcal C_m\times\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}\mathcal C_m\right).
\tag{TB10}
\]
The first factor records \(S_\infty\); every other factor records
the actual prime subset \(S_p\). Each component is clopen,
since \(m_\infty\) takes finitely many values.
In particular
\[
|Z_{r,F}|=\sum_{m=0}^r\binom rm^{|F|+1}.
\tag{TB11}
\]
For \(r=1\), both factors \(\mathcal C_0,\mathcal C_1\) are
singletons, and \(Q_1=\mathbb C^2\), recovering AB23.
For \(r\ge2\), the dimensions in TB11 grow without bound.
The prime set is countable and its increasing finite initial
segments are cofinal; thus \(Q_r\) is a union of countably many
finite-dimensional spaces. Its dimension is exactly countably
infinite. The components \(m=0,r\) each contribute one dimension;
each \(1\le m\le r-1\) contributes countably infinite dimension.

The tensor of the original endpoint quotient AB23 is restriction
to the finite subset
\[
E_r=\{x:S_p=S_\infty\text{ for every }p\}\subset Z_r.
\tag{TB12}
\]
It has one point \(x_S\) for each \(S\subset[r]\), because each
tensor row at such a point is either all zero or all one.
Consequently the exact factorization is
\[
A_r\longrightarrow Q_r
 \xrightarrow{\operatorname{ev}_{E_r}}\mathbb C^{\mathcal P([r])}
   =(\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes r}.
\tag{TB13}
\]
On a pure tensor this sends
\(f_1\otimes\cdots\otimes f_r\) to the product of the specified
zero or integral endpoint coordinates in each row, so it is
precisely the original endpoint tensor map, not a new evaluation.

Its exact kernel is
\[
\mathcal J_r=\{\phi\in Q_r:\phi(x_S)=0\text{ for every }S\subset[r]\}.
\tag{TB14}
\]
At a finite stage it consists of the functions on \(Z_{r,F}\)
vanishing on the diagonal subset
\[
E_{r,F}=\{(S,(S)_{p\in F}):S\subset[r]\}.
\]
Thus its finite-stage dimension is
\(\sum_m\binom rm^{|F|+1}-2^r\).
Its ideal is generated by the indicators
\(\mathbf1_{\{S_p\ne S_\infty\}}\) on \(Z_r\):
the union of these clopen sets is the complement of \(E_r\),
and a function vanishing on \(E_r\) has compact support covered
by finitely many of them. A finite clopen partition subordinate
to that cover expresses it as a sum of their multiples.
This proves an explicit description of the complete kernel.

The restriction in TB13 is onto and has the specified algebra
section
\[
(\sigma u)(x)=u(S_\infty(x)),\qquad
u\in\mathbb C^{\mathcal P([r])},\quad x\in Z_r.
\tag{TB15}
\]
This section is equivariant on \(Q_r\), whose action is trivial.
It is not an equivariant section into \(A_r\).
If \(\mathcal K_r=\ker(A_r\to\mathbb C^{2^r})\), the entire
kernel comparison is
\[
\mathcal I_r\subset\mathcal K_r,\qquad
\mathcal K_r/\mathcal I_r=\mathcal J_r,\qquad
0\to\mathcal J_r\to Q_r\to\mathbb C^{2^r}\to0.
\tag{TB16}
\]
For \(r=1\), \(\mathcal J_1=0\). For every \(r\ge2\), it has
countably infinite dimension.

An explicit nonzero degree-two class invisible to every endpoint
tensor evaluation is
\[
\phi_2=\left.
\mathbf1_{\{S_\infty=\{1\},\,S_2=\{2\}\}}
\right|_{Z_2}.
\tag{TB17}
\]
Its actual representative at \(F=\{2\}\) is
\(e_{\{\infty\}}\otimes e_{\{2\}}\); its two original eigenvalues
at \(2\) are \(2\) and \(2^{-1}\), whose product is exactly one.
It is nonzero at the point with \(S_\infty=\{1\}\),
\(S_2=\{2\}\), and \(S_p=\{1\}\) for all \(p\ne2\).
It vanishes at every \(x_S\), where \(S_2=S_\infty\).
Evaluation at the displayed point is a linear functional
annihilating all rational coinvariant relations and taking the
value one on \(\phi_2\); thus nonvanishing is proved in the actual
coinvariant quotient.
Replacing \(2\) by each prime \(p\) gives linearly independent
classes: evaluate a finite linear combination at the point
where precisely that one prime is changed from \(\{1\}\) to
\(\{2\}\). The same construction works for any \(r\ge2\), keeping
every other row zero. This also directly proves the asserted
dimension of \(\mathcal J_r\).

\subsection{The full prime-weight filtration and its finite multiplicities}

For each original rational prime \(p\), define
\[
A_{r;p,d}=\mathbf1_{\{\delta_p=d\}}A_r,\quad -r\le d\le r,
\qquad
W^{(p)}_w A_r=\bigoplus_{2d\le w}A_{r;p,d}
\quad(w\in\mathbb Z).
\tag{TB18}
\]
These are exact subspaces of the original representation,
\[
A_r=\bigoplus_{d=-r}^r A_{r;p,d},\qquad
D_p|_{A_{r;p,d}}=p^d I=p^{(2d)/2}I.
\tag{TB19}
\]
The prime weight is therefore \(2(m_\infty-m_p)\), with its sign
and full factor retained. For any integer \(n\), the actual
prime-power action is \(D_{p^n}=p^{nd}I\) on this summand.
These are weights of the actual rational dilation operators.
No operator has been renamed a geometric Frobenius.

For \(p\in F\), its finite-stage weight-\(2d\) multiplicity is
\[
2^{\,r(|F|-1)}\binom{2r}{r+d}.
\tag{TB20}
\]
Indeed it is
\(2^{r(|F|-1)}\sum_m\binom rm\binom r{m-d}\).
The sum counts choices of \(r+d\) elements from two disjoint
\(r\)-element sets, choosing \(m\) from the first and
\(r-m+d\) from the second; this proves the displayed binomial.
More generally, for a prescribed finite prime tuple
\((d_p)_{p\in F}\), the exact joint multiplicity is
\[
\sum_{m=0}^r\binom rm\prod_{p\in F}\binom r{m-d_p},
\tag{TB21}
\]
with binomial coefficient zero outside \(0,\ldots,r\).
It counts the subsets \(S_\infty,S_p\) with their actual
cardinality differences, and includes every multiplicity.
Setting every \(d_p=0\) recovers TB11.

The filtrations for different primes commute. The tensor map
\(A_r\otimes A_s\to A_{r+s}\), with row coordinates concatenated,
satisfies
\[
W^{(p)}_u A_r\otimes W^{(p)}_v A_s
       \longrightarrow W^{(p)}_{u+v}A_{r+s},
\]
because the exact cardinality differences add.
Every relation quotient in TB7 kills the nonzero \(p\)-weight
spaces and retains only weight zero for that prime:
its induced filtration is zero for \(w<0\) and all of \(Q_r\)
for \(w\ge0\). Surjectivity for \(w=0\) follows by applying
\(\Pi_{p,0}\) to any lift of a function on \(Z_r\).
This does not identify \(Q_r\) with the simultaneous weight-zero
subspace of \(A_r\), which is zero by TB9.

\subsection{Dual inverse limits, with all pushforward factors}

The full algebraic dual of \(A_r\) is exactly
\[
A_r^\vee
=\varprojlim_F(\mathbb C^{X_{r,F}})^\vee
=\varprojlim_F\mathbb C^{X_{r,F}}.
\tag{TB22}
\]
Here a finite dual vector \(\nu_F\) pairs with a function \(f_F\)
by \(\sum_{x\in X_{r,F}}\nu_F(x)f_F(x)\); no conjugation is used
in this algebraic duality. If \(q_{FF'}\) forgets the added prime
coordinates, the exact bonding map is
\[
(q_{FF'*}\nu_{F'})(x)
   =\sum_{y:q_{FF'}y=x}\nu_{F'}(y).
\tag{TB23}
\]
Every fiber has \(2^{r|F'\setminus F|}\) points. Formula TB23 is
the transpose of TB1 and has no division by that number.
A coherent family defines a functional on the direct limit
by this pairing; coherence makes it well-defined. Conversely,
restriction of any algebraic functional to each finite stage
gives such a coherent family. These two constructions are inverse,
proving TB22 without a boundedness or measure-completion assumption.

Similarly,
\[
Q_r^\vee=\varprojlim_F\mathbb C^{Z_{r,F}},
\qquad
(q_{FF'*}\nu_{F'})(x)
  =\sum_{\substack{y\in Z_{r,F'}\\q_{FF'}y=x}}\nu_{F'}(y).
\tag{TB24}
\]
Above a point with \(m_\infty=m\), the fiber now has exactly
\(\binom rm^{|F'\setminus F|}\) points. The sum still has no
averaging coefficient.
Extension by zero from \(Z_{r,F}\) to \(X_{r,F}\) commutes with
these pushforwards: a point outside \(Z_{r,F}\) has no preimage
inside \(Z_{r,F'}\), and a point inside has precisely the fiber
just stated. Thus TB24 embeds canonically into TB22 and
\[
Q_r^\vee=(A_r^\vee)^\Gamma.
\tag{TB25}
\]
One proof is to dualize the exact quotient TB7: invariant
functionals are exactly those annihilating all its relations.
Equivalently, a finite functional invariant under all \(p\in F\)
has zero coefficient at every \(x\notin Z_{r,F}\), since some
nonzero scalar \(p^{\delta_p(x)}-1\) then multiplies its coefficient.
This proves the same statement in the inverse limit.
For the contragredient convention
\((D_\gamma^\vee\ell)(f)=\ell(D_{\gamma^{-1}}f)\), the finite
dual prime weights are exactly \(-2\delta_p\).
The transpose without the inverse has the other sign; these are
distinct conventions, and TB25 is valid with the stated
contragredient convention.

These inverse-limit vectors are algebraic functionals, equivalently
coherent weights on cylinder sets. Countable additivity and bounded
total variation are not imposed. Endpoint evaluation embeds
\((\mathbb C^{2^r})^\vee\) into TB24 as the coherent finite sums
of point evaluations at \(x_S\). TB17 supplies invariant
functionals and coinvariant classes beyond those endpoints.

For comparison with AB24, retain its actual complement pairing
on the finite tensor space:
\[
\mathscr B_{r,F}(f,g)
 =\sum_{x\in X_{r,F}}\overline{f(x)}\,g(x^{\rm c}),
\]
where every row and every place bit is complemented. Then
\[
\mathscr B_{r,F'}(j_{FF'}f,j_{FF'}g)
 =2^{r|F'\setminus F|}\mathscr B_{r,F}(f,g).
\tag{TB26}
\]
There is one summand for each added bit choice, proving this
factor directly. On the finite balanced quotient, restricting
the same sum to \(Z_{r,F}\), its component with \(m_\infty=m\)
instead picks up the factor
\(\binom rm^{|F'\setminus F|}\). Complementation changes \(m\)
to \(r-m\), whose binomial has the same value.
Only the endpoint tensor pairing is unchanged, because its
finite diagonal points extend uniquely. No rescaling of these
pairings or their measures has been introduced.

\subsection{The original endpoint extension and support receiver}

At the stage \(F=\varnothing\), let
\[
b_S=\mathbf1_{\{S_\infty=S\}}\in A_r,\qquad S\subset[r].
\]
These vectors form the specified vector-space section of the
endpoint tensor quotient, and give the actual exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal K_r\longrightarrow A_r
 \longrightarrow\mathbb C^{2^r}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{TB27}
\]
It has no equivariant section, by TB9.
Its connecting cocycles are explicitly
\[
c_S(\gamma)=(D_\gamma-I)b_S\in\mathcal K_r,
\]
and on the original generator \(p\) they have the full finite
coordinates
\[
c_S(p)=
\sum_{T\subset[r]}
\bigl(p^{|S|-|T|}-1\bigr)
       \mathbf1_{\{S_\infty=S,\,S_p=T\}}.
\tag{TB28}
\]
The group law in TB4 proves
\(c_S(\gamma\eta)=D_\gamma c_S(\eta)+c_S(\gamma)\).
If \(\sum_S a_Sc_S\) is a coboundary \(D_\gamma k-k\) with
\(k\in\mathcal K_r\), then \(\sum_Sa_Sb_S-k\) is invariant.
It vanishes by TB9, and applying the endpoint quotient gives
every \(a_S=0\). Thus these \(2^r\) cocycles are linearly
independent in \(H^1(\Gamma,\mathcal K_r)\).
For \(r=1\), TB28 is exactly ME7 at its original value \(s=1\):
\((p^{-1}-1)e_{\{p\}}\) and \((p-1)e_{\{\infty\}}\).
This verifies the connection to ME without changing its
representation parameter.

Finally the original scalar receivers are actual surjective maps
\[
\mathcal T_{\rm all}^{\otimes r}
 \xrightarrow{\,b^{\otimes r}\,} A_r
 \longrightarrow Q_r
 \longrightarrow(\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes r},
\qquad
\mathcal T_{\rm all}
=\mathcal S(\mathbb R)\otimes
 \bigotimes_p'(\mathcal S(\mathbb Q_p),\mathbf1_{\mathbb Z_p}).
\tag{TB29}
\]
Here each local coordinate is still
\(b_v(f)=(f(0),\int f)\), and a face coordinate integrates
exactly its indicated places and evaluates the remaining
places at their supported zeros, as in AB2--3.
Surjectivity follows from the actual finite local sections
of AB4 and tensoring them: each element is represented at a
finite stage and lifts there; the reference functions have
both boundary entries equal to one, so the maps commute with
TB1. This also proves that all maps in TB29 receive the actual
scalar boundary of the original test space.

For the original bounded distributive support lattice
\(\mathcal L\), every linear map in TB29 and every proved
linear identification has the same-label lift
\[
(v,1_{\mathcal L})\longmapsto(Uv,1_{\mathcal L}),
\qquad
(0,\ell)\longmapsto(0,\ell)\quad(\ell\ne1_{\mathcal L})
\]
on \(G_{\mathcal L}(V)\). Linearity and the retained support
join prove addition and composition preservation.
An amplitude in a relation kernel therefore goes to supported
zero, while each lower zero remains at its original label;
for \(0_{\mathcal L}\ne1_{\mathcal L}\), this is distinct from
absence. In the one-element lattice the formulas still hold
and there is no distinct lower label.
These are lifts of the proved scalar boundary maps on the original
full-support arithmetic stratum. TB3 concerns the specified
algebraic amplitude tensor product; it does not silently identify
that tensor product with a tensor product over a support semiring.

The spaces computed here are the scalar boundary receivers,
their actual rational coinvariants, their full algebraic duals,
and the specific connecting cocycles just proved. No statement
identifies them with the whole arithmetic cohomology, with a
theta cohomology, or with a new Frobenius operator. The surviving
mixed quotient TB14 is an exact retained space of boundary
objects, not a reason to discard the original endpoint maps.
