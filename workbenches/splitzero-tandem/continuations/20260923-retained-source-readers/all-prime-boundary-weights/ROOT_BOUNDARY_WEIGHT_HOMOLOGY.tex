\section{The object and the part of weight control computed here}
The absolute base remains the whole spectrum of the original globalization
semiring. For a bounded distributive lattice $L$ and a ring $R$, its
coefficient-and-support object is
\[
 G_L(R)=\{(0,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}\cup\{(a,1_L):a\in R\},
 \qquad \tau=(0,0_L),\quad e=(0,1_L).
 \tag{BW1}
\]
Addition uses coefficient addition and lattice join; multiplication uses
coefficient multiplication and lattice meet. The overlap in this union is
exactly $e$. In particular, the base for $L=\{0,1\}$ and $R=\Z$ retains
$(\tau)\subsetneq(e)\subsetneq(p)$. We do not replace this space by its
open Boolean localization or by the closed arithmetic stratum.

The full function-valued support faces of the programme have an actual
scalar boundary map: on each place, evaluate at supported zero and
integrate with the original self-dual additive Haar measure. Its precise
definition and original rational action are AB1--5, AB20--24 of
\cite{AB}. This paper computes weights on all tensor powers of that
specific boundary receiver and their rational group homology. It also
gives the exact connecting map back into the original test-space kernel.
It does not identify this boundary homology with the interior cohomology
of the zeta programme.

The reason to compute tensors before quotients is mathematical:
Deligne's weight argument uses tensor constituents and coinvariants in
its cohomological trace calculation, followed by duality
\cite[1.3.13, 1.4.1--1.5.3, 1.8.4, 3.3.1--3.3.6]{Deligne}.
Below, mixed tensor coinvariants survive which are killed by the
tensor power of the two-endpoint quotient. All their actual weights
and multiplicities are calculated. No Frobenius on this coefficient
space is assumed: the operator used here is the original idele dilation.

\section{The full boundary and all its tensor coinvariants}
Write $\mathcal P$ for all rational primes and
$\Omega=\{0,1\}^{\{\infty\}\sqcup\mathcal P}$, with product topology.
For a finite $F\subset\mathcal P$ put
\[
 \Omega_F=\{0,1\}^{\{\infty\}\sqcup F},\qquad
 B_F=\C^{\Omega_F},\qquad
 j_{FF'}e_A=\sum_{C\subseteq F'\setminus F}e_{A\cup C}.
 \tag{BW2}
\]
Thus $j_{FF'}$ is exactly tensoring by $(1,1)$ at every new prime.
Here $e_A$ is a coordinate vector, not the supported scalar zero $e$.
It is the characteristic function of the cylinder with that coordinate
pattern. A locally constant function on a compact space has finite
image. On this product, each such function depends on finitely many
coordinates: cover the space by cylinders on which it is constant,
take a finite subcover, and take the union of their coordinate sets.
Consequently
\[
 B=\varinjlim_F B_F=\LC(\Omega,\C),\qquad
 V_r=B^{\otimes_\C r}=\LC(\Omega^r,\C)\quad(r\ge1).
 \tag{BW3}
\]
The tensor is algebraic. The second equality follows at finite stages
by the characteristic-function bases and then by the same finite
cylinder argument. The diagonal family of stages $B_F^{\otimes r}$
is cofinal among all choices of $r$ finite stages.

For $\omega=(\eps_{v,j})\in\Omega^r$ set
$m_v(\omega)=\sum_{j=1}^r\eps_{v,j}$. The actual diagonal action of
$\gamma=\pm\prod_pp^{n_p}\in\Q^\times$ is
\[
 (D_\gamma^{(r)}f)(\omega)=
 \prod_p p^{n_p(m_\infty(\omega)-m_p(\omega))}f(\omega).
 \tag{BW4}
\]
Only finitely many $n_p$ are nonzero. This is obtained by tensoring
AB5; no factor in the original action has been removed.

Define the following closed set and its finite stages:
\[
 Z_r=\{\omega:m_p(\omega)=m_\infty(\omega)\ \text{for all }p\},
 \qquad Z_{r,F}=\{\omega\in\Omega_F^r:m_p=m_\infty\ (p\in F)\}.
 \tag{BW5}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Every all-prime tensor coinvariant]
Restriction gives a surjection $\rho_r:V_r\to\LC(Z_r,\C)$ with
\[
 \ker\rho_r=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}(D_p^{(r)}-I)V_r.
 \quad\text{Hence}\quad
 (V_r)_{\Q^\times}=\LC(Z_r,\C).
 \tag{BW6}
\]
The sum consists of finite sums. Also $(V_r)^{\Q^\times}=0$.
Writing $\binom{[r]}m$ for the set of $m$-element subsets of
$\{1,\ldots,r\}$, there is the exact decomposition
\[
 Z_r=\coprod_{m=0}^r Z_{r,m},\qquad
 Z_{r,m}=\binom{[r]}m\times\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}\binom{[r]}m,
 \qquad
 |Z_{r,F}|=\sum_{m=0}^r\binom rm^{|F|+1}.
 \tag{BW7}
\]
Each component is both open and closed in $Z_r$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every multiplier $D_p^{(r)}-I$ vanishes on $Z_r$. Conversely let
$f\in V_r$ vanish there. Its nonzero locus is a compact open and closed
set, disjoint from $Z_r$. Every point of that locus has
$m_p\ne m_\infty$ for at least one prime. Finitely many such primes
cover the locus. Partition it into finitely many disjoint cylinders
subordinate to this cover. On one cylinder $U$ choose its indicated
prime $p$. The locally constant multiplier
$u_p=p^{m_\infty-m_p}-1$ is nonzero there, so
$g=\mathbf1_Uf/u_p$, extended by zero, lies in $V_r$ and
$(D_p^{(r)}-I)g=\mathbf1_Uf$. Summing proves the kernel identity.

Restriction is onto. A locally constant function on the compact closed
subset $Z_r$ has a finite partition into relatively open and closed
sets of constant values. Each piece is the intersection with an open
and closed set of $\Omega^r$: choose cylinders inside its relative
open neighbourhood and use a finite cover of that compact piece.
Successive differences make the chosen extensions disjoint without
altering their intersections with $Z_r$. Extending the values there
and assigning zero elsewhere gives the desired preimage.

Relations from primes generate the relations of the whole group:
$D_{ab}-I=D_a(D_b-I)+(D_a-I)$ and
$D_{p^{-1}}-I=-D_{p^{-1}}(D_p-I)$; the sign acts trivially.
This proves the coinvariant assertion.

An invariant $f$ must be supported on $Z_r$. The latter has empty
interior: after fixing finitely many coordinates, choose a new prime
and a column with sum different from the prescribed infinity sum.
Since $r\ge1$, such a column exists. Every nonzero locally constant
function is nonzero on some open cylinder, so no such $f$ exists.
Finally, for fixed $m$, each column records exactly an $m$-element
subset. The choices at all places are independent. This proves BW7,
including the finite count.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact loss under the endpoint tensor quotient]
Let $\epsilon:B\to\C^2$ evaluate at the two constant patterns
$0^\infty,1^\infty$. Its tensor power factors as
\[
 V_r\xrightarrow{\rho_r}\LC(Z_r)
 \xrightarrow{\operatorname{res}_{E_r}}\C^{E_r}
       =(\C^2)^{\otimes r},\qquad
 E_r=\{(\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_r):
              \omega_j\in\{0^\infty,1^\infty\}\}.
 \tag{BW8}
\]
For $r\ge2$ the kernel of the second map is infinite dimensional.
For $r=2$ and any prime $p$, the actual finite-stage tensor
\[
 e_{\{\infty\}}\otimes e_{\{p\}}\in B_{\{p\}}^{\otimes2}
 \tag{BW9}
\]
has a nonzero image in $\LC(Z_2)$ and zero image under
$\epsilon^{\otimes2}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each endpoint tuple is balanced, so BW8 is the stated restriction.
For BW9 the infinity column is $\{1\}$ and the $p$ column is
$\{2\}$; their sums are both one. Complete all other prime columns
arbitrarily with sum one. This gives a point of $Z_2$ where BW9
has value one. On an endpoint tuple every prime column equals the
infinity column, so BW9 vanishes there.
For general $r\ge2$ choose $0<m<r$ and a prime column different
from the infinity column, with both of size $m$. This defines a
nonempty cylinder in $Z_{r,m}$ disjoint from $E_r$. Refining it at
arbitrarily many other primes yields arbitrarily many linearly
independent disjoint cylinder functions in the kernel. The whole
locally constant space has countable dimension, because its finite
stages form a countable exhaustion. Thus this kernel has countably
infinite dimension.
\end{proof}

\section{Compatible prime-weight filtrations before the quotient}
The finite prime weights of the actual operator on $V_r$ are
\[
 w_p(\omega)=2(m_\infty(\omega)-m_p(\omega)),\qquad
 D_p^{(r)}f=p^{w/2}f
 \quad\text{on }V_{r,p,w}=\mathbf1_{\{w_p=w\}}V_r.
 \tag{BW10}
\]
Every even $w$ between $-2r$ and $2r$ occurs. Therefore the following
is a filtration of the original module, defined at all primes:
\[
 W_n^pV_r=\bigoplus_{w\le n}V_{r,p,w},\qquad
 \Pi_{p,2a}=\prod_{\substack{-r\le b\le r\\b\ne a}}
       \frac{D_p^{(r)}-p^bI}{p^a-p^b}\quad(-r\le a\le r).
 \tag{BW11}
\]
The denominators are nonzero since $p>1$. On each cylinder the
polynomial is one for its named exponent and zero for every other
exponent, proving the formula. These projectors commute at different
primes and commute with every later support inclusion after that
prime has been included. Thus every finite collection of these
filtrations is compatible with the actual direct limit. Tensoring
coefficient directions adds $w_p$ exactly. BW9 is the explicit
cancellation of weights $+2$ and $-2$ into weight zero. The original
operator, rather than a scalar change of it, gives these weights.

At a finite stage the projection to simultaneous weight zero is
restriction to $Z_{r,F}$ followed by extension by zero. Under a
new prime this is not the old projection followed by the unrestricted
support inclusion: one must retain exactly the new columns with the
same sum $m$. The induced map on coinvariants is
\[
 \LC(Z_{r,F})\longrightarrow\LC(Z_{r,F'}),\qquad
 f\longmapsto f\circ\operatorname{pr}_{F'F}.
 \tag{BW12}
\]
On the $m$ component each coordinate is repeated exactly
$\binom rm^{|F'\setminus F|}$ times. Hence in the original counting
norm its squared norm is multiplied by that precise integer.
Before restriction to balanced columns, the squared-norm multiplier
is $2^{r|F'\setminus F|}$. Both statements follow by counting the
actual extended columns; no factor is absorbed in the transition map.

\section{All-degree rational homology and actual idele weights}
Let $E=\bigoplus_{p\in\mathcal P}\C\theta_p$, with primes ordered
by their ordinary increasing order. Use algebraic group homology.

\begin{theorem}[Complete homology of the boundary tensor]
For every $r\ge1$ and $j\ge0$ there is the natural isomorphism
\[
 H_j(\Q^\times,V_r)
   =\LC(Z_r,\C)\otimes_\C\Lambda^jE.
 \tag{BW13}
\]
Let $a=(a_v)$ be an idele, with original modulus
$|a|=|a_\infty|_\infty\prod_p|a_p|_p$. On the summand
$\LC(Z_{r,m})\otimes\Lambda^jE$, its original unweighted
dilation acts exactly by
\[
 D_a^{(r)}=|a|^m I.
 \tag{BW14}
\]
Thus the global modulus weights, in the convention
$|a|^{w/2}$, are exactly $w=0,2,\ldots,2r$. For $j=0$ the
two extreme components have multiplicity one; every interior
component has countably infinite dimension. For each $j\ge1$,
every component has countably infinite dimension.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a finite prime set $F$, put
$R_F=\C[t_p^{\pm1}:p\in F]$. The sequence $(t_p-1)_{p\in F}$
is regular: successive quotients are Laurent polynomial rings on
the remaining variables, and multiplication by the next $t_p-1$
is injective. Its Koszul resolution is consequently free and exact;
this also follows inductively by the mapping cone of the
one-variable resolution. The Laurent ring $R$ on all primes is free
over each $R_F$, with basis the Laurent monomials in the other
variables. Extending the finite resolution to $R$ therefore resolves
$R/(t_p-1:p\in F)$. The filtered union of these augmented complexes
resolves $R/(t_p-1:p\in\mathcal P)=\C$. Filtered unions preserve
exactness: an element and its proposed preimage or relation are
represented at a finite stage.

After tensoring with $V_r$ the resulting chain complex is the
union of
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 C_j(F)=B_F^{\otimes r}\otimes\Lambda^j\C^F,\\
 \begin{aligned}
 d(b\otimes\theta_{p_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta_{p_j})
 &=\sum_{h=1}^j(-1)^{h-1}(D_{p_h}^{(r)}-I)b\\
 &\hspace{8mm}\otimes\theta_{p_1}\wedge\cdots
              \widehat{\theta_{p_h}}\cdots\wedge\theta_{p_j}.
 \end{aligned}
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{BW15}
\]
Every coefficient vector and every exterior vector occurs in some
common finite stage. The support inclusions commute with the old
prime actions, so BW15 is a chain inclusion into every larger stage.

Decompose at stage $F$ into the original coordinate lines of
$\Omega_F^r$. On a line set $c_p=p^{m_\infty-m_p}-1$.
If one $c_p\ne0$, the degree-raising map
$c_p^{-1}\theta_p\wedge(-)$ satisfies $dh+hd=I$, by the
exterior contraction identity. On the balanced lines every $c_p=0$,
so the differential is zero. Projection to those lines therefore
gives exactly
\[
 H_j(C_\bullet(F))=\LC(Z_{r,F})\otimes\Lambda^j\C^F.
 \tag{BW16}
\]
For a larger stage, projecting an included balanced cycle discards
the unbalanced new columns and leaves precisely BW12, together
with the ordinary exterior inclusion. Homology commutes with this
filtered union: a cycle and a proposed bounding chain each have a
finite stage representative. Hence BW16 gives BW13 for the positive
rational group. The sign subgroup has order two, acts trivially,
and its trivial module over $\C$ is projective by the averaging
idempotent $(1+[-1])/2$. Tensoring the resolution with this
projective resolution concentrated in degree zero proves BW13 for
all of $\Q^\times$.

The actual local integral scales by $|a_v|_v$ and the local
evaluation is unchanged. On $V_r$ the multiplier is therefore
$\prod_v|a_v|_v^{m_v}$. The product is finite away from infinity,
because an idele has $|a_p|_p=1$ at all but finitely many primes.
This action commutes with rational dilation, acts trivially on the
named group generators in BW15, and on $Z_{r,m}$ is exactly
$\prod_v|a_v|_v^m=|a|^m$. This proves BW14 without a new operator.
BW7 gives the multiplicities in degree zero. For $j\ge1$,
$\Lambda^jE$ has a countably infinite basis of $j$-element prime
sets. This proves all the remaining multiplicities.
\end{proof}

This is weight control on the computed boundary homology. It is
not the assertion that cohomological degree itself adds a weight:
the factor in BW14 has been calculated and depends on $m$, not on
$j$. Nor is $H_j(\Q^\times,V_r)$ being relabelled
$H^j(\Q^\times,V_r)$. The latter has a different degree-zero group,
already visible in BW6 and its invariant statement.

\section{The connecting map from these weights to the original kernel}
Let
\[
 \mathcal T=\mathcal S(\R)\otimes
       \bigotimes_p'(\mathcal S(\Q_p),\mathbf1_{\Z_p}),
 \qquad b=\bigotimes_v(f\mapsto(f(0),\int f)):\mathcal T\to B.
 \tag{BW17}
\]
This is the original algebraic restricted Schwartz tensor product.
At each place its two displayed functionals are independent. At a
finite prime, $\mathbf1_{\Z_p}$ and $\mathbf1_{p\Z_p}$ give moment
columns $(1,1)$ and $(1,p^{-1})$, whose determinant is nonzero.
At infinity, a nonzero nonnegative smooth compact function supported
away from zero has zero evaluation and positive integral; combine
it with a smooth compact function of nonzero value at zero. These
give an explicit two-dimensional section at every place. Tensoring
finite sections proves that $b$ is onto. Its compatibility with the
restricted-product reference functions follows from their moments
$(1,1)$, including every Haar factor.

Put $\mathcal T_r=\mathcal T^{\otimes r}$ and
\[
 J_r=\ker(b^{\otimes r})
 =\sum_{i=1}^r\mathcal T^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes\ker b
                    \otimes\mathcal T^{\otimes(r-i)}.
 \tag{BW18}
\]
The equality follows by choosing a vector-space section of the
surjective linear map $b$ and expanding the tensor of its direct
sum with its kernel. Both sides are independent of that choice.
The maps are equivariant for rational and idele dilation, so
\[
 0\longrightarrow J_r\longrightarrow\mathcal T_r
       \xrightarrow{b^{\otimes r}}V_r\longrightarrow0
 \tag{BW19}
\]
is an exact sequence of the original modules. Its homology sequence
contains the exact connecting maps
\[
 H_j(\Q^\times,\mathcal T_r)\longrightarrow
 \bigoplus_{m=0}^r\LC(Z_{r,m})\otimes\Lambda^jE
 \xrightarrow{\partial_r}H_{j-1}(\Q^\times,J_r).
 \tag{BW20}
\]
For this connecting map the sign subgroup must be kept as well:
it acts trivially on $V_r$ but can act nontrivially on
$\mathcal T_r$ and $J_r$. Write $\iota=U_{-1}^{\otimes r}$ and
retain the two exact projectors
$P_+=(I+\iota)/2$, $P_-=(I-\iota)/2$.
They sum to the identity, multiply to zero and commute with
every rational and idele action. Taking $P_+$ is exact on
equivariant vector-space sequences, since an arbitrary lift can
be replaced by its $P_+$ image. The odd summand has zero
homology for the full group: the trivial $\C[\{\pm1\}]$ module
is its even projective summand, whose tensor with an odd module
is zero. Tensoring with the positive-prime resolution proves
this in every degree for $\Q^\times$.
Thus the original full-group homology is calculated by the
positive-prime Koszul complex on the even summands, with this
explicit comparison from the entire coefficient module.

To calculate $\partial_r$, represent a cycle in BW15 and lift
its finitely many coefficient vectors to $\mathcal T_r$.
Replace each lift $t$ by the exact even lift
$P_+t=(t+U_{-1}^{\otimes r}t)/2$, whose boundary image is unchanged.
Apply the same differential BW15 to these lifts.
Its boundary image is zero because the original vector was a
cycle, so the result belongs to $J_r$ and represents
$\partial_r$. Changing a lift adds a chain in $J_r$ and changes
the answer by its differential. Changing a cycle by a boundary
changes the answer by a boundary by $d^2=0$. These observations
prove the formula, its well-definedness and the usual exactness
directly. Every operation commutes with idele dilation. Therefore
\[
 D_a\partial_r(c)=|a|^m\partial_r(c)
 \quad\text{for }c\in\LC(Z_{r,m})\otimes\Lambda^jE.
 \tag{BW21}
\]
This computes the weight of every nonzero class actually reached
by this connecting map; it does not assert that all its classes
are nonzero or that it exhausts the interior.

In degree one, a vector $b_0$ fixed by $D_p^{(r)}$ gives the cycle
$b_0\otimes\theta_p$. For any original test lift $t$,
\[
 \partial_r[b_0\otimes\theta_p]=[(U_p^{\otimes r}-I)t]
       \quad\text{in }(J_r)_{\Q^\times},\qquad b^{\otimes r}t=b_0.
 \tag{BW22}
\]
For BW9 this applies at its named prime $p$: its two factors have
eigenvalues $p$ and $p^{-1}$ and their product is one. It supplies
the exact map into the original kernel for the mixed class erased
by the endpoint tensor quotient.

Formula BW22 allows any lift because $t-P_+t$ belongs to $J_r$,
and $(U_p^{\otimes r}-I)(t-P_+t)$ is already a coinvariant
relation inside that original kernel. The factor $1/2$ and the
odd summand have thus both been accounted for explicitly.

\section{Keeping the support lattice on every receiving map}
For a complex vector space $M$ use the labelled coefficient
semimodule
\[
 G_L(M)=\{(0,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}\cup\{(v,1_L):v\in M\}.
 \tag{BW23}
\]
Its addition and the action of $G_L(\C)$ use precisely coefficient
addition/scalar multiplication and join/meet of the labels.
For every linear map $f:M\to N$, define
$G_L(f)(0,\lambda)=(0,\lambda)$ and
$G_L(f)(v,1_L)=(f(v),1_L)$. Checking the two operations shows this
is a semimodule map; it preserves all labels, including $e$ and
$\tau$. This is a coefficient receiver over the retained geometric
base, not a replacement of that base by $\Spec G_L(\C)$.

In particular $G_L(\rho_r)$ is onto and its fibres are exact:
all labels $(0,\lambda)$ with $\lambda\ne1_L$ have singleton
fibres, while the fibre at $(h,1_L)$ is
\[
 \{(f,1_L):f|_{Z_r}=h\}.
 \tag{BW24}
\]
For $h=0$ this fibre contains $e$ and every supported amplitude
vanishing on $Z_r$, and contains no other zero label. Thus the
amplitude relation in BW6 never identifies supported zero with
$\tau$. Likewise $G_L(W_n^pV_r)$ is the exact labelled filtration
subobject, retaining the entire zero-label lattice at every level.
Apply the same explicit functor to BW20--22 and their actual
linear maps. On a supported vector its weight factor is the
unchanged scalar $|a|^m$; on a zero label the label is fixed.
Every square commutes because the original linear square does
and every map is the identity on the labels.

\section{Consequence for the continuing weight construction}
The first tensor quotient has two endpoint classes. In every
higher tensor order there are further mixed classes, whose
complete support space, rational homology and original idele
weights are BW7, BW13 and BW14. The map from them into the actual
test-space kernel is BW20--22. Taking the endpoint quotient first
deletes these classes, with the explicit nonzero lost class BW9.
Consequently the tensor and cohomological stages of the weight
construction must use BW19 before making that quotient.

Deligne's image-purity theorem concerns an actual map
$H_c^i\to H^i$ and follows from the upper direct-image weight
theorem together with duality \cite[3.3.1, 3.3.5--3.3.6]{Deligne}.
The present result computes the full tensor boundary term and
its connecting map in our existing object. The construction of
the corresponding interior image and its weight control is
still the next mathematical task. No unknown purity hypothesis
has been used to prove any formula above.
