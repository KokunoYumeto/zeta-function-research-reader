\begin{abstract}
The original sum-polynomial source admits two positive covariance evolutions with exactly the same arithmetic heat clock: independent slot heat and synchronized slot heat. Their relative covariance annihilates the cyclic source in complex amplitudes. Over the full split-support base, its action is instead the supported heat identity, and the complete tensor factorization acquires an explicit idempotent. Translation enlarges the parity support flag to the total-degree flag. These effects are computed on the independent-support source, with exact maps to the function carrier and the original attained metric. A regular moving-coordinate heat transformation is then classified in a specified quadratic-gauge/affine-coordinate class. Its logarithmic-derivative Cauchy kernel differs from its actual zero-trace kernel by a rank-one growth term. The entire negative index of that extra term is calculated using the newly published rational-test density theorem. A direct full-theta asymptotic also proves rigidity of the unchanged arithmetic initial function in this remapping class. The collision-contact coefficient is invariant under these regular heat remappings, while the marked residue curvature transforms by exactly the same quadratic-gauge coefficient. Finally, the published all-order mixed-prime recurrence is realized by explicit half-line shifts, with a two-sided operator-tail bound and a complete original-source Taylor remainder. The newly published prime-window criterion is also propagated through Gaussian heat in its translation variable, producing an entire one-point positivity tower with an explicit finite prime-tail certificate. These are new connecting calculations and bounds, not a proof of global Weil positivity or a disproof of RH.
\end{abstract}

\section{Heat and translation on independent split supports}
Let $L$ be any nontrivial bounded distributive lattice and
\begin{equation}
 S=G_L(\C)=\{(0,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}
 \cup\{(c,1_L):c\in\C\},\qquad
 \tau=(0,0_L),\quad e=(0,1_L).                       \tag{ST1}
\end{equation}
Addition is amplitude addition with support join; multiplication is amplitude multiplication with support meet. Write $c^\bullet=(c,1_L)$ and $z_\lambda=(0,\lambda)$. In $eS\cong L$ the identity is $e$ and $e^{-1}=e$. No localization is required for this identity. For any $S$-semimodule the map $m\mapsto em$ is the original support retraction; its two adjunctions and its local module fibres are retained from \cite{Prior}.

Fix $N\ge2$. In the coefficient order $1,Z,\ldots,Z^N$, let $D$ be differentiation, with each structural zero equal to $\tau$ and each nonzero integer coefficient fully supported. Define
\begin{equation}
 F_{a,b}=\exp(-aD^2+bD),\qquad a,b\in S.             \tag{ST2}\label{ST2}
\end{equation}
Every minus sign in a scalar coefficient denotes multiplication by $(-1)^\bullet$, not an additive inverse relative to $\tau$. The exponential is a finite polynomial, since $D^{N+1}=0$ with the global matrix zero. More explicitly, for $j\ge i$, $d=j-i$,
\begin{equation}
 (F_{a,b})_{ij}=
 \sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}
 \left(\frac{(-1)^k j!}{i!k!(d-2k)!}\right)^\bullet
 a^k b^{d-2k};                                     \tag{ST3}\label{ST3}
\end{equation}
entries below the diagonal are $\tau$. A power with exponent zero is the ambient identity.

\begin{theorem}[Full two-parameter law and its supported identities]
For every four parameters in $S$,
\begin{equation}
 F_{a,b}F_{c,d}=F_{a+c,b+d},\qquad F_{\tau,\tau}=I.
                                                               \tag{ST4}\label{ST4}
\end{equation}
At labelled-zero parameters $a=z_\lambda,b=z_\mu$, write $R_{\lambda,\mu}=F_{a,b}$. Its diagonal is $1^\bullet$, and its strict upper entries are
\begin{equation}
 (R_{\lambda,\mu})_{ij}=
 \begin{cases}
 z_\mu,&j-i\text{ odd},\\
 z_{\lambda\vee\mu},&j-i>0\text{ even}.
 \end{cases}                                                    \tag{ST5}\label{ST5}
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}
 R_{\lambda,\mu}^2=R_{\lambda,\mu},\qquad
 R_{\lambda,\mu}R_{\lambda',\mu'}
 =R_{\lambda\vee\lambda',\mu\vee\mu'}.
                                                               \tag{ST6}\label{ST6}
\end{equation}
The exact parameter image for these idempotents is the join-semilattice
$\{(\nu,\mu):\mu\le\nu\}$, through the join-preserving map $(\lambda,\mu)\mapsto(\lambda\vee\mu,\mu)$. This parameter map is not asserted to preserve the componentwise meet on $L^2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
All derivative powers commute. Multiplying the finite exponentials and grouping total powers is justified by distributivity; the factorial convolution is the binomial identity, which holds in a commutative semiring. This proves \eqref{ST4}, including cases where amplitudes cancel to $e$. For \eqref{ST5}, a positive power of $z_\lambda$ is $z_\lambda$. When $d$ is odd, every term includes a positive power of $b$, and the $k=0$ term has label $\mu$; the other labels are at most $\mu$. When $d$ is positive and even, one term uses $a^{d/2}$ and no $b$, another uses $b^d$ and no $a$, and every other term has label $\lambda\wedge\mu$. Their join is $\lambda\vee\mu$. Each displayed nonzero rational scalar fixes any labelled zero. The diagonal is unchanged. The two entries with $d=1,2$ recover $\mu$ and $\lambda\vee\mu$, proving the exact image assertion and, in particular, not claiming that all of $L^2$ embeds faithfully.
\end{proof}

If an input vector has coordinate labels $\nu_i$, the new label at $i$ is exactly
\begin{equation}
 \nu_i\vee\left(\mu\wedge\bigvee_{j>i}\nu_j\right)
 \vee\left(\lambda\wedge
       \bigvee_{\substack{j>i\\j-i\ {\rm even}}}\nu_j\right).
                                                               \tag{ST7}\label{ST7}
\end{equation}
This follows by matrix action and distributivity of $L$. Its amplitudes are unchanged. Put
\begin{equation}
 P=F_{e,\tau},\qquad Q=F_{e,e}=F_{\tau,e}.
 \qquad PQ=QP=Q.                                               \tag{ST8}\label{ST8}
\end{equation}
The image of $P$ has nested same-parity supports. The image of $Q$ has the entire nested flag $\nu_j\le\nu_i$ for $j>i$. Thus $Q:\im P\to\im Q$ is an amplitude-preserving retraction with nontrivial support congruence.

For real or complex supported parameters,
\begin{equation}
 F_{t^\bullet,b^\bullet}F_{(-t)^\bullet,(-b)^\bullet}=Q.
                                                               \tag{ST9}\label{ST9}
\end{equation}
They form a group on $QS^{N+1}$ with identity $Q$, not on the whole free source. Even $b=0^\bullet=e$ differs from an absent translation parameter $\tau$. For a finite path of parameter additions, the final operator is obtained by the literal semiring sums in \eqref{ST4}; cancelled amplitudes leave their joined zero labels. If the entire amplitude path closes, its full return may be an idempotent $R_{\lambda,\mu}$ instead of $I$. This is support retraction, not an asserted nontrivial commutator in the abelian parameter semigroup.

\subsection{The exact comparison with functions and with a moving frame}
The map to a common-labelled polynomial is
\begin{equation}
 \eta_N:S^{N+1}\longrightarrow G_L(\C[Z]_{\le N}),\quad
 ((c_i,\nu_i))_i\longmapsto
 \left(\sum_i c_iZ^i,\bigvee_i\nu_i\right).                     \tag{ST10}\label{ST10}
\end{equation}
It is an $S$-linear surjection. If the polynomial amplitude is nonzero, some input label is top, so the output lies in the stated carrier. Distributivity proves the scalar meet law. Distinct patterns with the same polynomial and same total support are its exact fibre congruence. The diagonal entries of $F_{a,b}$ preserve each original input label somewhere, and no output label exceeds the join of those inputs. Hence
\begin{equation}
 \eta_NF_{a,b}
 =\left[e^{-\amp(a)\partial_Z^2+\amp(b)\partial_Z}\right]^\uparrow
 \eta_N.                                                        \tag{ST11}\label{ST11}
\end{equation}
The arrow on the right acts on amplitudes and fixes the declared label, including at a zero result. This is an explicit comparison, not an identification of the free independent-support source with its common-labelled image. In particular $\eta_NP=\eta_NQ=\eta_N$ does not imply $P=Q$.

For a differentiable ordinary shift $b(t)$, the amplitude operator
$T_{b(t)}e^{-tD^2}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \partial_t(T_be^{-tD^2})=(-D^2+b'D)T_be^{-tD^2}.
                                                               \tag{ST12}\label{ST12}
\end{equation}
Thus the covariant derivative $\partial_t-b'D$ recovers the unchanged heat generator. On split sources this is differentiated within each fixed supported coefficient chart; no differentiable path from the distinct absent point $\tau$ to $e$ is assumed. Its free-source initial identity is as in \eqref{ST9}, and its projective initial identity is $Q$.

\section{Independent and synchronized heat on the original cyclic source}
Let $r\ge2$, and use the total-degree-$N$ polynomial source
$\mathcal P_N^{(r)}=\C[s_1,\ldots,s_r]_{\le N}$. Denote its partial derivatives by $D_i$, and retain the original cyclic substitution
\begin{equation}
 \beta:\C[S]_{\le N}\longrightarrow\mathcal P_N^{(r)},\quad
 \beta P=P(s_1+\cdots+s_r).                                    \tag{SY1}\label{SY1}
\end{equation}
The variable $S$ in \eqref{SY1} is the arithmetic sum coordinate, not the semiring $S$ of Section 2. These polynomial maps are applied before the original quotient and primitive-unit maps. In particular no derivative descent to $\C[S]/h$ is presumed.

There are two useful restrictions, with different factors:
\begin{align}
 (\Delta F)(s)&=F(s,\ldots,s),&\Delta\beta P&=P(rs),\notag\\
 (\Delta_{\rm sum}F)(S)&=F(S/r,\ldots,S/r),&
 \Delta_{\rm sum}\beta&=I.                                    \tag{SY2}\label{SY2}
\end{align}
Their matrix entries are literal multinomial coefficients and the indicated powers of $r^{-1}$. The split lifts satisfy the same identities: each homogeneous degree is retained, and the nonzero multinomial coefficients sum to $r^j$ or $1$ respectively. This gives an actual split injection for the cyclic source.

For a real symmetric matrix $C$ of slot covariances set
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal U_C=\exp\left(\frac14\sum_{i,j=1}^r C_{ij}D_iD_j\right).
                                                               \tag{SY3}\label{SY3}
\end{equation}
On this finite polynomial source the series terminates. Since every $D_iD_j\beta=\beta\partial_S^2$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\mathcal U_C\beta
 =\beta\exp\left(\frac{\ve^{\mathsf T}C\ve}{4}\partial_S^2\right).}
                                                               \tag{SY4}\label{SY4}
\end{equation}
Thus $\ve^{\mathsf T}C\ve$ is the exact heat-clock functional seen by the cyclic source.

\begin{theorem}[Calibrated synchronization and a positive relative covariance]
Put $\Pi_\perp=I-\ve\ve^{\mathsf T}/r$ and
\begin{equation}
 C_{\rm ind}=\frac trI,\qquad
 C_{\rm sync}=\frac t{r^2}\ve\ve^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 C_{\rm rel}=\frac tr\Pi_\perp.                                \tag{SY5}\label{SY5}
\end{equation}
For $t\ge0$ all three matrices are positive semidefinite, and
\begin{align}
 \mathcal U_{\rm ind}&=\mathcal U_{\rm sync}\mathcal U_{\rm rel},\notag\\
 \mathcal U_{\rm ind}\beta&=\mathcal U_{\rm sync}\beta
     =\beta e^{t\partial_S^2/4},\qquad
 \mathcal U_{\rm rel}\beta=\beta.                             \tag{SY6}\label{SY6}
\end{align}
More generally every covariance $C$ with $\ve^{\mathsf T}C\ve=t$ has this same cyclic action. If $C\succeq0$, its action is identically the cyclic identity for all degrees precisely when $C\ve=0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The covariance sum is $C_{\rm ind}=C_{\rm sync}+C_{\rm rel}$. Partial derivatives commute, so finite exponentials factor. The three clock functionals are $t,t,0$. Formula \eqref{SY4} proves \eqref{SY6}. A positive matrix has $\ve^{\mathsf T}C\ve=\|C^{1/2}\ve\|^2=0$ exactly when $C\ve=0$. Conversely the degree-two cyclic polynomial detects a nonzero clock functional, proving the final necessity.
\end{proof}

The adjective positive in this theorem refers to the explicitly given covariance matrix. It does not assert positivity of an indefinite Weil pairing, or an isometry for an untransported original source norm.

\subsection{The full split factorization has an additional identity}
Lift the independent covariance sparsely: its off-diagonal zeros are $\tau$. Lift every coefficient of $C_{\rm sync}$ and $C_{\rm rel}$ as fully supported, including an amplitude cancellation at $t=0^\bullet$. Denote the resulting operators by $\mathcal U_{\rm ind}^{\uparrow}$, $\mathcal U_{\rm sync}^{\uparrow}$, $\mathcal U_{\rm rel}^{\uparrow}$. The positive factor $1/4$ in \eqref{SY3} is unchanged.

Let
\begin{equation}
 P_\Sigma=\exp\left(\frac e4(D_1+\cdots+D_r)^2\right).
                                                               \tag{SY7}\label{SY7}
\end{equation}
Its diagonal is the ambient identity. The entry from $s^{\boldsymbol j}$ to $s^{\boldsymbol i}$ is $e$ if $\boldsymbol i\le\boldsymbol j$ coordinatewise and $|\boldsymbol j|-|\boldsymbol i|$ is positive even, and $\tau$ otherwise. Thus $P_\Sigma^2=P_\Sigma$.

\begin{theorem}[Synchronization over the unchanged full support lattice]
For every supported complex $t$, including $t=0^\bullet=e$,
\begin{align}
 \boxed{\mathcal U_{\rm sync}^{\uparrow}
            \mathcal U_{\rm rel}^{\uparrow}
        =\mathcal U_{\rm ind}^{\uparrow}P_\Sigma,}\notag\\
 P_\Sigma\beta^\uparrow&=\beta^\uparrow P,\qquad
 \mathcal U_{\rm rel}^{\uparrow}\beta^\uparrow=\beta^\uparrow P,
                                                               \tag{SY8}\label{SY8}\\
 \mathcal U_{\rm ind}^{\uparrow}\beta^\uparrow
 &=\mathcal U_{\rm sync}^{\uparrow}\beta^\uparrow
 =\beta^\uparrow\mathcal E^+_{t^\bullet},
 \qquad \mathcal E^+_a=\exp(a\partial_S^2/4).                 \tag{SY9}\label{SY9}
\end{align}
Here $P$ is the same parity-supported identity as Section 2. In general
$\mathcal U_{\rm ind}^{\uparrow}P_\Sigma\ne
\mathcal U_{\rm ind}^{\uparrow}$ on the full tensor source.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The diagonal coefficients of the sum of the synchronized and relative covariances are $(t/r)^\bullet$. Every off-diagonal coefficient cancels to $e$, not to $\tau$. The resulting differential generator equals the sparse independent generator plus $e(D_1+\cdots+D_r)^2/4$: the added diagonal $e$ leaves an already supported entry unchanged, while the added cross terms supply precisely the missing $e$ entries. The finite exponential identity proves the boxed factorization. Its entries also give the description of $P_\Sigma$; composing two degree-lowering even-support patterns again produces that same pattern.

For the cyclic assertions, every $D_iD_j\beta^\uparrow$ has the exact scalar derivative coefficients. In the relative generator their amplitude sum is zero and their support join is top, so it maps each positive two-degree decrease to a supported zero. Repeating this calculation gives $\beta^\uparrow P$ after exponentiation. Equivalently, every nonzero coefficient of $(s_1+\cdots+s_r)^j$ is supported, and each admissible output monomial of lower same-parity degree receives at least one supported contribution. The amplitude is the ordinary identity by \eqref{SY6}. These two coordinates prove the equality without discarding any label. The independent and synchronized clock sums are $t$, and the same coefficient calculation proves \eqref{SY9}. Finally, a mixed monomial $s_1s_2$ has a supported constant output under $P_\Sigma$, whereas the sparse independent heat has no such structural output. This proves the final distinction.
\end{proof}

This is the dynamic support contribution hidden by the ordinary equality $\mathcal U_{\rm rel}\beta=\beta$. It is an identity on the correct parity-projective cyclic source, not on an arbitrarily chosen free source. It does not synchronize all tensor labels into one common label.

\subsection{A varying synchronization schedule and the actual metric}
For any differentiable real $\rho(t)$ define
\begin{equation}
 C_\rho(t)=\frac{t}{r^2}\ve\ve^{\mathsf T}
             +\frac{t(1-\rho(t))}{r}\Pi_\perp.
                                                               \tag{SY10}\label{SY10}
\end{equation}
The clock functional is identically $t$, so \eqref{SY4} is the same scalar heat for every schedule. Its full generator is
\begin{equation}
 \frac14\sum_{i,j}
 \left[\frac{\ve\ve^{\mathsf T}}{r^2}
 +\frac{1-\rho-t\rho'}r\Pi_\perp\right]_{ij}D_iD_j.
                                                               \tag{SY11}\label{SY11}
\end{equation}
The coefficient $1-\rho-t\rho'$ may be negative even when every accumulated covariance in \eqref{SY10} is positive. This relative component is invisible on cyclic amplitudes, but its full support source must still use the charts and identities just proved. Changes of a structural-zero convention at a vanishing coefficient are not differentiations inside one fixed support chart.

Let $\mathcal A$ be the original fixed tensor-source realization, including its complete primitive units, and let $J$ be its original value map. The identities above may be composed with $\mathcal A$ and $J$ because they hold before those maps. In particular the two calibrated cyclic source columns are literally identical. If $H>0$ is their original coefficient Gram and $E=e^{t\partial_S^2/4}$ is used as a coordinate change, the transported pair is
\begin{equation}
 H_t=E^*HE,\qquad J_t=JE,
 \qquad J_tH_t^{-1}J_t^*=JH^{-1}J^*.                          \tag{SY12}\label{SY12}
\end{equation}
Its attained arithmetic metric $(JH^{-1}J^*)^{-1}$ is unchanged. The proof is the explicit inverse $H_t^{-1}=E^{-1}H^{-1}E^{-*}$. In the split source the inverse is relative to its projective identity. If one instead keeps $H$ fixed while changing the value map, the covariance is $JEH^{-1}E^*J^*$, a different expression.

Adding genuinely new tensor representatives is also different from this coordinate change. For an old source Gram $H_X$, full cross Gram $C_X$, new Gram $H_Y$, and original value map $J_X$, the complete normal quantities are
\begin{equation}
 H_Z=H_Y-C_X^*H_X^{-1}C_X,\qquad
 F_Z=J_Y-J_XH_X^{-1}C_X,
 \qquad G_{\rm joint}^{-1}=G^{-1}+F_ZH_Z^+F_Z^*.
                                                               \tag{SY13}\label{SY13}
\end{equation}
This is the retained original complete-source minimum, not a new synchronization theorem. It follows by orthogonalizing the actual new source against the old source; equality of representatives implies $\ker H_Z\subseteq\ker F_Z$. The inverse is on the positive range of that full normal Gram. Thus no independent bound or metric choice deletes the source defect.

\subsection{The diagonal product receiver is not the cyclic sum receiver}
For the other map $\Delta$ in \eqref{SY2}, the chain rule gives
\begin{equation}
 e^{t\partial_s^2/4}\Delta
 =\Delta\exp\left(\frac t4(D_1+\cdots+D_r)^2\right)
 =\Delta e^{t\sum_iD_i^2/4}
             e^{t\sum_{i<j}D_iD_j/2}.                        \tag{SY14}\label{SY14}
\end{equation}
Every exponential terminates on the polynomial source. This is the exact mixed derivative correction when separately evolving factors and then multiplying them. The covariance difference $\ve\ve^{\mathsf T}-I$ has eigenvalues $r-1,-1,\ldots,-1$, unlike the positive relative covariance in \eqref{SY5}. The distinction is accounted for by $\Delta\beta P=P(rs)$ and by the factors $r^{-1},r^{-2}$ in \eqref{SY5}; they solve different clock-normalization problems.

For any differentiable covariance path $C(t)$, a fixed tensor polynomial $F$, and $f_t=\Delta\mathcal U_{C(t)}F$, commutation of the finite differential operators gives
\begin{equation}
 (\partial_t-\tfrac14\partial_s^2)f_t
 =\frac14\Delta\sum_{i,j}(C'_{ij}-1)D_iD_j\mathcal U_{C(t)}F.
                                                               \tag{SY15}\label{SY15}
\end{equation}
It vanishes for all tensor polynomials exactly when $C'=\ve\ve^{\mathsf T}$; testing $s_i^2$ and $s_is_j$ proves necessity. For the uncalibrated interpolation $C=t[(1-\rho)I+\rho\ve\ve^{\mathsf T}]$ it is

 -\frac{1-\rho-t\rho'}2\,

\end{equation}
Thus a time-dependent synchronization can genuinely correct a product evolution, but the exact mixed term must be included. Applying this finite identity to an infinite Euler product without convergence would not be justified. The actual arithmetic infinite source is handled by the published convergent derivative recurrence and the explicit bounds in Sections 8--9.

\section{Regular moving heat coordinates and their exact residual}
We next calculate a specified class of coordinate changes of the entire arithmetic heat equation. The heat symmetry itself is classical; the new receiving calculations are its full split-source comparison, Cauchy growth correction, contact coefficient, and residue-data transport. Nothing in this section identifies an arbitrary time-dependent coordinate change with a heat symmetry.

Let $r=s-1/2$, and consider
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde g_t(s)=e^{\phi(t,r)}g_{\theta(t)}(\varphi_t(s)),
 \quad \phi=ar^2+ibr+c,
 \quad\varphi_t(s)=\frac12+\alpha r+iy,                         \tag{RG1}\label{RG1}
\end{equation}
where $a,b,c,\alpha,y,\theta$ are real differentiable functions of real $t$ and $\alpha>0$. The target domains of the tests are restricted so $\varphi_t(z)$ does not meet any source zero; where the original half-plane estimates are used we impose $\Re\varphi_t(z)>1$. The formula preserves the reflected operation $f^\sh(s)=\overline{f(1-\bar s)}$. It need not preserve the separate unshifted functional equation $f(1-s)=f(s)$; that additional symmetry is not assumed.

For each fixed $t$, the spatial map $f\mapsto e^\phi f\circ\varphi_t$ is an invertible complex-linear map of entire functions. Its split lift sends $(f,1_L)$ to $(e^\phi f\circ\varphi_t,1_L)$ and fixes every labelled zero. If a coefficient-wise source representation is used instead, the map to this function carrier is precisely \eqref{ST10}, with its support congruence retained.

\begin{theorem}[Residual and exact clocks in the affine/quadratic class]
Write $g,g_\xi,g_{\xi\xi}$ for the source function and its derivatives at $(\theta(t),\varphi_t(s))$. If $g_\theta=g_{\xi\xi}/4$, then
\begin{align}
 e^{-\phi}(\partial_t-\tfrac14\partial_s^2)\widetilde g
 ={}&\frac{\theta'-\alpha^2}{4}g_{\xi\xi}
 +\left[(\alpha'-a\alpha)r+i(y'-\alpha b/2)\right]g_\xi\notag\\
 &+\left[(a'-a^2)r^2+i(b'-ab)r+c'-a/2+b^2/4\right]g.
                                                               \tag{RG2}\label{RG2}
\end{align}
This transformation carries \emph{every} solution of the heat equation to a solution exactly when
\begin{equation}
 \theta'=\alpha^2,\quad \alpha'=a\alpha,\quad
 y'=\alpha b/2,\quad a'=a^2,\quad b'=ab,\quad
 c'=a/2-b^2/4.                                                 \tag{RG3}\label{RG3}
\end{equation}
With initial constants subscripted $0$, the solution on $q(t)=1-a_0t>0$ is
\begin{align}
 a&=a_0/q,&b&=b_0/q,&\alpha&=\alpha_0/q,\notag\\
 y&=y_0+\frac{\alpha_0b_0t}{2q},&
 \theta&=\theta_0+\frac{\alpha_0^2t}{q},&
 c&=c_0-\frac12\log q-\frac{b_0^2t}{4q}.                      \tag{RG4}\label{RG4}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

 I_{\theta,y}(r)=\int_0^\infty

This use of the Gamma probability is an exact change of variable for the displayed original integral, not a replacement of its measure.

$A=|\theta|/16$, $B=|y|/2$, $\ell=\log(p/\pi)$,
$\epsilon=p^{-1/4}$, and assume $p\ge\max(16,8A)$.

 \mathbb P(|X/p-1|>\epsilon)\le2e^{-\sqrt p/4}.              \tag{AR3}\label{AR3}
\end{equation}
Indeed the upper Chernoff exponent is $p(\epsilon-\log(1+\epsilon))\ge p\epsilon^2/4$ and the lower one is at least $p\epsilon^2/2$. These inequalities follow by differentiating both sides, starting at zero.

\[
 \left|\log\frac{\varphi(X)}{\varphi(p)}\right|
 \le d_p:=\epsilon(4A\ell+4A+2B).

For $X\ge\pi$, the elementary bound

$\sqrt2\exp(3A\ell^2+4A-\sqrt p/8)$. The positive weight

 \mathbb EW\ge1-\frac{\pi+3/2}{p-1}>\frac12.

It tends to zero, uniformly on the indicated compact parameter sets.

                                                               \tag{PWG15}\label{PWG15}
\end{equation}
where the factor with $m-2j=0$ is one. This convention is a value of a continuous nonnegative scalar maximum, not a change to supported zero. The finite derivative expansion of a Gaussian proves
\begin{equation}
 |\gamma_\epsilon^{(m)}(x)|\le
 (4\pi\epsilon)^{-1/2}B_m(\epsilon)e^{-x^2/(8\epsilon)}.
                                                               \tag{PWG16}\label{PWG16}
\end{equation}
Indeed its $j$th coefficient is
$m!(-1)^{m-j}x^{m-2j}/[2^m\epsilon^{m-j}j!(m-2j)!]$ times the Gaussian. Split its exponential in half and use the exact maximum
$\sup_x|x|^d e^{-x^2/(8\epsilon)}=(4\epsilon d/\mathrm e)^{d/2}$ for $d>0$.
Put
\begin{equation}
 C_{\epsilon,m}=(4\pi\epsilon)^{-1/2}
 \left[B_{m+4}(\epsilon)+\tfrac12B_{m+2}(\epsilon)
                                      +\tfrac1{16}B_m(\epsilon)\right].
                                                               \tag{PWG17}\label{PWG17}
\end{equation}
Since $k_r$ is a probability measure on $[-2r,2r]$, for $|a|\le A$ and $\log n>A+2r$,
\[
 |h_{r,\epsilon}^{(m)}(a\pm\log n)|
 \le C_{\epsilon,m}
        e^{-(\log n-A-2r)^2/(8\epsilon)}.
\]
For $B=A+2r$, $c_B=B+2\epsilon$, and any integer $N$ with
$\log N\ge\max(B,2)$, define
\begin{align}
 J_{\epsilon,B}(N)=e^{B/2+\epsilon/2}
 \bigg[&4\epsilon e^{-(\log N-c_B)^2/(8\epsilon)}\notag\\
 &+c_B\sqrt{2\pi\epsilon}\,
  \operatorname{erfc}\left(\frac{\log N-c_B}{\sqrt{8\epsilon}}\right)\bigg].
                                                               \tag{PWG18}\label{PWG18}
\end{align}
The complete prime-tail bound is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\sup_{|a|\le A}|\partial_a^m(P_\epsilon-P_{\epsilon,N})(a)|
       \le2C_{\epsilon,m}J_{\epsilon,B}(N).}                 \tag{PWG19}\label{PWG19}
\end{equation}
For proof use $0\le\Lambda(n)\le\log n$. The scalar majorant is decreasing once $\log n\ge\max(B,2)$, since the logarithmic derivative in $u=\log n$ is
$1/u-1/2-(u-B)/(4\epsilon)\le0$. Its sum is bounded by
\[
 \int_{\log N}^\infty u\exp\left(u/2-(u-B)^2/(8\epsilon)\right)\,du.
\]
Completing the square gives exactly \eqref{PWG18}. This retains every integer, hence every omitted prime power, without a prime-counting or RH estimate.

\begin{corollary}[A finite prime-tail value for the original smoothed receiver]
At $\epsilon=1/8$, $a=0$, derivative order $m=0$ and integer cutoff $N=2^{16}$, the omitted prime contribution in \eqref{PWG14} satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{|P_{1/8}(0)-P_{1/8,65536}(0)|<6\cdot10^{-44}.}
                                                               \tag{PWG28}\label{PWG28}
\end{equation}
This is the prime-tail error of the first entry of \eqref{PWG11}, not an evaluation of that complete entry.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The displayed constants give
$B_2=32/\mathrm e+4$ and
$B_4=1024/\mathrm e^2+384/\mathrm e+48$ at $\epsilon=1/8$.
Using $\mathrm e>2$ and $\pi>2$ proves $C_{1/8,0}<507$.
Here $B=1/32$, $c_B=9/32$ and
$x=16\log2-9/32>997/96>10$.
The elementary Gaussian-tail inequality

 J_{1/8,1/32}(65536)
 <\frac{64}{59}\left(\frac12+\frac9{640}\right)
       e^{-(997/96)^2}=\frac{329}{590}e^{-994009/9216}.
\]
We used $e^{5/64}\le(1-5/64)^{-1}$, obtained by termwise comparison of the exponential and geometric series. Finally $\log2>2/3$ follows from its positive atanh series; $\log10<7/3$ follows because the sixth exponential partial sum at $7/3$ is $1071641/104976>10$.
Since $994009/9216>46(7/3)$, its exponential is less than $10^{-46}$.
Multiplying by $2C_{1/8,0}$ gives a prefactor below $166803/295<600$, proving \eqref{PWG28}. All omitted prime powers are included in this absolute majorant.
\end{proof}

The classical infinite bump also has a controlled finite source approximation. Let $k_{r,J}=b_{r,J}*b_{r,J}$ be the two finite box convolutions from UP. Its omitted convolution factor is a probability measure supported in
$[-2r2^{-J},2r2^{-J}]$. By the mean value theorem and \eqref{PWG17},
\begin{equation}
 \sup_a|h_{r,\epsilon}^{(m)}(a)-h_{r,\epsilon,J}^{(m)}(a)|
 \le\eta_m:=2r2^{-J}C_{\epsilon,m+1}.                        \tag{PWG20}\label{PWG20}
\end{equation}
The derivative orders and the factor two from the two convolution legs are explicit.

Let $W_N=\sum_{2\le n\le N}\Lambda(n)/\sqrt n$ and
\begin{equation}
 C_A=|\gamma_{\rm E}+\log\pi|+\frac32+
                    \frac{e^{-1}+4e^{-1/4}}{1-e^{-1}}.
                                                               \tag{PWG21}\label{PWG21}
\end{equation}
Using the finite source in the whole Archimedean expression and only prime powers up to $N$ gives the derivative error
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{2C_{\epsilon,m}J_{\epsilon,B}(N)
       +(C_A+2W_N)\eta_m+\frac18\eta_{m+2}.}                 \tag{PWG22}\label{PWG22}
\end{equation}
To verify its Archimedean part, integrate the preceding cancellation formula on $0<x\le1$. The inequalities $1-e^{-x}\ge x/2$ and
$|h(a+x/2)+h(a-x/2)-2h(a)|\le x^2\|h''\|_\infty/4$
give $3\eta_m/2+\eta_{m+2}/8$. On $x\ge1$ use the uniform difference $\eta_m$ and integrate the two exponentials; the constant term gives the remaining part of $C_A$. The finite prime part is at most $2W_N\eta_m$, and \eqref{PWG19} supplies the infinite tail.

For a completely finite integral one may retain $d\le x\le X$ in the Archimedean term. The omitted intervals have respective bounds
$3C_{\epsilon,m}d/2+C_{\epsilon,m+2}d^2/8$ for $0<d\le1$, and
$C_{\epsilon,m}(e^{-X}+4e^{-X/4})/(1-e^{-X})$ for $X\ge1$.
The middle quadrature has a completely explicit derivative bound as well. Write $q_d=e^{-d}$ and
\[
 A_0=(1-q_d)^{-1},\quad A_1=q_d(1-q_d)^{-2},\quad
 A_2=q_d(1+q_d)(1-q_d)^{-3}.
\]
For the $m$th translation derivative of its integrand, the numerator and its first two $x$ derivatives have respective bounds
\[
 2C_{\epsilon,m},\qquad
 \tfrac54C_{\epsilon,m}+\tfrac12C_{\epsilon,m+1},\qquad
 \tfrac{17}{16}C_{\epsilon,m}+\tfrac14C_{\epsilon,m+1}
                          +\tfrac14C_{\epsilon,m+2}.
\]
These follow by differentiating both $h(a+x/2)$ and $h(a-x/2)$ with their factors $1/2$, and $e^{-x/4}$ with its factor $1/4$. The reciprocal denominator and its first two derivatives are bounded by $A_0,A_1,A_2$. Consequently define
\begin{align}
 \mathfrak Q_{\epsilon,m}(d)={}&2C_{\epsilon,m}A_2
 +\left(\tfrac52C_{\epsilon,m}+C_{\epsilon,m+1}\right)A_1\notag\\
 &+\left(\tfrac{17}{16}C_{\epsilon,m}
    +\tfrac14C_{\epsilon,m+1}+\tfrac14C_{\epsilon,m+2}\right)A_0.
                                                               \tag{PWG25}\label{PWG25}
\end{align}
The composite midpoint rule on $n$ equal panels of $[d,X]$ then has error at most
\begin{equation}
 \frac{(X-d)^3}{24n^2}\mathfrak Q_{\epsilon,m}(d).
                                                               \tag{PWG26}\label{PWG26}
\end{equation}
To prove the constant, subtract the affine Taylor polynomial about each panel midpoint, integrate the bound $\|I''\|_\infty(x-x_0)^2/2$, and sum all $n$ panels. The same constants apply to the finite source $k_{r,J}$ because it too is a probability measure supported in $[-2r,2r]$.

Each integrand value itself has a finite formula. Let $b_1,\ldots,b_{2J}$ be the list $a_1,\ldots,a_J$, each repeated twice, and set
\[
 F_n(x)=\frac1{(n-1)!}\int_{-\infty}^x(x-y)^{n-1}\gamma_\epsilon(y)\,dy
 \quad(n\ge1),\qquad F_0=\gamma_\epsilon.
\]
Repeated one-dimensional integration gives
\begin{equation}
 (\gamma_\epsilon*k_{r,J})(x)=
 \frac1{\prod_{\ell=1}^{2J}(2b_\ell)}
 \sum_{\sigma\in\{\pm1\}^{2J}}\left(\prod_\ell\sigma_\ell\right)
 F_{2J}\left(x+\sum_\ell\sigma_\ell b_\ell\right).
                                                               \tag{PWG27}\label{PWG27}
\end{equation}
Indeed convolution with one uniform interval replaces $F_n$ by
$[F_{n+1}(x+b)-F_{n+1}(x-b)]/(2b)$, since $F_{n+1}'=F_n$.
For an explicit evaluation in elementary functions and the error function, put
\[
 I_0(x)=\tfrac12[1+\operatorname{erf}(x/(2\sqrt\epsilon))],\quad
 I_1(x)=-2\epsilon\gamma_\epsilon(x),\quad
 I_k(x)=-2\epsilon x^{k-1}\gamma_\epsilon(x)
                   +2\epsilon(k-1)I_{k-2}(x).
\]
These are the truncated Gaussian moments, by integration by parts using
$\gamma_\epsilon'=-x\gamma_\epsilon/(2\epsilon)$.
Expansion of $(x-y)^{n-1}$ gives
$F_n=(n-1)!^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{k}x^{n-1-k}(-1)^kI_k$.
Derivatives of \eqref{PWG27}, with the fixed filter $T^2$, give the required finite-source $h^{(m)}$. Outward bounds for the error function and exponentials and all finite cancellations must be included when these expressions are evaluated numerically; no floating-point cancellation is declared exact. The formula is finite, with no claim of optimal computational complexity.
