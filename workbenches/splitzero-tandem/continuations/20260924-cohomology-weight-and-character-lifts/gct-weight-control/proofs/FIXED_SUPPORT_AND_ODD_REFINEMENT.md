## OR1. The support proof does not require coordinates

Let X be the actual three-point topological space of the source. Its points are +, − and η. Its opens are

\[
\varnothing,\quad \{\eta\},\quad \{+,\eta\},\quad
\{-,\eta\},\quad X.
\]

Every nonempty open contains η. Therefore the closure of {η} is X. The points + and − are closed, since their complements are opens, so neither is generic. This proves that η is the unique generic point.

Every homeomorphism carries the closure of a singleton to the closure of its image. It must therefore carry a generic point to a generic point. Uniqueness gives αη=η for the source homeomorphism exchanging + and −. Thus fixedness follows from the topology; no midpoint or prescribed distance was used.

Let Q be the two-element label set with involution flip exchanging its elements. An exchanged-label map on a point x must obey

\[
p(\alpha x)=\operatorname{flip}(p(x)).
\]

At η this would imply p(η)=flip(p(η)), which no element of Q satisfies. No such label can be assigned at η. At + and − there are exactly two assignments, obtained by choosing either label at + and the other at −.

This is the exact proved interpretation of τ〈Z_1; no exchanged Z_2 label〉 in the source support. It is not a claim that η has no isotropy or that its stalk has no parity information: in the source quotient η has stabilizer C_2, and its sections transform. OR4 and OR5 retain those facts.

## OR2. An infinite record whose finite calibrations keep refining

Choose a positive duration c only to write the test record. Do not declare c to be its global primitive tick. The full ordered record has times

\[
t_0=c,\qquad t_r=\left(r+\frac{1}{3^r}\right)c
\quad(r\geq1).
\tag{OR2.1}
\]

The symbols r here index our formula in the surrounding mathematics. They are not the reconstructed arithmetic labels of the events. The record is strictly increasing: t_1−t_0=c/3>0, and

\[
t_{r+1}-t_r=\left(1-\frac{2}{3^{r+1}}\right)c>0.
\tag{OR2.2}
\]

It has no accumulation in a bounded time interval, since t_r≥rc. Every event is included. Attach any specified sequence of absorption/non-absorption marks to these times; retain it without alteration. The following assertions depend on the times, and do not erase those marks. A law constraining times from marks would be additional information to derive and use, not something supplied by the two labels alone.

For the prefix through t_r, let D_r be the additive subgroup of the duration line generated by t_0,…,t_r. For r=0 it is cZ. For r≥1 every displayed duration belongs to (c/3^r)Z, so D_r is contained in that group. Conversely

\[
t_r-r t_0=\frac{c}{3^r}
\]

belongs to D_r. Consequently

\[
D_r=\frac{c}{3^r}\mathbb Z.
\tag{OR2.3}
\]

The complete record generates the union

\[
D=\bigcup_{r\geq0}D_r=c\mathbb Z[1/3].
\tag{OR2.4}
\]

Every positive d in D has d/3 in D with 0<d/3<d. Hence D has no least positive element. More strongly, no positive duration δ can divide every event timing in the sense t_r∈δZ. Such a δ would imply D⊆δZ; but for large r the element c/3^r lies strictly between 0 and δ and belongs to D, contradicting that inclusion.

This proves an exact failure of the implication “every event has been included, therefore there is a positive common tick.” Each finite prefix has a primitive tick, but the complete record has none. Completeness is not the same property as having a discrete duration group.

The failure does not depend on observing the starting instant used in (OR2.1). The group generated by the consecutive gaps t_j−t_{j-1}, 1≤j≤r, is also (c/3^r)Z for r≥1. For r=1 the gap is c/3. Inductively the new gap is (3^r−2)c/3^r, while the old gap group contains c/3^{r-1}. Subtracting (3^{r-1}−1) times this old generator from the new gap leaves c/3^r. All gaps through that stage are multiples of this duration, giving equality. Therefore the complete record of inter-event durations alone generates the same D and has the same obstruction.

## OR3. Odd refinement preserves the full parity observation

Define

\[
\chi:D\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,
\qquad \chi\left(\frac{ac}{3^r}\right)=a\pmod2.
\tag{OR3.1}
\]

This is well defined. If a/3^r=b/3^s, then a3^s=b3^r; the powers of three are odd, so reducing this equality modulo two gives a=b modulo two. Bringing two fractions to a common denominator proves additivity. It is onto because χ(c) is the nonzero class.

Each stage D_r has its own positive generator c/3^r, and χ(c/3^r) is odd. In the next stage the same old generator is three copies of c/3^{r+1}, so its parity is unchanged. More generally the inclusion D_r→D_{r+1}, written in those respective generators, sends m to 3m. It preserves every old parity label.

Thus retention of parity at every stage does not prevent an indefinite sequence of finer compatible durations. This is why the denominator three was chosen: the example does not acquire its failure by destroying the odd/even observation.

## OR4. Full signed section group over the fixed support

Let C_4 be the additive group of residues modulo four, and set

\[
G_D=D\times C_4.
\]

Write its operation multiplicatively as

\[
(d,k)(d',k')=(d+d',k+k'),
\quad \mathbf1=(0,0),\quad J=(0,1),\quad\epsilon=(0,2).
\tag{OR4.1}
\]

Here 0 in the first coordinate is zero duration. It is not τ and is not a new definition of the user's numbered zero. The four finite phases have not been removed: J²=ε and ε²=1. If a pointed monoid is required, adjoin a distinct absorbing symbol \(\bot\) with g\(\bot\)=\(\bot\); it too is distinct from η, τ and every group element.

Define the full signed involution

\[
A_D(d,k)=\bigl(-d,\ 2\chi(d)-k\bigr).
\tag{OR4.2}
\]

The expression 2χ(d) is unambiguous in C_4 because the homomorphism C_2→C_4 sends the nonzero class to 2. Additivity of χ proves that A_D is a group homomorphism. Since χ(−d)=χ(d), applying it twice gives

\[
A_D^2(d,k)=\bigl(d,2\chi(-d)-2\chi(d)+k\bigr)=(d,k).
\tag{OR4.3}
\]

It therefore is an automorphism, not a projection discarding the winding. Multiplication by the reversed section gives

\[
(d,k)A_D(d,k)=(0,2\chi(d))=\epsilon^{\chi(d)}.
\tag{OR4.4}
\]

The actual CC section group in these coordinates is G_Z=Z×C_4, with

\[
A_Z(m,k)=(-m,2m-k).
\]

There is an injective comparison

\[
\iota:G_Z\longrightarrow G_D,
\qquad \iota(m,k)=(mc,k).
\tag{OR4.5}
\]

It preserves the product, J and ε. Since χ(mc)=m modulo two, it satisfies A_D∘ι=ι∘A_Z. In particular T=(1,0) maps to (c,0), and

\[
A_D(\iota(T))=\epsilon\iota(T)^{-1},\qquad
A_D(J)=\epsilon J.
\]

These are the full signed formulas of the source, not an unsigned replacement.

## OR5. Charts, twisted restrictions, and the parityless point survive

Use the same topological space X as OR1. Set

\[
M_+=D_{\geq0}\times C_4,\quad
M_-=D_{\leq0}\times C_4,
\]

with the products inherited from (OR4.1), and with the distinct absorbing symbol if working with pointed monoids. Define restrictions into the generic group by

\[
\rho_+(d,k)=(d,k),\qquad \rho_-(d,k)=(d,-k).
\tag{OR5.1}
\]

In particular J_+ maps to J and J_- maps to J^{-1}=εJ, exactly retaining the twisted imaginary restriction. The local structure on X has M_+ on Ω_+, M_- on Ω_-, and G_D on Ω={η}. On X take the fiber product M_+×_{G_D}M_-; on the empty set take the terminal monoid. These definitions satisfy the sheaf condition: the only cover of an open not containing that open itself which needs checking is {Ω_+,Ω_-} covering X, and its compatible sections are exactly that fiber product. All restrictions are specified by projection or (OR5.1).

The generic group G_D is the group completion of either unpointed chart. For M_+, every d∈D is a difference of two nonnegative durations, and the C_4 coordinate already has inverses. If f:M_+→K is a homomorphism to an abelian group, define its extension at (d,k), with d=a−b and a,b≥0, by

\[
\widetilde f(d,k)=f(a,k)f(b,0)^{-1}.
\]

If a−b=a'−b', then a+b'=a'+b. Applying f to this equality and cancelling proves that the formula is independent of the expression. It preserves products and is the unique extension because every group element has such an expression. The proof for the negative chart uses −a,−b, and its generic identification is composed with the C_4 inversion in ρ_-.

The local involution maps have domains β_+:M_-→M_+ and β_-:M_+→M_-. Their formulas are

\[
\beta_\pm(d,k)=\bigl(-d,2\chi(d)+k\bigr).
\tag{OR5.2}
\]

They square to the identity since 4χ(d)=0 in C_4. Directly,

\[
\rho_+\beta_+(d,k)=A_D\rho_-(d,k),\qquad
\rho_-\beta_-(d,k)=A_D\rho_+(d,k),
\tag{OR5.3}
\]

because −2χ(d)=2χ(d) in C_4. Thus they define an involution of the entire sheaf over the exchange of the two closed points. The topological proof of fixedness and the impossibility of an exchanged parity label at η are unchanged. All of the refining duration data nevertheless remains in the stalk.

The only finite-order elements of G_D are H={0}×C_4: if a positive multiple of (d,k) is the identity, the torsion-free subgroup D of the duration line forces d=0; conversely every (0,k) has finite order. Hence the exact sequence is

\[
0\longrightarrow C_4\longrightarrow G_D
\longrightarrow D\longrightarrow0,
\tag{OR5.4}
\]

and the involution on the quotient is d↦−d. The four-state phase data remains present in G_D and in this sequence. The user's unbounded retained torsion/winding is recorded by D; it is not being equated with the finite-order subgroup H.

The fixed elements in G_D are exactly {1,ε}: the equation d=−d gives d=0, and k=−k in C_4 gives k=0 or 2. The difference between a fixed support and its transforming sections has therefore been calculated, not asserted.

## OR6. The refinement system and its exact obstruction object

At each finite stage use G_r=D_r×C_4 with the restrictions and involution just given. The inclusions G_r→G_{r+1} preserve the products, positive and negative charts, restrictions, four phase elements, parity observations and signed involutions. In the chosen duration coordinates their formula is (m,k)↦(3m,k). It commutes with the full signed involution because 2(3m)−k and 2m−k differ by 4m. Their union is exactly G_D. If T_r denotes the section (c/3^r,0), the map sends T_r to T_{r+1}^3; it preserves the old section but does not preserve its status as a primitive generator. This is the exact change being tested, not a generator-preserving isomorphism of cyclic sources.

Consequently the complete signed source is an explicit direct limit of the finite-stage signed cyclic sources. The support remains η at every stage, old parity labels agree at every stage, no history is deleted, and nevertheless the global positive duration monoid has no indecomposable nonzero element: every d>0 decomposes as

\[
d=\frac d3+\frac{2d}3.
\tag{OR6.1}
\]

This is the object defined by the obstruction. It records the compatible refinements instead of forcing a limiting positive generator into existence. There is no claim that a finite-stage generator ceases to be a duration; it ceases to be primitive when the next stage is included.

## OR7. The endomorphism ring records the arithmetic effect exactly

Calculate End_Ab(D), with addition of endomorphisms defined pointwise on the duration group and multiplication given by composition. Every endomorphism f is determined by f(c). Write f(c)=ac with a∈Z[1/3]. For each r, the equality 3^r(c/3^r)=c implies

\[
3^r f(c/3^r)=ac.
\]

There is exactly one solution in D, since D is torsion-free, and it is ac/3^r. Additivity then gives f(bc/3^r)=abc/3^r. Conversely multiplication by any a∈Z[1/3] gives an endomorphism of D. Thus

\[
\mathbb Z[1/3]\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Ab}}(D),
\qquad a\longmapsto(d\mapsto ad)
\tag{OR7.1}
\]

is a bijection and preserves addition, composition and the identity. It is therefore a ring isomorphism.

The receiving ring is End_Ab(D), the endomorphism ring of the explicitly displayed quotient in (OR5.4). It is not the endomorphism ring of the full signed sheaf with J, ε and χ required to remain fixed. More precisely, multiplication by a=A/3^r satisfies χ(ad)=χ(d) for every d exactly when A is odd: its action on χ is multiplication by A modulo two, and necessity follows by testing d=c. These parity-preserving multipliers are closed under composition but not under pointwise addition, because the sum of two odd numerators after a common odd denominator is even. Retaining the full group, its quotient map and χ allows both kinds of structure to be compared; it does not identify their morphism sets.

This ring has a unique identity, namely the identity map on D, although D has no primitive positive tick. Indeed any identity endomorphism must act as the identity under composition with id_D and hence equal id_D. Thus unique ring identity and unique positive duration generator are different properties, related here by the fully computed receiver rather than by renaming one as the other.

There is also an exact downstream spectrum comparison. We use ordinary integer names only now, after (OR7.1) identifies the receiving ring. Every nonzero ideal I of Z[1/3] intersects Z nontrivially: multiply any nonzero a/3^r∈I by 3^r. Let d be the least positive member of I∩Z. Integer division gives I∩Z=dZ. For any a/3^r∈I, a∈dZ, and conversely all d/3^r lie in I. Hence I=dZ[1/3]. Moreover 3 does not divide d, since otherwise d/3∈I∩Z would be a smaller positive member.

The proper nonzero prime ideals are exactly pZ[1/3] for ordinary primes p other than three. If d is composite, a factorization d=ab with 1<a,b<d disproves primality because neither factor belongs to I but their product does. If d=p is prime and p≠3, reduction modulo p extends uniquely to Z[1/3], since the image of 3 has a multiplicative inverse. Its kernel is pZ[1/3], and its image is the field with p elements, proving primality and residue norm p. The zero ideal is prime because the ring lies in Q and has no zero divisors. These exhaust the possibilities.

Thus the exact resulting scheme is Spec Z[1/3], and its natural map to Spec Z is the open inclusion of the points other than (3). On a basic open determined by a fraction a/3^r, nonvanishing is the same as nonvanishing of a; the section ring is Z[1/3,1/a], as is the corresponding restriction of the usual localization sheaf. This proves the comparison including its structure sheaf.

The removed arithmetic point is the precisely calculated consequence of the indefinite odd refinement. This does not contradict the user's demand to recover every prime; it shows that that demand is substantive, and that fixed parityless support alone has not already enforced it.

## OR8. What the calculation proves about the user's chain

The following pieces have been proved together: the actual CC generic point is unique and fixed; it admits no equivariant exchanged two-state label; a full signed stalk can preserve winding at that point; and compatible odd refinements preserve the source parity formulas at every stage. A complete, locally finite, unskipped timing record can satisfy these particular properties and fail to possess any positive common tick.

Therefore the inference from those properties to the existence of the global primitive quotient is not valid without using further structure. The failure is not at an invented addition on τ, nor at a selected pair of already named primes. It is the explicit lack of a least positive duration in the complete receiver. The constructed object retaining that failure is the signed refinement system (OR4.1)–(OR6.1), and its arithmetic receiving map is (OR7.1).

The already-written CC reconstruction starts with the source's full cyclic chart and so legitimately reconstructs an integer endomorphism ring from that chart. It cannot be cited as having proved that the user's timing process produces the cyclic chart: CC.tex lines 509–511 already put integer exponents into that input. Both statements remain on record with their exact domains.

This derivation does not decide the user's entire intended construction by replacing it with (OR2.1). It disproves only the displayed inference from the features retained here. An intrinsic rule on the original complete history could exclude this receiver; no such rule is silently assumed in this proof. Neither a purity conclusion nor an RH verdict follows from this calculation, and neither is labelled a completed result.
