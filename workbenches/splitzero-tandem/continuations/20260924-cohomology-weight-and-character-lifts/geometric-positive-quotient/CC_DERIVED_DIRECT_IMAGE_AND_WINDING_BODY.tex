\subsection{CDW1. The original coefficient
diagram}\label{cdw1.-the-original-coefficient-diagram}

The exact spaces are \[
S=\{f\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R;\mathbb C):
f(-v)=f(v),\ f(0)=0,\ \int_{\mathbb R}f(v)\,dv=0\},
\quad
V_\pm=S\oplus E_\pm,\quad E_\pm=\mathbb C^2,
\] \[
A=\left\{a\in C^\infty(\mathbb R_{>0}):
p_{N,j}(a)=\sup_{u>0}(u^N+u^{-N})
|(u\partial_u)^ja(u)|<\infty\quad(N,j\ge0)\right\}.
\tag{CDW1.1}
\] Both \(S\) and \(A\) are Fréchet spaces. The two coordinates of each
\(E_\pm\) retain the source labels value at zero and real integral.

With the stated Fourier convention, \[
\Sigma f(u)=2\sum_{k\ge1}f(ku),\qquad
Ra(u)=u^{-1}a(u^{-1}),\qquad
\widehat f(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}f(v)e^{-2\pi ivt}\,dv,
\] \[
r_+(f,c_0,c_1)=\Sigma f,\qquad
r_-(h,d_0,d_1)=R\Sigma h=\Sigma\widehat h.
\tag{CDW1.2}
\] The last identity follows from the full Poisson formula \[
\Sigma\widehat h(u)=u^{-1}\Sigma h(u^{-1})
+u^{-1}h(0)-\int_{\mathbb R}h(v)\,dv
\tag{CDW1.3}
\] and the two source conditions. No endpoint summand is identified with
the other chart's summand.

The sheaf \(\Omega\) on \(X\) is the diagram \[
V_+\xrightarrow{r_+}A\xleftarrow{r_-}V_-.
\tag{CDW1.4}
\] Its global sections are the fibre product. All morphisms of diagrams
below consist of three continuous linear maps commuting with these two
arrows. The pullback \(\mathscr F=\pi^{-1}\Omega\) has the concrete
presentation \[
\mathscr F=
\underline A_Y\times_{i_*(A\oplus A)}
i_*(V_+\oplus V_-),
\quad
i:\{0,\infty\}\hookrightarrow Y,
\tag{CDW1.5}
\] with difference map
\((a,v_+,v_-)\mapsto(a|_0-r_+v_+,a|_\infty-r_-v_-)\). CSP2 proves this
identification by an explicit adjunction map and identities on every
stalk.

The actual image \[
J=\Sigma(S)=r_+(V_+)=r_-(V_-)
\tag{CDW1.6}
\] is closed, and \(\Sigma:S\to J\) is a topological isomorphism by
SSI1--SSI7. In particular \(Q=A/J\) is Fréchet. Closedness is available
here, but the derived splitting below does not use division by
\(\Sigma\) or a choice of a continuous section \(Q\to A\).

\subsection{CDW2. A single resolution on the entire
sphere}\label{cdw2.-a-single-resolution-on-the-entire-sphere}

Let \(\mathscr E_A^q\) be the sheaf of smooth \(A\)-valued differential
\(q\)-forms on the underlying real two-dimensional manifold \(Y\), with
complex coefficients. Smoothness means that all coordinate derivatives
exist in the Fréchet space \(A\); sections have the compact-set
derivative seminorms induced by \(p_{N,j}\).

The radial Poincaré operator on a coordinate ball, \[
(K\omega)_x=\int_0^1 t^{q-1}\iota_x\omega_{tx}\,dt,
\tag{CDW2.1}
\] exists because \(A\) is complete. For every continuous seminorm
\(p\), \[
p\left(\int_a^b f(t)\,dt\right)\le\int_a^b p(f(t))\,dt.
\tag{CDW2.2}
\] Differentiation under the integral on compact sets gives the same
bound for derivatives. The usual differentiation of the radial pullback,
integrated from zero to one, proves \(dK+Kd=1\) in positive degree, and
\(Kd=1-\mathrm{ev}_0\) in degree zero. Thus the coefficient-valued
Poincaré lemma holds with continuous local primitives. Partitions of
unity multiply these forms, making the sheaves fine. This is also the
resolution used in CSP3.

Write \(\mathrm{ev}:\mathscr E_A^0\to i_*(A\oplus A)\) for evaluation at
the two poles. Define the single complex of sheaves on \(Y\) \[
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr K^0&=\mathscr E_A^0\oplus i_*(V_+\oplus V_-),\\
\mathscr K^1&=\mathscr E_A^1\oplus i_*(A\oplus A),\\
\mathscr K^2&=\mathscr E_A^2,
\end{aligned}
\] \[
d^0(f,v)=(df,\mathrm{ev}(f)-rv),\qquad
d^1(\omega,a)=d\omega.
\tag{CDW2.3}
\] The coordinates at a pole absent from an open are omitted. One has
\(d^1d^0=d^2f=0\). The augmentation \(\mathscr F\to\mathscr K^0\) sends
the locally constant function and its pole coordinates in (CDW1.5) to
those same coordinates.

We verify exactness on every stalk. In degree zero, \(df=0\) says that
\(f\) is locally constant; the second equation is exactly the
fibre-product condition. Away from the poles the positive-degree
assertion is the Poincaré lemma. At a pole \(p\), a degree-one cocycle
is a closed one-form \(\omega\) and an arbitrary \(a\in A\). Choose a
local primitive \(g\) of \(\omega\), and replace it by \(f=g+a-g(p)\).
Then \(df=\omega\) and \(f(p)=a\), so \[
d^0(f,0)=(\omega,a).
\tag{CDW2.4}
\] Every degree-two germ has a one-form primitive by (CDW2.1). This
proves exactness of \[
0\to\mathscr F\to\mathscr K^0\to\mathscr K^1\to\mathscr K^2\to0.
\tag{CDW2.5}
\] The zero-pole adjustment in (CDW2.4) is an operation on \(A\), not on
the supporting point \(\tau\).

Each \(\mathscr K^q\) is acyclic on every open subset of \(Y\). The form
part is fine on this paracompact manifold and on its open subsets. The
skyscraper summands are flabby: every restriction is a projection onto
the pole coordinates still present and is surjective. For a direct image
to the finite space \(X\), a stalk of \(R^q\pi_*\mathscr K^r\) is
\(H^q(\pi^{-1}U_x,\mathscr K^r)\), because \(x\) has its smallest open
\(U_x\). Those groups vanish for \(q>0\). Consequently \[
\boxed{\mathscr C^\bullet:=\pi_*\mathscr K^\bullet
\quad\text{represents }R\pi_*\mathscr F.}
\tag{CDW2.6}
\] This is one compatible resolution on all three preimage opens, not
separate complexes whose gluing maps have been inferred from cohomology.

\subsection{CDW3. The actual diagram of resolution
sections}\label{cdw3.-the-actual-diagram-of-resolution-sections}

On either pole chart \(Y_p\simeq\mathbb C\), the complex
\(\mathscr C(U_p)\) is \[
\bigl[C^\infty(Y_p,A)\oplus V_p\bigr]
\longrightarrow
\bigl[\mathscr E_A^1(Y_p)\oplus A\bigr]
\longrightarrow\mathscr E_A^2(Y_p),
\tag{CDW3.1}
\] with exactly (CDW2.3). The inclusion \[
\iota_p:V_p[0]\longrightarrow\mathscr C(U_p),\qquad
v\longmapsto(\operatorname{constant}(r_pv),v)
\tag{CDW3.2}
\] is a continuous quasi-isomorphism. Indeed global radial primitives on
the plane chart repeat (CDW2.4); the projection of a degree-zero cocycle
onto \(v\) is its continuous inverse on cohomology. Every
positive-degree cocycle has the continuous primitive already written
down.

On \(Y^\circ\), the skyscrapers vanish, so \[
\mathscr C(U)=\mathscr E_A^\bullet(\mathbb C^\times).
\tag{CDW3.3}
\] Keep the explicit real one-form \[
\vartheta=\frac{d\arg z}{2\pi}
=\frac1{4\pi i}\left(\frac{dz}{z}-\frac{d\overline z}{\overline z}\right).
\tag{CDW3.4}
\] It is globally defined on \(\mathbb C^\times\), closed, and has
integral one on the positive unit circle. It is not defined at either
pole.

For completeness, in \(z=e^{t+i\theta}\), write a closed form as
\(\omega=P(t,\theta)dt+Q(t,\theta)d\theta\). The closure equation is
\(\partial_\theta P=\partial_tQ\). Its positive-circle period is \[
a=\int_0^{2\pi}Q(0,\theta)d\theta.
\] After subtracting \(a\vartheta\), the explicit periodic primitive is
\[
g(t,\theta)=\int_0^tP(x,0)dx+
\int_0^\theta\left(Q(t,\phi)-a/(2\pi)\right)d\phi.
\tag{CDW3.5}
\] Its two derivatives are \(P\) and \(Q-a/(2\pi)\). The period is
independent of \(t\), so \(g(t,2\pi)=g(t,0)\). Every two-form
\(G(t,\theta)dt\wedge d\theta\) has primitive
\((\int_0^tG(x,\theta)dx)d\theta\). These formulas prove the continuous
quasi-isomorphism \[
A[0]\oplus A[-1]\longrightarrow\mathscr C(U),
\quad a\text{ in degree0}\mapsto\operatorname{constant}(a),\quad
a\text{ in degree1}\mapsto a\vartheta.
\tag{CDW3.6}
\] In particular, the periods give a continuous inverse on \(H^1\), and
exact forms are its closed kernel inside the closed one-forms. The two
cohomology spaces have precisely the original Fréchet topology of \(A\).
There is no passage from a completion or an algebraic direct sum of
spectral blocks in (CDW3.6).

The restrictions from (CDW3.1) to (CDW3.3) restrict the
differential-form component and forget the pole coordinate. On the
included constants (CDW3.2) these restrictions are exactly \(r_p\), in
degree zero, with no degree-one component.

\subsection{CDW4. The explicit sheaf-complex
splitting}\label{cdw4.-the-explicit-sheaf-complex-splitting}

Let \(j_X:\{\eta\}\hookrightarrow X\). To retain the winding
orientation, write \(\mathbb C[\vartheta]\) for the one-dimensional
vector space with basis the cohomology class of (CDW3.4), and define \[
\mathscr W=j_{X!}\bigl(A\otimes_{\mathbb C}\mathbb C[\vartheta]\bigr).
\tag{CDW4.1}
\] The tensor factor is one-dimensional, so this is just a labelled copy
of the same Fréchet space \(A\) on \(\{\eta\}\); no choice of an
infinite tensor completion is involved. Its chart sections on \(U_+\)
and \(U_-\) are zero. The label records the precise period orientation
and its transformation law.

Define the split complex \[
\mathscr P^\bullet=\Omega[0]\oplus\mathscr W[-1].
\tag{CDW4.2}
\] Thus its degree-zero diagram is (CDW1.4), its degree-one diagram is
\(0\to A[\vartheta]\leftarrow0\), and its differential is zero. The
restrictions out of the degree-zero charts are \(r_\pm\).

Define \[
\Phi:\mathscr P^\bullet\longrightarrow\mathscr C^\bullet
\tag{CDW4.3}
\] as follows: \[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{open}&\Phi^0&\Phi^1\\ \hline
U_+&v_+\mapsto(\operatorname{constant}(r_+v_+),v_+)&0\\
U_-&v_-\mapsto(\operatorname{constant}(r_-v_-),v_-)&0\\
U&a\mapsto\operatorname{constant}(a)&
a[\vartheta]\mapsto a\vartheta.
\end{array}
\tag{CDW4.4}
\] The degree-zero images are cocycles by (CDW2.3); the degree-one form
is closed. Restrictions commute exactly because the chart constants
restrict to the same \(r_\pm v_\pm\). In degree one both chart domains
are zero, so there is no condition requiring \(\vartheta\) to extend
through a pole.

This last point is part of the sheaf construction: a map out of
\(j_{X!}A[\vartheta]\) may have a nonzero value on the open \(\{\eta\}\)
and zero domain on the pole charts. It does not purport to define a
global one-form on the sphere.

A complex of sheaves on \(X\) is a diagram of complexes on its three
smallest opens. At \(x_\pm\), (CDW4.4) is the chart quasi-isomorphism
(CDW3.2); at \(\eta\), it is (CDW3.6). Therefore \(\Phi\) is a stalkwise
quasi-isomorphism, and \[
\boxed{
\Omega[0]\oplus\mathscr W[-1]=\mathscr P^\bullet
\xrightarrow[\ \simeq\ ]{\ \Phi\ }
\mathscr C^\bullet\simeq R\pi_*\mathscr F.
}
\tag{CDW4.5}
\] Here \(\mathscr C^\bullet\) is the specified representative of
\(R\pi_*\mathscr F\), and the actual comparison arrow is
\(\Phi:\mathscr P^\bullet\to\mathscr C^\bullet\). This invertible
comparison does not require a strict chain-level projection from
arbitrary forms to both chart stalks.

Every map is continuous. For a compact \(K\subset\mathbb C^\times\), a
derivative order \(r\), and a coefficient seminorm \(p\), \[
\|a\vartheta\|_{K,r,p}\le C_{K,r}p(a).
\tag{CDW4.6}
\] The constants-inclusion bound is immediate, and chart continuity uses
the known continuous maps \(r_\pm\). The inverses on chart and overlap
cohomology were explicitly checked in CDW3. Thus this is a
quasi-isomorphism with continuous maps and homeomorphisms on the stalk
cohomology spaces. It is stronger than an abstract vector-space
splitting inferred from hereditary properties of the underlying diagram.

\subsection{CDW5. The geometric and arithmetic actions on the
resolution}\label{cdw5.-the-geometric-and-arithmetic-actions-on-the-resolution}

The coefficient-only action for \(a>0\) is \[
T_ab(u)=b(u/a),
\] \[
\rho_+(a)(f,c_0,c_1)=(f(\cdot/a),c_0,a c_1),\qquad
\rho_-(a)(h,d_0,d_1)=(a h(a\,\cdot),a d_0,d_1).
\tag{CDW5.1}
\] CS8 gives \(r_\pm\rho_\pm(a)=T_ar_\pm\). Apply \(T_a\) to every
coefficient of a differential form and to the two skyscraper
\(A\)-coordinates, and apply \(\rho_\pm(a)\) to the pole
\(V_\pm\)-coordinates. Evaluation and differentiation commute with these
continuous linear maps, so this is a strict action on
\(\mathscr K^\bullet\) and \(\mathscr C^\bullet\). It acts by \(T_a\) on
the \(A\)-coefficient of \(\mathscr W\). The map \(\Phi\) strictly
intertwines it.

For a recovered positive integer \(n\), pullback by \(b_n(z)=z^n\) is an
additional, geometric chain map on \(\mathscr C^\bullet\). It fixes the
poles, acts identically on their \(V_\pm\) and extra \(A\) coordinates,
and pulls back the differential forms. Evaluation at a fixed pole
commutes with pullback, so (CDW2.3) commutes with this operation even at
a ramified pole. Directly from (CDW3.4), \[
b_n^*\vartheta=n\vartheta.
\tag{CDW5.2}
\] Thus the geometric action on \(\mathscr P\) is the identity on
\(\Omega\), and \(n\,\mathrm{id}\) on \(\mathscr W\). The two types of
action commute because one changes the geometric argument and the other
acts on the coefficient.

Their combined operation \(\mathsf B_n\) has \[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
&V_+&V_-&A\text{ in }\Omega\\ \hline
\mathsf B_n&\rho_+(n)&\rho_-(n)&T_n
\end{array},
\qquad
\mathsf B_n(a[\vartheta])=nT_na[\vartheta].
\tag{CDW5.3}
\] The equality \[
T_n(a)\,b_n^*\vartheta=nT_na\,\vartheta
\] proves strict equivariance of (CDW4.3), not merely equivariance of
its cohomology.

The coefficient estimates are explicit. For \(a>0\), \[
p_{N,j}(T_ab)\le\max(a^N,a^{-N})\,p_{N,j}(b).
\tag{CDW5.4}
\] Pullback of smooth forms is continuous for compact derivative
seminorms: on a fixed compact set the derivatives of \(b_n\) up to a
prescribed order are bounded, and the chain rule bounds the result by
finitely many seminorms on its compact image. The same statement holds
in the \(w=1/z\) chart. No unbounded inverse-image set enters this
estimate.

These are actual receiving-space covers for every \(n\ge1\). They are
not asserted to extend the full signed CC structure to every even index.
Composition \(b_mb_n=b_{mn}\), together with (CDW5.1), gives
\(\mathsf B_m\mathsf B_n=\mathsf B_{mn}\). The cohomological
positive-rational and strongly continuous real extensions are those
proved in CSP8; a nonintegral power is not being used as a globally
defined holomorphic map.

\subsection{CDW6. Both mirrors on the complete derived
object}\label{cdw6.-both-mirrors-on-the-complete-derived-object}

Retain the two different geometric maps \[
w(z)=-1/z,\qquad
\alpha(z)=-1/\overline z.
\tag{CDW6.1}
\] They have the same swap of closed points on \(X\), but \(w\) is
holomorphic and \(\alpha\) is the antiholomorphic real structure from
the later CC source.

Use the coefficient morphism already constructed in CS8: exchange the
two pole spaces by identity on their correspondingly labelled source
coordinates and act by \(R\) on all \(A\)-coordinates. Since
\(r_-=Rr_+\), this gives a map over either base swap. On the resolution,
pull back the forms by the specified geometric map and apply \(R\) to
their coefficients; on the extra skyscraper coordinates use \(R\) and
the pole swap. These operations commute with (CDW2.3).

The exact form identities are \[
w^*\vartheta=-\vartheta,\qquad
\alpha^*\vartheta=+\vartheta.
\tag{CDW6.2}
\] They follow either from (CDW3.4) or from the angle maps
\(\theta\mapsto\pi-\theta\) and \(\theta\mapsto\pi+\theta\). Hence on
the split object \[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
&\Omega&\mathscr W\\ \hline
w&\text{chart swap; overlap }R&-R\\
\alpha&\text{chart swap; overlap }R&+R.
\end{array}
\tag{CDW6.3}
\] All these maps square to the identity and are strictly intertwined by
\(\Phi\). The coefficient \(R\) is complex linear; if complex
conjugation on coefficients is included, its separate conjugate-linear
action must also be applied. The real form \(\vartheta\) itself is
unchanged by conjugating its scalar coefficients, so that additional
convention has no hidden angular sign.

The maps \(w\) and \(\alpha\) commute, with composite
\(z\mapsto\overline z\); with the two \(R\)-factors their composite on
\(\mathscr W\) is \(-\mathrm{id}\). This is the action of complex
conjugation on the positive angular class. On \(\Omega\) the two swaps
and two \(R\)-factors compose to the identity.

For precision about actual geometric composition, \[
w b_n(z)=-z^{-n},\qquad b_n w(z)=(-1)^n z^{-n},
\] \[
\alpha b_n(z)=-\overline z^{-n},\qquad
b_n\alpha(z)=(-1)^n\overline z^{-n}.
\tag{CDW6.4}
\] Thus these maps commute with \(b_n\) for odd \(n\), and for even
\(n\) differ by \(z\mapsto-z\). That rotation preserves constants and
\(\vartheta\) and therefore acts trivially on the split model. It is not
asserted to be the identity on every smooth form in the resolution. The
strict map \(\Phi\) respects the actual transformations and these
identities with their rotations retained.

\subsection{CDW7. The Postnikov class and what has actually
vanished}\label{cdw7.-the-postnikov-class-and-what-has-actually-vanished}

Write \(\mathscr R=R\pi_*\mathscr F\). The cohomology sheaves of
(CDW4.3) are identified as \[
\mathcal H^0(\mathscr R)=\Omega,\qquad
\mathcal H^1(\mathscr R)=\mathscr W,\qquad
\mathcal H^{q\ne0,1}(\mathscr R)=0.
\tag{CDW7.1}
\] The comparison on \(\mathcal H^0\) is the original restriction
diagram; on \(\mathcal H^1\) it is the positive-period identification.
Its Postnikov triangle is \[
\Omega\longrightarrow\mathscr R\longrightarrow
\mathscr W[-1]\xrightarrow{\ \kappa\ }\Omega[1].
\tag{CDW7.2}
\] The class \(\kappa\) is the particular extension datum that
cohomology sheaves alone would leave unspecified.

Here it is calculated: \[
\boxed{\kappa=0.}
\tag{CDW7.3}
\] Indeed (CDW4.3) is a continuous strict equivariant quasi-isomorphism
from the direct sum, inducing the two specified cohomology
identifications. The two inclusions followed by \(\Phi\) give maps
\(j_0:\Omega\to\mathscr R\) and \(j_1:\mathscr W[-1]\to\mathscr R\). The
projections are represented by the explicit roofs \[
\mathscr C^\bullet
\xleftarrow[\simeq]{\,\Phi\,}
\mathscr P^\bullet
\xrightarrow{\operatorname{pr}_0}\Omega,
\qquad
\mathscr C^\bullet
\xleftarrow[\simeq]{\,\Phi\,}
\mathscr P^\bullet
\xrightarrow{\operatorname{pr}_1}\mathscr W[-1].
\tag{CDW7.4}
\] Every arrow in these roofs is a displayed continuous map. Inverting
the specified quasi-isomorphism is the definition of these derived
morphisms, not an assumed inverse of an analytic quotient. Their
compositions satisfy \[
r_i j_i=1,\qquad r_0j_1=r_1j_0=0,\qquad
j_0r_0+j_1r_1=1.
\tag{CDW7.5}
\] This splits (CDW7.2), proving (CDW7.3) with all the arithmetic and
mirror actions retained.

One may express this in the ordinary equivariant derived category of
vector-space sheaves, or in the localization of the displayed continuous
complexes at stalkwise quasi-isomorphisms inducing homeomorphisms on
cohomology. The same actual comparison \(\Phi\) exists before either
localization, so neither assertion requires projectivity in a category
of Fréchet spaces.

There is an additional uniqueness statement after forgetting topology
and equivariance. For an arbitrary vector-space sheaf \(G\) on \(X\), \[
\operatorname{Hom}_X(\mathscr W,G)
=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb C}
\bigl(A\otimes\mathbb C[\vartheta],G_\eta\bigr).
\tag{CDW7.6}
\] This follows directly from the diagram: the two chart maps from zero
impose no restriction on its central map. Stalk evaluation is exact, and
every complex vector space is projective, so the right-hand functor is
exact. Therefore \(\mathscr W\) is projective in that ordinary category
and \[
\operatorname{Ext}^k_X(\mathscr W,\Omega)=0\quad(k>0).
\tag{CDW7.7}
\] The Postnikov class lies in its degree-two group; the difference
between splittings inducing the fixed cohomology identifications lies in
its degree-one group. Both vanish. This proves underlying derived
uniqueness. It does not claim projectivity or uniqueness in a
topological or equivariant exact category. Existence there has already
been proved by the stronger explicit map, without using (CDW7.7).

The vanishing in (CDW7.3) concerns this actual derived direct image and
its winding extension. It is not an identification of every lifting
obstruction in Deligne's different geometric setting with this class.

\subsection{CDW8. Naturality and the complete original source
sequence}\label{cdw8.-naturality-and-the-complete-original-source-sequence}

Let \((V_\pm,A,r_\pm)\to(V'_\pm,A',r'_\pm)\) be any continuous
coefficient-diagram morphism \((f_+,f_-,g)\), so
\(gr_\pm=r'_\pm f_\pm\). Its map on the resolution is \(g\) applied to
each form coefficient and skyscraper \(A\)-coordinate, and \(f_\pm\) on
the pole coordinates. Differentiation and evaluation commute with these
maps. Moreover \[
g(\operatorname{constant}(a))=\operatorname{constant}(ga),
\qquad g(a\vartheta)=(ga)\vartheta.
\tag{CDW8.1}
\] Thus the square of sheaf complexes \[
\begin{array}{ccc}
\Omega\oplus j_{X!}A[\vartheta][-1]&\xrightarrow{\Phi}&\pi_*\mathscr K_\Omega\\
\downarrow&&\downarrow\\
\Omega'\oplus j_{X!}A'[\vartheta][-1]&\xrightarrow{\Phi'}&\pi_*\mathscr K_{\Omega'}
\end{array}
\tag{CDW8.2}
\] commutes strictly. This is naturality on the actual complexes and
restrictions.

Apply it to the closed source \(J\subset A\). Let \(\Omega_J\) have the
same pole spaces \(V_\pm\), overlap \(J\), and restrictions \(r_\pm\)
with that codomain. Let \(\Omega_Q=j_{X!}Q\), with zero pole spaces and
overlap \(Q=A/J\). Then \[
0\to\Omega_J\to\Omega\to\Omega_Q\to0
\tag{CDW8.3}
\] is an exact sequence of vector-space sheaves with the specified
continuous maps. On the pole stalks its maps are
\(V_\pm\xrightarrow{1}V_\pm\to0\); on the generic stalk it is the actual
strict sequence \(0\to J\to A\to Q\to0\). Inverse image is exact, as can
be checked on stalks, and gives precisely \[
0\to\mathscr F_J\to\mathscr F\to
j_{Y!}\underline Q_{Y^\circ}\to0.
\tag{CDW8.4}
\] Its last sheaf identification follows from zero pole stalks and
constant \(Q\) on the interior with zero pole generization; it is also

\subsection{CDW9. The exact comparison with positive sphere
orientation}\label{cdw9.-the-exact-comparison-with-positive-sphere-orientation}

On the ordered cover \((U_+,U_-)\), use the Čech differential
\(r_+-r_-\). Applying its derived global-section complex to (CDW4.2)
gives the total model \[
V_+\oplus V_-\xrightarrow{\ r_+-r_-\ }A
\quad\text{in total degrees }0,1,
\qquad
A[\vartheta]\quad\text{in total degree }2.
\tag{CDW9.1}
\] There is no map into or out of that last coefficient in this model.
It is the group \(H^1(X,\mathscr W)\), with its positive angular
generator.

Its comparison with the positive-integration \(H^2(Y,\mathscr F)=A\)
coordinate of CSP has a minus sign. To compute it, write \(r=|z|\) and
define smooth forms on the two respective charts \[
\beta_+=\frac{r^2}{1+r^2}\vartheta,\qquad
\beta_-=-\frac1{1+r^2}\vartheta.
\tag{CDW9.2}
\] The first extends smoothly through \(z=0\); the second extends
smoothly through \(w=1/z=0\). Direct differentiation gives \[
d\beta_+=d\beta_-=\omega_Y
=\frac1\pi\frac{dx\wedge dy}{(1+|z|^2)^2},
\qquad
\beta_+-\beta_-=\vartheta,\qquad
\int_Y\omega_Y=1.
\tag{CDW9.3}
\] The Mayer--Vietoris fibre-cone differential is
\(d(v,b)=(dv,\mathrm{res}(v)-db)\). Therefore \[
d\bigl((\beta_+,\beta_-),0\bigr)
=\bigl((\omega_Y,\omega_Y),\vartheta\bigr).
\tag{CDW9.4}

\(((\omega_Y,\omega_Y),0)\) is consequently cohomologous to
\((0,-\vartheta)\). Equivalently, \[
\boxed{
H^1(X,\mathscr W)\ni a[\vartheta]
\longmapsto -a\in H^2(Y,\mathscr F)
\text{ measured by positive complex integration}.}
\tag{CDW9.5}
\] The projection \(\mathscr F\to\underline A_Y\) is an isomorphism on
degree-two cohomology, so this scalar computation fixes the same sign
for the original coefficient sheaf, not only for constant coefficients.

The two mirror actions now recover the CSP signs with no lost factor. On
\(H^1(X,\Omega)\) the chart swap gives the Čech sign, so both act as
\(-R\). On \(H^1(X,\mathscr W)\), the extra chart-order minus multiplies

Covers retain \(nT_n\).

the derived splitting, the costalk calculation and the global
