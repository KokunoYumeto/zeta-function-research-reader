\tightlist
\item
  Every bounded subset of \(A\), and every bounded subset of \(Q\), has

\item

\item

\end{enumerate}

No nuclearity theorem or general bounded-lifting assertion is assumed.
The compactness and lifting arguments are constructed below. In
particular the statement is not deduced merely from the fact that a map
of Fréchet spaces is onto.

\subsection{SDT1. The original weighted topology and its quotient
seminorms}\label{sdt1.-the-original-weighted-topology-and-its-quotient-seminorms}

Use the analytic change of variable \(x=\log u\) only on \(A\), writing
\[
g(x)=a(e^x),\qquad
w_n(x)=e^{nx}+e^{-nx},\qquad
P_n(a)=\max_{0\le j\le n}\sup_{x\in\mathbb R}
w_n(x)|g^{(j)}(x)|.
\tag{SDT1.1}
\] Thus \(P_n=\max_{j\le n}p_{n,j}\) is expressed in the original
seminorms, including both summands of the weight. No summand has been
removed. The sequence \(P_n\) is increasing: \(w_n(x)=2\cosh(nx)\) is
increasing with \(n\ge0\), and the derivative range increases. For every
\(N,j\), choosing \(n\ge\max(N,j)\) gives \[
p_{N,j}\le P_n.
\tag{SDT1.2}
\] Conversely every \(P_n\) is a finite maximum of defining seminorms.
Hence this is exactly the original topology. Each \(P_n\) is a norm on
\(A\).

For completeness, a sequence Cauchy in every \(P_n\) is Cauchy with
every derivative uniformly on every compact interval in \(x\). The
corresponding uniform limits have the derivative compatibility obtained
by integrating each first derivative on a compact interval. Inducting
over derivatives gives a smooth limit \(g\). For every \(n\), the
weighted derivatives are uniform Cauchy functions on the whole real
axis, so their uniform limits are precisely \(w_n g^{(j)}\). Set
\(a(u)=g(\log u)\). Then \(P_n(a-a_k)\to0\) and \(P_n(a)<\infty\). This
proves that \(A\) is complete. The metric \[
d_A(a,b)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty 2^{-n-1}
\min\{1,P_n(a-b)\}
\tag{SDT1.3}
\] generates its topology. Thus \(A\) is a Hausdorff Fréchet space.

Define actual quotient seminorms \[
\overline P_n(q(a))=\inf_{j\in J}P_n(a+j).
\tag{SDT1.4}
\] They are well defined, increasing, and subadditive, and they satisfy
\[
q\{P_n<\varepsilon\}=\{\overline P_n<\varepsilon\}.
\tag{SDT1.5}
\] The equality uses the strict inequality: an infimum below
\(\varepsilon\) supplies a representative with \(P_n<\varepsilon\).
These sets generate exactly the quotient topology. Images of open sets
under \(q\) are open because their inverse images are the open sets
\(U+J\).

If all \(\overline P_n(y)\) vanish, a representative \(a\) of \(y\) can
be approximated by elements of \(J\) in every neighborhood defined by
some \(P_n\). Thus \(a\in\overline J=J\) and \(y=0\). The quotient is
therefore Hausdorff.

We also give its completeness argument. Let \((y_\ell)\) be Cauchy for
every \(\overline P_n\). Choose a subsequence \((y_{\ell_k})_{k\ge1}\)
so that \[
\overline P_k(y_{\ell_{k+1}}-y_{\ell_k})<2^{-k-1}.
\] Choose \(b_k\in A\) with \[
q(b_k)=y_{\ell_{k+1}}-y_{\ell_k},\qquad P_k(b_k)<2^{-k}.
\tag{SDT1.6}
\] For every fixed \(m\), the tail from \(k\ge m\) is absolutely
summable in \(P_m\), since \(P_m\le P_k\). The series
\(\sum_{k\ge1}b_k\) therefore converges in \(A\). Adding any
representative of \(y_{\ell_1}\), then applying \(q\), gives a limit of
the chosen subsequence. The original Cauchy sequence has that same
limit. The metric obtained from (SDT1.3) by replacing \(P_n\) by
\(\overline P_n\) is complete, so \(Q\) is Fréchet.

This proof uses the quotient's actual closed kernel, not a completion in
which nonzero quotient classes are discarded.

\subsection{SDT2. A finite-net estimate with the two original weights
retained}\label{sdt2.-a-finite-net-estimate-with-the-two-original-weights-retained}

Fix \(n\ge0\) and \(M>0\). Consider \[
\mathcal U_{n+1,M}=\{a\in A:P_{n+1}(a)\le M\}.
\tag{SDT2.1}
\] This set need not be bounded in the full Fréchet topology. We prove
the exact assertion needed: it is totally bounded for the single norm
\(P_n\).

For all \(x\in\mathbb R\), \[
\frac{w_n(x)}{w_{n+1}(x)}
=\frac{e^{nx}+e^{-nx}}{e^{(n+1)x}+e^{-(n+1)x}}
\le 2e^{-|x|}.
\tag{SDT2.2}
\] Indeed the numerator is at most \(2e^{n|x|}\), while the denominator
is at least \(e^{(n+1)|x|}\). If \(a,b\in\mathcal U_{n+1,M}\), then for
\(0\le j\le n\), \[
\sup_{|x|>T}w_n(x)|g_a^{(j)}(x)-g_b^{(j)}(x)|
\le 4M e^{-T}.
\tag{SDT2.3}
\] For a prescribed \(\varepsilon>0\), choose \(T>0\) with
\(4Me^{-T}<\varepsilon/2\).

On \([-T,T]\), each \(g_a^{(j)}\), \(j\le n+1\), has absolute value at
most \(M/2\), because \(w_{n+1}\ge2\). The functions \(g_a^{(j)}\),
\(j\le n\), are therefore Lipschitz with constant at most \(M/2\). Put
\(W=w_n(T)\). Choose a finite mesh of \([-T,T]\) with every point at
distance at most \(\delta\) from a mesh point, where \[
0<\delta<\frac{\varepsilon}{4MW}.
\tag{SDT2.4}
\] At every mesh point, quantize each of the finitely many complex
numbers \(g_a^{(j)}\), \(j\le n\), to a finite set with error at most
\(\eta\), where \[
0<\eta<\frac{\varepsilon}{8W}.
\tag{SDT2.5}
\] The bound \(M/2\) makes that quantization finite. There are finitely
many resulting joint codes. For each occupied code choose one function
\(b\) in the set with that code. If \(a\) has the same code, the two
Lipschitz bounds and the two quantization errors give \[
\sup_{|x|\le T}w_n(x)|g_a^{(j)}(x)-g_b^{(j)}(x)|
\le W(M\delta+2\eta)<\varepsilon/2
\quad(0\le j\le n).
\tag{SDT2.6}
\] Combining (SDT2.3) and (SDT2.6) proves \(P_n(a-b)<\varepsilon\). The
finitely many selected \(b\)'s form a finite \(P_n\)-net. For \(M=0\)
the set is the singleton zero, so the same conclusion holds.

Every bounded \(B\subset A\) has a finite bound for \(P_{n+1}\), for
each \(n\). It is therefore totally bounded for every \(P_n\). To see
that this is total boundedness for \(d_A\), first make the tail
\(\sum_{n>N}2^{-n-1}\) small, and then use a finite sufficiently small
\(P_N\)-net to control all terms with \(n\le N\). Since \(A\) is
complete, \(\overline B\) is compact.

The final metric fact can be verified directly: a totally bounded metric
space permits extraction of a Cauchy subsequence from every sequence by
successive finite covers of radii tending to zero; completeness supplies
its limit. Thus its closed completion is compact. No additional locally
convex compactness theorem is needed.

\subsection{SDT3. Bounded quotient sets have compact
closure}\label{sdt3.-bounded-quotient-sets-have-compact-closure}

Let \(B\subset Q\) be bounded in the actual quotient topology. For each
\(n\), put \[
M_n=\sup_{y\in B}\overline P_{n+1}(y)<\infty.
\tag{SDT3.1}
\] For each \(y\in B\) choose a representative \(a_y\in A\) with \[
q(a_y)=y,\qquad P_{n+1}(a_y)<M_n+1.
\tag{SDT3.2}
\] These choices are made separately for the present index \(n\); no
assertion that they form a single Fréchet-bounded set is used here.

By SDT2, the set of these representatives admits a finite \(P_n\)-net.
Applying \(q\) and using \(\overline P_n(q(a))\le P_n(a)\) gives a
finite \(\overline P_n\)-net for \(B\). Thus \(B\) is totally bounded in
every quotient seminorm and hence in the complete quotient metric of
SDT1. Its closure is compact: \[
\boxed{B\subset Q\ \text{bounded}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\overline B^{\,Q}\ \text{compact}.}
\tag{SDT3.3}
\] The same statement for \(A\) was proved in SDT2. In the usual
terminology these Fréchet spaces are Montel: they are barrelled, and
their closed bounded sets are compact. The barrelled assertion follows
from the Baire argument supplied in SDT6. No quotient-stability
assertion about general Montel spaces is required here.

Closed subspaces of \(A\), including \(J\), have the same
bounded-compact property by intersection with their closed ambient
subspace. Finite products of such spaces retain the property, because
projections of a bounded set have compact closures and its closure is a
closed subset of the product of those compact sets. In particular the
actual graph coefficient \(Z=J\oplus E_+\oplus E_-\) also has this
property.

\subsection{SDT4. Constructive compact lifting through the actual
quotient}\label{sdt4.-constructive-compact-lifting-through-the-actual-quotient}

We prove a compact lifting statement for this quotient directly. The
argument works for a quotient of any Fréchet space by a closed subspace,
but its hypotheses and construction will remain explicit.

For \(n\ge1\), define \[
U_n=\{a\in A:P_n(a)<2^{-n}\},\qquad
W_n=\{y\in Q:\overline P_n(y)<2^{-n-1}\},
\] \[
C_n=\{y\in Q:\overline P_n(y)\le2^{-n-1}\}.
\tag{SDT4.1}
\] Then \(C_n\) is closed, \(W_n\) is an open neighborhood of zero, \[
\overline{W_n}\subset C_n\subset q(U_n).
\tag{SDT4.2}
\] The last inclusion follows from the strict gap \(2^{-n-1}<2^{-n}\)
and (SDT1.5).

Let \(K\subset Q\) be compact and nonempty. Choose
\(y_{0,1},\ldots,y_{0,r_0}\in K\) such that the translates
\(y_{0,i}+W_1\) cover \(K\). Choose arbitrary lifts \(a_{0,i}\in A\)
with \(q(a_{0,i})=y_{0,i}\), and put \[
F_0=\{a_{0,1},\ldots,a_{0,r_0}\},\qquad
K_1=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r_0}\bigl((K-y_{0,i})\cap C_1\bigr).
\tag{SDT4.3}
\] Each intersection is a closed subset of a compact translate of \(K\).
Hence \(K_1\) is compact and lies in \(C_1\subset q(U_1)\). Every
\(y\in K\) has a representation \(y=q(a_0)+r_1\) with \(a_0\in F_0\) and
\(r_1\in K_1\).

Inductively suppose the compact set \(K_n\subset C_n\) is constructed.
Choose finitely many centers \(y_{n,i}\in K_n\) whose translates
\(y_{n,i}+W_{n+1}\) cover \(K_n\). Since \(K_n\subset q(U_n)\), choose
lifts \[
q(a_{n,i})=y_{n,i},\qquad a_{n,i}\in U_n.
\] Define \[
F_n=\{a_{n,i}\}_i,\qquad
K_{n+1}=\bigcup_i\bigl((K_n-y_{n,i})\cap C_{n+1}\bigr).
\tag{SDT4.4}
\] Again \(K_{n+1}\) is compact and contained in \(C_{n+1}\). The finite
cover ensures that every residual \(r_n\in K_n\) can be written \[
r_n=q(a_n)+r_{n+1},\qquad
a_n\in F_n,\quad r_{n+1}\in K_{n+1}.
\tag{SDT4.5}
\] These finite sets are nonempty: each chosen center gives residual
zero in the next residual set. Thus the displayed finite products are
nonempty when \(K\) is nonempty.

Every choice from the product \(\prod_{n=0}^\infty F_n\) has a
convergent sum in \(A\). For a fixed \(m\) and \(N\ge\max(m,1)\), its
tails satisfy the uniform estimate \[
P_m\left(\sum_{n>N}a_n\right)
\le\sum_{n>N}P_m(a_n)
\le\sum_{n>N}2^{-n}=2^{-N}.
\tag{SDT4.6}
\] Completeness of \(A\) proves existence of each sum. The sum map \[
\Psi:\prod_{n=0}^\infty F_n\longrightarrow A,\qquad
\Psi((a_n))=\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n
\tag{SDT4.7}
\] is continuous: finite partial sums are continuous and (SDT4.6)
controls the remainder uniformly in every defining seminorm.

The product of these finite discrete sets is compact. One can see this
without an uncountable-product assertion: give it the metric recording
the first coordinate at which two sequences differ; a diagonal
subsequence argument gives a convergent subsequence from every sequence.
Its image \[
B_K^*=\Psi\left(\prod_{n=0}^\infty F_n\right)
\tag{SDT4.8}
\] is therefore compact.

For any \(y\in K\), choose a compatible residual path using (SDT4.3) and
(SDT4.5). After stage \(N\), \[
y-q\left(\sum_{n=0}^N a_n\right)=r_{N+1}\in C_{N+1}.
\] For \(N+1\ge m\), \[
\overline P_m(r_{N+1})
\le\overline P_{N+1}(r_{N+1})
\le2^{-N-2}.
\tag{SDT4.9}
\] Thus the residual tends to zero in \(Q\), and continuity of \(q\)
gives \(q(\Psi((a_n)))=y\). This proves \(K\subset q(B_K^*)\). The
compact set \[
B_K=B_K^*\cap q^{-1}(K)
\tag{SDT4.10}
\] then satisfies \(q(B_K)=K\). The intersection is closed in \(B_K^*\),
because the compact set \(K\) is closed in the Hausdorff space \(Q\).
Empty \(K\) has the empty lift.

We have constructed, rather than assumed, \[
\boxed{K\subset Q\ \text{compact}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\exists B_K\subset A\ \text{compact with }q(B_K)=K.}
\tag{SDT4.11}
\] The construction supplies a compact set of lifts. It makes no
assertion that \(q\) has a continuous linear section, and it does not
choose a new topology on either coefficient.

\subsection{SDT5. Bounded lifting and equality of the strong-dual
topologies}\label{sdt5.-bounded-lifting-and-equality-of-the-strong-dual-topologies}

Let \(B\subset Q\) be bounded. SDT3 gives the compact closure
\(K=\overline B\). Lift \(K\) by SDT4 and define \[
\widetilde B=B_K\cap q^{-1}(B).
\tag{SDT5.1}
\] This set need not be closed or compact, but it is a subset of the
compact set \(B_K\), hence is bounded. Every \(y\in B\) has a preimage
in it. Therefore \[
\boxed{q(\widetilde B)=B,\qquad \widetilde B\subset A\ \text{bounded}.}
\tag{SDT5.2}
\] Compact sets are bounded because every continuous seminorm has a
finite maximum on them; the seminorm characterization of boundedness
then gives the assertion for their subsets.

For a locally convex space \(E\), retain the strong-dual seminorms \[
p_D(\lambda)=\sup_{x\in D}|\lambda(x)|,
\qquad D\subset E\ \text{bounded}.
\tag{SDT5.3}
\] CDI1 proves the algebraic identification \(q'Q'=J^\perp\). If
\(C\subset A\) is bounded, then \(q(C)\) is bounded and \[
p_C(q'\mu)=p_{q(C)}(\mu).
\tag{SDT5.4}
\] This proves continuity of \(q':Q'_\beta\to A'_\beta\). Conversely,
for every bounded \(B\subset Q\), choose its exact bounded lift from
(SDT5.2). Then \[
p_B(\mu)=p_{\widetilde B}(q'\mu).
\tag{SDT5.5}
\] Every defining strong seminorm on \(Q'\) is thus the restriction of a
defining strong seminorm from \(A'\). Equations (SDT5.4) and (SDT5.5)
prove \[
\boxed{Q'_\beta\xrightarrow[\mu\mapsto\mu\circ q]{\ \sim\ }
(J^\perp,\text{subspace topology from }A'_\beta).}
\tag{SDT5.6}
\] The annihilator \(J^\perp\) is closed, being the intersection of the
continuous evaluation kernels at \(j\in J\). This resolves the specific
topology left open in CDI3.2--CDI3.3.

No openness conclusion for the separate restriction map
\(A'_\beta\to J'_\beta\) is needed for (SDT5.6), and no such conclusion
is asserted here.

\subsection{SDT6. The full strong bidual is the original
coefficient}\label{sdt6.-the-full-strong-bidual-is-the-original-coefficient}

Let \(E\) be \(A\) or \(Q\), with exactly the topology already proved.
The argument also applies to the closed subspace \(J\) and the
finite-product graph coefficient \(Z\) from SDT3.

First let \(\Lambda:E'_\beta\to\mathbb C\) be continuous and linear.
Continuity at zero gives finitely many bounded sets
\(B_1,\ldots,B_r\subset E\) and a finite constant \(C\) such that \[
|\Lambda(\lambda)|\le C\max_i p_{B_i}(\lambda)
=p_{C B}(\lambda),\qquad B=\bigcup_i B_i.
\tag{SDT6.1}
\] If \(C=0\), then \(\Lambda=0\). Otherwise let \[
D=\overline{\operatorname{aconv}(CB)}^{\,E},
\tag{SDT6.2}
\] where the absolutely convex hull consists of finite sums
\(\sum_i c_i b_i\) with \(b_i\in CB\) and \(\sum_i|c_i|\le1\). Every
continuous seminorm is bounded on \(D\) by its supremum on \(CB\). Thus
\(D\) is bounded. By SDT2 or SDT3 it is compact, as well as closed and
absolutely convex. Equation (SDT6.1) becomes \[
|\Lambda(\lambda)|\le\sup_{x\in D}|\lambda(x)|.
\tag{SDT6.3}
\]

For any finite family \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r\in E'\), the vector
\((\Lambda(\lambda_1),\ldots,\Lambda(\lambda_r))\) belongs to the
compact absolutely convex set \[
\{(\lambda_1(x),\ldots,\lambda_r(x)):x\in D\}\subset\mathbb C^r.
\tag{SDT6.4}
\] Indeed, otherwise finite-dimensional real separation would give a
complex linear combination \(\lambda=\sum_i c_i\lambda_i\) whose real
value at that vector exceeds its supremum on the displayed set. Absolute
convexity identifies that supremum with the supremum of the modulus.
This contradicts (SDT6.3).

Hence the closed subsets \(\{x\in D:\lambda(x)=\Lambda(\lambda)\}\),
indexed by \(\lambda\in E'\), have the finite intersection property.
Compactness of \(D\) gives \(x\in D\) with \[
\Lambda(\lambda)=\lambda(x)\quad\text{for every }\lambda\in E'.
\tag{SDT6.5}
\] Hahn--Banach separation of points in the Hausdorff locally convex
space \(E\) makes \(x\) unique. Therefore \[
(E'_\beta)'=E
\tag{SDT6.6}
\] under the specified evaluation map, as vector spaces.

We prove equality of topologies as well. A bounded set
\(M\subset E'_\beta\) is pointwise bounded, since each singleton
\(\{x\}\subset E\) is bounded. Put \[
C_k=\{x\in E:\sup_{\lambda\in M}|\lambda(x)|\le k\},
\qquad k=1,2,\ldots.
\tag{SDT6.7}
\] Each \(C_k\) is closed and their union is \(E\). The Baire theorem
for the complete metric space \(E\) gives an interior point \(x_0\) of
one \(C_k\). For a neighborhood \(U\) of zero with \(x_0+U\subset C_k\),
\[
\sup_{\lambda\in M}|\lambda(u)|\le2k\quad(u\in U).
\tag{SDT6.8}
\] Thus \(M\) is equicontinuous, and the seminorm
\(x\mapsto\sup_{\lambda\in M}|\lambda(x)|\) is continuous on \(E\). This
proves continuity of evaluation into the strong bidual.

For the reverse topology, let \(P\) be any continuous seminorm on \(E\),
and set \[
M_P=\{\lambda\in E':|\lambda(x)|\le P(x)\text{ for all }x\in E\}.
\tag{SDT6.9}
\] For every bounded \(B\subset E\), \[
\sup_{\lambda\in M_P}p_B(\lambda)\le\sup_{x\in B}P(x)<\infty.
\] Hence \(M_P\) is bounded in \(E'_\beta\). Complex Hahn--Banach,
applied to each one-dimensional span, gives \[
P(x)=\sup_{\lambda\in M_P}|\lambda(x)|.
\tag{SDT6.10}
\] Every original continuous seminorm is therefore a strong-bidual
seminorm. Together with (SDT6.5)--(SDT6.8), this proves the topological
isomorphism \[
\boxed{E\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }(E'_\beta)'_\beta,\qquad
x\longmapsto(\lambda\mapsto\lambda(x)).}
\tag{SDT6.11}
\]

For the terminology used in SDT3, the same Baire argument proves that
\(E\) is barrelled. If \(C\subset E\) is a closed, balanced, convex,
absorbing set, then \(E=\bigcup_{k\ge1}kC\). Baire gives an interior
point of some \(kC\); differences of two points in a small neighborhood
of that point lie in \(2kC\). Balanced convexity then makes \(C\) a
neighborhood of zero. This proves the barrelled property from its
definition.

\subsection{SDT7. Actual zero jets are strongly
dense}\label{sdt7.-actual-zero-jets-are-strongly-dense}

Retain the exact comparison coordinate, including its factor: \[
\mathcal M_0a(s)=\int_0^\infty a(u)u^s\frac{du}{u},
\qquad
\kappa:Q\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\mathcal Q=\mathcal B/\mathcal I,
\qquad
\kappa([a])=\left[\frac12\mathcal M_0a\right].
\tag{SDT7.1}
\] Here \(\mathcal B\) is the entire strip-Schwartz space with seminorms
\[
b_{R,M}(F)=\sup_{\substack{|\sigma|\le R\\t\in\mathbb R}}
(1+|t|)^M|F(\sigma+it)|,
\] and \[
\mathcal I=\{F\in\mathcal B:
F^{(j)}(\rho)=0\text{ for every actual nontrivial zero }\rho
\text{ of the original }\zeta,\ 0\le j<m_\rho\}.
\tag{SDT7.2}
\] These are the full actual multiplicities. The raw source identity
remains \[
\mathcal M_0\Sigma f(s)
=2\zeta(s)\int_0^\infty f(v)v^s\frac{dv}{v}
\qquad(\Re s>1).
\tag{SDT7.3}
\] SSI's continuous inverse for a raw target \(F\in\mathcal I\) is \[
f_F(x)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
\frac{F(2+it)}{2\zeta(2+it)}x^{-2-it}\,dt\quad(x>0),
\] \[
f_F(-x)=f_F(x),\quad f_F(0)=0,\quad\int_{\mathbb R}f_F=0,\quad
\frac{f_F^{(2r)}(0)}{(2r)!}=\frac{F(-2r)}{2\zeta'(-2r)}
\quad(r\ge1).
\tag{SDT7.4}
\] The actual pole at 1 and trivial-zero residues are therefore not
erased by the quotient-topology argument. In (SDT7.1)'s coordinate
\(G\), the raw target is \(2G\).

Let \[
\delta_{\rho,j}([F])=F^{(j)}(\rho),\qquad
\mathscr D_{\mathrm{fin}}
=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}
\{\delta_{\rho,j}:\rho,\ 0\le j<m_\rho\}\subset\mathcal Q'.
\tag{SDT7.5}
\] Each jet is continuous: choose a circle about \(\rho\) contained in a
fixed strip and use Cauchy's formula to bound its derivative by one
\(b_{R,0}\). It vanishes on \(\mathcal I\), so factors continuously
through the quotient. Most importantly, these functionals together
separate every class: \[
\bigcap_{\rho,j}\ker\delta_{\rho,j}=\{0\}\subset\mathcal Q.
\tag{SDT7.6}
\] This is exactly the defining full-jet ideal (SDT7.2), not a finite
truncation or a simplicity assumption.

The isomorphism \(\kappa\) transfers SDT3 and SDT6 to \(\mathcal Q\).
Suppose the strong closure of \(\mathscr D_{\mathrm{fin}}\) were a
proper closed linear subspace of \(\mathcal Q'_\beta\). Hahn--Banach
separation in that Hausdorff locally convex space would give a nonzero
continuous linear functional \(\Lambda\) vanishing on that subspace. By
(SDT6.6), \(\Lambda\) is evaluation at a unique class
\(x\in\mathcal Q\). Since it vanishes on every \(\delta_{\rho,j}\),
(SDT7.6) gives \(x=0\), contrary to \(\Lambda\ne0\). Therefore \[
\boxed{\overline{\mathscr D_{\mathrm{fin}}}^{\,\beta(\mathcal Q',\mathcal Q)}
=\mathcal Q'_\beta.}
\tag{SDT7.7}
\] Equivalently, for every \(\lambda\in\mathcal Q'\), every finite
family of bounded subsets \(B_1,\ldots,B_r\subset\mathcal Q\), and every
\(\varepsilon>0\), there is a finite linear combination \(d\) of the
actual full zero jets such that \[
\max_i\sup_{x\in B_i}|\lambda(x)-d(x)|<\varepsilon.
\tag{SDT7.8}
\] This is stronger than matching finitely many evaluations. It asserts
density in the uniform-on-bounded-sets topology, with exactly the bounds
that define that topology.

For precision about completion, \(E'_\beta\) is complete for each
Fréchet \(E\) used here. Indeed a strong Cauchy net \((\lambda_\alpha)\)
is pointwise Cauchy and defines a linear limit \(\lambda\). On every
bounded set \(B\), the Cauchy estimate passes to the pointwise limit, so
convergence is uniform on \(B\). To show that \(\lambda\) is continuous,
take any sequence \(x_n\to0\) in \(E\). The compact set
\(\{0,x_1,x_2,\ldots\}\) is bounded, and uniform convergence on it makes
the restriction of \(\lambda\) a uniform limit of continuous functions.
Thus \(\lambda(x_n)\to0\). Metrizability of \(E\) turns sequential
continuity at zero into continuity. Hence \(\lambda\in E'\), and the
convergence already proved on every bounded set is strong convergence.

Consequently the Hausdorff completion of \(\mathscr D_{\mathrm{fin}}\),
with the topology induced from \(\mathcal Q'_\beta\), is canonically
\(\mathcal Q'_\beta\). This is a completion statement about jet
functionals. It does not assert that the finite-support class space
\(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{fin}}\) is dense in \(\mathcal Q\), nor that an
unproved topology on that class space is its original quotient subspace
topology.

\subsection{SDT8. Strong-dual cohomology and the original residue
map}\label{sdt8.-strong-dual-cohomology-and-the-original-residue-map}

Retain CDI's full endpoint and complex data: \[
E_+=\mathbb Ce_{+,0}\oplus\mathbb Ce_{+,1},\qquad
E_-=\mathbb Ce_{-,0}\oplus\mathbb Ce_{-,1},\qquad
V_\pm=S\oplus E_\pm,
\] \[
r_+(f,e)=\Sigma f,\qquad
r_-(h,e)=R\Sigma h=\Sigma\widehat h,\qquad
Ra(u)=u^{-1}a(u^{-1}),
\] \[
W=V_+\oplus V_-,\qquad
d(v_+,v_-)=r_+v_+-r_-v_-,\qquad Z=\ker d.
\tag{SDT8.1}
\] Every original endpoint line remains present. The inverse
\(\Sigma^{-1}:J\to S\) gives the continuous splittings in CDI1: \[
V_\pm\simeq E_\pm\oplus J,\qquad
W\simeq Z\oplus J,\qquad
Z\simeq J\oplus E_+\oplus E_-.
\tag{SDT8.2}
\] Finite topological direct sums transpose to finite topological direct
sums for strong duals. To check this, a bounded set has bounded
projections, and the image of a bounded set under either continuous
coordinate inclusion is bounded. The strong seminorms in each direction
are therefore bounded by finite sums or maxima of the coordinate strong
seminorms.

For the actual global complex \[
D=[W\xrightarrow d A\xrightarrow0 A]
\quad\text{in degrees }0,1,2,
\] the Hom convention
\(d_{D^\vee}^n\lambda=(-1)^{n+1}\lambda\circ d_D^{-n-1}\) gives \[
D^\vee=[A'_\beta\xrightarrow0 A'_\beta
\xrightarrow{\lambda\mapsto(r_+'\lambda,-r_-'\lambda)}
V_+'{}_\beta\oplus V_-'{}_\beta]
\quad\text{in degrees }-2,-1,0.
\tag{SDT8.3}
\] CDI1's exact transpose computation gives \(\ker d'=J^\perp\) and
\(\operatorname{im}d'=Z^\perp\). Equation (SDT5.6) identifies the kernel
topology, while (SDT8.2) identifies the quotient topology in degree
zero. Thus all three identifications are now strong topological
isomorphisms: \[
\boxed{
H^{-2}(D^\vee)=A'_\beta,\qquad
H^{-1}(D^\vee)\simeq Q'_\beta,\qquad
H^0(D^\vee)\simeq Z'_\beta.}
\tag{SDT8.4}
\] Here cohomology has its actual kernel/subspace and quotient
topologies in the displayed coefficient complex.

For each actual pole-costalk complex \(L_\pm=[V_\pm\to A\to A]\), the
same argument gives \[
\boxed{
H^{-2}(L_\pm^\vee)=A'_\beta,\qquad
H^{-1}(L_\pm^\vee)\simeq Q'_\beta,\qquad
H^0(L_\pm^\vee)\simeq E_\pm'{}_\beta.}
\tag{SDT8.5}
\] The transposed two-support maps retain their original signs: \[
A'_\beta\longrightarrow A'_\beta\oplus A'_\beta,\quad
\lambda\longmapsto(\lambda,\lambda)
\quad\text{in degree }-2,
\] \[
Q'_\beta\longrightarrow Q'_\beta\oplus Q'_\beta,\quad
\mu\longmapsto(\mu,-\mu)
\quad\text{in degree }-1.
\tag{SDT8.6}
\] They are continuous for the proved topologies. No sign is changed by
strengthening the identification in degree minus one.

Let \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{fin}}\) denote the classes whose full jet
family vanishes outside a finite set of actual zeros; every order less
than the actual multiplicity remains in each retained block. The
original-zeta residue comparison of CDI9 and
\href{https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/zeta-function-research-reader/blob/main/workbenches/splitzero-tandem/continuations/20260924-cohomology-weight-and-character-lifts/geometric-positive-quotient/ORIGINAL_ZETA_RESIDUE_DUAL_AND_SUPPORT_MAPS.md}{RD2--RD4}
remains \[
\iota(G)(F)=\sum_\rho\operatorname{Res}_{s=\rho}
\frac{F(s)G(1-s)}{\zeta(s)}\,ds,
\qquad
\iota:\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{fin}}\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
\mathscr D_{\mathrm{fin}}.
\tag{SDT8.7}
\] The sum is finite for \(G\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{fin}}\). Its
denominator is the original \(\zeta\). At an actual zero \(\rho\) of
multiplicity \(m\), write \[
\zeta(\rho+t)=t^m u_\rho(t),\qquad u_\rho(0)\ne0.
\] In the monomial jet bases at \(\rho\) and \(1-\rho\), its full matrix
and determinant are \[
P_{ij}=(-1)^j[t^{m-1-i-j}]\frac1{u_\rho(t)}
\quad(0\le i,j<m),\qquad
\det P=u_\rho(0)^{-m}\ne0.
\tag{SDT8.8}
\] Thus SDT7 proves that the actual image of (SDT8.7) is strongly dense.
It does not equip its domain with a new unstated topology or extend the
residue formula to an unproved infinite sum.

The unshifted arithmetic action remains \(T_a[F(s)]=[a^sF(s)]\) for
\(a>0\). Its source multiplier satisfies
\(b_{R,M}(a^sF)\le\max(a^R,a^{-R})b_{R,M}(F)\), so it is continuous on
\(\mathcal B\), preserves \(\mathcal I\), and acts continuously on
\(\mathcal Q\). The exact covariance is \[
\iota(T_aG)=a(T_{a^{-1}})'\iota(G).
\tag{SDT8.9}
\] For the normal coefficient with action \(aT_a\), the
invariant-pairing contragredient is \[
\mathsf N_a^\vee=a^{-1}(T_{a^{-1}})',\qquad
\iota(T_aG)=a^2\mathsf N_a^\vee\iota(G).
\tag{SDT8.10}
\] The strong topology does not remove either factor or the inverse
parameter. Continuous linear maps take bounded sets to bounded sets, so
all these individual transposed operators are strongly continuous. No
positivity conclusion follows from reflexivity or strong density.

One can now construct an exact completed residue receiver. Give
\(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{fin}}\) the initial locally convex topology of
\(\iota\), explicitly \[
p_B^{\mathrm{res}}(G)
=\sup_{F\in B}\left|
\sum_\rho\operatorname{Res}_{s=\rho}
\frac{F(s)G(1-s)}{\zeta(s)}\,ds\right|,
\qquad B\subset\mathcal Q\ \text{bounded}.
\tag{SDT8.11}
\] The local determinant (SDT8.8) makes this topology Hausdorff. By
definition \(\iota\) is a topological isomorphism onto
\(\mathscr D_{\mathrm{fin}}\) with its induced strong topology. SDT7
proves that the latter is dense in the complete space
\(\mathcal Q'_\beta\). Therefore the actual Hausdorff completion is \[
\boxed{
\widehat{\bigl(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{fin}},
(p_B^{\mathrm{res}})_B\bigr)}
\xrightarrow[\ \widehat\iota\ ]{\ \sim\ }\mathcal Q'_\beta.}
\tag{SDT8.12}
\] This is a constructed residue completion. Its topology has been
specified by (SDT8.11); it is not identified with the pre-existing
subspace topology from \(\mathcal Q\).

For completeness, the ordinary-transpose comparison also has a full
formula. In this paragraph \(R[F(s)]=[F(1-s)]\) is the coefficient
reflection, so the ordered global degree-one mirror is \(-R\), not
\(R\). Set \[
\mathsf V_a=aT_{a^{-1}}=RT_aR.
\tag{SDT8.13}
\] On \(\mathcal B\), reflection satisfies
\(b_{R_0,M}(F(1-s))\le b_{R_0+1,M}(F)\); the different letter \(R_0\)
here is a strip width. The original functional equation preserves the
full multiplicities at \(\rho\) and \(1-\rho\), so reflection preserves
\(\mathcal I\). Both \(R\) and \(T_a\) therefore act continuously on
\(\mathcal Q\) and preserve the finite full-zero support, including the
reflected multiplicities. Equation (SDT8.9) at the parameter \(a^{-1}\),
with the prefactor \(a\) retained, gives \[
\iota(\mathsf V_aG)
=a\,a^{-1}(T_a)'\iota(G)
=(T_a)'\iota(G).
\tag{SDT8.14}
\] The operator \(\mathsf V_a\) is therefore continuous for (SDT8.11)
and extends to the actual ordinary transpose \(T_a'\) on its completion.
For the original action \(T_a\), the extension is instead
\(a(T_{a^{-1}})'\), exactly as in (SDT8.9).

There is also an unreflected residue map \[
\phi=\iota R,\qquad
\phi(G)(F)=\sum_\rho\operatorname{Res}_{s=\rho}
\frac{F(s)G(s)}{\zeta(s)}\,ds,\qquad
\phi T_a=(T_a)'\phi.
\tag{SDT8.15}
\] The middle formula substitutes \(RG(1-s)=G(s)\) before taking
residues, so it introduces no change-of-variable sign. The last identity
follows either directly by multiplying the integrand by \(a^s\), or from
\(RT_a=\mathsf V_aR\) and (SDT8.14).

Its exact topological receiver can be specified without assuming
continuity of \(R\) in an unexamined topology: equip
\(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{fin}}\) with \[
p_B^{\mathrm{unref}}(G)
=\sup_{F\in B}\left|
\sum_\rho\operatorname{Res}_{s=\rho}
\frac{F(s)G(s)}{\zeta(s)}\,ds\right|
=p_B^{\mathrm{res}}(RG).
\tag{SDT8.16}
\] Since \(R^2=1\) and
\(R\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{fin}}=\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{fin}}\), it is a
homeomorphism from this unreflected topology to the reflected topology
(SDT8.11). The map \(\phi\) has the same range
\(\mathscr D_{\mathrm{fin}}\), so its completion is also canonically
\(\mathcal Q'_\beta\), now intertwining \(T_a\) with the ordinary
transpose \(T_a'\). These are two explicitly related residue topologies;
neither has been silently substituted for the inherited topology of
\(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{fin}}\subset\mathcal Q\).

\subsection{SDT9. Exact implication and limits of this
calculation}\label{sdt9.-exact-implication-and-limits-of-this-calculation}

The proved chain is \[
\begin{gathered}
\begin{array}{c}
\text{original weighted derivative estimates on }A\\
\Downarrow\ {\rm SDT2\text{--}SDT3}\\
\text{every bounded subset of }Q\text{ has compact closure}\\
\Downarrow\ {\rm SDT4\text{--}SDT5}\\
\text{compact lifts and exact bounded lifts through }q\\
\Downarrow\\

\end{array}\\[1em]
\begin{array}{c}
\text{bounded-compact property and the actual Fréchet topology}\\
\Downarrow\ {\rm SDT6}\\
(\mathcal Q'_\beta)'=\mathcal Q\\
\Downarrow\ {\rm SDT7}\\
\overline{\mathscr D_{\rm fin}}^{\,\beta}=\mathcal Q'_\beta.
\end{array}
\end{gathered}
\tag{SDT9.1}
\] The arrows name the exact proofs that justify the topology change.
The compact lift is the finite-choice series (SDT4.7), not a claimed
linear section. The dense space at the bottom right consists of
functionals, not finite-support classes in the primal quotient.

This establishes the full strong-dual cohomology topology for the actual
CDI coefficient complexes and strengthens their finite-jet detection to
uniform approximation on bounded subsets. It neither constructs a
Verdier dualizing sheaf nor identifies these infinite-dimensional
coefficient duals with a finite-rank étale receiver. No
weight-separation or RH conclusion is inferred from these topological
statements.
