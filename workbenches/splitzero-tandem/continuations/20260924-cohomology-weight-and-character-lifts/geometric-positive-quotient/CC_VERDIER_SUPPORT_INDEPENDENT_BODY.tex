\subsection{VSD1. Constant coefficients of arbitrary
dimension}\label{vsd1.-constant-coefficients-of-arbitrary-dimension}

The dualizing complex on the oriented real two-manifold \(Y\) is \[
\omega_Y=\underline{\mathbb C}_Y[2].
\tag{VSD1.1}
\] Its orientation is fixed by integration of a compactly supported
two-form with positive integral one in each positive complex coordinate.
The local compact-support de Rham calculation on an oriented disk gives
one coefficient in degree two and zero in the other degrees; extension
by zero and integration give the trace map. These local orientation
identifications glue to the displayed dualizing complex. Neither pole
orientation is reversed.

Define ordinary Verdier duality by \[
\mathbb D_Y K=R\mathcal Hom_Y(K,\omega_Y).
\tag{VSD1.2}
\] For every vector space \(E\), including arbitrary infinite dimension,
\[
\boxed{\mathbb D_Y\underline E_Y\simeq\underline{E^*}_Y[2].}
\tag{VSD1.3}
\] To verify the coefficient claim without finite rank, on any open disk
\(B\) constant-sheaf adjunction gives \[
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Sh}(B)}(\underline E_B,K)
=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb C}(E,\Gamma(B,K)).
\tag{VSD1.4}
\] A sheaf map is determined by the images of constant sections;
conversely a linear map into sections extends to locally constant
sections by gluing on their regions of constancy. Resolve \(K\) by
injective sheaves. Vector-space Hom from \(E\) is exact: choose a basis
and lift each basis image through any surjection. The Hom complex
therefore has cohomology \[
\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathrm{Sh}(B)}^q(\underline E_B,K)
=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb C}(E,H^q(B,K)).
\tag{VSD1.5}
\] For constant \(K=\underline{\mathbb C}_B\), the disk contraction
gives \(H^0=\mathbb C\), \(H^{q>0}=0\). Internal derived-Hom stalks are
the filtered colimit of these local Ext groups; higher internal Ext
stalks vanish. In degree zero the internal Hom is constant with
coefficient \(E^*\): on a connected disk each scalar image of a constant
section is constant, defining a single linear functional on all of
\(E\). Thus (VSD1.3) follows.

For a point \(p\) and every vector space \(B\), \[
i_p^!\underline B_Y=B[-2],\qquad i_p^!\underline B_Y[2]=B.
\tag{VSD1.6}
\] Indeed the disk/punctured-disk relative complex has identity
restriction in degree zero, while the punctured disk has its independent
degree-one angular coefficient \(B\). Its fibre is the local fundamental
class in degree two. This is the coefficient version of CSP6 and uses no
dimension bound.

\subsection{VSD2. Pole duals and the signed Gysin

For a skyscraper coefficient, \[
\boxed{\mathbb D_Yi_{p*}E=i_{p*}E^*.}
\tag{VSD2.1}

no higher Ext remains. This proves (VSD2.1) in arbitrary dimension.

For any vector space \(B\), define \[
g_{p,B}:i_{p*}B\longrightarrow\underline B_Y[2]
\tag{VSD2.2}
\] as the adjunction counit corresponding to the identity of
\(B=i_p^!\underline B_Y[2]\), where the identification is positive local

\operatorname{Hom}_{D(Y)}(i_{p*}B,\underline B_Y[2])
=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb C}(B,B).
\tag{VSD2.3}
\] The dual of restriction \(\underline E_Y\to i_{p*}E\) is

\(\lambda\); the corresponding local compactly supported two-form has
integral \(\lambda(e)\), using a positive fundamental form. Extension by
zero to the sphere preserves that integral, so the global scalar is
\(+\lambda(e)\).

The angular connecting sign is separate. For \(\chi=1\) near the pole
and zero outside a disk, \[
-d\chi\wedge\frac{d\theta_p}{2\pi},

For the two poles, \[
g_B=g_{p_+,B}+g_{p_-,B}:i_*(B\oplus B)\to\underline B_Y[2]
\tag{VSD2.5}

\tag{VSD2.6}

\subsection{VSD9. Nonzero defect for the actual infinite
coefficients}\label{vsd9.-nonzero-defect-for-the-actual-infinite-coefficients}

Every infinite-dimensional Hausdorff Fréchet space \(E\) has
discontinuous algebraic functionals. For a defining seminorm sequence
\(p_1,p_2,\ldots\), choose independent \(x_n\) and scale them by nonzero
constants to obtain independent \(v_n\) with \[
\max_{1\le j\le n}p_j(v_n)\le2^{-n}.
\tag{VSD9.1}
\] Then \(v_n\to0\). Assign \(\lambda(v_n)=1\) on their span and extend
the independent family to an algebraic basis of \(E\), assigning zero to
the additional basis vectors. This defines an algebraic functional which
is not continuous. Hence \(E^\dagger\ne0\).

The actual \(A\) is infinite dimensional: smooth functions with pairwise
disjoint compact supports in \(\mathbb R_{>0}\) lie in \(A\) and are
independent. The actual \(S\) is infinite dimensional too. In pairwise
disjoint positive intervals choose nonzero compactly supported smooth
functions of integral zero, for example derivatives of interior bump
functions, and extend them evenly. They vanish at zero, retain integral
zero on the real line, and are independent members of \(S\). The proved
topological isomorphism \(\Sigma:S\to J\) gives \[
A^\dagger\ne0,\qquad J^\dagger\ne0.
\tag{VSD9.2}
\] Consequently \(c\) is not a quasi-isomorphism: its open-stratum
degree-minus-two cokernel is the nonzero \(A^\dagger\). Its pole costalk
comparison also has nonzero quotient \(J^\dagger\). This proves the
precise difference between the two defined dual constructions; it says
nothing against the original support \(\tau\) or its lifting programme.

\subsection{VSD10. Weak completion and the original residue
family}\label{vsd10.-weak-completion-and-the-original-residue-family}

For every Hausdorff locally convex \(E\), \[
\overline{E'}^{\,\sigma(E^*,E)}=E^*.
\tag{VSD10.1}
\] Indeed fix \(\lambda\in E^*\) and finitely many \(e_1,\ldots,e_m\).
On their finite-dimensional span \(L\), the restriction of \(\lambda\)
is continuous, because the Hausdorff induced topology on \(L\) is its
ordinary finite-dimensional topology. It is bounded by a continuous
seminorm induced from \(E\). Hahn--Banach extends it continuously to all
of \(E\), agreeing exactly on the prescribed vectors. Every pointwise
neighborhood of \(\lambda\) therefore meets \(E'\).

The full algebraic dual is complete in pointwise convergence. It is the
closed subspace of the complete product \(\mathbb C^E\) cut out by every
linearity equation \[
f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y),\qquad f(cx)=cf(x).
\tag{VSD10.2}
\] Its topology on \(E'\) is exactly the weak dual topology. Thus \[
\boxed{\text{the Hausdorff completion of }E'_\sigma
\text{ is }E^*\text{ with its pointwise topology}.}
\tag{VSD10.3}
\] Weak completion and weak closure inside a specified continuous dual
are different operations; neither is substituted for the other.

The quotient topology on \(E^*/E'\) from \(\sigma(E^*,E)\) is
indiscrete. To see it, let a nonempty open saturated set contain
\(x+U\), with \(U\) an open neighborhood of zero. For any \(y\), density
makes \(y-x-U\) meet \(E'\), so \(y\in x+U+E'\), and saturation places
every \(y\) in the set. Its only open subsets are therefore empty and
whole. The nonzero algebraic quotients in VSD9 are not Hausdorff
quotients for this particular topology. They remain the exact
ordinary-sheaf coefficients in VSD8.

RD's finite-support original-zeta residue family \(\mathcal D\)
satisfies both \[
\overline{\mathcal D}^{\sigma(\mathcal Q',\mathcal Q)}=\mathcal Q',
\qquad
\overline{\mathcal D}^{\sigma(\mathcal Q^*,\mathcal Q)}=\mathcal Q^* .
\tag{VSD10.4}
\] The first is RD4. For the second, take any algebraic \(\lambda\) and
finitely many \(x_1,\ldots,x_m\in\mathcal Q\). If their
\(\lambda\)-values were outside the finite-dimensional image
\(\{(d(x_i))_i:d\in\mathcal D\}\), a separating linear relation would
annihilate every full zero jet of \(\sum c_ix_i\), hence that class
would be zero by the definition of \(\mathcal Q\), while its

\subsection{VSD13. The exact comparison defect admits every rational
