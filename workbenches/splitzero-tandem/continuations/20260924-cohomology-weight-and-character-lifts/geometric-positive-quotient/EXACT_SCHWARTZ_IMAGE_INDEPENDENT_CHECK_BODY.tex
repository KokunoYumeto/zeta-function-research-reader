\subsection{ESI0. Spaces, conventions, and the exact
statement}\label{esi0.-spaces-conventions-and-the-exact-statement}

Let \[
\mathcal S=\{f\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R):f(-x)=f(x),\ f(0)=0,\ \int_{\mathbb R}f(x)\,dx=0\}.
\tag{ESI0.1}
\] Its topology is induced by the Schwartz seminorms \[
q_{R,j}(f)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb R}(1+|x|)^R|f^{(j)}(x)|,
\qquad R,j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.
\tag{ESI0.2}
\] Let \[
\mathcal A=\left\{b\in C^\infty(\mathbb R_{>0}):
\sup_{u>0}(u^N+u^{-N})|(u\partial_u)^j b(u)|<\infty
\text{ for all }N,j\ge0\right\},
\tag{ESI0.3}
\] and let \(\mathcal B\) consist of entire functions with seminorms \[
\beta_{A,M}(F)=\sup_{|\sigma|\le A,\,t\in\mathbb R}
(1+|t|)^M|F(\sigma+it)|<\infty.
\tag{ESI0.4}
\] The raw Mellin map and raw summation map are \[
\mathcal M_0b(s)=\int_0^\infty b(u)u^s\frac{du}{u},
\qquad
\Sigma f(u)=2\sum_{n=1}^\infty f(nu).
\tag{ESI0.5}
\] The comparison with S1's centered transform is the exact identity
\(\mathcal M_0b(s)=\mathcal Mb(s+\tfrac12)\). Thus S1 gives a
topological isomorphism \(\mathcal M_0:\mathcal A\to\mathcal B\), with
inverse, for every real \(c\), \[
b(u)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}F(c+it)u^{-c-it}\,dt.
\tag{ESI0.6}
\] The shift in this comparison changes the transform convention; it
does not change the original zeta variable in the formulas below.

Let \[
\mathcal I=\{F\in\mathcal B:F^{(j)}(\rho)=0
\text{ for every nontrivial zero }\rho\text{ of }\zeta,
\ 0\le j<m_\rho\}.
\tag{ESI0.7}
\] Every zero is included with its actual multiplicity. The closedness
of \(\mathcal I\) follows from continuity of holomorphic jet
evaluations: Cauchy's formula on any fixed circle about the evaluation
point bounds a jet by a finite \(\beta_{A,0}\) seminorm.

The exact result is the topological linear isomorphism \[
\boxed{\Sigma:\mathcal S\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
\mathcal M_0^{-1}(\mathcal I).}
\tag{ESI0.8}
\] In particular, with \(J=\Sigma\mathcal S\subset\mathcal A\), \[
J=\overline J^{\mathcal A}=\mathcal M_0^{-1}(\mathcal I).
\tag{ESI0.9}
\]

\subsection{ESI1. Forward map, all factors, and
injectivity}\label{esi1.-forward-map-all-factors-and-injectivity}

For \(f\in\mathcal S\), the differentiated series defining \(\Sigma f\)
converges locally uniformly with all derivatives on \(u>0\). At
\(u\ge1\), for each \(L>1\), a finite collection of Schwartz seminorms
bounds its logarithmic derivatives by a constant times
\(u^{-L}\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-L}\).

Use the Fourier convention \[
\widehat f(y)=\int_{\mathbb R}f(x)e^{-2\pi ixy}\,dx.
\] Evenness and the two source constraints give \(\widehat f(0)=0\),
\(\int\widehat f=f(0)=0\), and \(\widehat f\in\mathcal S\). Poisson
summation gives, retaining the raw factor of two, \[
\Sigma f(u)=u^{-1}\Sigma\widehat f(1/u).
\tag{ESI1.1}
\] Indeed both complete integer sums have vanishing zero terms. If
\(D=u\partial_u\), differentiation of the right side gives \[
D^j\bigl(u^{-1}\Sigma\widehat f(1/u)\bigr)
=u^{-1}(-1-D_v)^j\Sigma\widehat f(v)\big|_{v=1/u}.
\tag{ESI1.2}
\] The estimates at infinity therefore give arbitrary power decay at
zero too. Fourier transformation is continuous on Schwartz space, so
these estimates prove continuity \(\Sigma:\mathcal S\to\mathcal A\).

For \(\Re s>1\), absolute convergence and the substitution \(v=nu\) give
\[
\mathcal M_0\Sigma f(s)
=2\zeta(s)\int_0^\infty f(v)v^s\frac{dv}{v}.
\tag{ESI1.3}
\] The last integral is holomorphic on \(\Re s>-2\), since an even
smooth function with \(f(0)=0\) satisfies \(f(v)=O(v^2)\). Its value at
\(s=1\) is zero. Consequently (ESI1.3), continued to the nontrivial
zeros, proves \(\mathcal M_0\Sigma f\in\mathcal I\), including every
required jet order.

If \(\Sigma f=0\), then on \(s=2+it\) the Euler product gives
\(\zeta(s)\ne0\), so the Mellin integral of \(f\) there vanishes
identically. Fourier uniqueness in the variable \(\log v\) gives
\(f=0\). Thus \(\Sigma\) is injective.

\subsection{ESI2. The quotient and its possible
poles}\label{esi2.-the-quotient-and-its-possible-poles}

Fix \(F\in\mathcal I\) and put \[
H(s)=\frac{F(s)}{2\zeta(s)}.
\tag{ESI2.1}
\] Local Taylor division at every nontrivial zero gives a holomorphic
extension there. The pole of \(\zeta\) at 1 makes \(H\) holomorphic with
\[
H(1)=0,\qquad H'(1)=\frac{F(1)}2.
\tag{ESI2.2}
\] The only possible poles of \(H\) are simple poles at the trivial
zeros \(-2r\), \(r\ge1\), with residues \[
c_r:=\mathop{\rm Res}_{s=-2r}H(s)
=\frac{F(-2r)}{2\zeta'(-2r)}.
\tag{ESI2.3}
\] If the numerator also vanishes, this residue is zero and the apparent
pole is removable. At zero, \[
H(0)=-F(0),
\tag{ESI2.4}
\] because \(\zeta(0)=-\tfrac12\).

The simplicity and nonvanishing derivatives of the trivial zeros follow
from the full functional equation \[
\zeta(s)=\chi(s)\zeta(1-s),\qquad
\chi(s)=2^s\pi^{s-1}\sin(\pi s/2)\Gamma(1-s)
=\pi^{s-1/2}\frac{\Gamma((1-s)/2)}{\Gamma(s/2)}.
\tag{ESI2.5}
\] At \(-2r\), the sine has a simple zero, while the other factors and
\(\zeta(1+2r)\) are finite and nonzero. More precisely, differentiating
its simple sine zero gives \[
\zeta'(-2r)=(-1)^r2^{-2r-1}\pi^{-2r}(2r)!\zeta(1+2r).
\tag{ESI2.6}
\] Thus no completion cancellation is erased: its exact coefficient
remains in (ESI2.3) and (ESI2.6).

The auxiliary function used only to prove a growth estimate is the
complete retained S2 expression \[
F_0(s)=K(s)\zeta(s),\qquad
K(s)=\frac{s(s-1)}8\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2).
\tag{ESI2.7}
\] By S3, \(Q=F/F_0\) is entire and, on each bounded real strip, \[
|Q(\sigma+it)|\le C_{A,F}
\exp\!\left(C(|t|+2)^{3/2}\log(|t|+2)\right).
\tag{ESI2.8}
\] The exact relationship is \[
H(s)=\frac12Q(s)K(s)
=Q(s)\frac{s(s-1)}{16}\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2).
\tag{ESI2.9}
\] It holds meromorphically at all exceptional points too.

For later use, on a fixed real strip and \(|t|\ge1\), \(K\) has at most
polynomial growth. To verify this without an interior lower bound for
zeta, choose an integer \(m\) making \(\Re(s/2)+m\ge1\) throughout the
strip. Gamma recurrence and its defining integral give \[
\Gamma(s/2)=\frac{\Gamma(s/2+m)}{\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(s/2+j)},
\qquad
|\Gamma(s/2+m)|\le\Gamma(\Re(s/2)+m).
\tag{ESI2.10}
\] The numerator is bounded on the resulting compact interval of
positive real parts; each denominator factor has modulus at least
\(|t|/2\). Including the polynomial \(s(s-1)\) and the bounded modulus
of \(\pi^{-s/2}\) proves the assertion. Together with (ESI2.8), this
proves strictly subquadratic logarithmic growth of \(H\), and of any
fixed polynomial times \(H\), on the horizontal parts \(|t|\ge1\) of
each strip.

\subsection{ESI3. Exact boundary
estimates}\label{esi3.-exact-boundary-estimates}

On a right boundary \(\Re s=b>1\), the absolutely convergent Euler
product, or its reciprocal Dirichlet series, gives \[
\frac1{|\zeta(b+it)|}\le\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{|\mu(n)|}{n^b}
\le\zeta(b).
\tag{ESI3.1}
\] Thus \(H\) has rapid vertical decay there, bounded continuously by
the corresponding seminorms of \(F\).

For a left boundary choose \(a=-2N-1\), \(N\ge0\), and write \(y=t/2\).
The exact Gamma identity is \[
\boxed{
|\chi(a+it)|^2=
\pi^{-4N-3}\,y\coth(\pi y)
\prod_{k=1}^{N}(k^2+y^2)
\prod_{j=0}^{N}\bigl((j+\tfrac12)^2+y^2\bigr).}
\tag{ESI3.2}
\] Here the first product is 1 when \(N=0\), and \(y\coth(\pi y)\) has
value \(1/\pi\) at \(y=0\).

For completeness, the Gamma numerator in (ESI2.5) is \(\Gamma(N+1-iy)\);
recurrence gives its squared modulus as
\(\prod_{k=1}^N(k^2+y^2)|\Gamma(1-iy)|^2\). The denominator is
\(\Gamma(-N-\tfrac12+iy)\); recurrence to \(\Gamma(\tfrac12+iy)\)
contributes the second product in the numerator of the modulus ratio.
The reflection formula gives \[
|\Gamma(1-iy)|^2=\frac{\pi y}{\sinh(\pi y)},
\qquad
|\Gamma(\tfrac12+iy)|^2=\frac{\pi}{\cosh(\pi y)}.
\tag{ESI3.3}
\] Their ratio is \(y\coth(\pi y)\), and the squared modulus of
\(\pi^{s-1/2}\) is \(\pi^{-4N-3}\). This proves every factor in
(ESI3.2).

This expression is strictly positive for every real \(t\). It yields the
explicit lower estimate \[
|\chi(a+it)|^2\ge
\frac{\pi^{-4N-4}}{2\,8^{2N+1}}
(1+|t|)^{4N+3}.
\tag{ESI3.4}
\] Indeed \(y\coth(\pi y)\ge\max(|t|/2,1/\pi)\ge(1+|t|)/(2\pi)\). Each
of the \(2N+1\) quadratic factors is at least \((1+|t|)^2/8\). The first
inequality follows from \(\coth x\ge1\) and \(\tanh x\le x\) for
\(x>0\), with continuity at zero.

Because \(1-a=2N+2>1\), (ESI2.5) and (ESI3.1) give \[
\frac1{|\zeta(a+it)|}
\le \zeta(2N+2)\sqrt{2\,8^{2N+1}}\,
\pi^{2N+2}(1+|t|)^{-2N-3/2}.
\tag{ESI3.5}
\] In particular \(H\) is rapidly decreasing on this left boundary too,
with continuous bounds in \(F\). This proves the boundary estimates on
complete unbounded lines, not only on a finite height range.

\subsection{ESI4. The strip argument and its
uniformity}\label{esi4.-the-strip-argument-and-its-uniformity}

Fix \(N\ge0\), \(a=-2N-1\), and any \(b>1\). Put \[
P_N(s)=\prod_{r=1}^N(s+2r),\qquad V(s)=P_N(s)H(s).
\tag{ESI4.1}
\] For \(N=0\), \(P_0=1\). The poles described in ESI2 show that \(V\)
is holomorphic on a neighborhood of the closed strip \(a\le\Re s\le b\).
No poles lie on either boundary.

Choose a real number \(L\) with \(a+L\ge1\). For any integer \(M\ge0\),
set \[
W_M(s)=(s+L)^M V(s),\qquad
B_M(F)=\max_{c\in\{a,b\}}\sup_{t\in\mathbb R}|W_M(c+it)|.
\tag{ESI4.2}
\] ESI3 proves that \(B_M(F)\) is finite and at most a constant times a
finite \(\beta_{A,M'}(F)\) seminorm. This constant depends on the fixed
strip, \(L\), and \(M\), not on \(F\).

For \(\varepsilon>0\), apply the maximum-modulus principle on the
rectangle \([a,b]+i[-T,T]\) to \[
e^{\varepsilon s^2}W_M(s).
\tag{ESI4.3}
\] On either vertical side its modulus is at most
\(e^{\varepsilon\max(a^2,b^2)}B_M(F)\), since
\(|e^{\varepsilon(c+it)^2}|=e^{\varepsilon c^2-\varepsilon t^2}\). On
the two horizontal sides ESI2 gives a bound of the form \[
C_{F,N,b,M,\varepsilon}(1+T)^d
\exp\!\left(-\varepsilon T^2+C(T+2)^{3/2}\log(T+2)\right),
\tag{ESI4.4}
\] which tends to zero as \(T\to\infty\), uniformly in the real
coordinate. The exponent's second term divided by \(T^2\) tends to zero.
No growth constant uniform in \(F\) is needed for this limiting
assertion.

For any fixed \(s\) in the strip, first let \(T\to\infty\). This yields
\[
|e^{\varepsilon s^2}W_M(s)|
\le e^{\varepsilon\max(a^2,b^2)}B_M(F).
\] Now let \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), with \(s\) fixed. The exact
consequence is \[
|W_M(s)|\le B_M(F)\quad(a\le\Re s\le b).
\tag{ESI4.5}
\] Since \(|s+L|\ge\sqrt{1+t^2}\ge(1+|t|)/\sqrt2\), this proves \[
\sup_{a\le\sigma\le b}(1+|t|)^M|P_N(\sigma+it)H(\sigma+it)|
\le2^{M/2}B_M(F).
\tag{ESI4.6}
\] This is an interior rapid-decay estimate with constants controlled by
boundary seminorms of \(F\). The use of a potentially uncontrolled
constant in (ESI2.8) does not compromise (ESI4.6); that constant has
disappeared after taking the two limits in their stated order.

For \(|t|\ge1\), \(|P_N(\sigma+it)|\ge|t|^N\). Thus \(H\) itself has
arbitrarily rapid vertical decay there uniformly on the strip. On
compact regions away from its finitely many poles it is bounded by
holomorphicity. Cauchy's formula applied on small circles in a slightly
larger strip gives the same rapid bounds for all complex derivatives on
any vertical line avoiding the poles. All contour shifts and
differentiations used next are therefore justified on complete lines.

\subsection{ESI5. The actual inverse source and every endpoint
derivative}\label{esi5.-the-actual-inverse-source-and-every-endpoint-derivative}

Define, for \(x>0\), \[
f(x)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}H(2+it)x^{-2-it}\,dt.
\tag{ESI5.1}
\] ESI4 proves absolute convergence and permits all ordinary derivatives
in \(x\). Define the rising product \((s)_0=1\),
\((s)_j=s(s+1)\cdots(s+j-1)\) for \(j\ge1\); then \[
\partial_x^j x^{-s}=(-1)^j(s)_j x^{-s-j}.
\tag{ESI5.2}
\]

For every real \(c>1\), shifting between 2 and \(c\) crosses no poles.
The horizontal integrals tend to zero by ESI4, giving \[
f^{(j)}(x)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
(-1)^j(c+it)_jH(c+it)x^{-c-it-j}\,dt.
\tag{ESI5.3}
\] For \(x\ge1\), this is at most \(C_{c,j,F}x^{-c-j}\) in modulus.
Since \(c\) can be chosen arbitrarily large, every derivative has
arbitrary power decay at infinity.

Now shift the original contour to \(a=-2N-1\), crossing exactly the
possible poles \(-2,-4,\ldots,-2N\). Residue calculus with the original
orientation gives \[
f(x)=\sum_{r=1}^N c_r x^{2r}+R_N(x),
\qquad
R_N(x)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}H(a+it)x^{-a-it}\,dt.
\tag{ESI5.4}
\] Every residue has the positive sign displayed. For every \(j\ge0\),
differentiation under this last integral yields \[
|R_N^{(j)}(x)|\le C_{N,j,F}x^{2N+1-j}
\quad(0<x\le1).
\tag{ESI5.5}
\] For a given derivative order \(j\), choose \(2N\ge j\). The remainder
derivative then tends to zero at 0. The polynomial in (ESI5.4) proves
that every right derivative has a finite limit, equal to zero for odd
\(j\), equal to \((2r)!c_r\) for \(j=2r\ge2\), and zero for \(j=0\).

Here finite right derivative limits really give a smooth extension. For
each \(j\), \(f^{(j+1)}\) is bounded near zero and has a finite limit.
The fundamental theorem of calculus on \([\delta,x]\), followed by
\(\delta\downarrow0\), gives \[
f^{(j)}(x)-\lim_{v\downarrow0}f^{(j)}(v)
=\int_0^x f^{(j+1)}(v)\,dv.
\tag{ESI5.6}
\] This identity proves differentiability at zero with the asserted next
derivative. Iterating in \(j\) proves smoothness on the half-line. All
its odd derivatives there are zero, so the extension \(f(-x)=f(x)\),
\(f(0)=0\), is smooth on \(\mathbb R\). Together with the infinity
estimates, it belongs to Schwartz space.

The complete even Taylor data are \[
\boxed{\frac{f^{(2r)}(0)}{(2r)!}
=\frac{F(-2r)}{2\zeta'(-2r)}\quad(r\ge1),}
\qquad f^{(2r+1)}(0)=0,
\quad f(0)=0.
\tag{ESI5.7}
\] Thus the cancelled trivial-zero locations return as actual source
derivatives; they have not been discarded by the inverse construction.

\subsection{ESI6. Mellin recovery, source moment, and
surjectivity}\label{esi6.-mellin-recovery-source-moment-and-surjectivity}

ESI4 and Cauchy's formula show that \(t\mapsto H(2+it)\) is a Schwartz
function of \(t\). Formula (ESI5.1) says that \(y\mapsto e^{2y}f(e^y)\)
is its inverse Fourier transform with phase \(e^{-ity}\) and factor
\(1/(2\pi)\). Fourier inversion therefore gives \[
\int_0^\infty f(x)x^{2+it}\frac{dx}{x}=H(2+it).
\tag{ESI6.1}
\] By ESI5, the Mellin integral of \(f\) is holomorphic on \(\Re s>-2\).
There are no possible poles of \(H\) in that open half-plane. The
identity theorem applied to (ESI6.1) gives \[
\int_0^\infty f(x)x^s\frac{dx}{x}=H(s)
=\frac{F(s)}{2\zeta(s)}\quad(\Re s>-2),
\tag{ESI6.2}
\] where the quotient uses its holomorphic extensions at the nontrivial
zeros and at 1.

Taking \(s=1\), ESI2 gives \(\int_0^\infty f(x)dx=0\). Evenness then
gives \(\int_{\mathbb R}f=0\). Hence the constructed function is in the
precise source \(\mathcal S\), including both constraints.

The endpoint identities are \[
\int_0^\infty f(x)\frac{dx}{x}=-F(0),
\qquad
\int_0^\infty f(x)\log x\,dx=\frac{F(1)}2.
\tag{ESI6.3}
\] The logarithmic integral is the derivative of (ESI6.2) at 1,
justified by absolute convergence. These factors differ from S6's
centered, half-sum source because the present map is the raw sum
\(2\sum f(nu)\).

By ESI1, \[
\mathcal M_0\Sigma f(s)=2\zeta(s)H(s)=F(s)
\quad(\Re s>1).
\tag{ESI6.4}
\] Both sides are entire, so the identity holds everywhere. Mellin
injectivity now gives \(\Sigma f=\mathcal M_0^{-1}F\). Together with
ESI1's injectivity, this proves the algebraic bijection in (ESI0.8).

\subsection{ESI7. Continuity of the inverse and
closedness}\label{esi7.-continuity-of-the-inverse-and-closedness}

The inverse constructed above is linear. Its continuity follows directly
from the two boundary integrals and the retained residues, without an
unproved uniform estimate for the quotient \(F/F_0\).

Fix a source seminorm \(q_{R,j}\). On \(x\ge1\), choose a real
\(c>\max(1,R)\). Formula (ESI5.3), the polynomial bound on \((c+it)_j\),
and (ESI3.1) bound \[
(1+x)^R|f^{(j)}(x)|
\le C_{R,j,c}\,\beta_{A,j+2}(F)
\quad(x\ge1)
\tag{ESI7.1}
\] for any \(A\ge c\). Indeed \((1+x)^R\le2^Rx^R\) there,
\(x^{R-c-j}\le1\), and \(\int_{\mathbb R}(1+|t|)^{-2}dt<\infty\).

On \(0<x\le1\), choose \(N\) with \(2N+1\ge j\). Formula (ESI5.5)
follows directly from the boundary \(a=-2N-1\), where (ESI3.5) bounds
the reciprocal zeta factor by a fixed constant, and in fact by a
decaying power. Thus \[
\sup_{0<x\le1}|R_N^{(j)}(x)|
\le C_{N,j}\beta_{A,j+2}(F)
\quad(A\ge2N+1).
\tag{ESI7.2}
\] The remaining polynomial derivatives in (ESI5.4) are controlled by
the finitely many continuous evaluations \[
|c_r|\le\frac{\beta_{A,0}(F)}{2|\zeta'(-2r)|},\qquad 1\le r\le N,
\tag{ESI7.3}
\] with \(A\ge2N\). Terms whose degree is below \(j\) have zero \(j\)-th
derivative. All other powers of \(x\) are bounded by 1 on this interval.
The same estimates hold at 0 by the proved smooth extension, and on
negative \(x\) by evenness. Consequently there exist \(A\), \(M\), and a
constant \(C_{R,j}\), independent of \(F\), such that \[
q_{R,j}(f)\le C_{R,j}\beta_{A,M}(F).
\tag{ESI7.4}
\] This proves continuity \(\mathcal I\to\mathcal S\). Combining it with
the continuous Mellin isomorphism and the continuity of \(\Sigma\)
proves (ESI0.8) as a topological isomorphism. Since \(\mathcal I\) is
closed, its inverse Mellin image is closed in \(\mathcal A\). This
proves (ESI0.9).

In particular, if \(C=\overline{\Sigma\mathcal S}^{\mathcal A}\), then
the previously possible difference is exactly \[
C/\Sigma\mathcal S=0.
\tag{ESI7.5}
\] The ordinary quotient \(\mathcal A/\Sigma\mathcal S\) is already
Hausdorff and maps isomorphically to \(\mathcal A/C\) and then to
\(\mathcal B/\mathcal I\). This assertion concerns the specific source
map in (ESI0.5). Any further identification with a differently defined
adelic source must still use its actual proved source comparison.

\subsection{ESI8. The exact Möbius inverse and its relation to the
analytic
inverse}\label{esi8.-the-exact-muxf6bius-inverse-and-its-relation-to-the-analytic-inverse}

For \(b=\Sigma f\), the inverse is also, for every \(x>0\), \[
\boxed{f(x)=\frac12\sum_{n=1}^\infty\mu(n)b(nx).}
\tag{ESI8.1}
\] For every compact interval \(0<\delta\le x\le R\), the series and
each ordinary derivative converge absolutely and uniformly. This follows
from arbitrary power decay of the derivatives of \(b\) at infinity; its
logarithmic seminorms imply those derivative bounds by the identities
expressing \(x^j\partial_x^j\) as a polynomial in \(x\partial_x\). In
particular a sufficiently large exponent controls \(n^j b^{(j)}(nx)\) by
a summable power of \(n\).

Substituting \(b=\Sigma f\) into the right side of (ESI8.1) gives the
absolutely convergent double series \[
\frac12\sum_{n\ge1}\mu(n)\,2\sum_{m\ge1}f(mnx)
=\sum_{k\ge1}\left(\sum_{n\mid k}\mu(n)\right)f(kx)=f(x).
\tag{ESI8.2}
\] Absolute convergence follows, for each fixed \(x>0\), by choosing
\(L>1\) and bounding \(|f(mnx)|\le C_x(mn)^{-L}\); the sum is then at
most \(C_x\zeta(L)^2\). The divisor sum is 1 for \(k=1\), and 0 for
\(k>1\), by the full prime factorization identity
\(\prod_{p\mid k}(1-1)\). This uses the globally reconstructed prime
system, not a truncated list of primes.

For an arbitrary \(b\in\mathcal M_0^{-1}(\mathcal I)\), ESI5--ESI7
already produce the unique Schwartz \(f\) with \(b=\Sigma f\); (ESI8.2)
therefore identifies its pointwise inverse with (ESI8.1). No termwise
evaluation of the Möbius series at zero is asserted. The smooth
extension and all its derivatives at zero are instead proved by
(ESI5.4)--(ESI5.7), which retain the complete trivial-zero residue data.

\subsection{ESI11. The full and moment-null adelic difference

Write \(E=\mathcal S(\mathbb A_{\mathbb Q})\),

\mathcal R=\operatorname{span}\{F-F(q\,\cdot):F\in E,\ q\in\mathbb Q^\times\},
\qquad
\mathcal R_{\rm null}=\operatorname{span}\{F-F(q\,\cdot):F\in E_0,\ q\in\mathbb Q^\times\},
\] \[
D=E_0/\overline{\mathcal R},\qquad
D_{\rm null}=E_0/\overline{\mathcal R_{\rm null}}.
\tag{ESI11.1}
\] The adelic product formula \(|q|_{\mathbb A}=1\) ensures that both
displayed relation spaces lie in \(E_0\). Their difference is therefore
a genuine choice of the generating domain, not a failure of the endpoint
constraints.

Let \(K=\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{\times}\). On each test-function stage,
averaging over \(K\) is a finite averaging operation after taking the
stabilizer of that stage. A \(K\)-invariant finite Schwartz function is
a finite linear combination of the indicators
\(1_{d\widehat{\mathbb Z}}\), \(d\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\). At each
exceptional prime, compact support gives a lower valuation bound and
local constancy gives a terminal ball; their shells are differences of
nested balls. Finite tensor products of these expansions give the global
statement. These ball indicators are linearly independent: evaluations
on successive local shells give an invertible triangular evaluation
matrix, and tensor products preserve independence.

Thus the following maps have unique well-defined formulas: \[
jf=[f\otimes1_{\widehat{\mathbb Z}}],\qquad
B\left(\sum_d f_d\otimes1_{d\widehat{\mathbb Z}}\right)(v)
=\sum_d f_d^{\rm ev}(dv),\qquad B(F)=B(P_KF).
\tag{ESI11.2}

inductive limit. The two exact endpoint identities are \(B(F)(0)=F(0)\)
and \(\int_{\mathbb R}B(F)=\int_{\mathbb A}F\), using

sends the ball index to \(d/|q|\) and its even real function to
\(f^{\rm ev}(|q|v)\), so \(B(F(q\cdot))=B(F)\). Consequently \(B\)
descends to \(\bar B:D\to\mathcal S\).

For a ball tensor \(G=f\otimes1_{d\widehat{\mathbb Z}}\), put
\(H_d=G(d\cdot)\). The complete representative identity is \[
G-\widetilde j(BG)
=(G-G(d\cdot))+\frac12(H_d-H_d(-\cdot))\in\mathcal R.
\tag{ESI11.3}
\] Although individual tensors need not lie in \(E_0\), both differences
in this equation do; summing the equation for a moment-null input before
taking its class is legitimate. It follows that \(\bar Bj=1\) and
\(j\bar B=P_K\) on \(D\). A \(K\)-fixed class is equal to its average
because its representative has a finite averaging orbit. Thus \[
\boxed{D^K\xleftrightarrow[\ j\ ]{\ \bar B\ }\mathcal S}
\tag{ESI11.4}
\] is an actual topological isomorphism. Its overlap sum is precisely
\(2\sum_{n\ge1}f(nu)\), since
\(\mathbb Q\cap\widehat{\mathbb Z}=\mathbb Z\); there is no lost
negative-integer contribution.

To check the entire alternative quotient, let \[
V=\bigoplus_{p\ \mathrm{prime}}\mathbb C^2,
