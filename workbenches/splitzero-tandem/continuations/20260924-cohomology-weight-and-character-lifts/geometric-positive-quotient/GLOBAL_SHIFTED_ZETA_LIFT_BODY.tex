\subsection{GSL1. The Fréchet spaces, the full original multiplier, and
translation}\label{gsl1.-the-fruxe9chet-spaces-the-full-original-multiplier-and-translation}

Let \(\mathcal B\) consist of entire functions for which \[
b_{A,M}(F)=\sup_{|\operatorname{Re}s|\le A}
 (1+|\operatorname{Im}s|)^M|F(s)|<\infty
 \quad(A,M\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}).
\tag{GSL1.1}
\] These seminorms give the complete Fréchet topology proved in S1 and
RZ2. Let \(\mathscr Z\) be the actual distinct nontrivial zeros of the
original \(\zeta\), with full multiplicities \(m_\rho\). They lie in
\(0<\operatorname{Re}\rho<1\); the boundary nonvanishing used here is
the already proved DB9 statement. Set \[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal I&=\{F\in\mathcal B:F^{(j)}(\rho)=0
       \text{ for all }\rho\in\mathscr Z,\ 0\le j<m_\rho\},\\
\mathcal I_-&=\{F\in\mathcal B:F^{(j)}(\rho-1)=0
       \text{ for all }\rho\in\mathscr Z,\ 0\le j<m_\rho\},\\
\mathcal J&=\mathcal I\cap\mathcal I_-,\qquad
\mathcal Q=\mathcal B/\mathcal I,\quad
\mathcal Q_- =\mathcal B/\mathcal I_-,\quad
\mathcal Q_{\cup}=\mathcal B/\mathcal J.
\end{aligned}
\tag{GSL1.2}
\] Every jet evaluation is continuous by Cauchy's formula, so these
ideals are closed. The three quotients carry their actual quotient
Fréchet topologies.

The retained source element is \[
F_0(s)=\frac{s(s-1)}8\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s).
\tag{GSL1.3}
\] It is entire and belongs to \(\mathcal B\), by the original Mellin
calculation S2. Its zeros are exactly \(\mathscr Z\), with their
original multiplicities. The full exceptional values are \[
F_0(0)=F_0(1)=\frac18,
\tag{GSL1.4}
\] \[
\begin{aligned}
F_0(-2r)
 &=\frac{r(2r+1)(-1)^r\pi^r}{2r!}\zeta'(-2r)\\
 &=F_0(1+2r)
 =\frac{(1+2r)(2r)}8\pi^{-(1+2r)/2}
       \Gamma((1+2r)/2)\zeta(1+2r)\ne0\quad(r\ge1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{GSL1.5}
\] These account for the original endpoint factors, Gamma poles and
trivial zeros; none is declared absent from the original function.

Translation defines a topological automorphism of \(\mathcal B\): \[
(UF)(s)=F(s+1),\qquad (U^{-1}F)(s)=F(s-1),\qquad
b_{A,M}(U^{\pm1}F)\le b_{A+1,M}(F).
\tag{GSL1.6}
\] It satisfies \(U\mathcal I=\mathcal I_-\). Consequently it induces a
topological isomorphism \(\bar U:\mathcal Q\to\mathcal Q_-\), with the
same inverse translation.

In particular the shifted full source multiplier is \[
F_1(s)=F_0(s+1)
 =\frac{(s+1)s}{8}\pi^{-(s+1)/2}
       \Gamma((s+1)/2)\zeta(s+1).
\tag{GSL1.7}
\] Its endpoint values are \(F_1(-1)=F_1(0)=1/8\); its values at
\(-2r-1\) and \(2r\) are precisely the two original expressions in
(GSL1.5). Its zero divisor is \((\rho-1,m_\rho)\). The original and
shifted divisors are disjoint because their real parts lie respectively
in \((0,1)\) and \((-1,0)\).

We retain throughout the proof the entire function \[
G(s)=F_0(s)F_1(s),
\tag{GSL1.8}
\] with both full factors (GSL1.3), (GSL1.7). It belongs to
\(\mathcal B\), since multiplication by a fixed member of \(\mathcal B\)
is continuous there. Its zero divisor is exactly the union with all
multiplicities, and \(G(0)=1/64\).

\subsection{GSL2. Polynomial bounds from the original zeta
integral}\label{gsl2.-polynomial-bounds-from-the-original-zeta-integral}

The original identity \[
\zeta(s)=\frac{s}{s-1}-s\int_1^\infty\{x\}x^{-s-1}\,dx
\quad(\operatorname{Re}s>0)
\tag{GSL2.1}
\] has the simple pole at one retained. It follows from the Dirichlet
series by integration of the counting function and then by locally
uniform continuation. On \(3/4\le\operatorname{Re}s\le2\),
\(|\operatorname{Im}s|\ge1\), it and its derivative give constants
\(C_0,C_1\) with \[
|\zeta(s)|\le C_0(1+|\operatorname{Im}s|),\qquad
|\zeta'(s)|\le C_1(1+|\operatorname{Im}s|).
\tag{GSL2.2}
\] For the derivative, the complete formula is \[
\zeta'(s)=-\frac1{(s-1)^2}
 -\int_1^\infty\{x\}x^{-s-1}\,dx
 +s\int_1^\infty\{x\}\log x\,x^{-s-1}\,dx.
\tag{GSL2.3}
\] The two integrals are bounded by \(1/\operatorname{Re}s\) and
\(1/(\operatorname{Re}s)^2\), respectively. This proves (GSL2.2) with
every derivative term retained.

We also need polynomial growth on any fixed vertical strip. It can be
proved without importing a bound in the critical strip. Define
\(P_1(x)=\{x\}-1/2\) away from integers, and recursively let \(P_{j+1}\)
be its periodic antiderivative of mean zero. Each \(P_j\) is bounded,
\(P_j\) is continuous for \(j\ge2\), and \(P_{j+1}'=P_j\) almost
everywhere. To construct it, integrate \(P_j\) over a period and
subtract the mean of the resulting primitive; the zero mean of \(P_j\)
makes the primitive periodic.

Repeated integration by parts in (GSL2.1) gives, for every integer
\(K\ge2\), \[
\zeta(s)=\frac1{s-1}+\frac12
 +\sum_{j=2}^K(s)_{j-1}P_j(1)
 -(s)_K\int_1^\infty P_K(x)x^{-s-K}\,dx,
\quad\operatorname{Re}s>1-K.
\tag{GSL2.4}
\] Here \((s)_j=s(s+1)\cdots(s+j-1)\). For the first step,
\(\int_1^\infty P_1(x)x^{-s-1}dx=-P_2(1)+(s+1)\int_1^\infty P_2(x)x^{-s-2}dx\).
Each following integration gives the next displayed boundary constant
with its positive sign and the final negative remainder. All boundary
terms at infinity vanish initially where the integrals converge
absolutely; the successively enlarged half-planes follow by the new
convergent remainder and uniqueness of continuation. The constants, pole
and \(1/2\) are retained even when some periodic boundary constants are
zero.

For \(|\operatorname{Re}s|\le A\), choose \(K>A+2\). The last integral
is bounded by \(\|P_K\|_\infty/(K-1-A)\). For
\(|\operatorname{Im}s|\ge1\) the pole term is bounded as well, so \[
|\zeta(s)|\le C_A(1+|\operatorname{Im}s|)^{K}
\quad(|\operatorname{Re}s|\le A,\ |\operatorname{Im}s|\ge1).
\tag{GSL2.5}
\] This estimate is used only as an inequality for the original
function; no new working zeta function has been defined.

\subsection{GSL3. A quantitative zero-free corridor around the
separating
line}\label{gsl3.-a-quantitative-zero-free-corridor-around-the-separating-line}

For \(\delta>0\), the absolutely convergent original Euler product gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&3\log\zeta(1+\delta)
 +4\log|\zeta(1+\delta+it)|
 +2\log|\zeta(1+\delta+2it)|\\
&\qquad=\sum_p\sum_{n\ge1}
 \frac{p^{-n(1+\delta)}}n
 \bigl(3+4\cos(nt\log p)+2\cos(2nt\log p)\bigr)\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{GSL3.1}
\] Every prime and positive prime power is present. The summand's
trigonometric factor is exactly \((1+2\cos(nt\log p))^2\).
Exponentiating gives \[
\zeta(1+\delta)^3
 |\zeta(1+\delta+it)|^4
 |\zeta(1+\delta+2it)|^2\ge1.
\tag{GSL3.2}
\] For \(0<\delta\le1/4\), the Dirichlet-series integral bound gives
\(\zeta(1+\delta)\le1+1/\delta\le2/\delta\). Put \(T=1+|t|\), with
\(|t|\ge1\). By (GSL2.2), the last zeta factor is at most a constant
times \(T\). Consequently there is \(c_0>0\), independent of
\(\delta,t\), such that \[
|\zeta(1+\delta+it)|\ge c_0\delta^{3/4}T^{-1/2}.
\tag{GSL3.3}
\] Choose a positive constant \(c\le1/4\) small enough that
\(2C_1c\le(c_0/2)c^{3/4}\), enlarging \(C_1\) in (GSL2.2) if required to
cover the same horizontal segments. Set \(\delta=cT^{-6}\). For every
real \(x\) with \(|x|\le cT^{-6}\), (GSL2.3) integrated along that
segment gives \[
\begin{aligned}
|\zeta(1+x+it)|
&\ge|\zeta(1+\delta+it)|-2C_1T\delta\\
&\ge\frac{c_0c^{3/4}}2T^{-5}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{GSL3.4}
\] Thus the width and lower bound have been derived from the original
function, not assumed as an RH consequence.

The full functional equation gives \(F_0(s)=F_0(1-s)\). For an exact
growth comparison, retain both complete factors after that substitution:
\[
\begin{aligned}
G(s)
={}&\left[\frac{(1-s)(-s)}8
 \pi^{-(1-s)/2}\Gamma((1-s)/2)\zeta(1-s)\right]\\
&\quad\cdot\left[\frac{(1+s)s}8
 \pi^{-(1+s)/2}\Gamma((1+s)/2)\zeta(1+s)\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{GSL3.5}
\] The exact Gamma reflection identity gives the comparison \[
\Gamma((1-s)/2)\Gamma((1+s)/2)
 =\frac\pi{\cos(\pi s/2)}.
\tag{GSL3.6}
\] This identity follows from \(\Gamma(z)\Gamma(1-z)=\pi/\sin(\pi z)\),
with \(z=(1-s)/2\). For example the latter follows from the Euler
products for \(1/\Gamma\) and \(\sin\), multiplying the factors at
\(z,-z\) and then using \(\Gamma(1-z)=-z\Gamma(-z)\). It is a
meromorphic identity retaining both Gamma factors, not a change to the
function in (GSL1.8).

Combining the two powers of \(\pi\) with (GSL3.6) gives the exact
additional expression \[
G(s)=\frac{(1-s)(-s)(1+s)s}{64\cos(\pi s/2)}
 \zeta(1-s)\zeta(1+s).
\tag{GSL3.7}
\] All four endpoint factors, the denominator \(64\), both original zeta
factors and the Gamma comparison remain explicit. At apparent
exceptional points the expression has the entire continuation specified
by (GSL1.3)--(GSL1.8); it is used for estimates only where \(|t|\ge1\).

For \(s=x+it\), \(|t|\ge1\), \(|x|\le cT^{-6}\), the product of the four
endpoint magnitudes is bounded below by a positive constant times
\(T^4\). Further, \[
|\cos(\pi(x+it)/2)|^2
 =\cos^2(\pi x/2)+\sinh^2(\pi t/2).
\tag{GSL3.8}
\] The reciprocal cosine has magnitude comparable to \(e^{-\pi|t|/2}\)
in this region. Apply (GSL3.4) to \(1+x+it\) and to \(1-x-it\);
conjugation preserves the same bound. Therefore \[
|G(x+it)|\ge c_2T^{-6}e^{-\pi|t|/2}
\quad(|t|\ge1,\ |x|\le cT^{-6}).
\tag{GSL3.9}
\] On every fixed vertical strip, (GSL2.5), (GSL3.7) and (GSL3.8) also
give an integer \(k_A\ge0\) and a constant \(C_A\) such that \[
|G(x+it)|\le C_A(1+|t|)^{k_A}e^{-\pi|t|/2}
\quad(|x|\le A,\ t\in\mathbb R).
\tag{GSL3.10}
\] For bounded \(t\), this follows by the entire continuation and
compactness; no pole in (GSL3.7) is retained spuriously.

The line \(x=0\) has no zero of \(G\). For \(t\ne0\), this follows from
DB9 at \(1\pm it\), (GSL3.5), and the absence of a Gamma zero or pole
there. At \(t=0\), it is the exact value \(G(0)=1/64\). Compactness
gives a positive-width zero-free neighborhood of the bounded segment
\(|t|\le1\). Choose \(\varepsilon>0\) sufficiently small, with
\(\varepsilon\le1/4\), so that \[
r(t)=\varepsilon(1+t^2)^{-3}
\tag{GSL3.11}
\] lies within that compact neighborhood and within the corridor in
(GSL3.9) for \(|t|\ge1\). This is possible because
\((1+t^2)^{-3}\le8(1+|t|)^{-6}\). Shrinking the constants to include the
compact segment proves \[
G(x+it)\ne0,\qquad
\frac1{|G(x+it)|}\le C(1+|t|)^6e^{\pi|t|/2}
\quad(|x|\le r(t)).
\tag{GSL3.12}
\] The entire original and shifted zero sets are outside this corridor,
on its right and left respectively. No separation estimate between zeros
within either set was needed.

\subsection{GSL4. An explicit entire separator with controlled strip
growth}\label{gsl4.-an-explicit-entire-separator-with-controlled-strip-growth}

Choose a real smooth function \(\eta\) with \(0\le\eta\le1\),
\(\eta(v)=0\) for \(v\le-1\), and \(\eta(v)=1\) for \(v\ge1\). For an
explicit choice let \(b(v)=0\) for \(v\le0\), \(b(v)=e^{-1/v}\) for
\(v>0\), and take \[
\eta(v)=\frac{b(v+1)}{b(v+1)+b(1-v)}.
\tag{GSL4.1}
\] The denominator is positive everywhere, and the vanishing of all
right derivatives of \(b\) at zero proves the stated smoothness. Define
on the complex plane \[
\chi(x+it)=\eta\bigl(x/r(t)\bigr),\qquad
\bar\partial=\tfrac12(\partial_x+i\partial_t).
\tag{GSL4.2}
\] Its derivative is supported in the corridor. There, \[
\bar\partial\chi(x+it)
 =\tfrac12\eta'(x/r(t))
 \left(\frac1{r(t)}-i\frac{x r'(t)}{r(t)^2}\right).
\tag{GSL4.3}
\] Since \(|x|\le r(t)\), \(r'/r=-6t/(1+t^2)\), and
\(1/r\le C(1+|t|)^6\), we have \[
|\bar\partial\chi(x+it)|\le C(1+|t|)^6.
\tag{GSL4.4}
\] Define the smooth function \[
h(w)=\frac{\bar\partial\chi(w)}{G(w)}
\tag{GSL4.5}
\] on the corridor, extended by zero outside it. Smoothness across its
edges follows because the derivative of \(\eta\) vanishes to every order
there. There is no division at a zero of \(G\). With \(\alpha=\pi/2\),
(GSL3.12) gives \[
\operatorname{supp}h\subset\{|\operatorname{Re}w|\le\varepsilon\},\qquad
|h(u+iv)|\le C e^{\alpha|v|}(1+|v|)^{12}.
\tag{GSL4.6}
\]

Use the explicit Cauchy kernel with its Gaussian factor: \[
u(s)=\frac1\pi\int_{\mathbb C}
 \frac{e^{(s-w)^2}}{s-w}h(w)\,dA(w),
\tag{GSL4.7}
\] where \(dA\) is ordinary planar area. This integral is absolutely
convergent. Its local singularity has order \(1/|s-w|\), which is
integrable in two real dimensions. For the tails, writing
\(s=x+it,w=u+iv\), its exponential modulus is exactly \[
|e^{(s-w)^2}|=e^{(x-u)^2-(t-v)^2}.
\tag{GSL4.8}
\] The negative quadratic in \(t-v\) dominates the fixed exponential and
polynomial in (GSL4.6), while \(|u|\le\varepsilon\).

Here is a uniform strip estimate, including the local singularity. For
\(y\ne0\), integration in \(u\) gives \[
\sup_{x\in\mathbb R}\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}
 \frac{du}{\sqrt{(x-u)^2+y^2}}
 \le C_\varepsilon\left(1+\log_+\frac1{|y|}\right).
\tag{GSL4.9}
\] One can see this by translating the integration interval towards
zero, which increases the integral of the even decreasing function of
\(|x-u|\), and integrating \((v^2+y^2)^{-1/2}\) there. The result is
bounded by a constant plus \(2\log(1/|y|)\) when \(|y|\le1\), and by
\(2\varepsilon/|y|\) otherwise. The infinite value at \(y=0\) is on a
set of one-dimensional measure zero; the displayed logarithm is
integrable near zero.

For \(|x|\le A\), use \((1+|v|)\le(1+|t|)(1+|t-v|)\) and
\(e^{\alpha|v|}\le e^{\alpha|t|}e^{\alpha|t-v|}\).
Equations(GSL4.6)--(GSL4.9) yield \[
|u(x+it)|\le C_A e^{\alpha|t|}(1+|t|)^{12},
\tag{GSL4.10}
\] because the remaining integral \[
\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-y^2+\alpha|y|}(1+|y|)^{12}
 \left(1+\log_+\frac1{|y|}\right)dy
\tag{GSL4.11}
\] is finite, both near zero and at infinity.

The fundamental-solution identity is
\(\bar\partial(1/(\pi s))=\delta_0\). It follows directly by integrating
a compactly supported smooth test function over the punctured plane and
taking the boundary integral on a shrinking circle; the factor \(1/\pi\)
and the convention in (GSL4.2) give mass one. Multiplication by the
holomorphic function \(e^{s^2}\), whose value at zero is one, preserves
that identity. Thus (GSL4.7) satisfies \[
\bar\partial u=h.
\tag{GSL4.12}
\] For precision the identity may first be applied to compactly
supported truncations of \(h\); Gaussian domination makes their
integrals and distributional derivatives converge on each compact set.
Locally the compact Cauchy transform of a smooth function is smooth:
transfer its derivatives to that function using a smooth cutoff, while
the complementary kernel has no singularity. The same Gaussian tail
estimates apply to the complementary part. Hence \(u\) is smooth, and
(GSL4.12) is also a pointwise identity.

Define \[
E(s)=\chi(s)-G(s)u(s).
\tag{GSL4.13}
\] Its \(\bar\partial\) derivative is \(\bar\partial\chi-Gh=0\), so it
is entire. Combining (GSL3.10) and (GSL4.10) proves, for each strip, an
integer \(d_A\ge0\) and constant \(C_A'\) with \[
|E(x+it)|+|1-E(x+it)|
 \le C_A'(1+|t|)^{d_A}\quad(|x|\le A).
\tag{GSL4.14}
\] The exponential factors \(e^{-\pi|t|/2}\) and \(e^{\pi|t|/2}\) have
both been retained in the proof before this estimate is taken.

Every original zero is to the right of the corridor, where \(\chi=1\);
every shifted zero is to the left, where \(\chi=0\). In a neighborhood
of any such zero, \(h=0\), so \(u\) is holomorphic and (GSL4.13) gives
the complete jet identities \[
\begin{aligned}
E(s)-1&\in(s-\rho)^{m_\rho}\mathcal O_\rho,\\
E(s)&\in(s-(\rho-1))^{m_\rho}\mathcal O_{\rho-1}
\qquad(\rho\in\mathscr Z).
\end{aligned}
\tag{GSL4.15}
\] Every multiplicity is preserved. The constructed function \(E\) is a
multiplier of \(\mathcal B\); it is not assumed to be a member of
\(\mathcal B\). This distinction matters because it takes the value one
at the entire original zero set.

\subsection{GSL5. Continuous multiplication and the full CRT
inverse}\label{gsl5.-continuous-multiplication-and-the-full-crt-inverse}

Equation(GSL4.14) gives the explicit seminorm estimates \[
b_{A,M}(EF)\le C_A'b_{A,M+d_A}(F),\qquad
b_{A,M}((1-E)F)\le C_A'b_{A,M+d_A}(F).
\tag{GSL5.1}
\] Thus both are continuous linear operators
\(\mathcal B\to\mathcal B\). Their full ideal behavior is \[
\begin{array}{lll}
E\mathcal B\subset\mathcal I_-, & (1-E)\mathcal B\subset\mathcal I,\\
E\mathcal I\subset\mathcal J, & (1-E)\mathcal I_-\subset\mathcal J.
\end{array}
\tag{GSL5.2}
\] Each inclusion follows from the appropriate two sets of jets in
(GSL4.15) and the full Leibniz formula. In particular \[
F=EF+(1-E)F\quad\text{proves}\quad
\mathcal I+\mathcal I_-=\mathcal B,
\tag{GSL5.3}
\] with an explicit continuous choice of the two summands.

The canonical map and its constructed inverse are \[
\Phi:\mathcal Q_{\cup}\longrightarrow\mathcal Q\oplus\mathcal Q_-,
\qquad [F]_{\mathcal J}\longmapsto([F]_{\mathcal I},[F]_{\mathcal I_-}),
\tag{GSL5.4}
\] \[
\Psi([f]_{\mathcal I},[g]_{\mathcal I_-})
 =[Ef+(1-E)g]_{\mathcal J}.
\tag{GSL5.5}
\] The final two inclusions in (GSL5.2) prove independence of the
representatives in (GSL5.5). At every original zero its representative
has exactly the jets of \(f\); at every shifted zero it has exactly the
jets of \(g\). Thus \(\Phi\Psi=1\). For a common representative \(F\),
its image under the opposite composition is
\([EF+(1-E)F]_{\mathcal J}=[F]_{\mathcal J}\), so \(\Psi\Phi=1\). This
proves surjectivity onto the full direct sum of the two actual quotient
spaces, not merely a dense range.

For explicit topological control, let \(q_{A,M}^{\mathcal K}\) be the
quotient seminorm obtained from \(b_{A,M}\) by a closed subspace

\subsection{GSL6. Original arithmetic action, degree shift, and

On \(\mathcal B\) use the actual operators \[

\tag{GSL6.1}

\qquad L_-\bar U=\bar U(L-1).

\] For example \(U(sF)(s)=(s+1)F(s+1)\), proving the second identity
including its sign. Thus (GSL5.4) also gives a topological

Then \(w_a(a^{\rho-1})=2\operatorname{Re}\rho-2\) and
\(w_a(a^\rho)=2\operatorname{Re}\rho\), so their logarithmic-modulus
weights occupy \((-2,0)\) and \((0,2)\), respectively, without any
critical-line assumption. The action itself remains defined for every
\(a>0\), including its identity at \(a=1\); no weight is assigned using
a denominator \(\log1\).

There is also an exact version with the higher-weight term as the
kernel, obtained by translating the proved construction, rather than
positing another splitting. Set \[
\mathcal I_+=U^{-1}\mathcal I
 =\{F\in\mathcal B:F^{(j)}(\rho+1)=0\ (0\le j<m_\rho)\},
\quad\mathcal Q_+=\mathcal B/\mathcal I_+,
\quad\mathcal Q_{\cup,+}=\mathcal B/(\mathcal I\cap\mathcal I_+).
\tag{GSL6.5}

\(\mathcal I_+\cap\mathcal I\) and gives a topological character
comparison from \(\mathcal Q_{\cup}(-1)\) to \(\mathcal Q_{\cup,+}\).
Its full translated multiplier remains \[
G(s-1)=
\left[\frac{(s-1)(s-2)}8\pi^{-(s-1)/2}

estimate follows from (GSL4.14) with \(A+1\) in place of \(A\). Applying

continuously split row \[

 \longrightarrow\mathcal Q_{\cup,+}
 \longrightarrow\mathcal Q\longrightarrow0.
\tag{GSL6.7}
\] The section is
\([f]_{\mathcal I}\mapsto[E_+f]_{\mathcal I\cap\mathcal I_+}\); the
inclusion of the kernel uses \(1-E_+\). On its kernel, translation gives
\(a^{\rho+1}\exp((\log a)M_w)\), retaining every nilpotent coefficient.
The original scalar weights are in \((0,2)\), the kernel weights in
\((2,4)\). Thus the higher-weight version of the separated lift is also
realized on a fully specified Fréchet quotient of the original source,
with both original and translated full factors retained.

Here are the full maps and exceptional values for that counterpart.
Write \(VF(s)=F(s-1)\), so \(V=U^{-1}\), and put \[
F_{-1}(s)=F_0(s-1)
 =\frac{(s-1)(s-2)}8\pi^{-(s-1)/2}
       \Gamma((s-1)/2)\zeta(s-1).
\tag{GSL6.8}
\] Its endpoint values are \(F_{-1}(1)=F_{-1}(2)=1/8\). The entire

 &=\frac{r(2r+1)(-1)^r\pi^r}{2r!}\zeta'(-2r)\\

Translation induces \(\bar V:\mathcal Q\to\mathcal Q_+\), with inverse

T_{a,+}\bar V=a\bar VT_a,
\qquad L_+\bar V=\bar V(L+1).
\tag{GSL6.10}
\] For instance \(V(sF)(s)=(s-1)F(s-1)\), so \(sVF=V(sF)+VF\); also
\(V(a^sF)(s)=a^{s-1}F(s-1)\), giving exactly the first factor in

\(\mathcal Q(-1)\) to \(\mathcal Q_+\). The high-weight row, including
its injection from that domain, its quotient map and its section, is \[
\begin{aligned}
j_{\mathrm{high}}([h]_{\mathcal I})
 &=[(1-E_+(s))h(s-1)]_{\mathcal I\cap\mathcal I_+},\\
\pi_{\mathrm{high}}([F]_{\mathcal I\cap\mathcal I_+})
 &=[F]_{\mathcal I},\\
s_{\mathrm{high}}([f]_{\mathcal I})
 &=[E_+(s)f(s)]_{\mathcal I\cap\mathcal I_+}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{GSL6.11}
\] The first displayed domain is the underlying vector space of
\(\mathcal Q(-1)\), with its specified twisted action. The ideal
identities follow by the same two full jet conditions on \(E_+\),
proving independence of every representative. The CRT inverse applied to
\(([f]_{\mathcal I},\bar V[h]_{\mathcal I})\) is the sum of the first

The equivariant section is unique in a precise continuous linear
category. Let \(H:\mathcal Q\to\mathcal Q_-\) be a continuous linear map
commuting with \(L\). For \(\lambda=\rho-1\) and \(m=m_\rho\), the first
\(m\) jets of \((L_--\lambda)^mH(x)\) vanish at \(\lambda\). By
linearity and intertwining these are the same jets as those of

Hence \((L-\lambda)^m\) is onto \(\mathcal Q\), so every jet of order
less than \(m\) of every \(H(x)\) vanishes at \(\lambda\). This holds
for all shifted zeros. The definition of \(\mathcal I_-\) implies
\(H=0\). The identical argument with \(\lambda=\rho+1\notin\mathscr Z\)
proves that every continuous linear \(L\)-intertwiner
\(\mathcal Q\to\mathcal Q_+\) is zero.

Consequently two continuous linear \(L\)-equivariant sections of
\(\pi_+\) must coincide: their difference lands in
\(\ker\pi_+=\iota_-\mathcal Q_-\), and the preceding argument applies.
The same proof gives uniqueness for the high-weight row. A continuous
linear map commuting with every recovered positive rational \(T_a\) also
commutes with the real group and its generator \(L\). Indeed the
positive rationals are dense in the positive reals after the full
arithmetic is identified, RZ8.5 proves strong continuity and
differentiability, and continuity of the map permits passage first to
the real parameter limit and then to its derivative. The same uniqueness
therefore holds for continuous linear sections respecting the full
recovered arithmetic action. This proof uses the actual global resolvent
and the separating jet family, not a claimed convergence of an infinite
sum of finite projectors.

Different allowed cutoffs or corridor constants in GSL4 may produce

continuous quotient lift is therefore independent of those auxiliary
choices.

\subsection{GSL7. Finite-source projectors inside the actual global
