\subsection{GTC1. The full quotient map and
topology}\label{gtc1.-the-full-quotient-map-and-topology}

With the actual ideals \(\mathcal I,\mathcal I_+\) and
\(\mathcal J_+=\mathcal I\cap\mathcal I_+\), the canonical continuous
CRT map is \[
C:\mathcal B/\mathcal J_+\to
(\mathcal B/\mathcal I)\oplus(\mathcal B/\mathcal I_+).
\] Translation \(U:F(s)\mapsto F(s+1)\) induces the topological
isomorphism \(\bar U:\mathcal Q_+\to\mathcal Q\). The proposed map is
exactly \[
\Theta=(1\oplus\bar U)C
\] followed by the stated two-cell geometric coordinate identification.
Its inverse is \[
(x,y)\mapsto
[E_+(s)f(s)+(1-E_+(s))g(s-1)]_{\mathcal J_+}
\] for any representatives \(f,g\in\mathcal B\) of \(x,y\).

If \(f\) changes by \(\mathcal I\), its multiplied difference vanishes
on the original divisor by that ideal and on the shifted divisor by
\(E_+\). If \(g\) changes by \(\mathcal I\), its translate vanishes on
the shifted divisor, while \(1-E_+\) supplies the original divisor. Thus
the inverse is defined on both quotient classes. The two jet identities
for \(E_+\) prove both compositions are identities, including every
derivative through \(m_\rho-1\).

For an explicit topology check, let \(q^{\mathcal K}_{A,M}\) be the
quotient seminorm induced by \(b_{A,M}\), and let multiplication by
\(E_+\), \(1-E_+\) have bound \(C_A b_{A,M+d_A}\). Then \[
q^{\mathcal J_+}_{A,M}(\Theta^{-1}(x,y))
\le C_A\left(
q^{\mathcal I}_{A,M+d_A}(x)
+q^{\mathcal I}_{A+1,M+d_A}(y)\right).
\] This follows by choosing representatives independently, applying the
multiplier and translation estimates, and taking their two infima. The
forward map is bounded by the quotient seminorms with strip indices
\(A\) and \(A+1\). Hence the claimed inverse is continuous in the actual
quotient topology.

\subsection{GTC3. The geometric degree and
representation}\label{gtc3.-the-geometric-degree-and-representation}

Write \(b_n(z)=z^n\), \(I(z)=-1/z\), and \(c(n)=(n-1)/2\bmod2\) for odd
\(n\). Then \(I^2=1\), \(b_nI=Ib_n\), and \(c(mn)=c(m)+c(n)\bmod2\).
Consequently the CC maps \(f_n=I^{c(n)}b_n\) satisfy \(f_mf_n=f_{mn}\),
including all four congruence cases.

Their degree is \(n\): the polynomial map pulls \(\mathcal O(1)\) back
to \(\mathcal O(n)\), and \(I\) is a projective linear automorphism of
degree \(1\). Thus \(f_n^*h=nh\), with positive sign and the specified
integral orientation.

On the shifted quotient, \[
U(T_nF)(s)=n^{s+1}F(s+1)=nT_n(UF)(s).
\] It follows that \(\Theta\) intertwines \(T_n\) with
\(T_n\oplus nT_n\). At a multiplicity-\(m_\rho\) point this identity
reads \[
n^{\rho+1}\sum_{j=0}^{m_\rho-1}
\frac{(\log n)^j}{j!}t^j
=n\left(n^\rho\sum_{j=0}^{m_\rho-1}
\frac{(\log n)^j}{j!}t^j\right).
\] The coordinates on the two germs are identified by translation with
no sign change. This verifies all jets and every factorial. It also
proves the three identities for the kernel inclusion, quotient

\subsection{GTC4. Original factors and exceptional

\frac{r(2r+1)(-1)^r\pi^r}{2r!}\zeta'(-2r).

\subsection{GTC5. Products and the coefficient-unit
distinction}\label{gtc5.-products-and-the-coefficient-unit-distinction}

Both \(\mathcal B\) and its quotient are being used as algebras without
an assumed identity. The geometric notation must be read as \[
x1+yh:=x\otimes1+y\otimes h,
\] where \(1,h\) are the scalar geometric cohomology basis. It must not
mean that \(\mathcal Q\) has an element \(1\) supplied by an entire
constant function: that constant is not in \(\mathcal B\).

Indeed the actual unbounded zero set also precludes a multiplicative
identity in \(\mathcal Q\). If \([u]\) were such an identity, evaluate
its product with \(e^{(s-\rho)^2}\in\mathcal B\) at any zero \(\rho\).
Since this test function has value \(1\) there, \(u(\rho)=1\). This
holds for every zero, contradicting the rapid decay of \(u\) on a strip
containing \(0<\Re s<1\) along the unbounded zero ordinates. This
observation does not affect any of the continuous linear comparison
maps.

The multiplication in the quotient is transported to coordinatewise
multiplication because both reduction and translation preserve products:
\[
(x,y)\diamond(x',y')=(xx',yy').
\] The geometric cup product with these coefficients is instead \[
(x,y)\smile(x',y')=(xx',xy'+yx').
\] Their difference is the second-coordinate class \(yy'-xy'-yx'\),
exactly GTZ7.3. The identities remain valid for nonunital coefficient
algebras. The monoid operators \(T_n\) are linear representation
operators; they are not asserted to be algebra homomorphisms for either
product.

With the explicit tensor interpretation of the geometric basis, the
comparison is mathematically valid in the stated category. Its geometric
Tate factor is proved; the original spectral coefficient purity is not
inferred from that factor.
