## PTQ1. The closed space generated by the positive-adjoint defects

Define the actual subsets
\[
\mathscr Z_{\rm line}=\{\rho\in\mathscr Z:\Re\rho=1/2\},
\qquad\mathscr Z_{\rm off}=\mathscr Z\setminus\mathscr Z_{\rm line},
\]
\[
H_{\rm line}=\{x\in H:x_\rho=0\text{ for }\rho\in\mathscr Z_{\rm off}\},
\qquad H_{\rm off}=H_{\rm line}^{\perp}.
\tag{PTQ1.1}
\]
These definitions are applied to the actual full zero set. They assume neither an off-line zero nor its absence. Denote the respective orthogonal projections by \(P_{\rm line}\) and \(P_{\rm off}\).

For each recovered \(n>1\), monotonicity of \(x\mapsto n^x\) proves
\[
\ker D_n=\ker(D_n^*D_n)=H_{\rm line},
\qquad
\overline{\operatorname{Ran}D_n}
=\overline{\operatorname{Ran}(D_n^*D_n)}=H_{\rm off}.
\tag{PTQ1.2}
\]
Indeed the multiplier in (PTQ0.4) is zero exactly at \(\sigma=1/2\). Both ranges are supported in \(\mathscr Z_{\rm off}\). Conversely each coordinate vector at an off-line zero is in either range, by dividing that single vector by its specified nonzero scalar multiplier. Finite coordinate vectors are dense in the stated closed coordinate subspace; this proves the closure assertions. No uniformly bounded reciprocal multiplier is presumed, so the ranges themselves are not declared closed.

In particular the closed span of the ranges of all the actual defects, or of all their positive squares, is exactly \(H_{\rm off}\). The entire-family quotient is therefore independent of which recovered \(n>1\) is used to express this same closed space.

The projections can also be obtained from the positive defect itself by the exact strong limits
\[
P_{\rm line}=\operatorname*{s-lim}_{r\to\infty}
\exp(-rD_n^*D_n),\qquad
P_{\rm off}=\operatorname*{s-lim}_{r\to\infty}
\bigl(I-\exp(-rD_n^*D_n)\bigr).
\tag{PTQ1.3}
\]
For every coordinate, the exponential tends to one if its defect is zero, and to zero otherwise. Its modulus is at most one. Applying dominated convergence to the complete weighted square sum of any fixed \(x\in H\) proves strong convergence. This constructs the closed quotient from the actual bounded positive operators, without selecting a numerical zero cutoff or assuming an entire-function projector.

## PTQ3. Universal property among bounded intertwiners to positive adjoint representations

Here is the category in which maximality is proved. A receiving Hilbert representation consists of a Hilbert space \(K\) and bounded operators \(A_n,B_n\), indexed by the recovered positive integers, with the full relations (PTQ0.3) after replacing \(T,U\) by \(A,B\), with \(A_1=B_1=I\), and with \(B_n=A_n^*\). A map from the actual representation is a bounded linear operator \(X:H\to K\) satisfying
\[
XT_n=A_nX,\qquad XU_n=B_nX
\quad\text{for every recovered }n\ge1.
\tag{PTQ3.1}
\]
No surjectivity, isometry, simplicity of zeros, or finite dimension of the target is required.

Every such map has a unique bounded factorization
\[
\boxed{X=Yq_{\rm pos},\qquad
Y:H_{\rm pos}\to K,}
\tag{PTQ3.2}
\]
and \(Y\) intertwines every original retained pullback and transfer. Under the identification with \(H_{\rm line}\), it is simply \(X|_{H_{\rm line}}\), and \(\|Y\|=\|X\|\).

To prove the assertion, let \(\varepsilon_\rho\) be the coordinate vector with value one at \(\rho\). Its squared norm is \(m_\rho\); no multiplicity is suppressed. Put \(v=X\varepsilon_\rho\). Then \(A_nv=n^\rho v\), while \(A_n^*A_n=nI\) gives
\[
n\|v\|_K^2=\|A_nv\|_K^2=n^{2\Re\rho}\|v\|_K^2.
\tag{PTQ3.3}
\]
For \(\rho\in\mathscr Z_{\rm off}\) and any recovered \(n>1\), the two real scalars differ, so \(v=0\). Boundedness of \(X\) then kills the closure of all off-line coordinate vectors, namely \(H_{\rm off}\). This proves existence of the bounded factorization through the Hilbert quotient. The quotient is onto, so its factor is unique. Restriction to the reducing subspace proves the intertwining assertions, and \(X=XP_{\rm line}\) proves equality of norms.

This universal property proves maximality rather than merely exhibiting one positive receiver: every bounded positive-adjoint receiver of the unchanged geometric transfer must discard at least this entire closed space, and the quotient constructed in PTQ2 discards exactly that space. In fact the proof needs only (PTQ3.1) and \(B_n=A_n^*\) on the closed span of \(X(H)\); the original degree relation then implies \(A_n^*A_n=nI\) there by density. The category stated above retains all original relations on the whole receiving space.

## PTQ4. All bounded positive transfer-adjoint forms on the original value space

The universal quotient has a complete form-level classification. Let \(B\) be a bounded positive semidefinite Hermitian form on \(H\), linear in its first variable. The condition
\[
B(T_nx,y)=B(x,U_ny)\quad\text{for every }n\ge1
\tag{PTQ4.1}
\]
is equivalent to
\[
B(T_nx,T_ny)=nB(x,y)\quad\text{for every }n\ge1.
\tag{PTQ4.2}
\]
One implication uses \(U_nT_n=nI\). Conversely replace \(y\) in (PTQ4.2) by \(T_n^{-1}y\), retaining the real scalar \(n\), and use \(U_n=nT_n^{-1}\).

All such bounded forms are precisely
\[
\boxed{B(x,y)=\sum_{\rho\in\mathscr Z_{\rm line}}
m_\rho c_\rho x_\rho\overline{y_\rho},
\qquad 0\le c_\rho\le C\text{ for a finite }C.}
\tag{PTQ4.3}
\]
Here is a full proof. Substituting \(x=y=\varepsilon_\rho\) in (PTQ4.2) forces the diagonal value to be zero at every off-line zero, by the same scalar argument as (PTQ3.3). Positivity implies Cauchy–Schwarz: expanding \(B(x+zy,x+zy)\ge0\) and minimizing its quadratic in the complex scalar \(z\) proves \(|B(x,y)|^2\le B(x,x)B(y,y)\). Thus each off-line coordinate pairs to zero with every vector.

For two distinct critical-line zeros \(\rho=1/2+i\gamma\), \(\eta=1/2+i\gamma'\), (PTQ4.2) says
\[
\bigl(e^{i(\gamma-\gamma')\log n}-1\bigr)
B(\varepsilon_\rho,\varepsilon_\eta)=0
\quad\text{for every recovered }n\ge1.
\tag{PTQ4.4}
\]
If this cross value were nonzero, every displayed phase would be one. Ratios at consecutive integers would then give
\(e^{i(\gamma-\gamma')\log((n+1)/n)}=1\) for every \(n\). If \(\gamma-\gamma'\ne0\), the absolute value of its real exponent is strictly between zero and \(2\pi\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), a contradiction. Hence the frequencies would be equal, and two critical-line zeros with equal frequency are the same point. Thus every distinct cross value vanishes. This argument uses the entire recovered integer family, rather than taking a finite pair of named primes as the source of arithmetic.

Set \(c_\rho=B(\varepsilon_\rho,\varepsilon_\rho)/m_\rho\) on the line. Positivity gives \(c_\rho\ge0\), and boundedness of the form gives a common upper bound. Its values on finite coordinate sums are exactly (PTQ4.3). Density of those vectors in \(H\), and boundedness of the form and of the displayed weighted sum, prove the identity on all of \(H\). Conversely every family in (PTQ4.3) defines a bounded positive semidefinite form and satisfies (PTQ4.1) directly.

The maximal form within the bound \(B(x,x)\le\|x\|_+^2\) is therefore \(\langle P_{\rm line}x,P_{\rm line}y\rangle_+\). More generally any form in (PTQ4.3) is bounded above by a constant times this form. This is a classification for bounded forms on the specified actual Hilbert space; no unrestricted form on an untopologized Ext group is being classified. For every intertwiner in PTQ3, its pulled-back form \(\langle Xx,Xy\rangle_K\) belongs to this list.

## PTQ5. The full Weil form does not automatically descend through this quotient

The original form remains
\[

+\langle P_{\rm off}x,J P_{\rm off}y\rangle_+.}
\tag{PTQ5.1}
\]

The second term is the complete discarded indefinite form, with its domain \(H_{\rm off}\) and involution retained.

A form descends through a vector-space quotient exactly when its kernel space pairs to zero with every vector: this is necessary by replacing either argument by an equivalent representative, and sufficient by the same calculation. In this case \(W\) is nondegenerate on \(H\), since \(J\) is invertible. Therefore
\[
W\text{ descends through }q_{\rm pos}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H_{\rm off}=0.
\tag{PTQ5.2}
\]
One can check the failure on every applicable actual pair without invoking general nondegeneracy. If \(\rho\in\mathscr Z_{\rm off}\), then \(\rho^\#\ne\rho\), both coordinate vectors are killed by \(q_{\rm pos}\), but
\[
W(\varepsilon_\rho,\varepsilon_{\rho^\#})=m_\rho\ne0.
\tag{PTQ5.3}

Their two combinations have
\[
W(\varepsilon_\rho+\varepsilon_{\rho^\#},

\[
W(\varepsilon_\rho-\varepsilon_{\rho^\#},
\varepsilon_\rho-\varepsilon_{\rho^\#})=-2m_\rho.

The universal positive-transfer quotient discards both directions of each such orbit, including its positive direction. ## PTQ8. Multiplicity trace of the retained and discarded sectors

Human-source attribution: the unconditional zero-counting estimate is the classical Riemann–von Mangoldt theorem. The inspected native-TeX witness is Alain Connes, [The Riemann Hypothesis: Past, Present and a Letter Through Time](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04022v1), the Hardy–Littlewood discussion immediately before “Zero-free regions and zero-density estimates” (author TeX line499). It states the asymptotic that implies the bound used here; this is not a new zero-counting theorem.

The required trace is the original multiplicity-weighted diagonal trace, not the ordinary Hilbert operator trace after rescaling an orthonormal basis:
\[
\operatorname{tr}_\zeta(M_f)=\sum_\rho m_\rho f(\rho).
\tag{PTQ8.1}
\]
For nonnegative diagonal functions it is allowed to take the value \(+\infty\); for complex functions it is used when the absolute weighted sum converges. Its multiplicity is the trace of multiplication on the full local algebra \(\mathbb C[t]/t^{m_\rho}\), whose diagonal entries are all \(f(\rho)\), and is also the residue of \(f\zeta'/\zeta\) for a holomorphic local germ. This explains the factor \(m_\rho\) without replacing the entire jet algebra by a single trace coordinate.

For every nonnegative diagonal observable,
\[
\operatorname{tr}_\zeta(M_f)
=\operatorname{tr}_\zeta(P_{\rm line}M_f)
+\operatorname{tr}_\zeta(P_{\rm off}M_f),
\]
\[
\operatorname{tr}_\zeta(P_{\rm line}M_f)=
\sum_{\rho\in\mathscr Z_{\rm line}}m_\rho f(\rho),
\quad
\operatorname{tr}_\zeta(P_{\rm off}M_f)=
\sum_{\rho\in\mathscr Z_{\rm off}}m_\rho f(\rho).
\tag{PTQ8.2}
\]
The same identity holds for absolutely summable complex observables. In particular for every \(t>0\), the strictly positive weight \(k_t(\rho)=e^{-t(\Im\rho)^2}\) gives finite quantities on both sectors. The unconditional count \(\sum_{|\Im\rho|\le R}m_\rho=O(R\log(R+2))\) bounds the complete sum by a convergent sum of a polynomial-logarithmic factor times \(e^{-tj^2}\). Thus the discarded trace is the exact nonnegative quantity
\[
\operatorname{tr}_\zeta(P_{\rm off}M_{k_t})
=\sum_{\rho\in\mathscr Z_{\rm off}}m_\rho e^{-t\gamma^2}
=\sum_{\{\rho,\rho^\#\}\subset\mathscr Z_{\rm off}}
2m_\rho e^{-t\gamma^2}.
\tag{PTQ8.3}
\]
The second sum is over the actual two-element reflection orbits, each once. It is zero exactly when that actual sector is empty, because every weight is strictly positive. Its value has not been assumed zero.

Every positive-adjoint defect is entirely in the discarded sector:
\[
P_{\rm line}D_n^*D_n=0,
\qquad P_{\rm off}D_n^*D_n=D_n^*D_n.
\]
\[
\operatorname{tr}_\zeta(M_{k_t}D_n^*D_n)
=\sum_{\rho\in\mathscr Z_{\rm off}}
m_\rho e^{-t\gamma^2}(n^\sigma-n^{1-\sigma})^2.
\tag{PTQ8.4}
\]
The sum converges because the squared scalar is bounded by \((n-1)^2\). Within each two-point orbit the two terms are equal and add. The zero weighted trace of the off-diagonal exchange matrix \(J\) on such a two-dimensional orbit does not remove this positive trace or its two value directions: its matrix is \(\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\), while the positive trace of the identity on that orbit, with the original multiplicity, is \(2m_\rho\). The two signs in (PTQ5.4) therefore cannot be discarded merely because the exchange matrix has zero trace.

The functions \(k_t\) and the sector indicators are value observables. No assertion here inserts these nonholomorphic functions into the original holomorphic prime explicit formula. The complete original arithmetic of the removed form is a separate source comparison, for which the exact removed term is already (PTQ7.5).

## PTQ11. Established outcome and exact remaining source question

The quotient of the actual positive-adjoint defect has now been constructed and proved universal in the stated bounded Hilbert category. Its kernel is exactly the closed off-line coordinate space; its retained operators satisfy the original geometric degree and every integer composition law, and every bounded positive-adjoint receiver factors through it uniquely.
