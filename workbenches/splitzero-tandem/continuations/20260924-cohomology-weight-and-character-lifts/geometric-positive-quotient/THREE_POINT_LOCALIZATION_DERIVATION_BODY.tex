The instruction

reread before this derivation. These are sheaves of complex vector
spaces after their coefficients and arithmetic have been constructed. No
finite prime prefix defines those coefficients. No equality
\(\tau=\eta\), addition on \(\tau\), numerical coordinate on \(\tau\),

Complexes are cohomological, with \(C[k]^n=C^{n+k}\),
\(d_{C[k]}=(-1)^k d_C\). For \(f:C\to D\), use
\(\operatorname{Cone}(f)^n=D^n\oplus C^{n+1}\), with differential
\((y,x)\mapsto(d_Dy+fx,-d_Cx)\). Thus
\(\operatorname{Cone}(r:A[0]\to B[0])[-1]\) is \(A\xrightarrow{-r}B\) in
degrees \(0,1\). For the cone triangle take the final arrow to be
projection to \(C[1]\). Under the cone-to-quotient identification for a
short exact sequence, its connecting representative is minus the
differential of a lift: a cone cocycle \((y,x)\) satisfies
\(d_Dy+fx=0\). This records the connecting-arrow convention explicitly;
the opposite lift-and-differentiate convention changes those arrows by a
minus sign. The ordinary vector-space calculations come first;
TPL11--TPL12 specify the topological qualifications.

\subsection{TPL1. The diagram describing every
sheaf}\label{tpl1.-the-diagram-describing-every-sheaf}

A sheaf \(F\) is specified by \[
A_+=F(U_+),\quad A_-=F(U_-),\quad B=F(U_\eta),
\qquad r_\pm:A_\pm\to B.
\tag{TPL1.1}
\] Its global sections are \[
\Gamma(X,F)=A_+\times_BA_-=
\{(a_+,a_-):r_+a_+=r_-a_-\}.
\tag{TPL1.2}
\] Indeed the sheaf condition for \(X=U_+\cup U_-\) is exactly (TPL1.2).
Every cover of \(U_+\) contains \(U_+\), because no smaller open
contains \(+\); similarly for \(U_-\), and a cover of \(U_\eta\)
contains \(U_\eta\). No additional gluing condition occurs. Conversely,
(TPL1.2) and the three assigned spaces therefore define a sheaf on every
open.

The stalks are \(F_+=A_+\), \(F_-=A_-\), \(F_\eta=B\), since each point
has the displayed smallest open neighborhood. Morphisms are triples
commuting with \(r_\pm\). Kernels and cokernels are computed at these
stalks. This proves the equivalence with the category of diagrams
\(A_+\to B\leftarrow A_-\).

\subsection{TPL2. An explicit resolution and complete Čech
cohomology}\label{tpl2.-an-explicit-resolution-and-complete-ux10dech-cohomology}

For any vector space \(V\), put \[
I_+(V)=(V\to0\leftarrow0),\quad
I_-(V)=(0\to0\leftarrow V),\quad
I_\eta(V)=(V\xrightarrow{1}V\xleftarrow{1}V).
\tag{TPL2.1}
\] The displayed order is plus stalk, generic stalk, minus stalk. A
morphism \(F\to I_+(V)\) is uniquely a map \(A_+\to V\); similarly for
minus. A morphism \(F\to I_\eta(V)\) is uniquely a map \(B\to V\), its
other components being obtained by restriction. These functors are right
adjoint to the three exact stalk evaluations. Since linear maps from a
subspace extend after choosing a basis, every vector space is injective;
the adjunctions therefore prove that each sheaf in (TPL2.1) is
injective.

There is the injective resolution \[
0\to F\to
I^0=I_+(A_+)\oplus I_-(A_-)\oplus I_\eta(B)
\xrightarrow D I^1=I_+(B)\oplus I_-(B)\to0.
\tag{TPL2.2}
\] At plus, the injection is \(a\mapsto(a,r_+a)\) into \(A_+\oplus B\),
and \(D_+(a,b)=b-r_+a\). At minus the formulas use \(r_-\). At \(\eta\)
the injection is the identity \(B\to B\), followed by zero. Restrictions
in \(I^0\) project onto the \(B\) coordinate. These formulas commute
with restriction and are exact at every stalk, proving (TPL2.2).

Global sections of this resolution give \[
R\Gamma(X,F)=
[A_+\oplus A_-\oplus B\xrightarrow D B^2],
\quad D(a_+,a_-,b)=(b-r_+a_+,b-r_-a_-).
\tag{TPL2.3}
\] It maps onto the Čech complex \[
C(F)=[A_+\oplus A_-\xrightarrow d B],
\qquad d(a_+,a_-)=r_+a_+-r_-a_-,
\tag{TPL2.4}
\] by forgetting \(b\) in degree zero and by \[
(v_+,v_-)\mapsto v_--v_+
\tag{TPL2.5}
\] in degree one. Applying (TPL2.5) to \(D\) gives \(d\), so this is a
chain map. Its kernel is the acyclic complex
\(B\xrightarrow{b\mapsto(b,b)}\operatorname{diag}B\). Hence it is a
quasi-isomorphism. It follows that \[
H^0(X,F)=A_+\times_BA_-,\qquad
H^1(X,F)=B/(r_+A_++r_-A_-),\qquad H^q(X,F)=0\ (q\ge2).
\tag{TPL2.6}
\] The image here is the algebraic sum of the actual images, with no
closure.

\subsection{TPL3. Open and closed
functors}\label{tpl3.-open-and-closed-functors}

Let \(j:\{\eta\}\hookrightarrow X\), \(i:Z=\{+,-\}\hookrightarrow X\).
The topology on \(Z\) is discrete. Directly on diagrams, \[
j^*F=B,\quad j_*V=(V\xrightarrow1V\xleftarrow1V),
\quad j_!V=(0\to V\leftarrow0),
\tag{TPL3.1}
\] \[
i^*F=(A_+,A_-),\quad
i_*(W_+,W_-)=(W_+\to0\leftarrow W_-),\quad
i^!F=(\ker r_+,\ker r_-).
\tag{TPL3.2}
\] Every nonempty open meets \(\eta\), giving the formula for \(j_*\).
Extension by zero has zero closed stalks, giving \(j_!\). The
adjunctions \(j_!\dashv j^*\dashv j_*\) follow by writing the three
components of a diagram morphism. A map \(i_*W\to F\) must land in the
kernels of \(r_\pm\), proving \(i_*\dashv i^!\); the other adjunction is
\(i^*\dashv i_*\).

All these functors except \(i^!\) are exact, as their stalk formulas
show. In particular \(Rj_*V=j_*V\). The higher functor \(R^1i^!\) will
retain a cokernel.

\subsection{TPL4. Both costalks and the first localization
triangle}\label{tpl4.-both-costalks-and-the-first-localization-triangle}

Apply \(i^!\) to (TPL2.2). The plus-supported part of \(I^0\) is
\(\{(a_+,0)\}\), whose differential is \(-r_+a_+\); the minus case is
identical. Therefore \[
Ri^!F=(K_+,K_-),\qquad
K_\pm=[A_\pm\xrightarrow{-r_\pm}B]
=\operatorname{Cone}(r_\pm)[-1].
\tag{TPL4.1}
\] In particular \[
H^0K_\pm=\ker r_\pm,\quad H^1K_\pm=B/r_\pm A_\pm,
\quad H^qK_\pm=0\ (q\ne0,1).
\tag{TPL4.2}
\]

There is a short exact sequence of sheaf complexes \[
0\to i_*Ri^!F\to I^\bullet\to j_*B[0]\to0.
\tag{TPL4.3}
\] The last map in degree zero projects \(A_\pm\oplus B\) to \(B\) and
is the identity at \(\eta\); in degree one it is zero. Its kernel is
exactly (TPL4.1) at the two closed points. Consequently the actual
localization triangle is \[
\boxed{i_*Ri^!F\to F\to Rj_*j^*F\to(i_*Ri^!F)[1].}
\tag{TPL4.4}
\] This follows from displayed complexes, rather than identification
with a different geometric lifting problem.

\subsection{TPL5. The connecting map on
representatives}\label{tpl5.-the-connecting-map-on-representatives}

Global sections of (TPL4.3) give \[
0\to K\to[A_+\oplus A_-\oplus B\xrightarrow D B^2]
\xrightarrow{(a_+,a_-,b)\mapsto b}B[0]\to0,
\quad K=[A_+\oplus A_-\xrightarrow{(-r_+,-r_-)}B^2].
\tag{TPL5.1}
\] Lift \(b\in B\) to \((0,0,b)\). Its differential is \((b,b)\), so
with the cone convention of TPL0 the boundary is \[
\boxed{\delta:B\to(B/r_+A_+)\oplus(B/r_-A_-),
\qquad b\mapsto([-b],[-b]).}
\tag{TPL5.2}
\] Changing each costalk differential from \(-r_\pm\) to \(+r_\pm\) uses
the chain isomorphism which is identity in degree zero and minus
identity in degree one; it changes the boundary to \(([b],[b])\). With
the alternative lift-and-differentiate convention the signs of both
boundary formulas are reversed. Their kernels and images are unchanged.

The complete long exact sequence is \[
\begin{aligned}
0\to&\ker r_+\oplus\ker r_-\to A_+\times_BA_-
\xrightarrow{\mathrm{res}}B\\
\xrightarrow{\delta}&(B/r_+A_+)\oplus(B/r_-A_-)
\xrightarrow q B/(r_+A_++r_-A_-)\to0,
\end{aligned}
\tag{TPL5.3}
\] where \[
\mathrm{res}(a_+,a_-)=r_+a_+=r_-a_-,
\qquad q([v_+],[v_-])=[v_--v_+].
\tag{TPL5.4}
\] The last map is induced by (TPL2.5), retaining the cover ordering.

In particular \[
\ker\delta=r_+A_+\cap r_-A_-=\mathrm{im}(\mathrm{res}).
\tag{TPL5.5}
\] Indeed a zero boundary means the existence of actual \(a_\pm\) with
the same image \(b\); these form a global section. Any two lifts differ
by \(\ker r_+\oplus\ker r_-\).

Exactness at \(q\) can be checked without derived terminology. If
\(v_--v_+=r_+a_++r_-a_-\), then \(b=v_++r_+a_+=v_--r_-a_-\) represents
both residue classes, so the pair is \(\delta(-b)\). Conversely every
diagonal pair has zero difference. Thus the obstruction to lifting a
prescribed generic section is the pair (TPL5.2), while Čech \(H^1\) is
the receiving quotient under the difference map. Neither replaces the
other.

\subsection{TPL6. The second localization triangle and single-point
versions}\label{tpl6.-the-second-localization-triangle-and-single-point-versions}

There is a stalkwise short exact sequence \[
0\to j_!j^*F\to F\to i_*i^*F\to0.
\tag{TPL6.1}
\] At \(\eta\) its nonzero map is \(B\xrightarrow1B\); at each closed
point it is \(A_\pm\xrightarrow1A_\pm\). The maps commute with
restriction. Hence \[
\boxed{j_!j^*F\to F\to i_*i^*F\to(j_!j^*F)[1].}
\tag{TPL6.2}
\] TPL2 gives \(R\Gamma(X,j_!B)=B[-1]\) and
\(R\Gamma(X,i_*i^*F)=(A_+\oplus A_-)[0]\). Under (TPL2.5) and the cone
convention TPL0 its boundary is \(-r_+a_++r_-a_-\): a lift
\((a_+,a_-,0)\) has differential \((-r_+a_+,-r_-a_-)\), whose negative
has difference \(-r_+a_++r_-a_-\). Replacing the identification of
\(H^1(j_!B)\) with \(B\) by its negative makes this boundary \(d\). Thus
the Čech kernel/cokernel sequence is recovered with an explicit
degree-one sign change.

For \(i_+:\{+\}\hookrightarrow X\), the open complement is \(U_-\),
whose sections are the exact evaluation \(A_-\). The corresponding
sequence is \[
0\to\ker r_+\to H^0(X,F)\to A_-
\xrightarrow{a_-\mapsto[-r_-a_-]}B/r_+A_+
\to H^1(X,F)\to0.
\tag{TPL6.3}
\] The representative lift \((0,a_-,r_-a_-)\) has differential
\((r_-a_-,0)\), proving the boundary with the negative prescribed in
TPL0. Its costalk-to-Čech map is \([v_+]\mapsto[-v_+]\) by (TPL2.5). For
\(i_-\), interchange the two stalks; the boundary is
\(a_+\mapsto[-r_+a_+]\), and its costalk-to-Čech map is
\([v_-]\mapsto[v_-]\). All signs are therefore explicit.

\subsection{TPL7. Operators and exchange of
branches}\label{tpl7.-operators-and-exchange-of-branches}

A coefficient or dilation operator is a triple \[
S=(S_+,S_-,S_\eta),\qquad r_\pm S_\pm=S_\eta r_\pm.
\tag{TPL7.1}
\] It acts on (TPL2.3) diagonally in each degree, on the costalks by
\(S_\pm,S_\eta\), and on the Čech complex by \(S_+\oplus S_-\),
\(S_\eta\). Equation (TPL7.1) proves commutation with every
differential, and direct substitution proves \[
\delta S_\eta=(\bar S_\eta\oplus\bar S_\eta)\delta,
\quad q(\bar S_\eta\oplus\bar S_\eta)=\bar S_\eta q.
\tag{TPL7.2}
\] The bars denote the operators on the stated quotients. Thus all
sequences are equivariant for any given commuting family satisfying the
actual restriction equations; no weights are assigned to make them
commute.

A symmetry covering \(+\leftrightarrow-\) has maps \(s_+:A_+\to A_-\),
\(s_-:A_-\to A_+\), \(s_\eta:B\to B\), with \[
r_-s_+=s_\eta r_+,\qquad r_+s_-=s_\eta r_-.
\tag{TPL7.3}
\] Its Čech action is \[
(a_+,a_-)\mapsto(s_-a_-,s_+a_+),\qquad b\mapsto-\,s_\eta b.
\tag{TPL7.4}
\] Indeed its degree-zero action changes \(d\) to \(-s_\eta d\). On the
costalk sum the degree-one action is instead \[
(b_+,b_-)\mapsto(s_\eta b_-,s_\eta b_+).
\tag{TPL7.5}
\] It intertwines the diagonal boundary with the generic action
\(s_\eta\), and (TPL2.5) intertwines (TPL7.5) with (TPL7.4). This Čech
cover-orientation sign is not identified with the degree of a
projective-line map in the different ring-realized scheme calculation.

\subsection{TPL8. Full nilpotent jets}\label{tpl8.-full-nilpotent-jets}

Let \(J_m=\mathbf C[t]/(t^m)\), \(m\ge1\), and \(N(v)=tv\). Then
\(N^m=0\), and \(N^{m-1}(1)=t^{m-1}\ne0\). These formulas specify an
actual nilpotent operator on the full multiplicity jet.

Tensor any diagram \(F^0\) over \(\mathbf C\) with \(J_m\), using
restrictions \(r_\pm^0\otimes1\). Tensoring over a field is exact, as is
seen by choosing a basis and taking copies of the original sequence.
Consequently all complexes and exact sequences above tensor with
\(J_m\), and \[
H^q(X,F^0\otimes J_m)=H^q(X,F^0)\otimes J_m.
\tag{TPL8.1}
\] The full jet operator has \[
\ker N=H^q(X,F^0)\otimes\mathbf C t^{m-1},
\quad
\operatorname{coker}N=H^q(X,F^0)\otimes(J_m/tJ_m).
\tag{TPL8.2}
\] These are named subquotients of the retained jet factor, not
replacements for it.

For a recovered \(a>0\) and specified exponent \(\lambda\), \[
T_a=a^\lambda\exp((\log a)N)
=a^\lambda\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{(\log a)^j}{j!}N^j.
\tag{TPL8.3}
\] The inverse uses \(a^{-\lambda}\) and \(-\log a\); multiplication of
the polynomials cancels all positive-degree coefficients below \(m\),
and higher terms vanish. With this action on all three stalks, the
restrictions commute with \(T_a\). With different stalk actions, the
requirement remains (TPL7.1). Nilpotence supplies no unproved weight
relation.

\subsection{TPL9. Restriction maps given by actual nilpotent
powers}\label{tpl9.-restriction-maps-given-by-actual-nilpotent-powers}

Take all three stalks to be \(J_m\), with \[
r_+=N^a,\qquad r_-=N^b,\qquad 0\le a,b\le m.
\tag{TPL9.1}
\] Put \(k=\min(a,b)\), \(h=\max(a,b)\). Directly in the basis
\(1,t,\ldots,t^{m-1}\), \[
H^0K_+=t^{m-a}J_m,\quad H^1K_+=J_m/t^aJ_m,
\quad
H^0K_-=t^{m-b}J_m,\quad H^1K_-=J_m/t^bJ_m.
\tag{TPL9.2}
\] These include zero and full-power cases. For example \(a=0\) gives
zero kernel and cokernel, while \(a=m\) gives \(J_m\) for both.

The global groups and boundary are \[
H^0(X,F)=\{(x,y):t^ax=t^by\},\quad H^1(X,F)=J_m/t^kJ_m,
\tag{TPL9.3}
\] \[
\delta(z)=(-z\bmod t^aJ_m,-z\bmod t^bJ_m),
\qquad \ker\delta=t^hJ_m.
\tag{TPL9.4}
\] The image-sum and intersection identities follow coefficient by
coefficient: \((t^a)+(t^b)=(t^k)\), \((t^a)\cap(t^b)=(t^h)\).

For \(a\le b\), the map \[
J_m\oplus t^{m-a}J_m\to H^0(X,F),
\quad(y,u)\mapsto(t^{b-a}y+u,y)
\tag{TPL9.5}
\] is an \(N\)-equivariant isomorphism. Its inverse is
\((x,y)\mapsto(y,x-t^{b-a}y)\); the defining equation puts the second
entry in \(\ker N^a\). For \(b<a\), interchange the two stalks. In
particular \[
\dim H^0=m+k,\quad\dim H^1=k,\quad
\dim\operatorname{im}(\mathrm{res})=m-h.
\tag{TPL9.6}
\] For \(a=b=1\), the generic lift exists exactly for \(z\in tJ_m\), and
its boundary is minus the diagonal pair of constant terms in
\(\mathbf C^2\). Both costalk quotients and Čech \(H^1\) are
\(\mathbf C\), with their difference map as in TPL5. This is a proved
family for arbitrary multiplicity \(m\), not a search over finitely many
zeros.

\subsection{TPL10. Unipotent invariants without dropping kernels or
cokernels}\label{tpl10.-unipotent-invariants-without-dropping-kernels-or-cokernels}

Let a general diagram have nilpotent operators \(N_\pm,N_B\), with
\(r_\pm N_\pm=N_Br_\pm\). Put \(A=A_+\oplus A_-\),
\(N_A=N_+\oplus N_-\), \(d=r_+-r_-\). The actual unipotent automorphism
is \(M=\exp N\). The finite polynomial \[
H(N)=\sum_{j\ge0}\frac{N^j}{(j+1)!}
\tag{TPL10.1}
\] has constant term one and therefore a finite-polynomial inverse.
Since \(M-1=NH(N)\), \[
\ker(M-1)=\ker N,\quad \operatorname{im}(M-1)=\operatorname{im}N,
\quad\operatorname{coker}(M-1)=\operatorname{coker}N.
\tag{TPL10.2}
\] The chain isomorphism \([V\xrightarrow{M-1}V]\to[V\xrightarrow N V]\)
is identity in degree zero and \(H(N)^{-1}\) in degree one.

If an actual infinite cyclic action is included with generator \(M\),
its derived invariants are computed by that two-term complex. This
follows by applying \(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf C[z,z^{-1}]}(-,V)\) to
\[
0\to\mathbf C[z,z^{-1}]\xrightarrow{z-1}
\mathbf C[z,z^{-1}]\to\mathbf C\to0.
\] This resolution is exact because evaluation at \(z=1\) has kernel
\((z-1)\) and multiplication by \(z-1\) is injective. The boundary on
\(V\) is \(M-1\).

Combining the nilpotent form with the Čech complex gives \[
\boxed{A\xrightarrow{d_0}B\oplus A\xrightarrow{d_1}B,\quad
d_0(a)=(da,N_Aa),\quad d_1(b,a')=da'-N_Bb.}
\tag{TPL10.3}
\] The composite is zero because \(dN_A=N_Bd\). Its complete cohomology
is \[
H^0_{\mathrm{tot}}=\ker d\cap\ker N_A,
\] \[
H^1_{\mathrm{tot}}=
\{(b,a'):da'=N_Bb\}/\{(da,N_Aa):a\in A\},
\quad
H^2_{\mathrm{tot}}=B/(dA+N_BB).
\tag{TPL10.4}
\] Degree two comes from the added cyclic-invariants direction, not from
ordinary sheaf \(H^2(X,F)\).

The exact invariant/coinvariant sequence is \[
0\to\operatorname{coker}(N:H^0(X,F)\to H^0(X,F))
\xrightarrow{z\mapsto[(0,z)]}H^1_{\mathrm{tot}}
\xrightarrow{[(b,a')]\mapsto[b]}
\ker(N:H^1(X,F)\to H^1(X,F))\to0.
\tag{TPL10.5}
\] If \(N[b]=0\) in \(B/dA\), an actual \(a'\) with \(N_Bb=da'\)
supplies a cocycle, proving surjectivity. If \(b=dv\), subtract the
coboundary \((dv,N_Av)\); the result is \((0,a'-N_Av)\), whose second
entry lies in \(\ker d\). Two such representatives differ by \(N_Aw\)
for \(dw=0\), precisely the displayed cokernel. This proves exactness.
Also \(H^2_{\mathrm{tot}}\) is the cokernel of \(N\) on \(H^1(X,F)\).

Apply the same two-term construction to the localization short exact
sequence (TPL5.1). All its maps commute with \(N\), so it gives a short
exact sequence of total complexes and hence its derived localization
triangle. Its boundary is obtained by taking minus the differential of a
lift, as fixed in TPL0. No underived invariant kernel is interchanged
with cohomology without this total complex.

\subsection{TPL11. Fréchet qualifications and
strictness}\label{tpl11.-fruxe9chet-qualifications-and-strictness}

Suppose now that \(A_\pm,B\) are Fréchet spaces and \(r_\pm\) are
continuous. Finite products, kernels, and all the displayed maps are
continuous. The space \(H^0(X,F)=\ker d\) is closed in
\(A_+\oplus A_-\), hence Fréchet. Forgetting topology gives all the
already proved algebraic exact sequences.

The quotient topology on \[
H^1_{\mathrm{alg}}(X,F)=B/(r_+A_++r_-A_-)
\tag{TPL11.1}
\] is Hausdorff exactly when the actual sum is closed; it is then
Fréchet. Each costalk quotient \(B/r_\pm A_\pm\) is Hausdorff and
Fréchet exactly when \(r_\pm A_\pm\) is closed.

If both images and their sum are closed, every term of (TPL5.3) is
Fréchet and each map is continuous. Algebraic exactness makes all its
images kernels of continuous maps to Hausdorff spaces, hence closed. The
induced maps from quotient by kernel onto image are topological
isomorphisms by the open mapping theorem. Thus the sequence is strict in
this case.

Without those facts, Hausdorff reduction changes the object: \[
H^1_{\mathrm{red}}(X,F)=B/\overline{r_+A_++r_-A_-}.
\tag{TPL11.2}
\] The natural map from (TPL11.1) has kernel \[
\overline{r_+A_++r_-A_-}/(r_+A_++r_-A_-).
\tag{TPL11.3}
\] The reduced branch boundary has kernel \[
\overline{r_+A_+}\cap\overline{r_-A_-},
\tag{TPL11.4}
\] not necessarily the actual liftable intersection (TPL5.5).

Nilpotence alone does not imply closed image. On \(\ell^2\oplus\ell^2\),
set \(S(x_n)=(x_n/n)\) and \(N(x,y)=(Sy,0)\). Then \(N^2=0\), while its
image is \(S(\ell^2)\oplus0\), proper and dense in \(\ell^2\oplus0\).
Every finite sequence belongs to \(S(\ell^2)\), proving density; the
sequence \((1/n)\) has the non-square-summable putative preimage
\((1,1,\ldots)\), proving properness.

For a finite jet factor \(J_m\), however, tensoring a Fréchet space with
\(J_m\) is a finite product. Multiplication by \(t^a\) has a closed
coordinate image and a continuous coordinate section onto that image.
Thus the special family TPL9 remains strict when each scalar coefficient
is replaced by a fixed Fréchet space. In a general diagram tensored with
\(J_m\), the original restriction-map strictness questions remain.

For the total complex TPL10, the displayed formulas always compute
algebraic kernels and quotients. Its Hausdorff cohomology requires
closed images of the actual total differentials. Closedness of all
cohomological maps used in an invariant long exact sequence must
likewise be checked; neither the existence of a nilpotent operator nor
the name ``jet'' proves it.

\subsection{TPL12. An exact demonstration of the closure
distinction}\label{tpl12.-an-exact-demonstration-of-the-closure-distinction}

Take \(A_+=A_-=B=\ell^2\), \(r_+=S\) as above, and \(r_-=1\). Then \[
H^0(X,F)=\{(x,Sx):x\in\ell^2\},\qquad H^1_{\mathrm{alg}}(X,F)=0,
\tag{TPL12.1}
\] because \(r_-A_-=B\). The actual generic restriction image is
\(S(\ell^2)\), and \[
\delta(b)=([-b]_{B/S(\ell^2)},0).
\tag{TPL12.2}
\] For \(b=(1/n)\), the class is nonzero: its plus lift would be
\((1,1,\ldots)\notin\ell^2\), although its minus lift exists. But
\(\overline{S(\ell^2)}=B\), so both reduced costalk quotients are zero
and the reduced boundary vanishes on every \(b\). Replacing image by
closure has therefore lost the actual lifting question, even though
