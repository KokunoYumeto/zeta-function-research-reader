## FOD1. Entire coefficients, both full ideals, and the original factors

Let \(M\) be the unital commutative algebra of entire \(h\) such that for every \(A\ge0\) there are \(C_A,N_A\) with
\[
 |h(x+it)|\le C_A(1+|t|)^{N_A}\quad(|x|\le A).
\tag{FOD1.1}
\]
Let \(\mathcal B\) be the Fréchet space of entire \(F\) with
\[
 b_{A,N}(F)=\sup_{|\Re s|\le A}(1+|\Im s|)^N|F(s)|<\infty
 \quad\text{for every }A,N\ge0.
\tag{FOD1.2}
\]
Pointwise multiplication makes \(\mathcal B\) an ideal in \(M\); a fixed \(h\) acts continuously by \(b_{A,N}(hF)\le C_A b_{A,N+N_A}(F)\).

Write \(\mathscr Z\) for the distinct actual nontrivial zeros of the original \(\zeta\), each with multiplicity \(m_\rho\). Their established open strip is \(0<\Re\rho<1\). Put
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal I&=\{F\in\mathcal B:\operatorname{ord}_\rho F\ge m_\rho\ \forall\rho\},\\
\mathcal I_+&=\{F\in\mathcal B:\operatorname{ord}_{\rho+1}F\ge m_\rho\ \forall\rho\},\\
\mathcal K&=\mathcal I\cap\mathcal I_+,\qquad
\mathcal Q=\mathcal B/\mathcal I,\qquad
\mathcal Q_+=\mathcal B/\mathcal I_+.
\end{aligned}
\tag{FOD1.3}
\]
The derivative evaluations are continuous by Cauchy's formula on a larger strip, so the three ideals are closed. All their displayed quotient spaces carry their actual Fréchet quotient topologies.

The original source comparison is
\[
\Theta a(s)=\frac12\int_0^\infty a(u)u^s\frac{du}{u},\qquad
\Theta^{-1}F(u)=\frac{u^{-1/2}}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}F(1/2+it)u^{-it}\,dt,
\]
\[
\Sigma f(u)=2\sum_{n\ge1}f(nu),\qquad
\Theta\Sigma f(s)=\zeta(s)\int_0^\infty f(v)v^s\frac{dv}{v}
\quad(\Re s>1).
\tag{FOD1.4}
\]
Here \(S\) consists of even Schwartz functions satisfying \(f(0)=0\) and \(\int_{\mathbb R}f=0\); the proved exact image is \(J=\Sigma S=\Theta^{-1}\mathcal I\), with the continuous inverse \(\Sigma^{-1}\). The coefficient space \(A=\Theta^{-1}\mathcal B\) has all seminorms \(\sup_{u>0}(u^N+u^{-N})|(u\partial_u)^j a(u)|\).

Retain the full source functions
\[
 f_0(v)=\frac{\pi}{2}v^2(2\pi v^2-3)e^{-\pi v^2},\qquad
 F_0(s)=\Theta\Sigma f_0(s)
 =\frac{s(s-1)}8\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s),
\]
\[
 F_+(s)=F_0(s-1)
 =\frac{(s-1)(s-2)}8\pi^{-(s-1)/2}\Gamma((s-1)/2)\zeta(s-1).
\tag{FOD1.5}
\]
Both belong to \(\mathcal B\subset M\). Their divisors are exactly \(\mathscr Z\) and \(\mathscr Z+1\), respectively, with the complete multiplicities. They are not substituted for the original function:
\[
\zeta(s)=1+\sum_{n\ge2}n^{-s}=\prod_p(1-p^{-s})^{-1},\qquad
-\zeta'(s)/\zeta(s)=\sum_p\sum_{k\ge1}(\log p)p^{-ks}
\quad(\Re s>1).
\tag{FOD1.6}
\]
The endpoint and trivial-zero comparisons retain
\[
F_0(0)=F_0(1)=\tfrac18,\quad F_0(-1)=\tfrac{\pi}{24},\quad
F_0(-2r)=\frac{r(2r+1)(-1)^r\pi^r}{2\,r!}\zeta'(-2r)\ne0\quad(r\ge1).
\tag{FOD1.7}
\]
Every value and derivative of \(F_+\) is the corresponding value and derivative of \(F_0\) at \(s-1\). At an original zero,
\[
F_0(\rho+z)=z^{m_\rho}U_\rho(z),\qquad
U_\rho(z)=\frac{(\rho+z)(\rho+z-1)}8
 \pi^{-(\rho+z)/2}\Gamma((\rho+z)/2)
 \frac{\zeta(\rho+z)}{z^{m_\rho}},
\tag{FOD1.8}
\]
and \(U_\rho(0)\ne0\). All products and divisions below use this complete local unit, not a replacement of it by one.

The proved separator is \(c(s)=E_+(s)=1-E(s-1)\in M\), with
\[
c-1\in(s-\rho)^{m_\rho}\mathcal O_\rho,\qquad
c\in(s-\rho-1)^{m_\rho}\mathcal O_{\rho+1}.
\tag{FOD1.9}
\]
GSL constructs \(E\) by its full Gaussian Cauchy integral and proves the strip estimates; GMS3 recalls that construction. Hence
\[
c\mathcal B\subset\mathcal I_+,\qquad
(1-c)\mathcal B\subset\mathcal I,\qquad
c_{\mathcal Q}=1,\quad c_{\mathcal Q_+}=0.
\tag{FOD1.10}
\]

## FOD2. The complete source extension before discarding its kernel

Let \(q,q_+\) be the two quotient maps. The actual joint map is
\[
q_{\rm joint}:\mathcal B\longrightarrow\mathcal Q\oplus\mathcal Q_+,
\qquad F\longmapsto(qF,q_+F).
\tag{FOD2.1}
\]
It is onto: for representatives \(f,g\in\mathcal B\), the function \(cf+(1-c)g\) has the requested two quotient images by (FOD1.10). Its kernel is exactly \(\mathcal K\), and
\[
\overline q_{\rm joint}:\mathcal B/\mathcal K\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal Q\oplus\mathcal Q_+,\qquad
[F]_{\mathcal K}\longmapsto(qF,q_+F),
\]
\[
\overline q_{\rm joint}^{-1}:\mathcal Q\oplus\mathcal Q_+\longrightarrow\mathcal B/\mathcal K,\qquad
([f]_{\mathcal I},[g]_{\mathcal I_+})\longmapsto[cf+(1-c)g]_{\mathcal K}
\tag{FOD2.2}
\]
is a continuous inverse. Independence of representatives uses \(c\mathcal I\subset\mathcal K\) and \((1-c)\mathcal I_+\subset\mathcal K\). To check continuity of the inverse, the continuous map on \(\mathcal B\oplus\mathcal B\) kills \(\mathcal I\oplus\mathcal I_+\) and therefore descends through that product quotient. Thus the full exact sequence is strict:
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal K\longrightarrow\mathcal B
\xrightarrow{q_{\rm joint}}\mathcal Q\oplus\mathcal Q_+
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{FOD2.3}
\]
Equation (FOD2.2) splits the quotient into two summands. It does not split (FOD2.3).

Pulling (FOD2.3) back along the two actual summand inclusions gives
\[
e_{\rm low}:\quad0\to\mathcal K\to\mathcal I_+
\xrightarrow{q|_{\mathcal I_+}}\mathcal Q\to0,
\]
\[
e_{\rm high}:\quad0\to\mathcal K\to\mathcal I
\xrightarrow{q_+|_{\mathcal I}}\mathcal Q_+\to0.
\tag{FOD2.4}
\]
These are actual pullbacks: the zero normal quotient condition is precisely membership in \(\mathcal I_+\), and the zero original quotient condition is precisely membership in \(\mathcal I\).

The first row is strict with explicit quotient inverse \([f]_{\mathcal I}\mapsto[cf]_{\mathcal K}\). For \(b\in\mathcal I_+\), \(cb-b=-(1-c)b\in\mathcal K\), which proves the reverse composition. The second row has inverse \([g]_{\mathcal I_+}\mapsto[(1-c)g]_{\mathcal K}\), by the same two full jet conditions. All these maps are continuous by the actual multiplier estimates.

## FOD3. Pushouts recover the original and normal rows exactly

Push the first row of (FOD2.4) out along \(\mathcal K\hookrightarrow\mathcal I\). Its middle module and comparison map are
\[
\mathcal P_0=(\mathcal I\oplus\mathcal I_+)/\{(k,-k):k\in\mathcal K\},
\qquad [(i,b)]\longmapsto i+b\in\mathcal B.
\tag{FOD3.1}
\]
This map is onto by \(F=(1-c)F+cF\). Its kernel is exactly the relation subspace, since \(i+b=0\) forces \(i=-b\in\mathcal K\). Its continuous inverse is
\[
F\longmapsto[((1-c)F,cF)].
\tag{FOD3.2}
\]
To verify the inverse on a pair \((i,b)\), subtract \((i,b)\) from the pair in (FOD3.2) at \(F=i+b\). The result is \((k,-k)\), where \(k=-ci+(1-c)b\in\mathcal K\). This also proves the inverse without selecting representatives of either quotient. The relation subspace is closed because \(\mathcal K\) is closed, so all middle spaces have the stated Fréchet quotient topology.

Consequently the pushout is exactly
\[
e_0:\quad0\to\mathcal I\to\mathcal B\xrightarrow q\mathcal Q\to0.
\tag{FOD3.3}
\]
Interchanging the ideals and replacing \(c\) by \(1-c\) gives, with all signs the same,
\[
(\mathcal I_+\oplus\mathcal I)/\{(k,-k):k\in\mathcal K\}
\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal B,\quad[(i,b)]\mapsto i+b,
\]
\[
e_+:\quad0\to\mathcal I_+\to\mathcal B\xrightarrow{q_+}\mathcal Q_+\to0.
\tag{FOD3.4}
\]
Its inverse is \(F\mapsto[(cF,(1-c)F)]\). Thus \(e_0\) and \(e_+\) are the specified pushouts of the same actual full-source extension.

## FOD4. Every multiplier that kills each component

Use algebraic \(M\)-module Ext, with the strict continuous conclusions proved separately below. Define two ideals of the multiplier ring itself:
\[
\mathfrak a=\{h\in M:\operatorname{ord}_\rho h\ge m_\rho\ \forall\rho\},
\qquad
\mathfrak a_+=\{h\in M:\operatorname{ord}_{\rho+1}h\ge m_\rho\ \forall\rho\}.
\tag{FOD4.1}
\]
These are not renamed copies of \(\mathcal I,\mathcal I_+\): their functions obey polynomial strip bounds, not necessarily rapid strip decay. Precisely, \(\mathcal I=\mathcal B\cap\mathfrak a\) and \(\mathcal I_+=\mathcal B\cap\mathfrak a_+\).

For an actual row \(0\to N\to X\to Y\to0\), the pushout along multiplication by \(h\) has middle module
\[
P_h=(N\oplus X)/\{(hn,-n):n\in N\}.
\tag{FOD4.2}
\]
It splits as an \(M\)-extension exactly when there is an \(M\)-linear \(r:X\to N\) with \(r|_N=h\). In one direction, such \(r\) gives the retraction \([(n,x)]\mapsto n+r(x)\), and hence a splitting. Conversely, a split extension has an \(M\)-linear retraction onto its kernel, obtained by subtracting its section; composing that retraction with \(x\mapsto[(0,x)]\) gives \(r\), because \([(0,n)]=[(hn,0)]\).

Apply this first to \(e_{\rm low}\), with \(N=\mathcal K\) and \(X=\mathcal I_+\). Put
\[
G(s)=F_0(s)F_+(s)\in\mathcal K\subset M.
\tag{FOD4.3}
\]
This is a nonzero entire function, with all factors from (FOD1.5). For \(b\in\mathcal I_+\), \(Gb\in\mathcal K\). Therefore any \(r\) supplied by a splitting satisfies
\[
G\,r(b)=r(Gb)=h\,G b.
\tag{FOD4.4}
\]
Cancellation is valid as an identity of entire functions: off the discrete zeros of \(G\) the two functions are equal, and the identity theorem extends that equality everywhere. Hence \(r(b)=hb\).

In particular \(hF_+\in\mathcal K\). At each original \(\rho\), the function \(F_+\) is a unit, since \(-1<\Re(\rho-1)<0\) and the divisor of \(F_0\) is \(\mathscr Z\). Thus \(h\) vanishes to order at least \(m_\rho\) at every \(\rho\), proving \(h\in\mathfrak a\). Conversely, \(h\in\mathfrak a\) gives \(h\mathcal I_+\subset\mathcal K\) by the full Leibniz formula, so \(r(b)=hb\) constructs the splitting. We have proved
\[
\operatorname{Ann}_M(e_{\rm low})=\mathfrak a.
\tag{FOD4.5}
\]
The identical argument for \(e_{\rm high}\) uses \(F_0\), which is a unit at every \(\rho+1\), and proves
\[
\operatorname{Ann}_M(e_{\rm high})=\mathfrak a_+.
\tag{FOD4.6}
\]

For the original row \(e_0\), use \(G=F_0\) in the cancellation argument with \(N=\mathcal I,X=\mathcal B\). It forces \(r(b)=hb\). The function \(b(s)=e^{s^2}\) belongs to \(\mathcal B\), since its modulus is \(e^{x^2-t^2}\) on \(s=x+it\), and it is nowhere zero. Thus \(hb\in\mathcal I\) forces all the original zero jets of \(h\). The converse is again multiplication. Replacing \(F_0,\mathcal I\) by \(F_+,\mathcal I_+\) proves the normal result. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Ann}_M(e_0)=\mathfrak a,\qquad
\operatorname{Ann}_M(e_+)=\mathfrak a_+.
\tag{FOD4.7}
\]

Every splitting in the sufficient direction is continuous and explicit:
\[
[(n,x)]\longmapsto(n+hx,[x]),\qquad
(n_0,[x])\longmapsto[(n_0-hx,x)].
\tag{FOD4.8}
\]
The second formula is independent of the lift \(x\), since changing it by \(n\) changes the pair by \((-hn,n)\), a relation. Its continuity follows by descending the continuous map on \(N\oplus X\) through the quotient \(N\oplus X\to N\oplus Y\). The relation subspace in (FOD4.2) is closed: convergence of \((hn_\nu,-n_\nu)\) forces convergence of \(n_\nu\) in the closed space \(N\), and continuity of multiplication gives the first coordinate. Thus (FOD4.8) is an actual Fréchet isomorphism. Necessity was proved even for algebraic splittings, so these annihilator criteria are also exact for continuous \(M\)-splittings.

## FOD5. The full obstruction decomposition and the separator

Let \(e_{\rm joint}\) denote the Yoneda class of (FOD2.3). Resolving the two summands separately and taking their direct sum gives the exact identification
\[
\operatorname{Ext}^1_M(\mathcal Q\oplus\mathcal Q_+,\mathcal K)
\simeq
\operatorname{Ext}^1_M(\mathcal Q,\mathcal K)\oplus
\operatorname{Ext}^1_M(\mathcal Q_+,\mathcal K),
\]
\[
e_{\rm joint}=(e_{\rm low},e_{\rm high}).
\tag{FOD5.1}
\]
The identification is pullback along the two summand inclusions, which were calculated in (FOD2.4). Thus
\[
\operatorname{Ann}_M(e_{\rm joint})=\mathfrak a\cap\mathfrak a_+.
\tag{FOD5.2}
\]
By (FOD1.9), \(c\in\mathfrak a_+\), \(1-c\in\mathfrak a\), so these two multiplier ideals are comaximal. Their intersection equals their product: if \(z\) belongs to both, \(z=zc+z(1-c)\) is a sum of two products, and the reverse inclusion follows from their being ideals. Consequently the actual cyclic image of the full extension has the exact description
\[
M e_{\rm joint}\simeq M/(\mathfrak a\cap\mathfrak a_+)
\xrightarrow{\sim}M/\mathfrak a\oplus M/\mathfrak a_+.
\tag{FOD5.3}
\]
The first isomorphism sends the class of \(h\) to \(h e_{\rm joint}\), with kernel just proved. The second has inverse \(([h],[k])\mapsto[ch+(1-c)k]\); independence and both compositions follow from the same full jet identities as in (FOD2.2).

The complete resulting actions are
\[
\begin{aligned}
c\,e_{\rm joint}&=(e_{\rm low},0),&
(1-c)e_{\rm joint}&=(0,e_{\rm high}),\\
c\,e_{\rm low}&=e_{\rm low},&
c\,e_{\rm high}&=0,\\
c\,e_0&=e_0,&
c\,e_+&=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{FOD5.4}
\]
None of \(e_{\rm low},e_{\rm high},e_0,e_+\) is zero: the original zeta has a nontrivial zero, so each ideal in (FOD4.1) is proper, and its annihilator does not contain one. A direct witness for \(e_{\rm low}\) is
\[
v_\rho(s)=F_0(s)/(s-\rho),\quad qv_\rho\ne0,\quad
(L-\rho)qv_\rho=0.
\tag{FOD5.5}
\]
The function is entire, rapidly decreasing on closed vertical strips, and has exact order \(m_\rho-1\) at \(\rho\). Any \(M\)-section into \(\mathcal I_+\) would send this nonzero quotient class to an entire function killed by \(s-\rho\), which must be zero. The normal witness is \(F_+(s)/(s-\rho-1)\). These use actual zeros and are not hypothetical alternative arithmetic.

The pushouts in FOD3 induce isomorphisms on the respective cyclic class modules \(M e_{\rm low}\to M e_0\) and \(M e_{\rm high}\to M e_+\): they are onto by their displayed generators, and injective by the equal annihilators (FOD4.5)–(FOD4.7). No claim of an isomorphism of their entire Ext groups follows.

## FOD6. The exact original quotient retained in the normal ideal

The strict map \(q_{\rm res}=q|_{\mathcal I_+}:\mathcal I_+\to\mathcal Q\) satisfies
\[
q_{\rm res}\beta=q,\qquad
\beta:\mathcal B\to\mathcal I_+,\quad\beta(F)=cF.
\tag{FOD6.1}
\]
Both equalities are for the actual source maps, not an inverse of \(q_{\rm res}\). Their continuous strong transposes satisfy
\[
\beta'\,q_{\rm res}'=q'.
\tag{FOD6.2}
\]
Indeed composition with any continuous linear functional proves the identity; continuity for strong duals follows because a continuous linear map sends bounded sets to bounded sets. The kernel of \(q_{\rm res}\) remains \(\mathcal K\), and FOD4–FOD5 show exactly why \(c\) does not split this row.

For any continuous pairing \(P\) already defined on \(\mathcal Q\) and any of its established dual receivers, composing its first argument with \(q_{\rm res}\beta\) gives the original value \(P(qF,\cdot)\) by (FOD6.1). This is an identity for existing pairings; it introduces neither a positive form nor a replacement of the complete quotient by values alone.

Define \(V:\mathcal B_s\to\mathcal B_\lambda\) by \((VF)(\lambda)=F(\lambda-1)\). This is the actual coordinate transport from the original normal variable \(s=\lambda-1\); it sends \(\mathcal I\) to \(\mathcal I_+\). In that variable the representative \(\Theta^{-1}V^{-1}\beta V\Theta:A\to J\) is exactly
\[
a\longmapsto\Theta^{-1}\!\left[(1-E(s))\Theta a(s)\right](u)
=\frac{u^{-1/2}}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}

followed by the proved inverse \(\Sigma^{-1}:J\to S\) when a Schwartz representative is wanted. The original normal multiplier is \(h(s+1)\), and the dilation is \(a^{s+1}\), retaining its factor \(a\). Translating to \(\lambda\) changes that action to \(h(\lambda)\). The map \(q_{\rm res}\) in (FOD6.1) is stated in this translated coordinate, as are the two ideals in FOD1.3. Its source-coordinate identity is \(q_{\rm res}\beta V=qV\); thus (FOD6.3) does not silently replace the translated quotient by the original source-variable quotient.

## FOD7. The actual localization boundary and the two actions on it

The proved actual coefficient complexes are
\[
D_J=(W\xrightarrow{r_+-r_-} J\xrightarrow{0}J(-1)),\quad

D_J\simeq Z[0]\oplus\mathcal I_+[-2],\quad
D_F\simeq Z[0]\oplus\mathcal Q[-1]\oplus\mathcal B[-2],\quad

\tag{FOD7.2}
\]
Here the normal terms have been transported by \(\Theta\) and \(V\), and \(Z=\ker(r_+-r_-)\). These are the already constructed quasi-isomorphisms with explicitly contractible kernels, not inserted sections of \(\mathcal B\to\mathcal Q\).

The actual connecting morphism \(\delta:D_G\to D_J[1]\) is zero on the low summand and is \(e_+[-2]\) on the normal summand. Let

\mathcal O_{\rm loc}
=\{h\delta:h\in M\}
\subset\operatorname{Hom}_{D(M)}(D_G,D_J[1]),
\tag{FOD7.3}

where \(h\delta\) is central postcomposition on the target, equivalently precomposition on the source. Taking the normal inclusion and projection in (FOD7.2) gives

h\delta=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ h e_+=0,\qquad
\mathcal O_{\rm loc}\simeq M/\mathfrak a_+,\qquad c\delta=0.
\tag{FOD7.4}

The forward implication is obtained by that inclusion and projection; the reverse follows from the zero low component. Thus this is the cyclic image of the actual localization boundary, with its full normal ideal retained.

For every derived \(M\)-morphism \(f:\mathcal Q[k]\to D_G\), centrality and \(c_{\mathcal Q}=1\) give

=\delta c_{D_G}f=c_{D_J[1]}\delta f=0.

This proves the actual lifting obstruction for this specified derived source test is zero. The exact Hom sequence of the actual localization triangle therefore gives a lift \(\widetilde f:\mathcal Q[k]\to D_F\). More concretely, (FOD7.2) and the complete contraction of \(\operatorname{RHom}_M(\mathcal Q,\mathcal Q_+)\) in GMS4 show that \(f\) has zero normal component in the derived category; retaining its low component as the \(Q[-1]\) component of \(D_F\) constructs a lift. The action used in (FOD7.3) must be distinguished from conjugation. For the actual arithmetic operator \(T_a\), equivariance is

(T_a)_{D_J[1]}\delta=\delta(T_a)_{D_G}.
\]
Consequently
\[
(T_a)_{D_J[1]}\delta(T_a)_{D_G}^{-1}=\delta.
\tag{FOD7.6}
\]
The boundary is fixed under this conjugation. Its cyclic module under postcomposition in (FOD7.3) is different; a weight calculated for that module is not a conjugation weight of the boundary map.

## FOD8. Exact comparison with the lifting target

The construction now retains three different facts simultaneously. The quotient \(\mathcal B/\mathcal K\) splits into its original and normal pieces by (FOD2.2). The source extension before that quotient has two nonzero components, and the separator retains one while annihilating the other, exactly as in (FOD5.4). The actual derived localization boundary uses the normal component, so its composition with every displayed complete-original-quotient source class vanishes by (FOD7.5).

The two full cyclic class modules are not declared to be the full coefficient ideals or the full quotients \(\mathcal Q,\mathcal Q_+\). Their exact descriptions are \(M/\mathfrak a\) and \(M/\mathfrak a_+\), with every jet retained. The calculation is an actual boundary calculation, not the splitting of an independently chosen extension. It also proves that moving an original class into the full normal ideal through \(\beta\) does not erase the original class or its existing pairing.

Deligne's precise obstruction receiver is \(\operatorname{im}\partial/\partial j(K)\), with its specialization and inertia maps, DC5.3–DC5.4. The module \(\mathcal O_{\rm loc}\) in (FOD7.3) is instead the cyclic image of a specified derived boundary under central coefficients. Equations (FOD7.4)–(FOD7.5) prove its actual effect without identifying those two receivers by their names. No new mixed étale realization, common modulus of original zeros, or RH conclusion is inferred. The source and full kernel comparisons proved above remain available for constructing that further geometric identification.
