\section{The full support contribution to the bounded section module}
\label{sec:supported-rr-sections}

Alain Connes and Caterina Consani, \emph{Riemann--Roch for the ring
$\mathbb Z$}, \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00456}{arXiv:2306.00456},
define $(HA)_X$ by requiring every partial sum to belong to $X$
(Lemma \texttt{xlem}, equation \texttt{xdefn}). Their Definition
\texttt{generator} defines dimension over the spherical base
$\mathbb S$ by the smallest number of distinct generators, with
coefficients in $\{0,1\}$ and with the stated tolerance relation.
We apply those exact definitions to the additive monoid of the full
Split-Zero semiring. The following calculation concerns that explicitly
constructed module. It does not presume a new definition of $H^1$ or a
Riemann--Roch identity for an unspecified cohomology theory.

\subsection{All levels, all fold maps, and the amplitude fibre}

Let $L$ be a bounded distributive lattice with $0_L\ne1_L$.
Its full carrier and operations are those of \emph{Split Support
Geometry}, version 11, by The Clankers,
\href{https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/zeta-function-research-reader/blob/a2851f9eb352e7e4376bc629de86fa4ed9c1c434/workbenches/splitzero-tandem/foundations/split-support-geometry-v11/split_support_geometry_arithmetic_curve_v11.tex#L1226}{Definition \texttt{def:lattice-split}}.
Keep
\begin{equation}\tag{SRR1}
 M=G_L(\mathbb Z)=\{z_\lambda=(0,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}
       \cup\{\widehat a=(a,1_L):a\in\mathbb Z\},\qquad
 \tau=z_{0_L},\quad e=z_{1_L}=\widehat0.
\end{equation}
The operations remain
$(a,\lambda)+(b,\mu)=(a+b,\lambda\vee\mu)$ and
$(a,\lambda)(b,\mu)=(ab,\lambda\wedge\mu)$.
The additive identity is $\tau$, and the amplitude map is
$q(a,\lambda)=a$. Fix an integer $m\ge0$ and put
$X_m=q^{-1}([-m,m]\cap\mathbb Z)$.

For the finite pointed set $r_+=\{*,1,\ldots,r\}$ define
\begin{equation}\tag{SRR2}
 \mathcal E_{L,m}(r_+)=
 \left\{(x_1,\ldots,x_r)\in M^r:
           \sum_{j\in Z}x_j\in X_m\text{ for every }
                    Z\subseteq\{1,\ldots,r\}\right\}.
\end{equation}
An empty sum is $\tau$. A pointed map $f:r_+\to s_+$ acts by
\begin{equation}\tag{SRR3}
 (x_j=(a_j,\lambda_j))_{j=1}^r\longmapsto
 \left(\sum_{f(j)=i}a_j,\ \bigvee_{f(j)=i}\lambda_j\right)_{i=1}^s.
\end{equation}
An empty join is $0_L$. The pointed value in every level is the tuple
whose entries are all $\tau$. For a subset $W$ of the target indices,
the sum of the resulting entries is the sum over the disjoint union
$\bigcup_{i\in W}f^{-1}(i)$. Thus (SRR3) preserves (SRR2).
Associativity of finite sums and joins proves composition, and the
identity map acts identically. These are all the structure maps of the
resulting $\Gamma$-set, or $\mathbb S$-module.

Write
$\mathcal E_m=(H\mathbb Z)_{[-m,m]}$, with the same all-subset
definition used by Connes--Consani. Its full relation to (SRR2) is
\begin{equation}\tag{SRR4}
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal E_{L,m}(r_+)
 =\{((a_j),(\lambda_j))\in\mathcal E_m(r_+)\times L^r:
                  a_j\ne0\Longrightarrow\lambda_j=1_L\},\\
 q_r:\mathcal E_{L,m}(r_+)\longrightarrow\mathcal E_m(r_+),
             \qquad ((a_j),(\lambda_j))\longmapsto(a_j).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Indeed the amplitude of a subset sum is its integer sum, independently
of its retained join support. This proves the equality. The maps $q_r$
are surjective: assign $1_L$ to every nonzero coordinate and $0_L$ to
every zero coordinate. Formula (SRR3) proves naturality. This last
choice is a set-theoretic section in each level. For $m\ge1$ no
pointed natural section exists: the only possible lifts of $1$ and $-1$
are $\widehat1$ and $\widehat{-1}$. Naturality for the two coordinate
projections forces their two-coordinate lift to be that pair. It is
admissible, but its fold is $e$, while pointedness forces the lift of
the integer zero to be $\tau$. Since $e\ne\tau$, fold naturality
fails. This proves the exact obstruction to a section of (SRR4).

For a fixed integer tuple $a\in\mathcal E_m(r_+)$, its fibre is
exactly $L^{\{j:a_j=0\}}$: all the other supports are forced to be
$1_L$. In particular
\begin{equation}\tag{SRR5}
 q_r^{-1}(0)=\{(z_{\lambda_1},\ldots,z_{\lambda_r}):
                                     (\lambda_j)\in L^r\}.
\end{equation}
The maps induced on this fibre are finite joins along $f$-fibres.
It is therefore exactly the $\Gamma$-set $H(L,\vee,0_L)$, with its
explicit inclusion into $\mathcal E_{L,m}$. No support label is
identified at this stage.

\subsection{The original dimension definition on this module}

Give $\mathcal E_{L,m}(1_+)=X_m$ the equality tolerance relation.
For a finite set $F\subset X_m$ and a subset $Z\subseteq F$, the
Connes--Consani sum $\sum_Z x$ exists precisely when every partial
sum of $Z$ has amplitude in $[-m,m]$. This follows because the
projection maps $\delta(j,r)$ in their equation \texttt{defnsum}
force the tuple in (SRR2) to be exactly the listed summands, while
the fold map is (SRR3). With equality tolerance, $F$ generates if
and only if every $x\in X_m$ is one of these admissible sums.
The empty set generates only $\tau$.

Let $J(L)$ denote the set of nonzero join-irreducible elements:
$j\ne0_L$ is in $J(L)$ if $j=u\vee v$ implies $j=u$ or $j=v$.
Put $J_{<}(L)=J(L)\setminus\{1_L\}$.
Every element of a finite lattice is a join of join-irreducible
elements below it. To prove this, induct on the number of elements
below $x$. The bottom is the empty join. If $x$ is join-irreducible
there is nothing further to prove; otherwise $x=u\vee v$ with both
$u,v<x$, and the induction hypothesis on those two smaller lower
sets gives the required finite join. If a finite join equals a
join-irreducible $j$, at least one of its terms equals $j$, by repeated
application of its defining binary property.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:srr-dimension}
For finite $L$, the dimension defined above is exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{SRR6}
 \dim_{\mathbb S}\mathcal E_{L,m}=
 \begin{cases}
 |J(L)|,&m=0,\\
 n+1+|J_{<}(L)|,&2^{n-1}\le m<2^n,\quad m\ge1.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
In the second line $n\ge1$ is the unique integer with the displayed
inequalities. In particular the number $n+1$ is the unchanged
Connes--Consani value for $\mathcal E_m$; the additional term is the
exact contribution of the proper nonzero join-irreducible supports.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
When $m=0$, every point is some $z_\lambda$ and every tuple is
admissible. Sums are exactly joins. The preceding finite-lattice
argument proves that the points $z_j$ for $j\in J(L)$ generate,
and that every generating set must contain each one. This proves
the first line.

Now let $m\ge1$. Partition any generating set into its nonzero
amplitude elements and its zero amplitude elements. A sum containing
a nonzero amplitude summand has support $1_L$, even when its
amplitudes cancel. Thus any representation of $z_j$, for
$j\in J_{<}(L)$, uses only zero amplitude summands. Their labels
have join $j$, so some summand must be $z_j$ itself. Every such
generator is therefore compulsory.

Project the nonzero amplitude generators to a subset
$A\subset[-m,m]\setminus\{0\}$. This is a bijection on those
generators, because nonzero amplitudes have unique lifts. For each
integer $a\in[-m,m]$, project an admissible representation of
$\widehat a$ and delete its zero amplitude terms. Every partial
sum of the remaining terms is still in $[-m,m]$, so $A$ generates
$\mathcal E_m$. The empty subset represents integer zero.
Connes--Consani, Lemma \texttt{sequencebleft}(ii), proves that the
least number of such integer generators is $n+1$. Their counting
lower bound can also be seen directly here: $2^{|A|}$ subsets must
represent $2m+1>2^n$ different integers, hence $|A|\ge n+1$.
Together with the compulsory distinct zero generators this proves
the lower bound in (SRR6).

For the upper bound take an optimal integer generating set $A_m$
from that same lemma. It can be chosen to admit a nonempty
admissible zero sum. We verify this extra property, which is needed
to generate the supported zero $e$ rather than just integer zero.
For $n>4$ the source construction is
$A_m=\{-m\}\cup\{\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n\}$, where
the distinct $\alpha_i$ are positive, sum to $m$, and have subset
sums covering $[0,m]$. The full set has sum zero, and every partial
sum lies in $[-m,m]$, since the positive total is $m$ and its only
negative summand is $-m$. This proves the required property in
that range, using the exact source construction.

For the remaining values the following are the source's sets,
with their values of $m$ stated explicitly:
\begin{equation}\tag{SRR7}
\begin{array}{c|l@{\qquad}c|l}
m&A_m&m&A_m\\\hline
1&\{-1,1\}&2&\{-2,1,2\}\\
3&\{-3,1,2\}&4&\{-3,-1,1,3\}\\
5&\{-4,-1,2,3\}&6&\{-6,1,2,3\}\\
7&\{-7,1,2,4\}&8&\{-7,-1,1,2,5\}\\
9&\{-8,-1,1,3,5\}&10&\{-10,1,2,3,4\}\\
11&\{-11,1,2,3,5\}&12&\{-12,1,2,3,6\}\\
13&\{-13,1,2,3,7\}&14&\{-14,1,2,4,7\}\\
15&\{-15,1,2,4,8\}&&
\end{array}
\end{equation}
Except at $m=2$, each whole set has sum zero, positive total $m$
and negative total $-m$; hence its full zero sum is admissible.
At $m=2$, the subset $\{-2,2\}$ is the required admissible
zero sum. The source proves these sets generate their respective
integer intervals with the stated admissibility condition.

Lift $A_m$ to $\widehat A_m\subset M$ and adjoin
$\{z_j:j\in J_{<}(L)\}$. All nonzero amplitude targets are
generated by the lifted admissible integer sums. Every proper
support zero is generated by its join-irreducible decomposition,
and $\tau$ is the empty sum. Finally the nonempty admissible
zero sum just verified generates $e$, since all its summands have
top support. Thus this set generates every point of $X_m$ and has
exactly $n+1+|J_{<}(L)|$ elements. This attains the lower bound.
\end{proof}

For infinite $L$, the construction and exact maps (SRR1)--(SRR5)
remain unchanged, and no finite set generates
$\mathcal E_{L,m}$ for any $m\ge0$. Indeed a generating set of
size $t$ has at most $2^t$ subset sums, whereas the target contains
the distinct points $z_\lambda$ for every $\lambda\in L$.
If one extends the minimum-cardinality definition to arbitrary
generating sets with finitely supported sums, its value is exactly
$|L|$: an infinite set of size $\kappa$ has $\kappa$ finite
subsets, forcing $\kappa\ge|L|$, and the full point set $X_m$
has cardinality $|L|$ and generates by its singletons. This states
the cardinal convention explicitly; the failure of finite generation
needs no such extension of the convention.

\subsection{Exact consequences for the stated archimedean bound}

Retain a real parameter $\lambda>0$ and the original bound
$[-\lambda,\lambda]\cap\mathbb Z$. Put $m=\lfloor\lambda\rfloor$;
this equality identifies precisely the same subset of integers and
does not rescale its metric or change its endpoints. Define
$\mathcal E_{L,\lambda}$ by (SRR2) using that interval. Then
\begin{equation}\tag{SRR8}
\dim_{\mathbb S}\mathcal E_{L,\lambda}=
\begin{cases}
 |J(L)|,&0<\lambda<1,\\
 n+1+|J_{<}(L)|,&2^{n-1}\le\lambda<2^n,\quad\lambda\ge1.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
For the second line, since each $2^{n-1}$ and $2^n$ is an integer,
the displayed inequalities on $\lambda$ are equivalent to the
same inequalities on $m$. Formula (SRR6) proves (SRR8).
For the particular divisor $D=\delta\{\infty\}$ used in the
source's calculation, keep $\lambda=e^\delta$ exactly. Thus the
full-support module over its integer sections has dimension $|J(L)|$
for $\delta<0$, and $n+1+|J_{<}(L)|$ for
$(n-1)\log2\le\delta<n\log2$. This is a calculation of the
specified module and its surjection (SRR4), with the entire support
fibre (SRR5) retained.

For the two-element lattice, $J(L)=\{1_L\}$: the dimension is one
when $0<\lambda<1$ and equals the original value $n+1$ for
$\lambda\ge1$. For a chain with four elements
$0_L<u<v<1_L$, $J(L)=\{u,v,1_L\}$: the two respective values are
$3$ and $n+3$. For the Boolean lattice of subsets of $\{u,v\}$,
$J(L)=\{\{u\},\{v\}\}$ and its top is not join-irreducible:
the two values are $2$ and $n+3$. These follow from the definition
of join-irreducibility; they show why retaining only the two-element
support lattice would miss the exact dimension contribution.
