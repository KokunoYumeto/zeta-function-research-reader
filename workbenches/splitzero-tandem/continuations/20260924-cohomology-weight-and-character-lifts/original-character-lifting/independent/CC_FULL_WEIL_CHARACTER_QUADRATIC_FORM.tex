\subsection{Original test space, sharp, and exact character diagonalization}

Retain the original coordinates and measures
\begin{equation}\tag{WQP1}
 C_{\mathbb Q}=\mathbb R_{>0}\times K,\quad
 K=\prod_p\mathbb Z_p^\times,\quad
 dg=\frac{dy}{y}\,du,\quad \int_Kdu=1.
\end{equation}
Fix any positive integer $N$, write $U_N=(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^\times$,
and view every $\chi\in\widehat U_N$ as its actual character on $K$.
For $N=1$ the group and its character set have one element. Let
$h_\chi,k_\chi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R_{>0})$, allowing zero functions,
and retain the entire original tests
\begin{equation}\tag{WQP2}
 f(y,u)=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat U_N}h_\chi(y)\chi(u),\qquad
 k(y,u)=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat U_N}k_\chi(y)\chi(u).
\end{equation}
All sums are finite. These tests are smooth and compactly supported in
the real multiplicative variable, locally constant in the compact
variable, and belong to the source's test algebra. The finite inverse is
$h_\chi(y)=\int_K f(y,u)\overline{\chi(u)}du$, or the same sum with
weight $1/\varphi(N)$ on $U_N$. This retains its original Haar factor.

The original convolution and source involution are
\begin{equation}\tag{WQP3}
 (f*k)(g)=\int_{C_{\mathbb Q}}f(a)k(a^{-1}g)\,da,\qquad
 k^\sharp(g)=|g|^{-1}\overline{k(g^{-1})}.
\end{equation}
In particular
\[
 k^\sharp(y,u)=\sum_\chi y^{-1}\overline{k_\chi(y^{-1})}\chi(u).
\]
The same compact character occurs after sharp because
$\overline{\chi(u^{-1})}=\chi(u)$; the radial factor $y^{-1}$
remains. Integration of $\chi(v)\psi(v)^{-1}$ on $K$ is
$\delta_{\chi,\psi}$, by translating the integral when the character
is nontrivial. It follows by substituting WQP2--3 that
\begin{equation}\tag{WQP4}
 (f*k^\sharp)(y,u)=\sum_\chi H_\chi(y)\chi(u),\qquad
 H_\chi(y)=y^{-1}\int_0^\infty
           h_\chi(x)\overline{k_\chi(x/y)}\,dx.
\end{equation}
This is an exact diagonalization of the convolution in its compact
characters; it does not omit a mixed term. Those mixed terms have
the proved zero compact integral.

Every $H_\chi$ is smooth and compactly supported. If $h_\chi$ and
$k_\chi$ are supported in $[A,B]$ and $[C,D]$, respectively, with
positive endpoints, its support is contained in $[A/D,B/C]$.
Derivatives under the integral are justified on compact $y$ intervals
by smooth compactly supported majorants in $x$; outside the displayed
ratio interval the integrand is zero. The exact identity and symmetry are
\begin{equation}\tag{WQP5}
 \begin{gathered}
 H_{h,k}(1)=\int_0^\infty h(x)\overline{k(x)}\,dx,\qquad
 H_{h,k}(y^{-1})=y\,\overline{H_{k,h}(y)},\\
 H_{h,h}(y^{-1})=y\,\overline{H_{h,h}(y)}.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
For the second formula substitute $x=v/y$ in its left hand integral.
No replacement of $dx$ by $dx/x$ is permissible in the first formula.

Write the original Mellin transform with every factor visible:
\begin{equation}\tag{WQP6}
 M_h(s)=\int_0^\infty h(x)x^s\,\frac{dx}{x},\qquad
 M_{H_{h,k}}(s)=M_h(s)\overline{M_k(1-\bar s)}.
\end{equation}
The second identity follows from the original multiplicative convolution
and Fubini, or from $x/y=v$ in WQP4; compact support makes all these
integrals absolute for every complex $s$. Thus the two global endpoints,
which only receive the trivial compact character, are
\begin{equation}\tag{WQP7}
 E(h,k)=M_h(0)\overline{M_k(1)}
             +M_h(1)\overline{M_k(0)},\qquad
 E(h,h)=2\operatorname{Re}\bigl(M_h(0)\overline{M_h(1)}\bigr).
\end{equation}
The conjugations in this formula are required for complex tests. In
particular a real-test formula without them is not substituted here.

\subsection{The real term with its full original regularization}

The archimedean measure of the source is $d^*u=du/(2|u|)$ on
$\mathbb R^\times$. Its mass on $1\le |u|\le R$ is $\log R$.
For the parity $\epsilon\in\{0,1\}$ and a compact radial test $H$ put
$b(u)=H(|u|)(\operatorname{sgn}u)^\epsilon$. Connes's original
Appendix II (36)--(37), for $e_{\mathbb R}(x)=e^{-2\pi ix}$, says
\begin{equation}\tag{WQP8}
 \mathcal A_\epsilon(H)=
 (\log(2\pi)+\gamma)H(1)
 +\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}
 \left\{\int_{|1-u|\ge\delta}
       \frac{b(u^{-1})}{|1-u|}\frac{du}{2|u|}
             +(\log\delta)H(1)\right\}.
\end{equation}
This cited principal value, including its Euler constant, is the
definition of the real-place term in the original global trace.

Separate $u>0$ and $u<0$, then substitute $y=|u|^{-1}$ in each.
The positive numerator becomes $H(y)$, and its measure-denominator
product becomes $dy/(2|1-y|)$. The negative branch gives
$(-1)^\epsilon H(y)\,dy/(2(1+y))$. Its entire contribution is
ordinary integration. The positive cutoff is initially
$|1-y|\ge\delta y$. Replacing it by $|1-y|\ge\delta$ changes
the integral by a quantity tending to zero: its two threshold
distances are $\delta/(1+\delta)$ and $\delta/(1-\delta)$;
the integrals of $1/|1-y|$ over the differences are
$|\log(1+\delta)|$ and $|\log(1-\delta)|$, multiplied by a
bound for the smooth $H$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\tag{WQP9}
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal A_\epsilon(H)={}&
   (\log(2\pi)+\gamma)H(1)\\
 &+\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}
  \left\{\frac12\int_{\substack{y>0\\|1-y|\ge\delta}}
      \frac{H(y)}{|1-y|}\,dy+(\log\delta)H(1)\right\}\\
 &+\frac{(-1)^\epsilon}{2}\int_0^\infty\frac{H(y)}{1+y}\,dy .
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
This includes the negative real component and its parity.

An entirely convergent version, with the subtracted term still displayed,
is obtained by substituting $y\mapsto1/y$ only in the integrals over
$(1,\infty)$. Define
\begin{equation}\tag{WQP10}
 B_H(y)=H(y)+y^{-1}H(y^{-1}),\qquad0<y\le1.
\end{equation}
The exact result is
\begin{equation}\tag{WQP11}
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal A_\epsilon(H)={}&(\log(2\pi)+\gamma)H(1)\\
 &+\frac12\int_0^1
  \left\{\frac{B_H(y)-2H(1)}{1-y}
              +(-1)^\epsilon\frac{B_H(y)}{1+y}\right\}dy .
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
To check the constant, integration of the retained $2H(1)/(1-y)$
up to $1-\delta$, with the factor $1/2$, is
$-H(1)\log\delta$, cancelling exactly the logarithm in WQP9.
The transformed upper cutoff is $1/(1+\delta)$ rather than
$1-\delta$; the intervening logarithmic integral tends to zero
by the same bound used above. Near one the numerator
$B_H(y)-2H(1)$ is $O(1-y)$. Near zero the two terms of $B_H$
are zero because $H$ has compact support, and the remaining
$-2H(1)/(1-y)$ is integrable. This proves convergence and the
identity without an unrecorded constant.

\subsection{The complete global original-coordinate formula}

For each original compact character $\chi$ let $\chi_*$ be its primitive
integer character, $c_\chi$ its conductor, and
$\chi(-1)=(-1)^{\epsilon_\chi}$. Let
\[
 \mathcal Q(f,k)=
 \operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\underline\vartheta_m(f*k^\sharp)\mid H^1\bigr)
\]
denote the source's global trace pairing on the exact tests WQP2.
The source's differential idele is denoted $\mathfrak a$. Its
reference-character trace formula, used before assigning
$|\mathfrak a|=1$ over $\mathbb Q$, gives the following full expression:
\begin{equation}\tag{WQP12}
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal Q(f,k)=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat U_N}\bigg\{&
  1_{\chi=1}E(h_\chi,k_\chi)
 +\left(\sum_{p\mid c_\chi}v_p(c_\chi)\log p
                       -\log|\mathfrak a|\right)H_\chi(1)\\
 &-\sum_{p\nmid c_\chi}\log p\sum_{j\ge1}
     \left[\chi_*(p)^jH_\chi(p^j)
       +p^{-j}\chi_*(p)^{-j}H_\chi(p^{-j})\right]
 -\mathcal A_{\epsilon_\chi}(H_\chi)\bigg\}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Every prime-power sum is finite: the compact interval containing all
the supports of the finitely many $H_\chi$ bounds $p^j$ above and
$p^{-j}$ below. Every conductor sum is finite. The real term is the
entire expression WQP8, or exactly its convergent form WQP11.
Thus no local term has become an unspecified remainder.

Here is the direct derivation from the original trace, with all signs.
The two endpoint integrals of $f*k^\sharp$ are WQP7 for the trivial
compact character and zero otherwise by integration over $K$.
The differential contribution is
$-\log|\mathfrak a|\sum_\chi H_\chi(1)$.
At $p\nmid c_\chi$ the local unit character is trivial and the
uniformizer character is $\chi_*(p)^{-1}$ in the PLP20 coordinates.
Its two original valuation shells are the bracket in WQP12,
with multiplicative unit mass $\log p$, as proved in WLC5 and
WLC12--13. At $p\mid c_\chi$, the off-unit averages vanish and
the unit principal value is $-v_p(c_\chi)\log p\,H_\chi(1)$,
proved by the finite subgroup filtration WLC6--9. The global trace
subtracts this value; this gives the positive displayed conductor
summand in WQP12. Finally its real restriction is
$H_\chi(|u|)(\operatorname{sgn}u)^{\epsilon_\chi}$, giving
exactly the subtracted WQP8. These computations account for
every term of the original cited trace formula.

For the standard global additive character of
$\mathbb A_{\mathbb Q}/\mathbb Q$, the differential idele satisfies
$|\mathfrak a|=1$. Its displayed term is then zero for that
proved reference choice; local rescalings have the exact correction
$+\log|a_v|\,b_v(1)$ inside the local term, as in WLC10.
Those terms must not be inserted a second time while retaining
the already shifted global differential term.

The diagonal form is particularly explicit in the original variable
$x$. Put
\begin{equation}\tag{WQP13}
 J_\chi=\int_0^\infty|h_\chi(x)|^2dx,\qquad
 R_\chi(y)=y^{-1}\int_0^\infty
          h_\chi(x)\overline{h_\chi(x/y)}dx .
\end{equation}
Then $R_\chi(1)=J_\chi$ and
$R_\chi(y^{-1})=y\overline{R_\chi(y)}$ by WQP5.
Substituting the entire real term into WQP12 gives
\begin{equation}\tag{WQP14}
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal Q(f,f)=\sum_\chi\bigg\{&
  2\,1_{\chi=1}\operatorname{Re}
       \left(M_{h_\chi}(0)\overline{M_{h_\chi}(1)}\right)\\
 &+\left(\sum_{p\mid c_\chi}v_p(c_\chi)\log p
                -\log|\mathfrak a|-\log(2\pi)-\gamma\right)J_\chi\\
 &-2\sum_{p\nmid c_\chi}\log p\sum_{j\ge1}
       \operatorname{Re}\left[
         \chi_*(p)^j p^{-j}
           \int_0^\infty h_\chi(x)
                          \overline{h_\chi(x/p^j)}dx\right]\\
 &-\int_0^1
       \left\{\frac{\operatorname{Re}R_\chi(y)-J_\chi}{1-y}
          +(-1)^{\epsilon_\chi}
                 \frac{\operatorname{Re}R_\chi(y)}{1+y}\right\}dy
 \bigg\}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
In particular the conductor contribution is exactly
$\log(c_\chi)\int|h_\chi(x)|^2dx$, with its local sum displayed
in WQP14. It is one summand of a complete form whose sign has
not been determined here.

For an imprimitive modulus $N$, primes dividing $N$ but not
$c_\chi$ still appear in WQP12--14. This follows from the
compact character's actual domain and the original identity
\begin{equation}\tag{WQP15}
 L(s,\chi)=L(s,\chi_*)\!
     \prod_{\substack{p\mid N\\p\nmid c_\chi}}
                 (1-\chi_*(p)p^{-s}),\qquad\operatorname{Re}s>1.
\end{equation}
Its proof is the absolute Euler-product comparison in PHS16
and WLC16. No imprimitive zero extension is substituted for
the nonzero phase at those primes in the idele-class test.

\subsection{The exact Gamma representation of that same real term}

The original $h$ remains the input. Its full transform WQP6 on
the line $s=1/2+it$ has the inversion and pairing identities
\begin{equation}\tag{WQP16}
 \begin{gathered}
 h(x)=\frac{x^{-1/2}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
                   M_h(1/2+it)x^{-it}\,dt,\\
 H_{h,k}(1)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
                M_h(1/2+it)\overline{M_k(1/2+it)}\,dt .
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Indeed set $x=e^v$ in the defining Mellin integral. It is the
Fourier transform, with positive Fourier sign, of the explicitly
weighted function $e^{v/2}h(e^v)$, which is smooth and compactly
supported in $v$. Fourier inversion and its pairing identity
give WQP16 with the displayed factors. The latter pairing is
$\int h(x)\overline{k(x)}dx$, not a different original measure.
These are invertible comparison maps; the weighted function
is not used as an unrecorded replacement for $h$.

Let $\psi(z)=\Gamma'(z)/\Gamma(z)$ on $\operatorname{Re}z>0$.
The same principal value WQP11 is
\begin{equation}\tag{WQP17}
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal A_\epsilon(H_{h,k})={}&\frac1{2\pi}
  \int_{\mathbb R}M_h(1/2+it)\overline{M_k(1/2+it)}
          m_\epsilon(t)\,dt,\\
 m_\epsilon(t)={}&
 \log(2\pi)+\gamma-\log2-\gamma\\
 &-\frac12\psi\left(\frac{1/2+\epsilon+it}{2}\right)
  -\frac12\psi\left(\frac{1/2+\epsilon-it}{2}\right).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The constant contributions in its second line have all been
retained. Their exact value is $\log\pi$, so equivalently the
same multiplier is
$\log\pi-\operatorname{Re}\psi((1/2+\epsilon+it)/2)$.
This last identity is a proved comparison of terms in WQP17,
not a completed-zeta substitution.

Here is a direct calculation of its Gamma term and constants.
From WQP16 applied to $H$, the symmetric numerator $B_H(y)$
is the integral of
$2y^{-1/2}\cos(t\log y)$ times
$M_h(1/2+it)\overline{M_k(1/2+it)}/(2\pi)$.
Insert this in WQP11. The resulting multiplier is
\begin{equation}\tag{WQP18}
 \log(2\pi)+\gamma+
 \int_0^1\left\{
       \frac{y^{-1/2}\cos(t\log y)-1}{1-y}
       +(-1)^\epsilon
          \frac{y^{-1/2}\cos(t\log y)}{1+y}\right\}dy .
\end{equation}
At zero, the absolute integral is bounded by a constant times
$\int_0^{1/2}y^{-1/2}dy$. At one, the numerator difference is
bounded by $C(1+|t|)(1-y)$, using the mean-value formula
for $y^{-1/2}\exp(it\log y)$. Thus the absolute integral is
at most $C'(1+|t|)$. Both Mellin factors decrease faster than
every power of $|t|$, by integration by parts in $v$ in
WQP16. This justifies the interchange by an integrable
majorant and proves convergence of WQP17.

For $\alpha=1/2+it$ combine the two displayed rational
denominators without changing any summand: their integral
is the real part of
\[
 \int_0^1\left\{\frac{2y^{\alpha+\epsilon-1}}{1-y^2}
                              -\frac1{1-y}\right\}dy
 =\int_0^1\frac{v^{(\alpha+\epsilon)/2-1}-1}{1-v}\,dv
                                  -\int_0^1\frac{dy}{1+y}.
\]
The equality follows by first writing its numerator as
$2y(y^{\alpha+\epsilon-2}-1)/(1-y^2)-1/(1+y)$ and
then substituting $v=y^2$ in the first term. The last
integral is exactly $\log2$.
For $\operatorname{Re}z>0$,
\begin{equation}\tag{WQP19}
 \int_0^1\frac{v^{z-1}-1}{1-v}\,dv=-\psi(z)-\gamma .
\end{equation}
For completeness, integration by parts in the beta integral gives
$B(z,n+1)=n!/[z(z+1)\cdots(z+n)]$. Substitution $v=x/n$
shows $n^zB(z,n+1)\to\Gamma(z)$ locally uniformly for
$\operatorname{Re}z>0$, including one $z$ derivative:
$(1-x/n)^n\le e^{-x}$ on $0\le x\le n$ supplies integrable
majorants for $x^{\operatorname{Re}z-1}(1+|\log x|)$.
Logarithmic differentiation of the displayed finite product
therefore gives
$\psi(z)=\lim_n(\log n-\sum_{j=0}^n(z+j)^{-1})$.
Subtract the harmonic sums with limit defining $\gamma$.
Expanding $(1-v)^{-1}$ and integrating its convergent
differences gives WQP19; near one those differences have a
bounded quotient, and near zero $v^{\operatorname{Re}z-1}$
is integrable, which justifies the limit. This proves WQP19
and hence every Gamma, Euler-constant and $\log2$ term in WQP17.

Consequently the conductor, differential and real-place portion
of the full form, still with all constants displayed, is
\begin{equation}\tag{WQP20}
 \begin{aligned}
 &\frac1{2\pi}\sum_\chi\int_{\mathbb R}
 M_{h_\chi}(1/2+it)\overline{M_{k_\chi}(1/2+it)}
 \\
 &\quad{}\times\Bigl[
 \sum_{p\mid c_\chi}v_p(c_\chi)\log p-\log|\mathfrak a|
 -\log(2\pi)-\gamma+\log2+\gamma
 \\
 &\qquad
 +\operatorname{Re}\psi\left(\frac{1/2+\epsilon_\chi+it}{2}\right)
 \Bigr]dt .
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Adding the exact endpoints and subtracting the exact prime-power
correlations in WQP12 gives the same full original form.
No weight or sign in WQP20 alone determines its total sign.

\subsection{Global implications and the entire common-label map}

WQP12 proves the complete pairing is diagonal between distinct
compact characters, not merely that its conductor operator is
diagonal. Each single-character form is Hermitian: WQP7 is
Hermitian, the conductor and differential constants are real,
WQP5 exchanges the two prime-power terms under conjugation,
and WQP17 has a real multiplier. Thus
\begin{equation}\tag{WQP21}
 \mathcal Q(f,f)=\sum_\chi \mathcal Q_\chi(h_\chi,h_\chi)
               \in\mathbb R .
\end{equation}
A negative value for the finite tuple therefore implies a negative
value in at least one of its actual character components, and every
such component is realized by the original test $h(y)\chi(u)$.
This is an exact characterization of the test decomposition, not
an assertion that a negative component exists.

The trivial compact-character component has $c_\chi=1$ and
$\epsilon_\chi=0$. Its conductor sum is empty, its Euler series
is the original $\zeta(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-s}$ for
$\operatorname{Re}s>1$, and every prime appears in WQP14.
Thus a positive conductor contribution on other components is
not a positive missing term of this component. The source's
Weil explicit formula identifies the trivial component's entire
value with
\begin{equation}\tag{WQP22}
 \sum_{\substack{\rho:\zeta(\rho)=0\\0<\operatorname{Re}\rho<1}}
       m_\rho M_h(\rho)\overline{M_k(1-\bar\rho)} .
\end{equation}
This is an application of the cited human explicit formula to
the original test, with its multiplicities. WQP12--20 calculate
its other side without replacing $\zeta$ by a completed function.
The original Gamma and endpoint contributions remain present
in that side. No conclusion about these zeros is inferred merely
from the character decomposition.

Let $\mathcal T_N$ denote exactly the complex test space WQP2.
For any bounded distributive lattice $L$ retain the common-label
carrier
\begin{equation}\tag{WQP23}
 G_L(\mathcal T_N)=
   \{(0,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}\cup
                          (\mathcal T_N\times\{1_L\}).
\end{equation}
The finite character coefficient map and its inverse are linear
maps of the entire vector, so their lifts retain its one label.
They give an isomorphism to
$G_L(\bigoplus_\chi C_c^\infty(\mathbb R_{>0}))$.
This target does not have independent support labels on its
character components. The conjugate-linear sharp map lifts as
$(f,\lambda)\mapsto(f^\sharp,\lambda)$. It respects addition and
conjugates scalar amplitudes, keeping their original support.

Convolution and the full sesquilinear form have the exact lifts
\begin{equation}\tag{WQP24}
 \begin{gathered}
 (f,\lambda)*_L(k,\mu)=(f*k,\lambda\wedge\mu),\\
 \mathcal Q_L((f,\lambda),(k,\mu))
                   =(\mathcal Q(f,k),\lambda\wedge\mu),\qquad
 \mathcal Q_L((f,\lambda),(f,\lambda))
                   =(\mathcal Q(f,f),\lambda).
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
If the output amplitude is nonzero, neither input amplitude is
zero, so both input labels are top. Otherwise the zero amplitude
admits the displayed meet. These facts prove carrier membership.
Additivity in the first input and conjugate additivity in the
second, including their labels, follow from distributivity
$(\lambda\vee\lambda')\wedge\mu=
(\lambda\wedge\mu)\vee(\lambda'\wedge\mu)$ and the corresponding
amplitude identities. The scalar rules follow from the same meet
laws and sesquilinearity. Equivalently the ordinary linear
functional on $\mathcal T_N\otimes\overline{\mathcal T_N}$
induced by $\mathcal Q$ lifts with one common label, and WQP24
is its composition with
$(f,\lambda),(k,\mu)\mapsto(f\otimes\bar k,\lambda\wedge\mu)$.
Thus a zero value on a top-supported test is $e=(0,1_L)$,
not $\tau=(0,0_L)$.

To avoid confusing the compact group $K$ in WQP1 with the
tropical coefficient semifield, denote that same semifield here
by $K_{\mathrm{trop}}$, with multiplicative notation, zero $0$
and unit $1$; $\mathbb R_+^{\max}$ is one permitted choice.
Retain the exact tropical
receiver $K_{\mathrm{trop},L}=K_{\mathrm{trop}}\otimes_{\mathbb B}L$
and the original map
$\Phi(v,\lambda)=(v,1_{K_{\mathrm{trop}}}\otimes\lambda)$.
It commutes with each of the preceding labelled formulas.
The function $K_{\mathrm{trop}}\to\mathbb B$ sending zero to
zero and every nonzero element to one is a semiring morphism:
a maximum is nonzero exactly when a summand is nonzero, and
a product is nonzero exactly when both factors are nonzero.
Tensoring it with $L$ gives a label retraction which recovers
$\lambda$, proving injectivity. At a support prime
$J$ the actual localization is $(v,\lambda)\mapsto[\lambda]_J$.
It sends WQP24 to the meet $[\lambda]_J\wedge[\mu]_J$;
on the diagonal this is $[\lambda]_J$. No numerical positivity
or Haar measure on the arbitrary lattice has been postulated.
The calculation retains the full global original form and its
exact support maps, with no claim that its sign has been settled.
