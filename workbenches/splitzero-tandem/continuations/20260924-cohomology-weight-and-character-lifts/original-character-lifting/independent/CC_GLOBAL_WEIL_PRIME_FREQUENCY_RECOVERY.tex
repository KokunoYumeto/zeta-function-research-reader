\subsection{The entire original form and an explicit remainder}

Retain the original test domain
\[
 \mathcal B_\infty=\left\{h\in C^\infty(0,\infty):
 \sup_{x>0}(x^A+x^{-A})|(x\partial_x)^k h(x)|<\infty
 \text{ for all integers }A,k\ge0\right\}.
\]
For a compact-unit character $\chi$ at a finite modulus, retain its
primitive integer character $\chi_*$, conductor $c_\chi$, parity
$\epsilon_\chi$, and $\alpha=(1/2+\epsilon_\chi)/2>0$.
Use the full original quantities
\begin{equation}\tag{GPR1}
 \begin{gathered}
 \mathcal Mh(s)=\int_0^\infty h(x)x^s\frac{dx}{x},\qquad
 h_T(x)=x^{iT}h(x),\qquad
 H_h(y)=y^{-1}\int_0^\infty h(x)\overline{h(x/y)}\,dx,\\
 M=\int_0^\infty|h(x)|^2dx,\qquad
 K_h=\int_0^\infty|xh'(x)+h(x)/2|^2dx,\qquad
 \rho_h(u)=\frac{|\mathcal Mh(1/2+iu)|^2}{2\pi},\\
 E_h(T)=\delta_{\chi_*,1}
 \left[\mathcal Mh(iT)\overline{\mathcal Mh(1+iT)}
       +\mathcal Mh(1+iT)\overline{\mathcal Mh(iT)}\right].
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Thus $\int\rho_h=M$ and $\int u^2\rho_h=K_h$ by the exact
Parseval calculation GWM8 and GWM13. The measure in both physical
norms is $dx$, and no factor $x^{-1}$ is suppressed.

For $n=p^j$ with $p\nmid c_\chi$ and $j\ge1$ define
\begin{equation}\tag{GPR2}
 a_{\chi,h}(p^j)=\log p\,\chi_*(p)^j H_h(p^j),\qquad
 R_{\chi,h}(T)=
 \sum_{p\nmid c_\chi}\sum_{j\ge1}
 \left[a_{\chi,h}(p^j)e^{iTj\log p}
       +\overline{a_{\chi,h}(p^j)}e^{-iTj\log p}\right].
\end{equation}
GWM6 proves absolute convergence of the coefficient sum, hence
uniform absolute convergence for every real $T$.
This is the full original prime contribution, including every
prime power. Indeed
$H_h(p^{-j})=p^j\overline{H_h(p^j)}$ and
$H_{h_T}(y)=y^{iT}H_h(y)$, so GPR2 is exactly the bracketed
prime sum in GWM5. Its absolute bound is the full quantity
$P_{\chi,h}=2\sum_{p\nmid c_\chi}\log p\sum_{j\ge1}|H_h(p^j)|$.

For every real $T$ with $t=|T|\ge\max\{1,4\alpha\}$ the complete
global form has the exact identity
\begin{equation}\tag{GPR3}
 \begin{split}
 Q_\chi(h_T)={}&E_h(T)-R_{\chi,h}(T)+r_{\alpha,h}(T)\\
 &+M\left[
 \log(t/2)-\log(2\pi)-\gamma+\log2+\gamma
 +\sum_{p\mid c_\chi}v_p(c_\chi)\log p-\log|\mathfrak a|
 \right],\\
 r_{\alpha,h}(T)={}&
 \int_{\mathbb R}\rho_h(u)
 \left[
   \Re\psi\left(\alpha+\frac{i(u-T)}2\right)-\log(t/2)
 \right]du.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The original Euler constant and both logarithms from the real
principal value remain in the first display. Their evaluated
sum $-\log(2\pi)-\gamma+\log2+\gamma=-\log\pi$ is proved in
WQP17--19, rather than omitted from the formula.
GPR3 follows directly from GWM5 and GWM7 after the exact change
$u=t_{\mathrm{Mellin}}+T$ in the latter integral.
The different, endpoints, conductor and all prime correlations
remain summands of the same original form.

The remainder in that exact identity satisfies
\begin{equation}\tag{GPR4}
 \begin{split}
 |r_{\alpha,h}(T)|\le{}&
 \frac{4M+2\sqrt{MK_h}+3K_h/\alpha}{t}\\
 &+\frac{
 8\alpha^2M+
 4K_h\bigl(|\log\alpha|+\alpha^{-1}+\log2+\log t\bigr)}
 {t^2}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
In particular this remainder tends to zero; it has not been
discarded or absorbed into a change of the arithmetic function.

\paragraph{Proof of GPR4.}
For every $\alpha>0$, $b\in\mathbb R$, the same sum--integral
calculation as GWM12 gives the two-sided estimate
\[
 \left|\Re\psi(\alpha+ib)
       -\frac12\log(\alpha^2+b^2)\right|
 \le \int_0^\infty
 \left|\frac{d}{dx}
       \frac{x+\alpha}{(x+\alpha)^2+b^2}\right|dx
 \le\frac1\alpha.
\]
This follows by comparing every value at an integer with the
integral on its unit interval and taking the limit in the
digamma series. If $|b|\ge\alpha$, the same total variation is
$1/|b|-\alpha/(\alpha^2+b^2)\le1/|b|$; at $|b|=\alpha$
the formula agrees with the monotone case.

On $|u|\le t/2$, put $b=(u-T)/2$.
Then $|b|\ge t/4\ge\alpha$. Also
\[
 \begin{split}
 &\frac12\log\left(\alpha^2+\frac{(u-T)^2}{4}\right)
                          -\log(t/2)\\
 &\hspace{12pt}=
 \log|1-u/T|+
 \frac12\log\left(1+\frac{4\alpha^2}{(u-T)^2}\right).
 \end{split}
\]
The absolute value is at most
$2|u|/t+8\alpha^2/t^2$, because $|u/T|\le1/2$ and
$\log(1+v)\le v$ for $v\ge0$. Thus the central part of the
absolute remainder integral is at most
\[
 \frac{4M+2\int |u|\rho_h(u)du}{t}
       +\frac{8\alpha^2M}{t^2}
 \le \frac{4M+2\sqrt{MK_h}}{t}
       +\frac{8\alpha^2M}{t^2}.
\]
The final inequality is Cauchy--Schwarz for the displayed
original positive density $\rho_h$.

On $|u|>t/2$ one has
$|b|\le(|u|+t)/2\le3|u|/2$ and
\[
 \left|\frac12\log(\alpha^2+b^2)\right|
 \le|\log\alpha|+\frac{|b|}{\alpha}.
 \]
The global digamma estimate therefore bounds the integrand's
absolute factor by
$|\log\alpha|+\alpha^{-1}+
 3|u|/(2\alpha)+\log t+\log2$.
Finally
\[
 \int_{|u|>t/2}\rho_h(u)du\le\frac{4K_h}{t^2},
 \qquad
 \int_{|u|>t/2}|u|\rho_h(u)du\le\frac{2K_h}{t}.
\]
These follow directly from $u^2\ge t^2/4$ and
$|u|\le2u^2/t$ on that set. They give precisely the remaining
terms in GPR4. This proves the full remainder bound.

The endpoint function $E_h(T)$ tends to zero faster than every
inverse power of $|T|$. To prove this without deleting either
endpoint, write each original integral, for $\sigma=0,1$, as
\[
 \mathcal Mh(\sigma+iT)=
 \int_{\mathbb R}e^{\sigma v}h(e^v)e^{iTv}\,dv.
\]
Every derivative of $e^{\sigma v}h(e^v)$ decreases
exponentially at both ends to every prescribed rate, by the
specified seminorms of $\mathcal B_\infty$.
Repeated integration by parts, with vanishing endpoint terms,
proves the assertion for each Mellin factor, and hence for
both terms of $E_h(T)$ separately.

\subsection{Direct recovery from the full global form}

Every original coefficient in GPR2 can be recovered from
$Q_\chi(h_T)$ itself; no removed real term is used as a
replacement form. Fix $T_0=\max\{1,4\alpha\}$. For every
$\nu>0$,
\begin{equation}\tag{GPR5}
 \lim_{R\to\infty}\frac1{R-T_0}
 \int_{T_0}^{R}Q_\chi(h_T)e^{-i\nu T}\,dT
 =
 \begin{cases}
  -\log p\,\chi_*(p)^jH_h(p^j),
       &\nu=j\log p,\ p\nmid c_\chi,\ j\ge1,\\
  0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
There is at most one pair $(p,j)$ in the first line.
Indeed $p^j=q^k$ for primes $p,q$ implies $p=q$ by unique
factorization and then $j=k$.

To prove the limit, apply GPR3 retaining all its terms.
The mean of $r_{\alpha,h}(T)e^{-i\nu T}$ tends to zero
because $r_{\alpha,h}(T)\to0$; splitting the integral at a
fixed large point proves this directly.
The same argument applies separately to each endpoint term.
Every constant contribution in GPR3 has a vanishing oscillatory
mean since $\nu\ne0$.
For the remaining logarithm, integration by parts gives
\[
 \int_{T_0}^R\log(T/2)e^{-i\nu T}dT
 =
 \left[\frac{\log(T/2)e^{-i\nu T}}{-i\nu}\right]_{T_0}^R
 +\frac1{i\nu}\int_{T_0}^R\frac{e^{-i\nu T}}T\,dT.
\]
The absolute value is $O_\nu(1+\log R)$, and division by
$R-T_0$ gives zero.
Each positive frequency in GPR2 contributes its coefficient
exactly when that frequency equals $\nu$ and zero otherwise;
the negative frequencies never equal $\nu>0$.
Uniform absolute convergence of GPR2 permits passage from
finite sums to the entire prime sum: the integral mean of
any omitted tail is bounded by its absolute coefficient
sum, uniformly in $R$. This proves GPR5 for all original
primes simultaneously.

For the trivial compact character, every rational prime is
unramified, and hence every $H_h(p^j)$ is obtained from GPR5
by dividing by the explicitly nonzero number $-\log p$.
For other characters, division is by
$-\log p\,\chi_*(p)^j$, also nonzero at every prime present
in their original valuation-shell term.
The ramified receiving contribution remains
$\sum_{p\mid c_\chi}v_p(c_\chi)\log p\,M$ in GPR3.
The original mass itself is recovered by
\begin{equation}\tag{GPR6}
 \lim_{|T|\to\infty}\frac{Q_\chi(h_T)}{\log|T|}=M.
\end{equation}
This follows from boundedness of the complete prime series,
the proved vanishing of the exact remainder and endpoints,
and GPR3. It accounts for every ramified term without
inventing a missing prime-power correlation at such a prime.

 D_T:\mathcal V\longrightarrow\mathcal B/\mathcal W,

\subsection{The original prime powers and original zeta from the entire curve}

Now fix the actual original test $h=f_1$ from GPR7, with no
change of variable in its definition, and put
$q_h(T)=Q_1(x^{iT}h)$ for every real $T$.
This is a continuous function: GWM5's prime series converges
locally uniformly, its endpoints are continuous, and the
digamma integral is continuous by a Schwartz majorant for
$T$ in each compact interval.
The exact positivity needed for the inverse is
\begin{equation}\tag{GPR13}
 h(y)>0\quad(y>0),\qquad
 H_h(y)=y^{-1}\int_0^\infty h(x)h(x/y)\,dx>0\quad(y>0).
\end{equation}
For $y\ge1$, each term
$\eta(ny)=\pi(ny)^2(\pi(ny)^2-3/2)e^{-\pi(ny)^2}$
is positive, since $\pi>3/2$ and $n\ge1$.
The defining sum is convergent, so $h(y)>0$ there.
For $0<y<1$, the proved original Poisson identity ETC9 gives
$h(y)=y^{-1}h(1/y)>0$.
The integrand defining $H_h(y)$ is therefore continuous
and strictly positive. Its integral is finite by the already
proved endpoint estimates, and its integral on any compact
interval of positive length is strictly positive.
This proves both assertions.

For every nonzero real $\omega$, the following symmetric
average of the whole original global form exists:
\begin{equation}\tag{GPR14}
 \begin{split}
 B_h(\omega)
 &:=\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac1{2R}
                 \int_{-R}^Rq_h(T)e^{-i\omega T}\,dT\\
 &=
 \begin{cases}
 -\log p\,H_h(p^j),&
        \omega=j\log p\ \text{or}\ \omega=-j\log p,\quad j\ge1,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
To prove this, use GPR3 separately on the two tails
$|T|\ge T_0$. The bounded middle interval has an integral
which, divided by $2R$, tends to zero by continuity.
The exact remainder and endpoints have vanishing mean
as before. The retained constants have vanishing
nonzero-frequency mean.
On each tail, integration by parts of
$\log(|T|/2)e^{-i\omega T}$ gives a bound
$O_\omega(1+\log R)$, so this term also has mean zero.
Finally the absolutely convergent prime series has the
symmetric average of its individual exponential terms;
the uniform tail estimate used in GPR5 justifies passing
to its whole sum. Its coefficients are real in this
actual case. These facts prove GPR14.
No growing term was subtracted from $q_h$ in the
definition of $B_h$, and no completed arithmetic
function occurs in this averaging operation.

Every coefficient at $\pm j\log p$ is nonzero by GPR13.
Thus the full nonzero-frequency support of GPR14 is
exactly the set $\{\pm j\log p:p\text{ prime},j\ge1\}$.
More precisely define, for every integer $n\ge2$,
\begin{equation}\tag{GPR15}
 \Lambda_q(n)=-\frac{B_h(\log n)}{H_h(n)}.
\end{equation}
The denominator is an explicitly defined strictly
positive original correlation, so this is an everywhere
defined inverse for all those integers. GPR14 proves
\[
 \Lambda_q(n)=
 \begin{cases}
 \log p,&n=p^j,\ p\text{ prime},\ j\ge1,\\
 0,&n\text{ is not a prime power}.
 \end{cases}
\]
In particular it recovers the original von Mangoldt
function, with every prime power and its original
multiplicity index $j$. The rational primes themselves
are exactly
$\{n\ge2:\Lambda_q(n)=\log n\}$:
at $n=p^j$ equality means $j=1$, and a zero value cannot
equal $\log n>0$. This reconstructs the arithmetic
prime set from the full global curve and the specified
original positive test, rather than testing only a
finite list of frequencies.

For $\operatorname{Re}s>1$ form both original series
from those recovered coefficients:
\begin{equation}\tag{GPR16}
 \begin{gathered}
 D_q(s)=\sum_{n\ge2}\Lambda_q(n)n^{-s},\qquad
 L_q(s)=\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda_q(n)}{\log n}\,n^{-s},\\
 Z_q(s)=\exp(L_q(s)).
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
They retain the original exponent $s$.
The coefficient bounds
$0\le\Lambda_q(n)\le\log n$ and
$0\le\Lambda_q(n)/\log n\le1$
show absolute, locally uniform convergence of both
series on this half-plane. For every fixed
$\delta>0$ the majorants
$\sum(\log n)n^{-1-\delta}$ and
$\sum n^{-1-\delta}$ converge, by integration
comparison; any fixed number of derivatives adds
only a power of $\log n$, which obeys the same
comparison after decreasing $\delta$.
Consequently differentiation term by term gives
$L_q'(s)=-D_q(s)$.

The exact original arithmetic reconstruction is
\begin{equation}\tag{GPR17}
 \begin{gathered}
 Z_q(s)=\prod_{p\ {\rm prime}}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}
       =\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-s}=\zeta(s),\\
 -\frac{\zeta'(s)}{\zeta(s)}=D_q(s),\qquad
 \log\zeta(s)=L_q(s),\qquad \operatorname{Re}s>1.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Here $\log\zeta$ denotes the analytic branch with
$\log\zeta(\sigma+it)\to0$ uniformly in $t$ as
$\sigma\to+\infty$.
To prove every equality, GPR15 gives
\[
 L_q(s)=\sum_p\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{p^{-js}}j.
\]
Its absolute convergence follows either from GPR16 or
from
$\sum_p p^{-\sigma}/(1-p^{-\sigma})
 \le(1-2^{-\sigma})^{-1}\sum_{n\ge2}n^{-\sigma}<\infty$.
For each individual prime the series is the analytic
function $-\log(1-p^{-s})$, as follows by integrating
the absolutely convergent geometric series from zero
to $p^{-s}$ inside the unit disk.
Exponentiating finite sums and taking their absolutely
convergent limit proves the product formula.
A finite such product expands into
$\sum n^{-s}$ over integers whose prime divisors occur
in that product. Unique factorization proves the
coefficient is exactly one at each such integer.
As the finite sets of primes increase, every positive
integer is eventually included, and domination by
$\sum n^{-\sigma}$ proves the original full Dirichlet
series formula. This proves $Z_q=\zeta$.
The exponential formula shows nonvanishing on the
half-plane, and its derivative gives the stated original
logarithmic derivative, with the sign retained.
Finally $|L_q(\sigma+it)|\le\sum_{n\ge2}n^{-\sigma}\to0$
as $\sigma\to\infty$, which proves the specified branch.
Any two analytic logarithms on this connected domain
differ by a constant integer multiple of $2\pi i$;
this limiting condition selects exactly the one in
GPR17. Thus neither a multiplicative constant nor an
unrecorded branch choice survives the inverse.

GPR17 determines the original meromorphic zeta uniquely
by continuation from its half-plane of absolute
convergence. The already proved exact expression GPR8,
with $b=1$, also supplies its continued comparison:
the entire original Mellin integral is the product
of that same $\zeta(s)$ and its two displayed Gamma
terms. Its exceptional-point interpretation and
divisor remain those of that full product, not an
assertion that either Gamma summand is an entire unit.
No information about the location of nontrivial

\paragraph{Full support maps at the exponential stage.}
For any fixed nonzero frequency, taking its existing
average is a complex-linear functional on the space
of curves for which the average exists. Its
common-label lift preserves the entire label.
In particular on the explicit line
$\mathbb C q_h$ all the maps
$q\mapsto B_q(\log n)$,
$q\mapsto-B_q(\log n)/H_h(n)$,
$q\mapsto D_q$, and $q\mapsto L_q$
are linear, defined on the whole line, and have the
exact lifts $(q,\lambda)\mapsto(F(q),\lambda)$.
Every required infinite series converges there
because its coefficients are a fixed complex
multiple of those proved in GPR16.
At the original top-supported curve, a prime-power
coefficient has nonzero amplitude and top support;
at a non-prime-power integer the returned value
is $e=(0,1_L)$ rather than $\tau=(0,0_L)$.
Thus the zero outputs themselves retain the
programme's supported-zero distinction.

Exponentiation is not a linear or zero-preserving
map: $\exp(0)=1$. Its faithful support accounting
cannot be obtained by falsely preserving a
non-top label on that nonzero amplitude.
The exact full retained reconstruction is instead
\begin{equation}\tag{GPR18}
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal R_L:G_L(\mathbb C q_h)&\longrightarrow
 G_L(\mathbb C q_h)\times
 G_L\bigl(\mathcal O(\{\operatorname{Re}s>1\})\bigr),\\
 (q,\lambda)&\longmapsto
       \bigl((q,\lambda),(\exp(L_q),1_L)\bigr).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The second label is necessarily top because this
amplitude is nowhere zero. It is the top label
inserted by the nonzero constant term of the
original exponential series. Projection onto
the first coordinate is a left inverse, proving
that every original label survives the complete
record, even for zero input amplitudes.
At $q=q_h$ its second component is the original
$\zeta$ of GPR17. At zero amplitude its second
component is one and its first component still
distinguishes every $(0,\lambda)$.
This is a map of sets with the displayed linear
submaps, not a claim that exponentiation is a
semimodule homomorphism.

The tropical receiver sends every retained
original label to $1_{K_{\mathrm{trop}}}\otimes\lambda$
as proved in WQP24, and its retraction recovers
that label. The actual support localization sends
it to $[\lambda]_J$. These apply separately to
the two explicitly retained factors in GPR18;
no replacement of $\operatorname{Spec}G_L(\mathbb Z)$
by its recovered arithmetic prime set has been
made. GPR15 reconstructs the arithmetic prime
data, while these maps retain the entire original
lattice geometry and its supported zeros.
