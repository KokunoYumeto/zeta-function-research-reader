\subsection{All finite divisor coefficients and the archimedean bound}

Let $L$ be a bounded distributive lattice, $0_L\ne1_L$. Retain an
arbitrary Arakelov divisor with finite support at the finite places:
\begin{equation}\tag{RDT1}
\begin{gathered}
D=\sum_p d_p[p]+d_\infty[\infty],\quad
d_p\in\mathbb Z,\quad d_\infty\in\mathbb R,\\
I(D)=\{q\in\mathbb Q:v_p(q)\ge-d_p\text{ for every }p\},\quad
v_p(0)=+\infty,\\
N=\prod_p p^{-d_p}>0,\quad a=e^{d_\infty}>0,\quad b=a/N,\qquad
\deg D=\sum_p d_p\log p+d_\infty.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Here $N$ is a positive rational number, not necessarily an integer.
For nonzero rational $q$, the inequalities defining $I(D)$ say that
$v_p(q/N)\ge0$ at every prime. Writing a rational number as a reduced
fraction proves that this is equivalent to $q/N\in\mathbb Z$.
The assertion also holds at zero. Hence, with the original parameters
still present,
\begin{equation}\tag{RDT2}
I(D)=N\mathbb Z,\qquad
\prod_p\operatorname{vol}(p^{-d_p}\mathbb Z_p)
=\prod_p p^{d_p}=N^{-1},\qquad b=e^{\deg D}.
\end{equation}
The local measure is $\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb Z_p)=1$, and the
real measure is Lebesgue measure. No finite-prime contribution is
absorbed into a replacement for $D$.

The additive monoid used for sections is
$G_L(I(D))=\{(0,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}\cup
(I(D)\times\{1_L\})$, with addition
$(x,\lambda)+(z,\mu)=(x+z,\lambda\vee\mu)$ and identity
$\tau=(0,0_L)$. A fractional ideal need not be a ring, so this
notation here denotes the additive monoid, not an assertion of
multiplicative closure. Its supported zero is $e=(0,1_L)$.

\subsection{The complete supported bounded section module}

For $r_+=\{*,1,\ldots,r\}$, define
\begin{equation}\tag{RDT3}
\begin{split}
\mathcal H_L^0(D)(r_+)=\{((q_j,\lambda_j))_{j=1}^r:\;&q_j\in I(D),
\ q_j\ne0\Rightarrow\lambda_j=1_L,\\[-2pt]
&-a\le\sum_{j\in J}q_j\le a
\text{ for every }J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,r\}\}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The empty sum is $\tau$ and has amplitude zero. The maximum of the
subset amplitudes is the sum of positive coordinates, and the minimum
is the sum of negative coordinates. Consequently the complete
condition is precisely
\begin{equation}\tag{RDT4}
q_j=Nn_j,\quad n_j\in\mathbb Z,\qquad
N\sum_{n_j>0}n_j\le a,\qquad
N\sum_{n_j<0}|n_j|\le a.
\end{equation}
These are two separate bounds. They do not impose a single bound on
$\sum_j|q_j|$. This distinction is the one between the source's
$\mathbb S$ construction and its preceding
$\mathbb S[\pm1]$ construction.
For a pointed map $f:r_+\to s_+$ the full structure map is
\begin{equation}\tag{RDT5}
((q_j,\lambda_j))_j\longmapsto
\left(\sum_{f(j)=i}q_j,\ \bigvee_{f(j)=i}\lambda_j\right)_{i=1}^s.
\end{equation}
Empty fibres give $(0,0_L)$; coordinates sent to $*$ are discarded.
Every subset sum of the output is a subset sum of the input over a
disjoint union of fibres, hence remains in $[-a,a]$. If an output
amplitude is nonzero, one summand was nonzero and had top support,
so its output label is top. Composition follows from associativity
of sums and joins. Thus these are actual pointed $\Gamma$-module
maps at all levels.
Division of each amplitude by the retained positive number $N$ gives
the exact pointed natural isomorphism
\begin{equation}\tag{RDT6}
\mathcal H_L^0(D)\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\mathcal E_{L,b},\qquad
((q_j,\lambda_j))\longmapsto((q_j/N,\lambda_j)),\qquad
((n_j,\lambda_j))\longmapsto((Nn_j,\lambda_j))
\end{equation}
as its inverse. The target is exactly the module in SRR2--8, with
the bound $[-b,b]$; (RDT4) proves the domain and range, and (RDT5)
proves naturality. We retain the source, target, $N$, and both maps.
The amplitude map onto Connes--Consani's section module has, over
each admitted tuple $(q_j)$, exactly the fibre
$L^{\{j:q_j=0\}}$. Its zero fibre is $H(L,\vee,0_L)$. For $b\ge1$
it has no pointed natural section: the admitted pair $(N,-N)$ has
unique coordinate lifts, and its fold is $e$; the pointed lift of
zero is $\tau$. Projection naturality and fold naturality would
identify these two distinct labels. For $0<b<1$, (RDT4) forces
every amplitude to be zero in every level, and gives exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{RDT7}
\mathcal H_L^0(D)=H(L,\vee,0_L)
\qquad(0<b<1).
\end{equation}
The absence of nonzero amplitude sections does not remove this fibre.
For finite $L$, the existing complete SRR6--8 proof transported by
(RDT6) gives, with $J(L)$ the nonzero join-irreducibles and
$J_{<}(L)=J(L)\setminus\{1_L\}$,
\begin{equation}\tag{RDT8}
\dim_{\mathbb S}\mathcal H_L^0(D)=
\begin{cases}
|J(L)|,&0<b<1,\\
n+1+|J_{<}(L)|,&2^{n-1}\le b<2^n,\quad b\ge1.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The exact generator definition is the equality-tolerance version of
the source's Definition \texttt{generator}. The proof is the cited
SRR proof with the inverse in (RDT6) applied to each generator and
each admissible sum, so both minimality and attainability transfer.
For infinite $L$ no finite set generates: a set of $t$ generators
has at most $2^t$ subset sums while all points $(0,\lambda)$ remain.
\subsection{Exact counts in every level}
Let $m=\lfloor b\rfloor$. For a tuple in (RDT4), let $u$ be its
number of positive coordinates, $v$ its number of negative
coordinates and $w=r-u-v$ its number of zero coordinates. The number
of positive integer $u$-tuples with total at most $m$ is
$\binom m u$, including the value one when $u=0$ and the value zero
when $u>m$. To prove this for $u>0$, the partial sums
$n_1,n_1+n_2,\ldots,n_1+\cdots+n_u$ are exactly increasing
$u$-element subsets of $\{1,\ldots,m\}$, with inverse successive
differences. The same count applies independently to the magnitudes
of the negative coordinates. Choosing their disjoint positions gives
\begin{equation}\tag{RDT9}
\#\mathcal H_L^0(D)(r_+)
=\sum_{\substack{u,v\ge0\\u+v\le r}}
\frac{r!}{u!v!(r-u-v)!}
\binom m u\binom m v\,|L|^{r-u-v}
\qquad(L\text{ finite}).
\end{equation}
The factor $|L|^{r-u-v}$ counts all zero labels independently.
Deleting this factor gives the amplitude-tuple count, not the full
supported count. In particular the amplitude count in level $1_+$
is $2m+1$, while the full supported count is $2m+|L|$.
At $r=0$ the formula gives the single pointed empty tuple. For
arbitrary infinite $L$ the fibre formula preceding (RDT7), rather
than a finite cardinality assertion, remains exact.
Vary only the original archimedean bound: for $R>0$ the divisor
$D_R=D+\log R[\infty]$ retains $I(D)$ and has bound $aR$.
The nonzero amplitude count in level $1_+$ is
\begin{equation}\tag{RDT10}
A_D(R)=\#\{q\in I(D)\setminus\{0\}:|q|\le aR\}
=2\lfloor bR\rfloor,\qquad
dA_D=2\sum_{n\ge1}\delta_{n/b}.
\end{equation}
This is an exact image of the all-level module under amplitude
projection and degree-one counting. It is not a faithful replacement
for that module, and counting is not asserted to be a $\Gamma$-module
morphism. The full fibres and folds remain (RDT3)--(RDT5).
\subsection{The original theta source and its diagonal antecedent}
Retain the entire real function and every finite factor:
\begin{equation}\tag{RDT11}
\begin{gathered}
\eta(x)=\left(\pi^2x^4-\frac{3\pi}{2}x^2\right)e^{-\pi x^2},\\
\xi_D(x)=\eta(x_\infty/a)\prod_p\mathbf1_{p^{-d_p}\mathbb Z_p}(x_p),\\
\xi_D(0)=0,\qquad
\int_{\mathbb A}\xi_D
=\frac{a}{N}\left(\pi^2\frac{3}{4\pi^2}
                    -\frac{3\pi}{2}\frac{1}{2\pi}\right)=0.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
There are finitely many nonreference finite factors, so this is an
actual Bruhat--Schwartz function in the original adelic space. For
$y>0$ and $u\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{\times}$, the admissible rational
summands are exactly $q=Nn$, $n\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$.
Its original theta image is therefore
\begin{equation}\tag{RDT12}
f_D(y,u)=E\xi_D(y,u)
=2\sum_{n\ge1}\eta(Nny/a)=2\sum_{n\ge1}\eta(ny/b).
\end{equation}
The first expression retains the original finite and real parameters;
the second records their exact comparison. The sums and all fixed
$y>0$ derivatives converge absolutely by Gaussian decay.
For the diagonal source of DOR20a, keep $y_1,y_2>0$ and put
\begin{equation}\tag{RDT13}
\begin{gathered}
F_a(z)=\pi(z/a)(\pi(z/a)^2-\tfrac32)e^{-\pi(z/a)^2/2},\quad
G_a(z)=(z/a)e^{-\pi(z/a)^2/2},\\
t_{D,y}(x_1,x_2)=F_a(x_{1,\infty}/y_1)G_a(x_{2,\infty}/y_2)
\prod_p\mathbf1_{p^{-d_p}\mathbb Z_p}(x_{1,p})
          \mathbf1_{p^{-d_p}\mathbb Z_p}(x_{2,p}),\\
\mu_y t_{D,y}(x)=t_{D,y}(y_1x_\infty,x_f;y_2x_\infty,x_f)
=\xi_D(x).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Both real factors are odd. Each has zero evaluation and zero additive
integral; pairing rational summands $q,-q$ gives zero independent
theta image for each factor. The product on the diagonal is exactly
$F_a(z)G_a(z)=\eta(z/a)$, proving (RDT13), with diagonal moment
zero by (RDT11). The DMR/DOR maps therefore receive this actual
tensor in their stipulated joint kernel. This is not a freely
chosen function without an original source antecedent.
Combining (RDT10) and (RDT12) gives the exact whole-scale transform
\begin{equation}\tag{RDT14}
\begin{split}
f_D(y,u)&=\int_{(0,\infty)}\eta(yR)\,dA_D(R),\\
&=-y\int_0^\infty A_D(R)\eta'(yR)\,dR,\\
\eta'(x)&=\left[4\pi^2x^3-3\pi x
-2\pi x\left(\pi^2x^4-\frac{3\pi}{2}x^2\right)\right]e^{-\pi x^2}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The first integral is absolutely convergent, since its total variation
integral is $2\sum_{n\ge1}|\eta(ny/b)|<\infty$. Integration by parts
on finite intervals, then passage to the endpoints, proves the
second equality. Near zero $A_D$ is zero; at infinity
$A_D(R)\le2bR$ and $\eta(yR)$ and its derivative have Gaussian
decay. These estimates prove both vanished boundary terms and
absolute integrability of the second expression. No endpoint Mellin
integral is exchanged with the integer sum in this argument.
\subsection{Full Mellin factors, endpoints and recovery}
Use $M_D(s)=\int_0^\infty f_D(y,u)y^s\,dy/y$.
For $\operatorname{Re}s>1$, absolute integration of the original
Gaussian monomials gives
\begin{equation}\tag{RDT15}
M_D(s)=2\left(\frac aN\right)^s\zeta(s)
\left\{
\frac{\pi^2\Gamma((s+4)/2)}{2\pi^{(s+4)/2}}
-\frac{3\pi\Gamma((s+2)/2)}{4\pi^{(s+2)/2}}
\right\}.
\end{equation}
For completeness its original Poisson comparison is exact. For the
Fourier convention $\widehat f(t)=\int f(x)e^{-2\pi ixt}dx$,
\[
\widehat{x^2e^{-\pi x^2}}(t)
=\left(\frac1{2\pi}-t^2\right)e^{-\pi t^2},\qquad
\widehat{x^4e^{-\pi x^2}}(t)
=\left(t^4-\frac{3t^2}{\pi}+\frac3{4\pi^2}\right)e^{-\pi t^2}.
\]
These follow by differentiating the Gaussian Fourier identity twice
and four times, using $\widehat{xf}=-(2\pi i)^{-1}\partial_t\widehat f$.
Multiplying by the two coefficients in (RDT11) gives
$\widehat\eta=\eta$, including cancellation of the two constant terms.
Poisson summation for this Schwartz function, whose value and integral
at zero vanish, gives for $h(z)=2\sum_{n\ge1}\eta(nz)$
$h(z)=z^{-1}h(z^{-1})$. The Gaussian series for $z\ge1$ and this
identity prove faster-than-every-power decay at both ends, including
all logarithmic derivatives. Hence $M_D$ is entire, and (RDT15)
continues as that entire integral with all displayed factors retained.

The entire adelic Fourier comparison retains the opposite divisor and
the full volume factor as well. At each finite place take the original
additive character of conductor $\mathbb Z_p$ and its self-dual
measure. The annihilator of $p^{-d_p}\mathbb Z_p$ is
$p^{d_p}\mathbb Z_p$. The integral of its character is the ball's
volume $p^{d_p}$ on that annihilator; off the annihilator, translation
by an element of the ball multiplies the integral by a scalar
different from one and therefore forces it to zero. Real scaling in
the proved Gaussian Fourier formula then gives
\begin{equation}\tag{RDT15a}
\begin{gathered}
\widehat{\mathbf1_{p^{-d_p}\mathbb Z_p}}
=p^{d_p}\mathbf1_{p^{d_p}\mathbb Z_p},\qquad
\widehat{\eta(\,\cdot\,/a)}(t)=a\eta(at),\\
\widehat{\xi_D}=\frac aN\xi_{-D},\qquad
f_D(y,u)=\frac{a}{Ny}f_{-D}(1/y,u^{-1}),\qquad
M_D(s)=\frac aN M_{-D}(1-s).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Poisson summation on the original adelic source has zero source
endpoints by (RDT11), so it proves the middle theta identity,
including the modulus $y^{-1}$. Its compact-unit arguments are
retained even though these particular functions are independent of
them. The last identity follows by an absolutely convergent Mellin
substitution. Thus self-duality of the real polynomial Gaussian has
not been incorrectly transferred to the whole source: its full dual
is the displayed multiple of the source for $-D$.

At $s=0$ the expression in braces has value
$1/2-3/4=-1/4$, while $\zeta(0)=-1/2$; thus $M_D(0)=1/4$.
The Mellin integral of $h(z)=z^{-1}h(z^{-1})$ gives $M_h(1)=M_h(0)$
by an absolutely convergent substitution. Real scaling in the original
integral then proves
\begin{equation}\tag{RDT16}
M_D(0)=\frac14,\qquad M_D(1)=\frac{a}{4N},\qquad
\frac{M_D(1)}{M_D(0)}=\frac aN=e^{\deg D}.
\end{equation}
Thus both endpoint constants and the entire finite additive volume
are explicit. In particular the zero additive integral (RDT11) is
not the endpoint integral in (RDT16).

The section module can now be recovered from the retained data
$(N,L,f_D)$, by the exact inverse formulas
\begin{equation}\tag{RDT17}
d_p=-v_p(N),\qquad
a=N\frac{M_D(1)}{M_D(0)},\qquad
d_\infty=\log\left(N\frac{M_D(1)}{M_D(0)}\right),
\end{equation}
followed by (RDT3)--(RDT5). This is a proved inverse on the specified
divisor family. An arbitrary test function is not asserted to lie in
that family. The theta function alone recovers $b$; retaining $N$
and $L$ is necessary to recover the full original presentation and
all its labelled section fibres.

\subsection{Exact principal-divisor covariance}

For $c\in\mathbb Q^\times$, use the full principal divisor and
the original dilation convention
\begin{equation}\tag{RDT18}
\begin{gathered}
\operatorname{div}(c)=\sum_pv_p(c)[p]-\log|c|_\infty[\infty],
\qquad (U_c\xi)(x)=\xi(c^{-1}x),\\
D'=D+\operatorname{div}(c),\quad
N'=N/|c|_\infty,\quad a'=a/|c|_\infty,\quad b'=b,\\
\mathcal H_L^0(D)\longrightarrow\mathcal H_L^0(D'),\qquad
((q_j,\lambda_j))\longmapsto((q_j/c,\lambda_j)),\\
\xi_{D'}=U_{1/c}\xi_D,\qquad
t_{D',y}=(U_{1/c}\otimes U_{1/c})t_{D,y},\qquad f_{D'}=f_D.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The rational product formula here is the elementary equality
$\prod_pp^{v_p(c)}=|c|_\infty$. It gives the displayed $N'$ and
$b'$, while the valuation and real inequalities give the section
map and its inverse, multiplication by $c$. These maps commute with
all folds and keep every label. For negative $c$, the two signed
bounds are interchanged. The finite indicators in $\xi_D(cx)$ give
exactly $p^{-d_p-v_p(c)}\mathbb Z_p$; the real function is even.
In the tensor identity both odd factors change sign when $c<0$,
so the two signs cancel. Finally $q\mapsto cq$ permutes
$\mathbb Q^\times$ in the absolutely convergent theta sum, proving
$EU_{1/c}\xi_D=E\xi_D$.

Two divisors have the same $f_D$ if and only if they have the same
$b$, by (RDT12) and (RDT16). This is equivalent to their difference
being principal: choose the positive rational $c=N/N'$, and equality
of $a/N$ and $a'/N'$ gives the infinite coefficient in (RDT18).
Thus the fibres of $D\mapsto f_D$ are exactly its principal-divisor
orbits. The actual representative and label data are restored by
(RDT17), rather than assumed recoverable from the quotient alone.

\subsection{Effectivity, positive transforms, and the original trace}

For $z\ge1$ every term of $h(z)$ is strictly positive, because
$\pi(nz)^2>3/2$. For $0<z<1$ its exact Poisson identity gives
$h(z)=z^{-1}h(z^{-1})>0$. Therefore
\begin{equation}\tag{RDT19}
f_D(y,u)>0\quad\text{for all }y>0,\ u\in K,
\quad\text{for every original divisor }D.
\end{equation}
On the other hand a nonzero amplitude section at the original bound
exists exactly when $b\ge1$, by (RDT4). For the concrete original
divisor $D=-(\log2)[\infty]$ one has
\[
\begin{gathered}
N=1,\quad a=b=1/2,\quad
\mathcal H_L^0(D)=H(L,\vee,0_L),\\
f_D(y)=2\sum_{n\ge1}\eta(2ny)>0,\qquad
(M_D(0),M_D(1))=(1/4,1/8).
\end{gathered}
\]
There is no contradiction: (RDT14) uses sections of every $D_R$,
with its first nonzero amplitude at $R=1/b$, not only sections at
$R=1$. It is a proved relation between two different effectivity
tests. Moreover $f_D\in E(T_0)=V$ by (RDT11), so the original
CCM trace pairing satisfies
\begin{equation}\tag{RDT20}
\langle f_D,g\rangle=0\qquad(g\in B).
\end{equation}
This is the exact cited theorem \texttt{pairTrvanishV} on the original
test space, with the actual antecedent already supplied. In the
endpoint-retaining quotient of CTO21--22a the same source has
coordinates $(0,1/4,a/(4N))$. Thus its positive function, original
degree, labelled sections, and zero arithmetic trace all coexist
in one explicit diagram. A divisor on the arithmetic curve has not
been silently identified with an effective correspondence on its
square. The latter comparison in the Weil argument remains a
different mathematical map.

All scalar linear comparisons above lift at a fixed admissible
support by $(x,\lambda)\mapsto(Fx,\lambda)$. Every nonzero amplitude
has label $1_L$; an amplitude cancellation keeps that label and gives
$e$, while a zero input with lower label retains that exact label.
The entire section module was defined with coordinatewise labels
in (RDT3), so it is not reconstructed from scalar counting alone.
For an infinite series or integral, take its proved amplitude limit
at a fixed label; no infinite join in an arbitrary lattice is assumed.

\subsection{The canonical divisor, finite prime two, and the full Fourier operator}

The two dualities just encountered have different original formulas.
Connes--Consani's signed Serre duality uses $K-D$ with $K=-2[2]$
(their \texttt{serredual}, calculated with all labels in CTD16--20),
whereas the original self-dual adelic Fourier measure in (RDT15a)
gives $-D$. Their exact relation uses the finite idele
\begin{equation}\tag{RDT21}
\begin{gathered}
v_\infty=1,\quad v_2=4,\quad v_p=1\ (p\ne2),\qquad
|v|=|4|_2=\frac14,\\
D^\vee=K-D,\qquad
N^\vee=\prod_p p^{-(K_p-d_p)}=\frac4N,\qquad
a^\vee=e^{-d_\infty}=\frac1a,\qquad
b^\vee=\frac{a^\vee}{N^\vee}=\frac{N}{4a}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
This idele is not the rational diagonal element $4$: its infinite
component is $1$. With $(U_v\xi)(x)=\xi(v^{-1}x)$, its action on
the full original seed is
\begin{equation}\tag{RDT22}
\begin{gathered}
\xi_{-D}(x)=\eta(ax_\infty)
               \prod_p\mathbf1_{p^{d_p}\mathbb Z_p}(x_p),\\
U_v\xi_{-D}(x)=\eta(ax_\infty)
 \mathbf1_{4\,2^{d_2}\mathbb Z_2}(x_2)
 \prod_{p\ne2}\mathbf1_{p^{d_p}\mathbb Z_p}(x_p)
 =\xi_{K-D}(x).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The prime-two lattice is $4\,2^{d_2}\mathbb Z_2$.
At every other prime $4$ is a unit.
These local equalities prove (RDT22), including its real factor.

Define the actual operator $T_K=U_v\mathcal F$ on the original
adelic Schwartz space, where $\mathcal F$ has the original additive
character and self-dual measures of (RDT15a). A Haar change of
variables proves
\[
\mathcal F U_v=|v|U_{v^{-1}}\mathcal F:
\quad\int\xi(v^{-1}x)\psi(-xy)\,dx
       =|v|\int\xi(z)\psi(-zvy)\,dz.
\]
Writing $\mathcal R\xi(x)=\xi(-x)$, Fourier inversion gives
\begin{equation}\tag{RDT23}
T_K\xi_D=\frac aN\xi_{K-D},\qquad
T_K^2=\frac14\mathcal R,\qquad
T_K^{-1}=4\mathcal R T_K.
\end{equation}
Reflection commutes with $U_v$ and $\mathcal F$, which verifies both
sides of the inverse formula. The original zero-value, zero-integral
source space is preserved: Fourier exchanges these two zero
conditions, and dilation multiplies the integral by $|v|$ while
leaving the value at zero unchanged. Thus these are operators on the
actual theta source, not only identities on a scalar family. On the
even seeds, the product of the two displayed family coefficients is
$\frac aN\frac{N}{4a}=1/4$, as the operator square requires.
The involution $D\mapsto K-D$ has therefore not been incorrectly
transferred to the unscaled operator $T_K$.

The entire rational summation and its Mellin map retain the same
factor. Since the nonzero rational points of $I(K-D)$ are
$q=4n/N$, $n\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$, direct substitution gives
\begin{equation}\tag{RDT24}
\begin{gathered}
f_{K-D}(y,u)=2\sum_{n\ge1}\eta(4any/N)
                       =f_{-D}(4y,u),\\
f_D(y,u)=\frac{a}{Ny}
                  f_{K-D}\bigl(1/(4y),u^{-1}\bigr),\\
M_D(s)=\frac aN\,4^{1-s}M_{K-D}(1-s).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The first equality is the absolutely convergent original theta
series. The second follows from that equality and (RDT15a).
For the third, the Mellin integrals converge absolutely for every
$s$ by the two-end decay already proved. Substituting
$z=1/(4y)$ yields $y^{s-1}=(4z)^{1-s}$ and proves exactly the
stated factor. In original zeta and Gamma factors this says
\begin{equation}\tag{RDT25}
\begin{aligned}
