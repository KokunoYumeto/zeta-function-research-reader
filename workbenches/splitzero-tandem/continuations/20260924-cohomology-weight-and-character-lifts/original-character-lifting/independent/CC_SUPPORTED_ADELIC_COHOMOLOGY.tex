\label{sec:supported-adelic-cohomology}

\paragraph{Original source and exact scope.}

\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.01391v2}{\emph{Riemann--Roch for
$\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z}$}}, original author source

\subsection{The divisor and the full two-leg source}

Let $L$ be a bounded distributive lattice with $0_L\ne1_L$. For an
additive abelian group $A$ put
\begin{equation}\tag{ASC1}
G_L(A)=\{(0,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}\cup(A\times\{1_L\}),\qquad
(x,\lambda)+(y,\mu)=(x+y,\lambda\vee\mu),\quad
\tau=(0,0_L),\quad e=(0,1_L).
\end{equation}
The symbol $H(M)$ for an additive pointed monoid means the functor
$H(M)(r_+)=M^r$, with sums over fibres of a pointed map and empty
sum the monoid identity. No additive inverse relative to $\tau$ is
asserted for a nonzero amplitude.

Retain the original divisor and every local restriction:
\begin{equation}\tag{ASC2}
\begin{gathered}
D=\sum_p d_p[p]+d_\infty[\infty],\qquad
N=\prod_p p^{-d_p}>0,\qquad a=e^{d_\infty}>0,\\
O_f(D)=\prod_p p^{-d_p}\mathbb Z_p=N\widehat{\mathbb Z},\qquad
I(D)=\mathbb Q\cap O_f(D)=N\mathbb Z.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The equality of the finite ideals holds place by place because
$v_p(N)=-d_p$. The last equality follows by writing $q/N$ as a
reduced rational number and testing its valuation at every prime.
All appearances of $N,a$ below refer to these retained original data.

Set $B_L=G_L(\mathbb A_{\mathbb Q})$. The source uses the product
of two monoids, with the two labels independent:
\begin{equation}\tag{ASC3}
P_L=G_L(\mathbb Q)\times G_L(\mathbb A_{\mathbb Q}),\qquad
\Phi_L((q,\alpha),(o,\beta))=(q+o,\alpha\vee\beta).
\end{equation}
Here $q$ is embedded diagonally. This is an additive pointed monoid
map: both orders of adding two inputs give amplitude
$q+q'+o+o'$ and label $\alpha\vee\alpha'\vee\beta\vee\beta'$.
Its value is admissible, since a nonzero output amplitude requires
at least one nonzero input amplitude and hence top output support.

For $n\ge0$ and $r_+=\{*,1,\ldots,r\}$ define $X_n(D)(r_+)$ by
the following full arrays:
\begin{equation}\tag{ASC4}
\begin{gathered}
b_j=(x_j,\lambda_j)\in B_L\quad(1\le j\le r),\\
z_{\ell j}=((q_{\ell j},\alpha_{\ell j}),
             (o_{\ell j},\beta_{\ell j}))\in P_L
       \quad(1\le\ell\le n,\ 1\le j\le r),\\
o_{\ell j,f}\in O_f(D),\qquad
\sum_{\ell=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^r|o_{\ell j,\infty}|\le a.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The empty interior array at $n=0$ has sum zero. At $r=0$ there is
one empty array. The vertex $b$ has no size restriction. Every
amplitude-zero source coordinate has its full independent $L$-label.
In particular $G_L(\mathbb Q)\times G_L(\mathbb A)$ has not been
replaced by $G_L(\mathbb Q\times\mathbb A)$.

\subsection{Every simplicial and pointed-set map}

Write $z_\ell=(z_{\ell j})_j$, with addition componentwise.
For $n\ge1$ use the following face convention, which gives the
source's two faces $b$ and $b+\Phi_Lz$ in degree one:
\begin{equation}\tag{ASC5}
\begin{aligned}
d_0(b;z_1,\ldots,z_n)&=(b;z_1,\ldots,z_{n-1}),\\
d_i(b;z_1,\ldots,z_n)
 &=(b;z_1,\ldots,z_{n-i}+z_{n-i+1},\ldots,z_n)
                 &&(0<i<n),\\
d_n(b;z_1,\ldots,z_n)&=(b+\Phi_Lz_1;z_2,\ldots,z_n).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For $0\le i\le n$, $s_i$ inserts the identity
$0_{P_L}=(\tau,\tau)$ immediately after $z_{n-i}$; $s_n$
inserts it before $z_1$, and $s_0$ appends it after $z_n$.
This convention includes $s_0(b)=(b;0_{P_L})$ when $n=0$.

All these arrays remain admitted. Dropping an interior coordinate
decreases the real absolute sum; merging two decreases it by the
triangle inequality; insertion contributes zero. The finite
components remain in the additive subgroup $O_f(D)$. No support
label is removed from a surviving coordinate except by its displayed
join with another label.

For completeness, the simplicial identities can be checked without
an inverse in either monoid. Form the category whose objects are
$B_L^r$ and whose arrow labelled $z\in P_L^r$ goes from $b$ to
$b+\Phi_Lz$. Composition adds arrow labels; its associativity and
identity laws follow from (ASC3). Its ordinary nerve has the usual
faces deleting a vertex and composing the adjacent arrows.
Reversing the ordinal gives exactly (ASC5) and the stated
degeneracies. Deleting two ordered vertices in either order deletes
the same vertices and sums the same consecutive arrow blocks.
Inserting two identity arrows in either order gives the same array;
deleting an inserted identity either reverses that insertion or
commutes past it. These are respectively the face--face,
degeneracy--degeneracy and face--degeneracy identities.
The restrictions in (ASC4) are stable under every such operation,
so they define a simplicial subset of that nerve.

For a pointed map $f:r_+\to s_+$, sum the vertices and each
interior row over each nonbasepoint fibre of $f$. On amplitudes
these are the original sums; on every leg's supports they are
joins; an empty fibre gives $\tau$ on that leg. The resulting
absolute sum is at most the old sum, and its finite restrictions
hold. These maps commute with (ASC5) and all degeneracies by
additivity of $\Phi_L$. Associativity of sums and joins proves
composition in $\Gamma$. Thus (ASC4)--(ASC5) define the entire
supported $\Gamma$-space, not just its first two sets.

The construction uses the nerve formulas directly. No Dold--Kan
equivalence for noncancellative monoids is invoked. Let $X^{\rm amp}(D)$
be the same restricted arrays and maps on the original amplitude
groups, as in the cited Connes--Consani construction. Forgetting every
label gives the pointed simplicial $\Gamma$-map
\begin{equation}\tag{ASC5a}
p_D:X(D)\longrightarrow X^{\rm amp}(D).
\end{equation}
It is surjective in every bidegree: attach top labels to all nonzero
coordinates and any labels to zero coordinates. It commutes with
faces, degeneracies and pointed-set maps because their amplitude
formulas are unchanged. Over a fixed admitted amplitude array its
fibre has one independent copy of $L$ for each zero vertex amplitude,
each zero rational-leg amplitude, and each zero adelic-leg amplitude.
Nonzero coordinates force top labels independently. In particular
the fibre over the entirely zero array in bidegree $(n,r)$ is
\begin{equation}\tag{ASC5b}
p_D^{-1}(0)=L^{(2n+1)r}.
\end{equation}
The maps between these fibres are the actual joins and identity
insertions already specified, rather than unconstrained product maps.
There is no pointed simplicial $\Gamma$-section of (ASC5a): in degree
zero such a section would be a pointed additive section
$\mathbb A\to G_L(\mathbb A)$. A nonzero $x$ and $-x$ have forced
top lifts whose sum is $e$, whereas the pointed lift of zero is
$\tau$. This contradicts fold naturality. Degreewise surjectivity
therefore does not provide the missing natural section.

\subsection{The native relation and its exact symmetric part}

Let $\mathcal R_{D,r}$ be the original Connes--Consani relation
on $\mathbb A^r$: $(x,y)\in\mathcal R_{D,r}$ means that there
are $q_j\in\mathbb Q$, $o_{j,f}\in O_f(D)$ with
$y_j-x_j=q_j+o_j$ and $\sum_j|o_{j,\infty}|\le a$.
It is reflexive and symmetric, by zero choices and simultaneous
negation. The degree-zero relation furnished by (ASC5), directed
from $d_0$ to $d_1$, is exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{ASC6}
\begin{split}
((x_j,\lambda_j),(y_j,\mu_j))\in\mathcal R^{\to}_{L,D,r}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(x,y)\in\mathcal R_{D,r}\ \text{and}\
\lambda_j\le\mu_j\quad\hbox{for every }j.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Necessity follows because the output label is
$\lambda_j\vee\alpha_j\vee\beta_j$.
For sufficiency choose the original amplitude antecedents. If
$\mu_j<1_L$, admissibility and $\lambda_j\le\mu_j$ force
$x_j=y_j=0$. At that coordinate choose $q_j=o_j=0$ instead;
this does not increase the absolute sum. Give these zero legs
labels $\mu_j$ and $0_L$. If $\mu_j=1_L$, give both selected
legs top support. These choices realize the desired label and
amplitude in every coordinate and prove the converse.

Consequently the native relation is generally directed:
$\tau\mathrel{\mathcal R^{\to}}e$, whereas
$e\not\mathrel{\mathcal R^{\to}}\tau$. Its maximal symmetric
subrelation is precisely
\begin{equation}\tag{ASC7}
\mathcal R^{=}_{L,D,r}
 =\mathcal R^{\to}_{L,D,r}\cap
      (\mathcal R^{\to}_{L,D,r})^{\mathrm{op}}
 =\{((x,\lambda),(y,\mu)):
       (x,y)\in\mathcal R_{D,r},\ \lambda_j=\mu_j\ \forall j\}.
\end{equation}
Indeed a symmetric subrelation can contain a pair only when it
and its reverse occur; the displayed intersection is itself
symmetric and reflexive. It is preserved by every $\Gamma$ map:
the amplitude relation is preserved by the triangle inequality
and output joins of equal input labels are equal. This gives a
specified tolerant $\Gamma$-object while keeping $e$ distinct
from $\tau$ in its relation as well as its carrier.

For comparison the amplitude-pullback relation
$\mathcal R^{\rm amp}_{L,D,r}$ imposes only
$(x,y)\in\mathcal R_{D,r}$. The identity carrier map
$(B_L^r,\mathcal R^=)\to(B_L^r,\mathcal R^{\rm amp})$ is a
tolerance morphism. The reverse identity is not, since the
amplitude-pullback relation relates $\tau$ and $e$. Both exact
relations are retained; neither is silently substituted for the
other. The duality comparison CTD below calculates their maps.

\subsection{Spherical simplices over the entire zero section}

The global pointed vertex at level $r$ is $(\tau,\ldots,\tau)$.
A degree-one simplex $(b;z)$ is spherical there precisely when
$b=\tau^r$ and $\Phi_Lz=\tau^r$. This forces
$\alpha_j\vee\beta_j=0_L$, hence both source legs are $\tau$.
The ordinary global pointed spherical set is therefore a singleton:
\begin{equation}\tag{ASC8}
\operatorname{Sph}_1(X(D)(r_+),\tau^r)=\{(\tau^r;0_{P_L}^r)\}.
\end{equation}
This statement does not say that the original supported sections
are absent. Nor is it a calculation of the fundamental group of a
geometric realization: no Kan condition for these bounded simplicial
sets has been asserted. Their actual relative spherical object is
calculated next.

Let $\sigma=(\sigma_j)\in L^r$ and
$b_\sigma=((0,\sigma_j))_j$. Both faces of $(b_\sigma;z)$ equal
$b_\sigma$ exactly when
\begin{equation}\tag{ASC9}
\begin{gathered}
z_j=((q_j,\alpha_j),(-q_j,\beta_j)),\quad
q_j\in N\mathbb Z,\quad \sum_j|q_j|\le a,\\
q_j\ne0\ \Longrightarrow\ \alpha_j=\beta_j=1_L,
\qquad \alpha_j\vee\beta_j\le\sigma_j.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Necessity and sufficiency follow directly from the two faces and
the original finite and real restrictions. This proves the
relative spherical object over every zero-section label, including
all its proper support strata.

The degree-one homotopy relation used in the original source is
equality on these spherical simplices. In the explicit degree-two
array its faces are
\begin{equation}\tag{ASC10}
d_0(b;z_1,z_2)=(b;z_1),\qquad
d_1(b;z_1,z_2)=(b;z_1+z_2),\qquad
d_2(b;z_1,z_2)=(b+\Phi_Lz_1;z_2).
\end{equation}
The source's degree-one homotopy requires the first face to be
the degenerate vertex $s_0(b_\sigma)$. It forces $b=b_\sigma$
and $z_1=0_{P_L}^r$. The two remaining faces then coincide.
Conversely any spherical simplex gives such a homotopy by taking
$z_1=0$, $z_2=z$. Thus no unmentioned quotient of (ASC9) is taken.
The base labels are themselves pushed forward by joins under
$\Gamma$ maps; fixing the vector of top labels alone would not
be natural at an output with an empty fibre.

Let $\mathscr Z_L^1(D)$ denote the full relative object (ASC9),
and let $\mathscr K_L^{\rm ind}(D)$ forget $\sigma$ but keep
both source labels. Define the signed section object
\begin{equation}\tag{ASC11}
\mathscr S_L^0(D)(r_+)=
\{((q_j,\nu_j)):\ q_j\in N\mathbb Z,\
  \sum_j|q_j|\le a,\ q_j\ne0\Rightarrow\nu_j=1_L\}.
\end{equation}
There are two natural retractions with their displayed sections:
\begin{equation}\tag{ASC12}
\begin{gathered}
\mathscr Z_L^1(D)\longrightarrow\mathscr K_L^{\rm ind}(D)
       \longrightarrow\mathscr S_L^0(D),\\
(q,\alpha,\beta,\sigma)\longmapsto(q,\alpha,\beta)
       \longmapsto(q,\alpha\vee\beta),\\
(q,\alpha,\beta)\longmapsto
       (q,\alpha,\beta,\alpha\vee\beta),\qquad
(q,\nu)\longmapsto(q,\nu,\nu).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Each composite with its section is the identity. All formulas
commute with sums and joins over fibres and keep empty fibres.
For the middle section this uses
$(\bigvee\alpha_j)\vee(\bigvee\beta_j)
=\bigvee(\alpha_j\vee\beta_j)$. The first section sets the
base to the least label that makes the loop spherical. Thus this
is an exact relation to the original signed section amplitudes,
not a claim that the ordinary $\tau$-pointed kernel was nonzero.

Over an admitted $(q_j,\nu_j)_j$ the fibre of the composite in
(ASC12) is the product, over coordinates with $q_j=0$, of
\begin{equation}\tag{ASC13}
\{(\alpha,\beta,\sigma)\in L^3:
       \alpha\vee\beta=\nu_j,\quad\nu_j\le\sigma\}.
\end{equation}
At a nonzero coordinate all three labels are forced to $1_L$.
Formula (ASC13) is retained even when $L$ is infinite; no finite
cardinality or infinite join is imposed. In particular, replacing
the two source legs by one common label would lose real fibres.

The signed bound in (ASC11) implies every original subset bound
$-a\le\sum_{j\in J}q_j\le a$. It gives the natural injection
\begin{equation}\tag{ASC14}
\mathscr S_L^0(D)\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow
\mathcal H_L^0(D)
\end{equation}
into RDT3, keeping all labels. The injection is generally proper:
for $N\le a<2N$ the tuple $((N,1_L),(-N,1_L))$ belongs to the
all-subset object but fails the signed bound $2N\le a$.
Thus the two original Connes--Consani constructions are related
by an explicit map and an explicit difference, not by an assumed
identification.

\subsection{The exact adelic circle map and its retained fibres}

For $x=(x_f,x_\infty)\in\mathbb A$ choose $q\in\mathbb Q$
with $x_f-q\in O_f(D)$ and put
\begin{equation}\tag{ASC15}
\pi_D(x)=[x_\infty-q]\in\mathbb R/N\mathbb Z.
\end{equation}
Here is a direct existence argument. Set $u_f=x_f/N$ and choose
a positive integer $M$ with $Mu_f\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}$.
By the Chinese remainder theorem choose $h\in\mathbb Z$ such
that $h\equiv Mu_p\pmod {p^{v_p(M)}\mathbb Z_p}$ at each
prime dividing $M$. Then $u_f-h/M\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}$:
the assertion follows by division at primes dividing $M$ and
integrality at all others. Take $q=Nh/M$.
Two choices differ by $\mathbb Q\cap O_f(D)=N\mathbb Z$,
so (ASC15) is independent of the choice. Adding two choices
proves additivity. On an open finite-adelic coset of $O_f(D)$
the choice can be held fixed, proving continuity. Every real
class is reached by $(0,t)$, choosing $q=0$.

Its exact kernel is
\begin{equation}\tag{ASC16}
\ker\pi_D=\mathbb Q+j(O_f(D)),\qquad j(o_f)=(o_f,0).
\end{equation}
If $x_\infty-q=Nn$, replace $q$ by $q+Nn$ to express $x$
in the right hand side. The reverse inclusion follows at once
from (ASC15). This also proves that the kernel is closed,
as the inverse image of the identity in the Hausdorff circle.
It is not correct to use $x_\infty\bmod N\mathbb Z$ alone
as (ASC15): the finite-adelic choice of $q$ is essential.
There is no additive section of $\pi_D$. The class of $N/m$
for $m\ge2$ has order $m$, whereas the additive adele group
is torsion-free. A group-homomorphic section would send this
nonzero class to a torsion element and could not be a section.

Let $d_N$ be the metric on $\mathbb R/N\mathbb Z$ induced by
the original absolute value on $\mathbb R$, so its circumference
is $N$. Then the entire original relation is
\begin{equation}\tag{ASC17}
(x,y)\in\mathcal R_{D,r}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sum_{j=1}^r d_N(\pi_Dx_j,\pi_Dy_j)\le a.
\end{equation}
For the forward implication use the admitted real components
$o_{j,\infty}$ as representatives of the circle differences.
For the reverse choose $q_j$ matching each finite difference;
vary $q_j$ by $N\mathbb Z$ so that the real difference has
minimum absolute value. Such a minimum exists in every real
coset. Those finitely many minimum representatives give
$o_j=y_j-x_j-q_j$ with exactly the bound in (ASC17).

The full label map is
\begin{equation}\tag{ASC18}
\Pi_D:G_L(\mathbb A)\longrightarrow G_L(\mathbb R/N\mathbb Z),
\qquad (x,\lambda)\longmapsto(\pi_Dx,\lambda).
\end{equation}
It is an additive pointed map. It commutes with the underlying
$G_L(\mathbb Z)$ scalar action
$(n,\mu)(x,\lambda)=(nx,\mu\wedge\lambda)$ by additivity of
$\pi_D$. The scalars $(0,\mu)$ give the explicit zero-amplitude masks
$Z_\mu(x,\lambda)=(0,\mu\wedge\lambda)$, and
$\Pi_DZ_\mu=Z_\mu\Pi_D$. These masks preserve the displayed
tolerances. Multiplication by an integer of absolute value greater
than one need not preserve the fixed tolerance bound $a$. Meeting
a nonzero amplitude's label with a proper label while keeping that
amplitude would leave the carrier, and is not an operation used here.
Equations (ASC7),(ASC17)
show that it pulls back the label-exact circle tolerance to the
native symmetric part; it also pulls back the amplitude-pullback
circle tolerance to the stated amplitude relation. The fibre
over $(0,\lambda)$ for $\lambda<1_L$ is its one labelled zero.
The fibre over $e$ is $\ker\pi_D\times\{1_L\}$. Every nonzero
circle class has its full adele coset at top support. The
ordinary pointed kernel is again the singleton $\{\tau\}$,
although this map has the full nontrivial fibres just calculated.
A pointed additive section of $\Pi_D$ would, on amplitudes of
the top group sector, yield an additive section of $\pi_D$;
the torsion argument excludes it.

This gives the complete original divisor relation, both source
supports, the relative loop object, and the exact map into the
tolerant circle used in Serre duality. It leaves no inference
from a singleton pointed kernel to absence of supported sections.
Nor does it identify this divisor cohomology with the spectral
cohomology of the original Weil trace. The next comparison is
the explicit duality calculation CTD, applied to (ASC18); the
RDT theta map retains its original zeta and Gamma factors.
