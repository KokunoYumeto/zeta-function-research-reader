\subsection{All places, both labels, and the rational inverse monoid}

Let $L$ be a nontrivial bounded distributive lattice, and let
$\Sigma=\{\infty\}\cup\{p:p\text{ prime}\}$. Put
$\Lambda=L^\Sigma$, with coordinatewise order and joins,
$\boldsymbol0=(0_L)_v$, and $\boldsymbol1=(1_L)_v$. Retain
\begin{equation}\tag{SAP1}
\begin{gathered}
G_L(K)=\{(0,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}\cup(K\times\{1_L\}),\\
(c,\lambda)+(d,\mu)=(c+d,\lambda\vee\mu),\qquad
(c,\lambda)(d,\mu)=(cd,\lambda\wedge\mu),\\
\mathbb A_{G,L}=G_L(\mathbb R)\times\prod_p'G_L(\mathbb Q_p),
\qquad Z=\mathbb A_{G,L}^{2}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The restricted product is relative to $G_L(\mathbb Z_p)$.
An element of $Z$ is denoted $(y,x;\alpha,\beta)$, where
$y,x\in\mathbb A_{\mathbb Q}$ and
\begin{equation}\tag{SAP2}
y_v\ne0\Longrightarrow\alpha_v=1_L,\qquad
x_v\ne0\Longrightarrow\beta_v=1_L.
\end{equation}
All zero coordinates have their independent labels. For an adele
$w$, write $Z(w)=\{v:w_v=0\}$. A tuple in $L^{Z(w)}$ is always
extended by top labels outside $Z(w)$ when used below.

The original rational action, including both constants, is
\begin{equation}\tag{SAP3}
(a,b)(y,x)=(ay,ax+b),\qquad
(a,b)(a',b')=(aa',ab'+b),\qquad a,a'\in\mathbb Q^\times.
\end{equation}
The rational $b$ is diagonal in \emph{every} local field.
To give its exact supported partial action, define the monoid
\begin{equation}\tag{SAP4}
\begin{gathered}
M=\mathbb Q^\times\ltimes G_\Lambda(\mathbb Q),\qquad
m=(a,b,\gamma),\quad b\ne0\Longrightarrow\gamma=\boldsymbol1,\\
(a,b,\gamma)(a',b',\gamma')
 =(aa',ab'+b,\gamma\vee\gamma'),\qquad
1_M=(1,0,\boldsymbol0).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The product belongs to this carrier: a nonzero resulting translation
requires a nonzero translation in at least one factor and therefore
top joined support. Its unique inverse in the inverse-monoid sense is
\begin{equation}\tag{SAP5}
(a,b,\gamma)^\dagger=(a^{-1},-a^{-1}b,\gamma),\qquad
m^\dagger m=mm^\dagger=(1,0,\gamma).
\end{equation}
Associativity follows by expanding (SAP4). The displayed inverse
satisfies $mm^\dagger m=m$ and $m^\dagger mm^\dagger=m^\dagger$.
If $n=(c,d,\delta)$ satisfies both equations, their affine amplitude
parts force $c=a^{-1}$ and $d=-a^{-1}b$, while the label equations
give $\gamma\vee\delta=\gamma$ and
$\delta\vee\gamma=\delta$. Thus $\delta=\gamma$ and the inverse
is unique. The idempotents are exactly $(1,0,\gamma)$ and commute.

For $\gamma\in\Lambda$ define
$D_\gamma=\{(y,x;\alpha,\beta)\in Z:\gamma\le\beta\}$.
Then the faithful representation of $M$ by partial bijections is
\begin{equation}\tag{SAP6}
T_{a,b,\gamma}:D_\gamma\longrightarrow D_\gamma,
\qquad (y,x;\alpha,\beta)\longmapsto(ay,ax+b;\alpha,\beta).
\end{equation}
If $b=0$, multiplication by nonzero $a$ changes no zero place.
If $b\ne0$, $\gamma=\boldsymbol1$ forces $\beta=\boldsymbol1$,
so every translated coordinate is admitted. The source and target
labels agree and the inverse is (SAP5), proving bijectivity.
Composition has domain
$D_\gamma\cap D_{\gamma'}=D_{\gamma\vee\gamma'}$ and formula
(SAP4). The identity has domain all of $Z$. Distinct $\gamma$
have distinct domains, since zero amplitudes permit every $\beta$;
the amplitude formula on the common top-supported part determines
$a,b$. This proves faithfulness.

Pure dilations $(a,0,\boldsymbol0)$ act everywhere. Every nonzero
translation acts only on $\beta=\boldsymbol1$. The element
$(1,0,\gamma)$ is the identity on its displayed domain; it does
not discard the points outside that domain or identify their labels.

\subsection{Every rational orbit and every amplitude fibre}

Let $\mathscr C_L=Z/M$ be the equivalence relation generated by
these partial bijections, and let
$\mathscr C=P(\mathbb Q)\backslash\mathbb A^2$ be the original
Connes--Consani set quotient. Amplitudes induce the surjection
\begin{equation}\tag{SAP7}
\rho:\mathscr C_L\longrightarrow\mathscr C.
\end{equation}
It has the explicit section assigning top labels at every place
on both entries. That section is well defined under all original
affine changes of representative, and $\rho$ followed by this
choice in the reverse order is not asserted to be the identity
on $\mathscr C_L$.

Each allowed arrow preserves both complete tuples $\alpha,\beta$.
At $\beta=\boldsymbol1$, its orbits are the original affine
orbits, retaining the admissible tuple $\alpha$. At each
$\beta\ne\boldsymbol1$, every available affine arrow has $b=0$;
the orbits are precisely diagonal $\mathbb Q^\times$-orbits of
$(y,x)$, retaining both labels. A chain of partial arrows cannot
leave this label tuple to gain a translation and return: every
arrow preserves the tuple. This proves the full orbit assertion,
not only a description of the top corner.

An ordinary point $(y,x)$ has trivial stabilizer under (SAP3)
unless $y=0$ and $x$ is a diagonal rational. Indeed a nonzero
coordinate of $y$ forces $a=1$, then $b=0$. When $y=0$, any
stabilizer with $a\ne1$ gives $x=-b/(a-1)\in\mathbb Q$ at
every place. Conversely for $y=0,x=r\in\mathbb Q$, all
$(a,(1-a)r)$ stabilize the pair. These exceptional points make
one ordinary affine orbit, that of $(0,0)$.

Fix a free ordinary representative $(y,x)$. There is the following
exact representative-dependent parametrization of its entire fibre:
\begin{equation}\tag{SAP8}
\rho^{-1}([y,x])\ \simeq\
L^{Z(y)}\times
\left(\{*\}\ \sqcup\
\coprod_{t\in\mathbb Q}
       \bigl(L^{Z(x+t)}\setminus\{\boldsymbol1\}\bigr)\right).
\end{equation}
For the $*$ summand the representative is
$(y,x;\alpha,\boldsymbol1)$. For the $(t,\beta)$ summand it is
$(y,x+t;\alpha,\beta)$, with all forced top entries restored.
Here the notation $\boldsymbol1$ in each factor means that
factor's unique all-top tuple, including the unique empty tuple
when the zero-place set is empty.

To prove surjectivity of (SAP8), an amplitude in the ordinary
orbit is $(ay,ax+b)$. The supported dilation by $a^{-1}$ is
always available and gives $(y,x+b/a)$ with the same labels.
If $\beta=\boldsymbol1$, translation returns to $(y,x)$.
Otherwise set $t=b/a$; the labels are exactly those permitted
by $Z(y)$ and $Z(x+t)$. To prove injectivity, labels themselves
cannot change. In the non-top part, an identification must have
the form
$c(y,x+t)=(y,x+t')$, $c\in\mathbb Q^\times$.
If $y\ne0$ then $c=1$ and $t=t'$. If $y=0$, its free-orbit
hypothesis says $x\notin\mathbb Q$; $c\ne1$ would give
$x=(t'-ct)/(c-1)\in\mathbb Q$. Again $c=1,t=t'$.
Top and non-top labels cannot be identified. This proves the
bijection, and proves why the rational translation parameter remains.

For another free representative
$(y',x')=(a_0y,a_0x+b_0)$ the exact transition is
\begin{equation}\tag{SAP9}
\alpha'=\alpha,\qquad *=*,\qquad
t'=a_0t-b_0,\qquad \beta'=\beta,\qquad
x'+t'=a_0(x+t),\quad Z(y')=Z(y).
\end{equation}
Consequently $Z(x'+t')=Z(x+t)$ as subsets of the original place
set; there is no relabelling of places. Composition of two
changes gives the corresponding affine transformation of $t$,
by (SAP3). Thus (SAP8) is canonical relative to the chosen
representative, with the full transition law recorded.

Above the exceptional orbit the answer is instead
\begin{equation}\tag{SAP10}
\rho^{-1}([0,0])\simeq\Lambda\times\Lambda,
\qquad(\alpha,\beta)\longmapsto[0,0;\alpha,\beta].
\end{equation}
Every ordinary amplitude in that orbit is $(0,r)$ with
$r\in\mathbb Q$. For $r\ne0$ all local $x$-coordinates are
nonzero, forcing $\beta=\boldsymbol1$; the available translation
by $-r$ gives the indicated representative. When $r=0$ all
labels already occur. Distinct labels cannot be identified, proving
both existence and uniqueness in (SAP10). This also exhibits the
nonfree stabilizer explicitly rather than applying (SAP8) to it.

The cardinality formula for (SAP8) is the cardinality of that
displayed disjoint union and product; subtraction of infinite
cardinals is not used. For finite $L$ of size $m\ge2$, when
$Z(y)$ and every $Z(x+t)$ are finite it reads
\begin{equation}\tag{SAP11}
\bigl|\rho^{-1}([y,x])\bigr|
 =m^{|Z(y)|}\left(1+\sum_{t\in\mathbb Q}
                      (m^{|Z(x+t)|}-1)\right).
\end{equation}
The sum is an ordinary sum of nonnegative integers if it has
finite support, and is countably infinite otherwise. Different
sets $Z(x+t)$ are disjoint: $x_v+t=x_v+t'=0$ forces $t=t'$.
If $Z(y)$ or one $Z(x+t)$ is infinite, countability of $\Sigma$
gives the exact cardinal $m^{\aleph_0}$ instead. Indeed the
corresponding factor or summand has that cardinal, and the whole
expression has at most a countable union of products of that
cardinal. The exceptional cardinal is always $m^{\aleph_0}$.
For an arbitrary infinite $L$, (SAP8),(SAP10) are the exact
cardinal expressions without a finiteness assumption. For example,
the free point $(y,x)=(1,0)$ has fibre $\Lambda$: only $t=0$
has zero coordinates in $x+t$, and $Z(y)$ is empty.

\subsection{The actual top-supported right real formula}

Write $g=(u,v)\in P(\mathbb R)$, $u\ne0$, with multiplication
$(u,v)(u',v')=(uu',uv'+v)$. At the infinite place the original
matrix product is $(y_\infty,x_\infty)\mapsto
(u y_\infty,x_\infty+v y_\infty)$. The finite components are
unchanged. Lifting every real scalar $c$ as $(c,1_L)$, including
zero as $e=(0,1_L)$, gives the full formula
\begin{equation}\tag{SAP12}
F_g(y,x;\alpha,\beta)_\infty
 =\bigl((u y_\infty,\alpha_\infty),
        (x_\infty+v y_\infty,\beta_\infty\vee\alpha_\infty)\bigr),
\qquad (F_gz)_p=z_p\ (p<\infty).
\end{equation}
The zero contribution when $v=0$ still has label
$\alpha_\infty$; replacing it by $\tau$ would change the formula.
In particular define
\begin{equation}\tag{SAP13}
\begin{gathered}
C=F_{(1,0)},\qquad
C(y,x;\alpha,\beta)
 =(y,x;\alpha,\beta^{\sharp}),\\
\beta^{\sharp}_\infty=\beta_\infty\vee\alpha_\infty,
\quad\beta^{\sharp}_p=\beta_p,\\
\qquad W=\operatorname{im}C
 =\{\alpha_\infty\le\beta_\infty\}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
All entries remain in their original carriers, and $C^2=C$.
Direct expansion of both real amplitudes and both labels gives
\begin{equation}\tag{SAP14}
F_h\circ F_g=F_{gh},\qquad
F_gC=CF_g=F_g,\qquad
F_{g^{-1}}F_g=F_gF_{g^{-1}}=C.
\end{equation}
For the translation entry this expansion is
$x_\infty+v y_\infty+v'u y_\infty
=x_\infty+(uv'+v)y_\infty$; the repeated join with
$\alpha_\infty$ is unchanged. Thus the identity element of the
real group acts as $C$, not as the identity of the full source.

The restrictions $A_g=F_g|_W$ give a genuine right group action,
with inverse $A_{g^{-1}}$. It is the maximal subset of $Z$ on
which the \emph{displayed full formula} is a unital group action:
any such subset must be fixed by $F_{(1,0)}=C$, and hence must
lie in $W$. The exact factorization on the whole source is
\begin{equation}\tag{SAP15}
F_g:\ Z\xrightarrow{C}W\xrightarrow{A_g}W\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow Z.
\end{equation}
For fixed finite entries and fixed real $y_\infty,x_\infty,
\alpha_\infty,\beta^{\sharp}_\infty$, the fibre of $C$ consists
exactly of
\begin{equation}\tag{SAP16}
\{\beta_\infty\in L:
  \beta_\infty\vee\alpha_\infty=\beta^{\sharp}_\infty,
  \quad x_\infty\ne0\Rightarrow\beta_\infty=1_L\}.
\end{equation}
The other entries are fixed. This equation retains the complete
defect introduced by the right matrix identity.

\subsection{Descent and two distinct repairs on the full carrier}

The saturation $C$, and every $F_g$, send each left rational
partial orbit into a left rational partial orbit. On the domain
of $T_{a,b,\gamma}$ one has exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{SAP17}
F_gT_{a,b,\gamma}z=T_{a,b,\gamma}F_gz,
\qquad CT_{a,b,\gamma}z=T_{a,b,\gamma}Cz.
\end{equation}
For amplitudes, both sides give $au y_\infty$ and
$a(x_\infty+v y_\infty)+b$ at infinity and the original
affine formula at finite places. For labels, left maps preserve
$\alpha,\beta$ and the right map joins only the displayed
infinite-place label. The right image remains in $D_\gamma$,
since its $\beta$ is greater than or equal to the original one.
If $b\ne0$, the original $\beta$ was top at all places and
remains so. These checks prove the domains as well as the equations.

The set $W$ is saturated for the left partial-orbit relation,
because that relation preserves labels. It follows that the real
group acts on $W/M$, that $C$ induces an idempotent
$\overline C:\mathscr C_L\to W/M$, and that (SAP15) descends.
Its fixed set is exactly $W/M$: an orbit cannot identify two
different label tuples. The original amplitude quotient map is
equivariant for the right real action wherever that action is
defined, and the all-top section of (SAP7) intertwines the entire
original real action.

One repair retains the coefficient-exact reversible part and
adds the already-defined global dilations. Define $D_u$ on all
of $Z$ by scaling $y_\infty$ by $u\ne0$ and retaining every
other amplitude and label. It corresponds to using $\tau$ for
the zero shear coefficient; it is therefore a \emph{different,
explicitly declared lift} from $F_{(u,0)}$ outside $W$. There is
the partial right $P(\mathbb R)$ action
\begin{equation}\tag{SAP18}
\theta_{(u,v)}=
\begin{cases}
D_u:Z\to Z,&v=0,\\
A_{(u,v)}:W\to W,&v\ne0.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Its inverse has the same domain, because the inverse translation
coefficient is $-v/u$. Composing two maps has domain $Z$ if both
are pure dilations, and $W$ otherwise. On that domain the amplitude
law is $(u,v)(u',v')=(uu',uv'+v)$ and labels are unchanged.
If two nonzero shear coefficients cancel, the result is the
restriction to $W$ of the resulting global dilation, as required
for a partial action. The resulting inverse monoid of partial
bijections is exactly
$\{D_u:u\in\mathbb R^\times\}\cup
 \{A_g:W\to W:g\in P(\mathbb R)\}$,
with idempotents $\operatorname{id}_Z,\operatorname{id}_W$.
Its two parts are the dilation group and the affine group on
their stated domains. The sets and formulas are left-orbit
saturated, preserve all left-action domains, and commute with
the left action. Thus (SAP18) descends to $\mathscr C_L$.

This inverse monoid also has its exact supported matrix
realization, rather than only a description by partial maps.
At the real place put $\mathbf1=(1,1_L)$,
$e=(0,1_L)$ and $\tau=(0,0_L)$, and retain the two matrix families
\begin{equation}\tag{SAP18a}
\mathcal D_u=
\begin{pmatrix}(u,1_L)&\tau\\ \tau&\mathbf1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\mathcal B_{u,v}=
\begin{pmatrix}(u,1_L)&(v,1_L)\\ \tau&\mathbf1\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The original full right map is multiplication of the row by
$\mathcal B_{u,v}$. The matrix $\mathcal B_{u,0}$ has an $e$
entry and is different from $\mathcal D_u$. Multiplication in
the original semiring gives all four products
\begin{equation}\tag{SAP18b}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal D_u\mathcal D_w&=\mathcal D_{uw},&
\mathcal D_u\mathcal B_{w,t}&=\mathcal B_{uw,ut},\\
\mathcal B_{u,v}\mathcal D_w&=\mathcal B_{uw,v},&
\mathcal B_{u,v}\mathcal B_{w,t}&=\mathcal B_{uw,v+ut}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
In the last product, cancellation of $v+ut$ leaves the upper
right entry $e$, not $\tau$. The two idempotents are
$\mathcal D_1$ and $\mathcal E=\mathcal B_{1,0}$, which commute.
The unique inverse of $\mathcal D_u$ is $\mathcal D_{u^{-1}}$;
the unique inverse of $\mathcal B_{u,v}$ is
$\mathcal B_{u^{-1},-v/u}$. The latter pair multiplies to
$\mathcal E$ in both orders. An inverse between the two
different families is impossible because a product containing
a $\mathcal B$ factor still has top upper-right support; within
each family its amplitude equations force the displayed inverse.
This proves the inverse-monoid assertion directly for matrices.
Its faithful right action by partial bijections sends
$\mathcal D_u$ to $D_u$ on $Z$ and $\mathcal B_{u,v}$ to
$A_{u,v}$ on $W$, with idempotents
$\operatorname{id}_Z,\operatorname{id}_W$. The different domains
distinguish the two families. At top-supported $y_\infty=1,
x_\infty=0$, the output amplitudes distinguish each $u,v$.
The action convention is $R_{mn}=R_n\circ R_m$, as required
for multiplication of rows on the right; it is not silently
changed to a left action.

A second, larger partial action can be defined if the requirement
is to keep every label and the original amplitude formula, rather
than to be the reversible restriction of (SAP12). Its exact
maximal full-group domain is
\begin{equation}\tag{SAP19}
\Omega=\{z:\beta_\infty=1_L\ \text{or}\ y_\infty=0\},
\qquad W\subset\Omega.
\end{equation}
On $\Omega$ use $(u y_\infty,x_\infty+v y_\infty)$ with both
labels unchanged. If $\beta_\infty=1_L$ this is always
admissible; if $y_\infty=0$, its $x$-entry is unchanged. These
two conditions persist under every group element, so this is
a group action on $\Omega$. Outside $\Omega$, one has
$y_\infty\ne0$, $\beta_\infty<1_L$ and $x_\infty=0$.
Any $v\ne0$ would give nonzero $v y_\infty$ with a non-top
label, outside the carrier. This proves maximality under the
stated label-preserving amplitude requirement. The same definition
as (SAP18), with $W$ replaced by $\Omega$, is therefore a
partial action on all $Z$, and descends to the left orbit set.
On $\Omega\setminus W$ it differs from the full supported
matrix product: it retains $\beta_\infty$ instead of replacing
it by $\beta_\infty\vee\alpha_\infty$. Equations (SAP12),
(SAP16), and (SAP19) specify that difference; these lifts are
not identified with each other.
If $T_g$ denotes this larger group action on $\Omega$, its
comparison to the full coefficient action is the exact equation
\begin{equation}\tag{SAP19a}
C\circ T_g=A_g\circ C=F_g|_\Omega.
\end{equation}
Both sides keep the same amplitudes and all finite labels and
replace only $\beta_\infty$ by $\beta_\infty\vee\alpha_\infty$.
On $\Omega\setminus W$ both real amplitudes are zero and
$T_g$ fixes the point, whereas $F_g$ performs that displayed
support saturation.

There is also an exact groupoid which keeps the full original
map (SAP12) and all its antecedents. Its objects are all $z\in Z$,
and its arrows are
\begin{equation}\tag{SAP20}
\mathcal G_C^{(1)}
 =\{(z,g,z'):g\in P(\mathbb R),\ C(z')=A_gC(z)\},
\qquad (z,g,z'):(z\longrightarrow z').
\end{equation}
For arrows $(z,g,z')$ and $(z',h,z'')$, composition is
$(z,gh,z'')$; its defining equation follows from the right
action law on $W$. The identity is $(z,1,z)$ and the inverse
is $(z',g^{-1},z)$. Thus all groupoid laws hold. The arrows
over the group identity are precisely all pairs in the fibres
of $C$, with those fibres given by (SAP16). In particular the
graph $(z,g,F_gz)$ supplies the original maps as arrows, and
their inverses remain arrows without pretending that
$F_{g^{-1}}F_gz=z$. The orbit set of this groupoid is exactly
$W/P(\mathbb R)$: every $z$ is connected to $Cz$ by an
identity-labelled arrow, and the remaining relation is the
original group action on $W$. Replacing $Z,W,C$ by
$\mathscr C_L,W/M,\overline C$ gives the same exact construction
after the rational partial quotient. Its objects still retain
every supported rational orbit.

The saturation is also the universal quotient on which the
original maps become a genuine group action. Precisely, if
$f:Z\to B$ intertwines every $F_g$ with a genuine right group
action on $B$, then the group identity gives
\begin{equation}\tag{SAP20a}
f(Cz)=f(F_1z)=f(z),\qquad
f=\widetilde f\circ C,\qquad \widetilde f=f|_W.
\end{equation}
This factor is unique because $C$ is onto, and it is equivariant
because $C|_W$ is the identity and $F_g|_W=A_g$.
Conversely every equivariant $\widetilde f:W\to B$ yields
such an $f$ by (SAP15). Thus $Z/\ker C\simeq W$ has the
stated universal property. After the left partial quotient,
the same proof gives the quotient by
$[z]\sim[z']$ if and only if $Cz$ and $Cz'$ lie in the same
left partial orbit, with target $W/M$.

\subsection{A full-fibre obstruction, beyond failure of one formula}

There is already an obstruction to \emph{any} global bijective
action with the prescribed original amplitudes and unchanged
finite data, even allowing the real support labels to change.
Choose two points with identical finite data and
$y_\infty=1,x_\infty=0,\alpha_\infty=1_L$, but respectively
$\beta_\infty=0_L$ and $\beta_\infty=1_L$. A group element
$(u,v)$ with $u\ne0,v\ne0$ would send both points to real
amplitudes $(u,v)$; each is nonzero and forces both real labels
to be top. All finite data were required to remain the same.
The two images would therefore coincide, contradicting
injectivity of a group-action map. This argument does not
assume the particular label formula (SAP12).

There cannot be a global group action by bijections on $Z$ making
$C:Z\to W$ equivariant for the given action $A$ on $W$.
Indeed fix all finite coordinates and labels, take
$y_\infty=1$, $x_\infty=0$, $\alpha_\infty=1_L$, and let
$w_0\in W$ have $\beta_\infty=1_L$. Equation (SAP16) gives
$C^{-1}(w_0)\simeq L$. The real shear $g=(1,v)$ for $v\ne0$
sends $w_0$ to $w_v$ with $x_\infty=v\ne0$, whose $C$-fibre
is a singleton. An equivariant bijection for $g$ would restrict
to a bijection of these two fibres, impossible for nontrivial $L$.
This rules out every such global action, not merely the specific
formula (SAP12).

The corresponding obstruction persists after taking the actual
left rational partial quotient. Choose full adeles and labels
\begin{equation}\tag{SAP21}
y_v=1\ \ (v\in\Sigma),\qquad x_v=0\ \ (v\in\Sigma),
\qquad \alpha=\beta=\boldsymbol1,
\end{equation}
and let $\overline w_0$ be their class in $W/M$.
Since all $y$-coordinates are nonzero, every antecedent under
$\overline C$ has $\alpha=\boldsymbol1$, and its finite
$\beta$-coordinates must be top. Formula (SAP8) shows
\begin{equation}\tag{SAP22}
\overline C^{-1}(\overline w_0)\simeq L:
\quad\beta_\infty\text{ is arbitrary, all finite }\beta_p=1_L.
\end{equation}
For a proper $\beta_\infty$, the representative has $t=0$,
because $x_\infty+t=0$ is necessary. The top value is the
top branch of (SAP8), so it contributes one further value of
that same $L$ parametrization.
Now choose $v\in\mathbb R\setminus\mathbb Q$ and apply the
right shear $(1,v)$. Its amplitude representative has
$y=1$, $x_p=0$ for finite $p$, and $x_\infty=v$.
An antecedent with proper $\beta_\infty$ would require a
rational $t$ satisfying $v+t=0$, impossible. Consequently
\begin{equation}\tag{SAP23}
\bigl|\overline C^{-1}(\overline w_0\cdot(1,v))\bigr|=1
\quad (v\notin\mathbb Q),\qquad
\overline C^{-1}(\overline w_0)\simeq L.
\end{equation}
Thus no global bijective real affine action on the supported
rational orbit set can make $\overline C$ equivariant either.
The partial actions and the groupoid above are specified
mathematical replacements with proved maps, not assumptions
that this obstruction has disappeared.

\subsection{The retained free real leaves}

On the part of $W/M$ with $y_f\ne0$ and $y_\infty\ne0$,
the right real affine action is free. If $[z]\cdot(u,v)=[z]$
in this supported quotient, the same holds in the original
amplitude quotient. There is therefore $(a,b)\in P(\mathbb Q)$
with $y_f=ay_f$, $x_f=ax_f+b$, $u y_\infty=a y_\infty$,
and $x_\infty+v y_\infty=a x_\infty+b$.
A nonzero finite coordinate of $y_f$ forces $a=1$; the finite
$x$-equation then forces $b=0$; the two real equations force
$u=1,v=0$. This reproduces the exact argument in the cited
Lemma \texttt{Pdescrofgamma}, with every support retained.

For a fixed label tuple these free $P_+(\mathbb R)$ orbits
therefore carry the original homogeneous complex-leaf structure.
This means the manifold structure transported from the free
orbit parametrization by $P_+(\mathbb R)$; no new topology on
the entire label set is being declared. The actual real formulas,
with their original constants, are
\begin{equation}\tag{SAP24}
X=y_\infty\partial_{x_\infty},\qquad
Y=y_\infty\partial_{y_\infty},\qquad
ds^2=\frac{dx_\infty^2+dy_\infty^2}{y_\infty^2},\qquad
\Delta=X^2+(Y-\tfrac12)^2-\tfrac14.
\end{equation}
The vector fields follow by differentiating the original right
shear and dilation, respectively. For a base point $(y_0,x_0)$,
the orbit coordinates are $y_\infty=u y_0$,
$x_\infty=x_0+v y_0$, so the metric pullback is exactly
$y_0^2(du^2+dv^2)/(u^2y_0^2)$. The Laplacian expression retains
both scalar terms and agrees with
$y_\infty^2(\partial_{x_\infty}^2+\partial_{y_\infty}^2)$ by
expanding $Y^2-Y$ and $X^2$. These are the original formulas
of Proposition \texttt{ax+bact} and equation \texttt{lapxy}.
They are supported real-leaf statements on the indicated locus,
not a global complex structure on the full supported carrier.
No spectral positivity or RH conclusion follows from the orbit
or fibre calculations alone.
