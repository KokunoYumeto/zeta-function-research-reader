\subsection{The matrices and their exact product}

Let $R$ be a commutative ring with identity and let $L$ be a bounded
distributive lattice, initially with $0_L\ne1_L$. Retain
\begin{equation}\tag{SPA1}
\begin{gathered}
G_L(R)=\{(0,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}\cup(R\times\{1_L\}),\\
(x,\lambda)+(z,\nu)=(x+z,\lambda\vee\nu),\qquad
(x,\lambda)(z,\nu)=(xz,\lambda\wedge\nu),\\
\tau=(0,0_L),\quad e=(0,1_L),\quad\mathbf1=(1_R,1_L).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}\tag{SPA2}
\mathcal P_L(R)=
\left\{
M(a,b,\beta)=
\begin{pmatrix}(a,1_L)&(b,\beta)\\\tau&\mathbf1\end{pmatrix}:
a\in R^\times,\ b\in R,\ \beta\in L,\
b\ne0\Rightarrow\beta=1_L
\right\}.
\end{equation}
An element is also denoted $(a,b,\beta)$. Entrywise multiplication
using (SPA1) gives
\begin{equation}\tag{SPA3}
(a,b,\beta)(c,d,\delta)
=(ac,ad+b,\beta\vee\delta),\qquad
1_{\mathcal P}=E_{0_L}:=(1_R,0,0_L).
\end{equation}
In particular the upper-right entry is
$(a,1_L)(d,\delta)+(b,\beta)\mathbf1=(ad+b,\delta\vee\beta)$.
The lower-left entry stays $\tau$, and the diagonal entries
have the stated form. A nonzero $ad+b$ forces $b\ne0$ or
$d\ne0$, hence top output support. This proves closure.
Both triple-product bracketings give
$(acr,act+ad+b,\beta\vee\delta\vee\theta)$, proving associativity.
The displayed identity is two-sided.

\subsection{Generalized inverse and every local group}

The unique inverse in the inverse-semigroup sense is
\begin{equation}\tag{SPA4}
s^*=(a^{-1},-a^{-1}b,\beta),\qquad
ss^*=s^*s=E_\beta:=(1_R,0,\beta),\qquad
ss^*s=s,\quad s^*ss^*=s^*.
\end{equation}
It is admitted because multiplication by a unit preserves
nonvanishing. The equalities follow from (SPA3).
For uniqueness, if $t=(c,d,\delta)$ satisfies $sts=s$ and $tst=t$,
the first amplitude equation gives $aca=a$, hence $c=a^{-1}$,
and the translation equation then gives $ad+b=0$.
The first support equation gives $\delta\le\beta$, and the
second gives $\beta\le\delta$. Thus $t=s^*$ with the same label.

Every idempotent is exactly an $E_\beta$, and
\begin{equation}\tag{SPA5}
E_\beta E_\delta=E_{\beta\vee\delta},\qquad
E_\delta s=sE_\delta.
\end{equation}
Indeed $a^2=a$ with $a$ a unit gives $a=1_R$; the remaining
amplitude equation $2b=b$ gives $b=0$.
The converse and the displayed formulas follow by multiplication.
Therefore $\mathcal P_L(R)$ is a Clifford inverse monoid:
it is an inverse monoid and all its idempotents are central.

The natural inverse-semigroup order, defined by $s\le_{\mathcal P}t$
if $s=Et$ for some idempotent $E$, is
\begin{equation}\tag{SPA6}
(a,b,\beta)\le_{\mathcal P}(c,d,\delta)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
a=c,\quad b=d,\quad\delta\le_L\beta.
\end{equation}
Multiplication by $E_\eta$ proves necessity, and $\eta=\beta$
proves sufficiency. The idempotent order reverses the given
lattice order; this reversal is retained.

The maximal subgroup with identity $E_\beta$ is
\begin{equation}\tag{SPA7}
H_\beta=
\begin{cases}
\{(a,0,\beta):a\in R^\times\}\cong R^\times,&\beta<1_L,\\
\{(a,b,1_L):a\in R^\times,\ b\in R\}\cong P(R),&\beta=1_L.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
These are exactly the elements with $s^*s=ss^*=E_\beta$, and
(SPA3)--(SPA4) prove their group laws and inverses.
For nontrivial $L$, the global units are only $H_{0_L}$.
A top-supported translation has an inverse in its corner,
not a global inverse.

Even an inverse sought in the entire matrix monoid cannot
change that conclusion. Suppose that $M(a,b,\beta)X=I_2$.
Its bottom
row forces $X_{21}=\tau$ and $X_{22}=\mathbf1$.
The upper-right sum can equal $\tau$ only if both summands
are $\tau$, because a joined support is bottom only when
both supports are bottom. Since $(a,1_L)$ is a scalar unit,
this forces $X_{12}=\tau$ and $(b,\beta)=\tau$.
Conversely those conditions give the diagonal inverse.
Thus the off-diagonal supported zero cannot be treated as
the global matrix zero.

\subsection{The exact universal group image}

The amplitude map is the surjective unital homomorphism
\begin{equation}\tag{SPA8}
\pi:\mathcal P_L(R)\to P(R),\qquad
(a,b,\beta)\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\pi^{-1}(a,b)=
\begin{cases}
\{(a,b,1_L)\},&b\ne0,\\
\{(a,0,\beta):\beta\in L\},&b=0.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
It is universal for homomorphisms into groups. To prove this,
let $f:\mathcal P_L(R)\to G$ be a unital monoid homomorphism
with $G$ a group. Every $f(E_\beta)$ is idempotent and hence
equals $1_G$. Therefore
\[
f(a,b,\beta)=f((a,b,\beta)E_{1_L})=f(a,b,1_L).
\]
The map $(a,b)\mapsto f(a,b,1_L)$ is the unique group
homomorphism through which $f$ factors. The multiplicative
top-corner section sends the classical identity to $E_{1_L}$,
not to the global identity $E_{0_L}$.
Thus group completion identifies all distinct support
idempotents, with its complete fibres given in (SPA8).

\subsection{The total affine action and exact inverse domains}

Put $X_L=G_L(R)$. Multiplying the affine column with lower
coordinate $\mathbf1$ gives
\begin{equation}\tag{SPA9}
F_s(x,\lambda)=(ax+b,\lambda\vee\beta),
\qquad s=(a,b,\beta).
\end{equation}
The output is admitted: nonzero amplitude requires a nonzero
input amplitude or translation, and the corresponding label
is top. Substitution proves $F_sF_t=F_{st}$ and
$F_{E_{0_L}}=\mathrm{id}$.
The action is faithful: $F_s(\tau)=(b,\beta)$ recovers
$b,\beta$, and $F_s(\mathbf1)=(a+b,1_L)$ recovers $a$.

The full inverse identities are
\begin{equation}\tag{SPA10}
F_{E_\beta}(x,\lambda)=(x,\lambda\vee\beta),\qquad
F_{s^*}F_s=F_sF_{s^*}=F_{E_\beta}.
\end{equation}
The maximal domain on which these displayed inverse maps
recover the input exactly is
\begin{equation}\tag{SPA11}
D_\beta=\operatorname{Fix}(F_{E_\beta})
=\{(x,\lambda):\beta\le\lambda\}
=(R\times\{1_L\})\cup\{(0,\lambda):\lambda\ge\beta\}.
\end{equation}
Restricted to $D_\beta$, the map $F_s$ is a bijection onto
$D_\beta$ with inverse $F_{s^*}$ and unchanged label.
Both necessity and sufficiency follow from
$\lambda\vee\beta=\lambda$ in (SPA10).
Also $F_s(X_L)=D_\beta$: the output label is above $\beta$,
and the restriction is already onto.
No larger domain containing $D_\beta$ keeps that restriction
injective. A point $z$ outside it has the same image as
$F_{E_\beta}z\in D_\beta$, by $F_sF_{E_\beta}=F_s$.

Every fibre of the total action is exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{SPA12}
F_s^{-1}(y,\nu)=
\{(a^{-1}(y-b),\lambda):
\lambda\vee\beta=\nu,\
a^{-1}(y-b)\ne0\Rightarrow\lambda=1_L\}.
\end{equation}
For $b\ne0$, every zero label is sent to $(b,1_L)$; the
generalized inverse then returns $e$. At the nonzero input
$x=-a^{-1}b$, cancellation produces $e$, not $\tau$, and
the inverse on $D_{1_L}$ recovers the input.
For $b=0$ and proper $\beta$, unit multiplication preserves
nonzero amplitudes and raises each zero label by the stated join.

The restrictions form a faithful representation by partial bijections:
\begin{equation}\tag{SPA13}
\alpha_s=F_s|_{D_\beta},\quad
\alpha_s^{-1}=\alpha_{s^*},\quad
D_\beta\cap D_\delta=D_{\beta\vee\delta},\quad
\alpha_s\alpha_t=\alpha_{st}.
\end{equation}
Indeed $\alpha_t$ preserves its input label, so the composition
domain consists exactly of labels above both $\delta$ and
$\beta$. Its formula is that of $st$.
Equality of two partial maps implies equality of their
zero-label upsets, hence of $\beta,\delta$, and their values
on the top copy of $R$ recover both affine coefficients.
This proves faithfulness.

\subsection{The Karoubi category and its action}

The Karoubi category $\mathcal K_L(R)$ has objects $E_\beta$
and hom-sets $E_\delta\mathcal P_L(R)E_\beta$ from $E_\beta$
to $E_\delta$. Their exact form is
\begin{equation}\tag{SPA14}
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal K_L(R)}(E_\beta,E_\delta)
=\{(a,b,\gamma)\in\mathcal P_L(R):\gamma\ge\beta\vee\delta\}.
\end{equation}
This follows by multiplying on both sides and comparing
the resulting label $\delta\vee\gamma\vee\beta$.
The identity is $E_\beta$. For
$s=(a,b,\gamma):E_\beta\to E_\delta$ and
$t=(c,d,\eta):E_\delta\to E_\epsilon$,
\[
t\circ s=(ca,cb+d,\eta\vee\gamma).
\]
Its support is above $\beta,\epsilon$, and associativity
and identities follow from (SPA3).

Every arrow factors exactly as
\begin{equation}\tag{SPA15}
E_\beta\xrightarrow{E_\gamma}E_\gamma
\xrightarrow{(a,b,\gamma)}E_\gamma
\xrightarrow{E_\gamma}E_\delta.
\end{equation}
The middle arrow lies in $H_\gamma$.
An arrow is a categorical isomorphism precisely when
$\beta=\delta=\gamma$. In fact a categorical inverse of
support $\eta$ would give composites with support
$\gamma\vee\eta$ equal to both $\beta$ and $\delta$.
Since $\gamma\ge\beta,\delta$, this forces equality of all
four labels; the converse follows from (SPA4).
Thus distinct support objects are not isomorphic, and
$\operatorname{Aut}(E_\beta)=H_\beta$.

The total affine action defines
\begin{equation}\tag{SPA16}
\mathcal D:\mathcal K_L(R)\to\mathrm{Sets},\qquad
E_\beta\mapsto D_\beta,\qquad
s\mapsto F_s|_{D_\beta}:D_\beta\to D_\delta.
\end{equation}
For an arrow of support $\gamma\ge\beta,\delta$, its output
label $\lambda\vee\gamma$ is above $\delta$. The object
identity acts as the identity, and composition is (SPA9).
This functor is faithful on each hom-set: evaluation at
$(0,\beta)$ recovers $(b,\gamma)$; evaluation at
$(1_R,1_L)$ then recovers $a$.
A general arrow need not act bijectively, because its
inverse-pair composites are $F_{E_\gamma}$ rather than
the source and target identities.

The full one-object top subcategory is
\begin{equation}\tag{SPA17}
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal K_L(R)}(E_{1_L})
=E_{1_L}\mathcal P_L(R)E_{1_L}=H_{1_L}\cong P(R).
\end{equation}
Here \emph{full} means that every arrow between the chosen
object and itself is included. It does not mean a generating
or Morita-full idempotent: for nontrivial $L$,
\[
\mathcal P_L(R)E_{1_L}\mathcal P_L(R)=H_{1_L}
\ne\mathcal P_L(R),
\]
since the left side omits $E_{0_L}$.

The action category has objects $(E_\beta,z)$, $z\in D_\beta$.
An arrow labelled $s:E_\beta\to E_\delta$ has target
$(E_\delta,F_sz)$; composition retains the actual $s$ label.
Its full subcategory over $E_{1_L}$ is exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{SPA18}
P(R)\ltimes R,
\end{equation}
via $(x,1_L)\leftrightarrow x$. All stabilizer arrows remain.
No germ quotient identifying arrows that agree at a single
point is taken.

\subsection{Both original Connes--Consani coordinates}

Retain independent coordinate supports in
\begin{equation}\tag{SPA19}
W(y,\lambda;x,\nu)=
\begin{pmatrix}(y,\lambda)&(x,\nu)\\\tau&\mathbf1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad (y,\lambda),(x,\nu)\in G_L(R).
\end{equation}
This is $G_L(R)_y\times G_L(R)_x$, not $G_L(R^2)$ with
one common label. The original matrix
$\iota(x,y)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}y&x\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$
is its all-top subspace, including both zero amplitudes.

The two entrywise products are
\begin{equation}\tag{SPA20}
\begin{aligned}
M(a,b,\beta)W(y,\lambda;x,\nu)
 &=W(ay,\lambda;\ ax+b,\nu\vee\beta),\\
W(y,\lambda;x,\nu)M(u,v,\delta)
 &=W(yu,\lambda;\ yv+x,\nu\vee(\lambda\wedge\delta)).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
They follow directly from (SPA1). Associativity proves
that the left and right total actions commute. Explicitly
both orders give amplitudes $(ayu,a(x+yv)+b)$ and labels
$(\lambda,\nu\vee\beta\vee(\lambda\wedge\delta))$.

Their maximal inverse-pair domains are
\begin{equation}\tag{SPA21}
\begin{aligned}
D^{\mathrm{left}}_\beta
 &=\{W(y,\lambda;x,\nu):\beta\le\nu\},\\
D^{\mathrm{right}}_\delta
 &=\{W(y,\lambda;x,\nu):\lambda\wedge\delta\le\nu\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
These are the fixed sets of the corresponding idempotent
maps in (SPA20). On the stated domain each action preserves
both labels, and its generalized inverse is a genuine
inverse. Necessity follows by computing its inverse-pair
composite. Maximality as an injective domain containing the
stated domain follows because any additional point has the
same image as its idempotent image inside that domain.

Right multiplication is a right action:
$\rho_t\circ\rho_s=\rho_{st}$ when $\rho_s(W)=Ws$.
The corresponding partial maps have this same right
composition convention. Moreover
\[
D^{\mathrm{right}}_\delta\cap D^{\mathrm{right}}_\eta
=D^{\mathrm{right}}_{\delta\vee\eta},
\]
because $\lambda\wedge(\delta\vee\eta)
=(\lambda\wedge\delta)\vee(\lambda\wedge\eta)$.
On their respective inverse domains both actions preserve
both coordinate labels. Consequently the two partial
actions commute on exactly their common domain.
All affine cancellations keep the joined supports in (SPA20).
For example a right translation with $\delta=1_L$ requires
$\lambda\le\nu$, not that both coordinates be top-supported.

\subsection{The classical action groupoid and its larger supported corner}

The top-translation fixed set is
\begin{equation}\tag{SPA22}
X_L^{\mathrm{tr}}(R)
=G_L(R)_y\times(R\times\{1_L\})_x.
\end{equation}
Its $x$ label is top, and every $y$ label remains.
It is invariant under the top affine group on the left
and on the right: $\nu=1_L$ satisfies every condition
in (SPA21), and both inverse-domain actions preserve it.
The original classical subspace is the smaller invariant set
\begin{equation}\tag{SPA23}
X^{\mathrm{cl}}(R)
=(R\times\{1_L\})_y\times(R\times\{1_L\})_x
\cong R^2.
\end{equation}
The restrictions of (SPA20) there are exactly
$(y,x)\mapsto(ay,ax+b)$ and
$(y,x)\mapsto(yu,yv+x)$.

For $R=\mathbb A_{\mathbb Q}$ and rational left coefficients,
use the injective diagonal map $\mathbb Q\to R$ to let
$\mathcal P_L(\mathbb Q)$ act. Apply the Karoubi action
construction to the left fixed sets in (SPA21).
Its full subcategory on $E_{1_L}$ and states in (SPA23) is
precisely
\begin{equation}\tag{SPA24}
P(\mathbb Q)\ltimes\mathbb A_{\mathbb Q}^2.
\end{equation}
The bijection records each original adelic state and each
rational affine coefficient. All source, target, identity,
and composition formulas agree by (SPA20). This is the
transformation groupoid presenting the original
Connes--Consani quotient \texttt{adelicomp3}.
The source uses $\iota(x,y)$; the matrix first row is
$(y,x)$, as retained throughout (SPA19)--(SPA24).

The entire top-translation corner is larger when $L$ is
nontrivial: it still contains
$((0,\lambda),(x,1_L))$ for every $\lambda<1_L$.
Thus the classical groupoid is a full invariant subcategory
of that corner, not the entire corner.
The amplitude projection of (SPA22), with $x$ fixed, has
one-point fibres at $y\ne0$ and fibre $L$ at $y=0$.

A placewise adelic carrier has a separate label at each
zero place; it is not $G_L(\mathbb A_{\mathbb Q})$ with
one global label. Formulas (SPA20) apply at every place,
and the separately constructed adelic product retains its
placewise fibres. No replacement of that product by one
global label is asserted.

\subsection{The original real action and its analytic content}

For the source's right real action, the multiplier is
the identity at every finite place and has real component
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}u&v\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
Thus finite coordinates are unchanged and at infinity
\begin{equation}\tag{SPA25}
(y_\infty,x_\infty)\longmapsto
(u y_\infty,\ x_\infty+v y_\infty).
\end{equation}
Differentiating $(u,v)=(e^\epsilon,0)$ and $(1,\epsilon)$
on the original top-supported complex leaf gives
\[
Y=y_\infty\partial_{y_\infty},\qquad
X=y_\infty\partial_{x_\infty},\qquad [Y,X]=X,\qquad
X+iY=y_\infty(\partial_{x_\infty}+i\partial_{y_\infty}).
\]
There is a typographical error in the original Section 6.6
display for $u(\epsilon)$: its upper-left entry is printed
as $0$, although it is called a one-parameter subgroup
and its following multiplication retains $y$.
The correct entry is $1$, as follows from the source's
definition \texttt{alggroupP} and the entrywise product
(SPA20). No calculation here uses that printed $0$ as
a group element.

Right scaling $(u,v)=(\mu,0)$ sends the original coordinate
$z=x_\infty+i y_\infty$ to $x_\infty+i\mu y_\infty$.
With the source's operator convention,
\begin{equation}\tag{SPA26}
(\partial_{x_\infty}+i\partial_{y_\infty})
 (x_\infty+i\mu y_\infty)=1-\mu.
\end{equation}
It is not holomorphic in that unchanged complex structure
unless $\mu=1$. The support construction retains this exact
calculation on its faithful classical subcategory.
It does not by itself replace the distinct Witt-coefficient
mechanism in \texttt{holom}--\texttt{holom1}.
This identifies the surviving analytic issue while retaining
the complete algebraic comparison.

\subsection{Boundary conventions}

The two identity roles remain distinct:
\begin{equation}\tag{SPA27}
E_{0_L}\ne E_{1_L},\qquad
F_{E_{0_L}}(\tau)=\tau,\qquad
F_{E_{1_L}}(\tau)=e,\qquad F_{E_{1_L}}(e)=e.
\end{equation}
Every intermediate idempotent and zero label remains in
the category and its action. No completeness assumption
on $L$ is used: all joins and meets are finite.
Zero divisors in $R$ do not change the proofs, because
every division is by an explicitly required unit.

If $L$ has one element, its bottom and top coincide and
\begin{equation}\tag{SPA28}
G_L(R)=R,\qquad \mathcal P_L(R)=P(R),\qquad D_{0_L}=R.
\end{equation}
There is one Karoubi object and the original affine group
action. If $R$ is the zero ring, its unit set has one
element, every amplitude is zero, and the formulas reduce
to $(L,\vee,0_L)$ acting on the zero labels by joins.
Every local group is then a singleton, while the category
and the inverse-domain formulas remain valid.
