\subsection{The complete coordinate carrier and the admitted prime strata}

Let $L$ be a nontrivial bounded distributive lattice. Retain, for every
commutative ring $R$,
\begin{equation}\tag{SPL1}
\begin{gathered}
G_L(R)=\{(0,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}\cup\{(r,1_L):r\in R\},\\
(r,\lambda)+(s,\mu)=(r+s,\lambda\vee\mu),\qquad
(r,\lambda)(s,\mu)=(rs,\lambda\wedge\mu),\\
\tau=(0,0_L),\qquad e=(0,1_L),\qquad {\bf1}=(1,1_L).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Put $\Sigma=\{\infty\}\cup\{\hbox{rational primes}\}$,
$\mathbb A=\mathbb A_{\mathbb Q}$, and
\begin{equation}\tag{SPL2}
S_{\rm loc}=G_L(\mathbb R)\times\prod_q'G_L(\mathbb Q_q),
\qquad Z_L=S_{\rm loc}\times S_{\rm loc}.
\end{equation}
The restricted factors are $G_L(\mathbb Z_q)$; thus an element of $S_{\rm loc}$
is $(z,\boldsymbol\lambda)$ with $z\in\mathbb A$ and
$z_v\ne0\Rightarrow\lambda_v=1_L$. There is no finiteness restriction
on labels at zero coordinates. All sums and products are coordinatewise.
The original common-label carrier has the injective map
$(z,\lambda)\mapsto((z_v,\lambda))_v$; it is not identified with
$S_{\rm loc}$. Write a point of $Z_L$ as
$((y,\boldsymbol\alpha),(x,\boldsymbol\beta))$.
Thus this calculation forms the local fields and their restricted
product after applying the original $G_L$ construction at each place.
It does not assume that $G_L$ commutes with forming the adelic ring.
Coordinatewise projection and the displayed common-label injection
are its exact comparisons with that other order of construction.

The rational left matrix action with translation label
$\boldsymbol\gamma\in L^\Sigma$ is
\begin{equation}\tag{SPL3}
((y,\boldsymbol\alpha),(x,\boldsymbol\beta))
\longmapsto
((ay,\boldsymbol\alpha),(ax+b,\boldsymbol\beta\vee\boldsymbol\gamma)),
\quad a\in\mathbb Q^\times,\quad
b\ne0\Rightarrow\boldsymbol\gamma=\boldsymbol1.
\end{equation}
Its inverse matrix, with amplitude translation $-a^{-1}b$, restores a
point precisely when $\boldsymbol\beta\ge\boldsymbol\gamma$.
Indeed the double product has amplitude $(y,x)$ and second label
$\boldsymbol\beta\vee\boldsymbol\gamma$. Consequently nonzero
rational translations are partial bijections on the all-top $x$ labels;
on any fixed $\boldsymbol\beta\ne\boldsymbol1$ the permitted invertible
actions are exactly simultaneous rational dilations. This is an actual
partial-action quotient, not the equivalence closure of noninvertible
support-raising maps. The latter would discard some of the labels.

Fix a rational prime $p$, a label $\lambda\in L$, and a nonempty subset
$T$ of the finite rational primes; $T$ may be infinite. Fix exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{SPL4}
\begin{gathered}
\alpha_p=\lambda,\quad \alpha_v=1_L\ (v\ne p),\qquad
\beta_\infty=1_L,\quad
\beta_q<1_L\ (q\in T),\quad \beta_q=1_L\ (q\notin T),\\
y_p=0,\quad y_q\in\mathbb Z_q^\times\ (q\ne p,\infty),\quad
y_\infty=r>0,\qquad x_q=0\ (q\in T).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Every specified lower label is retained, including labels strictly
between bottom and top. Let
\begin{equation}\tag{SPL5}
B_T=\{x\in\mathbb A:x_q=0\ (q\in T)\}
=\mathbb R\times\prod_{q\notin T}'\mathbb Q_q,
\end{equation}
where the equality inserts zero at all omitted places. None of the
strata SPL4 lies in the image of the common-label $G_L(\mathbb A)^2$:
its $x$ labels are top at infinity and non-top at a place in $T$.
These are features of the explicitly constructed placewise adelic
object, not extra points assigned to the smaller common-label carrier.
The group $B_T$ is locally compact in its original restricted-product
topology. Dividing the $y$ coordinate by
$K=\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{\times}$ makes it uniquely represented by
$y_q=1$ for $q\ne p,\infty$, $y_p=0$, $y_\infty=r$.
Two such representatives are related by positive rational multiplication
and $K$ exactly when the multiplier is $p^n$, $n\in\mathbb Z$:
its valuation is zero at every prime other than $p$, and its sign is
positive. The surviving quotient is therefore exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{SPL6}
\Gamma_{p,T;\lambda,\boldsymbol\beta}
=p^{\mathbb Z}\backslash(B_T\times\mathbb R_+^*),\qquad
p^n\cdot(x,r)=(p^n x,p^n r).
\end{equation}
There is no quotient by additive $\mathbb Q$ in SPL6, since
$\boldsymbol\beta\ne\boldsymbol1$ in SPL3. This proves the stated
description directly from the full original coordinate operations.
For the separate all-top $x$ stratum, rational translations are available
and the identical calculation instead gives the original compact lift
\begin{equation}\tag{SPL7}
\Gamma_{p;\lambda}^{\rm top}
=p^{\mathbb Z}\backslash(G\times\mathbb R_+^*),
\qquad G=\mathbb A/\mathbb Q.
\end{equation}
Each fixed $\lambda$ gives that original lift, with $\lambda$ still
attached to $y_p=0$. Taking all labels is not permission to multiply
any original trace by $|L|$.

\subsection{Exact return, density and the lost additive quotient}

Define $\mathbb A_T=\prod_{q\in T}'\mathbb Q_q$, restricted relative
to $\mathbb Z_q$. Projection onto the $T$ coordinates is a continuous
open surjection $\mathbb A\to\mathbb A_T$ with kernel $B_T$:
all assertions follow from the restricted-product basic open sets,
and a tuple is lifted by inserting zero at all other places.
The diagonal $\mathbb Q\to\mathbb A_T$ is injective since $T$ is
nonempty. There is an exact sequence of additive groups
\begin{equation}\tag{SPL8}
0\longrightarrow B_T\xrightarrow{\iota_T}G
\xrightarrow{\pi_T}\mathbb A_T/\mathbb Q\longrightarrow0,
\qquad\iota_T(x)=[x],\quad\pi_T([z])=[(z_q)_{q\in T}].
\end{equation}
Well-definedness of $\pi_T$ follows by changing $z$ by a diagonal
rational. Its kernel consists precisely of classes having a
representative whose $T$ coordinates vanish, proving exactness.
The first map is injective because a diagonal rational that vanishes
at one place is zero. The last map is onto by the preceding lift.

The subgroup $\mathbb Q$ is dense in $\mathbb A_T$ even if $T$ is
infinite. To prove this, fix a basic open set, specifying sufficiently
small balls at a finite set $F\subset T$ and integrality elsewhere.
Choose $N$ with prime factors in $F$ so that all specified centres
become integral after multiplication by $N$. The Chinese remainder
theorem gives an integer $m$ meeting the finitely many required
congruences, so $m/N$ belongs to the original open set and is integral
at every place outside $F$. It follows from the openness of projection
that $B_T+\mathbb Q$ is dense in $\mathbb A$. Thus $\iota_T(B_T)$
is dense in $G$. It is proper: choose a nonrational element of
$\mathbb Q_q$ at one $q\in T$, and zero elsewhere, to obtain a
nonzero class in $\mathbb A_T/\mathbb Q$.
In particular the final quotient in SPL8 has the indiscrete quotient
topology; SPL8 is not a short exact sequence of Hausdorff locally
compact groups. No topology of $B_T$ is replaced by its image topology.

All maps in SPL8 commute with multiplication by $p$. They induce the
explicit continuous injective return
\begin{equation}\tag{SPL9}
\mathcal I_T:\Gamma_{p,T;\lambda,\boldsymbol\beta}
\longrightarrow\Gamma_{p;\lambda}^{\rm top},
\qquad[(x,r)]\longmapsto[([x],r)].
\end{equation}
For injectivity, equality of images gives $r'=p^n r$ and
$x'-p^n x\in\mathbb Q$. This difference lies in $B_T$, so it is
zero. The image is dense and proper: both mapping tori locally have
product charts over $r\bmod p^{\mathbb Z}$, and the fibre map is
the dense proper injection $B_T\to G$. This also shows the return
retains the same base circle and its literal circumference $\log p$.

The supported mapping torus is noncompact. The continuous function
\begin{equation}\tag{SPL10}
[(x,r)]\longmapsto x_\infty/r
\end{equation}
is well defined and onto $\mathbb R$, since both numerator and
denominator acquire the same factor $p^n$. In contrast the original
mapping torus SPL7 is compact: it is the quotient of
$G\times[1,p]$ identifying $(g,1)$ with $(pg,p)$.
The same description with $B_T$ proves that SPL6 is its mapping
torus, in the original noncompact fibre topology.

\subsection{The original real action and its entire leaf metric}

Right multiplication by the original real matrix
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}u&v\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
$u>0$, induces
\begin{equation}\tag{SPL11}
(x,r)\longmapsto(x+j_\infty(vr),ur),
\end{equation}
where $j_\infty(t)$ is the adele with real coordinate $t$ and all
finite coordinates zero. SPL11 preserves $B_T$, all fixed labels and
the relation SPL6. The upper-right label at infinity remains top,
and no finite label changes. Composition agrees with the original
matrix product, as follows either by multiplying the two matrices
or by substituting twice in SPL11. The return SPL9 intertwines
SPL11 with the Connes--Consani action on $G$.

On the positive real coordinate plane of each parametrized orbit,
the original fields and metric are retained:
\begin{equation}\tag{SPL12}
X=r\partial_{x_\infty},\qquad Y=r\partial_r,\qquad
ds^2=\frac{dx_\infty^2+dr^2}{r^2},\qquad
X+iY=r(\partial_{x_\infty}+i\partial_r),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{SPL13}
\Delta=X^2+(Y-\tfrac12)^2-\tfrac14
=r^2(\partial_{x_\infty}^2+\partial_r^2).
\end{equation}
For verification, $Y^2=r^2\partial_r^2+r\partial_r$ and
$(Y-\tfrac12)^2=Y^2-Y+\tfrac14$; the displayed constants cancel
in the stated identity and have not been omitted from its original
operator. The equality is on smooth functions before taking any
Hilbert completion. It does not identify a spectrum or a trace.
At a point with some nonzero finite $x_q$, a stabilizer in SPL11
would require $p^n x_q=x_q$, hence $n=0$, then $u=1,v=0$.
The corresponding orbit is a copy of the upper half plane in its
intrinsic leaf topology; no equality with an ambient subspace topology
is asserted. When all
finite coordinates of $x$ vanish, all such points belong to one orbit;
its parametrization is the upper half plane modulo simultaneous
$(x_\infty,r)\mapsto(p^n x_\infty,p^n r)$. This proves the exact
exceptional cylinder, including the case when $T$ is every finite
prime and there are no other orbits. The metric descends because
each of these simultaneous dilations preserves SPL12.

\subsection{All Haar factors and the prime-dependent defect}

Give $B_T$ its product Haar measure $\mu_T$: Lebesgue measure at
infinity and Haar measures with $\mu_q(\mathbb Z_q)=1$ at every
remaining finite place. For any $a\in\mathbb Q^\times$, multiplication
by $a$ is an automorphism, with exact modulus
\begin{equation}\tag{SPL14}
\mu_T(aE)=\mathfrak m_T(a)\mu_T(E),\qquad
\mathfrak m_T(a)=|a|_\infty\prod_{q\notin T}|a|_q
=\prod_{q\in T}|a|_q^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Only finitely many factors differ from one. Local changes of variables
prove the first expression on product rectangles, hence on Borel sets
by uniqueness of Haar measure; the rational product formula proves
the second. In particular, without changing any measure,
\begin{equation}\tag{SPL15}
\mathfrak m_T(p^n)=p^{n\epsilon_{p,T}},\qquad
\epsilon_{p,T}=\begin{cases}1,&p\in T,\\0,&p\notin T.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The original compact group $G$ carries its Haar probability measure
$m_G$. Multiplication by $p$ preserves $m_G$: it is an automorphism,
so the pushforward is again Haar measure of total mass one. Thus
the injective equivariant map SPL8 has different specified Haar
moduli on its two sides when $p\in T$. This is possible because
it is not a Haar-measure-preserving identification.

More strongly, $(\iota_T)_*\mu_T$ is not a Radon measure on compact
$G$: its mass on $G$ is $\mu_T(B_T)=\infty$. The latter follows
from the real factor, by taking arbitrarily long real intervals and
the product of all remaining finite integral subgroups of additive
volume one. A Radon measure on a compact space has finite total
mass. Thus using the original compact Haar measure in place of the
supported source measure would require an additional, nontrivial
construction; SPL9 alone does not justify it.

For a direct operator check put $V_p f(x)=f(x/p)$ on
$L^2(B_T,\mu_T)$. Full change of variables gives
\begin{equation}\tag{SPL16}
\begin{gathered}
\|V_p f\|^2=p^{\epsilon_{p,T}}\|f\|^2,\qquad
V_p^*g(x)=p^{\epsilon_{p,T}}g(px),\\
V_p^*V_p=p^{\epsilon_{p,T}}I,\qquad
U_p=p^{-\epsilon_{p,T}/2}V_p,\quad U_p^*U_p=I.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The last line is an explicit comparison with a different operator;
it does not replace $V_p$. Its factor is derived from the Haar
modulus, not an asserted spectral weight of the original zeta.
On the cover $B_T\times\mathbb R_+^*$, the original product measure
$d\mu_T(x)\,dr/r$ has change-of-set factor $p^{\epsilon_{p,T}}$
under $(x,r)\mapsto(px,pr)$. The additional density
\begin{equation}\tag{SPL17}
r^{-\epsilon_{p,T}}d\mu_T(x)\,\frac{dr}{r}
\end{equation}
is invariant, since its pointwise multiplier contributes the
opposite factor $p^{-\epsilon_{p,T}}$. Both measures, and their
literal relation, are retained. No change of scalar norm or
Archimedean endpoint convention is implicit.

SPL6--17 apply to every prime $p$, every nonempty set $T$ of finite
primes and every specified admissible label tuple. They compute an
entire supported family of complex lifts and its return, rather
than a finite sample of zero coordinates. The compact top stratum
recovers the Connes--Consani lift exactly; the full supported object
also retains the noncompact strata and their different Haar factors.
No positivity conclusion for the arithmetic trace follows merely
from this geometry. These are the exact spaces and measure maps
through which a proposed trace or Riemann--Roch comparison must act.
