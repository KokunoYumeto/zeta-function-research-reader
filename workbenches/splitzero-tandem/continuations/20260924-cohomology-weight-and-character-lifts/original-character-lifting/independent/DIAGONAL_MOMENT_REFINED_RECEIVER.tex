\subsection{The original diagonal map and its moment}

Put
\begin{equation}\tag{DMR1}
\begin{gathered}
G=\mathbb Q^\times,\quad G_+=\mathbb Q_{>0}^\times,\quad
T=\mathcal S(\mathbb R)\otimes\bigotimes_p'
       (\mathcal S(\mathbb Q_p),\mathbf1_{\mathbb Z_p}),\\
(U_a\xi)(x)=\xi(a^{-1}x),\qquad
E\xi(x)=\sum_{q\in\mathbb Q^\times}\xi(qx),\\
B=\operatorname{LC}(\{0,1\}^{\{\infty\}\sqcup\mathcal P},\mathbb C),
\qquad
b=\bigotimes_v\left(\xi_v\mapsto
                  (\xi_v(0),\int_{\mathbb Q_v}\xi_v)\right),\\
K=\ker(b\otimes b)\cap\ker(E\otimes E)\subset T\otimes T.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Here $dx_\infty$ is Lebesgue measure and
$\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb Z_p)=1$. For an idele $a$ its original
modulus is
$|a|=|a_\infty|_\infty\prod_p|a_p|_p$.
The action on $T\otimes T$ is the diagonal $U_a\otimes U_a$;
both factors and their order are retained.

Fix $y_1,y_2>0$ and let $a_i=(y_i,1_f)$. Define
\begin{equation}\tag{DMR2}
\begin{aligned}
\mu_y:T\otimes T&\longrightarrow T,&
(\mu_yt)(z)&=t(a_1z,a_2z),\\
m_y:T\otimes T&\longrightarrow\mathbb C,&
m_y(k)&=\int_{\mathbb A_\mathbb Q}(\mu_yk)(z)\,dz.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The moment is defined on the entire displayed tensor source; its
restriction to $K$ is the map whose exact sequence is computed below.
This also fixes its domain on the GSB antecedents $z_p$, which need
not belong to $K$.
For a pure tensor of elementary adelic tests, the real factor of
its pullback is $f_\infty(y_1z)g_\infty(y_2z)$, a Schwartz
function. Its finite factors are the products $f_p(z)g_p(z)$,
which are locally constant with compact support and equal the
reference indicator outside finitely many primes. This proves
that DMR2 is an actual linear map on the stated algebraic tensor.
It does not require an extension to a completed tensor product.

The multiplication of the two arguments commutes with every idele
and every rational dilation. Thus
\begin{equation}\tag{DMR3}
\mu_y(U_a\otimes U_a)=U_a\mu_y,\qquad
m_y((U_a\otimes U_a)k)=|a|m_y(k),\qquad
(\mu_yk)(0)=0\quad(k\in K).
\end{equation}
The middle identity is the original additive change of variable
$z=aw$, including all local factors. The last follows by evaluating
$(b\otimes b)k=0$ at the pattern selecting evaluation at every
place in both columns; its value is exactly $k(0,0)$.
It does not assert that every coefficient in a finite-ball expansion
of $\mu_yk$ has real evaluation zero. DMR24 below shows why that
stronger assertion would be false.

The map $m_y$ is onto for every positive pair. An explicit witness
retains the function
\begin{equation}\tag{DMR4}
\begin{gathered}
h(v)=\left(\pi^2v^4-\frac{3\pi}{2}v^2\right)e^{-\pi v^2},
\qquad
f(x)=h(x_\infty)\mathbf1_{\widehat{\mathbb Z}}(x_f),\\
g_y(x)=e^{-\pi(y_1/y_2)^2x_\infty^2}
                 \mathbf1_{\widehat{\mathbb Z}}(x_f),
\qquad
u_y=((U_2-I)f)\otimes g_y.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The function $h$ is the original Gaussian-polynomial test used in
GEC1; no change to its constants is made here. Its two moments
are
\[
h(0)=0,\qquad
\int_{\mathbb R}h(v)\,dv
=\pi^2\frac3{4\pi^2}
 -\frac{3\pi}{2}\frac1{2\pi}=0.
\]
Consequently $bf=0$ and $bU_2f=0$. Rational reindexing in the
original sum gives $E(U_2-I)f=0$. These two identities prove
$u_y\in K$ before any quotient is taken.

The finite support of the first term of $\mu_yu_y$ is
$2\mathbb Z_2\times\prod_{p\ne2}\mathbb Z_p$, with additive
volume $1/2$. That of its second term is $\widehat{\mathbb Z}$,
with volume one. The real substitution $v=y_1z_\infty$ therefore
gives
\begin{equation}\tag{DMR5}
\begin{split}
C_y:=m_y(u_y)
 &=\frac1{y_1}\left\{
 \frac12\int_{\mathbb R}h(v/2)e^{-\pi v^2}\,dv
       -\int_{\mathbb R}h(v)e^{-\pi v^2}\,dv\right\}\\
 &=\frac3{4y_1}\left\{\frac1{2^{5/2}}
                            -\frac4{5^{5/2}}\right\}>0.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
For verification, direct differentiation of
$\int e^{-\pi Av^2}dv=A^{-1/2}$ yields
$\int v^2e^{-\pi Av^2}dv=(2\pi A^{3/2})^{-1}$ and
$\int v^4e^{-\pi Av^2}dv=3/(4\pi^2A^{5/2})$ for $A>0$.
The first integral in braces, with its factor $1/2$, is
$\int h(w)e^{-4\pi w^2}dw=-3/5^{5/2}$;
the second is $-3/(4\,2^{5/2})$.
Finally $5^5=3125>512=16\cdot2^5$ proves the strict inequality
in DMR5 without a numerical estimate.

Put $v_y=u_y/C_y$. This is an explicitly specified element of
the original space, with the scalar $C_y$ and every summand of
$u_y$ retained, and $m_y(v_y)=1$. It supplies a linear section
of the moment map. In particular, with
$K_y^0=\ker(m_y:K\to\mathbb C)$, the actual coefficient sequence is
\begin{equation}\tag{DMR6}
0\longrightarrow K_y^0\longrightarrow K
   \xrightarrow{\ m_y\ }\mathbb C(1)\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
The idele action on $\mathbb C(1)$ is multiplication by $|a|$;
its rational action is trivial by the product formula. DMR3
proves equivariance. The chosen vector $v_y$ is a linear
section, not an equivariant section.

\subsection{The ABR target with its full adelic action}

Let $Q:T\to T$ be the exact compact projection
\begin{equation}\tag{DMR7}
Q\xi=\frac12\int_{\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{\times}}
              (U_{(1,u)}\xi+U_{(-1,u)}\xi)\,du,
\qquad \int_{\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{\times}}du=1.
\end{equation}
For a finite Bruhat test this is a finite average, and the real
sign average is the displayed factor $1/2$. It preserves the two
original moments and commutes with rational and idele dilation.
Write $T_0=\{\xi:\xi(0)=0,\int\xi=0\}$ and
$T_{[0]}=\{\xi:\xi(0)=0\}$. Then DMR3 gives the commutative
diagram of exact coefficient rows
\begin{equation}\tag{DMR8}
\begin{array}{ccccccccc}
0&\longrightarrow&K_y^0&\longrightarrow&K
 &\xrightarrow{m_y}&\mathbb C(1)&\longrightarrow&0\\
&&\big\downarrow Q\mu_y&&\big\downarrow Q\mu_y
 &&\big\downarrow I\\
0&\longrightarrow&QT_0&\longrightarrow&QT_{[0]}
 &\xrightarrow{\int}&\mathbb C(1)&\longrightarrow&0.
\end{array}
\end{equation}
The last row is onto because
$2\pi x_\infty^2e^{-\pi x_\infty^2}\mathbf1_{\widehat{\mathbb Z}}$
has evaluation zero and integral one.
The restriction to the spherical source $(Q\otimes Q)K$ also
has all these maps, and the explicit moment witness DMR4 lies
in that source. The full-source definition in DMR8 instead
uses $Q\mu_y$: independent averaging in the two tensor factors
has not been identified with averaging their diagonal pullback.
In particular no identity $\mu_y(Q\otimes Q)=Q\mu_y$, and no
preservation of $K_y^0$ by $Q\otimes Q$, is needed or asserted.

For clarity the ABR target is the spherical source coinvariant,
not all of $H_0(G,T_0)$:
\begin{equation}\tag{DMR9}
\begin{gathered}
R=\mathbb C[G_+],\quad I=\ker\varepsilon_R,
\quad H=\mathcal S_{\rm even}(\mathbb R),
\quad M_0=\ker(\varepsilon_R\otimes m),\\
m(h)=(h(0),\int h),\quad H_{00}=\ker m,\quad
W_{\rm pr}=\bigoplus_p\mathbb C\ell_p,\\
\Theta(t_a\otimes h)(x)=h(x_\infty/a)
                          \mathbf1_{a\widehat{\mathbb Z}}(x_f),
\quad Q_0=H_0(G,QT_0)=M_0/IM_0.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
ABR3--10 proves that $\Theta$ is an exact isomorphism, and gives
the exact coordinates
\begin{equation}\tag{DMR10}
\begin{gathered}
(\Phi,\beta):Q_0\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }
        H_{00}\oplus(W_{\rm pr}\otimes\mathbb C^2),\\
\Phi[\textstyle\sum_a t_a\otimes h_a]=\sum_a h_a,\qquad
\beta[\textstyle\sum_a t_a\otimes h_a]
 =\sum_p\ell_p\otimes\sum_a v_p(a)m(h_a),\\
j_i\left(\sum_p w_p\ell_p\right)
 =\sum_p[(t_p-1)\otimes w_p\eta_i],\\
\eta_0(x)=(1-2\pi x^2)e^{-\pi x^2},\quad
\eta_1(x)=2\pi x^2e^{-\pi x^2}\quad(i=0,1).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Here $\beta=(\beta_0,\beta_1)$ refers to the evaluation and
integral entries respectively. Each $j_i$ is injective;
$\Phi j_i=0$, $\beta_i j_i=I$ and $\beta_{1-i}j_i=0$.
The section-independence of $j_i$ follows exactly as in ABR10:
the difference of two tests having the same two moments is in
$H_{00}$, and its product with $t_p-1$ belongs to $IM_0$.

The full idele action, including every finite-place modulus, has
the following exact description in these coordinates. For an idele
$a$ put $r=\prod_p p^{v_p(a_p)}>0$, a rational number, and
$b=|a_\infty|_\infty/r=|a|$. Compact units and real sign act
trivially on $QT$. On a spherical coefficient
\begin{equation}\tag{DMR11}
U_a\Theta(t_c\otimes h)=\Theta(t_{rc}\otimes R_bh),
\quad R_bh(x)=h(x/b),\quad
m(R_bh)=\operatorname{diag}(1,b)m(h).
\end{equation}
Indeed the finite ball becomes $rc\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ and the
real argument is $x_\infty/(|a_\infty|_\infty c)$ on both
sides. For $\sum_ct_c\otimes h_c\in M_0$, the extra contribution
$v_p(r)\sum_cm(R_bh_c)$ to $\beta$ vanishes because its sum
is $m(R_b\Phi)=0$. It follows that
\begin{equation}\tag{DMR12}
\Phi U_a=R_{|a|}\Phi,\qquad
\beta_0U_a=\beta_0,\qquad \beta_1U_a=|a|\beta_1,
\qquad U_aj_1(w)=j_1(|a|w).
\end{equation}
This derives the full idele statement from the original finite
and real scales; it does not substitute a real-only action for it.

\subsection{The entire all-prime ambiguity and its exact splitting}

For any $G$-module $M$ put
$N_GM=\sum_{g\in G}(U_g-I)M$, with finite sums, so
$H_0(G,M)=M/N_GM$. The sign group contributes no positive
homology over $\mathbb C$ because its averaging projection is
exact. Prime factorization therefore identifies
$H_1(G,\mathbb C)=W_{\rm pr}$; its basis is the original ordered
prime Koszul basis. The connecting map of DMR6 is
\begin{equation}\tag{DMR13}
\delta_y:W_{\rm pr}\longrightarrow H_0(G,K_y^0),\qquad
\delta_y\left(\sum_pw_p\ell_p\right)
   =\left[\sum_pw_p(U_p\otimes U_p-I)v_y\right].
\end{equation}
Each coefficient has moment zero by rational invariance and
belongs to $K$. Changing $v_y$ to any other moment-one lift
changes the result by relations from $K_y^0$, so DMR13 is the
canonical connecting map, independent of that chosen section.

Let
\begin{equation}\tag{DMR14}
F_y=H_0(Q\mu_y):H_0(G,K_y^0)\longrightarrow Q_0,
\qquad B_y=\beta_1F_y.
\end{equation}
These are defined on the specified source quotient: an actual
relation from $K_y^0$ maps to a relation from $QT_0$.
For any $w\in QT$ with moment vector $(0,c)$, ABR9 gives
\[
\Phi[(U_p-I)w]=0,\qquad
\beta[(U_p-I)w]=\ell_p\otimes(0,c).
\]
To check directly, write $w=\Theta(\sum_at_a\otimes h_a)$.
The change $a\mapsto pa$ leaves the augmented sum unchanged
and adds $\ell_p\otimes\sum_am(h_a)$. This proves the
displayed identity with no restriction on the shape of $w$.
Apply it to $Q\mu_yv_y$, whose moments are $(0,1)$, to obtain
\begin{equation}\tag{DMR15}
F_y\delta_y=j_1,\qquad B_y\delta_y=I_{W_{\rm pr}}.
\end{equation}
In particular $\delta_y$ is injective. Thus the map
$H_1(G,K)\to H_1(G,\mathbb C(1))$ preceding it in the homology
sequence is zero; this is a conclusion of the calculation, not
an assumption about the homology of $K$.

The moment descends to an onto map
$\overline m_y:H_0(G,K)\to\mathbb C(1)$. Define
$S_y=\ker\overline m_y$ and let $\pi_y$ be the map induced by
the inclusion $K_y^0\subset K$. Its image is exactly $S_y$:
moment is constant on every coinvariant class, so every class
of moment zero has a representative in $K_y^0$.
The exact result is
\begin{equation}\tag{DMR16}
\begin{gathered}
0\longrightarrow W_{\rm pr}(1)
 \xrightarrow{\ \delta_y\ }H_0(G,K_y^0)
 \xrightarrow{\ \pi_y\ }S_y\longrightarrow0,\\
0\longrightarrow S_y\longrightarrow H_0(G,K)
 \xrightarrow{\ \overline m_y\ }\mathbb C(1)\longrightarrow0,\\
N_GK\subset K_y^0,\qquad
N_GK/N_GK_y^0\xleftarrow{\ \cong\ }W_{\rm pr}(1),
\quad w\longmapsto\delta_y(w).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The last line states the complete relation defect, with its
actual coefficient representative supplied by DMR13. Exactness
can also be seen without abbreviating the relations: write
$k=m_y(k)v_y+k^0$, $k^0\in K_y^0$. Then
$(U_g\otimes U_g-I)k$ is congruent modulo $N_GK_y^0$ to
$m_y(k)(U_g\otimes U_g-I)v_y$.
The identity
$U_{ab}-I=(U_a-I)+(U_b-I)+(U_a-I)(U_b-I)$
reduces the latter classes to their prime valuations; the last
product is a relation from $K_y^0$. The sign class is zero
since the sign has order two over $\mathbb C$. This proves
spanning by DMR13. DMR15 proves independence of every prime
coefficient and completes the direct verification of the last
line of DMR16.

The first row of DMR16 has a canonical splitting obtained from
the actual receiver, while retaining both components. For $s\in S_y$
choose any $c\in H_0(G,K_y^0)$ with $\pi_yc=s$ and put
\begin{equation}\tag{DMR17}
\begin{gathered}
\lambda_y(s)=c-\delta_y B_yc,\qquad
\Psi_y(c)=(\pi_yc,B_yc),\\
\Psi_y:H_0(G,K_y^0)\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }
                S_y\oplus W_{\rm pr}(1),\qquad
\Psi_y^{-1}(s,w)=\lambda_y(s)+\delta_y(w).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
If another lift is $c+\delta_yw$, DMR15 shows that its displayed
expression for $\lambda_y$ is identical. Thus $\lambda_y$ is
well-defined, $\pi_y\lambda_y=I$, and $B_y\lambda_y=0$.
These identities prove both inverse identities in DMR17. No
term of the original class is discarded: its second coordinate
and its exact inverse inclusion $\delta_y$ are retained.
All maps are equivariant for the full idele action, with
$W_{\rm pr}(1)$ carrying $|a|$, by DMR3, DMR12 and naturality
of the connecting map.

The resulting exact receiver is
\begin{equation}\tag{DMR18}
\begin{gathered}
\widehat F_y=F_y\lambda_y:S_y\longrightarrow Q_0,
\qquad \beta_1\widehat F_y=0,\\
F_y(c)=\widehat F_y(\pi_yc)+j_1(B_yc),\qquad
\widehat F_y(s)\in H_{00}\oplus j_0(W_{\rm pr})
                    \quad\hbox{under DMR10},\\
\begin{array}{ccccccccc}
0&\longrightarrow&W_{\rm pr}(1)&\xrightarrow{\delta_y}&
 H_0(G,K_y^0)&\xrightarrow{\pi_y}&S_y&\longrightarrow&0\\
&&\big\downarrow j_1&&\big\downarrow F_y&&\big\downarrow\overline F_y\\
0&\longrightarrow&j_1(W_{\rm pr})&\longrightarrow&Q_0
 &\longrightarrow&Q_0/j_1(W_{\rm pr})&\longrightarrow&0.
\end{array}
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Here $\overline F_y$ is induced on the quotient, and
$\widehat F_y$ is its unique lift having $\beta_1=0$.
The kernel of the quotient in the bottom row is specified and
carried separately in the middle formula. This proves the
strongest descent supplied by this calculation rather than
merely reporting the failure of a proposed map.

There cannot be a map $S_y\to Q_0$ whose composite with
$\pi_y$ equals $F_y$: it would send $\pi_y\delta_y(w)=0$ to
$F_y\delta_y(w)=j_1(w)\ne0$ for $w\ne0$.
This is the exact ambiguity of attempting to use $\mu_y$ on
the original $K$ coinvariants. Moreover $\mu_yK$ is not even
contained in $T_0$ before quotienting, since DMR5 is nonzero.
The corrected source, the lost relation subspace, its entire
prime image, and the surviving quotient receiver are all part
of DMR8 and DMR16--18.

\subsection{The complete GSB image under the diagonal receiver}

Let $\mathcal I_2$ be the actual GSB17 coefficient space
\begin{equation}\tag{DMR19}
\begin{gathered}
X=\{1,2\}^{\{\infty\}\sqcup\mathcal P},\qquad
\mathcal I_2=\{f\in\operatorname{LC}(X,\mathbb C):
                f(1,1,\ldots)=f(2,2,\ldots)=0\},\\
S^{\rm GSB}=\operatorname{im}\partial^\theta_{2,1}
 \cong\mathcal I_2\otimes\bigoplus_p\mathbb C\vartheta_p
 \subset H_0(G,K).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Every class has a representative $k=\sum_p(U_p\otimes U_p-I)z_p$
from GSB18--20, where only finitely many primes occur in that
representative, $z_p\in\ker(E\otimes E)$ and
$(b\otimes b)z_p\in\ker(\epsilon\otimes\epsilon)$.
All-prime means that no fixed finite prime set is imposed on
the space or the calculation. Rational invariance of the
diagonal integral gives $m_y(k)=0$, so $S^{\rm GSB}\subset S_y$.
Every $z_p$ has $z_p(0,0)=0$ by its endpoint condition.
Writing $\widetilde k$ for the class of this same coefficient
in $H_0(G,K_y^0)$, DMR10 and equivariance give
\begin{equation}\tag{DMR20}
\begin{gathered}
\Phi F_y(\widetilde k)=0,\qquad
\beta_0F_y(\widetilde k)=0,\qquad
\beta_1F_y(\widetilde k)=\sum_pm_y(z_p)\ell_p,\\
F_y(\widetilde k)=j_1\left(\sum_pm_y(z_p)\ell_p\right),
\qquad
\widehat F_y|_{S^{\rm GSB}}=0.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
For the first equality each rational difference has augmented
ABR coefficient zero. For the second, the evaluation moment
of every $\mu_yz_p$ is zero; for the third, its integral is
exactly $m_y(z_p)$. These establish the complete ABR coordinates,
not only the vanishing of periodization. DMR17--18 then prove
the last equality.

In particular, set $P_y=\pi_y^{-1}(S^{\rm GSB})$. The entire
reached preimage and all its receiver components are
\begin{equation}\tag{DMR21}
\begin{gathered}
P_y=\lambda_y(S^{\rm GSB})\oplus\delta_y(W_{\rm pr}(1)),\qquad
F_y(P_y)=j_1(W_{\rm pr}),\\
\ker(F_y|_{P_y})=\lambda_y(S^{\rm GSB}),\qquad
\lambda_y(S^{\rm GSB})\cong S^{\rm GSB}\ne0.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Changing a lift $z_p$ by $u\in K$ changes the raw prime
coordinate in DMR20 by $m_y(u)\ell_p$. DMR5 shows that this
change can be any complex coefficient at any selected prime.
Thus the raw prime coordinate is not an invariant of the GSB
class in $H_0(G,K)$. The pair in DMR21 retains it as the
second component of the refined source; it is not discarded
or mistaken for a nontrivial arithmetic interior class.

For the particular GSB21 representative retain
\begin{equation}\tag{DMR22}
\begin{gathered}
\phi_{p,0}=\frac{p\mathbf1_{p\mathbb Z_p}
                             -\mathbf1_{\mathbb Z_p}}{p-1},\qquad
\phi_{p,1}=\frac{p(\mathbf1_{\mathbb Z_p}
                             -\mathbf1_{p\mathbb Z_p})}{p-1},\\
a_p=\eta_1\otimes\phi_{p,0}\otimes
              \bigotimes_{\ell\ne p}\mathbf1_{\mathbb Z_\ell},\qquad
d_p=\eta_0\otimes\phi_{p,1}\otimes
              \bigotimes_{\ell\ne p}\mathbf1_{\mathbb Z_\ell},\\
z_p=((U_p-I)a_p)\otimes d_p/(p-1),\qquad
k_p=(U_p\otimes U_p-I)z_p,\\
(\mu_yz_p)(z)=\frac p{(p-1)^3}
 \eta_1(y_1z_\infty)\eta_0(y_2z_\infty)
 \mathbf1_{\widehat{\mathbb Z}}(z_f)
 \mathbf1_{\mathbb Z_p^\times}(\operatorname{pr}_p z).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
In the last line $\operatorname{pr}_p z$ denotes the local
component of the adele $z$; the preceding lines use $z_p$ for
the named tensor lift.
On this local unit shell, $\phi_{p,1}=p/(p-1)$,
$\phi_{p,0}=-1/(p-1)$ and $\phi_{p,0}(z_p/p)=0$.
Off it the factor $\phi_{p,1}$ vanishes. This proves the full
pullback formula including its sign and coefficient.
Its finite integral is
$p(1-p^{-1})/(p-1)^3=1/(p-1)^2$. The two real contributions
are retained in
\begin{equation}\tag{DMR23}
\begin{gathered}
R_p(y):=m_y(z_p)=\frac1{(p-1)^2}
\left\{\frac{y_1^2}{(y_1^2+y_2^2)^{3/2}}
       -\frac{3y_1^2y_2^2}{(y_1^2+y_2^2)^{5/2}}\right\},\\
F_y([k_p]_{K_y^0})=j_1(R_p(y)\ell_p),\qquad
\lambda_y([k_p]_K)=[k_p]_{K_y^0}-\delta_y(R_p(y)\ell_p),\\
F_y\lambda_y([k_p]_K)=0.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Combining the two displayed real contributions gives
$y_1^2(y_1^2-2y_2^2)/[(p-1)^2(y_1^2+y_2^2)^{5/2}]$;
the preceding unsuppressed contributions specify its origin.
Its possible vanishing when $y_1^2=2y_2^2$ does not make
$[k_p]_K$ zero: GSB21 independently proves that class nonzero
for every prime, and DMR17 supplies an injective lift of it.

\subsection{The evaluation boundary that survives for general source classes}

The vanishing of $\beta_0$ in DMR20 uses the actual GSB lift
and does not hold on every element of $K_y^0$. Here is an
explicit counterexample to that stronger statement, with
every finite coefficient retained. Fix a prime $p$ and put
\begin{equation}\tag{DMR24}
\begin{gathered}
\chi'_p=\mathbf1_{p^{-1}\mathbb Z_p}
              -(p+1)\mathbf1_{\mathbb Z_p}
              +p\mathbf1_{p\mathbb Z_p},\\
a=\eta_0\otimes\chi'_p\otimes
                  \bigotimes_{\ell\ne p}\mathbf1_{\mathbb Z_\ell},
\quad d=\eta_0\mathbf1_{\widehat{\mathbb Z}},
