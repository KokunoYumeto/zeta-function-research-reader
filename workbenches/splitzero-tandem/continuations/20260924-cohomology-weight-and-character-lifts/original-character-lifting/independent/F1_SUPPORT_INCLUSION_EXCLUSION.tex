\subsection{Every prime coordinate, with no branch selected}

Let \(L\) be a bounded distributive lattice with bounds \(0_L,1_L\).
An ideal is a nonempty downward closed subset closed under finite joins.
A proper ideal \(P\) is prime if
\(a\wedge b\in P\) implies \(a\in P\) or \(b\in P\).
Put \(X=\operatorname{SpecLat}(L)\), the set of all such ideals, and define
\begin{equation}\tag{FSI1}
\chi_\lambda:X\longrightarrow\{0,1\},\qquad
\chi_\lambda(P)=
\begin{cases}0,&\lambda\in P,\\1,&\lambda\notin P.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
All character coordinates are retained simultaneously.

For completeness, if \(a\not\le b\), there is \(P\in X\) containing
\(b\) and not \(a\). To prove this using Zorn's lemma, the principal
ideal \(\downarrow b\) and filter \(\uparrow a\) are disjoint.
Consider ideals containing \(\downarrow b\) and disjoint from
\(\uparrow a\). The union of a chain is an ideal with both properties:
any two elements belong together to one ideal of the chain.
A maximal such ideal \(P\) exists and is proper because \(1_L\)
belongs to \(\uparrow a\).

The ideal generated by \(P\) and \(x\) is exactly
\[
P[x]=\{z:z\le p\vee x\text{ for some }p\in P\}.
\]
This is downward closed, contains \(P\) and \(x\), and is closed
under joins since
\(z\le p\vee x,w\le q\vee x\) imply
\(z\vee w\le(p\vee q)\vee x\).
Every ideal containing \(P\) and \(x\) contains this set.
If \(x,y\notin P\), maximality gives
\(a\le p\vee x\) and \(a\le q\vee y\) for some \(p,q\in P\).
Were \(x\wedge y\in P\), distributivity would give
\begin{equation}\tag{FSI2}
\begin{split}
a&\le(p\vee x)\wedge(q\vee y)\\
&=(p\wedge q)\vee(p\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge q)\vee(x\wedge y)\\
&\le p\vee q\vee(x\wedge y)\in P,
\end{split}
\end{equation}
contradicting \(a\notin P\). Hence \(P\) is prime. This proves
separation for arbitrary, including infinite, bounded distributive
lattices with the stated use of Zorn's lemma.

Ideal closure gives
\(a\vee b\in P\) exactly when both \(a,b\in P\).
Primeness and downward closure give
\(a\wedge b\notin P\) exactly when neither belongs to \(P\).
Consequently, in the ordinary integer-valued function ring
\(\mathbb Z^X\),
\begin{equation}\tag{FSI3}
\begin{gathered}
\chi_{0_L}=0,\qquad \chi_{1_L}=1,\qquad
\chi_{\lambda\wedge\mu}=\chi_\lambda\chi_\mu,\\
\chi_{\lambda\vee\mu}
=\chi_\lambda+\chi_\mu-\chi_\lambda\chi_\mu .
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The sum and product on the right are ordinary pointwise integer
operations. The first sum does not stand for a second integer
addition. Its subtracted intersection term is part of the exact map.

The map \(\chi:L\to\{0,1\}^X\) reflects order:
if \(a\not\le b\), separation gives a coordinate with
\(\chi_a=1,\chi_b=0\). If \(a\le b\), downward closure gives
\(\chi_a\le\chi_b\) at every coordinate. It is therefore injective.
For the singleton lattice, \(0_L=1_L\), no proper prime ideals exist.
Then \(X=\varnothing\) and \(\{0,1\}^{\varnothing}\) is a singleton,
so the same map is an isomorphism of singleton lattices.
Here its zero and one functions are the same empty function.
No nonexistent character of the singleton lattice is chosen.

\subsection{Every finite overlap and the universal integer valuation}

For \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n\in L\), all \(n\ge1\), (FSI3) gives
\begin{equation}\tag{FSI4}
\begin{split}
\chi_{\bigvee_{i=1}^n\lambda_i}
&=1-\prod_{i=1}^n(1-\chi_{\lambda_i})\\
&=\sum_{\varnothing\ne J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}}
(-1)^{|J|+1}\prod_{j\in J}\chi_{\lambda_j}\\
&=\sum_{\varnothing\ne J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}}
(-1)^{|J|+1}\chi_{\bigwedge_{j\in J}\lambda_j}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The first identity follows by induction from (FSI3); multiplying out
the finite product proves the second, retaining every subset and
its sign. Meet multiplicativity proves the third.
For \(n=0\), the empty join is \(0_L\) and the empty product is \(1\),
giving \(0=1-1\) and the empty sum. No infinite join or convergence
operation is asserted for a lattice not supplied with such joins.

The global additive receiver can be described exactly. Let
\begin{equation}\tag{FSI5}
V(L)=
\mathbb Z^{(L)}
\Big/
\left\langle
[0_L],\
[\lambda]+[\mu]-[\lambda\vee\mu]-[\lambda\wedge\mu]
:\lambda,\mu\in L
\right\rangle ,
\end{equation}
where \(\mathbb Z^{(L)}\) is the free abelian group on the displayed
symbols. For an abelian group \(A\), every map \(v:L\to A\) with
\(v(0_L)=0\) and
\(v(\lambda)+v(\mu)=v(\lambda\vee\mu)+v(\lambda\wedge\mu)\)
extends uniquely to a homomorphism \(V(L)\to A\).
Indeed the extension from the free group exists uniquely and kills
exactly the displayed relations. This proves the universal
valuation property directly.

There is a well-defined commutative ring structure
\begin{equation}\tag{FSI6}
[\lambda][\mu]=[\lambda\wedge\mu],\qquad 1=[1_L].
\end{equation}
To check its compatibility with the relations, multiply the relation
for \(\lambda,\mu\) by \([\nu]\). Distributivity of the lattice gives
the relation
\[
[\lambda\wedge\nu]+[\mu\wedge\nu]
-[(\lambda\wedge\nu)\vee(\mu\wedge\nu)]
-[(\lambda\wedge\nu)\wedge(\mu\wedge\nu)]=0.
\]
The \([0_L]\) relation also stays zero. Bilinearity proves the
well-defined product. The lattice meet laws prove associativity,
commutativity and its unit, and every \([\lambda]\) is idempotent.
Formula (FSI3) induces a ring homomorphism
\begin{equation}\tag{FSI7}
J:V(L)\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^X,\qquad [\lambda]\longmapsto\chi_\lambda .
\end{equation}
This homomorphism is injective, as the following finite calculation
proves for the whole, possibly infinite, lattice.

Take a finite family \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n\) occurring in a
given expression \(v=\sum_i c_i[\lambda_i]\), and for
\(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) define
\begin{equation}\tag{FSI8}
\begin{gathered}
A_S=\prod_{i\in S}[\lambda_i]\prod_{j\notin S}(1-[\lambda_j]),\\
C_S=\{P:\chi_{\lambda_i}(P)=1\ (i\in S),\
\chi_{\lambda_j}(P)=0\ (j\notin S)\},\\
a_S=\bigwedge_{i\in S}\lambda_i,\qquad
b_S=\bigvee_{j\notin S}\lambda_j,
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
with empty meet \(1_L\) and empty join \(0_L\).
The full signed expansion is
\[
A_S=\sum_{J\subseteq S^c}(-1)^{|J|}
\left[\bigwedge_{i\in S\cup J}\lambda_i\right].
\]
The set \(C_S\) is nonempty exactly when \(a_S\not\le b_S\).
In one direction its point witnesses \(\chi_{a_S}=1,\chi_{b_S}=0\).
In the other, prime separation supplies a prime containing \(b_S\)
and excluding \(a_S\), which has exactly the stipulated pattern.

The valuation relation and (FSI6) give inside \(V(L)\) the same
finite identity
\([\bigvee_j\lambda_j]=1-\prod_j(1-[\lambda_j])\).
Therefore
\(A_S=[a_S](1-[b_S])\).
If \(C_S\) is empty, \(a_S\le b_S\), so
\([a_S][b_S]=[a_S\wedge b_S]=[a_S]\), and \(A_S=0\).
Finally, expanding every factor \([\lambda_i]+(1-[\lambda_i])=1\)
gives
\begin{equation}\tag{FSI9}
1=\sum_S A_S,\qquad
v=\sum_S\left(\sum_{i\in S}c_i\right)A_S,\qquad
J(A_S)=\mathbf1_{C_S}.
\end{equation}
For the middle equality multiply the first by each \([\lambda_i]\);
idempotence makes the result \(A_S\) when \(i\in S\), and zero
otherwise. If \(J(v)=0\), evaluating on every nonempty \(C_S\)
makes its integer coefficient vanish; empty cells already have
\(A_S=0\). Thus \(v=0\), proving injectivity without a finiteness
assumption on \(L\).

Here is the exact image. Let \(\mathcal A_L\) be the Boolean algebra
of subsets of \(X\) generated by
\(U_\lambda=\{P:\lambda\notin P\}\).
Then
\begin{equation}\tag{FSI10}
J(V(L))=
\left\{\sum_{j=1}^m n_j\mathbf1_{E_j}:
m<\infty,\ n_j\in\mathbb Z,\ E_j\in\mathcal A_L\right\}.
\end{equation}
Every \(E\in\mathcal A_L\) is a finite union of cells from a finite
family of the \(U_\lambda\)'s; their indicators are (FSI8), so its
indicator lies in the image. Conversely, every \(\chi_\lambda\)
has this form and every element of \(V(L)\) is a finite integer
combination. This proves both inclusions. Equivalently these are
the integer-valued finite step functions determined by finitely many
of the support tests. No arbitrary function on an infinite
spectrum has been claimed to lie in this image.
For the singleton lattice both sides are the zero ring of functions
on the empty set, so this case is retained as well.

\subsection{The exact map of the entire original split carrier}

For a commutative ring \(R\), retain every original amplitude and label:
\begin{equation}\tag{FSI11}
\begin{gathered}
G_L(R)=\{(0,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}\cup(R\times\{1_L\}),\\
(a,\lambda)+(b,\mu)=(a+b,\lambda\vee\mu),\qquad
(a,\lambda)(b,\mu)=(ab,\lambda\wedge\mu),\\
\tau_L=(0,0_L),\qquad e_L=(0,1_L),\qquad
\mathbf1_{G_L}=(1_R,1_L).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Define the target to be the exact admissible image
\begin{equation}\tag{FSI12}
\mathcal C_L(R)=
\{(a,u)\in R\times\chi(L):a\ne0\Longrightarrow u=\chi_{1_L}\},
\qquad
\Phi(a,\lambda)=(a,\chi_\lambda).
\end{equation}
Injectivity of \(\chi\) proves injectivity of \(\Phi\), and the
definition proves that it is onto \(\mathcal C_L(R)\).
The complete operations, expressed using ordinary integer functions
in the second coordinate, are
\begin{equation}\tag{FSI13}
(a,u)\oplus(b,v)=(a+b,u+v-uv),\qquad
(a,u)\otimes(b,v)=(ab,uv).
\end{equation}
Formula (FSI3) proves both identities
\(\Phi(x+y)=\Phi(x)\oplus\Phi(y)\) and
\(\Phi(xy)=\Phi(x)\otimes\Phi(y)\).

Closure also follows directly with all support restrictions retained.
If \(a+b\ne0\), at least one of \(a,b\) is nonzero, so at least
one of \(u,v\) is the all-one function and \(u+v-uv=1\).
If \(ab\ne0\), both amplitudes are nonzero, so \(u=v=1\) and \(uv=1\).
All other cases have zero amplitude and the support lies in
\(\chi(L)\) by (FSI3).
No integral-domain assumption on \(R\) is used: zero divisors,
nilpotents, and every cancellation \(a+(-a)=0\) are retained.

The additive zero, supported ring zero and unit map respectively to
\begin{equation}\tag{FSI14}
\Phi(\tau_L)=(0,0),\qquad
\Phi(e_L)=(0,1),\qquad
\Phi(\mathbf1_{G_L})=(1_R,1).
\end{equation}
For a trivial lattice the two support functions coincide, as already
specified. For a zero ring all amplitudes are zero and the full
support lattice still survives. These edge cases do not require a
different formula.

This is a semiring isomorphism with the explicitly transported
operations \(\oplus,\otimes\), not a unital ring homomorphism for
ordinary coordinatewise addition in \(R\times\mathbb Z^X\).
The exact discrepancy from that other addition is
\begin{equation}\tag{FSI15}
\Phi(x)+_{\rm coord}\Phi(y)-_{\rm coord}\Phi(x+y)
=(0,\chi_\lambda\chi_\mu)
\quad(x=(a,\lambda),\ y=(b,\mu)).
\end{equation}
For example, the support part of distributivity is verified without
omitting any overlap:
\[
u(v+w-vw)=uv+uw-uvw
=uv+uw-(uv)(uw),
\]
because \(u^2=u\). Its amplitude part is
\(a(b+c)=ab+ac\) in \(R\). Associativity of the support addition
retains
\(u+v+w-uv-uw-vw+uvw\) under either association.
For any \(n\ge1\), the complete finite sum is
\begin{equation}\tag{FSI16}
\Phi\left(\sum_{i=1}^n(a_i,\lambda_i)\right)
=\left(\sum_{i=1}^n a_i,\
\sum_{\varnothing\ne J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}}
(-1)^{|J|+1}\chi_{\bigwedge_{j\in J}\lambda_j}\right).
\end{equation}
Every amplitude summand and every signed support intersection remain.
In particular equal top support does not replace \(a+a\) by \(a\);
the amplitude still adds by the original ring law.

\subsection{Truth actions on intrinsic integer data}

To make the requested truth-to-count comparison exact, let
\[
I=\{(n,\overline n):n\in\mathbb Z,\ \overline n=n\bmod2\}.
\]
It has one integer addition and one multiplication:
\begin{equation}\tag{FSI17}
(n,\overline n)+(m,\overline m)
=(n+m,\overline n+\overline m),\qquad
(n,\overline n)(m,\overline m)=(nm,\overline n\,\overline m).
\end{equation}
Parity is part of each element. It is not a label selecting a
different addition. Thus \(1_{\rm odd}+1_{\rm odd}=2_{\rm even}\).

For all prime coordinates together use the additive group \(I^X\).
Each support function \(u=\chi_\lambda\) defines an endomorphism
\begin{equation}\tag{FSI18}
(M_\lambda f)(P)
=\bigl(u(P)n_P,\ u(P)\overline n_P\bigr),
\quad f(P)=(n_P,\overline n_P).
\end{equation}
The second product is in \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\);
it is exactly the parity of the first integer, so the formula
stays inside \(I^X\). These maps are additive endomorphisms.
Their exact support-action laws in the ordinary endomorphism ring are
\begin{equation}\tag{FSI19}
M_{\lambda\wedge\mu}=M_\lambda M_\mu,\qquad
M_{\lambda\vee\mu}=M_\lambda+M_\mu-M_\lambda M_\mu,\qquad
M_{0_L}=0,\quad M_{1_L}=\operatorname{id}.
\end{equation}
They follow coordinatewise from (FSI3).
Applying them to the constant function \(1_{\rm odd}\) proves that
the whole map \(\lambda\mapsto M_\lambda\) is injective whenever
\(X\ne\varnothing\); when \(X=\varnothing\), both its source and
image are singleton objects. Thus every support label is still
distinguished. The map \(V(L)\to\operatorname{End}(I^X,+)\) obtained
from (FSI7) by multiplication operators is also injective: applying
a coefficient function to the constant \(1_{\rm odd}\) recovers
that entire function and its parity coordinate.

For the two-state truth object write \(\mathbf f,\mathbf t\),
with OR and AND, to keep it distinct from reused programme symbols.
At one coordinate its multiplicative action is
\begin{equation}\tag{FSI20}
\rho(\mathbf f)=0,\qquad \rho(\mathbf t)=\operatorname{id}_I,\qquad
\rho(b\vee c)=\rho(b)+\rho(c)-\rho(b)\rho(c).
\end{equation}
If the user denotes true by \(\tau\), this last \(\tau\) is the
truth-action input \(\mathbf t\). It is not assigned an intrinsic
parity and is not identified with \(1_{\rm odd}\).
It is also not silently identified with the earlier
\(\tau_L=(0,0_L)\) in (FSI11), which has the different explicitly
recorded role of absent support.
For \(b=c=\mathbf t\), the operator equation is
\[
\operatorname{id}_I
=\operatorname{id}_I+\operatorname{id}_I-\operatorname{id}_I.
\]
Evaluating at \(1_{\rm odd}\) gives the exact integer equation
\(1_{\rm odd}=2_{\rm even}-1_{\rm odd}\).
This preserves both truth idempotence and ordinary integer addition
through their proved map, including its overlap term.

The multiplicative truth action is not additive for uncorrected OR:
\begin{equation}\tag{FSI21}
\rho(\mathbf t)+\rho(\mathbf t)-\rho(\mathbf t\vee\mathbf t)
=\operatorname{id}_I .
\end{equation}
Dropping the last term in (FSI20) would therefore be a different,
false compatibility claim. Occurrence counts remain visible:
\(k\) copies of \(\operatorname{id}_I\) act on \(1_{\rm odd}\)
as \((k,k\bmod2)\), not as \(1_{\rm odd}\).
For every finite support family their corrected union action is
\begin{equation}\tag{FSI22}
M_{\bigvee_{i=1}^n\lambda_i}
=\sum_{\varnothing\ne J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}}
(-1)^{|J|+1}\prod_{j\in J}M_{\lambda_j}.
\end{equation}
This follows by applying (FSI4) to multiplication operators.
All multiplicities and all signed intersections have explicit
receivers; none are replaced by a choice of parity or by one
selected Boolean branch.

The construction maps the entire original support lattice and the
entire admissible split carrier. It establishes the precise valuation
and operator formulas needed to compare idempotent truth with integer
counts. It makes no cohomology, analytic trace, purity or positivity
identification without the additional objects and maps those statements
would require.
