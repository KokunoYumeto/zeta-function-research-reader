 S=G_L(R)=\{(0_R,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}

\subsection{The heat matrix, its exact composition and its independent labels}

For $x=(t,\lambda)\in S$ define $x^\dagger=(-t,\lambda)$ and
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL2}
 U_x(i,j)=
 \begin{cases}
  1_S,&j=i,\\
  \left(\displaystyle\frac{(-t)^m j!}{m!i!},\lambda\right),
       &j-i=2m>0,\\
  \tau,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
 \qquad(0\le i,j\le d).
\end{equation}
These are elements of the original $S$: if $\lambda\ne1_L$
then $t=0_R$, so every displayed off-diagonal amplitude is zero.
Let $D_d(i,j)=(j,1_L)$ when $j=i+1$, and $\tau$ otherwise.
Multiplying along the unique possible index path proves that
$D_d^n(i,j)=(j!/i!,1_L)$ for $j-i=n$, and is $\tau$ otherwise.
Also $D_d^{d+1}=0$. Thus (HSL2) is the actual finite semiring
exponential
\[
 U_x=I+\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}
       (1/m!,1_L)(x^\dagger)^mD_d^{2m}.
\]
The integer factorials, the factor $1/m!$, the sign and the label
are all retained. In this expression the empty higher terms are
absent; supported zero is not substituted for the additive zero.

The exact time law is
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL3}
 U_xU_y=U_{x+y},\qquad U_\tau=I,
 \qquad x=(t,\lambda),\quad y=(s,\mu).
\end{equation}
Here is a direct proof, including entries whose amplitudes cancel.
For $j-i=2n>0$, the amplitude in the product entry is
\[
 \sum_{m=0}^{n}
 \frac{(-t)^m(i+2m)!}{m!i!}
 \frac{(-s)^{n-m}j!}{(n-m)!(i+2m)!}
 =
 \frac{j!}{i!}\sum_{m=0}^{n}
       \frac{(-t)^m(-s)^{n-m}}{m!(n-m)!}
 =
 \frac{(-(t+s))^n j!}{n!i!}.
\]
The endpoint summands have labels $\mu$ and $\lambda$,
respectively. Every intermediate summand has label
$\lambda\wedge\mu$. Their joined label is therefore exactly
$\lambda\vee\mu$. This conclusion does not depend on whether
any amplitude vanishes. The diagonal has only the identity
summand. An entry below the diagonal or of different parity
has no nonzero-support path and is $\tau$.
This proves every entry of (HSL3).

Set
\[
 H_i=\{j:i<j\le d,\ j-i\text{ is even}\},\qquad
 T_i(\nu)=\bigvee_{j\in H_i}\nu_j,
\]
with an empty join equal to $0_L$. Let $E_t$ be the ordinary
ring heat matrix, with the same factorial amplitude as (HSL2)
also for $m=0$, and ring zero elsewhere. Direct matrix action gives
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL4}
 \begin{gathered}
 U_x(a,\nu)=(E_ta,F_\lambda\nu),\\
 (E_ta)_i=\sum_{\substack{j\ge i\\j-i=2m}}
                 \frac{(-t)^m j!}{m!i!}a_j,\qquad
 (F_\lambda\nu)_i=\nu_i\vee(\lambda\wedge T_i(\nu)).
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The diagonal contributes precisely $\nu_i$; each higher
same-parity coefficient contributes $\lambda\wedge\nu_j$.
Distributivity gives the second formula. This proves the action
on independent labels, including labelled zeros of the input.

The exact invariant used throughout the support calculation is
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL5}
 T_i(F_\lambda\nu)=T_i(\nu),\qquad
 F_\lambda F_\mu=F_{\lambda\vee\mu},\qquad
 F_\lambda^2=F_\lambda.
\end{equation}
For the first equality, $F_\lambda\nu\ge\nu$ gives one
inequality. If $j\in H_i$, both $\nu_j$ and
$\lambda\wedge T_j(\nu)$ are at most $T_i(\nu)$, since
$H_j\subset H_i$. Taking their joins proves the other inequality.
Consequently
$F_\lambda(F_\mu\nu)_i=
\nu_i\vee(\mu\wedge T_i(\nu))\vee(\lambda\wedge T_i(\nu))$;
distributivity proves the remaining assertions.

Every supported-zero time is therefore an idempotent:
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL6}
 P_\lambda:=U_{z_\lambda},\quad
 P_\lambda(a,\nu)=(a,F_\lambda\nu),\quad
 P_\lambda^2=P_\lambda,\quad
 U_xU_{x^\dagger}=U_{x^\dagger}U_x=P_\lambda,\quad
 P_\lambda U_x=U_xP_\lambda=U_x.
\end{equation}
The matrix identities follow either from (HSL3) or from the
explicit formulas. The associated image is
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL7}
 \begin{gathered}
 \operatorname{im}U_x=\operatorname{im}P_\lambda
 =:\mathcal H_{\lambda,d}\\
 =\{(b,\eta)\in M_d:
       \lambda\wedge T_i(\eta)\le\eta_i\text{ for every }i\}\\
 =\{(b,\eta)\in M_d:
       \lambda\wedge\eta_{i+2}\le\eta_i
                         \text{ whenever }i+2\le d\}.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
An idempotent has image equal to its fixed set; (HSL4) gives
the first description. The full-tail inequalities imply the
adjacent ones. Conversely the adjacent inequalities, iterated
along the parity chain after meeting with $\lambda$, imply
$\lambda\wedge\eta_j\le\eta_i$ for every $j\in H_i$.
Finite distributivity then gives the full-tail inequalities.
Finally $U_x=P_\lambda U_x$ and
$P_\lambda=U_xU_{x^\dagger}$ prove equality of the two images.

The complete finite fibres are explicit and require no choice
of a least label. For a proposed target $(b,\eta)$ set
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL8}
 \delta_i(\eta)=\lambda\wedge T_i(\eta),\qquad
 a_i=(E_{-t}b)_i
      =\sum_{\substack{j\ge i\\j-i=2m}}
                   \frac{t^m j!}{m!i!}b_j .
\end{equation}
The fibre is empty if the target is outside $\mathcal H_{\lambda,d}$.
For a target in that image it is exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL9}
 U_x^{-1}(b,\eta)=
 \left\{((a_i,\nu_i))_{i=0}^{d}:
 \ \nu_i\vee\delta_i(\eta)=\eta_i\ \text{for every }i,\quad
 a_i\ne0_R\Longrightarrow\nu_i=1_L\right\}.
\end{equation}
In particular the amplitude vector is unique and the permitted
label choices in this formula are independent between coordinates.
For necessity, $E_{-t}E_t=I$ follows from the amplitude computation
in (HSL3), and tail invariance (HSL5) gives the label equations.
For sufficiency, suppose those label equations hold.
Descending induction along either parity gives
$T_i(\nu)=T_i(\eta)$. At a terminal index both tails are empty.
If $k=i+2\le d$ and the equality is known at $k$, then
\[
 \begin{split}
 T_i(\nu)
 &=\nu_k\vee T_k(\nu)
  =\nu_k\vee T_k(\eta)\\
 &=(\nu_k\vee\delta_k(\eta))\vee T_k(\eta)
  =\eta_k\vee T_k(\eta)=T_i(\eta),
 \end{split}
\]
because $\delta_k(\eta)\le T_k(\eta)$.
Thus $F_\lambda\nu=\eta$, proving the converse. The carrier
condition in (HSL9) is exactly the remaining requirement.
The fibre is nonempty: $U_{x^\dagger}(b,\eta)=(a,\eta)$ is
a preimage by (HSL6). This also proves that $\eta_i=1_L$
whenever the recovered $a_i$ is nonzero.

\subsection{The flag retract and genuine heat automorphisms on it}

Write $U_t=U_{(t,1_L)}$ and $P=U_e$. Then
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL10}
 \begin{gathered}
 U_tU_{-t}=P,\qquad
 \mathcal H_{1,d}=\operatorname{im}P
       =\{(a,\nu):\nu_i\ge\nu_{i+2}\text{ when }i+2\le d\},\\
 U_t(a,\nu)=(E_ta,\nu)\quad\text{on }\mathcal H_{1,d},\qquad
 U_t|_{\mathcal H_{1,d}}\in
         \operatorname{Aut}_S(\mathcal H_{1,d}),\\
 (U_t|_{\mathcal H_{1,d}})^{-1}=U_{-t}|_{\mathcal H_{1,d}}.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
This is the pair of exact parity flags
\[
 \nu_0\ge\nu_2\ge\nu_4\ge\cdots,\qquad
 \nu_1\ge\nu_3\ge\nu_5\ge\cdots.
\]
The identity of the restricted group is $P$ restricted to its
image. The identity on the entire coefficient module is instead
$U_\tau=I$. Both statements follow from (HSL6) and the defining
fixed-set condition.

The image of every $P_\lambda$ is a finitely generated projective
$S$-semimodule in the lifting sense for surjective module maps.
Indeed it is a retract of the free module $M_d$, with inclusion
and retraction $P_\lambda$. Explicitly, if $A\to B$ is surjective
and $f:\mathcal H_{\lambda,d}\to B$ is linear, choose a preimage
in $A$ of each $f(P_\lambda\epsilon_i)$, where the $\epsilon_i$
are the free coordinate vectors. Extend those choices linearly
to $M_d$ and restrict to the retract. The composite to $B$ is
$f$, since $P_\lambda$ acts identically on the retract. The
vectors $P_\lambda\epsilon_i$ generate it, proving finite
generation as well as the asserted lifting property.

For $d\ge2$, the representation $x\mapsto U_x$ is injective:
the $(0,2)$ entry is $(-2t,\lambda)$, so its amplitude divided
by $-2$ recovers $t$ and its label recovers $\lambda$.
Moreover the only globally invertible operator in this family
on all of $M_d$ is $U_\tau$.
If $\lambda\ne0_L$, take two zero-amplitude vectors whose
index $2$ label is $\lambda$ and whose other labels are $0_L$,
except that their index $0$ labels are respectively $0_L$
and $\lambda$. They are distinct and have the same image under
$U_x$, by (HSL4). Thus $U_x$ is not injective. If $\lambda=0_L$,
the time carrier forces $t=0_R$ and $U_x=I$.
For $d=0,1$ every matrix in (HSL2) is $I$, and those two
qualifications about injectivity and units are not asserted.

For $L=\mathbb B$, the zero-amplitude part of the flag retract
has exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL11}
 (n_{\mathrm{even}}+1)(n_{\mathrm{odd}}+1)
 \quad\text{states},\qquad
 n_{\mathrm{even}}=\lfloor d/2\rfloor+1,\quad
 n_{\mathrm{odd}}=\lceil d/2\rceil .
\end{equation}
A decreasing Boolean chain of length $n$ consists of an initial
string of ones and a remaining string of zeros, giving $n+1$
choices; the two parity chains are independent. At $d=2$ this
gives six states. This Boolean-semimodule retract is not free:
a finite free Boolean semimodule has $2^r$ states for an integer
$r\ge0$, and an infinite free one has infinitely many states.
The flag module has a concrete nonfree geometry even though it
is a retract of a free coefficient module.

The automorphisms proved here are linear heat automorphisms of
a coefficient semimodule. They are not multiplicative
automorphisms of polynomial algebras. Already on amplitudes
$E_t(z)=z$ but $E_t(z^2)=z^2-2t$. Moreover for $d\ge2$ the
ordinarily labelled monomial $z$ lies in $\mathcal H_{1,d}$,
whereas $z^2$ does not: its index $0$ label is $0_L$ and its
index $2$ label is $1_L$. Thus closure of this retract under
polynomial multiplication has not been assumed.

\subsection{Exact amplitude, joint-coefficient and localization morphisms}

For the tropical semifield $K$ with zero, put
$K_L=K\otimes_{\mathbb B}L$ and
$\iota_L(\lambda)=1_K\otimes\lambda$.
The map $\iota_L$ is injective: the nonzero indicator
$\beta:K\to\mathbb B$ preserves maximum and product, and
$\beta\otimes\operatorname{id}_L$ is a retraction to $L$.
Thus the original joint map
$\Phi_R(r,\lambda)=(r,\iota_L(\lambda))$ is injective.
Apply it entrywise to (HSL2). It gives exactly the pair
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL12}
 \begin{gathered}
 \Phi_R(U_x)=(E_t,\widetilde B_\lambda),\\
 \widetilde B_\lambda(i,j)=
 \begin{cases}
  1_{K_L},&i=j,\\
  \iota_L(\lambda),&j>i,\ j-i\text{ even},\\
  0_{K_L},&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}\\
 q^{d+1}U_x=E_tq^{d+1},\qquad
 \Phi_R^{d+1}U_x=(E_t,\widetilde B_\lambda)\Phi_R^{d+1}.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The last identities follow by entrywise application of semiring
homomorphisms to finite matrix multiplication; they also follow
directly from (HSL4). They retain the full original amplitude
vector and all its independent support labels.

Supported-zero coefficient localization is
$S[e^{-1}]\simeq L$, $(r,\nu)\mapsto\nu$:
inverting the idempotent $e$ sets it equal to the new unit,
and $e(r,\nu)=z_\nu$ gives precisely that quotient.
Consequently the matrix specializes to
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL13}
 \begin{gathered}
 B_\lambda(i,j)=
 \begin{cases}
  1_L,&i=j,\\
  \lambda,&j>i,\ j-i\text{ even},\\
  0_L,&\text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}\\
 B_\lambda B_\mu=B_{\lambda\vee\mu},\qquad
 B_\lambda^2=B_\lambda,\qquad
 \sigma^{d+1}U_x=F_\lambda\sigma^{d+1}.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The composition proof is the support part of (HSL3), or (HSL5).
For top-labelled time every $U_t$ has the same specialized
matrix $B_{1_L}$, which is not the global identity for $d\ge2$.
For example at $d=2$ it is
\[
 B_{1_L}=
 \begin{pmatrix}1_L&0_L&1_L\\0_L&1_L&0_L\\0_L&0_L&1_L\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 B_{1_L}(0_L,0_L,1_L)=B_{1_L}(1_L,0_L,1_L).
\]
Thus making the coefficient $e$ invertible does not make the
heat matrix $U_e$ invertible on the whole coefficient module.
It makes that matrix the displayed support projection.

For any $S$-semimodule $M$, localization at $e$ is the corner
$eM$, with scalar action through $eS\simeq L$:
the map is $v\mapsto ev$. To check the universal property,
any linear map into a module in which $e$ acts invertibly
has $e$ acting as the identity, since $e^2=e$.
It therefore factors uniquely through $eM$.
Applied to the free module and the flag retract this proves
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL14}
 \begin{gathered}
 M_d[e^{-1}]\simeq L^{d+1},\\
 \mathcal H_{1,d}[e^{-1}]
   \simeq\{(\nu_i)\in L^{d+1}:\nu_i\ge\nu_{i+2}\},\\
 U_t\longmapsto B_{1_L},\qquad
 U_t|_{\mathcal H_{1,d}}\longmapsto
       \operatorname{id}_{\mathcal H_{1,d}[e^{-1}]} .
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
All these statements retain the original labels; zero-amplitude
vectors are the actual corners, rather than an imposed Boolean
replacement.

\subsection{An analytic completion on which every heat sum converges}

Finite polynomial truncation is not a substitute for an infinite
heat action. We first construct its amplitude completion exactly.
For a coefficient sequence $a=(a_n)_{n\ge0}$ set
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL15}
 \|a\|_h=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_n|\sqrt{n!}\,h^n,\qquad
 \mathcal E=\{a:\|a\|_h<\infty\text{ for every }h>0\}.
\end{equation}
The positive integer $h$ already give a defining countable
family of seminorms. Each is a weighted $\ell^1$ norm.
A Cauchy sequence for all these norms has coordinate limits;
completeness of every weighted $\ell^1$ space gives the same
limit in each of them. Thus $\mathcal E$ is complete.
Polynomial truncations converge in every norm, because the
corresponding positive summable tail tends to zero.
This constructs $\mathcal E$ as a Fréchet completion of the
polynomial amplitudes with this specified topology.

The corresponding entire-function description is exact:
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL16}
 \begin{gathered}
 f(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nz^n,\qquad
 \mathcal E=\left\{f\text{ entire}:
   p_\alpha(f):=\sup_{z\in\mathbb C}
                |f(z)|e^{-\alpha|z|^2}<\infty
                \text{ for every }\alpha>0\right\},\\
 p_{1/(2h^2)}(f)\le\|a\|_h,\qquad
 \|a\|_h\le
       \frac{p_\alpha(f)}{1-h\sqrt{2\mathrm e\alpha}}
       \quad\left(h\sqrt{2\mathrm e\alpha}<1\right).
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The symbol $\mathrm e$ here is the ordinary real exponential
constant, not the supported-zero element $e$.
For the first estimate, each
$|z|^n/(h^n\sqrt{n!})$ is at most
$\exp(|z|^2/(2h^2))$, since its square is a term in that
exponential series. Summing against the weighted coefficients
proves uniform convergence on compact sets and the first bound.
Conversely Cauchy's coefficient estimate on a circle of radius
$r$ gives $|a_n|\le p_\alpha(f)e^{\alpha r^2}r^{-n}$.
For $n\ge1$ put $r=\sqrt{n/(2\alpha)}$ and use
$n!\le n^n$ to obtain
$|a_n|\sqrt{n!}h^n\le
p_\alpha(f)(h\sqrt{2\mathrm e\alpha})^n$.
The $n=0$ estimate is $|a_0|\le p_\alpha(f)$.
The geometric sum proves the second bound and the equivalence.

For every complex time $t$ the following heat sum is defined
on all of $\mathcal E$:
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL17}
 (T_ta)_i=
 \sum_{m=0}^{\infty}
       \frac{(-t)^m(i+2m)!}{m!i!}\,a_{i+2m},\qquad
 \|T_ta\|_h\le\|a\|_{h+\sqrt{2|t|}}.
\end{equation}
In particular no formal interchange of divergent power series
is being used. To prove the bound, take absolute values before
any interchange and use nonnegative summation:
\[
 \begin{split}
 &\sum_{i,m\ge0}
  |a_{i+2m}|\frac{|t|^m(i+2m)!}{m!i!}\sqrt{i!}h^i\\
 &=\sum_{n\ge0}|a_n|\sqrt{n!}
     \sum_{m=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
       \frac{\sqrt{n!}}{m!\sqrt{(n-2m)!}}\,
                    |t|^m h^{n-2m}.
 \end{split}
\]
The inner coefficient is
$\sqrt{\binom n{2m}}\sqrt{\binom{2m}m}$, at most
$\binom n{2m}2^m$: the first binomial coefficient is a
positive integer, and $\binom{2m}m\le2^{2m}$.
Thus the inner sum is bounded by the even part of
$(h+\sqrt{2|t|})^n$, and hence by the full expression.
This proves absolute convergence and the literal estimate in
(HSL17).

The resulting operators satisfy
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL18}
 T_tT_s=T_{t+s},\qquad T_0=I,\qquad
 T_t^{-1}=T_{-t},\qquad
 \frac{\partial}{\partial t}T_tf=-\partial_z^2T_tf.
\end{equation}
For composition, the absolute double heat sum is bounded in
$\|\cdot\|_h$ by
$\|a\|_{h+\sqrt{2|t|}+\sqrt{2|s|}}$.
It can therefore be regrouped; for each final total derivative
order the finite binomial calculation in (HSL3) gives $t+s$.
For time differentiability, (HSL17) applied with $|t|\le A$
bounds the sum of norms of the power-series terms uniformly
on every compact time disc. Applying the same bound on a
larger disc justifies termwise differentiation there. The
power series is
$\sum_{m\ge0}(-t)^m\partial_z^{2m}f/m!$.
Differentiating it gives the displayed heat equation, with
the same locally uniform convergence in $z$ by (HSL16).
Thus the analytic amplitude flow is an everywhere-defined
complex-time group on this completion.

\subsection{The exact support completion and the original-lattice domain}

An arbitrary $L$ need not admit the countable joins occurring
in an infinite heat row. We therefore specify, rather than
silently assume, the support completion. Let
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL19}
 \widehat L=\operatorname{Idl}(L),\qquad
 \jmath(\lambda)=\downarrow\lambda,\qquad
 I\wedge J=I\cap J,\quad
 \bigvee_\alpha I_\alpha
      =\text{the ideal generated by }\bigcup_\alpha I_\alpha .
\end{equation}
The ideals are nonempty downward-closed subsets closed under
finite joins. The bottom is $\{0_L\}$ and the top is $L$.
The map $\jmath$ is an injective bounded lattice homomorphism:
finite joins of principal ideals give the principal ideal
of their join, and intersections give the principal ideal
of their meet. The displayed arbitrary join exists explicitly:
an element belongs to it exactly when it is below a finite
join of elements drawn from the indicated ideals.

Finite meets distribute over arbitrary joins in $\widehat L$.
For example if $a\in I\cap\bigvee_\alpha J_\alpha$, choose
$b_1,\ldots,b_r$ from the indicated $J_\alpha$ with
$a\le\bigvee b_k$. Then
$a=\bigvee_k(a\wedge b_k)$, and each $a\wedge b_k$ lies in
$I\cap J_{\alpha_k}$. This proves one inclusion of
$I\cap\bigvee J_\alpha=\bigvee(I\cap J_\alpha)$;
the opposite inclusion follows from containment.
This proves the exact infinite distributivity needed below.
It does not identify an infinite join in the original $L$
with one in $\widehat L$ without checking that it is principal.

Define the completed, independently labelled coefficient module
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL20}
 \widehat{\mathcal M}
 =\{(a,(I_i)_{i\ge0}):a\in\mathcal E,\ I_i\in\widehat L,\quad
                         a_i\ne0\Longrightarrow I_i=L\}.
\end{equation}
Addition has amplitude addition and coordinate ideal joins;
the original scalar $(r,\lambda)\in S=G_L(\mathbb C)$ acts
by amplitudes $ra_i$ and ideals $\jmath(\lambda)\cap I_i$.
These formulas preserve the carrier: a nonzero amplitude
after addition has a nonzero contributing summand, and a
nonzero amplitude after scalar multiplication has both factors
nonzero and hence both relevant labels top.
The module laws follow from (HSL19).
Every original finite vector embeds by principal coefficient
ideals and by $\{0_L\}$ above its degree.

The completed heat action of the original time parameter
$x=(t,\lambda)\in S$ is
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL21}
 \widehat U_x(a,I)=(T_ta,\widehat F_\lambda I),\qquad
 (\widehat F_\lambda I)_i
    =I_i\vee\left(\jmath(\lambda)\cap
                   \bigvee_{\substack{j>i\\j-i\text{ even}}}I_j\right).
\end{equation}
The amplitude sum converges by (HSL17), and the support join
exists by (HSL19). Admissibility also holds. If $t=0$, the
amplitude at $i$ remains $a_i$, whose nonvanishing already
forces $I_i=L$. If $t\ne0$, then $\lambda=1_L$; a nonzero
output sum has at least one nonzero summand, whose input
amplitude has top support. Its contribution therefore makes
the output support top. This proves that the formula defines
an actual map of the stated carrier.

With $\widehat T_i(I)=\bigvee_{j>i,\ j-i\ {\rm even}}I_j$,
the proof of (HSL5) uses only nested tails and distributivity.
It therefore gives exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL22}
 \begin{gathered}
 \widehat T_i(\widehat F_\lambda I)=\widehat T_i(I),\qquad
 \widehat F_\lambda\widehat F_\mu
                      =\widehat F_{\lambda\vee\mu},\\
 \widehat U_x\widehat U_y=\widehat U_{x+y},\qquad
 \widehat U_\tau=I,\qquad
 \widehat U_x\widehat U_{x^\dagger}
       =\widehat U_{z_\lambda}=:\widehat P_\lambda .
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The amplitude assertion follows from (HSL18). Addition and
scalar linearity follow from join preservation and the
distributivity proved in (HSL19), together with linearity of
$T_t$. In particular the completed supported-zero operator
is again an idempotent with unchanged amplitudes, and every
$\widehat U_x$ is an automorphism on its exact image
$\operatorname{im}\widehat P_\lambda$.

The complete infinite image and fibres can also be specified
without inventing a terminal coordinate at infinity:
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL23}
 \operatorname{im}\widehat U_x
 =\{(b,J)\in\widehat{\mathcal M}:
       \jmath(\lambda)\cap J_j\subset J_i
           \text{ for all }j>i\text{ of the same parity}\}.
\end{equation}
For a target in this set, put $a=T_{-t}b$.
Its fibre consists exactly of ideals $I_i$ satisfying
$I_i\subset J_i$, $a_i\ne0\Rightarrow I_i=L$, and the following
finite-witness generation condition for every $i$:
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL24}
 \begin{gathered}
 \text{for every }c\in J_i\text{ there are }v_i\in I_i,\quad
 j_1,\ldots,j_r>i\text{ of the same parity},\quad
 c_k\in I_{j_k}\text{ such that}\\
 c\le v_i\vee\bigvee_{k=1}^{r}(\lambda\wedge c_k).
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
An empty list is permitted. These conditions are necessary
and sufficient for (HSL21) to have target $J$: by (HSL19)
its right side is exactly the ideal generated by $I_i$ and
the elements $\lambda\wedge c$ with $c$ from a higher
same-parity $I_j$. All those generators lie in $J_i$
by (HSL23), and (HSL24) is the reverse containment.
The amplitude is unique by (HSL18); existence follows also
from the preimage $\widehat U_{x^\dagger}(b,J)$.
This proves the full infinite fibre, including its boundary
condition on arbitrarily high degrees.

The finite independent-coordinate formula (HSL9) must not be
extended without this condition. For example let $\lambda=1_L$,
put every $I_i=\{0_L\}$, and let $J_i=L$ at every even index
and $\{0_L\}$ at every odd index. The equations
$I_i\vee(\jmath(\lambda)\cap J_{i+2})=J_i$ all hold,
but $\widehat F_\lambda I$ is zero in every coordinate and
is not $J$. This exhibits the missing information at infinity
if the terminating induction used for finite fibres is omitted.

There is an invariant domain entirely within the original
lattice. Define
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL25}
 \begin{gathered}
 \mathcal M_L^{\mathrm{tail}}
 =\{(a,\nu):a\in\mathcal E,\ \nu_i\in L,\
       a_i\ne0\Rightarrow\nu_i=1_L,\
       \mathcal T_i(\nu)\text{ is principal for every }i\},\\
 \mathcal T_i(\nu)=
      \bigvee_{\substack{j\ge i\\j-i\text{ even}}}
                      \downarrow\nu_j
       =\downarrow s_i\quad(s_i\in L).
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The generator $s_i$ is unique. Being principal here is the
precise finite-generation assertion: $s_i$ is a finite join
of labels from that tail. Indeed $s_i$ belongs to the
generated ideal and hence is below such a finite join;
all those labels are at most $s_i$, giving equality.
Every finite polynomial belongs to this domain.
For finite $L$, every label sequence satisfies this condition;
no finiteness of $L$ is required for the definition.

On this domain the completed formula has values in the
original $L$ and reads
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL26}
 (U_x(a,\nu))_i
  =\left((T_ta)_i,\ \nu_i\vee(\lambda\wedge s_{i+2})\right),
 \qquad
 \mathcal T_i(F_\lambda\nu)=\mathcal T_i(\nu).
\end{equation}
The ideal $\mathcal T_{i+2}$ is the strict tail in (HSL21).
Distributivity and principality identify its meet with
$\downarrow\lambda$ and its join with $\downarrow\nu_i$
as the principal ideal of the displayed label.
The invariant equality follows as in (HSL22), now with the
inclusive tail: the output contains each input label,
and it imports labels only from that same inclusive tail.
Hence the new tails have the same principal generators,
proving invariance of the entire domain.
It is an $S$-subsemimodule: tails of a sum are joins of
two principal tail ideals, and tails after scalar support
multiplication are their meets with a principal ideal.
This establishes a heat action preserving the full original
lattice on an explicit infinite domain containing all
polynomials and the theta family considered below.

\subsection{Polynomial approximation, exact truncation defect and the theta lift}

Let $\iota_d:M_d\to\widehat{\mathcal M}$ extend a polynomial
by amplitude zero and label $0_L$ above degree $d$.
Then
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL27}
 \widehat U_x\iota_d=\iota_d U_x .
\end{equation}
The sums in (HSL21) terminate on this image, proving the
identity directly. In contrast let $\pi_d$ retain only
coordinates $0,\ldots,d$ of an infinite object. For $i\le d$,
its exact amplitude defect is
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL28}
 \begin{split}
 &\left(q\pi_d\widehat U_x(a,I)
           -q U_x\pi_d(a,I)\right)_i\\
 &\hspace{8mm}=
   \sum_{\substack{j>d\\j-i=2m>0}}
             \frac{(-t)^m j!}{m!i!}a_j .
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Here $U_x$ on the truncated completed module means the same
finite formula with ideals as labels. The series is
absolutely convergent by (HSL17). The support in the first
expression is the support in the second joined with exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL29}
 \jmath(\lambda)\cap
       \bigvee_{\substack{j>d\\j-i\text{ even}}}I_j .
\end{equation}
Both identities follow by splitting the row at $j=d$.
They give a connecting map for the entire discarded tail,
rather than treating truncation as an intertwiner.
For the amplitude polynomial $f(z)=z^{d+2}$ the truncated
input is zero, whereas the degree-$d$ coefficient after
heat is $-t(d+2)(d+1)$. Thus (HSL28) can be nonzero even
on polynomial inputs when the chosen truncation is too low.

For a general element of $\widehat{\mathcal M}$, amplitude
polynomial truncations converge in every norm (HSL15).
Their heat images converge in every such norm by (HSL17).
For each fixed coordinate their heat supports form an
increasing family of ideals whose join is exactly (HSL21),
by the explicit definition of joins in (HSL19).
Thus the analytic limit and the algebraic support limit
are both specified, with no unsupported infinite join in $L$.
Any ideal label can itself be obtained as the directed join
of the principal ideals of its elements, so the ideal
extension preserves the original labels by an explicit
injective map and a defined directed completion.

The actual theta family uses the kernel of Brad Rodgers and
Terence Tao, \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.05914v5}{\emph{The
De Bruijn--Newman constant is non-negative}, arXiv:1801.05914v5}.
It provides a particularly exact original-lattice case.
Its independently proved kernel
calculation has coefficients
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL30}
 \begin{gathered}
 H_t(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_n(t)z^n,\qquad a_{2j+1}(t)=0,\\
 a_{2j}(t)=
 \frac{(-1)^j}{(2j)!}
       \int_0^\infty e^{tu^2}\Phi_{\mathrm{theta}}(u)u^{2j}\,du,\\
 (-1)^j a_{2j}(t)>0\quad(t\in\mathbb R),\qquad H_t\in\mathcal E.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The symbol $\Phi_{\mathrm{theta}}$ denotes the analytic
theta kernel, not the coefficient map $\Phi_R$.
The full kernel proof and its human-source locators are

Equation (HSL30) records the exact input used here.
The following support and map calculation is independent

Retain the coefficient lift obtained from these real-time
nonzero coefficients and its complex-time continuation:
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL31}
 \mathbf H_t=
     ((a_i(t),\nu_i))_{i\ge0},\qquad

\subsection{The completed joint coefficient morphism for all support sequences}

The exact joint morphism on the general completed module
also exists, once its target is specified to admit the
required joins. It is not necessary to assume that tensoring
with $K$ preserves every infinite join of $L$.
For any commutative additively idempotent semiring $A$, let
$\operatorname{Idl}_{\mathrm{ord}}(A)$ be the nonempty lower
subsets for the order $a\le b\Longleftrightarrow a+b=b$
which are closed under finite sums. Define addition and
arbitrary joins by the lower ideal generated by the union,
and multiplication by the lower ideal generated by the
products of elements. The zero is $\downarrow0_A$ and the
unit is $\downarrow1_A$; the latter need not be the top ideal.
These operations form a complete idempotent semiring in
which multiplication distributes over arbitrary joins.
Indeed both sides of that distributivity statement are
the lower ideal generated by the same products; finite
distributivity in $A$ supplies equality of their finite
generating sums. The same generator argument proves
associativity and commutativity of multiplication.
The principal-ideal map $a\mapsto\downarrow a$ is an
injective unital semiring homomorphism.

Apply this construction to $K_L$, and put
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL33}
 \begin{gathered}
 \mathfrak K_L=\operatorname{Idl}_{\mathrm{ord}}(K_L),\\
 \iota^\#:\widehat L\longrightarrow\mathfrak K_L,\qquad
 \iota^\#(I)=
    \{w\in K_L:\exists a\in I,\ w\le\iota_L(a)\},\\
 \rho^\#:\mathfrak K_L\longrightarrow\widehat L,\qquad
 \rho^\#(J)=
    \{\lambda\in L:\exists w\in J,\ \lambda\le\rho_L(w)\},
       \qquad \rho^\#\iota^\#=\operatorname{id}_{\widehat L}.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Here $\rho_L=\beta\otimes\operatorname{id}_L$ is the
retraction used before (HSL12). The sets displayed in
(HSL33) are already ideals: finite sums in the target
are bounded by the image of the finite join or sum of
their witnesses. A unital semiring homomorphism between
idempotent semirings induces this lower-ideal map, and
the generator description proves that it preserves
arbitrary joins, products, zero and the stated units.
For $L$ viewed as a semiring, ideal multiplication is
intersection: the products are meets, and any element
of an intersection is its own meet. Thus the domain
really is precisely (HSL19).
The two induced maps compose to the identity because
$\rho_L\iota_L=\operatorname{id}_L$; explicitly,
$w\le\iota_L(a)$ implies $\rho_L(w)\le a$, and every
$a\in I$ is obtained by $w=\iota_L(a)$.
This proves injectivity of $\iota^\#$ as well as its
arbitrary-join compatibility.

The completed coefficient map and its restriction to
every original coefficient are therefore
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL34}
 \begin{gathered}
 \widehat\Phi:G_{\widehat L}(\mathbb C)
       \longrightarrow\mathbb C\times\mathfrak K_L,\qquad
       (a,I)\longmapsto(a,\iota^\#(I)),\\
 \widehat\Phi(a,\downarrow\nu)
       =(a,\downarrow\iota_L(\nu)).
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Both coordinates preserve zero, unit and the two operations;
the map is injective by the retraction in (HSL33).
The second formula proves the exact commuting square
with the original $\Phi_{\mathbb C}$ and the principal-ideal
embedding $K_L\to\mathfrak K_L$. The top ideal $L$ of
$\widehat L$ maps to $\downarrow1_{K_L}$, the multiplicative
identity of $\mathfrak K_L$, as required by the carrier.

Define the infinite support matrix $\mathfrak B_\lambda$
over $\mathfrak K_L$ to have diagonal $\downarrow1_{K_L}$,
strict same-parity upper entries
$\downarrow\iota_L(\lambda)$, and zero entries elsewhere.
Its action uses the existing arbitrary joins in
$\mathfrak K_L$. Let $\widehat\Phi^{\mathbb N}$ apply
(HSL34) to every coefficient. Then the exact completed
joint intertwiner is
\begin{equation}\tag{HSL35}
 \widehat\Phi^{\mathbb N}\widehat U_{(t,\lambda)}
       =(T_t,\mathfrak B_\lambda)
                         \widehat\Phi^{\mathbb N}.
\end{equation}
The amplitude equality is (HSL17). For the support equality,
apply the arbitrary-join and finite-product preserving
map $\iota^\#$ to (HSL21); its value is precisely the
row action of the stated support matrix. This proves
(HSL35) for every completed independent support sequence,
not only for the actual theta vector.
The theta calculation (HSL31)--(HSL32) descends to the
original coefficient target $K_L$ because its relevant
joins stabilize, as already proved there.
Thus the general completion and the original-family
map are connected by an explicit injective morphism,
with exact convergence on both coordinates.
