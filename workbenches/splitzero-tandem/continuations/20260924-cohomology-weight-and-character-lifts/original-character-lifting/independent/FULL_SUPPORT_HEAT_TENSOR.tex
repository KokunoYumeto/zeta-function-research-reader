\subsection{The actual completed tensor space and its diagonal map}

Fix an integer $k\ge2$. Retain the original Taylor coefficients
and norms
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK1}
 \mathcal E=\left\{f(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nz^n:
  \|f\|_h=\sum_{n\ge0}|a_n|\sqrt{n!}\,h^n<\infty
                 \text{ for every }h>0\right\}.
\end{equation}
For a vector $\mathbf h=(h_1,\ldots,h_k)$ of positive radii,
define the multivariate coefficient space by
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK2}
\begin{gathered}
 F(\mathbf z)=\sum_{\mathbf n\in\mathbb N_0^k}
                       a_{\mathbf n}\mathbf z^{\mathbf n},\\
 q_{\mathbf h}(F)=\sum_{\mathbf n}|a_{\mathbf n}|
               \prod_{i=1}^k\sqrt{n_i!}\,h_i^{n_i},\\
 \mathcal E^{[k]}=\{F:q_{\mathbf h}(F)<\infty
                                  \text{ for every }\mathbf h>0\}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Common radii $h_1=\cdots=h_k=h\in\mathbb N_{>0}$ suffice,
since every finite list of radii is bounded by a common integer.
Coordinate limits and completeness of each weighted $\ell^1$
space prove completeness exactly as for HTK1. Rectangular
polynomial truncations converge in every displayed norm.
Moreover
\[
 |F(\mathbf z)|\le q_{\mathbf h}(F)
             \exp\left(\sum_i\frac{|z_i|^2}{2h_i^2}\right),
\]
because $|z_i|^{n_i}/(\sqrt{n_i!}h_i^{n_i})$ is at most
$\exp(|z_i|^2/(2h_i^2))$. Thus these are actual entire
functions, with their original separate coordinates.

This space is the completed projective tensor product of the
specified coefficient spaces. Here is a direct proof rather
than an assumed identification. On the algebraic tensor product
define
\[
 \pi_{\mathbf h}(u)=\inf_{u=\sum_{r=1}^m
                  f_{r,1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{r,k}}
                   \sum_{r=1}^m\prod_i\|f_{r,i}\|_{h_i}.
\]
The coefficient map $f_1\otimes\cdots\otimes f_k\mapsto
\prod_i f_i(z_i)$ satisfies $q_{\mathbf h}\le\pi_{\mathbf h}$,
by expanding each finite tensor presentation and taking absolute
values. It is injective. To see this, restrict each tensor factor
to the finite-dimensional span occurring in a presentation.
Its coordinate coefficient functionals separate that span;
finitely many of them therefore span its dual. Products of those
functionals separate the resulting finite-dimensional tensor
product. If all multivariate coefficients vanish, every such
functional vanishes and hence the original tensor is zero.

For a polynomial tensor the monomial basis presentation gives
the reverse inequality $\pi_{\mathbf h}\le q_{\mathbf h}$.
For a general algebraic tensor $u$, truncate each factor at degree
$N$. The finite tensor presentation and a telescoping difference
give $\pi_{\mathbf h}(u-u_N)\to0$, because the factors converge
in their defining norms and their truncated norms are bounded.
Consequently $q_{\mathbf h}(u-u_N)\to0$ as well. Equality of the
two norms on $u_N$ gives equality on $u$. Polynomial tensors are
dense in HTK2, so completion gives the precise isomorphism
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK3}
 \mathcal E\widehat\otimes_\pi\cdots
       \widehat\otimes_\pi\mathcal E\ \simeq\ \mathcal E^{[k]}.
\end{equation}
The seminorms with separate radii generate the projective
topology: a finite collection of input seminorms is dominated
in each factor by one of the increasing norms in HTK1.
This specifies the completion used in HTK3 completely.

The diagonal map and the $k$-fold multiplication bound are
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK4}
\begin{gathered}
 (\Delta_kF)(z)=F(z,\ldots,z),\\
 \|\Delta_kF\|_h\le q_{(\sqrt{k}h,\ldots,\sqrt{k}h)}(F),
 \qquad
 \left\|\prod_{i=1}^kg_i\right\|_h
                       \le\prod_{i=1}^k\|g_i\|_{\sqrt{k}h}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
For $N=\sum_i n_i$, the multinomial theorem gives
$N!/\prod_i n_i!\le k^N$. Taking its square root and
summing absolute coefficients proves the first bound; the
second follows on a simple tensor. Each output coefficient
is a finite sum over $\sum_i n_i=N$, so the map is also
the literal diagonal substitution on entire functions.

For $n\ge0$, $b>0$ and $D=d/dz$, the exact derivative bound is
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK5}
 \|D^nf\|_h\le\sqrt{n!}\,b^{-n}
                      \|f\|_{\sqrt{h^2+b^2}}.
\end{equation}
Indeed the coefficient with original degree $m\ge n$ contributes
$|a_m|\sqrt{m!}\sqrt{n!}\sqrt{\binom mn}h^{m-n}$.
The single binomial term
$\binom mn h^{2(m-n)}b^{2n}\le(h^2+b^2)^m$
proves HTK5 after summation. Applying that argument in every
coordinate gives, with $D_i=\partial/\partial z_i$,
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK6}
 q_{\mathbf h}(D_1^{d_1}\cdots D_k^{d_k}F)
 \le\left(\prod_i\sqrt{d_i!}\,b_i^{-d_i}\right)
        q_{(\sqrt{h_i^2+b_i^2})_{i=1}^k}(F).
\end{equation}

\subsection{The full edge series and the exact heat comparison}

Let $\mathscr E_k=\{(i,j):1\le i<j\le k\}$ be the complete
set of unordered pairs. For a tuple $\mathbf n=(n_{ij})$ of
nonnegative integers indexed by these pairs, put
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK7}
 N(\mathbf n)=\sum_{i<j}n_{ij},\qquad
 d_i(\mathbf n)=\sum_{j<i}n_{ji}+\sum_{j>i}n_{ij},\qquad
 \sum_i d_i=2N.
\end{equation}
Every pair is retained. Define the coupling operator by
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK8}
 C_t^{(k)}F=\sum_{\mathbf n\in\mathbb N_0^{\mathscr E_k}}
    \left(\prod_{i<j}\frac{(-2t)^{n_{ij}}}{n_{ij}!}\right)
                         D_1^{d_1}\cdots D_k^{d_k}F.
\end{equation}
The term with all $n_{ij}=0$ has coefficient $1$, including
at $t=0$. For any $b_i>0$ with
$b_ib_j>2(k-1)|t|$ for every $i<j$, set
$r_{ij}=2(k-1)|t|/(b_ib_j)$. Then
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK9}
\begin{split}
 &\sum_{\mathbf n}
  \left(\prod_{i<j}\frac{(2|t|)^{n_{ij}}}{n_{ij}!}\right)
             q_{\mathbf h}(D_1^{d_1}\cdots D_k^{d_k}F)\\
 &\hspace{8mm}\le
       \left(\prod_{i<j}\frac1{1-r_{ij}}\right)
          q_{(\sqrt{h_i^2+b_i^2})_{i=1}^k}(F)<\infty.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
To prove it, the multinomial theorem at vertex $i$ gives
$d_i!\le(k-1)^{d_i}\prod_{j\ne i}n_{ij}!$, where
$n_{ij}=n_{ji}$ in this inequality. Multiplying the square
roots over all vertices yields
\[
 \prod_i\sqrt{d_i!}\le
      (k-1)^{N(\mathbf n)}\prod_{i<j}n_{ij}!.
\]
Insert this in HTK6. The factorials cancel, and the summand
is bounded by $\prod_{i<j}r_{ij}^{n_{ij}}$ times the input
norm in HTK9. The independent geometric sums prove the bound.
The parameters always exist, for example all $b_i$ equal to
any $b>\sqrt{2(k-1)|t|}$. Enlarging $|t|$ to its maximum
on a compact set proves locally uniform absolute convergence
in every norm. Thus HTK8 defines a continuous operator on the
complete space HTK2.

Keep $T_t=e^{-tD^2}$ with its proved HSL17--18 meaning:
\[
 T_tf=\sum_{m\ge0}\frac{(-t)^m}{m!}D^{2m}f,
 \quad \sum_{m\ge0}\frac{|t|^m}{m!}\|D^{2m}f\|_h
                  \le\|f\|_{h+\sqrt{2|t|}},
 \quad T_tT_s=T_{t+s}.
\]
On HTK2 let $A_t^{(k)}$ apply $T_t$ separately in every
coordinate. The same nonnegative coefficient proof in each
coordinate bounds its absolute multiple series by
$q_{(h_i+\sqrt{2|t|})_i}(F)$. It is a continuous group and
on simple tensors equals $T_t\otimes\cdots\otimes T_t$.
For a polynomial, $D\Delta_k=\Delta_k\sum_iD_i$ and all
relevant differential series terminate. The commuting finite
exponentials therefore give
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK10}
\begin{split}
 T_t\Delta_k A_{-t}^{(k)}F
 &=\Delta_k e^{-t(\sum_iD_i)^2}e^{t\sum_iD_i^2}F\\
 &=\Delta_k e^{-2t\sum_{i<j}D_iD_j}F
  =\Delta_k C_t^{(k)}F.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Each exponential identity follows by the finite multinomial
formula on that polynomial. HTK4, HTK9 and the heat bounds
make both ends continuous on HTK2. Polynomial density extends
HTK10 to every $F\in\mathcal E^{[k]}$. The same proof first
on polynomials and then by continuity gives
$C_t^{(k)}C_s^{(k)}=C_{t+s}^{(k)}$ and
$(C_t^{(k)})^{-1}=C_{-t}^{(k)}$. Thus the tensor correction
is itself an actual continuous group, with no infinite formal
cancellation required.

For $f_i\in\mathcal E$, the $k$-fold transported product
therefore is exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK11}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \mathop{\star_t}_{i=1}^k f_i
 &:=T_t\left(\prod_iT_{-t}f_i\right)\\
 &=\sum_{\mathbf n\in\mathbb N_0^{\mathscr E_k}}
     \left(\prod_{i<j}\frac{(-2t)^{n_{ij}}}{n_{ij}!}\right)
                      \prod_i D^{d_i(\mathbf n)}f_i.
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}
Its absolute convergence has the literal bound
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK12}
\begin{split}
 &\sum_{\mathbf n}
    \left(\prod_{i<j}\frac{(2|t|)^{n_{ij}}}{n_{ij}!}\right)
                  \left\|\prod_iD^{d_i}f_i\right\|_h\\
 &\hspace{8mm}\le
       \left(\prod_{i<j}\frac1{1-2(k-1)|t|/(b_ib_j)}\right)
                  \prod_i\|f_i\|_{\sqrt{kh^2+b_i^2}}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Apply HTK4 to each derivative product, HTK5 to each factor
at radius $\sqrt{k}h$, and the same factorial calculation
as in HTK9. This also proves multilinear continuity directly.
Every parenthesization of the binary product HTP8 gives HTK11:
applying the injective $T_{-t}$ to such a product gives the
ordinary product $\prod_iT_{-t}f_i$, by induction on the
binary parentheses. The independent tensor map $A_t^{(k)}$

\subsection{The original theta kernel on every tensor factor}

Use the unchanged kernel and family
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK13}
\begin{gathered}
 \Phi(u)=\sum_{m\ge1}
   (2\pi^2m^4e^{9u}-3\pi m^2e^{5u})e^{-\pi m^2e^{4u}},
       \quad u\ge0,\\
 H_a(z)=\int_0^\infty e^{au^2}\Phi(u)\cos(zu)\,du,\\
 c=e^{-3\pi},\quad
 C_\Phi=2\pi^2\frac{1+11c+11c^2+c^3}{(1-c)^5},\\
 I_p(A,B)=\int_0^\infty u^pe^{Au^2+Bu}\Phi(u)\,du,
 \quad
 \mathcal M_p(A,B)=\frac{C_\Phi}{2\pi}
     \exp\left(\frac{3A^2}{32\pi}
         +\frac{(B+9+p)^2}{8\pi}-\frac\pi2\right).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
For $A,B\ge0$ and integer $p\ge0$, THS3--5 prove
$0<\Phi(u)\le C_\Phi e^{9u-\pi e^{4u}}$ and
$I_p(A,B)\le\mathcal M_p(A,B)$. The positivity follows
term by term from
$\pi m^2e^{5u}(2\pi m^2e^{4u}-3)>0$. For the upper bound,
discard the negative prefactor and use
$m^2-1\ge3(m-1)$ to sum $\sum m^4c^{m-1}$.
For the moment bound use $u^p\le e^{pu}$, split the negative
exponent into quarters and a half, and apply
\[
 Au^2-\frac\pi4e^{4u}\le\frac{3A^2}{32\pi},\qquad
 (B+9+p)u-\frac\pi4e^{4u}\le\frac{(B+9+p)^2}{8\pi},
 \qquad
 \int_0^\infty e^{-\pi e^{4u}/2}du\le\frac{e^{-\pi/2}}{2\pi}.
\]
The first two inequalities follow respectively from
$e^{4u}\ge(32/3)u^4$ and $e^{4u}\ge8u^2$ by completing
the relevant square. The last uses $e^{4u}\ge1+4u$.
These give the constants displayed in HTK13 without an
unmentioned change of variables or function factor.

THS13--15, or the following direct estimates, give
$H_a\in\mathcal E$ and $T_rH_a=H_{a+r}$ for complex $a,r$.
Indeed $|\cos(zu)|\le e^{|z|u}$ and
$|z|u\le\alpha|z|^2+u^2/(4\alpha)$ give every positive
Gaussian growth bound. HSL16 then gives membership in HTK1.
The absolute heat-integrand sum is at most
$e^{(\operatorname{Re}a+|r|)u^2+|\operatorname{Im}z|u}\Phi(u)$;
HTK13 bounds its integral after enlarging a negative quadratic
coefficient to zero. Termwise heat differentiation has the
exact sign $(-r)^m(-1)^m=r^m$, giving $e^{ru^2}$ and hence
the asserted heat identity.

For $\boldsymbol\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}^k$, retain the signed
frequency
$s_{\boldsymbol\epsilon}(\mathbf u)=\sum_i\epsilon_i u_i$.
For arbitrary complex $a_1,\ldots,a_k,t,z$, the full tensor
integral is
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK14}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \left(\mathop{\star_t}_{i=1}^kH_{a_i}\right)(z)
 =2^{-k}\sum_{\boldsymbol\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}^k}
 \int_{[0,\infty)^k}&\left(\prod_i\Phi(u_i)\right)
   \exp\left(\sum_i a_i u_i^2
        +2t\sum_{i<j}\epsilon_i\epsilon_j u_i u_j\right)\\
 &\hspace{-12mm}\cdot
                  \cos\left(z\sum_i\epsilon_i u_i\right)d\mathbf u.
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}
All derivatives, integrals and sums used to establish this
formula are absolutely controlled in each norm of HTK1.
To verify this before exchanging them, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$\sum_{m\ge0}x^m/\sqrt{m!}\le\sqrt2e^{x^2}$ for $x\ge0$:
write the summands as
$2^{-m/2}(\sqrt2x)^m/\sqrt{m!}$.
Consequently sine and cosine of $uz$ have $h$-norm at most
$\sqrt2e^{h^2u^2}$. Their $d$th derivatives have norm at
most $\sqrt2u^de^{h^2u^2}$, since differentiation only
changes sine to cosine, a sign and the factor $u^d$.
By HTK4 the norm of the product of derivatives in HTK11
is therefore at most
$2^{k/2}\prod_i u_i^{d_i}e^{kh^2\sum_i u_i^2}$.
Summing its absolute edge weights yields
$\exp(2|t|\sum_{i<j}u_iu_j)$.
The inequality $2u_iu_j\le u_i^2+u_j^2$ now proves
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK15}
\begin{gathered}
 B_i(h)=\max\{\operatorname{Re}a_i+kh^2+(k-1)|t|,0\},\\
 \int_{[0,\infty)^k}\prod_i\Phi(u_i)e^{\sum_i(\operatorname{Re}a_i)u_i^2}
 \sum_{\mathbf n}\left(\prod_{i<j}\frac{(2|t|)^{n_{ij}}}{n_{ij}!}\right)
       \left\|\prod_iD^{d_i}\cos(zu_i)\right\|_h\\
 \hspace{8mm}\le 2^{k/2}\prod_i I_0(B_i(h),0)
                  \le2^{k/2}\prod_i\mathcal M_0(B_i(h),0)<\infty.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Here each $D$ in the product differentiates its own cosine
factor before multiplication. The finite derivative orders
and any parameter derivatives introduce only finite moment
powers; the corresponding $I_p$ bounds apply. Nonnegative
summation over absolute Taylor coefficients, followed by
Tonelli's theorem, proves all the stated interchanges in
each weighted $\ell^1$ norm. The resulting coefficients
belong to every such space, so they define the same element
of $\mathcal E$. On compact parameter sets a common bound
is obtained by enlarging the real parts and moduli in HTK15.
This also proves local uniformity and entire parameter
dependence. Expanding any of the original positive kernel
series is allowed by the same majorant, so no kernel term
has been dropped.

It remains to compute the integrand, including its signs.
The exact cosine expansion is
\[
 \cos(zu_i)=2^{-1}\sum_{\epsilon_i=\pm1}e^{\mathrm i\epsilon_i zu_i}.
\]
For an edge tuple $\mathbf n$, its derivatives contribute
$\prod_i(\mathrm i\epsilon_i u_i)^{d_i}$.
Since $\sum_i d_i=2N$, the factor $\mathrm i^{2N}=(-1)^N$
cancels the sign in $(-2t)^N$. The resulting edge series
is the product of the exponentials
$\exp(2t\epsilon_i\epsilon_j u_iu_j)$.
Thus the sum is first obtained with
$\exp(\mathrm i z s_{\boldsymbol\epsilon})$.
The substitution $\boldsymbol\epsilon\mapsto
-\boldsymbol\epsilon$ leaves the quadratic exponent
unchanged and changes $s$ to $-s$. Pairing those terms
replaces their exponential by the displayed cosine.
The factor $2^{-k}$ remains unchanged. This proves HTK14
with every pair, sign, coefficient and coordinate retained.

With $B=|\operatorname{Im}z|$ and
$A_i=\max\{\operatorname{Re}a_i+(k-1)|t|,0\}$, it follows that
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK16}
 \left|\left(\mathop{\star_t}_{i=1}^kH_{a_i}\right)(z)\right|
     \le\prod_i I_0(A_i,B)
     \le\prod_i\mathcal M_0(A_i,B).
\end{equation}
Indeed $|\cos(zs)|\le e^{B|s|}\le e^{B\sum_i u_i}$,
and the absolute cross exponent is at most
$(k-1)|t|\sum_i u_i^2$. Each of the $2^k$ terms has the
same separating bound, cancelling exactly the prefactor
$2^{-k}$. This proves the requested bound for arbitrary
complex parameters, without replacing $H_a$ by a quotient.

For real $a_i,t$, the even coefficients of HTK14 are all
strictly alternating and every odd coefficient is zero:
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK17}
 [z^{2j}]\left(\mathop{\star_t}_{i=1}^kH_{a_i}\right)
 =\frac{(-1)^j}{2^k(2j)!}
   \sum_{\boldsymbol\epsilon}\int
      \prod_i\Phi(u_i)e^{\sum_i a_i u_i^2+
                      2t\sum_{i<j}\epsilon_i\epsilon_j u_i u_j}
                 s_{\boldsymbol\epsilon}(\mathbf u)^{2j}d\mathbf u,
 \qquad (-1)^j[z^{2j}]>0.
\end{equation}
The moment bounds justify differentiation at zero.
Every integrand after removing $(-1)^j$ is nonnegative.
For $j=0$ it is positive everywhere; for $j>0$ the all-positive
sign tuple gives $s=\sum_i u_i>0$ on $(0,\infty)^k$.
That open set has positive measure and a positive kernel.
Thus its finite integral is strictly positive, proving the
assertion rather than assuming nonvanishing of a coefficient.

\subsection{Actual equal-time tensor powers and their characters}

Set every $a_i=t$ with $t\le0$ real. The exact exponent is
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK18}
 \sum_i t u_i^2+2t\sum_{i<j}\epsilon_i\epsilon_j u_iu_j
            =t\left(\sum_i\epsilon_i u_i\right)^2\le0,
 \qquad
 H_t^{\star_t k}=T_t(H_0^k).
\end{equation}
The second identity follows from $T_{-t}H_t=H_0$ and
HTK11. Consequently the exact bound with a base independent
of both $k$ and $t\le0$ is
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK19}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 |T_t(H_0^k)(z)|\le
       \left[\int_0^\infty\Phi(u)e^{|\operatorname{Im}z|u}du\right]^k
       = I_0(0,|\operatorname{Im}z|)^k.}
\end{equation}
Use HTK18 directly in HTK14 and then separate the bound
$e^{B\sum_i u_i}$. The proof works for $k=1$ as well.
The power on the right is part of the statement; no assertion
that its base is at most $1$ is used.

There is a concrete measure map behind this tensor identity.
Define a finite positive measure $\mu$ on $\mathbb R$ by
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK20}
 \int_{\mathbb R}\psi(x)d\mu(x)
   =\frac12\sum_{\epsilon=\pm1}\int_0^\infty
                         \Phi(u)\psi(\epsilon u)du,
 \qquad \mu(\mathbb R)=I_0(0,0).
\end{equation}
This is the pushforward of the two original $u$ copies,
with their explicitly displayed weight; it is not a
probability rescaling. Let $\Sigma(x_1,\ldots,x_k)=\sum_i x_i$
and define $\mu^{*k}=\Sigma_*(\mu^{\otimes k})$.
Absolute exponential moments from HTK13 give
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK21}
 \int e^{\mathrm i z s}d\mu^{*k}(s)=H_0(z)^k,
 \qquad
 T_t(H_0^k)(z)=\int e^{ts^2}\cos(zs)d\mu^{*k}(s).
\end{equation}
For the first equality, Fubini factors the integral and
the sign pairing in HTK20 gives $H_0$. For the second,
the pushforward definition and HTK18 give exactly HTK14.
Both measures are even, so exponential and cosine integrals
coincide. When $t\le0$ the positive measure
$e^{ts^2}\mu^{*k}$ is at most $\mu^{*k}$, and
\[
 \int e^{B|s|}d\mu^{*k}(s)
 \le\int e^{B\sum_i|x_i|}d\mu^{\otimes k}(\mathbf x)
 =I_0(0,B)^k.
\]
This gives a second proof of HTK19 and specifies the actual
map from the independent tensor factors to the signed-sum
coordinate. For unequal parameters, the weight on the
original tensor coordinates is exactly
$\exp(\sum_i a_ix_i^2+2t\sum_{i<j}x_ix_j)$; its
pushforward has the integral HTK14, by the same definition
and HTK16. In particular the tensor dependence has not been
replaced by a claim of independent heat after taking the sum.

The characters of the amplitude algebra are the actual HTP15
characters $\chi_{t,z}(f)=(T_{-t}f)(z)$. Their precise
comparison with these powers is
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK22}
\begin{gathered}
 \chi_{t,z}\left(\mathop{\star_t}_{i=1}^kH_{a_i}\right)
                  =\prod_iH_{a_i-t}(z),\\
 \chi_{t,z}(T_t(H_0^k))=H_0(z)^k,\\
 \chi_{t,z}(H_{2t}^{\star_t k})=H_t(z)^k.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Apply $T_{-t}$ in HTK11 and use the proved theta heat law.
For completeness, the character classification uses the
division identity
$f(w)-f(z)=(w-z)Q_zf(w)$ with $Q_zf\in\mathcal E$,
proved with Gaussian bounds in HTP14. For an ordinary
unital complex character put $z=\chi(w)$ and apply it to
this identity, obtaining $\chi(f)=f(z)$. Transport by
$T_t$ gives exactly the characters displayed here.
The distinction from evaluation is explicit:
$(w\star_tw)(z)=z^2-2t$, whereas $w(z)^2=z^2$.
Thus HTK19 controls ordinary values of the displayed
transported powers, and HTK22 proves the exact character
values to which they are related. It supplies no lower

proves no zero-location or RH conclusion.

\subsection{Every independent label and every iterated parity closure}

Let $L$ be the full nontrivial bounded distributive lattice,
with original carrier
$S=G_L(\mathbb C)=\{(0,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}
\cup\{(a,1_L):a\in\mathbb C\}$ and its original coordinate
join and meet operations. Write $\widehat L=\operatorname{Idl}(L)$;
its arbitrary joins are generated ideals, its meet is
intersection, and $\jmath(\lambda)=\downarrow\lambda$.
The finite-meet distributivity over arbitrary joins proved
in HSL19 and SHP3 also follows from
$c=\bigvee_r(c\wedge b_r)$ whenever
$c\le\bigvee_r b_r$. All infinite support joins below are
first computed there, without assuming completeness of $L$.

For coefficient support sequences define ordinary convolution
$*$ and inclusive parity closure by
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK23}
 (\nu^{(1)}*\cdots*\nu^{(k)})_n
       =\bigvee_{r_1+\cdots+r_k=n}\bigwedge_i\nu^{(i)}_{r_i},
 \qquad
 (P\nu)_n=\bigvee_{j\ge n,\ j\equiv n\ (2)}\nu_j.
\end{equation}
The full closed product of parity flags $P\nu^{(i)}=\nu^{(i)}$
has the following finite formula in the original $L$:
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK24}
\begin{gathered}
 \mathscr R_{k,n}=
 \{\mathbf r\in\mathbb N_0^k:\textstyle\sum_i r_i=n\}
 \ \cup\\
 \{\mathbf r\in\{0,1\}^k:\textstyle\sum_i r_i>n,
                                  \ \sum_i r_i\equiv n\ (2)\},\\
 \mathscr Q_k(\nu^{(1)},\ldots,\nu^{(k)})_n
       =\bigvee_{\mathbf r\in\mathscr R_{k,n}}
                                      \bigwedge_i\nu^{(i)}_{r_i}
       =P(\nu^{(1)}*\cdots*\nu^{(k)})_n.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
To prove equality, a term of the last expression has total
index at least $n$ of the same parity. If its total is larger
than $n$ and some index is at least $2$, lower that index
by $2$. The flag inequality weakly increases the label meet.
After finitely many such steps the total reaches $n$ or all
indices are $0$ or $1$. The resulting tuple is in
$\mathscr R_{k,n}$. Thus each term of the parity tail is
bounded by a term of the finite join in HTK24. Conversely
every tuple in that finite set occurs in the parity tail.
This proves both inequalities, and proves that the ideal
value is the principal ideal of the displayed original label.
No extra binary tuple can occur for $n\ge k-1$: its total
would have to be at least $n+2>k$. For $k=2$ the only extra
term is $\nu^{(1)}_1\wedge\nu^{(2)}_1$ at $n=0$,
recovering SHP5 exactly.

This is also the actual iterated product, for arbitrary
parentheses, and it includes raw independent labels.
For any sequences in $\widehat L$, one has
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK25}
 P\bigl((P\nu^{(1)})*\cdots*(P\nu^{(k)})\bigr)
                  =P(\nu^{(1)}*\cdots*\nu^{(k)}).
\end{equation}
Indeed, on expansion, an input label to a closed factor at
index $r_i$ comes from $a_i\ge r_i$ of the same parity.
Any outer parity-tail term therefore has
$\sum_i a_i\ge\sum_i r_i\ge n$ and total parity $n$;
it occurs in the right side. This gives one inequality.
The reverse follows from $\nu^{(i)}\le P\nu^{(i)}$.
Taking $k=2$ in this proved equality allows one to remove
each internal closure in any iterated closed product.
Induction on its parenthesization reduces it to the right
side of HTK25, proving that every intermediate closure is
accounted for by HTK24, not omitted.

The independently labelled domain $\mathcal D_L$ of SHP6
consists of $(f,\nu)$ with $f\in\mathcal E$,
$[z^n]f\ne0\Rightarrow\nu_n=1_L$, and principal ideal
parity tails $P\jmath(\nu)_n=\downarrow s_n$.
The labels $\nu_n$ have no further dependence requirement.
For $k$ such inputs their ordinary coefficient product has
principal parity-tail generators exactly
$\mathscr Q_k(s^{(1)},\ldots,s^{(k)})$:
apply HTK25 in $\widehat L$, and then HTK24 to the
principal tails. This proves closure within the original
lattice for the full $k$-fold product.

The flag algebra is the exact image
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK26}
 \mathcal A_L=\{(f,\nu):f\in\mathcal E, \nu_n\ge\nu_{n+2},
               \ [z^n]f\ne0\Rightarrow\nu_n=1_L\},
 \qquad \mathcal P(f,\nu)=(f,P\nu).
\end{equation}
Its binary product $\circ$ uses ordinary amplitude product
and the closed support product SHP5; HTK25 proves directly
that $\mathcal P$ takes raw products to these products.
Its heat group is $U_t(f,\nu)=(T_tf,\nu)$.
This preserves the carrier: a nonzero output coefficient
has a nonzero higher coefficient of matching parity, whose
top label propagates down the flag. Its inverse is $U_{-t}$,
by the amplitude heat law and the unchanged labels.
Hence its exact $k$-fold transported product is
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK27}
 \mathop{\star_t}_{i=1}^k(f_i,\nu^{(i)})
   =\left(T_t\left(\prod_iT_{-t}f_i\right),
                  \mathscr Q_k(\nu^{(1)},\ldots,\nu^{(k)})\right).
\end{equation}
This follows by invertible transport from the iterated
$\circ$ product just proved. It is in the carrier: each
intermediate ordinary coefficient product with a nonzero
amplitude has a nonzero contributing term, forcing a top
support meet; the outer heat map preserves the carrier as
above. In particular no support label in HTK27 is computed
by erasing a term of the amplitude series HTK11 when it
vanishes or when different amplitudes cancel.

\subsection{The full coefficient maps and the exact theta lift}

Retain $K_L=K\otimes_{\mathbb B}L$ and
$\iota_L(\lambda)=1_K\otimes\lambda$. The nonzero indicator
$\beta:K\to\mathbb B$ is a semiring map, because tropical
addition is zero exactly when both inputs are zero and
a product is zero exactly when a factor is zero. Thus
$\rho_L=\beta\otimes\operatorname{id}_L$ satisfies
$\rho_L\iota_L=\operatorname{id}_L$.
Coordinatewise application proves the injective map
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK28}
 \boldsymbol\Phi(f,\nu)
     =(f,(\iota_L(\nu_n))_{n\ge0}),\qquad
 \boldsymbol\Phi\left(\mathop{\star_t}_{i=1}^kv_i\right)
 =\left(\mathop{\star_t}_{i=1}^kf_i,
             \mathscr Q_k(\iota_L\nu^{(1)},\ldots,
                                           \iota_L\nu^{(k)})\right).
\end{equation}
In the right-hand $\mathscr Q_k$, finite joins and meets are
respectively addition and multiplication in $K_L$. Every
term of HTK24 is finite, so preservation follows directly
from the semiring laws of $\iota_L$. Applying $\rho_L$ to
every coefficient recovers all original labels. This is
the actual original coefficient map
$(a_n,\nu_n)\mapsto(a_n,1_K\otimes\nu_n)$, organized as a
function and its full flag, and not one global label.

For arbitrary ideal-labelled flags, the exact completed maps
are
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK29}
\begin{gathered}
 \mathfrak K_L=\operatorname{Idl}_{\mathrm{ord}}(K_L),\\
 \iota^\#(I)=\{w\in K_L:\exists a\in I,\ w\le\iota_L(a)\},\\
 \rho^\#(J)=\{\lambda\in L:\exists w\in J,
                                      \ \lambda\le\rho_L(w)\},\\
 \rho^\#\iota^\#=\operatorname{id},\qquad
 \iota^\#(\downarrow\lambda)=\downarrow\iota_L(\lambda).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Here ordered ideals are lower subsets closed under finite
idempotent sums, with joins generated by unions and products
generated by pairwise products. The displayed sets are
ideals: finite sums of two witnesses are witnessed by
their finite sum in the source ideal. The generator
description shows preservation of arbitrary joins and
products by the induced maps; principal units are
preserved as well. The retraction follows from
$\rho_L\iota_L=\operatorname{id}$ and monotonicity:
if $w\le\iota_L(a)$ then $\rho_L(w)\le a$, and each
$a$ is witnessed by $w=\iota_L(a)$. These are the actual
HSL33--35 completed maps, with their proof included.
Applying them coordinatewise to HTK23--27 proves HTK28
for all ideal-labelled inputs too. The original formula
is its restriction under the two principal embeddings.
Thus the arbitrary support completion and the full original
lattice have an explicit faithful connecting map.

For completeness, the exact passage to one global label is
$\epsilon(\nu)=\nu_0\vee\nu_1$.
For a parity flag this is the join of all its entries, since
every entry is below the first entry of its parity. Expanding
the finite distributive product gives
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK30}
 \epsilon\bigl(\mathscr Q_k(\nu^{(1)},\ldots,\nu^{(k)})\bigr)
                    =\bigwedge_i(\nu^{(i)}_0\vee\nu^{(i)}_1).
\end{equation}
Every term on the left is at most the right side.
Conversely a term of the distributed right side chooses
one binary index from each factor; its total has parity
$0$ or $1$, so it occurs in the corresponding parity-tail
output at index $0$ or $1$. HTK24 therefore contains that
term, proving the reverse inequality. The full coefficient
map commutes with this global map because $\iota_L$
preserves the finite join. To retain ordinary amplitude
multiplication after transport, the actual map is
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK31}
 \gamma_t(f,\nu)=(T_{-t}f,\epsilon(\nu))\in G_L(\mathcal E),
 \qquad
 \gamma_t\left(\mathop{\star_t}_{i=1}^k v_i\right)
                        =\prod_i\gamma_t(v_i).
\end{equation}
HTK11 and HTK30 prove every coordinate of this identity.
The unchanged amplitude map instead lands in
$G_L(\mathcal E_t)$, where multiplication is $\star_t$.
These are the precise synchronization maps proved in
SHP17--27; no ordinary-product target is silently assigned
to the unchanged amplitude map.

Finally let $\eta_{2j}=1_L$, $\eta_{2j+1}=0_L$, and use
the actual theta lift $\mathbf H_a=(H_a,\eta)$ of THS18.
HTK24 gives
\begin{equation}\tag{HTK32}
 \mathscr Q_k(\eta,\ldots,\eta)=\eta,\qquad
 \mathop{\star_t}_{i=1}^k\mathbf H_{a_i}
       =\left(\mathop{\star_t}_{i=1}^k H_{a_i},\eta\right),\qquad
 \mathbf H_t^{\star_t k}=(T_t(H_0^k),\eta).
\end{equation}
At any even output degree take $(n,0,\ldots,0)$ in HTK24;
its meet is top. At an odd output degree every admissible
tuple has an odd index, hence a bottom factor, so every
meet is bottom. This proves the first identity and then
HTK27 proves the other two. For real $a_i,t$, HTK17 forces
every even label to be top by actual nonvanishing; for
complex parameters the same prescribed labels are retained.
Applying HTK28 gives precisely $(a_{2j},1_{K_L})$ at even
