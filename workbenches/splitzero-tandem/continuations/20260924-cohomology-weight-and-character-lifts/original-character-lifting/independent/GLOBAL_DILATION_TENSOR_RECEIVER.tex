\subsection{The actual quotient and the exact dilation action}

Retain the original objects
\begin{equation}\tag{GDT1}
 \begin{gathered}

   =\mathbb R_{>0}\times K,\quad K=\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{\times},

 (E\xi)(z)=\sum_{r\in\mathbb Q^\times}\xi(rz),\qquad
 W=\overline V,\qquad H=B/W.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The two original defining conditions on the adelic source are
$\xi(0)=0$ and $\int_{\mathbb A}\xi=0$.
The closure is the weighted Bruhat closure of SWQ3: at a fixed
finite compact level its seminorms are
\[
 p_{N,m,j}(f)=\sup_{v,u}
 e^{N|v|}(1+|v|)^m
 |\partial_v^j f(e^v,u)|.
\]
SWQ4--15 proves that convolution and sharp are continuous and
that $W$ is a sharp-invariant convolution ideal.
The original trace and Hermitian pairing therefore descend:
\begin{equation}\tag{GDT2}
 \mathscr T(f)=\operatorname{Tr}(\underline\vartheta_m(f)\mid H^1),
 \qquad Q([f],[g])=\mathscr T(f*g^\sharp).
\end{equation}
In particular $\mathscr T(W)=0$ and $Q(W,B)=0$.
This notation does not assume that $Q$ is positive or nondegenerate.

For every real $a>0$ define the original dilation
\begin{equation}\tag{GDT3}
 R_a f(x,u)=f(x/a,u),\qquad
 \sigma_a\xi(x_\infty,x_f)=\xi(x_\infty/a,x_f).
\end{equation}
The real additive Jacobian is $a$:
$\sigma_a\xi(0)=\xi(0)$ and
$\int_{\mathbb A}\sigma_a\xi=a\int_{\mathbb A}\xi$.
Thus $\sigma_a$ preserves both source zero conditions and
$E\sigma_a\xi=R_aE\xi$, by the original absolutely convergent
rational sum. It follows that $R_aV=V$.
The explicit seminorm inequality
\[
 p_{N,m,j}(R_af)\le
 e^{N|\log a|}(1+|\log a|)^m p_{N,m,j}(f)
\]
follows by shifting $v$ to $v-\log a$. Its inverse is
$R_{1/a}$, so $R_aW=W$ and the same original action
descends to $H$. It is strongly smooth; differentiating
the shifted function gives its exact generator
\begin{equation}\tag{GDT4}
 Df=-x\partial_x f,\qquad
 R_{e^t}=\exp(tD),\qquad
 M(Df)(s)=sMf(s),\qquad M(R_af)(s)=a^sMf(s).
\end{equation}
The exponential here means the already defined strongly smooth
group, not convergence of a power series on all of $B$.
The Mellin equations follow by integration by parts and change
of variables, with boundary terms zero by the weighted
seminorms. Closedness and $R_a$-invariance of $W$ make the
difference quotients for $D$ remain in $W$, so $D$ descends too.

Directly in the original functions,
\begin{equation}\tag{GDT5}
 \begin{gathered}

under this possibly degenerate form. Differentiating the

\subsection{Actual Mellin jets and explicit primary chains}

Let $B_0$ be the trivial compact-character sector and
$H_0=B_0/(W\cap B_0)$. For each nontrivial zero $\rho$
of the original $\zeta$, write $m_\rho$ for its actual
multiplicity and define
\begin{equation}\tag{GDT11}
 J_\rho:H_0\longrightarrow
 A_\rho=\mathbb C[t]/(t^{m_\rho}),\qquad
 J_\rho([f])=\sum_{j=0}^{m_\rho-1}
       \frac{(Mf)^{(j)}(\rho)}{j!}\,t^j .
\end{equation}
Here is a direct check that this is a map on the actual
quotient. Average the finite components of any original
adelic $\xi$ over $K$. Its finite-place part becomes
a finite linear combination of products
$\prod_p1_{p^{k_p}\mathbb Z_p}$: at each of the finitely
many nonstandard primes a compact, locally constant
unit-invariant function is a finite sum of ball
indicators, by expressing its finitely many annuli
as differences of successive balls.
The archimedean factors are Schwartz.
The original rational reduction of each such term
is a sum over $n\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$ at
arguments $N(k)nx$. Combining the two signs, its Mellin
integral for $\Re s>1$ is $\zeta(s)$ times a finite sum
of integrals
$N(k)^{-s}\int_0^\infty[\phi(x)+\phi(-x)]x^{s-1}dx$.
Those integrals are holomorphic for $\Re s>0$ by
Schwartz decay at infinity and boundedness at zero.
The initial interchange is absolute, since it is
bounded by $\sum n^{-\Re s}$ times these absolute
integrals. Thus the trivial-sector reduction has a
factor $\zeta(s)$ with holomorphic complementary
factor at every such $\rho$.
For $\xi\in\mathcal S(\mathbb A)_0$ the reduction is in
$B$ and its Mellin transform is entire, as proved in
SWQ8. Continuation therefore gives vanishing of its
first $m_\rho$ Taylor coefficients at $\rho$.
These jet functionals are continuous in the original
weighted topology, by their absolutely convergent
integrals with powers of $\log x$, so they vanish
on $W$ as well. This proves representative independence.
Convolution's exact Mellin product identity proves
that $J_\rho$ is an algebra homomorphism.

It is onto, with an explicit finite right inverse.
Choose any nonnegative $w\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$
positive on a nonempty interval and put
$f_j(x)=w(\log x)x^{-\rho}(\log x)^j$ for
$0\le j<m_\rho$.
Set $G_{ij}=\int_{\mathbb R}w(v)v^{i+j}dv$.
For nonzero coefficients $c_j$,
$\sum c_i\overline{c_j}G_{ij}
=\int w(v)|\sum c_iv^i|^2dv>0$,
so $G$ is invertible.
The $i$th Taylor coefficient of $Mf_j$ at $\rho$ is
$G_{ij}/i!$.
Therefore, for desired coefficients $d_i$, the original
compact function
$\sum_j[G^{-1}(i!d_i)_i]_jf_j$
maps to $\sum_i d_it^i$. Its class supplies the required
right inverse without an assumed local spectral model.
The exact actions on this receiver are
\begin{equation}\tag{GDT12}
 J_\rho D=(\rho+t)J_\rho,\qquad
 J_\rho R_a=a^\rho
   \sum_{j=0}^{m_\rho-1}\frac{(\log a)^j t^j}{j!}J_\rho.
\end{equation}
All nilpotent coefficients are retained.

There are also actual primary vectors in $H_0$.
Put $k_{\rho,0}=h$, the original positive theta-image
function, and for $1\le r\le m_\rho$ define
\begin{equation}\tag{GDT13}
 \begin{split}
 k_{\rho,r}(x)
 &=\frac{x^{-\rho}}{(r-1)!}
   \int_x^\infty y^{\rho-1}
             (\log(y/x))^{r-1}h(y)\,dy,\\
 (D-\rho)k_{\rho,r}&=k_{\rho,r-1},\qquad
 Mk_{\rho,r}(s)=\frac{M(s)}{(s-\rho)^r}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
For $r=1$, the vanishing $Mh(\rho)=0$ changes the
integral into $-x^{-\rho}\int_0^x y^{\rho-1}h(y)dy$
near zero. At infinity the displayed upper integral
has arbitrarily rapid decay. For any desired
power at either endpoint, bound $h$ by a higher
power using its original seminorms; integrating the
result proves the desired estimate for $k_{\rho,1}$.
The differential equation follows by differentiating
the integral and proves the same bounds for every
logarithmic derivative.
Integration by parts gives the Mellin equation.
Its right hand side is entire because $M$ vanishes
at $\rho$.
Repeat this argument: at step $r\le m_\rho$ the
previous transform still vanishes at $\rho$.
Iterating the upper integrals, justified by their
absolute decay and Fubini, gives the displayed
factor $(r-1)!$ and logarithmic power.
This proves membership in $B_0$ and all equations
for every stated $r$.

Write $u_{\rho,r}=[k_{\rho,r}]\in H_0$. Since $h\in V$,
$u_{\rho,0}=0$. If
$M(\rho+t)=c_\rho t^{m_\rho}+O(t^{m_\rho+1})$, then

on the open critical strip. The jet of $k_{\rho,r}$

These different initial orders prove that

Indeed $Mk_{\rho,r}$ vanishes at every nontrivial

Substitution into

\subsection{Convolution, its clock defect, and the exact graded repair}

The $k$-fold original convolution defines
\begin{equation}\tag{GDT18}
 m_k:H^{\otimes k}\longrightarrow H,\qquad
 [f_1]\otimes\cdots\otimes[f_k]
                   \longmapsto[f_1*\cdots*f_k].
\end{equation}
Changing any representative by $w\in W$ changes the
product by a convolution containing a factor in
the ideal $W$; expanding a simultaneous change
gives a sum of such terms. This proves the map.
For the full independent dilation tuple,
\begin{equation}\tag{GDT19}
 \begin{gathered}
 m_k(R_{a_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes R_{a_k})
              =R_{a_1\cdots a_k}m_k,\\
 m_kR_a^{[k]}=R_{a^k}m_k,\qquad
 m_kD_i=Dm_k,\qquad m_kD^{[k]}=kDm_k,\\
 m_kR_a^{[k]}-R_am_k=(R_{a^k}-R_a)m_k.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
These identities follow directly from the original
convolution changes of variables, before passage to
the quotient. They show precisely which clock it
receives.
For every $a>1$, $k\ge2$, and any nontrivial zero
$\rho$, this last defect is nonzero on
$u_{\rho,m_\rho}^{\otimes k}$:
its constant jet is
$(a^{k\rho}-a^\rho)c_\rho^k\ne0$.
Here $\Re\rho>0$ makes the two absolute values
different, and $c_\rho\ne0$. This is a concrete
obstruction to same-clock equivariance.

The exact receiving algebra that restores equivariance is
\begin{equation}\tag{GDT20}
 \begin{gathered}
 \mathcal A=\bigoplus_{k\ge1}H[k],\qquad
 H[k]\text{ a specified copy of }H,\qquad
 \alpha_a|_{H[k]}=R_{a^k},\\
 H[r]\times H[s]\longrightarrow H[r+s],\qquad
 (v,w)\longmapsto v*w,\qquad
 \widetilde m_k:H^{\otimes k}\longrightarrow H[k].
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Only finite graded sums occur.
Associativity and commutativity follow from original
convolution. There is no inserted unit.
The independent-clock identity GDT19 gives
$(R_{a^r}v)*(R_{a^s}w)=R_{a^{r+s}}(v*w)$,
so $\alpha_a$ is a graded algebra automorphism and
$\widetilde m_k$ is equivariant for the original
diagonal tensor action. The exact sequence
$0\to\ker m_k\to H^{\otimes k}\to\operatorname{im}m_k\to0$
retains its kernel. More strongly the map
$v\mapsto(v,\widetilde m_kv)$ into
$H^{\otimes k}\times H[k]$ is injective with left
inverse the first projection, so it retains the
entire original tensor along with the receiving
value.
On the homogeneous grade $k$, the original pairing
has factor $a^k$:
\[
 Q(\alpha_av,\alpha_aw)=a^kQ(v,w).
\]
On the full direct sum, the grade-orthogonal pairing
is the sum of the grade pairings, whose terms have
their individual factors $a^k$.

The exact spectral information lost by $m_k$ is
also computable. For any actual finite primary
vectors $v_i$ satisfying
$(D-\rho_i)^{n_i}v_i=0$, their product is annihilated
by each polynomial $(D-\rho_i)^{n_i}$, by GDT19.
If two $\rho_i$ differ, these two polynomials are
coprime. Their Euclidean algorithm gives polynomial
coefficients with sum one, and applying that
identity to the product proves
\begin{equation}\tag{GDT21}
 v_1*\cdots*v_k=0
       \quad\text{whenever two primary weights differ}.
\end{equation}
For equal $\rho$ the product is annihilated by
$(D-\rho)^{\min n_i}$. In the trivial compact
sector its exact retained jet is
\begin{equation}\tag{GDT22}
 J_\rho(v_1*\cdots*v_k)=\prod_iJ_\rho(v_i)
             \pmod{t^{m_\rho}} .
\end{equation}
The multiplication here includes every finite
Taylor coefficient, with truncation only at its
specified original multiplicity.
Moreover $N(v*w)=(Nv)*w=v*(Nw)$ on that primary
part, rather than the sum of these two terms.
Consequently the tensor nilpotent of GDT17 maps to
$kN$ in the accelerated-clock grade.
The jet receiver is surjective and has the actual
chains above; its kernel is retained as
$\ker J_\rho$. No unsupported identification of
the entire quotient with one jet algebra has
been used.

The tensor pairing is not the pulled-back grade
pairing. In the original formulas they are respectively
\begin{equation}\tag{GDT23}
 \begin{gathered}
 Q_k(\otimes_i f_i,\otimes_i g_i)
                  =\prod_i\mathscr T(f_i*g_i^\sharp),\\
 Q(m_k(\otimes_i f_i),m_k(\otimes_i g_i))
                  =\mathscr T\!\left(*_{i=1}^k
                                     (f_i*g_i^\sharp)\right).
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
In the trivial sector their absolutely convergent
spectral expressions sum, in the first line, over
all tuples $(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_k)$ with factor
$\prod_i m_{\rho_i}$; the second sums only over
one common $\rho$ with factor $m_\rho$.
Both retain the original products of Mellin
values at $\rho_i$ and their partners
$1-\bar\rho_i$.
Even the retained diagonal tuples therefore have
different multiplicity factors. These exact
formulas show why replacing the full tensor by
its convolution value would lose information.

\subsection{Pointwise multiplication and its dense obstruction}

Pointwise multiplication on $B$ has precisely the
diagonal clock:
$(R_af)(R_ag)=R_a(fg)$.
The same is true for every $k$-fold pointwise
product, and it preserves $B$ by Leibniz's
formula and its weighted seminorms.
It nevertheless does not define a product on
the actual quotient $H$.
Here is an explicit original-source witness.
For a nontrivial zero $\rho$, choose
$w\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$ with
$w\ge0$ and $\int w=1$, and let
\begin{equation}\tag{GDT24}
 \phi(x,u)=w(\log x)x^{-\rho},\qquad
 g(x,u)=\frac{\phi(x,u)}{h(x)}.
\end{equation}
GTK4 proves that the original $h\in V$ is strictly
positive at every $x>0$. Thus $g$ is a smooth
compact test in $B$, and $hg=\phi$.
But $M\phi(\rho)=1$, whereas every member of $W$
has vanishing Mellin value at $\rho$ by GDT11.
Hence $[hg]\ne0$ although $[h]=0$.
This is a failure of representative independence,
not merely a difference between two written products.

The strongest associated obstruction map is
\begin{equation}\tag{GDT25}
 \Omega:W\otimes_{\mathbb C}B\longrightarrow H,\qquad
               w\otimes g\longmapsto[wg].
\end{equation}
It intertwines the diagonal original dilation.
Its image contains the class of every compact
Bruhat test $F$: the same exact antecedent
$h\otimes(F/h)$ maps to $[F]$.
Compact tests are dense in $B$. To verify this,
multiply at a fixed compact level by
$\chi((\log x)/R)$, with a smooth compact cutoff
equal to one near zero. Leibniz's formula and
a stronger exponential weight bound each
seminorm of the discarded tail and cutoff
derivatives by a quantity tending to zero.
Thus the image of $\Omega$ is dense in $H$.
In particular the closed pointwise ideal of
$B$ generated by $W$ is all of $B$: it contains
every compact test as just proved.
Every continuous homomorphism from the
pointwise algebra $B$ to a Hausdorff topological
algebra which kills $V$ is therefore zero.
Its closed kernel contains $W$, then every
compact test, then all of $B$.
The map GDT25 identifies exactly the defect
of using the pointwise product as a quotient
tensor receiver.

\subsection{What the tensor calculations do and do not supply}

The balanced mixed receiver is also an actual map.
Let $C f=\bar f$ be conjugation of the original
amplitude. It preserves $V$ because
$\overline{E\xi}=E\bar\xi$ and preserves both
source zero conditions; continuity then preserves
$W$. Define
\begin{equation}\tag{GDT26}
 \begin{gathered}
 \beta:H_0\otimes_{\mathbb C}H_0\longrightarrow\mathbb C,
 \qquad\beta(f\otimes g)=Q(f,Cg)
       =\sum_\rho m_\rho Mf(\rho)Mg(1-\rho),\\
 \beta(R_af\otimes R_ag)=a\beta(f\otimes g),\qquad
 \beta(Df\otimes g)+\beta(f\otimes Dg)=\beta(f\otimes g).
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Conjugate linearity in the second argument of
$Q$ and conjugate linearity of $C$ make this
bilinear; the displayed spectral expression
follows from $M(Cg)(s)=\overline{Mg(\bar s)}$.
It is symmetric by reindexing $\rho\mapsto1-\rho$,
which preserves the original multiplicities.
The target has the explicitly specified action
$z\mapsto az$; with that action $\beta$ is an
equivariant tensor receiver, without introducing
a positive norm on the source.

For the actual chains its value is exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{GDT27}
 \beta(u_{\rho,r}\otimes u_{\sigma,\ell})
 =
 \begin{cases}
 m_\rho c_\rho c_\sigma,&
 \sigma=1-\rho,\ r=m_\rho,\ \ell=m_\sigma,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Indeed conjugating the integral GDT13 gives
$Ck_{\sigma,\ell}=k_{\bar\sigma,\ell}$, and
GDT16 then gives the formula.
The original theta symmetry gives
$c_{1-\rho}=(-1)^{m_\rho}c_\rho$, so its
top value is
$m_\rho(-1)^{m_\rho}c_\rho^2\ne0$.
These balanced weights are always distinct.
To verify the only possible exception, the
positive original integral $M(1/2)>0$ together
with the retained exact multiplier
$s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)/4$ at $s=1/2$
gives $\zeta(1/2)<0$. Thus $\rho=1-\rho$
cannot be an original nontrivial zero.
GDT21 therefore kills every such balanced
primary tensor under convolution, while
GDT27 gives its nonzero bilinear contraction.

On the entire balanced primary block the
generator is $1+N_1+N_2$, with the full
nilpotent index from GDT17.
The contraction annihilates $N_1+N_2$ by GDT26;
on these actual chains it annihilates each
lower-chain direction separately by GDT27.
This describes its exact loss of nilpotent
data, rather than replacing that block by
its single output scalar.
The combined receiving map
$v\mapsto(m_2v,\beta(v))$
has target $H[2]\oplus\mathbb C$, with
action $(R_{a^2},a)$.
Appending $v$ itself gives the faithful
equivariant graph
$v\mapsto(v,m_2v,\beta(v))$.
The first projection is its explicit left
inverse, so all balanced mixed tensors and
their nilpotents survive this full object.

On actual primary tensors the original dilation
eigenfactor is $a^{\rho_1+\cdots+\rho_k}$, with the
full nilpotent exponential of GDT17; their pairing
has similitude factor $a^k$.
The graded receiver GDT20 gives the same factor
on surviving equal-primary tuples, with clock
$a\mapsto a^k$. These are exact tensor mechanisms
of the type that must be tracked in a comparison
with Deligne's product amplification.
They do not identify the tensor pairing with
its convolution pushforward: GDT21--23 compute
the difference, including its mixed-primary
kernel and multiplicity factors.

Nor does a similitude exponent itself force
the modulus $|a^\rho|=a^{1/2}$.
For an explicit exact model, take any complex
$z$ with $\Re z\ne1/2$ and the two-dimensional
space with
$R_a=\operatorname{diag}(a^z,a^{1-\bar z})$
and Hermitian matrix
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
Its pairing satisfies GDT5 for every $a>0$,
and all its algebraic tensor powers satisfy
GDT10, while its two moduli are different.
This calculation is a counterexample to
deducing purity from those action-and-pairing
identities alone. It is not asserted to be
a new zero of the original zeta.
The actual primary chains and receiving maps
remain GDT11--23, which preserve their
arithmetic source, nilpotents and full global
pairing. No finite cohomological dimension,
positive norm, or uniform power estimate has
been inserted into them.

\subsection{Every original support label}

For an arbitrary nontrivial bounded distributive
lattice $L$, use the entire original carrier
$G_L(U)=\{(0,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}
\cup(U\times\{1_L\})$ for every amplitude
space in this chapter.
The quotient is exactly the same-label
congruence of SWQ17; it preserves every
$(0,\lambda)$.
Each displayed linear map, including $R_a$,
$D$, $J_\rho$, $m_k$, $\widetilde m_k$,
the graded inclusion and projection, and
$\Omega$, lifts as $(v,\lambda)\mapsto(Fv,\lambda)$.
Nonzero output requires nonzero input,
whose label is top, so these lifts belong
to their declared carriers.
Linearity proves additivity with label join
and the scalar rule with label meet.
The multilinear original tensor map sends
$((v_i,\lambda_i))_i$ to
$(\bigotimes_i v_i,\bigwedge_i\lambda_i)$;
the same meet occurs for the $k$-fold
convolution before its linear receiving map.
No identification with an independently
labelled coordinate product is made.

The full tensor pairing is
$Q_{k,L}((v,\lambda),(w,\mu))
=(Q_k(v,w),\lambda\wedge\mu)$.
The linear contraction on the tensor amplitude
has the exact lift
$\beta_L(v,\lambda)=(\beta(v),\lambda)$;
conjugation has the lift
$(f,\lambda)\mapsto(\bar f,\lambda)$.
Its nonzero balanced top value therefore keeps
top support, and each vanished lower-chain
value retains its original zero label.
The combined tensor-convolution-contraction
graph uses one common label on the full
amplitude triple.
Its factor $a^k$ is represented by the
nonzero top-supported scalar $(a^k,1_L)$,
so it leaves every support label unchanged.
The graph retaining the whole tensor and
its grade receiver uses the single common
label on that paired amplitude.
For the pointwise obstruction, the two
equivalent top-supported representatives
$(h,1_L)$ and $(0,1_L)$ have outputs
$([\phi],1_L)$ and $e$ after multiplying
by $(g,1_L)$ and projecting.
The former has nonzero amplitude; the
latter is $e\ne\tau$.
Thus the proved failure of descent
survives the full original support map.
The tropical map
$(v,\lambda)\mapsto
(v,1_{K_{\rm trop}}\otimes\lambda)$ and its
label retraction commute with every linear
map above. Actual localization at a
support prime $J$ sends the retained label
to $[\lambda]_J$; it forgets the amplitude
as specified by the original localization,
without changing the global maps just
proved. No measure, positivity order or
infinite join on $L$ has been assumed.
