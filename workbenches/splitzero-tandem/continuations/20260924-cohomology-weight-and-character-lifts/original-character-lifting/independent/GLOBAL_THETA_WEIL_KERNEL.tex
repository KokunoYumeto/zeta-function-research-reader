\subsection{The actual adelic antecedent and a strictly positive original test}

Retain real additive measure $dx$, finite additive measures with
$\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb Z_p)=1$, and the original multiplicative
coordinates and measure
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK1}
 C_{\mathbb Q}=\mathbb R_{>0}\times K,\quad
 K=\prod_p\mathbb Z_p^\times,\quad
 dg=\frac{dy}{y}\,du,\quad \int_Kdu=1.
\end{equation}
The source test and its actual reduction are
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK2}
 \begin{gathered}
 \eta(x)=\pi x^2(\pi x^2-\tfrac32)e^{-\pi x^2},\qquad
 \xi(x_\infty,(x_p))=\eta(x_\infty)\prod_p1_{\mathbb Z_p}(x_p),\\
 \xi(0)=0,\quad \int_{\mathbb A_{\mathbb Q}}\xi=0,\quad
 h(y)=(E\xi)(y,u)=2\sum_{n\ge1}\eta(ny),\\
 V=E\mathcal S(\mathbb A_{\mathbb Q})_0,\qquad h\in V.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The factor two is the sum over both rational signs. Indeed the
finite factors allow exactly the rational integers; evenness of
$\eta$ gives the two signs. The separate Gaussian moments are
$\int x^2e^{-\pi x^2}dx=1/(2\pi)$ and
$\int x^4e^{-\pi x^2}dx=3/(4\pi^2)$, giving
$\int\eta=3/4-3/4=0$. These values follow by differentiating
$\int e^{-\pi t x^2}dx=t^{-1/2}$ with its original constants.
The Fourier transform with kernel $e^{-2\pi ixv}$ gives
\[
 \widehat{x^2e^{-\pi x^2}}=(1/(2\pi)-v^2)e^{-\pi v^2},\quad
 \widehat{x^4e^{-\pi x^2}}=
 (v^4-3v^2/\pi+3/(4\pi^2))e^{-\pi v^2}.
\]
Substituting their coefficients proves $\widehat\eta=\eta$,
including the cancellation of their constant terms. Finite
character orthogonality proves
$\widehat{1_{\mathbb Z_p}}=1_{\mathbb Z_p}$.
Periodizing the real Schwartz function and evaluating its
absolutely convergent Fourier series at zero therefore gives
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK3}
 h(y)=y^{-1}h(y^{-1}).
\end{equation}
The absent $n=0$ terms in this formula have value $\eta(0)=0$
on both sides, rather than an omitted nonzero endpoint.
This is also ETC7--9 with all widths equal to one.

The test is strictly positive everywhere. For $y\ge1$ every
summand in GTK2 is positive, because
$\pi n^2y^2>3/2$ for $n\ge1$; the first summand is nonzero.
For $0<y<1$, GTK3 is a positive factor times the already
proved positive value at $y^{-1}>1$. Thus
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK4}
 h(y)>0\quad(y>0).
\end{equation}
This assertion concerns this original theta-image test; it is
not positivity of the global Weil form.

For every logarithmic derivative, the series at $y\ge1$ is
bounded by a finite polynomial in $y$ times $e^{-\pi y^2}$.
To see this, differentiate its Gaussian terms, factor out
$e^{-\pi y^2}$, and bound the remaining series by a fixed
polynomial in $n$ times $e^{-\pi(n^2-1)}$. Reflection GTK3
gives the corresponding bounds at zero. In particular there
are positive constants $C_j,B_j,c_j$, for every $j$, such that
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK5}
 \left|\partial_u^j h(e^u)\right|
 \le C_j\exp(B_j|u|-c_j e^{2|u|}),\qquad u\in\mathbb R.
\end{equation}
Finite changes to $C_j$ cover the bounded interval around
$u=0$. All real weights and logarithmic derivatives are
therefore controlled.

\subsection{The original Mellin transform and both endpoint values}

Put
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK6}
 M(s)=\int_0^\infty h(y)y^s\frac{dy}{y}.
\end{equation}
GTK5 proves absolute convergence and local uniform convergence
of every $s$ derivative on $\mathbb C$, so $M$ is entire.
Changing $y$ to $1/y$ in GTK6 and using GTK3 proves
$M(s)=M(1-s)$. Reality of $h$ proves
$\overline{M(s)}=M(\bar s)$. For $\Re s>1$, absolute Fubini
on the original integer sum gives the full expression
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK7}
 \begin{split}
 M(s)=2\zeta(s)\left[
   \frac{\pi^2}{2\pi^{(s+4)/2}}\Gamma((s+4)/2)
  -\frac{3\pi}{4\pi^{(s+2)/2}}\Gamma((s+2)/2)\right].
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Indeed the absolute double integral is
$2\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-\Re s}\int_0^\infty|\eta(x)|
x^{\Re s-1}dx<\infty$, and substitution $\pi x^2=v$
integrates each Gaussian term separately. Equality to GTK6
specifies the entire continuation of the complete expression,
including every removable point of the separate summands.
The Gamma recurrence gives the exact comparison
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK8}
 2\left[
   \frac{\pi^2}{2\pi^{(s+4)/2}}\Gamma((s+4)/2)
  -\frac{3\pi}{4\pi^{(s+2)/2}}\Gamma((s+2)/2)\right]
 =\frac{s(s-1)}4\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2).
\end{equation}
GTK7 remains the working original-zeta formula. GTK8 records
its multiplier without replacing $\zeta$ by a completed function.
On $0<\Re s<1$ this multiplier is holomorphic and nowhere zero,
so every original nontrivial zero of $\zeta$ occurs in $M$
with the same multiplicity.

For completeness retain the whole Gaussian theta function,
including its zero term:
$\Theta(y)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}e^{-\pi n^2y^2}$.
Differentiating gives
$h(y)=\frac14(y^2\Theta'(y))'$ and
$h(y)/y=\frac14(y\Theta'(y)+\Theta(y))'$.
The theta reflection has leading term $1/y$ at zero and
constant term one at infinity, with exponentially small
remainders and all their derivatives. Hence
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK9}
 \begin{gathered}
 M(1)=\tfrac14[y^2\Theta'(y)]_0^\infty
        =\tfrac14[0-(-1)]=\tfrac14,\\
 M(0)=\tfrac14[y\Theta'(y)+\Theta(y)]_0^\infty
        =\tfrac14[1-0]=\tfrac14 .
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The calculation first integrates on a compact interval and
then uses these limits. It does not interchange the endpoint
integral with the integer sum: the absolute sum of degree
integrals is a positive constant times $\sum_{n\ge1}1/n$,
which diverges. Thus the actual additive zero moments in
GTK2 and the nonzero multiplicative endpoints GTK9 coexist
with their exact receiving maps.

\subsection{The full global correlation kernel}

For real $T$ retain the original modulated test
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK10}
 h_T(y)=y^{iT}h(y),\qquad
 h_T^\sharp(y)=y^{-1}\overline{h_T(y^{-1})}=h_T(y),\qquad

\end{equation}
The sharp equality follows by conjugating $y^{-iT}$ and

\begin{equation}\tag{GTK11}
 \begin{gathered}

   =y^{iS-1}\int_0^\infty
              x^{i(T-S)}h(x)h(x/y)\,dx,\\
 H_{T,S}(1)=\int_0^\infty
              x^{i(T-S)}h(x)^2\,dx,\qquad
 MH_{T,S}(s)=M(s+iT)\overline{M(1-\bar s+iS)}\\
 \hfill =M(s+iT)M(s+iS).
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
In the first line the factor $x/y$ from sharp multiplies the
original measure $dx/x$ to give $dx/y$. This explains every
power and the retained $dx$. The last equality follows from
both symmetries of $M$, not a change of Mellin convention.
All double integrals are absolute by GTK5. Since both input
tests are sharp-fixed and convolution is commutative,
$H_{T,S}=H_{S,T}$ and $H_{T,S}^\sharp=H_{T,S}$.
For $T=S$, GTK11 gives
$H_{T,T}(y)=y^{iT}H_{0,0}(y)$ and

Write the complete real-place functional, with parity

It equals the original real principal value for

\subsection{Strong topology and convergence of the entire prime sum}

Let $B_0$ be the radial part of the original test algebra, with
seminorms
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK14}
 p_{A,m,j}(f)=\sup_{u\in\mathbb R}
  e^{A|u|}(1+|u|)^m|\partial_u^jf(e^u)|,
             \qquad A,m,j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.
\end{equation}
These are the original strong weighted smooth seminorms of
SWQ3 at the trivial compact-unit level. Their inclusion of all
integer exponential weights is equivalent to retaining every
real weight. No real coordinate or test is replaced in its
convolution or pairing.
By Leibniz's formula,
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK15}
 p_{A,m,j}(h_T)\le
   \sum_{r=0}^j {j\choose r}|T|^{j-r}p_{A,m,r}(h).
\end{equation}
Each $T$ derivative inserts $(i\log y)^a$; the same proof
bounds every mixed derivative by a polynomial in $|T|$ with
finitely many fixed seminorms of $h$.

Convolution and sharp satisfy the explicit bounds from
the actual integral,
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK16}
 \begin{gathered}
 p_{A,m,j}(f*k)\le
       2p_{A+1,m,0}(f)p_{A,m,j}(k),\\
 p_{A,m,j}(k^\sharp)\le
       \sum_{r=0}^j {j\choose r}p_{A+1,m,r}(k).
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
To prove the first, write the logarithmic integral with variable
$v$, use $|u|\le|v|+|u-v|$ and
$1+|u|\le(1+|v|)(1+|u-v|)$, and integrate $e^{-|v|}$,
whose integral is exactly two. Derivatives can pass under that
integrable majorant. For sharp, differentiate
$e^{-u}\overline{k(e^{-u})}$, retaining the factor $e^{-u}$.
GTK15--16 prove polynomial bounds in $(T,S)$ for every
seminorm of $H_{T,S}$ and its parameter derivatives.

For every $H\in B_0$ and integer $A\ge2$,
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK17}
 \begin{split}
 &\sum_p\log p\sum_{j\ge1}
           (|H(p^j)|+p^{-j}|H(p^{-j})|)\\
 &\quad\le
 p_{A,0,0}(H)
 \sum_{n=2}^\infty\log n
       \left[\frac{n^{-A}}{1-n^{-A}}
                  +\frac{n^{-A-1}}{1-n^{-A-1}}\right]<\infty .
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The first bounds are $|H(p^j)|\le p^{-Aj}p_{A,0,0}(H)$
and $p^{-j}|H(p^{-j})|\le p^{-(A+1)j}p_{A,0,0}(H)$.
Summing their geometric series and then enlarging the prime
sum to all integers gives the displayed bound. The final
series converges since $A>1$. The same argument after
parameter differentiation proves uniform convergence of every
kernel derivative on compact parameter sets, and polynomial
bounds on the resulting all-prime functional.

The entire real functional GTK12 is also continuous on $B_0$.
At zero,
$|H(y)|\le p_{2,0,0}(H)y^2$ and
$|y^{-1}H(y^{-1})|\le p_{2,0,0}(H)y$.
The retained constant in its first numerator is integrable
on $(0,1/2)$. On $[1/2,1]$ the mean-value formula bounds
$|H(y)+y^{-1}H(y^{-1})-2H(1)|/(1-y)$ by the supremum
of the derivative of that smooth numerator. That derivative
is bounded by a fixed constant times
$p_{2,0,0}(H)+p_{2,0,1}(H)$. The denominator $1+y$ is
nonsingular. These estimates give a fixed finite-seminorm
bound for $\mathcal A_\epsilon(H)$ for either parity.
The two Mellin endpoint functionals are continuous too:
their absolute integrals are bounded by integrating
$e^{-(A-|s|)|u|}$ times $p_{A,0,0}(f)$ at $s=0,1$,
with $A>1$.
Consequently $Q$ is a continuous sesquilinear form on $B_0$,
and $K$ is smooth with at most polynomial growth, including
each of its derivatives. This proves convergence of its full

\subsection{An exact global synthesis of every compact radial test}

For $g\in C_c^\infty(0,\infty)$ define
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK18}
 b_g(u)=\frac{g(e^u)}{h(e^u)},\qquad
 a_g(T)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}b_g(u)e^{-iTu}\,du .
\end{equation}
Strict positivity GTK4 proves that this quotient is defined
everywhere and is smooth. Its support is compact because
$g(e^u)$ has compact support and the denominator never
vanishes. Integration by parts in $u$, with zero boundary
terms, proves that $a_g$ and each derivative decrease faster
than every power: $a_g$ is Schwartz. Fourier inversion,
with precisely the displayed $1/(2\pi)$, gives
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK19}
 g(y)=\int_{\mathbb R}a_g(T)y^{iT}h(y)\,dT
                 =\int_{\mathbb R}a_g(T)h_T(y)\,dT.
\end{equation}
For completeness the inversion may be obtained by inserting
$e^{-\varepsilon T^2}$ in the integral: its Fourier kernel
is the Gaussian approximate identity with total mass one.
Convolving this kernel with $b_g$ converges to $b_g$ and all
its derivatives. Since $a_g$ is integrable, dominated
convergence removes the inserted factor and proves GTK19.

The convergence is in every seminorm GTK14, not only
pointwise. In fact GTK15 gives the explicit tail bound
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK20}
 p_{A,m,j}\left(\int_{|T|>R}a_g(T)h_T\,dT\right)
 \le\sum_{r=0}^j {j\choose r}p_{A,m,r}(h)
           \int_{|T|>R}|a_g(T)||T|^{j-r}dT
        \longrightarrow0 .
\end{equation}
The integral on a bounded parameter interval is the limit
of Riemann sums in each seminorm, because its integrand is
continuous in those seminorms by GTK15. Completeness follows
directly by taking uniform limits of all weighted derivatives
and their compatibility on each compact $u$ interval.
This proves the specified vector integral and its strong
tail convergence without an unspecified weak topology.

Sesquilinearity, continuity, and the polynomial bounds just
proved give the complete reconstruction
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK21}

        a_g(T)\overline{a_k(S)}K(T,S)\,dT\,dS,
              \qquad g,k\in C_c^\infty(0,\infty).
\end{equation}
The double integral is absolutely convergent, since each
coefficient is Schwartz and $K$ has a finite polynomial
bound. First apply $Q$ to the bounded-interval Riemann sums
in GTK19, then pass to their strong limits using GTK16--17
and the real and endpoint estimates. The same absolute
majorants justify interchanging these parameter integrals
with every prime-power sum. At the real place the subtracted

\subsection{The full compact-unit sectors and their entire global kernels}

The radial kernel alone does not reconstruct a nontrivial
compact-unit character. To obtain the full original Bruhat
test algebra, retain every continuous character $\chi$ of
$K$ and define the single indexed family
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK22}
 F_{\chi,T}(y,u)=\chi(u)h_T(y),\qquad
 \mathbf K((\chi,T),(\psi,S))=Q(F_{\chi,T},F_{\psi,S}).
\end{equation}
Every continuous character factors through a finite
$U_N=(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^\times$: continuity gives an
open subgroup whose image lies in a circle arc containing
no nontrivial subgroup, hence in the character kernel;
a finite congruence subgroup lies inside it.
Let $c_\chi$ be its minimal conductor and $\chi_*$ the
corresponding primitive integer character, extended by
zero on integers not coprime to $c_\chi$. Keep
$\chi(-1)=(-1)^{\epsilon_\chi}$.

The original sharp fixes $F_{\chi,T}$:
$\overline{\chi(u^{-1})}=\chi(u)$ and GTK10 treat both
factors. Direct compact integration in convolution yields
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK23}
 F_{\chi,T}*F_{\psi,S}^{\sharp}
   =\delta_{\chi,\psi}\,\chi(u)H_{T,S}(y),\qquad
 \mathbf K((\chi,T),(\psi,S))
   =\delta_{\chi,\psi}K_\chi(T,S).
\end{equation}
The vanishing mixed integral is
$\int_K\chi(v)\overline{\psi(v)}du=0$ for $\chi\ne\psi$,
proved by translating it by a unit at which the quotient
character is not one. Its value is one when the characters
agree. No factor is lost by this character diagonalization.

 \begin{split}

   [\chi_*(p)^jH_{T,S}(p^j)
       +p^{-j}\chi_*(p)^{-j}H_{T,S}(p^{-j})].
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
This is WQP12 evaluated on the actual noncompact tests,
with extension justified by GTK14--17. It may also be
derived directly as follows. Endpoints integrate $\chi$
over $K$, giving precisely the indicator in the first

trivial. The local embedding of $p^j$ has real modulus

unit mass is $\log p$; for the negative shell the
denominator $|1-u|_p$ contributes $p^{-j}$.
These are the two original shell factors, with neither
orientation reversed.

Here is an independent calculation of the ramified unit
term. Write $a=v_p(c_\chi)>0$ and let $\vartheta$ be the
nontrivial local unit character. Its integral over
$B_r=1+p^r\mathbb Z_p$ is zero for $1\le r<a$ and is

Its integral over $\mathbb Z_p^\times$ is zero. The
canonical trivial-unit principal value is zero, so its
difference for $\vartheta$ is the convergent integral
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK25}
 \frac{\log p}{1-p^{-1}}
 \int_{\mathbb Z_p^\times}
         \frac{\vartheta(u)^{-1}-1}{|1-u|_p}\,du
                   =-a\log p .
\end{equation}
To calculate it, the shell outside $B_1$ contributes
$-1+p^{-1}$ for $a=1$. If $a>1$, that shell contributes
$-1+2p^{-1}$; each shell $B_r\setminus B_{r+1}$ for

factor $p^r$; the final shell $B_{a-1}\setminus B_a$
contributes $-1$. Their sum is exactly
$-a(1-p^{-1})$. Multiplication by the retained Haar
constant gives GTK25. Shells with $r\ge a$ vanish.
All nonunit shells have zero local-unit average because
their denominator and radial factor are constant on
units. This proves the local term $-a\log p\,H_{T,S}(1)$;
subtracting it in the global trace gives the positive
conductor sum in GTK24.
The trivial-unit principal value used here is exactly
the reference-character one in WLC6--9 and WQP12, not
an extra conductor convention.

At the real place the local negative sign embeds as
$(-1)_p$ in the compact coordinate, giving

\subsection{The exact spectral expression, all zeros, and the zero row}

For the trivial sector the source trace theorem gives
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK28}
 \begin{split}
 K(T,S)
 &=\sum_{\substack{\rho:\zeta(\rho)=0\\0<\Re\rho<1}}
       m_\rho M(\rho+iT)
                 \overline{M(1-\bar\rho+iS)}\\
 &=\sum_{\substack{\rho:\zeta(\rho)=0\\0<\Re\rho<1}}
       m_\rho M(\rho+iT)M(\rho+iS).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Every zero has its original multiplicity. The first
formula uses the original source Mellin transform and
sharp. The second uses the exact two identities after
GTK6. It does not replace a sum over possible off-line
zeros by a sum over zeros on the line.

Here is a direct convergence proof that does not use RH.
For $\sigma$ in a fixed bounded real interval, integration
by parts $r$ times in $u$ in
$M(\sigma+it)=\int h(e^u)e^{\sigma u}e^{itu}du$ yields
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK29}
 |M(\sigma+it)|\le C_{r,I}(1+|t|)^{-r},
                 \qquad \sigma\in I .
\end{equation}
The same estimate holds for every $s$ derivative because
it inserts a power of $u$ in the integral. All boundary
terms vanish by GTK5.
That bound also gives a direct polynomial zero-count
estimate for the zeros occurring in GTK28. GTK5 implies
$\log\max_{|s|\le R}|M(s)|\le C(R+1)\log(R+2)$.
Indeed integrate
$\exp((R+B)|u|-c e^{2|u|})$, substitute $v=e^{2|u|}$,
and bound $v^{(R+B)/2}e^{-cv/2}$ by its maximum;
the remaining exponential integral is finite. Jensen's
formula centered at zero, where $M(0)=1/4$, gives
$n_M(R)\log2\le\log\max_{|s|\le2R}|M(s)|-\log|M(0)|$.
If a boundary circle contains a zero, use slightly
larger radii and pass to the limit. Jensen's formula
itself follows by factoring the finitely many zeros in
the disk and applying the mean-value formula to the
logarithm of the remaining nonvanishing holomorphic
factor. Thus $n_M(R)=O(R\log(R+2))$. GTK8 shows that
the original zeta zeros counted here, with their
multiplicities, are included among these zeros of $M$.

Combining this count with GTK29 proves absolute and
locally uniform convergence of GTK28 and of every
$T,S$ derivative. It also proves a polynomial bound
for the sum of absolute values on the entire parameter
plane. For example let $R_0=2(|T|+|S|+2)$. Zeros with
$|\Im\rho|\le R_0$ contribute a bounded summand each
and number $O(R_0\log(R_0+2))$; for the remaining zeros
both shifted imaginary arguments have modulus at least
$|\Im\rho|/2$, and GTK29 with $r=3$ gives a summable
tail. This is enough to exchange the spectral sum
with the double Schwartz integral in GTK21.
For each $\rho$, its resulting factors are exactly
$Mg(\rho)$ and $\overline{Mk(1-\bar\rho)}$ by strong
synthesis and continuity of Mellin evaluation.
Thus GTK21 recovers the entire original spectral
pairing as well as every term of its explicit formula.

The kernel is real and symmetric. Symmetry follows
already from GTK10--11. For reality, conjugation of a
summand in the second line of GTK28 yields
$M(1-\bar\rho+iT)M(1-\bar\rho+iS)$.
The map $\rho\mapsto1-\bar\rho$ preserves the original
zero set and multiplicities, by the original functional
equation with its nonvanishing strip multiplier, also
encoded in GTK6--8. Absolute convergence permits that
reindexing, proving $\overline{K(T,S)}=K(T,S)$.
Hermitian symmetry follows. This is an exact reality
statement, not positive definiteness.

Every entry in its zero row vanishes:
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK30}
 K(0,S)=K(T,0)=0 .
\end{equation}
There are two exact proofs. In GTK28, $M(\rho)=0$
with each original zero by GTK7--8. On the actual
source quotient, $h\in V$ and $V$ is a convolution
ideal; thus $h*h_S^\sharp\in V$ and its induced
operator is zero by the author's \texttt{vanishV}.
The ideal and sharp maps, including endpoint terms,
are proved in SWQ7--15, so this second argument uses
the actual source image rather than only its zero
values. GTK13 at $T=0$ is consequently the exact
global identity
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK31}
 \begin{split}
 \tfrac14\overline{M(1+iS)}
       +\tfrac14\overline{M(iS)}
 ={}&(\log1)H_{0,S}(1)+\mathcal A_0(H_{0,S})\\
 &+\sum_p\log p\sum_{j\ge1}
       [H_{0,S}(p^j)+p^{-j}H_{0,S}(p^{-j})].
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Both endpoint constants and the complete prime and
real contributions remain in this vanishing identity.
No zero-row assertion for nontrivial $\chi$ is inferred:
the modulation seed in those sectors is not the
original adelic image element GTK2.

\subsection{The calculated receiving defect under modulation}

The concurrent global estimate GWM11--14 applies to
this very $h$ by GTK5, not merely to compact tests.
Its constants specialize exactly to
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK32}
 \begin{gathered}
 M_h=\int_0^\infty h(x)^2dx>0,\qquad
 K_h=\int_0^\infty|xh'(x)+\tfrac12h(x)|^2dx,\\
 A_{0,h}=M(0)=\tfrac14,\qquad
 A_{1,h}=M(1)=\tfrac14,\qquad
 P_h=2\sum_p\log p\sum_{j\ge1}|H_{0,0}(p^j)|<\infty .
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The equalities for the absolute endpoint integrals
use the proved positivity of $h$. The prime constant
retains the entire sum; GTK17 proves its finiteness.
With $\alpha=1/4$, conductor one and differential
modulus one, the full bound reads
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK33}
 \begin{split}
 K(T,T)\ge{}&
 M_h[\log(|T|/4)-4-\log\pi+\log1-\log1]\\
 &-\frac{4K_h}{T^2}\log|T|-P_h-\tfrac18 ,
                           \qquad |T|\ge1 .
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
For avoidance of an assumed missing estimate, the
underlying argument is as follows. The entire real
term GTK12 equals
$-\frac1{2\pi}\int |Mh_T(1/2+it)|^2
[\Re\psi(1/4+it/2)-\log\pi]dt$, with its sign as
in GWM7. The identity follows by Fourier inversion
of GTK11 on the critical vertical line and the
convergent digamma integral, retaining
$\log(2\pi)+\gamma-\log2-\gamma$ in that calculation.
The digamma series and comparison with its integral
give, for $\alpha>0$,
$\Re\psi(\alpha+ib)\ge
\frac12\log(\alpha^2+b^2)-1/\alpha$:
the error between the series
$(k+\alpha)/((k+\alpha)^2+b^2)$ and its integral
is bounded by its total variation, at most $1/\alpha$.
Set $u=t+T$ and use this bound separately on
$|u|\le|T|/2$ and its complement. Parseval gives the
complement mass at most $4K_h/T^2$, with its original
$dt/(2\pi)$ factor. The difference of the two
digamma lower bounds is $\log|T|$ here.
Every paired prime term has absolute value at most
$2|H_{0,0}(p^j)|$, and the two endpoints together
are at least $-2(1/4)(1/4)=-1/8$.
Combining these proves GTK33 on the full global form.

An explicit finite tail threshold is
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK34}
 T_h=\max\left\{1,\
 4\exp\left[
  1+4+\log\pi-\log1+\log1
  +\frac{P_h+1/8}{M_h}
  +\frac{2K_h}{\exp(1)M_h}\right]\right\}.
\end{equation}
For $t\ge1$, $\log t/t^2\le1/(2\exp(1))$,
by differentiation with its maximum at $\sqrt{\exp(1)}$.
Substituting this inequality and GTK34 in GTK33 proves
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK35}
 K(T,T)\ge M_h>0\qquad(|T|\ge T_h).
\end{equation}
This is a theorem about the entire specified modulation
tail and its actual seed. It leaves no uncomputed
prime window in the bound, although its exact constant
contains the convergent original prime series.

On the original algebra, $\Delta_{iT}f=|\,\cdot\,|^{iT}f$
is an invertible linear convolution-algebra map, with
inverse $\Delta_{-iT}$, because
$|ab|^{iT}=|a|^{iT}|b|^{iT}$ in the convolution
integral. It commutes with sharp by direct conjugation.
Let $W=\overline V$ in the topology SWQ3. The trace
vanishes on the entire closed ideal $W$, and therefore
on its pairing with every test, by SWQ15 and SWQ22--24.
GTK30 and GTK35 now prove the concrete non-descent
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK36}
 h\in V\subset W,\qquad
 \Delta_{iT}h=h_T\notin W
                         \quad(|T|\ge T_h).
\end{equation}
If $h_T$ belonged to $W$, its pairing with itself
would be zero, contradicting GTK35. Consequently
$\Delta_{iT}$ does not descend to an endomorphism
of this original spectral quotient for those $T$.
This proves the receiving defect on a whole
frequency range. It does not assert that every
nonzero frequency has that defect.

\subsection{The mixed divided-difference receiver at the entire zero row}

Retain the original kernel $K$, including its
vanishing axes, and define the actual functions
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK36a}
 w_T(x)=
 \begin{cases}
   \displaystyle\frac{x^{iT}-1}{iT}\,h(x),&T\ne0,\\[4pt]
   (\log x)h(x),&T=0.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
This is a strongly continuous family in $B_0$.
Indeed its exact integral formula is
$w_T(x)=(\log x)h(x)\int_0^1x^{i\theta T}d\theta$.
GTK5 and differentiation prove convergence
in every seminorm as $T\to0$, and prove all
parameter derivatives as well. For real $T$
one has $w_T^\sharp=-w_T$: the coefficient
$1/(iT)$ conjugates to its negative while
GTK10 fixes the numerator. At zero the same
identity follows directly from
$\log(x^{-1})=-\log x$ and GTK3.
Sesquilinearity and GTK30 consequently give
the exact mixed global receiver
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK36b}
 \begin{gathered}
 \mathcal R(T,S):=Q(w_T,w_S)
            =\frac{K(T,S)}{TS}\quad(TS\ne0),\\
 q(h_T)=iT\,q(w_T),\qquad
 q:B\longrightarrow B/W .
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The scalar in the first variable is $1/(iT)$
and the conjugated scalar in the second is
$\overline{1/(iS)}$; their product is
$1/(TS)$. This accounts for its sign.
The quotient identity uses $q(h)=0$ and
retains the original factor $iT$.
The axes of $\mathcal R$ are defined by
the actual tests in GTK36a and strong
continuity, rather than being discarded.

These axes in fact have an entire two-variable
extension. For complex $z$ define
$h_z(x)=x^{iz}h(x)$ and extend $w_z$
by the same integral formula. On each compact
set of $z$, GTK5 controls the additional
factor $e^{-\Im z\log x}$ and every power of
$\log x$. The exponential power series
therefore converges in every strong seminorm,
proving that both families are entire
$B_0$-valued functions. Define the complex
bilinear extension, with the trace functional
$\mathcal T$ on the original quotient,
\begin{equation}\tag{GTK36c}
 \begin{gathered}
 \mathcal K(z,w)=\mathcal T(h_z*h_w),\qquad
 \mathcal K(z,0)=\mathcal K(0,w)=0,\\
 \mathcal K(z,w)=zw\,\mathcal R_{\mathbb C}(z,w),
 \qquad
 \mathcal R_{\mathbb C}(z,w)=-\mathcal T(w_z*w_w).
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The zero axes follow because $h\in V$ and
$V$ is the actual convolution ideal.
Expanding
$(h_z-h)*(h_w-h)=(iz)(iw)w_z*w_w$
and applying the trace proves the factor
$-zw$ and the displayed identity everywhere.
For real arguments $h_S^\sharp=h_S$ and
$w_S^\sharp=-w_S$, so

$\mathcal R_{\mathbb C}(T,S)=\mathcal R(T,S)$.
This is an entire bilinear continuation of
the real-parameter kernel; it is not a

holomorphic in its second input.

Termwise differentiation in GTK28 is justified

\begin{equation}\tag{GTK36d}
 \begin{gathered}
 \mathcal R(0,0)
   =Q((\log x)h,(\log x)h)
   =-\sum_{\substack{\rho:\zeta(\rho)=0\\0<\Re\rho<1}}
                                      m_\rho[M'(\rho)]^2,\\
 \left.\frac{d^2}{dT^2}K(T,T)\right|_{T=0}
                       =2\mathcal R(0,0).
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
There is no absolute-value square in this
formula: the product is the square of the
complex derivative, paired over the entire

real because it equals the actual Hermitian
quadratic value. Directly,
$M'(1-s)=-M'(s)$ and conjugation of $M'$

the second derivative of the exact

To keep the original arithmetic multiplier

 M'(s)={}&2\zeta'(s)B(s)
   +2\zeta(s)\left[
       -\frac{\log\pi}{2}B(s)

Differentiating each original factor in
