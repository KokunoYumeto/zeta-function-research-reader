\subsection{The exact defect and all its ordinary root jets}

We shall use the following literal norms, with $b>0$:
\begin{equation}\tag{HAD3}
\begin{gathered}
 \left\|\prod_{j=1}^kf_j\right\|_h
        \le\prod_{j=1}^k\|f_j\|_{\sqrt k h},\qquad
 \|D^nf\|_h\le\sqrt{n!}\,b^{-n}\|f\|_{\sqrt{h^2+b^2}},\\
 \sum_{n\ge0}\frac{|t|^n}{n!}\|D^{2n}f\|_h
          \le\|f\|_{h+\sqrt{2|t|}},\qquad
 \|H_0\|_h\le\sqrt2 I_0(h^2,0).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
For the product bound, the multinomial coefficient
$(n_1+\cdots+n_k)!/(n_1!\cdots n_k!)$ is at most
$k^{n_1+\cdots+n_k}$. Taking square roots and summing
the nonnegative coefficient bound proves the assertion.
For the derivative use the single binomial term
$\binom qn h^{2(q-n)}b^{2n}\le(h^2+b^2)^q$ on each
original coefficient $a_q$.
For heat, after setting $q=i+2n$, the inner coefficient
$\sqrt{q!}/(n!\sqrt{(q-2n)!})$ is
$\sqrt{\binom q{2n}}\sqrt{\binom{2n}n}\le\binom q{2n}2^n$.
Its sum is bounded by $(h+\sqrt{2|t|})^q$.
These are positive-sum arguments, so they prove absolute
convergence before any reordering. Finally
$\sum_{n\ge0}x^n/\sqrt{n!}\le\sqrt2e^{x^2}$ by
Cauchy--Schwarz with weights $2^{-n/2}$. Integrating the
cosine coefficient subseries proves the last estimate.

For integers $k\ge2$, define the two actual entire functions
and their amplitude difference by
\begin{equation}\tag{HAD4}
 O_k(t)=H_t^k,\qquad P_k(t)=T_t(H_0^k)=H_t^{\star_t k},
 \qquad \Delta_k(t)=O_k(t)-P_k(t).
\end{equation}
All belong to $\mathcal E$ by HAD3. Their difference has the
exact, convergent integral formula
\begin{equation}\tag{HAD5}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \Delta_k(t)=k(k-1)t\int_0^1
 T_{(1-\theta)t}\!\left(H_{\theta t}^{k-2}(H'_{\theta t})^2\right)
                                                   d\theta.}
\end{equation}
Differentiate $T_{t-s}(H_s^k)$ along the segment $s=\theta t$.
The heat equation and ordinary Leibniz rule give
\[
 \frac{d}{ds}\bigl[T_{t-s}(H_s^k)\bigr]
 =T_{t-s}\left[D^2(H_s^k)-kH_s^{k-1}H_s''\right]
 =k(k-1)T_{t-s}\left[H_s^{k-2}(H_s')^2\right].
\]
The heat series is entire in time in every coefficient norm;
HAD3 proves continuity of products and derivatives there.
Thus this is a derivative in the complete space $\mathcal E$.
Integrating its continuous derivative from $0$ to $t$
gives HAD5. Equivalently the integral is the limit of
Riemann sums in each weighted $\ell^1$ norm; their limits
have the same coefficients, so define one element of
$\mathcal E$. There is also the explicit bound, for every
$h,b>0$,
\begin{equation}\tag{HAD6}
\begin{gathered}
 R=\sqrt k h+\sqrt{2(k+1)|t|},\qquad B=\sqrt{R^2+b^2},\\
 \|\Delta_k(t)\|_h
 \le k(k-1)|t|b^{-2}\|H_0\|_B^k
 \le k(k-1)|t|b^{-2}\bigl(\sqrt2\mathcal M_0(B^2,0)\bigr)^k.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
At parameter $\theta$, the outer heat first enlarges the
norm radius to $h+\sqrt{2(1-\theta)|t|}$. The product
then enlarges it by $\sqrt k$. Commuting each derivative
with the inner heat enlarges this radius again by
$\sqrt{2\theta|t|}$. Its resulting value is at most $R$,
because $\sqrt{k(1-\theta)}+\sqrt\theta\le\sqrt{k+1}$.
Apply the derivative estimate with parameter $b$ to the
two differentiated factors and increase every input
radius to $B$. This bounds the integrand by
$b^{-2}\|H_0\|_B^k$ and proves HAD6. These bounds are
uniform on compact time sets; no finite-degree heat
intertwining has been assumed.

Let $Z_t=\{\zeta\in\mathbb C:H_t(\zeta)=0\}$ be the
actual zero set, without any assertion that its off-real
part is empty or nonempty. Since $T_t$ is invertible and
$H_0(0)>0$, $H_t$ is not the zero function. Every member
of $Z_t$ consequently has a finite order $m\ge1$.
For every $q\ge0$ its exact ordinary derivative defect is
\begin{equation}\tag{HAD7}
 \Delta_k^{(q)}(t,\zeta)=
 q!\!\sum_{\substack{n_1+\cdots+n_k=q\\n_1,\ldots,n_k\ge m}}
       \prod_{j=1}^k\frac{H_t^{(n_j)}(\zeta)}{n_j!}
                              -P_k^{(q)}(t,\zeta).
\end{equation}
This is the finite Taylor-product rule; all terms with an
index below $m$ vanish. In particular
\begin{equation}\tag{HAD8}
\begin{gathered}
 \Delta_k^{(q)}(t,\zeta)=-P_k^{(q)}(t,\zeta)\quad(0\le q<km),\\
 \Delta_k^{(km)}(t,\zeta)
   =\frac{(km)!}{(m!)^k}\bigl(H_t^{(m)}(\zeta)\bigr)^k
                                      -P_k^{(km)}(t,\zeta).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Thus the entire defect class modulo $(z-\zeta)^{km}$ is
the negative class of the actual transported power.
The equation is a computed finite jet, not an equality
between ordinary and transported Taylor characters.

For $k=2$ the complete root calculation is particularly
explicit. HTP10 and continuity of $D^q$ in HAD3 give
\begin{equation}\tag{HAD9}
\begin{gathered}
 P_2(t,\zeta)=\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}
       \frac{(-2t)^n}{n!}\bigl(H_t^{(n)}(\zeta)\bigr)^2,\\
 P_2^{(q)}(t,\zeta)=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{(-2t)^n}{n!}
       \sum_{j=0}^q\binom qj
            H_t^{(n+j)}(\zeta)H_t^{(n+q-j)}(\zeta).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The undifferentiated product series is absolutely convergent
in every norm by HTP7. Apply HAD3 to the sum of its term
norms at the enlarged radius to prove absolute convergence
after $D^q$ as well. Evaluation is continuous, since
$|f(\zeta)|\le e^{|\zeta|^2/(2h^2)}\|f\|_h$.
Hence every sum and derivative in HAD9 is justified.
For the absolute sum after expanding its finite Leibniz
rule, choose $s>0$ with $s^2>2|t|$ and put $B=\sqrt{2h^2+s^2}$.
HAD3 and
$\sqrt{(n+j)!(n+q-j)!}/n!\le(n+q)^{q/2}$
bound its sum of term norms by
\[
 2^qs^{-q}\|H_t\|_B^2
       \sum_{n\ge0}\left(\frac{2|t|}{s^2}\right)^n
                                (n+q)^{q/2}<\infty.
\]
The factor at $n=q=0$ is $1$. The factorial bound follows
by writing its square as the product of the $j$ factors
$n+1,\ldots,n+j$ and the $q-j$ factors
$n+1,\ldots,n+q-j$, each at most $n+q$.

The first surviving root jet also gives a quantified local
calculation in the contraction parameter. Fix the actual
function $f=H_t$ and its root $\zeta$ of order $m$, but
allow the product parameter $r$ to vary. Then
\begin{equation}\tag{HAD10}
 (f\star_rf)(\zeta)=\frac{(-2r)^m}{m!}
                   \bigl(f^{(m)}(\zeta)\bigr)^2+\mathcal R_m(r).
\end{equation}
For any $h,b>0$, put
\[
 C=e^{|\zeta|^2/(2h^2)}\|f\|_{\sqrt{2h^2+b^2}}^2,
 \quad L=\frac{b^{2m}|f^{(m)}(\zeta)|^2}{m!}>0,
 \quad q=\frac{2|r|}{b^2}.
\]
The geometric tail of HTP7 gives the completely explicit
bound and consequence
\begin{equation}\tag{HAD11}
 |\mathcal R_m(r)|\le\frac{Cq^{m+1}}{1-q}\quad(q<1),\qquad
 |(f\star_rf)(\zeta)|\ge\frac L2q^m>0
 \quad\left(0<q\le\min\left(\frac12,\frac L{4C}\right)\right).
\end{equation}
The leading term has absolute value $Lq^m$. Under the
displayed bound on $q$, the remainder is at most
$2Cq^{m+1}\le Lq^m/2$. This proves the consequence.
The constants are determined by the actual root jet and
original coefficient norm. Setting $r=t$ recovers HAD9;
the exact remaining quantity is its displayed convergent
tail, which is not dropped when $t$ lies outside that
computed neighbourhood. At $t=0$ the original defect is
identically zero, directly from HAD4.

\subsection{The actual Gaussian comparison and the complex root Gram matrix}

For $a>0$, let
$d\gamma_a(y)=(4\pi a)^{-1/2}e^{-y^2/(4a)}dy$ on
$\mathbb R$. The following formula holds for every
$f\in\mathcal E$, with absolute convergence in every
coefficient norm:
\begin{equation}\tag{HAD12}
 T_{-a}f(z)=\int_{\mathbb R}f(z+y)\,d\gamma_a(y),\qquad
 \int_{\mathbb R}\|f(\cdot+y)\|_h\,d\gamma_a(y)
 \le\frac{\|f\|_{\sqrt{h^2+b^2}}}{\sqrt{1-2a/b^2}}
                       \quad(b^2>2a).
\end{equation}
Here is a direct norm proof. The absolute Taylor-translation
sum satisfies
\[
 \sum_{n\ge0}\frac{|y|^n}{n!}\|D^nf\|_h
 \le e^{|y|^2/(2b^2)}\|f\|_{\sqrt{h^2+b^2}}.
\]
For the coefficient $a_j$, its inner sum after extracting
$|a_j|\sqrt{j!}$ is
$\sum_{n=0}^j\sqrt{\binom jn}h^{j-n}|y|^n/\sqrt{n!}$.
Cauchy--Schwarz, placing $b^n$ in the binomial factor,
bounds it by $(h^2+b^2)^{j/2}e^{|y|^2/(2b^2)}$.
Summing proves the translation estimate; its Gaussian
integral is $(1-2a/b^2)^{-1/2}$.
The translation series equals $f(z+y)$ first for
polynomials and then by polynomial density and these
bounds. Gaussian integration of a polynomial gives
$T_{-a}f$, since its odd moments vanish and
$\int y^{2n}d\gamma_a=(2n)!a^n/n!$.
These moments follow by differentiating the Gaussian
integral or its elementary integration-by-parts recurrence.
Both sides are continuous in the displayed norms, so
polynomial approximation proves HAD12 on all of $\mathcal E$.

For every real $x$, every $a>0$ and every even $k\ge2$,
the actual sign is strict:
\begin{equation}\tag{HAD13}
             \Delta_k(-a,x)<0.
\end{equation}
Under $\gamma_a$, the real random variable $H_0(x+y)$ has
mean $H_{-a}(x)$ by HAD12. It is not almost surely
constant: continuity and the full support of the Gaussian
would make it constant on $\mathbb R$, contradicting
$H_0''(0)=-m_2<0$. For even $k$, $v\mapsto v^k$ is
strictly convex on $\mathbb R$, since its derivative
$kv^{k-1}$ is strictly increasing. Its tangent line at the
mean lies strictly below its graph away from that mean;
integrating this elementary tangent inequality gives
$\int H_0(x+y)^k d\gamma_a>H_{-a}(x)^k$.
All moments are integrable by HAD12 applied to $H_0^k$,
or by its absolute bound. This proves HAD13, including
at every actual real root. No sign for a complex square
has been inferred from this real inequality.

Now fix $t=-a<0$ and any complex point $z$. Define the
actual functions and sequences
\begin{equation}\tag{HAD14}
\begin{gathered}
 F_z(y)=H_0(z+y)\in L^2(\gamma_a),\qquad
 c_n(z)=\sqrt{\frac{(2a)^n}{n!}}\,H_{-a}^{(n)}(z),\\
 P(z)=P_2(-a,z)=\int F_z(y)^2\,d\gamma_a(y)
                         =\sum_{n\ge0}c_n(z)^2,\\
 Q(z)=\int|F_z(y)|^2\,d\gamma_a(y)
                         =\sum_{n\ge0}|c_n(z)|^2>0.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The bilinear identity is HAD9 or HTP10. For the Hermitian
identity set $f_z(w)=H_0(z+w)$ and $g_z(w)=H_0(\bar z+w)$.
The exact transported-product identity is
\[
 T_{-a}(f_zg_z)
       =(T_{-a}f_z)\star_{-a}(T_{-a}g_z).
\]
Apply HTP10 to the two inputs $T_{-a}f_z$ and $T_{-a}g_z$,
which equal $H_{-a}(z+w)$ and $H_{-a}(\bar z+w)$,
and then evaluate at $w=0$. Translation preserves
$\mathcal E$ by the estimate in the proof of HAD12.
It commutes with the heat operators, first on polynomials
and then by continuity. Also
$H_{-a}^{(n)}(\bar z)=\overline{H_{-a}^{(n)}(z)}$,
because the original kernel is real. HAD12 turns the
left side into $\int|F_z|^2d\gamma_a$; HTP7 justifies
the absolute derivative sum on the right.
The function $F_z$ cannot vanish almost everywhere:
continuity would force $H_0$ to vanish on a line, and
the identity theorem would contradict $H_0(0)>0$.
Hence $Q(z)>0$. In fact the original integral gives
\begin{equation}\tag{HAD15}
 Q(z)\le I_0(0,|\operatorname{Im}z|)^2,
 \qquad |P(z)|\le Q(z).
\end{equation}
Indeed $|H_0(z+y)|\le I_0(0,|\operatorname{Im}z|)$
uniformly in real $y$, and the Gaussian has mass one.
The second inequality is the triangle inequality for
the absolutely convergent squares in HAD14.

At an actual root $\zeta\in Z_{-a}$ of order $m$, the
first $m$ coordinates $c_0,\ldots,c_{m-1}$ vanish, and
\begin{equation}\tag{HAD16}
\begin{gathered}
 \int F_\zeta\,d\gamma_a=0,\qquad
 Q(\zeta)\ge\frac{(2a)^m}{m!}
                         |H_{-a}^{(m)}(\zeta)|^2>0,\\
 \Delta_2(-a,\zeta)=-P(\zeta),\qquad
 \zeta\in\mathbb R\ \Longrightarrow\ P(\zeta)=Q(\zeta)>0.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
For a real point all derivatives are real, so the last
equality follows term by term. The original positive
kernel also excludes every purely imaginary root:
$H_{-a}(ib)=\int e^{-au^2}\Phi(u)\cosh(bu)du>0$
for all real $b$.

At every nonreal point the complex comparison is strictly
different from equality:
\begin{equation}\tag{HAD17}
             |P(z)|<Q(z)\qquad(\operatorname{Im}z\ne0).
\end{equation}
We prove the equality obstruction exactly. If
$|\sum c_n^2|=\sum|c_n|^2$, its nonzero sum has a phase
$\eta$ of modulus one. The nonnegative differences
$|c_n|^2-\operatorname{Re}(\bar\eta c_n^2)$ have sum
zero, so each vanishes. This means
$c_n=\eta\overline{c_n}$ for every $n$, including the
zero coordinates. The same relation holds for every
derivative of $H_{-a}$ at $z$. By its entire Taylor
series and its real coefficients,
\[
 H_{-a}(z+w)=\eta H_{-a}(\bar z+w)
 \quad(w\in\mathbb C),\qquad
 H_{-a}(v+2i\operatorname{Im}z)=\eta H_{-a}(v).
\]
If $b=\operatorname{Im}z\ne0$, setting $v=0$ contradicts
\[
 H_{-a}(2ib)=\int_0^\infty e^{-au^2}\Phi(u)\cosh(2bu)du
                         > H_{-a}(0)>0.
\]
The strict inequality holds because $\cosh(2bu)>1$ on
$u>0$ and the kernel is positive there. The claimed
unit phase would give equal absolute values. This proves
HAD17. In particular it does not assign a sign to $P$
at a complex root and does not claim that $P$ cannot
vanish there.

The remaining root quantity is a concrete positive Gram
matrix, with every entry evaluated from the original
theta function. Write $u(z)=\operatorname{Re}(c_n(z))_n$
and $v(z)=\operatorname{Im}(c_n(z))_n$ in real $\ell^2$,
and put
\begin{equation}\tag{HAD18}
\begin{gathered}
 \mathsf G_a(z)=
 \begin{pmatrix}\|u(z)\|^2&\langle u(z),v(z)\rangle\\
                 \langle u(z),v(z)\rangle&\|v(z)\|^2\end{pmatrix}
 =\int_{\mathbb R}
 \begin{pmatrix}(\operatorname{Re}F_z)^2&
        \operatorname{Re}F_z\operatorname{Im}F_z\\
        \operatorname{Re}F_z\operatorname{Im}F_z&
        (\operatorname{Im}F_z)^2\end{pmatrix}d\gamma_a,\\
 \operatorname{tr}\mathsf G_a=Q,\qquad
 P=(\mathsf G_a)_{11}-(\mathsf G_a)_{22}
                               +2i(\mathsf G_a)_{12},\\
 Q^2-|P|^2=4\det\mathsf G_a,\qquad
 P=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \mathsf G_a=(Q/2)I_2.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The equality of the two matrix descriptions follows
from their equal trace $Q$ and complex traceless
coordinate $P$, already proved in HAD14. Expanding
these entries proves the determinant identity and
the final equivalence. At a nonreal point HAD17 gives
$\det\mathsf G_a>0$, so this matrix is positive definite.
At a root it is the centred covariance matrix, because
both real and imaginary means in HAD16 vanish.
Thus the exact double-power coincidence set on actual
nonreal roots is the inverse image
\begin{equation}\tag{HAD19}
 \mathscr C_a=
 (\mathsf G_a|_{Z_{-a}\setminus\mathbb R})^{-1}
                  (\{cI_2:c>0\}).
\end{equation}
This is one specified map and fibre of the original
function, rather than an arbitrary matrix model.
It retains the positive trace, original moments and
all derivatives. HAD17 proves positive definiteness
on this domain; it does not prove that this scalar
matrix fibre is empty or nonempty.

\subsection{The same comparison with every original support flag}

Let $L$ be the full bounded distributive lattice in SHP,
and let $\mathcal A_L$ be its actual coefficient-flag
algebra. At real time take any member of the complete
theta fibre
\begin{equation}\tag{HAD20}
 \mathbf H_t=(H_t,\nu),\qquad
 \nu_{2j}=1_L,\quad
 \nu_1\ge\nu_3\ge\nu_5\ge\cdots,
 \qquad \omega=\nu_1.
\end{equation}
The cosine lift has zero odd flag; the globally
constant-label lift has top odd flag. Neither is
substituted for the complete fibre above.
The exact closed support product is
$ (\nu\circledast\mu)_0=(\nu_0\wedge\mu_0)
 \vee(\nu_1\wedge\mu_1)$ and
$ (\nu\circledast\mu)_n=\bigvee_{r=0}^n
                \nu_r\wedge\mu_{n-r}$ for $n\ge1$,
as proved in SHP5. For every $k\ge2$, its evaluated
power on the actual fibre is
\begin{equation}\tag{HAD21}
 \kappa=\nu^{\circledast k},\qquad
             \kappa_{2j}=1_L,\qquad\kappa_{2j+1}=\omega.
\end{equation}
For two factors every even coefficient contains an
even-even top term. Every odd term has one odd
factor at most $\omega$, while indices $2j$ and $1$
attain $\omega$ at degree $2j+1$. This includes
degree one; degree zero is top by its even-even
term. Further multiplication by $\nu$ leaves this
same flag, by the same finite formula. This proves
HAD21 without requiring any infinite joins in $L$.

Both powers and their exact supported difference are
\begin{equation}\tag{HAD22}
\begin{gathered}
 \mathbf O_k(t)=\mathbf H_t^{\circ k}=(O_k(t),\kappa),\qquad
 \mathbf P_k(t)=\mathbf H_t^{\star_t k}=(P_k(t),\kappa),\\
 \mathbf D_k(t)=\mathbf O_k(t)+\widehat{-1}\,\mathbf P_k(t)
                           =(\Delta_k(t),\kappa).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The heat group preserves every label, and the two
products have the same support product by SHP11.
Multiplication by $\widehat{-1}$ changes the amplitude
sign and preserves the flag. Addition joins the
equal flags, proving the last equality. A vanishing
amplitude difference therefore gives $(0,\kappa)$,
not the global zero flag. Since the two powers have
the same flag, their equality is nevertheless
equivalent to equality of their entire amplitudes.

The complete original coefficient map is retained:
\begin{equation}\tag{HAD23}
\begin{gathered}
 \boldsymbol\Phi(f,\lambda)=
     \bigl(f,(\iota_L(\lambda_n))_{n\ge0}\bigr),\qquad
 \iota_L(\lambda_n)=1_K\otimes\lambda_n,\\
 \boldsymbol\Phi(\mathbf D_k)=
          \bigl(\Delta_k,(\iota_L(\kappa_n))_{n\ge0}\bigr),\\
 \operatorname{ev}^{\mathrm{amp}}_z(f,\lambda)
       =(f(z),\lambda_0\vee\lambda_1),\qquad
 \operatorname{ev}^{\mathrm{amp}}_\zeta(\mathbf D_k)
       =(\Delta_k(t,\zeta),1_L),\qquad
 \Phi_{\mathbb C}\operatorname{ev}^{\mathrm{amp}}_\zeta(\mathbf D_k)
       =(\Delta_k(t,\zeta),1_{K_L}).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The support map is injective: the tropical zero/nonzero
homomorphism $K\to\mathbb B$, tensored with $L$,
retracts $\iota_L$. Thus equality in the complete
coefficient image recovers each original support
label and the amplitude. The evaluation used here
is the ordinary amplitude evaluation, multiplicative
for $\circ$; it is not treated as a character for
$\star_t$. At a vanishing root defect its displayed
value is the supported zero $e=(0,1_L)$.
The actual homomorphism into the ordinary-product
global carrier is instead SHP26's
\begin{equation}\tag{HAD24}
