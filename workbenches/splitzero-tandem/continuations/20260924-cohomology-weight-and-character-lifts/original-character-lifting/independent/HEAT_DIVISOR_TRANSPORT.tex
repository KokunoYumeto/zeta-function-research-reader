\subsection{Which divisor is carried by the heat algebra isomorphism}

Retain the whole function space
\begin{equation}\tag{HDT1}
 \mathcal E=\left\{f(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nz^n:
       \sum_{n\ge0}|a_n|\sqrt{n!}\,h^n<\infty
                         \text{ for every }h>0\right\}.
\end{equation}
The ordinary product and the transported product are respectively
the Cauchy product and
\begin{equation}\tag{HDT2}
 f\star_t g=T_t\bigl((T_{-t}f)(T_{-t}g)\bigr),\qquad
 T_t:(\mathcal E,\cdot)\xrightarrow{\sim}(\mathcal E,\star_t).
\end{equation}
The complete convergence and multiplication proofs are in HTP.
In particular $T_t1=1$, $T_{-t}T_t=I$, and this is an actual
unital algebra isomorphism, whereas $T_t$ is generally not an
automorphism for the unchanged ordinary product.

Set $\Omega=\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb R$ and
$A=\mathcal O(\Omega)$ as in HZD7. The exact ring map into the
original off-real function ring is
\begin{equation}\tag{HDT3}
 \rho_t:(\mathcal E,\star_t)\longrightarrow A,
       \qquad \rho_t(f)=(T_{-t}f)|_\Omega.
\end{equation}
It preserves products by (HDT2), sums by linearity and the unit
because $T_{-t}1=1$. It is injective: if its value vanishes, the
entire function $T_{-t}f$ vanishes on a nonempty open set, so all
its analytic continuation and hence $f$ vanish. Most importantly,
the actual heat identity proved in THS13 gives
\begin{equation}\tag{HDT4}
 \rho_t(H_t)=H_0|_\Omega.
\end{equation}
This specifies the divisor transported by the algebra isomorphism.
Base change of its principal quotient along (HDT3) is exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{HDT5}
 A\otimes_{(\mathcal E,\star_t)}
       \bigl((\mathcal E,\star_t)/(H_t)_{\star_t}\bigr)
          \simeq A/(H_0|_\Omega).
\end{equation}
To prove the universal property directly, a pair of agreeing
unital ring maps from $A$ and the displayed quotient into a
ring $B$ is precisely a unital map from $A$ that sends
$\rho_t(H_t)=H_0|_\Omega$ to zero. Such maps factor uniquely
through the right side. Conversely every map from that right
side gives this agreeing pair. These two operations are inverse,
proving (HDT5) and its explicit quotient map.

The fully supported version imposes the supported equation, with
every label unchanged:
\begin{equation}\tag{HDT6}
\begin{CD}
 G_L(\mathcal E,\star_t) @>{G_L(\rho_t)}>> G_L(A)\\
 @VV{\widehat H_t=e}V @VV{\widehat H_0=e}V\\
 G_L((\mathcal E,\star_t)/(H_t)_{\star_t})
       @>>> G_L(A/(H_0)).
\end{CD}
\end{equation}
It is a pushout of unital semirings. Indeed HZD2 identifies
both vertical arrows with the congruences generated by the
indicated supported equations. A compatible map from the
upper-right object must identify the images of $\widehat H_t$
and $e$, which by (HDT4) are exactly $\widehat H_0$ and $e$.
HZD2 gives the unique factorization. The converse is immediate
by composing with the quotient maps. Applying the injective
complete map $\Phi$ to each corner gives the corresponding
ring-amplitude and $K_L$ square of HZD4. No support character
has been selected and no label has been collapsed in (HDT6).

By contrast ordinary restriction of the actual function $H_t$
uses the different ring map $(\mathcal E,\cdot)\to A$ and gives
$G_L(A/(H_t))$, the actual time-$t$ object in HZD7. Ordinary
evaluation is not silently used as a character of $\star_t$.
For example
\begin{equation}\tag{HDT7}
 z\star_t z=z^2-2t,\qquad
 \operatorname{ev}_{z_0}(z\star_t z)
  -\operatorname{ev}_{z_0}(z)^2=-2t.
\end{equation}
Here $T_{-t}z=z$ and $T_t(z^2)=z^2-2t$, proving both formulas
without any infinite sum. For $t\ne0$ this explicitly excludes
ordinary evaluation from being a character of that algebra.
The exact transported character is instead
\[
 \chi_{t,z_0}(f)=(T_{-t}f)(z_0).
\]
It is multiplicative by (HDT2). This proves both the obstruction
and the map that actually transports the point.

\subsection{Every transported primary character and its exact jet}

For any $z_0\in\mathbb C$ and integer $m\ge1$, define
\begin{equation}\tag{HDT8}
\begin{gathered}
 J^t_{z_0,m}(f)=
   \sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{(T_{-t}f)^{(j)}(z_0)}{j!}\epsilon^j
       \in\mathbb C[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^m),\\
 F_{t,z_0,m}(z)=T_t((z-z_0)^m)
   =\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}
       \frac{(-t)^k m!}{k!(m-2k)!}(z-z_0)^{m-2k}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The first map is a unital ring homomorphism on
$(\mathcal E,\star_t)$, because it is $T_{-t}$ followed by the
Taylor jet homomorphism with its actual factorials. It is
surjective: lift an arbitrary jet by its polynomial in $z-z_0$,
then apply $T_t$ to that polynomial. Its kernel is precisely
the $\star_t$ principal ideal generated by $F_{t,z_0,m}$.

For the kernel assertion, first prove division within the actual
space (HDT1). If $g\in\mathcal E$ vanishes to order at least
$m$ at $z_0$, the quotient $g/(z-z_0)^m$ is entire by its Taylor
expansion. Outside $|z-z_0|\ge1$ its absolute value is at most
$|g(z)|$; on the remaining compact disc it is bounded. Thus
every positive Gaussian weighted supremum of the quotient is
finite. The equivalence proved in HSL16 puts it back in
$\mathcal E$. Hence the ordinary jet kernel is
$(z-z_0)^m\mathcal E$. Applying the proved isomorphism $T_t$
gives exactly the asserted $\star_t$ ideal. The finite formula
for $F_{t,z_0,m}$ follows by differentiating the unchanged
polynomial; terms with $2k>m$ are zero. We have proved
\begin{equation}\tag{HDT9}
 G_L\bigl((\mathcal E,\star_t)/(F_{t,z_0,m})_{\star_t}\bigr)
       \simeq G_L(\mathbb C[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^m)),
\end{equation}
where the map is $(f,\lambda)\mapsto(J^t_{z_0,m}(f),\lambda)$.
This retains all labels, the exact coordinate $z_0$, and the
original Taylor constants. When $z_0$ is an actual root of
$H_0$ of multiplicity $m$, (HDT4) implies
$J^t_{z_0,m}(H_t)=0$. No existence of an off-real root is
assumed by this universally quantified construction.

For example, at $m=2$ the ordinary zeros of $F_{t,z_0,2}$
satisfy $(z-z_0)^2=2t$, whereas its transported primary
character is the single multiplicity-two character given by
$J^t_{z_0,2}$. Formula (HDT8) proves their exact relation.
This example does not replace the actual theta kernel by a
quadratic polynomial; it identifies precisely which point map
the heat algebra isomorphism uses.

\subsection{An explicit local bound at every actual simple zero}

The full map also identifies where analytic root transport is
legitimate in the original coefficient semiring. Let
$H_{t_0}(z_0)=0$ and put $a=\partial_zH_{t_0}(z_0)\ne0$.
This is a statement about any actual simple zero; none is
postulated off the real axis. Use the explicit constants of
THS4 and set
\begin{equation}\tag{HDT10}
\begin{gathered}
 A_0=\max\{\operatorname{Re}t_0+1,0\},\quad
 B_0=|\operatorname{Im}z_0|+1,\quad
 M_2=\mathcal M_2(A_0,B_0),\quad M_3=\mathcal M_3(A_0,B_0),\\
 \rho_z=\min\{1,|a|/(4M_2)\},\qquad
 \rho_t=\min\{1,|a|/(4M_3),|a|\rho_z/(2M_2)\}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
All these radii are positive and retain the original $t,z,a$.
On their closed bidisc, THS6 gives
\[
 |\partial_zH_t(z)-a|
 \le\rho_tM_3+\rho_zM_2\le |a|/2,
 \qquad |H_t(z_0)|\le|t-t_0|M_2\le |a|\rho_z/2.
\]
The first inequality follows by integrating $\partial_t\partial_zH$
along the time segment and $\partial_z^2H$ along the space
segment, both inside the unit bidisc; the second uses
$\partial_tH=-\partial_z^2H$. Thus
$F_t(z)=z-a^{-1}H_t(z)$ has derivative of absolute value at
most $1/2$ and maps $|z-z_0|\le\rho_z$ into itself.
Starting at $z_0$, its successive iterates have differences
bounded by a geometric sequence of ratio $1/2$, uniformly in
$t$ on the time disc. They therefore converge to a unique
fixed point $z(t)$ in that space disc. The iterates are
holomorphic in $t$, and their convergence is uniform on
compact subsets, so the limit is holomorphic there. Its
fixed-point equation is exactly $H_t(z(t))=0$.

Differentiating this proved identity and using THS7 yields
the exact root motion and bound
\begin{equation}\tag{HDT11}
\begin{gathered}
 z'(t)=\frac{\partial_z^2H_t(z(t))}{\partial_zH_t(z(t))},
 \qquad |\partial_zH_t(z(t))|\ge |a|/2,\\
 |z'(t)|\le 2M_2/|a|,\qquad
 |z(t)-z_0|\le 2M_2|t-t_0|/|a|.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The last inequality follows by integrating the derivative on
the straight time segment. In the original $G_L(\mathbb C)$
the denominator is the actual unit
$(\partial_zH_t(z(t)),1_L)$, whose inverse is
$(1/\partial_zH_t(z(t)),1_L)$. This proves the root-motion
division before localization, through the complete coefficient
map. The fraction is not obtained by inverting $e$.

\subsection{The full supported object where that inverse fails}

Let $\mathcal B=\mathcal O(\mathbb C^2)$ in the original
coordinates $(t,z)$ and let $\mathcal H(t,z)=H_t(z)$, which
is jointly entire by THS6. The exact supported critical-zero
object is
\begin{equation}\tag{HDT12}
 \mathscr C_L=
 G_L\left(\mathcal B/(\mathcal H,\partial_z\mathcal H)\right).
\end{equation}
It is the congruence quotient of $G_L(\mathcal B)$ by both
supported equations
$\widehat{\mathcal H}=e$ and
$\widehat{\partial_z\mathcal H}=e$.
The proof of HZD2 extends directly: a difference in this ideal
is $b\mathcal H+c\partial_z\mathcal H$, and multiplying the
two generating congruences by $\widehat b,\widehat c$ and
adding proves exactly that equality at top support. Different
labels remain different under the displayed quotient map, and
lower-labelled amplitudes were already zero. This proves the
whole kernel congruence and surjectivity.

For every actual parameter-point pair, evaluation factors
through the ring coordinate of (HDT12) exactly when both
$H_t(z)=0$ and $\partial_zH_t(z)=0$ hold. These are exactly
the points on $\mathcal H=0$ at which the coefficient inverse used in (HDT11)
is unavailable in the original semiring. The statement follows
by evaluating the two generators, and the converse follows
because evaluation then annihilates every ideal combination.
The complete coefficient map embeds (HDT12) into
$\bigl(\mathcal B/(\mathcal H,\partial_z\mathcal H)\bigr)
\times K_L$ by the same $\Phi$ as throughout this reader.

Supported-zero inversion sends (HDT12) to $L$, just as in
HZD6. It makes the image of the supported derivative zero
invertible there, but the entire derivative amplitude has
then been removed. The exact original critical object and
its map have now been constructed; no singular point is
declared absent on that basis. This is the location at which
further information about the actual arithmetic heat family
has to be calculated, not replaced by an unproved inverse
in its original amplitude field.
