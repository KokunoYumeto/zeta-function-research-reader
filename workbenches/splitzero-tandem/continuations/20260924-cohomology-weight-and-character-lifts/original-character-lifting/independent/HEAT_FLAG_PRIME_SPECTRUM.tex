\subsection{The original algebra and which prime ideals its localization retains}

Let $L$ be any nontrivial bounded distributive lattice. Retain
\begin{equation}\tag{HFP1}
\begin{gathered}
 \mathcal E=\left\{\sum_{n\ge0}a_nz^n:
       \sum_{n\ge0}|a_n|\sqrt{n!}\,h^n<\infty\ (h>0)\right\},\\
 \mathcal F(L)=\{\nu\in L^{\mathbb N_0}:\nu_i\ge\nu_{i+2}\},\qquad
 \mathcal A_L=\{(f,\nu):f\in\mathcal E,\ \nu\in\mathcal F(L),
                         \ a_i\ne0\Rightarrow\nu_i=1_L\}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Addition is $(f,\nu)+(h,\mu)=(f+h,\nu\vee\mu)$. The ordinary
amplitude product and the exact flag product are
\begin{equation}\tag{HFP2}
\begin{gathered}
 (f,\nu)\circ(h,\mu)=(fh,\nu\circledast\mu),\\
 (\nu\circledast\mu)_0=(\nu_0\wedge\mu_0)\vee(\nu_1\wedge\mu_1),
 \quad
 (\nu\circledast\mu)_n=\bigvee_{r=0}^n\nu_r\wedge\mu_{n-r}\quad(n\ge1),\\
 \mathbf u=(1_L,0_L,0_L,\ldots),\quad
 1_{\mathcal A}=(1,\mathbf u),\quad e_0=(0,\mathbf u),\quad
 \mathbf J=(1_L,1_L,\ldots),\quad J=(1,\mathbf J),\\
 \epsilon(\nu)=\nu_0\vee\nu_1,\qquad
 g(f,\nu)=(f,\epsilon(\nu)),\quad
 j(f,\lambda)=(f,(\lambda,\lambda,\ldots)),\quad Jv=jg(v).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
These are SHP5, SHP17--24, including $J^2=J$,
$\mathcal A_L[J^{-1}]\simeq G_L(\mathcal E)$ and
$\mathcal A_L[e_0^{-1}]\simeq\mathcal F(L)$.

An ideal below means a subset containing the additive zero, closed
under addition, and absorbing multiplication by every semiring
element. It is prime when it is proper and $ab$ in the ideal forces
one factor into it. An ideal is \emph{subtractive} when
$a,a+b\in I$ implies $b\in I$. This additional property is not
part of the definition of prime used here. Write
$D(c)=\{\mathfrak p:c\notin\mathfrak p\}$ and
$V(c)=\{\mathfrak p:c\in\mathfrak p\}$.

Every prime in $D(J)$ is one of the following actual ideals:
\begin{equation}\tag{HFP3}
\begin{aligned}
 \mathfrak a_{\mathfrak p}
   &=\{(f,\nu):f\in\mathfrak p\},
       &&\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal E,\\
 \mathfrak s_{\mathfrak q}
   &=\{(0,\nu):\nu_0\vee\nu_1\in\mathfrak q\},
       &&\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{SpecLat}L.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $\operatorname{SpecLat}L$ uses proper lattice ideals prime for
meet. The correspondence is exactly the homeomorphism
\begin{equation}\tag{HFP4}
 g^*:\operatorname{Spec}G_L(\mathcal E)\xrightarrow{\sim}D(J),
 \qquad \mathfrak r\longmapsto g^{-1}\mathfrak r.
\end{equation}
To check it without concealing an ideal condition, let $P$ be prime
and exclude $J$. If $g(v)=g(w)$, then $Jv=Jw$, and primeness gives
$v\in P\Longleftrightarrow w\in P$. Thus $g(P)$ is an ideal and
its inverse image is exactly $P$. It is proper: a preimage in $P$
of the target unit would give $J\in P$. Surjectivity of $g$ and
the same membership test prove that $g(P)$ is prime. Conversely
the inverse image of a prime excludes $J$, since $g(J)=1$.
The assignments are inverse. Basic opens satisfy
$g^*(D(g(v)))=D(v)\cap D(J)$, proving the topology assertion.

For completeness, the primes of $G_L(R)$ for a nonzero ring $R$
have exactly two forms. If $e=(0,1_L)$ belongs to the prime, then
all labelled zeros belong to it. Its top-supported amplitudes form
a ring prime: multiplication by $(-1,1_L)$ supplies additive
negatives, and the other ideal and prime laws follow by the
amplitude operations. Conversely each ring prime gives this ideal.
If $e$ is excluded, no nonzero-amplitude element can belong, since
multiplying such an element by $e$ gives $e$. The labels of the
remaining zeros form a proper lattice ideal prime for meet; absorption
makes it downward closed. Conversely such a lattice ideal gives
a semiring prime of labelled zeros. Applying this calculation to
$R=\mathcal E$ proves every assertion in (HFP3).

There is an exact distinction stronger than just existence of this
open set:
\begin{equation}\tag{HFP5}
 D(J)=\{\hbox{subtractive prime ideals of }\mathcal A_L\},
 \qquad V(J)=\{\hbox{non-subtractive prime ideals of }\mathcal A_L\}.
\end{equation}
Indeed both types in (HFP3) are subtractive. For the arithmetic
type this is subtraction in the ring prime. For the support type,
two zero amplitudes imply the third amplitude is zero, and the
downward lattice ideal recovers the label of either summand from
their join. Their inverse images under $g$ are subtractive.
Conversely put $c=(-1,\mathbf u)$. The exact identity
\begin{equation}\tag{HFP6}
             1_{\mathcal A}+Jc=e_0J
\end{equation}
has amplitude $1-1=0$ and constant top support on both sides.
Any ideal containing $J$ contains $Jc$ and $e_0J$. If it were
subtractive, (HFP6) would put the unit in it. This proves (HFP5).
It also proves that the congruence imposing $J=0$ is the total
congruence: it sets $Jc=e_0J=0$ in (HFP6), hence $1=0$.
Therefore the nonempty residual spectrum constructed below is
not the spectrum of a nontrivial congruence quotient $J=0$.
Its ordinary prime ideals must not be discarded by imposing
subtractive ideals after the calculation.

The other localization gives the additional exact open set
\begin{equation}\tag{HFP7}
 \operatorname{Spec}\mathcal F(L)\xrightarrow{\sim}D(e_0),
 \qquad P\longmapsto\{(f,\nu):\nu\in P\}.
\end{equation}
The proof is the one for (HFP4), replacing $Jv$ by $e_0v=(0,\nu)$.
In particular its part lying in $V(J)$ is exactly the spectrum
of flags whose primes contain $\mathbf J$.

In an additively idempotent semiring, subtractive ideals are exactly
ideals downward closed for its natural order: $b\le a$ means
$a+b=a$, so subtractivity implies downward closure; conversely
$b\le a+b$ gives subtractivity from downward closure.
The lower prime ideals of both $\mathcal F(L)$ and its finite-support
subsemiring are exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{HFP8}
 \epsilon^{-1}(\mathfrak q),\qquad
                       \mathfrak q\in\operatorname{SpecLat}L.
\end{equation}
Here is a direct proof valid even for an infinite flag. For any
flag $\nu$, $(\nu\circledast\nu)_0=\epsilon(\nu)$, so
$\epsilon(\nu)\mathbf u\le\nu\circledast\nu$. If a lower
prime contains $\nu$, it therefore contains
$\epsilon(\nu)\mathbf u$. Conversely
$(\epsilon(\nu)\mathbf u)\circledast\nu=\nu$, so absorption
gives the converse. Its intersection with the scalar copy
$\lambda\mapsto\lambda\mathbf u$ is a lattice prime; this
proves (HFP8). No claim classifying all flag primes has been
made using this stronger lower-ideal property.

\subsection{The exact residual ideal and explicit primes for every lattice}

For a proper lattice prime $\mathfrak q$, define its exact Boolean
character
\begin{equation}\tag{HFP9}
 \chi_{\mathfrak q}:L\longrightarrow\mathbb B,
 \qquad\chi_{\mathfrak q}(\lambda)=
       \begin{cases}0&\lambda\in\mathfrak q,\\1&\lambda\notin\mathfrak q.
       \end{cases}
\end{equation}
It preserves join, meet and both bounds by the ideal and prime
properties. Such characters exist for every nontrivial $L$ and
separate its elements. To verify the latter assertion, when
$a\not\le b$ choose by Zorn an ideal containing $b$ and disjoint
from the filter $\uparrow a$, maximal with those properties.
If $x,y$ are excluded but $x\wedge y$ belongs, the ideals
generated by adjoining $x$ and by adjoining $y$ each meet that
filter. Thus $a\le i\vee x$ and $a\le j\vee y$ for suitable
$i,j$ in the ideal (replace filter witnesses by their lower
bound $a$). Distributivity gives
$a\le i\vee j\vee(x\wedge y)$ in the ideal, a contradiction.
This is a prime excluding $a$ and containing $b$. Taking
$a=1_L,b=0_L$ proves existence; using either direction of an
unequal pair proves separation. Only the character receivers
are Boolean: the source lattice and every original label remain $L$.

The ideal generated by synchronization and its radical are
\begin{equation}\tag{HFP10}
\begin{gathered}
 (J)=J\mathcal A_L=jG_L(\mathcal E),\\
 \sqrt{(J)}=\{(f,\nu):\nu\circledast\nu
                    \text{ is the constant flag }\epsilon(\nu)\},\\
 (f,\nu)\in\sqrt{(J)}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \bigvee_{r=0}^{n}(\nu_r\wedge\nu_{n-r})=\nu_0\vee\nu_1
                         \quad\text{for every }n\ge1.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The first equality holds because a finite sum of $Jv_i$ is
$J(\sum v_i)$; SHP20 identifies its entire image. The last
equality is the explicit square formula (HFP2), whose degree-zero
entry already equals $\epsilon(\nu)$.

It remains to prove that any power entering $(J)$ forces the
square to enter. Apply an arbitrary character (HFP9) to the
flag. A Boolean parity flag is either zero, has finite support,
has just one parity present, or is mixed with infinite support.
A nonzero finite flag stays finite under every power. A nonzero
one-parity flag stays one-parity under every power; its parity
may change, but it cannot become the constant top flag. A mixed
infinite flag has constant-top square: if its even tail is
infinite, the even-even and even-odd products supply the two
infinite output tails; if its odd tail is infinite, the odd-odd
and odd-even products do so. A zero flag has constant-zero square.
These are all possibilities. Thus a power of a Boolean flag
is constant exactly when its square is constant. If a power
of $\nu$ is constant, its constant label must be $\epsilon(\nu)$,
because $\epsilon$ is multiplicative and the lattice product
is idempotent. Each Boolean character therefore makes the
square equal to that same constant. Character separation proves
the equality in $L$ at every coordinate. This establishes
(HFP10), with exponent two, for the full arbitrary lattice.

Every prime containing $J$ contains this radical, and conversely
every prime containing the radical contains $J$, so
$V(J)=V(\sqrt{(J)})$. This statement uses ordinary ideals and
does not pass to the total congruence from (HFP6).

There are two particularly explicit receivers. Let
$\mathscr P(C_2)$ be the power-set semiring of the group
$C_2=\{\bar0,\bar1\}$, with union as addition, setwise group
addition as multiplication, empty set as zero and
$\{\bar0\}$ as unit. Define
\begin{equation}\tag{HFP11}
 \pi_{\mathfrak q}(f,\nu)
   =\{\bar i:i\in\{0,1\},\ \nu_i\notin\mathfrak q\}
                         \in\mathscr P(C_2).
\end{equation}
This is onto and unital. Addition follows by character
preservation of joins. The even and odd heads of a product
are respectively
$(\chi\nu_0\chi\mu_0)\vee(\chi\nu_1\chi\mu_1)$ and
$(\chi\nu_0\chi\mu_1)\vee(\chi\nu_1\chi\mu_0)$, exactly
the group-set product. Onto follows using zero amplitudes
and head labels $0_L,1_L$ in degrees zero and one.

The two-element subset $\{\varnothing,C_2\}$ is a prime ideal
of this four-element semiring. Its sum and products with any
set remain inside it. Its complement consists of the two
singleton sets and is multiplicatively closed. Hence
\begin{equation}\tag{HFP12}
 P^{\mathrm{mix}}_{\mathfrak q}
  :=\pi_{\mathfrak q}^{-1}\{\varnothing,C_2\}
  =\{(f,\nu):
          \chi_{\mathfrak q}(\nu_0)=\chi_{\mathfrak q}(\nu_1)\}
\end{equation}
is an ordinary prime. It contains $J$ and excludes
$1_{\mathcal A}$ and $e_0$. The condition includes both
parities absent, because zero must belong to an ideal.
It is not a lower or subtractive prime; the two singleton
sets lie outside but their sum lies inside.

The second receiver uses the entire degree, including infinity:
\begin{equation}\tag{HFP13}
 d_{\mathfrak q}(\nu)=\sup\{n\ge0:\nu_n\notin\mathfrak q\}
          \in\{-\infty\}\cup\mathbb N_0\cup\{\infty\}.
\end{equation}
The supremum of the empty set is $-\infty$. Equip the target
with maximum as addition and degree addition as multiplication,
where $-\infty$ is absorbing, including
$(-\infty)+\infty=-\infty$. Its unit is degree zero.
The map $d_{\mathfrak q}$ is a unital semiring homomorphism.
For two nonzero finite Boolean flags the highest product
degree is the sum of their highest degrees, and that summand
is present. If one has unbounded degrees and the other has
a present degree, their product has unbounded degrees.
If one is empty, the product is empty. These prove every
case of multiplicativity; maximum under addition is immediate.
The set $\{-\infty,\infty\}$ is a proper prime ideal:
its complement consists exactly of finite nonnegative degrees
and is multiplicatively closed. Thus
\begin{equation}\tag{HFP14}
 P^{\infty}_{\mathfrak q}
   =\{(f,\nu):d_{\mathfrak q}(\nu)=-\infty
                        \text{ or }d_{\mathfrak q}(\nu)=\infty\}
\end{equation}
is another prime containing $J$ and excluding $e_0$.
It differs from (HFP12): the finite flag
$(1_L,1_L,0_L,\ldots)$ lies in the mixed prime and not the
infinite-degree prime, while $(1_L,0_L,1_L,0_L,\ldots)$
lies in the infinite-degree prime and not the mixed prime.
Both may be supplied with amplitude zero, so the examples
belong to the exact carrier for every $L$.

\subsection{An amplitude-sensitive degree receiver, including finite flags}

The preceding primes exclude $e_0$. There are also residual
primes containing $e_0$, and an exact receiver constructs them.
Define a semiring $\mathscr T_{\mathbb C}$ with elements
\begin{equation}\tag{HFP15}
 \{\bot,\Omega\}\ \sqcup\ (\mathbb C\times\mathbb N_0).
\end{equation}
Its additive zero is $\bot$, its unit is $(1,0)$, and $\Omega$
absorbs addition. For finite-level elements set
\begin{equation}\tag{HFP16}
\begin{gathered}
 (c,n)+(b,m)=
 \begin{cases}(c,n)&n>m,\\(b,m)&m>n,\\(c+b,n)&m=n,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad (c,n)(b,m)=(cb,n+m),\\
 \bot x=\bot,\qquad
 \Omega x=\Omega\quad(x\ne\bot).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
In particular $(0,n)$ is never identified with $\bot$, and
$\Omega(0,n)=\Omega$. Associativity of addition follows by
selecting the largest level and summing all coefficients at
that level; associativity of multiplication uses the complex
product and integer sum. For distributivity, unequal summand
levels retain the larger level after multiplication, and equal
levels use $c(b+d)=cb+cd$. The cases involving $\Omega$ use
the fact that a sum of two non-$\bot$ elements never equals
$\bot$. This proves all semiring laws, including cancellation
of coefficients to the retained element $(0,n)$.

There is an onto unital map preserving the actual coefficient
at the last visible support degree:
\begin{equation}\tag{HFP17}
 \ell_{\mathfrak q}(f,\nu)=
 \begin{cases}
 \bot&d_{\mathfrak q}(\nu)=-\infty,\\
 (a_d,d)&d=d_{\mathfrak q}(\nu)\in\mathbb N_0,\\
 \Omega&d_{\mathfrak q}(\nu)=\infty.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
To prove it, if $d<\infty$, every amplitude coefficient above
$d$ vanishes: a nonzero such coefficient would force its
label to top and hence be seen by $\chi_{\mathfrak q}$.
If $d=-\infty$, all amplitude coefficients vanish.
Consequently for a sum of distinct finite support degrees
the larger leading coefficient is unchanged, while equal
degrees give its exact complex sum, including zero. For a
product of finite support degrees $d,m$, its coefficient
in degree $d+m$ is exactly $a_db_m$, because the amplitudes
have no higher degrees. The support degree calculation is
(HFP13). The infinite and empty cases give precisely the
$\Omega$ and $\bot$ rules in (HFP16). This proves the map.
Every $(c,n)$ is received from amplitude $cz^n$ and the
parity-closed top support of that monomial, retaining that
support even when $c=0$; $\Omega$ is received from the
zero amplitude with infinite even support. Thus it is onto.

The explicit prime ideal and its pullback are
\begin{equation}\tag{HFP18}
 \mathfrak G=\{\bot,\Omega\}\cup\{(0,n):n\ge0\},
 \qquad P^{\mathrm{lead}}_{\mathfrak q}
                   =\ell_{\mathfrak q}^{-1}(\mathfrak G).
\end{equation}
Closure and absorption follow directly from (HFP16).
Its complement is all $(c,n)$ with $c\ne0$, and products
remain in the complement because $\mathbb C$ is a field.
Thus it is prime. Its pullback contains both $J$ and $e_0$
and excludes the unit. This explicitly constructs part of
$V(J)\cap V(e_0)$, in addition to the primes in (HFP12)--(HFP14).
No classification of all primes in this closed part is
inferred from these constructed examples.

Let $\mathcal A_{L,\mathrm{fin}}$ be the subsemiring of finite
flags; its amplitudes are actual polynomials. Products and
sums stay finite by (HFP2). The restriction of (HFP17) takes
values in $\mathscr T_{\mathbb C}\setminus\{\Omega\}$ and
the finite leading-zero ideal stays prime. For the exact
element
\begin{equation}\tag{HFP19}
 v=(1,(1_L,0_L,1_L,0_L,0_L,\ldots))
       \in\mathcal A_{L,\mathrm{fin}},
 \quad \ell_{\mathfrak q}(v)=(0,2),\quad g(v)=(1,1_L),
\end{equation}
the prime contains $v$ but excludes the unit, although $g$
sends them to the same element. Thus synchronization already
loses an actual prime distinction on finite flags. The
infinite idempotent $J$ itself is absent from this subsemiring;
one must not describe the finite restriction as localization
at an element that it does not contain. The finite restricted
map onto $G_L(\mathbb C[z])$ is nevertheless explicit: take
the parity closure of a polynomial's nonzero coefficient
labels, and use a constant-degree label for a zero amplitude.
This proves surjectivity and identifies its actual target.

\subsection{The exact relation of every receiver to the full coefficient map}

Retain $K_L$, its injection $\iota_L$ and retraction $\rho_L$
from SHP15--16. The complete coefficient map is
\begin{equation}\tag{HFP20}
 \boldsymbol\Phi(f,\nu)=(f,(\iota_L(\nu_i))_{i\ge0})
                 \in\mathcal E\times\mathcal F(K_L).
\end{equation}
The support homomorphism on the whole receiving semiring
\begin{equation}\tag{HFP21}
 (f,w)\longmapsto
           (\chi_{\mathfrak q}(\rho_L(w_i)))_{i\ge0}
                       \in\mathcal F(\mathbb B)
\end{equation}
is unital and multiplicative by the finite support formula.
Composing it with the parity-head and degree receivers gives
exactly (HFP11) and (HFP13) after $\boldsymbol\Phi$.
Their prime ideals are therefore inverse images of actual
prime ideals under the complete map, not artefacts of
discarding the lattice coordinate. The retraction recovers
all labels used in the calculation.

The amplitude-sensitive map (HFP17) is defined on the exact
constrained image of $\boldsymbol\Phi$ by applying (HFP21)
to find $d$ and taking $[z^d]f$ when it is finite. Its proof
uses the original carrier condition to show the absence
of coefficients above $d$. Since $\boldsymbol\Phi$ is an
isomorphism onto its image, this gives its full commuting
receiver map there. No extension of that leading-coefficient
formula to an unconstrained pair $(f,w)$ is asserted.
The unrestricted product target can have coefficients beyond
the displayed support, for which that formula would not
preserve multiplication. The constrained image and the
map just constructed retain the needed exact relation.

\subsection{Complete finite and infinite Boolean flag spectra as explicit witnesses}

The following specialization classifies all ordinary flag
primes for $L=\mathbb B$; it does not replace arbitrary $L$.
For a Boolean flag write its last even and odd supported
degrees as
\begin{equation}\tag{HFP22}
 e\in\{-\infty,0,2,4,\ldots,\infty\},\qquad
 o\in\{-\infty,1,3,5,\ldots,\infty\}.
\end{equation}
An absent parity has endpoint $-\infty$, an unbounded parity
has endpoint $\infty$. These endpoints determine the flag.
For endpoint addition below, $-\infty$ is absorbing, even
against $\infty$. The exact operations are
\begin{equation}\tag{HFP23}
\begin{gathered}
 e(a+b)=\max(e(a),e(b)),\quad o(a+b)=\max(o(a),o(b)),\\
 e(ab)=\max(e(a)+e(b),o(a)+o(b)),\\
 o(ab)=\max(e(a)+o(b),o(a)+e(b)).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
They follow by choosing the last degree of each parity in
(HFP2), including the degree-zero odd-odd contribution.
Set $u=(0,-\infty)$, $x=(-\infty,1)$,
$U=(\infty,-\infty)$, $V=(-\infty,\infty)$ and
$\mathbf J=(\infty,\infty)$. Call a nonzero flag pure when
one parity is present and mixed when both are present. For
a finite mixed flag its positive odd gap is
$\delta=|e-o|$ and its degree is $\max(e,o)$.

In the finite-support semiring all ordinary primes are exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{HFP24}
\begin{gathered}
 \{0\},\quad P_1,P_3,P_5,\ldots,P_\infty,
                         \quad M_{\mathrm{fin}},\\
 P_d=\{0\}\cup\{a\text{ finite mixed}:\delta(a)\le d\},\quad
 P_\infty=\{0\}\cup\{a\text{ finite mixed}\},\quad
 M_{\mathrm{fin}}=\mathcal F_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb B)\setminus\{u\}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
In the full flag semiring all ordinary primes are exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{HFP25}
\begin{gathered}
 \{0\},\quad Q_0,Q_1,Q_3,Q_5,\ldots,Q_\infty,
                           \quad P_{\mathrm{mix}},\quad M,\\
 Q_d=\{0\}\cup\{a\text{ of infinite support}\}
             \cup\{a\text{ finite mixed}:\delta(a)\le d\},\\
 P_{\mathrm{mix}}=\{0\}\cup\{a\text{ mixed}\},\qquad
 M=\mathcal F(\mathbb B)\setminus\{u\}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
For $d=0$ the finite mixed part is empty; for $d=\infty$
it contains every finite mixed flag. Every prime except
$\{0\}$ in (HFP25) contains $\mathbf J$.

Here is a complete proof of both classifications. Nonzero
degrees add under multiplication, so there are no zero
divisors, the only nonzero degree-zero flag is $u$, and
the two indicated $M$ are prime ideals. Every nonunit
flag $a$ has $a^2\in(x)$: its square has even endpoint
at least 2, possibly infinity, and an element with such
an even endpoint is divisible by $x$. Indeed multiplication
by $x$ sends $(e,o)$ to $(o+1,e+1)$; swap and subtract
1 to obtain the preimage, retaining absent and infinite
endpoints. Thus $\sqrt{(x)}=M$ in each semiring, with the
finite version of $M$ where appropriate. A prime containing
$x$ is therefore exactly $M$.

In a prime excluding $x$, membership of $a$ is equivalent
to membership of $x^ka$ for every $k\ge0$. Every finite
pure flag is a monomial $x^k$ and is excluded. Finite mixed
flags satisfy the exact formulas
\begin{equation}\tag{HFP26}
 \deg(ab)=\deg(a)+\deg(b),\qquad
 \delta(ab)=\min\{\delta(a),\delta(b)\}.
\end{equation}
For the second, put $m=\deg(a),n=\deg(b)$. The highest
endpoint is $m+n$, and the opposite-parity endpoint is
$\max\{m+n-\delta(a),m+n-\delta(b)\}$.
Multiplication by $x^k$ shifts degree by $k$ and preserves
gap. Two finite mixed flags of the same gap satisfy
$ax^{\deg(b)}=bx^{\deg(a)}$: the common degree and its
parity determine both endpoints from that gap. They
therefore have the same prime membership. If a gap $d$
occurs in the prime, multiplication by $u+x^c$, for any
positive odd $c\le d$, supplies the entire gap-$c$ class
by (HFP26). The accepted gaps form an initial segment of
the positive odd integers. This proves completeness of
the finite list.

To verify its listed ideals, the inequalities
$e(a)\le o(a)+d$ and $o(a)\le e(a)+d$ are preserved by
coordinatewise maximum. Hence addition preserves gap
at most $d$. Formula (HFP26) and monomial shifts prove
absorption. Its complement consists of pure flags and
mixed flags of gap greater than $d$, and is closed under
products. These statements prove primeness, including
$d=\infty$.

For the infinite list, $\mathbf Ja=\mathbf J$ for every
nonzero flag $a$, so every nonzero ideal contains
$\mathbf J$. Every mixed infinite flag has square
$\mathbf J$, hence belongs to every prime containing
$\mathbf J$. In a prime excluding $x$, the equations
$xU=V$ and $xV=U$ make the pure infinite endpoints occur
together or neither. If they occur, all infinite flags
occur and the finite part is one of the initial gap
segments already proved, giving $Q_d$. If they do not
occur, $Ua=\mathbf J$ for every finite mixed $a$ forces
all those $a$ into the prime, giving $P_{\mathrm{mix}}$.
This proves completeness. Each $Q_d$ is an ideal because
addition with an infinite flag remains infinite, and
its product with a nonzero flag remains infinite. Its
complement is the finite pure flags and finite mixed
flags with excessive gap, multiplicatively closed by
(HFP26). Mixed flags form the ideal $P_{\mathrm{mix}}$
together with zero, and its complement of pure flags
is multiplicatively closed. Thus all listed objects
are primes.

Their inclusions are exactly the chain of $Q$ ideals
ending in $Q_\infty\subsetneq M$, together with
$0\subsetneq P_{\mathrm{mix}}\subsetneq Q_\infty$;
$P_{\mathrm{mix}}$ is incomparable with each finite
$Q_d$. The finite primes form the chain printed in
(HFP24). Explicit infinite pure flags and finite mixed
flags of a specified excessive gap witness every asserted
strictness and incomparability. In particular

\subsection{The parity receiver is itself a full-lattice localization}

There is a localization retaining both full lattice labels before
any Boolean character is taken. The original even-support element

       \simeq L[C_2]=L\times L,\\

                     (a\wedge d)\vee(b\wedge c)),\\

 \qquad i(a,b)=(0,(a,b,a,b,\ldots)).

For the corner formula at an even positive degree, the term
