This calculation extends OLR13 and OLR28--OLR31 of

\nolinkurl{TAU_DELiGNE_PRIME_MEMORY.tex}.
Those are the actual input proofs; no theorem from SGA or from

$k\ge17$, $k\equiv1\pmod4$, $q=(k+1)^2$ and each literal cutoff
$q-1\le N\le2q$, with its positive metric $G_N$ on the
$q$-dimensional complex space $E$.
Keep the actual vectors $e,f\in E$ and coefficient $\epsilon>0$:
\[
\begin{gathered}
\langle v,w\rangle_N=v^*G_Nw,\qquad
\|e\|_N=\|f\|_N=1,\quad e^\dagger f=0,\\
R=\epsilon fe^\dagger,\quad
\Pi=ee^\dagger+ff^\dagger,\quad
W=\operatorname{im}\Pi=\mathbb Ce\oplus\mathbb Cf,\quad
U=\ker\Pi,\quad E=W\mathbin\perp U.
\end{gathered}
\tag{OLM1}
\]
Thus $Re=\epsilon f$, $Rf=0$, $R|_U=0$, and $\dim U=q-2$.
Every adjoint below uses this metric or its original tensor product.
The grade introduced below counts slots occupied by $f$. It is
not identified with an arithmetic weight or a purity filtration.

\subsection{Every active sector and its original adjoint}

For a positive integer $t$ let
\[
X=R^{[t]}=\sum_{a=1}^t I^{\otimes(a-1)}\otimes R
                         \otimes I^{\otimes(t-a)},
\qquad E_t=E^{\otimes t},\quad G_t=G_N^{\otimes t}.
\tag{OLM2}
\]
For each $J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,t\}$ let $E_J$ be the tensor
subspace with factor $W$ at slots in $J$ and factor $U$ elsewhere.
Then
\[
E_t=\mathop{\bigoplus}_{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,t\}}^{\perp}E_J,
\qquad
E_J\simeq W^{\otimes j}\otimes U^{\otimes(t-j)},
\quad j=|J|.
\tag{OLM3}
\]
The isomorphism moves the $W$ factors into increasing order before
the $U$ factors, without changing the order within either family.
It is an isometry, since the tensor inner product is the product
of the factor inner products. Distinct sectors are orthogonal
because some slot pairs $W$ with $U$. Expanding the identity
$I_E=\Pi+(I_E-\Pi)$ in every slot proves exhaustiveness.
Every sector reduces both $X$ and $X^\dagger$, and the restriction
of $X$ is $X_j\otimes I$, where $X_j$ is the sum of the same
original $R$ over the $j$ active slots.

For $0\le s\le j$, let $V_{j,s}\subset W^{\otimes j}$ be the
span of the original tensor words containing $s$ copies of $f$
and $j-s$ copies of $e$. These words are orthonormal already by
OLM1. Therefore the $V_{j,s}$ are mutually orthogonal and have
dimension $\binom js$.
Put $Y_j=X_j^\dagger$.
The operator $X_j$ replaces each occurrence of $e$ in turn by
$\epsilon f$, and $Y_j$ replaces each $f$ in turn by $\epsilon e$.
Terms from different slots commute. In one slot
$R^\dagger R-RR^\dagger=\epsilon^2(ee^\dagger-ff^\dagger)$,
so the complete commutator is
\[
[Y_j,X_j]|_{V_{j,s}}=\epsilon^2(j-2s)I_{V_{j,s}},
\quad
\|X_jv\|^2-\|Y_jv\|^2
 =\epsilon^2(j-2s)\|v\|^2.
\tag{OLM4}
\]
The second equality follows by pairing the first with $v$.
In particular $X_j:V_{j,s}\to V_{j,s+1}$ is injective for
$s<j/2$. The isometry interchanging $e$ and $f$ in every slot
interchanges $X_j$ and $Y_j$. It sends $V_{j,s}$ to $V_{j,j-s}$.
Thus $Y_j:V_{j,s+1}\to V_{j,s}$ is injective for $s\ge j/2$,
and its adjoint $X_j$ is surjective there.
These facts use the original $\epsilon$; it has not been replaced
by one.

\subsection{Actual primitive spaces and their orthogonal projections}

For $0\le r\le\lfloor j/2\rfloor$ define
\[
\mathcal P_{j,r}=\ker(Y_j:V_{j,r}\to V_{j,r-1}),
\qquad
m_{j,r}=\dim\mathcal P_{j,r}
         =\binom jr-\binom j{r-1},
\quad\binom j{-1}=0.
\tag{OLM5}
\]
For $r=0$, interpret $V_{j,-1}=0$.
For $r\ge1$, the map $X_j:V_{j,r-1}\to V_{j,r}$ is injective
by OLM4. Its adjoint $Y_j$ is consequently onto $V_{j,r-1}$:
the orthogonal complement of its image is the kernel of $X_j$,
which is zero. Rank and nullity prove the dimension in OLM5.
These are concrete kernels of the actual adjoint, not postulated
primitive classes.

The corresponding orthogonal projector is explicit. With all
restrictions to the displayed grades understood, put
\[
\mathsf P_{j,0}=I_{V_{j,0}},\qquad
\mathsf P_{j,r}
=I_{V_{j,r}}-X_j(Y_jX_j|_{V_{j,r-1}})^{-1}Y_j
\quad(r\ge1).
\tag{OLM6}
\]
The inverse exists because
$\langle v,Y_jX_jv\rangle=\|X_jv\|^2>0$ for nonzero
$v\in V_{j,r-1}$. The subtracted operator is self-adjoint and
idempotent, by multiplication and $Y_j=X_j^\dagger$.
It fixes $\operatorname{im}(X_j|_{V_{j,r-1}})$ and vanishes
on $\ker Y_j$. These two subspaces are orthogonal complements,
so OLM6 is exactly the claimed projector.

\subsection{Exact ladder lengths, lowering coefficients and norms}

Fix $j,r$ as above and write $d=j-2r$.
For every $u\in\mathcal P_{j,r}$ one has
\[
Y_jX_j^a u=\epsilon^2a(d-a+1)X_j^{a-1}u
\qquad(a\ge1).
\tag{OLM7}
\]
To prove it, expand
$[Y_j,X_j^a]=\sum_{b=0}^{a-1}X_j^b[Y_j,X_j]X_j^{a-1-b}$.
The vector to which the commutator in summand $b$ is applied
has grade $r+a-1-b$. OLM4 makes its coefficient
$\epsilon^2(j-2r-2a+2+2b)$.
Summing those $a$ coefficients gives
$\epsilon^2a(d-a+1)$, and the term $X_j^aY_ju$ is zero.
This proves OLM7 without deleting any power of $\epsilon$.

Define the literal factors
\[
s_{d,a}=\epsilon^{2a}a!\frac{d!}{(d-a)!}
\quad(0\le a\le d),\qquad s_{d,0}=1.
\]
Then the original inner products are
\[
\boxed{
\langle X_j^a u,X_j^b v\rangle
 =\mathbf1_{a=b}s_{d,a}\langle u,v\rangle,
\qquad u,v\in\mathcal P_{j,r},\quad0\le a,b\le d.}
\tag{OLM8}
\]
Different powers have different grades, hence are orthogonal.
At equal powers, move one $X_j$ from the first entry to the
second by its actual adjoint and use OLM7. Induction gives
$s_{d,a}=\epsilon^2a(d-a+1)s_{d,a-1}$, the displayed factorial
product. It is strictly positive for $a\le d$.
At the next power OLM7 gives $Y_jX_j^{d+1}u=0$, and therefore
$\|X_j^{d+1}u\|^2=\langle X_j^d u,Y_jX_j^{d+1}u\rangle=0$.
Thus a nonzero primitive $u$ has the exact ladder
\[
u,X_ju,\ldots,X_j^du\ne0,\qquad X_j^{d+1}u=0,
\qquad\text{length }d+1=j-2r+1.
\tag{OLM9}
\]
For its unaltered power vectors the raising coefficient is one,
while its lowering coefficient at level $a$ is exactly
$\epsilon^2a(d-a+1)$ from OLM7. The metric entries OLM8 record
why these are adjoints. No power vector has been divided by
its norm, and no source metric has been changed.

Ladders arising from different primitive grades are orthogonal.
It suffices to examine equal total grades: let $r<r'$ and
$r+a=r'+b$, so $a>b$. Moving $X_j^b$ from the second vector
to the first gives $Y_j^bX_j^a u$, a scalar multiple of
$X_j^{a-b}u$ by OLM7. Its pairing with
$v\in\mathcal P_{j,r'}$ is zero because the adjoint expression
contains $Y_j^{a-b}v=0$. Ladders from orthogonal primitive
vectors in the same primitive space are orthogonal by OLM8.

They fill every original grade. At grade $s$ the nonzero summands
are exactly $X_j^{s-r}\mathcal P_{j,r}$ for
$0\le r\le\min(s,j-s)$, and their dimensions sum to
\[
\sum_{r=0}^{\min(s,j-s)}m_{j,r}
=\binom j{\min(s,j-s)}=\binom js=\dim V_{j,s}.
\tag{OLM10}
\]
Their orthogonality and the nonzero factors in OLM8 prove
independence, so this dimension identity proves equality.
Equivalently the map
\[
\mathcal L_j:
\bigoplus_{r=0}^{\lfloor j/2\rfloor}
    \bigoplus_{a=0}^{j-2r}\mathcal P_{j,r}
\longrightarrow W^{\otimes j},\qquad
(u_{r,a})\longmapsto\sum_{r,a}X_j^a u_{r,a}
\tag{OLM11}
\]
is bijective and satisfies
$\|\mathcal L_j(u)\|^2=\sum_{r,a}s_{j-2r,a}\|u_{r,a}\|^2$.
This identity specifies the domain metric transported from the
original one. It is not an assertion that the unweighted domain
direct sum has that metric.

The inverse has an exact formula in the original operators.
Let $v_s$ be the orthogonal component in grade $s$ of $v$.
For $a=s-r$ in the indicated range, its primitive coordinate is
\[
u_{r,a}=s_{j-2r,a}^{-1}\mathsf P_{j,r}Y_j^a v_{r+a}.
\tag{OLM12}
\]
For its own summand, OLM7 iterated gives
$Y_j^aX_j^a u=s_{j-2r,a}u$.
For a summand of a lower primitive grade, the same iteration
leaves a positive power of $X_j$ in grade $r$, which OLM6
annihilates. For a higher primitive grade it applies more
lowerings than available powers and the result vanishes.
This proves OLM12 and supplies the connecting inverse, including
all factorials and powers of $\epsilon$.

\subsection{Full Jordan multiplicities over all original slots}

The decomposition in OLM11, followed by the sector maps OLM3,
proves that for each pair $(j,r)$ the original $X=R^{[t]}$ has
Jordan blocks of length $j-2r+1$ with total multiplicity
\[
\boxed{
M_{t;j,r}=\binom tj(q-2)^{t-j}
       \left(\binom jr-\binom j{r-1}\right),
\quad0\le j\le t,\quad0\le r\le\lfloor j/2\rfloor.}
\tag{OLM13}
\]
There are $\binom tj$ literal choices of active slots.
For each, the inactive factor has dimension $(q-2)^{t-j}$,
and each primitive vector supplies exactly the one ladder OLM9.
The sector decompositions exhaust $E_t$, so no blocks are missing.
For a fixed length $\ell\ge1$, the multiplicity, with equal
lengths combined, is
\[
\boxed{
M_{t,\ell}=\sum_{\substack{0\le j\le t\\
j\ge\ell-1,\ j-\ell+1\text{ even}}}
\binom tj(q-2)^{t-j}
\left[
\binom j{(j-\ell+1)/2}-
\binom j{(j-\ell-1)/2}
\right].}
\tag{OLM14}
\]
Binomial coefficients with negative lower index mean zero.
The longest block has length $t+1$ and multiplicity one,
arising from $j=t,r=0$.
The exact dimensions of every power kernel and image follow:
\[
\dim\ker X^a=\sum_{\ell=1}^{t+1}M_{t,\ell}\min(a,\ell),
\qquad
\operatorname{rank}X^a
=\sum_{\ell=1}^{t+1}M_{t,\ell}\max(\ell-a,0)
\quad(a\ge0).
\tag{OLM15}
\]
Within a length-$\ell$ ladder, applying $X^a$ shifts each
unchanged power vector by $a$ and kills exactly its last
$\min(a,\ell)$ positions; independence proves these identities.
In particular $\sum_\ell\ell M_{t,\ell}=q^t$.
At $a=1$, summing $m_{j,r}$ over $r$ telescopes to
$\binom j{\lfloor j/2\rfloor}$ and recovers OLR29 exactly.

\subsection{Every exponential entry and its complete Gram energy}

Let $h\in\mathbb C$. On a ladder with primitive grade $r$ and
$d=j-2r$, the original exponential has the finite exact action
\[
e^{hX_j}X_j^a u
=\sum_{b=a}^d\frac{h^{b-a}}{(b-a)!}X_j^b u,
\qquad
\langle X_j^b u,e^{hX_j}X_j^a v\rangle
=\begin{cases}
s_{d,b}\dfrac{h^{b-a}}{(b-a)!}\langle u,v\rangle,&b\ge a,\\
0,&b<a.
\end{cases}
\tag{OLM16}
\]
The first identity is the exponential series terminated by
OLM9; OLM8 gives the second with the stated complex phase.
No additional eigenvalue or unipotent coefficient has been
introduced: all powers of $\epsilon$ remain in the literal
vectors and the factors $s_{d,b}$.

The formula works directly with primitive-space-valued coordinates.
For one fixed sector and primitive grade, let
$v=\sum_{a=0}^dX_j^a u_a$, where
$u_a\in\mathcal P_{j,r}\otimes U^{\otimes(t-j)}$ and $X_j$
acts on its active factor. Then
\[
\boxed{
\|e^{hX}v\|_{G_t}^2
=\sum_{b=0}^d s_{d,b}
   \left\|\sum_{a=0}^b\frac{h^{b-a}}{(b-a)!}u_a\right\|^2.}
\tag{OLM17}
\]
This follows by collecting the coefficient of $X_j^b$ in
OLM16 and applying OLM8. The norm inside uses the original
primitive and inactive-factor metrics. The expression retains
every interference term between different positions in the
same ladder; such terms must not be discarded merely because
the original ladder positions were orthogonal.

More explicitly, define
\[
\mathsf G_{d;a,c}(h,z)
=\sum_{b=\max(a,c)}^d s_{d,b}
 \frac{\overline h^{\,b-a}z^{b-c}}
      {(b-a)!(b-c)!}.
\tag{OLM18}
\]
For $v=\sum_aX_j^a u_a$ and $w=\sum_cX_j^c v_c$ in the same
primitive sector, finite expansion gives
\[
\langle e^{hX}v,e^{zX}w\rangle_{G_t}
=\sum_{a,c=0}^d\mathsf G_{d;a,c}(h,z)\langle u_a,v_c\rangle.
\tag{OLM19}
\]
Indeed the coefficient in the first argument is conjugated and
the second is not, which fixes the order of the powers in
OLM18. At $h=z=0$ the matrix is the original diagonal
$s_{d,a}\mathbf1_{a=c}$.
For every $h$, its diagonal specialization $\mathsf G_d(h,h)$
is positive definite: the triangular coefficient map in OLM17
has diagonal entries one and inverse obtained with $-h$, and
every $s_{d,b}$ is positive. Its determinant is
$\prod_{b=0}^d s_{d,b}$, since the triangular map and its adjoint
have determinant one. These statements retain the original
Gram matrix and do not impose an arithmetic purity condition.

All sectors and primitive grades are orthogonal and reduce
both $X$ and $X^\dagger$. Consequently the norm and pairing on
the entire $E_t$ are exactly the sums of OLM17 and OLM19 over
the actual $J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,t\}$ and all its primitive
grades. These formulas give the full energy, including every
inactive slot, rather than only its largest Jordan block.

The exact exponential displacement is particularly explicit:
\[
\boxed{
\|(e^{hX}-I)v\|_{G_t}^2
=\sum_{b=1}^d s_{d,b}
 \left\|\sum_{a=0}^{b-1}\frac{h^{b-a}}{(b-a)!}u_a\right\|^2}
\tag{OLM20}
\]
on each primitive sector, with the same sums over sectors for
a general vector. It follows by removing the $a=b$ term in
OLM17. For $h\ne0$, the kernel is precisely the top position
$X_j^d\mathcal P_{j,r}$ in each ladder: the coefficient at
$b=1$ first forces $u_0=0$, then $b=2$ forces $u_1=0$, and
induction forces $u_0=\cdots=u_{d-1}=0$. The coefficient $u_d$
is unrestricted. Thus the displacement has the same kernel
as $X$, exactly as in the locality calculation OLR28.

For a nonzero vector in one ladder sector let $a_0$ be its
smallest occupied position. Along positive real $h\to\infty$,
the exact polynomial OLM17 has leading term
\[
\|e^{hX}v\|_{G_t}^2
=\frac{s_{d,d}}{(d-a_0)!^2}\|u_{a_0}\|^2h^{2(d-a_0)}
+O\bigl(h^{2(d-a_0)-1}\bigr)
\quad(a_0<d).
\tag{OLM21}
\]
Only $b=d$ and its smallest occupied $a=a_0$ contribute this
highest degree; every other term has smaller degree, proving
the formula without a possible cancellation of its positive
leading coefficient. If $a_0=d$, the vector is fixed and its
energy is exactly $s_{d,d}\|u_d\|^2$.
The same leading term holds for OLM20 when $a_0<d$.
Taking $h=p^n$ retains the exact $p$-adic-to-complex locality
failure in every nonfixed ladder position.

\subsection{Exact global singular energy as an independent consequence}

The tensor exponential identity in TDP23 can be proved directly:
the slot operators commute and each has square zero, so
\[
e^{hX}=(I_E+hR)^{\otimes t}.
\tag{OLM22}
\]
On the original orthonormal pair $(e,f)$, the one-factor matrix
of $I+hR$ is
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\h\epsilon&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$;
on $U$ it is $I_U$.
Writing $a=\epsilon^2|h|^2$, the two eigenvalues of its actual
positive Gram operator on $W$ are
\[
\lambda_\pm(h)=\frac{2+a\ \pm\sqrt{a^2+4a}}2,
\qquad\lambda_+\lambda_-=1.
\tag{OLM23}
\]
Indeed that Gram matrix is
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1+a&\epsilon\overline h\\
\epsilon h&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, whose trace is $2+a$
and determinant is one. Its characteristic polynomial proves
OLM23. The other $q-2$ eigenvalues are one.
The squared singular values of the entire tensor exponential
therefore consist of
\[
\lambda_+^{\,b}\lambda_-^{\,c},\qquad
\text{multiplicity }\frac{t!}{b!c!(t-b-c)!}(q-2)^{t-b-c},
\quad b,c\ge0,\quad b+c\le t.
\tag{OLM24}
\]
To prove this, take orthogonal eigenspaces of the positive
one-factor Gram operator, tensor them in the original slots,
and count choices of the two one-dimensional factors and
the $(q-2)$-dimensional factor. The tensor Gram operator is
the tensor of the original Gram operators by OLM22.
If eigenvalues coincide, the displayed positive multiplicities
add; the labelling remains valid, also at $h=0$.
Thus the complete operator norm and squared Hilbert--Schmidt
energy are exactly
\[
\boxed{
\|e^{hX}\|_{G_t}=\lambda_+(h)^{t/2},\qquad
\|e^{hX}\|_{\rm HS,G_t}^2=(q+\epsilon^2|h|^2)^t.}
\tag{OLM25}
\]
The first identity follows from the largest product eigenvalue,
and the second follows by summing OLM24, or multiplying the
one-factor traces. These are consequences in the same metric
as the full ladder Gram formulas; no norm estimate has replaced
the exact ladder entries.

\subsection{The full support lifts and free-mask correspondence}

For the full bounded distributive lattice $L$ keep
$V_L^\uparrow=\{(0,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}

\[
\langle(v,\lambda),(w,\mu)\rangle_L
=(\langle v,w\rangle_{G_t},\lambda\wedge\mu),
\qquad
(X^a)^\uparrow(X^b)^\uparrow=(X^{a+b})^\uparrow.
\tag{OLM26}
\]
The pair on the right of the first formula lies in

For $a+b\ge t+1$, the second formula is the supported-zero

\begin{gathered}

\end{gathered}
\tag{OLM27}
\]
OLM20 proves the amplitude kernel and its complete ladder

In the original free coordinates of the positive-dimensional

The free operator has amplitude $I$ and all-top support matrix,

There is no asserted change of free-semimodule basis to a
