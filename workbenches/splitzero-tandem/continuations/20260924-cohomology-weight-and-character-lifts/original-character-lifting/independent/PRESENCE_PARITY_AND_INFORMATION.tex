\section{Presence, parity, and the information retained by a map}
\label{sec:PPI}
\paragraph{The user's intended distinction.}
The ungraded presence unit $\tau$ has no parity. Integer $1$ is
present and odd. These descriptions specify different amounts of
data. We now construct their exact comparison. The labels $Z_0,Z_1,Z_2$ are the user's notation: absence,
ungraded presence, and integer parity respectively. The source
unit $\tau$ carries presence at $Z_1$ and has no $Z_2$ parity.
The notation $Z_0$ for absence is not treated
as a cyclic group with zero elements. The elementary set and linear
algebra proofs below are given in full. The quantum comparison uses
Arun Kumar Pati and Samuel L. Braunstein,
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9911090v2}{\emph{Impossibility of
deleting an unknown quantum state}}, original author source,
equations \texttt{del2}, \texttt{tran1}, \texttt{tran2},
\texttt{almostfinal}, and \texttt{final}. Its distinction between
erasure, transfer to an ancilla, and deletion is retained.

\subsection{Empty, present, and parity-labelled}
Define the label sets and their maps by
\begin{equation}\tag{PPI1}
Z_0=\varnothing,\qquad Z_1=\{\tau\},\qquad
Z_2=\{\mathrm{even},\mathrm{odd}\},\qquad
Z_0\longrightarrow Z_2\xrightarrow{q}Z_1,
\quad q(\mathrm{even})=q(\mathrm{odd})=\tau.
\end{equation}
A $Z_i$-labelled set is a pair $(A,\ell)$ with a function
$\ell:A\to Z_i$. A morphism $(A,\ell)\to(B,m)$ is a function
$f:A\to B$ satisfying $m f=\ell$. Composition preserves this
equation, and identity functions satisfy it, so these objects
and maps form the slice category $\mathrm{Set}/Z_i$.
Postcomposition defines the functor
\begin{equation}\tag{PPI2}
F:\mathrm{Set}/Z_2\longrightarrow\mathrm{Set}/Z_1,
\qquad F(A,\ell)=(A,q\ell),\quad F(f)=f.
\end{equation}
Indeed $(qm)f=q\ell$; identities and compositions are unchanged.
It forgets the parity label while retaining every underlying element.
Let $T=(\{*\},*\mapsto\tau)$ in $\mathrm{Set}/Z_1$, and let
$E$ and $O$ be the objects with carrier $\{*\}$ labelled even and odd in
$\mathrm{Set}/Z_2$. Then
\begin{equation}\tag{PPI3}
F(E)=T=F(O),\qquad E\not\cong O.
\end{equation}
The only function between their singleton carriers sends the single
element to the single element. It fails the label-preservation
equation because even and odd are distinct. There is therefore not
even a morphism $E\to O$ in this slice. In particular $F$ cannot
have an inverse functor: an inverse up to isomorphism would make
both $E$ and $O$ isomorphic to the inverse image of $T$.
Choosing $E$ or $O$ is possible, but does not recover which object
was supplied. The source unit $\tau$ is represented by unlabelled
presence $T$, not by a secretly even or odd version of $T$.
The complete space of parity refinements of $T$ has the two
objects $E,O$. The label exchange interchanges them and fixes
neither. There is also a canonical construction retaining both
choices: $q^*(A)=(A\times Z_2,\operatorname{pr}_2)$. For every
labelled $(B,\ell)$, the assignments
\[
f:B\to A\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
\bigl[b\mapsto(f(b),\ell(b))\bigr]:
(B,\ell)\to q^*(A)
\]
are inverse because the second coordinate of a label-preserving
map must be $\ell$. They commute with precomposition and
postcomposition, proving $F\dashv q^*$. For a singleton $A$,
this creates two new tagged elements. It does not give two
parities to the original ungraded unit.

The parity-labelled integer object is
\begin{equation}\tag{PPI4}
I=(\mathbb Z,\pi),\quad \pi(n)=n\bmod2,\qquad
\pi(n+m)=\pi(n)+\pi(m),\quad
\pi(nm)=\pi(n)\pi(m)\quad\text{in }\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
\end{equation}
The equations follow because writing $n=2a+r$, $m=2b+s$ gives
$n+m=2(a+b)+(r+s)$ and
$nm=2(2ab+as+br)+rs$. The element $1$ is an odd-labelled point of
$I$. Presence and oddness are two descriptors of that one point;
they are not two alternative states occupied by $1$, and presence
is not an additional independent binary choice once that point
has been specified. This statement imposes no parity on $\tau$.

The unique map $A\to\varnothing$ exists exactly when $A$ is empty:
if $a\in A$ it would require an image in the empty set; if $A$ is
empty the empty function exists. Thus demanding an actual label
map into $Z_0$ forces emptiness. Merely omitting a label map does
not: the singleton $\{*\}$ with no specified parity is nonempty.
This proves the exact distinction between an empty label target
and an unspecified grading.

\subsection{Boolean arithmetic and counts}
Let $\mathbf B$ be the ordered category with objects false and
true and a unique arrow $b\to c$ when $b\le c$. Define
\begin{equation}\tag{PPI5}
\operatorname{supp}(A)=
\begin{cases}\mathrm{false},&A=\varnothing,\\
\mathrm{true},&A\ne\varnothing.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
A function from a nonempty set to an empty set cannot exist, so
each set map induces the required arrow between supports. The
arrow is unique; therefore identities and compositions are
preserved. For all sets $A,B$,
\begin{equation}\tag{PPI6}
\operatorname{supp}(A\sqcup B)
=\operatorname{supp}(A)\vee\operatorname{supp}(B),\qquad
\operatorname{supp}(A\times B)
=\operatorname{supp}(A)\wedge\operatorname{supp}(B).
\end{equation}
A disjoint union has an element exactly when at least one summand
does; a product has an element exactly when both factors do.
These equivalences prove both formulas. Identifying false with
$\varnothing$ and true with $\{*\}$ as the two subsets of a
singleton gives Boolean union and intersection, with
$\mathrm{true}\vee\mathrm{true}=\mathrm{true}$.
Before this support map, $\{*\}\sqcup\{*\}$ has two distinct
tagged elements. For finite sets their cardinalities satisfy
\begin{equation}\tag{PPI7}
|A\sqcup B|=|A|+|B|,\quad |A\times B|=|A||B|,\quad
\mathbb N\xrightarrow{p}\mathbf B,quad
p(0)=\mathrm{false},\ p(n)=\mathrm{true}\ (n>0).
\end{equation}
To check the cardinality equations, enumerate the two disjoint
blocks consecutively, and enumerate the pairs in $|A|$ rows of
$|B|$ entries. PPI6 then proves that $p$ preserves semiring
addition, multiplication, zero, and unit. It does not preserve
parity information: $p(1)=p(2)$, while their parities differ.
This is the exact finite-set realization of the two procedures
$(\tau+\tau)1=1$ and $\tau1+\tau1=2$.

There are two questions about zero, with explicitly different
domains:
\begin{equation}\tag{PPI8}
\operatorname{supp}(\varnothing)=\mathrm{false},\qquad
\operatorname{supp}(\{0\})=\mathrm{true},\qquad
|\varnothing|=0,\quad\pi(0)=0.
\end{equation}
No new numerical zero is introduced. The set containing the
integer zero has an element, while the empty set has none.
Even if the integer zero is represented by $\varnothing$, the
singleton containing that representative is
$\{\varnothing\}\ne\varnothing$. Consequently ``every specified
integer is present'' concerns the point $\{n\}$, whereas a
cardinality-based presence test sends the value zero to false.
They cannot be interchanged as one map on one domain.
The truth map in PPI7 cannot extend to a unital semiring map
$\mathbb Z\to\mathbf B$: such a map would send
$0=1+(-1)$ to $\mathrm{true}\vee p(-1)=\mathrm{true}$,
contradicting preservation of zero. This determines the exact
domain of the comparison rather than discarding integer signs.

\subsection{Trivial symmetry is not emptiness}
Every set has an identity bijection. In particular the empty
function $\varnothing\to\varnothing$ is a bijection, as is the
identity on a singleton. These are their only self-maps, so
\begin{equation}\tag{PPI9}
\operatorname{Aut}(\varnothing)\cong\{1\}
\cong\operatorname{Aut}(\{*\}).
\end{equation}
Thus the automorphism group alone cannot distinguish these two
sets. The trivial group acts on every set by its identity; this
does not force a set to have one element. A $C_2$ action can be
restricted along the unique homomorphism $\{1\}\to C_2$ to its
trivial-group action. That is a precise sense in which this
particular symmetry can be forgotten. A parity labelling
$A\to Z_2$ is different data from an action $C_2\times A\to A$:
the odd-labelled singleton exists although every action on that
singleton fixes its only point. No parity-flipping bijection is
supplied by its label alone.

\subsection{An exact linear information receiver}
The set map $q$ has a complex-linear extension
\begin{equation}\tag{PPI10}
V=\mathbb C e_{\mathrm{even}}\oplus\mathbb C e_{\mathrm{odd}},
\quad W=\mathbb C t,\qquad
T:V\to W,\quad T(e_{\mathrm{even}})=T(e_{\mathrm{odd}})=t.
\end{equation}
For $v=a e_{\mathrm{even}}+b e_{\mathrm{odd}}$, one has
$T(v)=(a+b)t$. Hence
\begin{equation}\tag{PPI11}
\ker T=\mathbb C(e_{\mathrm{even}}-e_{\mathrm{odd}}).
\end{equation}
Both inclusions follow immediately from $a+b=0$.
This kernel is the entire lost parity-difference direction.
Give the displayed bases orthonormal inner products and define
$Z e_{\mathrm{even}}=e_{\mathrm{even}}$,
$Z e_{\mathrm{odd}}=-e_{\mathrm{odd}}$. There is no operator $A$
on $W$ satisfying $AT=TZ$: the even vector would require $At=t$
and the odd vector would require $At=-t$. Over $\mathbb C$ these
are incompatible because $t\ne0$. This proves exactly which
observable does not descend to presence alone.

Retaining the full record gives an isometry instead:
\begin{equation}\tag{PPI12}
J:V\longrightarrow W\otimes V,\quad
J(v)=t\otimes v,\qquad
J^*J=I_V,\qquad JZ=(I_W\otimes Z)J.
\end{equation}
The first equality follows from $\langle t,t\rangle=1$ and the
tensor-product inner product. The second follows by evaluating
both sides on each basis vector. The first factor represents
ungraded presence and the second retains the parity state.
It is the second factor, not $t$ or $\tau$, on which $Z$ acts.
This is a mathematical state-space comparison, without a claim
that the integers or $\tau$ thereby become physical particles.

\subsection{What the quantum no-deleting comparison proves}
Pati--Braunstein consider arbitrary unknown states, not just
distinguishable classical parity labels. The following exact
inner-product test gives the fixed-environment obstruction.
Let all displayed state vectors have norm one, and suppose an
isometry $U$ obeyed, for every input vector $\psi$,
\begin{equation}\tag{PPI13}
U(\psi\otimes\psi\otimes a)
=\psi\otimes\Sigma\otimes a_0,
\end{equation}
where $a$, $\Sigma$, and $a_0$ are fixed independently of $\psi$.
For two allowed inputs $\psi,\phi$ with inner product $c$, taking
inner products gives
\begin{equation}\tag{PPI14}
c^2=c.
\end{equation}
Indeed the input product is $c\cdot c\cdot1$ and the output
product is $c\cdot1\cdot1$. In dimension at least two, choose
$\psi=e_0$, $\phi=(e_0+e_1)/\sqrt2$; then $c=1/\sqrt2$ and
PPI14 is false. Thus this fixed-record deletion is impossible.
Allowing the environment to retain the state instead gives the
explicit unitary swap
\begin{equation}\tag{PPI15}
\psi\otimes\psi\otimes\Sigma
\longmapsto\psi\otimes\Sigma\otimes\psi.
\end{equation}
Swapping the last two tensor factors preserves the inner product
on pure tensors and is its own inverse, proving unitarity. It
transfers the information rather than deleting it; the original
paper explicitly excludes this as the sought deletion.

For the two orthogonal labels $b\in\{0,1\}$, there is also a
reversible map
\begin{equation}\tag{PPI16}
|b\rangle|c\rangle\longmapsto
|b\rangle|c\mathbin{\oplus}b\rangle,
\qquad |b\rangle|b\rangle\longmapsto|b\rangle|0\rangle.
\end{equation}
It permutes the four orthonormal basis vectors and is its own
inverse. Its linear extension is therefore unitary. It does not
delete an arbitrary superposition against a copy: applying it
to $(\alpha|0\rangle+\beta|1\rangle)^{\otimes2}$ gives
$\alpha^2|00\rangle+\alpha\beta|01\rangle+
\alpha\beta|11\rangle+\beta^2|10\rangle$, retaining every term.
For $\alpha\beta\ne0$ this is not the proposed blank-second-factor
state. Thus the quantum theorem does not forbid the mathematical
forgetful functor PPI2 or turn a classical label into a conserved
physical charge. The constructive information-preserving
comparison here is PPI12; the exact information lost by PPI10 is
PPI11. None of these results asserts an RH sign or purity bound.
