\section{The complete finite-lattice prime spectrum after tropical base change}
\label{sec:tropical-lattice-prime-spectrum}

The coefficient semiring throughout this calculation is exactly
$K=\mathbb R_+^{\max}=([0,\infty),\max,\cdot)$, with absorbing
zero $0$ and unit $1$. This is the tropical coefficient semifield of
Alain Connes and Caterina Consani,
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.05580}{\emph{Geometry of the Arithmetic
Site}}. The exact locators are lines 395--406 and Definition
\texttt{defnpt}, lines 685--690 of the original author source:

\end{center}
The complete support base $G_L(R)$ and its original spectrum are the
existing programme construction cited and reproduced in (ASL1),
(ASL12), and
\href{https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/zeta-function-research-reader/blob/3c78cfbd9e194d35117b33277c421c6564ce3dbf/workbenches/splitzero-tandem/continuations/20260923-full-prime-support/031/01_REBUILT_MATHEMATICS.tex}{031,
Theorem \texttt{thm:rebuilt-full-support}}.
The ordinary-prime classification proved below is a derivation for
the coefficient extension; it is not attributed to Connes--Consani.

Let $L$ be any nontrivial finite bounded distributive lattice.
Its original elements are retained through the exact isomorphism
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP1}
 L\longrightarrow\operatorname{Down}(J),\qquad
 \lambda\longmapsto\{j\in J:j\le\lambda\},
 \qquad J=J(L),
\end{equation}
whose inverse takes the join of a downset. The proof with all maps
is (ASL11): join-irreducibles in a distributive lattice are join-prime,
and every element is the join of those below it. In particular this
is not a replacement by independent Boolean coordinates. By the
faithful tensor calculation (ASL18)--(ASL21), the full coefficient
extension is exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP2}
 A_J:=K\otimes_{\mathbb B}L
 \simeq\{f:J\to[0,\infty):x\le y\Longrightarrow f(x)\ge f(y)\},
\end{equation}
with pointwise maximum and multiplication. For completeness the
finite version follows directly: the nonzero superlevel sets of
an antitone function are downsets, and its finitely many values
express it as a sum of those values tensored with those downsets.
Sorting the coefficient values and using their cumulative downsets
proves uniqueness by character evaluation, exactly as (ASL21).
For every downset $D\subset J$, write $e_D$ for its characteristic
function. These are precisely the multiplicative idempotents of
$A_J$; each coordinate of an idempotent is zero or one.

An ideal here means an ordinary semiring ideal, closed under addition
and multiplication by every element. It is not required to be
subtractive. A prime ideal is proper and its multiplicative
complement is closed. Retaining that distinction is necessary for
the complete answer.

\subsection{Exhaustive classification, including the primes with several labels}

For $j\in J$, put $J_{\le j}=\{x:x\le j\}$. Let $U$ be an
upward-closed subset of $J_{\le j}$ containing $j$. Define
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP3}
 \mathfrak p_{j,U}
 =\{f\in A_J:f(j)=0\ \hbox{or}
                  \ \exists i\in U:\ f(i)>f(j)\}.
\end{equation}
Then every ordinary prime ideal of $A_J$ is exactly one of
(LSP3), and the pair $(j,U)$ is unique.

First prove existence. If $f,g$ belong to (LSP3), put
$h=\max(f,g)$. When $h(j)=0$, it belongs to the ideal. Otherwise
one of $f,g$, say $f$, attains $h(j)>0$ at $j$. Since $f$ is in
the ideal, there is $i\in U$ with $f(i)>f(j)=h(j)$, whence
$h(i)>h(j)$. This proves additive closure. For a product $fg$, a
zero at $j$ persists. If $f(i)>f(j)>0$ and $g(j)>0$, then
$g(i)\ge g(j)>0$, so $f(i)g(i)>f(j)g(j)$. If $g(j)=0$, the
product has zero at $j$. This proves absorption. The constant unit
is outside the ideal. Finally its complement is exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP4}
 f(j)>0,\qquad f(i)=f(j)\quad(i\in U),
\end{equation}
and is multiplicatively closed. Thus (LSP3) is prime.

For exhaustion, let $\mathfrak p$ be any prime of $A_J$. Its
intersection with the $e_D$ is a prime of the lattice of downsets.
The complement of that lattice prime is a finite filter. Its least
member $D_0$ is nonempty and join-prime: if $D_0\subset B\cup C$
then $D_0\subset B$ or $D_0\subset C$, by primality of the
complementary ideal. Since $D_0$ is the union of its principal
downsets, $D_0=J_{\le j}$ for a unique $j$. It follows that
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP5}
 e_D\notin\mathfrak p\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad j\in D.
\end{equation}
In particular $e_{J_{\le j}}\notin\mathfrak p$, and primality
gives
\[
 f\in\mathfrak p\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 e_{J_{\le j}}f\in\mathfrak p.
\]
We can therefore work on the full subposet $B=J_{\le j}$, with
unit $e_B$. This is the exact truncation map $f\mapsto f|_B$,
followed by extension by zero outside $B$ when viewed in $A_J$.
The latter extension is a homomorphism with unit $e_B$ in its image.

Every function on $B$ with value zero at $j$ is in the prime:
it is multiplied identically by $e_{B\setminus\{j\}}$, which
belongs to the prime by (LSP5). All remaining functions are positive
at every coordinate, because $j$ is the maximum of $B$. The positive
constant functions are units, so multiplying by such a function
does not change ideal membership. For a positive function $f$ define
the exact coordinates
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP6}
 a_x=\log(f(x)/f(j)),\qquad
 C_j=\{a:B\to[0,\infty):a_j=0,\ a_x\ge a_y\text{ if }x\le y\}.
\end{equation}
This retains $f$ through $f(x)=f(j)\exp(a_x)$ and a positive
unit factor $f(j)$. Write $h_a(x)=\exp(a_x)$ and set
\[
 F=\{a\in C_j:h_a\notin\mathfrak p\}.
\]
The set $F$ contains zero and is closed under addition, by
$h_{a+b}=h_ah_b$ and the multiplicative closure of the complement.
It also has the stronger property
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP7}
 b\in F,\quad a\in C_j,\quad a_x\le b_x\ (x\in B)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad a\in F.
\end{equation}
Indeed let $g(x)=\exp(b_x)$ for $x\ne j$ and $g(j)=0$.
It is antitone, is supported on $B\setminus\{j\}$, and belongs
to the prime. If $h_a$ also belonged to it, then
$\max(h_a,g)=h_b$ would belong, a contradiction. This proves (LSP7).

Let $D$ be the union of the sets $\{x:a_x>0\}$ over $a\in F$.
It is a downset of $B$ and excludes $j$. Since $B$ is finite, there
is a single $b\in F$ whose positive-coordinate set is exactly $D$:
choose one member positive at each coordinate of $D$ and add them.
When $D$ is empty take $b=0$. For any $a\in C_j$ vanishing outside
$D$, some positive integer $N$ satisfies $a_x\le Nb_x$ at every
coordinate; take an integer at least every nonzero ratio $a_x/b_x$.
Then $Nb\in F$ and (LSP7) gives $a\in F$. Conversely every
member of $F$ vanishes outside $D$ by the definition of $D$. Hence
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP8}
 F=\{a\in C_j:a_x=0\text{ for every }x\in U\},
 \qquad U=B\setminus D.
\end{equation}
The set $U$ is upward closed and contains $j$. Returning through
(LSP6) and retaining the functions with zero at $j$ proves
$\mathfrak p=\mathfrak p_{j,U}$.

Uniqueness of $j$ follows from (LSP5). If $U\ne V$ have the same
$j$, take $i\in U\setminus V$, interchanging them if necessary.
Then $i\ne j$ and the principal downset $J_{\le i}$ is disjoint
from $V$. The function equal to $\exp(1)$ on $J_{\le i}$ and
to $1$ elsewhere belongs to $\mathfrak p_{j,U}$ and not to
$\mathfrak p_{j,V}$. This proves uniqueness completely.

For a three-point poset with incomparable $a,b$ both below $j$,
the upset $U=\{a,b,j\}$ gives the additional prime
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP9}
 \{f:f(j)=0\text{ or }f(a)>f(j)\text{ or }f(b)>f(j)\}.
\end{equation}
It is distinct from both comparable-pair primes with $U=\{a,j\}$
or $U=\{b,j\}$, by the uniqueness proof. Thus comparable pairs
alone do not exhaust the spectrum for a general finite lattice.

\subsection{Specialization and every basic open}

The complete specialization criterion is
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP10}
 \mathfrak p_{j,U}\subseteq\mathfrak p_{k,V}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 j\le k\ \hbox{and}\ U\setminus\{j\}\subseteq V.
\end{equation}
Necessity of $j\le k$ follows by contracting to the idempotents:
the lattice prime of $j$ is contained in that of $k$ exactly when
$j\le k$. Explicitly, if $j\nleq k$, the downset $J_{\le k}$
contains $k$ and excludes $j$, contradicting the required inclusion.
If $i\in U\setminus\{j\}$ but $i\notin V$, the function
equal to $\exp(1)$ on $J_{\le i}$ and $1$ elsewhere has value
$1$ at $j,k$ and all of $V$, but a strict excess at $i$.
It contradicts prime inclusion. This proves the other necessity.

For sufficiency, take $f\notin\mathfrak p_{k,V}$. Then $f(k)>0$
and $f(v)=f(k)$ for all $v\in V$. If $U=\{j\}$, the inequality
$f(j)\ge f(k)>0$ already puts $f$ outside $\mathfrak p_{j,U}$.
Otherwise every $i\in U\setminus\{j\}$ lies in $V$, and
upward closure with $i\le j\le k$ also puts $j$ in $V$.
Thus $f(i)=f(j)=f(k)$ for every $i\in U$, proving the implication.
The basic open of any original function is exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP11}
 D(f)=\{(j,U):f(j)>0,\ f(i)=f(j)\text{ for all }i\in U\}.
\end{equation}
Equations (LSP10)--(LSP11) determine the whole finite topological
spectrum, with every original support label still present.

For the chain $J=\{1<\cdots<n\}$, every possible upset in
$J_{\le j}$ is $\{i,i+1,\ldots,j\}$, with $1\le i\le j\le n$.
The list becomes
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP12}
 \mathfrak p_{ij}=\{f:f_i=0\text{ or }f_i>f_j\},
 \qquad 1\le i\le j\le n,
 \qquad |\operatorname{Spec}A_J|=\frac{n(n+1)}2.
\end{equation}
This formula equals (LSP3): when $f_j=0$, either $f_i=0$ or
$f_i>f_j$, and when $f_j>0$ the largest value in the indicated
upset is $f_i$. The inclusion criterion is
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP13}
 \mathfrak p_{ij}\subseteq\mathfrak p_{k\ell}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 j\le\ell\text{ and }(i=j\text{ or }k\le i).
\end{equation}
In particular, for $n=2$ the entire spectrum is the three-element
chain $\{0\}=\mathfrak p_{11}\subsetneq\mathfrak p_{22}
\subsetneq\mathfrak p_{12}$, including (ASL31).
For general $n$ its Krull dimension is exactly $2n-2$. The integer
$2j-i-1$ strictly increases under each strict inclusion in (LSP13)
and ranges from $0$ to $2n-2$, giving the upper bound. The chain
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP14}
 \mathfrak p_{11}\subsetneq\mathfrak p_{22}
 \subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathfrak p_{nn}
 \subsetneq\mathfrak p_{n-1,n}\subsetneq\cdots
 \subsetneq\mathfrak p_{1n}
\end{equation}
has exactly $2n-2$ strict inclusions, proving equality, including
the single-point case $n=1$.

\subsection{All localized coordinates and the exact maps between them}

Fix $(j,U)$ and put $B=J_{\le j}$. The localization at its prime
has the following concrete realization, with pointwise operations:
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP15}
 (A_J)_{\mathfrak p_{j,U}}\simeq
 \left\{h:B\to[0,\infty):
 \begin{array}{l}
 \{x:h(x)>0\}\text{ is a downset of }B,\\
 h|_U\text{ is antitone}
 \end{array}\right\}.
\end{equation}
The map sends a fraction $f/g$, $g\notin\mathfrak p_{j,U}$,
to $h(x)=f(x)/g(x)$ for $x\in B$. By (LSP4), every such denominator
is positive throughout $B$ and constant on $U$. Thus the indicated
support and order properties hold. Equality of fractions implies
coordinatewise equality there. Conversely coordinatewise equality
implies equality after multiplication by $e_B$, which is outside
the prime; hence this map is injective. This also shows explicitly
why coordinates outside $B$ disappear at this specified localization.

To prove surjectivity without guessing denominators, take any $h$
on the right side. Set $g(x)=1$ for $x\in U$, and construct its
other positive values in reverse order, processing all strict
successors of $x$ first. Choose
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP16}
 g(x)=\max\left(\{1\}\cup\{g(y):y>x\}
  \cup\{h(y)g(y)/h(x):y>x\}\right)\quad(h(x)>0),
\end{equation}
and omit the last set when $h(x)=0$. Every set here is finite.
For $x\in U$, its successors are also in $U$ and the antitone
condition on $h|_U$ already verifies the required inequalities with
$g(x)=1$. For every other $x$, formula (LSP16) makes both $g$ and
$hg$ antitone. In the zero case every successor also has $h(y)=0$
because the nonzero support is a downset, so no inequality was
lost by omitting the ratios. Thus $g$ is positive, antitone and
constant on $U$, while $f=hg$ is antitone. Extend both by zero
outside $B$. They are elements of $A_J$, $g$ is outside the prime,
and $f/g=h$. This proves (LSP15) with an explicit inverse construction.

The units and maximal ideal in this local semiring are therefore
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP17}
 \begin{gathered}
 h\text{ is a unit}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 h(x)>0\ (x\in B),\quad h(i)=h(j)\ (i\in U),\\
 \mathfrak m_{j,U}=\{h:h(j)=0\text{ or }
                         \exists i\in U:\ h(i)>h(j)\}.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Indeed an inverse must be the coordinatewise reciprocal, so support
must be all of $B$. Both $h|_U$ and its reciprocal are antitone
exactly when values agree along every comparable pair. Every element
of $U$ is below $j$, forcing the displayed constant value. Conversely
these conditions make the reciprocal belong to (LSP15). The
complement of the units is the ideal displayed in (LSP17), so it is
the unique maximal ideal.

If (LSP10) holds, the canonical map between the localizations is
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP18}
 (A_J)_{\mathfrak p_{k,V}}\longrightarrow
 (A_J)_{\mathfrak p_{j,U}},\qquad
 h\longmapsto h|_{J_{\le j}}.
\end{equation}
It has this value on every original fraction, hence is the required
localization map. Its image consists exactly of the functions in
(LSP15) whose restriction to $V\cap J_{\le j}$ is antitone.
Necessity follows by restriction. For sufficiency extend such a
function by zero on $J_{\le k}\setminus J_{\le j}$. Its nonzero
support remains a downset. Its restriction to $V$ is antitone:
the only cross-boundary comparisons start in $J_{\le j}$ and end
outside, and then the latter value is zero. It is therefore an
element of the source of (LSP18). The fibres of the map are exactly
the admissible functions agreeing on $J_{\le j}$. No additional
surjectivity assertion is needed or made.

For completeness, primes of the localized semiring correspond to
the pairs $(k,V)$ whose prime is contained in $\mathfrak p_{j,U}$.
Extension takes such a prime to the fractions with numerator in it.
This is well defined: if $f/g=f'/g'$ then some inverted $t$ satisfies
$tfg'=tf'g$; all inverted factors are outside the smaller prime,
so primality makes $f$ belong exactly when $f'$ does. The complement
is multiplicatively closed, and contraction recovers the same prime.
Conversely a prime of the localization cannot contain an inverted
element; its contraction has this property and the fraction rule
recovers it. Together with (LSP10), this proves the entire localized
prime geometry, not just the local coordinate formulas.

\subsection{Subtractive primes, quotient maps, and actual tropical residue maps}

An ideal $I$ is subtractive when $a+b\in I$ and $a\in I$ imply
$b\in I$. Precisely the primes
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP19}
 \mathfrak p_{j,\{j\}}=\{f:f(j)=0\}
\end{equation}
are subtractive. They are kernels of evaluation at $j$, and the
subtractive property follows by evaluating the defining implication
in $K$. For any $U\ne\{j\}$ choose $i\in U\setminus\{j\}$.
The function $b$ equal to $\exp(1)$ on $J_{\le i}$ and $1$
elsewhere satisfies
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP20}
 b\in\mathfrak p_{j,U},\qquad \max(1,b)=b,
 \qquad 1\notin\mathfrak p_{j,U}.
\end{equation}
This violates subtractivity. It also proves that the Bourne quotient
by this prime is the zero semiring: the relation generated by
$b=0$ and $1+b=b$ forces $1=0$. This is a property of that precise
quotient construction; the original prime and localization in
(LSP15) are still proper and nonzero.

For the subtractive prime the complete global Bourne quotient is
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP21}
 A_J/\mathfrak p_{j,\{j\}}\simeq A_{J_{\ge j}},
 \qquad f\longmapsto f|_{J_{\ge j}}.
\end{equation}
Here the quotient identifies $f,g$ when $f+a=g+b$ for some
$a,b$ in the ideal. Such functions $a,b$ vanish on $J_{\ge j}$,
so equivalence forces equality on that whole upset. Conversely,
if the restrictions agree, choose a real $M$ at least all the
finitely many values of $f,g$. The function $M e_{J\setminus
J_{\ge j}}$ is in the ideal, and adding it to both functions
makes them equal everywhere. This proves the kernel-congruence
assertion. Every antitone function on $J_{\ge j}$ extends by a
sufficiently large constant on its complementary downset, proving
surjectivity. Thus (LSP21) is an actual quotient isomorphism; it
need not equal $K$ before localization.

After localization at this prime the residue quotient is exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP22}
 (A_J)_{\mathfrak p_{j,\{j\}}}/\mathfrak m_{j,\{j\}}\simeq K,
 \qquad h\longmapsto h(j).
\end{equation}
Evaluation is surjective on constants. If $h(j)=h'(j)$, choose a
constant $M$ dominating both functions on $B\setminus\{j\}$.
The function equal to $M$ there and zero at $j$ belongs to the local
maximal ideal and to (LSP15); adding it makes $h,h'$ equal. Conversely
any such equivalence preserves the value at $j$. This proves (LSP22).
For $U\ne\{j\}$ the local Bourne quotient is also zero, by the
image of (LSP20).

Any unital homomorphism to a tropical semifield has a subtractive
zero kernel: if the image of $a+b$ and of $a$ is zero, the image
of $b$ is zero. Consequently none of the additional primes can be
the zero kernel of such a map. The exact tropical-valued-point
classification (ASL28) and the larger ordinary-prime classification
(LSP3) therefore agree through the specified kernel map; neither
geometry has been substituted for the other.

\subsection{The full original prime spectrum is recovered from the companion map}

For a nonzero commutative ring $R$, retain the original, injective
map (ASL22), with all amplitude and support coordinates:
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP23}
 G_L(R)\longrightarrow R\times A_J,
 \qquad(r,\lambda)\longmapsto
        \bigl(r,e_{\{j\in J:j\le\lambda\}}\bigr).
\end{equation}
Injectivity follows because both displayed coordinates are retained
and (LSP1) is injective; preservation of operations follows from
join being union and meet being intersection of these downsets.
Every prime of $R\times A_J$ comes from exactly one factor.
Indeed the two idempotents $(1,0),(0,1)$ multiply to zero and sum
to the unit; a proper prime therefore contains exactly one of them.
It then contains the whole corresponding factor and is the inverse
image of a prime of the other factor. Hence
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP24}
 \operatorname{Spec}(R\times A_J)
   =\operatorname{Spec}R\ \sqcup\ \operatorname{Spec}A_J.
\end{equation}
The contractions under (LSP23) are exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP25}
 \begin{gathered}
 \mathfrak r\in\operatorname{Spec}R\longmapsto
  \{(0,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}
      \cup\{(r,1_L):r\in\mathfrak r\},\\
 (j,U)\longmapsto
  \{(0,\lambda):j\nleq\lambda\}.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
To check the second formula, an idempotent $e_D$ belongs to
$\mathfrak p_{j,U}$ exactly when $j\notin D$, by (LSP5).
Every nonzero supported amplitude, and the supported zero at top
support, maps to the unit function. The first formula follows
directly from the amplitude coordinate. The original prime
classification (ASL12) now proves that (LSP24) maps surjectively
onto the whole original spectrum of $G_L(R)$. The fibre over an
arithmetic prime is its one $R$-prime; the fibre over a support
prime labelled $j$ is exactly all upsets $U\subseteq J_{\le j}$
containing $j$. This is the precise simultaneous relation between
the ring amplitudes, every support prime, and the additional tropical
prime geometry. It makes no cohomological or purity identification.

\subsection{The complete Frobenius action on these primes}

For every real $t>0$, coefficient Frobenius is the automorphism
$\operatorname{Fr}_t(f)(x)=f(x)^t$. It preserves every zero,
equality, and strict inequality in (LSP3), so
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP26}
 \operatorname{Fr}_t^{-1}(\mathfrak p_{j,U})
       =\mathfrak p_{j,U}\qquad(t>0).
\end{equation}
Thus its action on this ordinary prime spectrum is the identity,
although its action on coefficient functions and on the local
semirings remains the indicated power operation. At the additional
zero element of $\mathbb N_0^\times$, use the actual convention
(ASL32), which gives $\operatorname{Fr}_0(f)=e_{\operatorname{supp}f}$.
By (LSP5), the spectral action is precisely
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP27}
 \operatorname{Fr}_0^{-1}(\mathfrak p_{j,U})
      =\mathfrak p_{j,\{j\}},\qquad
 (j,U)\longmapsto(j,\{j\}).
\end{equation}
More explicitly, the semiring maps
$L\xrightarrow{D\mapsto e_D} A_J
\xrightarrow{f\mapsto\operatorname{supp}f} L$
compose to the identity on $L$, while their reverse composite is
$\operatorname{Fr}_0$. The corresponding spectral maps are the
projection $(j,U)\mapsto j$ and its section $j\mapsto(j,\{j\})$.
This proves an actual retraction onto the evaluation primes. It
does not identify the additional primes with them: their distinct
specializations and localized functions remain (LSP10)--(LSP18).

\subsection{Exact naturality for every finite bounded-lattice morphism}

Let $\phi:L\to L'$ preserve zero, unit, join, and meet, with both
lattices finite, nontrivial, and distributive. Put $J=J(L)$ and
$J'=J(L')$. The dual monotone map $g:J'\to J$ is uniquely specified
by
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP28}
 j'\le\phi(\lambda)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 g(j')\le\lambda\qquad(\lambda\in L).
\end{equation}
To prove existence, evaluate $\phi$ at the character of $j'$.
The resulting bounded lattice character of $L$ is the character
of a unique $j\in J$, by the proof of (LSP5), and this $j$ defines
$g(j')$. For monotonicity, if $j'\le k'$ then
$k'\le\phi(\lambda)$ implies $j'\le\phi(\lambda)$ for every
$\lambda$. Taking $\lambda=g(k')$ in (LSP28) gives
$g(j')\le g(k')$. Under (LSP1) the entire map is therefore
$\phi(D)=g^{-1}(D)$. Conversely inverse image under any monotone
$g$ preserves downsets, empty and full sets, unions, and
intersections, and hence gives exactly such a $\phi$.

The induced coefficient map has the full coordinate formula
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP29}
 \Phi=\operatorname{id}_K\otimes\phi:A_J\longrightarrow A_{J'},
 \qquad \Phi(f)=f\circ g.
\end{equation}
On a pure tensor $a\otimes D$ it sends its characteristic support
to $g^{-1}(D)$ and keeps the coefficient $a$, so (LSP29) follows
on all finite sums. Pointwise operations also prove directly that
this is a unital semiring map. It commutes with every positive real
Frobenius and with $\operatorname{Fr}_0$, since both power and
the nonzero-support test commute with precomposition.

Fix an arbitrary target prime $\mathfrak p_{j',U'}$. Its exact
contraction is
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP30}
 \Phi^{-1}(\mathfrak p_{j',U'})=\mathfrak p_{j,U},
 \qquad j=g(j'),\qquad
 U=\{x\le j:\ \exists u'\in U',\ g(u')\le x\}.
\end{equation}
The set $U$ is the upward closure of $g(U')$ inside $J_{\le j}$,
is upward closed there, and contains $j$. Membership of $\Phi(f)$
in the target prime says either $f(g(j'))=0$ or
$f(g(u'))>f(g(j'))$ for some $u'\in U'$. Every $g(u')$ belongs
to $U$, so this implies membership on the right of (LSP30).
Conversely, if $f(x)>f(j)$ for $x\in U$, choose $u'\in U'$
with $g(u')\le x$. Antitonicity gives
$f(g(u'))\ge f(x)>f(j)$. The zero alternative is identical on
both sides. This proves equality of the two ideals, with no loss
of the upward-closure labels.

The associated local map is just as explicit:
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP31}
 (A_J)_{\mathfrak p_{j,U}}\longrightarrow
 (A_{J'})_{\mathfrak p_{j',U'}},\qquad
 h\longmapsto h\circ(g|_{J'_{\le j'}}).
\end{equation}
Its domain is correct because $g(J'_{\le j'})\subseteq J_{\le j}$.
An allowed nonzero-support downset in (LSP15) pulls back to a
downset. On $U'$, the arguments of $h$ lie in $g(U')\subseteq U$,
where $h$ is antitone, so the image satisfies the target order
condition. Formula (LSP30) makes every denominator outside the
source prime map outside the target prime. Thus (LSP31) is the
canonical localization map, as can also be checked on its fractions
$\Phi(f)/\Phi(q)=(f/q)\circ g$. It is local: a value function
$h$ is a unit precisely when $h(j)>0$ and it is constant on $U$,
by (LSP17). Its image is a unit precisely when $h(g(j'))>0$ and
it is constant on $g(U')$. These conditions are equivalent, since
each $x\in U$ lies between some $g(u')$ and $j$, all in $U$;
the antitone inequalities force $h(x)=h(j)$ when both endpoint
values equal $h(j)$. This proves the inverse image of the target
maximal ideal is the source maximal ideal.

All maps compose with their full labels. If a second bounded-lattice
map $L'\to L''$ has dual $r:J''\to J'$, the dual of its composite
with $\phi$ is $g\circ r$ by (LSP28), and the coefficient maps
compose by the ordinary identity
$(f\circ g)\circ r=f\circ(g\circ r)$. The required equality for
prime labels is
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP32}
 \mathord{\uparrow}_{\le g(r(j''))}
       g\!\left(\mathord{\uparrow}_{\le r(j'')}r(U'')\right)
 =\mathord{\uparrow}_{\le g(r(j''))}(g\circ r)(U'').
\end{equation}
Indeed an intermediate element $y'$ obeys
$r(u'')\le y'\le r(j'')$ for some $u''\in U''$, hence
$g(r(u''))\le g(y')\le g(r(j''))$, proving one inclusion.
The other follows because each $r(u'')$ is itself in the
intermediate upward closure. This proves (LSP32) and the
composition of (LSP30). Localized precomposition in (LSP31)
then composes identically on every retained coordinate.

The maps also fit the original split-support base, not only its
tropical coefficient extension. For any fixed nonzero commutative
ring $R$, define
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP33}
 G_\phi:G_L(R)\longrightarrow G_{L'}(R),\qquad
 (r,\lambda)\longmapsto(r,\phi(\lambda)).
\end{equation}
This is defined on the original carriers: nonzero amplitudes have
top support, which $\phi$ preserves, while zero amplitudes admit
every image support. The operations in (ASL1) prove that it
preserves addition, multiplication, zero, and unit. It also sends
the global zero $\tau$ to $\tau$ and the supported zero $e$ to
$e$. The companion maps (LSP23) obey the exact equality
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP34}
 (\operatorname{id}_R\times\Phi)\circ\iota_L
       =\iota_{L'}\circ G_\phi.
\end{equation}
For verification at $(r,\lambda)$, both sides have amplitude $r$
and characteristic support $g^{-1}(\{j:j\le\lambda\})$, which
equals $\{j':j'\le\phi(\lambda)\}$ by (LSP28).

Write $\mathfrak s_j=\{(0,\lambda):j\nleq\lambda\}$ for an
original support prime and $Q_{\mathfrak r}$ for its original
arithmetic prime at $\mathfrak r\in\operatorname{Spec}R$, as
in (ASL12). Direct contraction gives
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP35}
 G_\phi^{-1}(\mathfrak s_{j'})=\mathfrak s_{g(j')},
 \qquad G_\phi^{-1}(Q_{\mathfrak r})=Q_{\mathfrak r}.
\end{equation}
The first follows from (LSP28), with no nonzero supported amplitude
entering a support prime. The second follows because amplitude is
unchanged and all zero-supported elements are in every arithmetic
prime. Consequently the spectral contraction square associated
to (LSP34) commutes explicitly: on tropical primes it is
$(j',U')\mapsto(g(j'),U)\mapsto g(j')$, which also results from
$(j',U')\mapsto j'\mapsto g(j')$; on arithmetic primes both
paths retain $\mathfrak r$. Each entire fibre of upsets in (LSP25)
therefore maps by the exact upward-closure formula (LSP30), not
merely by the map on its base point.

Finally the full original support stalks participate in the same
local square. In finite-lattice coordinates, (ASL15)--(ASL16) give
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP36}
 (G_L(R))_{\mathfrak s_j}\simeq
 \operatorname{Down}(J_{\le j}),\qquad
 (r,\lambda)\longmapsto
     \{x\le j:x\le\lambda\}.
\end{equation}
Indeed among the lattice denominators containing $j$, the least
is $J_{\le j}$, and localizing that idempotent restricts every
downset to it. The map of these stalks induced by $G_\phi$ is
\begin{equation}\tag{LSP37}
 D\longmapsto\{x'\le j':g(x')\in D\}.
\end{equation}
Their embeddings into the two local tropical semirings send $D$
to $e_D$, which satisfies every support and order condition in
(LSP15). Applying (LSP31) to such an idempotent yields exactly the
characteristic function of (LSP37). This proves commutation of
the local coefficient square, retaining each original stalk and
each chosen prime label $U,U'$ in its proper place.
