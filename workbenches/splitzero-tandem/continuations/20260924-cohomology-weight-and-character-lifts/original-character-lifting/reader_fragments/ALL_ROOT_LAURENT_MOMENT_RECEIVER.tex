\label{sec:all-root-laurent-moment-receiver}

\paragraph{Provenance and scope.}
The supplied \path{non_eulerian_modular_heat_attempt.md},
Sections 3--5 and equations (7.1)--(7.3), supplies the particular
full-root Laurent receiver, its original residue constants, and its

\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.05914v5}{\emph{The de Bruijn--Newman
constant is non-negative}}, equations defining $\Phi$, $H_t$, and

calculation supplied here is the explicit finite interpolation and controlled

It proves completeness of this particular receiver; it does not assert

\subsection{The original heat family and its entire zero multiset}

Retain the original real time $t$, coordinate $Z$, kernel, and mass:
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM1}
\begin{gathered}
 H_t(Z)=\int_0^\infty e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)\cos(Zu)\,du,\\
 \Phi(u)=\sum_{n\ge1}
 (2\pi^2n^4e^{9u}-3\pi n^2e^{5u})e^{-\pi n^2e^{4u}},\\
 M_j(t)=\int_0^\infty u^{2j}e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)\,du,
 \qquad H_t(0)=M_0(t)>0.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Every summand of $\Phi$ is positive on $[0,\infty)$, since
$2\pi n^2e^{4u}-3>0$. Moreover
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM2}
 0<\Phi(u)\le C e^{9u-(\pi/2)e^{4u}},\qquad
 C=2\pi^2\sum_{n\ge1}n^4e^{-(\pi/2)n^2}<\infty.
\end{equation}
Indeed, discard the negative summand and use
$n^2e^{4u}\ge(n^2+e^{4u})/2$. This bound justifies all derivatives of
(ALM1) locally uniformly in $(t,Z)\in\mathbb C^2$. The actual $H_t$
is entire, even in $Z$, real on the real axis for real $t$, and obeys
$\partial_t H_t=-\partial_Z^2H_t$.

For completeness, its order is at most one. For fixed real $t$ there
is a finite $C_t$ such that
$|t|u^2+9u\le (\pi/8)e^{4u}+C_t$ for all $u\ge0$.
Use $|\cos Zu|\le e^{|Z|u}$ and split the remaining exponential to get
\[
 |H_t(Z)|\le C e^{C_t}
 \left(\int_0^\infty e^{-(\pi/4)e^{4u}}du\right)
 \exp\!\left(\sup_{u\ge0}\{|Z|u-(\pi/8)e^{4u}\}\right).
\]
The supremum is bounded by $C'(1+|Z|\log(2+|Z|))$, by solving its
one-variable derivative equation, or by taking its endpoint value if
that critical point lies outside $[0,\infty)$. Thus
$\log\max_{|Z|\le R}|H_t(Z)|=O_t(1+R\log(2+R))$.
Jensen's formula on radius $2R$, with $H_t(0)>0$, gives the zero count
$n_t(R)=O_t(R\log(2+R))$: each zero in the radius-$R$ disk contributes
at least $\log2$ to the radius-$2R$ Jensen sum. Dyadic annuli then give
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM3}
 V_t:=\sum_{\zeta:H_t(\zeta)=0}\frac{m_\zeta}{|\zeta|^2}<\infty.
\end{equation}
The finitely many roots near zero cause no problem, because zero is
not a root. All sums over $\zeta$ below run over distinct roots with
their full multiplicities $m_\zeta$.

Hadamard factorization at order at most one, with the factors paired
under $\zeta\mapsto-\zeta$, now gives
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM4}
 H_t(Z)=H_t(0)\prod_{\{\zeta,-\zeta\}}
                  (1-Z^2/\zeta^2)^{m_\zeta},\qquad
 \frac{H_t'(Z)}{H_t(Z)}=-\sum_{n\ge1}S_n(t)Z^{2n-1},
 \quad S_n(t)=\sum_\zeta\frac{m_\zeta}{\zeta^{2n}}.
\end{equation}
The product ranges over one representative of each sign pair and
converges locally uniformly by (ALM3). In the Hadamard expression the
remaining exponential has degree at most one. The evenness of $H_t$
and of the paired product forces its linear coefficient to vanish:
$e^{aZ}=e^{-aZ}$ for all $Z$ implies $a=0$ by differentiation at zero.
Evaluation at zero fixes the remaining constant as $H_t(0)$.
Logarithmic differentiation and expansion on a disk containing no
root prove the second equality. In particular the $S_n$ include
\emph{both} members of every sign pair.

There are infinitely many distinct roots. Otherwise (ALM4) would be
a polynomial. But its Taylor coefficient of $Z^{2j}$ is
$(-1)^jM_j(t)/(2j)!$, which is nonzero for every $j$ by (ALM1).
Also $H_t(iy)>0$ for every real $y$, since its integrand becomes the
strictly positive function $e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)\cosh(yu)$. Thus the actual
theta family has no imaginary-axis roots. We shall nevertheless give
the negative-real reciprocal-node case explicitly, so that the
detection argument does not hide that possibility in its algebra.

\subsection{The full local algebras and the exact trace receiver}

Let $\mathcal Z$ be any finite conjugation-stable set of roots. Retain
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM5}
 A_{t,\mathcal Z}=\bigoplus_{\zeta\in\mathcal Z}
       \mathcal O_{\mathbb C,\zeta}/(H_t),\qquad
 E_{d,t,\mathcal Z}(c)=
 \left[Z^{-1}p_c(Z^{-2})\right]_{\mathcal Z},\qquad
 p_c(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}c_iX^i.
\end{equation}
Every inverse power in this expression is the inverse of the
holomorphic unit $Z$ at a nonzero root. No inverse of supported zero
or of a root collision gap occurs. The local algebra retains its
entire length $m_\zeta$. The compatible maps for finite $\mathcal Z$
also define a map to the product of all these local algebras.
The precise domain of the infinite pairing is the complex vector space
\[
 \mathscr R=\{Z^{-1}p(Z^{-2}):p\in\mathbb C[X]\},\qquad
 \mathcal E_d:\mathbb C^d\longrightarrow\mathscr R,
 \quad c\longmapsto Z^{-1}p_c(Z^{-2}).
\]
Let
\[
 j_t:\mathscr R\longrightarrow
        \prod_{\zeta:H_t(\zeta)=0}\mathcal O_{\mathbb C,\zeta}/(H_t),
 \qquad F\longmapsto([F]_\zeta)_\zeta,
 \qquad E_{d,t}=j_t\mathcal E_d.
\]
The map $j_t$ is injective: a nonzero Laurent polynomial has only
finitely many nonzero zeros, whereas $H_t$ has infinitely many
distinct roots. Thus $j_t(\mathscr R)$ is a second exact realization
of this test space. The pairing below has domain
$\mathcal T_t:\mathscr R\times\mathscr R\to\mathbb C$, or equivalently
$j_t(\mathscr R)\times j_t(\mathscr R)$ via this injection. It is not
defined here on an unrestricted product of local algebras. In
particular, the notation using $E_{d,t}$ below means this specified
image and its transported pairing.

The original $s$-coordinate and reflected involution are
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM6}
 \alpha(Z)=\tfrac12+\tfrac{iZ}{2},\quad
 g_t(s)=16H_t(-2i(s-\tfrac12)),\quad
 f^\dagger(s)=\overline{f(1-\bar s)}.
\end{equation}
Writing $F=\alpha^*f$, one has
$\alpha^*(f^\dagger)(Z)=\overline{F(\bar Z)}=:F^\dagger(Z)$ because
$\alpha(\bar Z)=1-\overline{\alpha(Z)}$. Direct substitution gives
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM7}
 \frac{ds}{g_t(s)}=\frac{i}{32}\frac{dZ}{H_t(Z)},\qquad
 \alpha^*g_t'=-32iH_t',\qquad
 (i/32)(-32i)=1.
\end{equation}
Consequently the complete Jacobian residue contraction is exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM8}
 \mathcal T_t(F,G)=\sum_\zeta
  \operatorname{Res}_{Z=\zeta}
  F^\dagger(Z)G(Z)\frac{H_t'(Z)}{H_t(Z)}dZ
 =\sum_\zeta m_\zeta\overline{F(\bar\zeta)}G(\zeta).
\end{equation}
Indeed, in a full local algebra write $H_t=u(Z)(Z-\zeta)^m$ with
$u(\zeta)\ne0$. Then $H_t'/H_t=m/(Z-\zeta)+u'/u$; the second term
has zero residue. The resulting contraction has a nilpotent radical,
but neither the local algebra nor its evaluation map has been
replaced by a reduced algebra. For the functions in (ALM5), the
summands in (ALM8) are $O(m_\zeta/|\zeta|^2)$ at infinity. They are
therefore absolutely summable by (ALM3).

For $c,b\in\mathbb C^d$, direct multiplication gives
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM9}
 \mathcal T_t(E_{d,t}c,E_{d,t}b)=c^*K_d(t)b,\qquad
 K_d(t)=(S_{i+j+1}(t))_{0\le i,j<d}.
\end{equation}
Conjugate roots have equal multiplicity, so every $S_n$ is real and
$K_d$ is real symmetric. Notice that at a nonreal root the summand is
$\overline{F(\bar\zeta)}F(\zeta)$, not $|F(\zeta)|^2$.

\subsection{Aggregation without deleting either root of a sign pair}

Fix $t$ for the following construction. Define the exact reciprocal
nodes and their aggregated multiplicities by
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM10}
 \Lambda=\{\zeta^{-2}:H_t(\zeta)=0\},\qquad
 M_\lambda=\sum_{\zeta^{-2}=\lambda}m_\zeta,\qquad
 R=\max_{\lambda\in\Lambda}|\lambda|>0,\qquad
 V=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}M_\lambda|\lambda|=V_t.
\end{equation}
Each nonzero fiber consists of the two distinct roots $\zeta,-\zeta$;
thus $M_\lambda=2m_\zeta$. This is an exact aggregation of the original
sum, with no halving of multiplicity. The set $\Lambda$ has no
accumulation except zero, and each subset bounded away from zero is
finite. The maximum defining $R$ exists because the nonempty discrete
root set has a positive minimum modulus. Conjugation preserves
$\Lambda$ and $M_{\bar\lambda}=M_\lambda$.

If $p$ has real coefficients, (ALM9) becomes
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM11}
 Q_t(p):=c^TK_d(t)c
       =\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}M_\lambda\lambda p(\lambda)^2.
\end{equation}
This converges absolutely and is real. For a complex polynomial the
correct expression is instead
$\sum_\lambda M_\lambda\lambda\overline{p(\bar\lambda)}p(\lambda)$.
The real polynomial subspace already detects every failure below.

\subsection{Explicit finite witnesses and a bound for the entire omitted tail}

Take a node $\lambda_0\in\Lambda$ which is not positive real. Choose
any real $r$ with $0<r<|\lambda_0|$, and retain the complete finite set
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM12}
 S=\{\lambda\in\Lambda:|\lambda|\ge r\},\qquad
 L_0(X)=\prod_{\mu\in S\setminus\{\lambda_0\}}
                    \frac{X-\mu}{\lambda_0-\mu},\qquad
 B=\prod_{\mu\in S\setminus\{\lambda_0\}}
                    \frac{r+|\mu|}{|\lambda_0-\mu|}.
\end{equation}
All denominators are nonzero, $L_0(\lambda_0)=1$, and $L_0$ vanishes
at the other nodes in $S$. On $|X|\le r$ one has $|L_0(X)|\le B$ by
the triangle inequality applied factor by factor. Put $q=r/|\lambda_0|<1$.

\paragraph{A negative real reciprocal node.}
If $\lambda_0<0$, the finite set $S\setminus\{\lambda_0\}$ is
conjugation-stable. Hence $L_0$ has real coefficients, as can also be
seen by pairing its conjugate factors. For every integer $N\ge0$ set
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM13}
 p_N(X)=(X/\lambda_0)^N L_0(X),\qquad
 d_N=N+|S|.
\end{equation}
Its degree is at most $d_N-1$, it is real, and its value at the target
node is one. It vanishes at every other node of $S$. The omitted tail
has the explicit bound
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM14}
 \left|Q_t(p_N)-M_{\lambda_0}\lambda_0\right|
 \le B^2q^{2N}\sum_{\lambda\notin S}M_\lambda|\lambda|
 \le B^2Vq^{2N}.
\end{equation}
Choose any $N$ with $B^2Vq^{2N}<M_{\lambda_0}|\lambda_0|/2$.
Then the complete form, including every root, satisfies
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM15}
 Q_t(p_N)<-M_{\lambda_0}|\lambda_0|/2<0.
\end{equation}
This case corresponds exactly to the nonzero imaginary roots
$\zeta^2=1/\lambda_0<0$. The positivity argument after (ALM4) already
rules them out for the actual family, but the receiver detects them
without using that argument.

\paragraph{A nonreal reciprocal node.}
If $\lambda_0\notin\mathbb R$, choose a unit complex number $a$ with
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM16}
 a^2=-\overline{\lambda_0}/|\lambda_0|.
\end{equation}
Either square root works. Define
$L_{\bar0}(X)=\overline{L_0(\bar X)}$. It is precisely the Lagrange
polynomial for the node $\bar\lambda_0$ on the same finite set $S$.
The polynomial
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM17}
 p_N(X)=a(X/\lambda_0)^NL_0(X)
       +\bar a(X/\bar\lambda_0)^NL_{\bar0}(X),\qquad
 d_N=N+|S|
\end{equation}
has real coefficients, because its two summands are coefficientwise
conjugates. Its values at the two target nodes are $a,\bar a$, and
it vanishes on $S\setminus\{\lambda_0,\bar\lambda_0\}$.
The two target contributions to (ALM11) add to
\[
 M_{\lambda_0}(\lambda_0a^2+
                       \bar\lambda_0\bar a^2)
                   =-2M_{\lambda_0}|\lambda_0|.
\]
For $|X|<r$ both Lagrange polynomials are bounded by $B$, so
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM18}
 \left|Q_t(p_N)+2M_{\lambda_0}|\lambda_0|\right|
 \le 4B^2Vq^{2N}.
\end{equation}
Choose $N$ with $4B^2Vq^{2N}<M_{\lambda_0}|\lambda_0|$. Then
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM19}
 Q_t(p_N)<-M_{\lambda_0}|\lambda_0|<0.
\end{equation}
Such an integer is specified finitely, for example by
\[
 N=\max\left\{0,
 1+\left\lceil
 \frac{\log(4B^2V/(M_{\lambda_0}|\lambda_0|))}
      {2\log(|\lambda_0|/r)}\right\rceil\right\}.
\]
For the negative-real case use the same formula with $4B^2$ replaced
by $2B^2$. The additional $1$ makes the required inequality strict
even if the logarithmic ratio is an integer. These bounds involve
the actual root multiset and its convergent total $V$; no finite
root list has been declared complete without a tail estimate.

An off-real root $\zeta$ always produces one of these two cases:
$\zeta^{-2}$ can be positive real only when $\zeta$ itself is real.
If its reciprocal square is negative real, $\zeta$ is imaginary;
otherwise the reciprocal square is nonreal. This proves that the
construction reaches every off-real root and every multiplicity.

\subsection{Exact rational coefficients and the all-zero equivalence}

The coefficients constructed above need not be rational. There is an
explicit perturbation bound producing a rational witness of the same
degree. For a real polynomial define
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM20}
 \|p\|_R=\sum_i|c_i|R^i.
\end{equation}
This is a coefficient bound, not a change to the original trace form.
Since $|\lambda|\le R$, if $p,q$ are real polynomials and
$\delta=\|p-q\|_R$, then (ALM11) gives
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM21}
 |Q_t(q)-Q_t(p)|
 \le V\delta(2\|p\|_R+\delta).
\end{equation}
Indeed, $q^2-p^2=(q-p)(q+p)$ pointwise on the reciprocal nodes,
$|p(\lambda)|\le\|p\|_R$, and
$|q(\lambda)|\le\|p\|_R+\delta$.

For either negative witness above take a positive proved bound
$\eta$ with $Q_t(p)\le-\eta$ (the right sides of (ALM15) or (ALM19)
supply one). Choose
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM22}
 0<\delta_0\le\min\{1,\eta/[4V(2\|p\|_R+1)]\}.
\end{equation}
For $d=1+\deg p$, choose rational numbers $b_i$ satisfying
$|b_i-c_i|<\delta_0/(dR^i)$, and put $q(X)=\sum_{i<d}b_iX^i$.
The density of the rationals supplies every one of these finite
choices; for example a sufficiently fine integer grid in each
coefficient does so. Then $\|q-p\|_R<\delta_0$ and
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM23}
 b^TK_d(t)b=Q_t(q)<-3\eta/4<0,\qquad b\in\mathbb Q^d.
\end{equation}
Multiplying by a common positive denominator even gives an integer
coefficient witness; its trace value is multiplied by the square of
that denominator, so its sign is preserved exactly.

We have therefore proved the complete equivalence for the actual
family, at every fixed real time $t$:
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM24}
\begin{aligned}
 &\text{every zero of }H_t\text{ is real}\\
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow K_d(t)\succeq0\text{ for every }d\ge1\\
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow K_d(t)\succ0\text{ for every }d\ge1\\
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow
       b^TK_d(t)b\ge0\text{ for every }d\ge1,\ b\in\mathbb Q^d.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The forward implication follows directly from (ALM8): all summands
then equal $m_\zeta|F(\zeta)|^2$. Strict positive definiteness holds
because there are infinitely many distinct reciprocal nodes and a
nonzero polynomial has only finitely many zeros. The reverse
implication and the rational version are (ALM12)--(ALM23).
Complex coefficient vectors add no new positivity condition because
$K_d$ is real symmetric: for real $a,b$,
$(a+ib)^*K_d(a+ib)=a^TK_da+b^TK_db$.
At physical time zero the original coordinate
$s=1/2+iZ/2$ converts (ALM24) to the assertion that the zeros of the
original $\xi$ lie on its critical line. A single fixed size, such as
$d=32$, does not establish the quantifier over every $d$ in (ALM24).

\subsection{The actual coefficient and heat production maps}

No root positions need be inserted into the finite matrices to
calculate their entries. Let $a_j(t)=(-1)^jM_j(t)/(2j)!$, retaining
$a_0=M_0=H_t(0)$. Multiplying (ALM4) by $H_t$ and taking the
coefficient of $Z^{2n-1}$ gives
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM25}
 M_0S_n=-2n a_n-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}a_jS_{n-j}\qquad(n\ge1).
\end{equation}
Only the nonzero original mass $M_0(t)$ is a denominator when this
recurrence is solved. The entire function has not been divided by its
mass or assigned a different value at zero. Since $M_0(t)>0$ for all
real $t$, (ALM25) also proves that all $S_n(t)$ are real analytic in
real time, including times at which other zeros collide.

Put $P=H_t'/H_t$ in a neighborhood of $Z=0$. The actual heat equation
gives $\partial_tP=-P''-2PP'$. Substituting
$P=-\sum_{n\ge1}S_nZ^{2n-1}$ proves
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM26}
 S_n'=2n\left[\sum_{a=1}^{n}S_aS_{n+1-a}-(2n+1)S_{n+1}\right].
\end{equation}
To check the convolution factor, the coefficient of $Z^{2n-1}$ in
$PP'$ is $\sum_{a+b=n+1}(2b-1)S_aS_b$; exchanging $a$ and $b$ shows
this equals $n\sum_{a=1}^nS_aS_{n+1-a}$. The coefficient in $-P''$
is $2n(2n+1)S_{n+1}$. Comparing with $-S_n'$ gives exactly (ALM26).
Thus the supplied production formula is correct with its original
time, signs, and factors. In particular, for a constant coefficient
vector $c$ the exact production is
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM27}
 \frac{d}{dt}(c^*K_dc)=
 \sum_{i,j<d}\bar c_i c_j\,2(i+j+1)
 \left[\sum_{a=1}^{i+j+1}S_aS_{i+j+2-a}
                  -(2i+2j+3)S_{i+j+2}\right].
\end{equation}
If $c=c(t)$ moves, the additional two terms are
$c'^*K_dc+c^*K_dc'$; omitting them would change the problem.
The first production value has the strictly positive exact expression
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM28}
 S_1'=\frac{M_2M_0-M_1^2}{M_0^2}>0.
\end{equation}
Here $M_0'=M_1$, $M_1'=M_2$, and the numerator is strictly positive
by Cauchy--Schwarz on the original measure
$e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)du$: the function $u^2$ is not constant on its support.
This proves directly that the full-root trace evolves under physical
heat; it is not an invariant obtained by dropping the production term.

\subsection{Full arbitrary-lattice realization of every map}

Let $L$ be the original arbitrary nontrivial bounded distributive
lattice. For every complex vector space $V$ retain
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM29}
 G_L(V)=\{(0,\ell):\ell\in L\}\cup(V\times\{1_L\}),\quad
 (v,\ell)+(w,k)=(v+w,\ell\vee k),\quad
 (c,h)(v,\ell)=(cv,h\wedge\ell).
\end{equation}
The scalar semiring is $G_L(\mathbb C)$. The carrier is closed under
these operations because a nonzero amplitude in a sum requires at
least one top input label, and a nonzero product requires both labels
to be top. The semimodule identities follow from the vector-space
identities and distributivity of $\wedge$ over $\vee$.

For a complex-linear map $A:V\to W$ define
$A^\uparrow(v,\ell)=(Av,\ell)$. Addition, scalar multiplication, and
every zero label are preserved by direct substitution. Apply this to
coefficient inclusions, polynomial evaluation, the entire local-jet
maps (ALM5), finite packet restriction, and each finite-packet linear
residue trace. For the infinite pairing use exactly $\mathscr R$ or
its injective image $j_t(\mathscr R)$ as specified after (ALM5).
The quadratic trace value is the sesquilinear lift
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM30}
 \mathcal T_t^L((F,\ell),(G,k))
            =(\mathcal T_t(F,G),\ell\wedge k).
\end{equation}
It is a well-defined element of $G_L(\mathbb C)$: a nonzero trace
requires two nonzero amplitudes and hence two top labels. Additivity
in either argument follows from the scalar trace and lattice
distributivity; scalar conjugation is
$(c,h)^*=(\bar c,h)$. Therefore
\[
 \mathcal T_t^L(E_{d,t}^\uparrow(c,\ell),
                       E_{d,t}^\uparrow(b,k))
                   =(c^*K_d(t)b,\ell\wedge k)
\]
is the full supported version of (ALM9), not an amplitude-only
replacement for it.

Retain the programme's idempotent semifield $K$, its Boolean base
$\mathbb B$, and $K_L=K\otimes_{\mathbb B}L$. With
$\iota_L(\ell)=1_K\otimes\ell$, the complete comparison is
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM31}
 \Phi_V(v,\ell)=(v,\iota_L(\ell))\in V\times K_L,\qquad
 (A\times I)\Phi_V=\Phi_W A^\uparrow.
\end{equation}
For clarity, the label injection has a retraction. The map
$\chi:K\to\mathbb B$, $\chi(0)=0$ and $\chi(x)=1$ for $x\ne0$,
is a semiring homomorphism: idempotent addition cannot sum a nonzero
element to zero, and the semifield has no zero divisors. Hence
$\rho_L=\chi\otimes I_L$ satisfies
$\rho_L\iota_L=I_L$. This proves that $\Phi_V$ is injective on the
whole carrier and retains each original lattice label. Neither a
Boolean-character replacement of $L$ nor a localization at $e$ occurs.

The exact residue factors $i/32$ and $-32i$ in (ALM7) are nonzero
scalar maps, so their lifts commute with (ALM31); their product has
amplitude one and top label. The full trace pairing also commutes
with this comparison, with its two input labels multiplied by meet.
Every negative rational witness in (ALM23) therefore has exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{ALM32}
 (b^TK_d(t)b,1_L)\ \xmapsto{\Phi_{\mathbb R}}\
       (b^TK_d(t)b,1_K\otimes1_L),\qquad b^TK_d(t)b<0.
\end{equation}
An amplitude-zero value at the same top support is
$e=(0,1_L)$, whereas external absence is $\tau=(0,0_L)$; (ALM31)
does not identify them. Thus (ALM24) is a complete detection result
inside the full lattice receiver: every off-real root has a finite
rational test with a negative retained amplitude, and that exact test
survives the injective $\Phi$ comparison.
