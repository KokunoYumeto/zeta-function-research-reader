\subsection{Inertia invariants without omitting ramification}

Let $E/\mathbb Q$ be finite Galois, let $G=\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q)$,
and set $n=[E:\mathbb Q]=|G|$. Fix a finite rational prime $p$ and one
place $w\mid p$. The classical field inputs just specified give
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC1}
 \begin{gathered}
 D=G_w,\qquad I=\ker(D\longrightarrow
          \operatorname{Gal}(\kappa_w/\mathbb F_p)),\qquad
 1\longrightarrow I\longrightarrow D\longrightarrow
          \operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb F_{p^f}/\mathbb F_p)
             \longrightarrow1,\\
 e=|I|,\qquad f=[\kappa_w:\mathbb F_p],\qquad
 d_w=[E_w:\mathbb Q_p]=ef,\qquad
 g=\#\{w'\mid p\}=\frac{n}{ef},\qquad q_w=|\kappa_w|=p^f.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
All places above $p$ have the same $e,f$, since $E/\mathbb Q$ is Galois.
Write $\overline\phi$ for the arithmetic residue Frobenius
$a\mapsto a^p$, and choose a lift $\phi\in D$. It generates $D/I$,
and $\phi^f\in I$. Changing the lift by an inertia element has no
effect on the operator on $I$-invariants constructed below.

Retain the left regular representation on the amplitude space
$V=\mathbb C[G]$, written as functions on $G$ with delta basis
$\delta_x$. Its conventions are
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC2}
 (\lambda(a)F)(x)=F(a^{-1}x),\qquad
 \lambda(a)\delta_x=\delta_{ax},\qquad
 \langle F,H\rangle=\frac1n\sum_{x\in G}F(x)\overline{H(x)}.
\end{equation}
The displayed Haar mass is one on $G$, so each delta vector has squared
norm $1/n$. No factor is absorbed into these vectors. Put
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC3}
 W_p=V^I,\qquad E_I=\frac1e\sum_{i\in I}\lambda(i),\qquad
 b_{Ix}=\sum_{i\in I}\delta_{ix},\qquad
 P_p=\lambda(\phi)|_{W_p}.
\end{equation}
The operator $E_I$ has image $W_p$: its image is fixed by $I$ by
permuting its summands, and it is the identity on an $I$-fixed vector.
Thus $E_I^2=E_I$. Since $I$ is normal in $D$, $\lambda(\phi)$
commutes with $E_I$, and $P_p$ is well-defined. Each $I$-fixed function
is constant on the left cosets $Ix$, so the $b_{Ix}$ form a basis.
Consequently
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC4}
 \dim W_p=\frac ne=gf,\qquad
 \|b_{Ix}\|^2=\frac en=\frac1{gf},\qquad
 P_pb_{Ix}=b_{I\phi x}.
\end{equation}
There is one label $Ix$ on a whole coset, rather than $e$ independent
copies of that coset in $W_p$. Formula GFC3 retains the precise averaging
factor responsible for this dimension.

For every $j\in\mathbb Z$, the equality $I\phi^jx=Ix$ is equivalent
to $\phi^j\in I$, hence to $f\mid j$. Every cycle of this permutation
therefore has exact length $f$. Its orbits are precisely $D\backslash G$,
since $D$ is generated by $I$ and $\phi$. There are exactly $g$ cycles.
The bijection from these cycles to the actual places above $p$ is
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC5}
 D\backslash G\longrightarrow\{w'\mid p\},\qquad
 Dx\longmapsto x^{-1}w.
\end{equation}
If $x$ is replaced by $dx$ with $d\in D$, its image is unchanged.
Conversely equal images force $yx^{-1}\in D$, or $Dy=Dx$.
Transitivity of the Galois action on the places proves surjectivity.

Choose one representative $x_a$ for each of these $g$ cycles, and let
$\omega_f=\exp(2\pi i/f)$. Every eigenvector and its exact inverse
finite Fourier formula can be written
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC6}
 \begin{gathered}
 v_{a,k}=\sum_{j=0}^{f-1}\omega_f^{-kj}b_{I\phi^jx_a},\qquad
 P_pv_{a,k}=\omega_f^k v_{a,k},\qquad
 \|v_{a,k}\|^2=\frac{fe}{n}=\frac1g,\\
 b_{I\phi^jx_a}=\frac1f\sum_{k=0}^{f-1}\omega_f^{kj}v_{a,k},
 \qquad 0\le k,j<f.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Translation of the index in the finite sum proves the eigenvalue.
The finite geometric sum $\sum_{k=0}^{f-1}\omega_f^{k(j-j')}=
f\,1_{j=j'}$ proves the inverse. Disjoint cosets and GFC4 prove the
norm, and the same geometric sum proves orthogonality. Thus every
$f$th root of unity occurs with multiplicity exactly $g$, including
when $p$ is ramified and $e>1$.

For all $j\in\mathbb Z$ the trace is
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC7}
 \operatorname{Tr}_{W_p}(P_p^j)
   =\operatorname{Tr}_{V}(\lambda(\phi^j)E_I)
   =\frac1e\sum_{i\in I}\operatorname{Tr}_V\lambda(\phi^ji)
   =\begin{cases}n/e=gf,&f\mid j,\\0,&f\nmid j.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The trace of left translation on $V$ is $n$ at the identity and zero
otherwise, as its delta permutation has no fixed point otherwise.
There is exactly one $i\in I$ with $\phi^ji=1$ when $f\mid j$,
and none otherwise. This proves every factor in the formula. In the
coset Haar measure $1/(gf)$ the permutation kernel has diagonal values
$gf$ at fixed cosets, so integration again gives the number of fixed
cosets, not their proportion.

\subsection{The original local and global Euler factors}

Keep $\ell=\log p$ and $q=p^{-s}=e^{-s\ell}$. GFC6 gives the finite
determinant, as a polynomial identity for every $q\in\mathbb C$,
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC8}
 \begin{split}
 D_{E,p}(s)&=\det(1-p^{-s}P_p\mid W_p)
   =\prod_{a=1}^{g}\prod_{k=0}^{f-1}
                  (1-\omega_f^k p^{-s})\\
   &=(1-p^{-fs})^g
    =\prod_{w'\mid p}(1-q_{w'}^{-s}).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The product of the $f$ linear factors is $1-q^f$, since they have
exactly the $f$th roots of unity as reciprocal roots and constant
coefficient one. This proves the displayed identity rather than
assuming a regular-representation Euler-factor theorem.
Replacing $P_p$ by $P_p^{-1}$ replaces each individual root
$\omega_f^k$ by $\omega_f^{-k}$; the full determinant agrees because
inversion permutes all $f$ roots. That agreement never removes the
individual inverse-character convention.

The original Dedekind local Euler product is therefore precisely
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC9}
 \zeta_{E,p}(s)=\prod_{w'\mid p}(1-q_{w'}^{-s})^{-1}
      =D_{E,p}(s)^{-1}=(1-p^{-fs})^{-g}.
\end{equation}
This identity includes every finite rational prime. At ramified $p$,
the ramification degree remains in $n=efg$ and in the projection
$E_I$; the residue norm in the Euler factor is $q_{w'}=p^f$, not
$p^{ef}$. No ramified-prime factor has been set to one or omitted.

For $\Re s>0$, geometric series and the logarithm which tends to zero
as $q\to0$ give absolutely convergent identities
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC10}
 \begin{split}
 \log\zeta_{E,p}(s)
   &=\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{p^{-js}}j\operatorname{Tr}_{W_p}(P_p^j)
     =g\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{p^{-frs}}r
     =\sum_{w'\mid p}\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{q_{w'}^{-rs}}r,\\
 -\frac{\zeta_{E,p}'(s)}{\zeta_{E,p}(s)}
   &=\frac{D_{E,p}'(s)}{D_{E,p}(s)}
     =\ell\sum_{j\ge1}p^{-js}\operatorname{Tr}_{W_p}(P_p^j)\\
   &=gf\ell\sum_{r\ge1}p^{-frs}
     =\frac{gf\ell\,p^{-fs}}{1-p^{-fs}}
     =\sum_{w'\mid p}(\log q_{w'})
                    \sum_{r\ge1}q_{w'}^{-rs}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Differentiation is justified uniformly on compact subsets of this
half-plane by the extra factor $j$ against a geometric series.
The determinant is entire in $s$. Its zeros are exactly
$s=2\pi i k/(f\ell)$, $k\in\mathbb Z$, each of multiplicity $g$.
Each reciprocal local Euler factor is meromorphic with the corresponding
poles. These are local-factor poles, not asserted zeros of a global
zeta function.

The original number-field zeta function retains its ideal series and
all finite factors:
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC11}
 \begin{gathered}
 \zeta_E(s)=\sum_{0\ne\mathfrak a\subset\mathcal O_E}
                       (N\mathfrak a)^{-s}
          =\prod_{p\ {\rm finite}}D_{E,p}(s)^{-1},
          \qquad \Re s>1,\\
 -\frac{\zeta_E'(s)}{\zeta_E(s)}
   =\sum_{p\ {\rm finite}}\sum_{w\mid p}
       (\log q_w)\sum_{r\ge1}q_w^{-rs}
   =\sum_{p\ {\rm finite}}\frac{g_pf_p\log p\,p^{-f_ps}}
                                     {1-p^{-f_ps}}.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Here the subscript $p$ makes explicit that $e,f,g$ change with the
rational prime. Unique factorization of nonzero integral ideals,
the classical field input stated at the start, identifies each term
in the expansion of the finite Euler products with its ideal.
For $\sigma>1$ their absolute logarithmic series is bounded by
$n\sum_p\sum_{r\ge1}p^{-r\sigma}/r$, since $g_p\le n$ and $f_p\ge1$.
This converges by comparison with $\sum_{m\ge2}m^{-\sigma}$ and
the convergent geometric tails. The differentiated series converges
uniformly on $\sigma\ge1+\varepsilon$ after inserting $\log p$,
using $g_pf_p=n/e_p\le n$ and
$\sum_{m\ge2}(\log m)m^{-1-\varepsilon}<\infty$.
These bounds justify the products, series, and differentiation in
their original common domain. No Gamma factor, completion, or
archimedean replacement has been introduced.

\paragraph{Direct comparison with the finite holonomy of PHS.}
For a positive integer $N$ coprime to this $p$, retain
$U_N=(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^\times$, $n_N=\varphi(N)$,
$d_N=\operatorname{ord}_{U_N}(p)$, and the exact operator
$P_NF(u)=F(pu)$ of PHS7--11. On the entire tensor amplitude
$\mathbb C^{U_N}\otimes W_p$ use the tensor product of the Haar
forms already specified, not a new rescaling. The original
characters $\chi$ of $U_N$ have squared norm one. Thus
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC11a}
 \begin{gathered}
 Q_{p,N}=P_N\otimes P_p,\qquad
 Q_{p,N}(\chi\otimes v_{a,k})
       =\chi(p)\omega_f^k(\chi\otimes v_{a,k}),\\
 \|\chi\otimes v_{a,k}\|^2=1/g,\qquad
 \det(1-p^{-s}\chi(p)P_p\mid W_p)
                  =(1-\chi(p)^f p^{-fs})^g.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The eigenvector statement follows by applying the two original
operators to the two factors. The last determinant follows from
GFC6 by retaining $\chi(p)$ in each of its $f$ eigenvalues.
The joint point return is $(u,c)\mapsto(p^{-1}u,Rc)$; inverse
pullback is exactly $Q_{p,N}F(u,c)=F(pu,A(c))$. Its suspension
has deck map $(t,u,c)\mapsto(t+\ell,pu,A(c))$, preserving both
original orientations.

Put $b=\operatorname{lcm}(d_N,f)$. A joint point is fixed by its
$j$th return exactly when both $d_N\mid j$ and $f\mid j$.
Every cycle therefore has length $b$, and there are $n_Ngf/b$
cycles. The full finite determinant and all its trace coefficients are
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC11b}
 \begin{gathered}
 \operatorname{Tr}(Q_{p,N}^j)
  =\operatorname{Tr}(P_N^j)\operatorname{Tr}(P_p^j)
  =\begin{cases}n_Ngf,&b\mid j,\\0,&b\nmid j,\end{cases}\\
 \det(1-p^{-s}Q_{p,N})
   =\prod_{\chi\in\widehat U_N}(1-\chi(p)^f p^{-fs})^g
   =(1-p^{-bs})^{n_Ngf/b},\\
 -\log\det(1-p^{-s}Q_{p,N})
   =\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{p^{-js}}j\operatorname{Tr}(Q_{p,N}^j)
   =\frac{n_Ngf}{b}\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{p^{-brs}}r,\\
 \frac{d}{ds}\log\det(1-p^{-s}Q_{p,N})
   =\frac{n_Ngf\,\ell\,p^{-bs}}{1-p^{-bs}},\qquad \Re s>0.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The trace product is the trace in the tensor delta basis. The
cycle decomposition gives the determinant, or equivalently its
eigenvalues in GFC11a do so. Absolute geometric convergence
proves both series calculations. A joint circle has length
$b\ell$ and measure $b\ell/(n_Ngf)$, with total measure $\ell$.
These finite statements continue to retain the inertia divisor $e$
in $gf=n/e$.

There is an original ideal-series receiver for each individual
character. Extend $\chi$ to integers by zero on nonunits and set
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC11c}
 Z_E(s,\chi)=\sum_{0\ne\mathfrak a\subset\mathcal O_E}
           \chi(N\mathfrak a)(N\mathfrak a)^{-s},\qquad \Re s>1.
\end{equation}
Its absolute convergence is bounded by GFC11. Multiplicativity of
the ideal norm, multiplicativity of this integer character including
its zero values, and unique ideal factorization give its full
original Euler product
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC11d}
 \begin{gathered}
 Z_E(s,\chi)=
   \prod_{r\nmid N}\prod_{w'\mid r}
      (1-\chi(r)^{f_{w'}}r^{-f_{w'}s})^{-1},\\
 Z_{E,p}(s,\chi)=
      (1-\chi(p)^fp^{-fs})^{-g}
      =\det(1-p^{-s}\chi(p)P_p\mid W_p)^{-1},\\
 -\frac{Z_{E,p}'(s,\chi)}{Z_{E,p}(s,\chi)}
   =gf\ell\sum_{m\ge1}\chi(p)^{fm}p^{-fms}
   =\frac{gf\ell\,\chi(p)^fp^{-fs}}{1-\chi(p)^fp^{-fs}},
       \qquad \Re s>0.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Every factor at $r\mid N$ is exactly one in this particular
series, because $\chi(r^{f_{w'}})=0$. This describes the specified
integer-character ideal series, without silently replacing it by
a primitive Hecke $L$-function after pullback. If $\chi_*$ is the
primitive integer character of conductor $c_\chi\mid N$ inducing
$\chi$, comparison of these explicit local factors gives the entire
omitted-prime correction
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC11e}
 Z_E(s,\chi)=Z_E(s,\chi_*)
   \prod_{\substack{r\mid N\\r\nmid c_\chi}}\ 
        \prod_{w'\mid r}
                (1-\chi_*(r)^{f_{w'}}r^{-f_{w'}s}),
                  \qquad \Re s>1.
\end{equation}
This includes every place over each omitted rational prime,
whether ramified in $E$ or not. In particular the trivial
character gives $\zeta_E(s)\prod_{r\mid N}\prod_{w'\mid r}
(1-r^{-f_{w'}s})$, with no missing factor.

For the unit character $\widetilde\chi$ on $C_{\mathbb Q}$ used
in PHS14a, the exact norm comparison later proved in GFC20 yields
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC11f}
 \widetilde\chi\!\left(\iota_p(N_{E_w/\mathbb Q_p}\varpi_w)\right)
    =\chi(p)^{-f},\qquad
 Z_{E,p}(s,\chi)=
   \prod_{w'\mid p}
     \left(1-\widetilde\chi^{-1}
          \!\left(\iota_p(N_{E_{w'}/\mathbb Q_p}\varpi_{w'})\right)

\end{equation}
Indeed $\widetilde\chi$ depends only on the away-$p$ unit

$p$-component is $\varepsilon_w$. Its absence from the value

dependence, not by dropping the unit from the idele.

\subsection{The finite suspension and the exact inverse-pullback orientation}

On the coset set $C_p=I\backslash G$ define the forward point return
$R(c)=I\phi x$ when $c=Ix$. This is well-defined because $\phi$ normalizes
$I$. Its inverse is $A(c)=I\phi^{-1}x$. Define the explicit finite
suspension
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC12}
 \mathcal S_{E,p}=(\mathbb R\times C_p)/
       ((t,c)\sim(t+\ell,A(c))),\qquad
 \Phi_a[t,c]=[t+a,c],\qquad
 U_aF(t,c)=F(t-a,c).
\end{equation}
Its forward return at time $\ell$ is $R$, since
$[\ell,c]=[0,Rc]$. Its Koopman return is
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC13}
 (U_\ell F)(0,c)=F(-\ell,c)=F(0,A(c))
               =(P_p F(0,\cdot))(c).
\end{equation}
This uses exactly the left-regular convention GFC2. Composition with
the forward point return is instead $P_p^{-1}$. This is the same
inverse-pullback rule proved in PHS7--9; it is not an assertion that
an unspecified reciprocity map sends a uniformizer to a particular
choice of Frobenius. Arithmetic Frobenius was fixed in GFC1, and
both orientations have been explicitly constructed.

Each cycle gives a circle of exact period $f\ell$. More precisely,
in the cycle represented by $Ix_a$ the map is
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC14}
 [t,I\phi^jx_a]\longmapsto [t+j\ell]
                   \quad\hbox{in }\mathbb R/(f\ell)\mathbb Z.
\end{equation}
The deck map sends $(t,j)$ to $(t+\ell,j-1)$ and hence preserves
$t+j\ell$. The only ambiguity in $j$ is an integer multiple of $f$,
which changes that value by $f\ell$. Conversely equality of these
values gives exactly a deck equivalence. The inverse sends $[T]$ to
$[T,Ix_a]$. Thus there are $g$ circles, not $n$ circles and not
$g$ circles of period $ef\ell$.

Retain the coset measure inherited from GFC2, of mass $1/(gf)$ at
each $c$, together with the real measure $dt$. The suspension has
total measure $\ell$; each component has measure $f\ell/(gf)=\ell/g$,
and its density in GFC14 is $dT/(gf)$. This is the finite coefficient
space measure constructed here. It is not asserted to equal a
Tamagawa measure or a pushforward of an unspecified adelic measure.

\subsection{Original norm clocks and the unit of a uniformizer norm}

Let $C_F=\mathbb A_F^\times/F^\times$ and
$C_{F,1}=\ker(|\cdot|_F)$, using the original Haar-module absolute
values of GTL13--14. Let $j_w:E_w^\times\to C_E$ and
$\iota_p:\mathbb Q_p^\times\to C_{\mathbb Q}$ be the local-idele
embeddings. The one-zero-place orbit is $C_E/H_w$, where
$H_w=j_w(E_w^\times)$; its base analogue is $C_{\mathbb Q}/H_p$.
These stabilizer identifications are GTL28 and PLP15--20.

For a chosen local uniformizer $\varpi_w$ the local norm has the
exact form
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC15}
 a_w=N_{E_w/\mathbb Q_p}(\varpi_w)=p^f\varepsilon_w,
 \qquad\varepsilon_w\in\mathbb Z_p^\times,\qquad
 |\varpi_w|_w=q_w^{-1}=p^{-f},\qquad |a_w|_p=p^{-f}.
\end{equation}
Indeed multiplication by $\varpi_w$ sends the integer ring to an
additive subgroup of index $q_w$, so its Haar module is $q_w^{-1}$.
The determinant of that multiplication is the field norm, and
GTL13 gives $|a_w|_p=|\varpi_w|_w$. Therefore its $p$-adic valuation
is exactly $f$, which proves the displayed unit factorization.
No equality $a_w=p^f$ is assumed. Replacing $\varpi_w$ by
$v\varpi_w$ with $v\in\mathcal O_w^\times$ changes this retained unit
to $N(v)\varepsilon_w$.

The modulus map gives a complete description of the clock quotient:
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC16}
 (C_E/H_w)/C_{E,1}\simeq
       \mathbb R/(f\ell)\mathbb Z,
 \qquad
 (C_{\mathbb Q}/H_p)/C_{\mathbb Q,1}\simeq
       \mathbb R/\ell\mathbb Z.
\end{equation}
The map sends an idele class to the logarithm of its modulus.
It is onto because positive archimedean ideles have every positive
modulus. Its kernel before dividing by $H_w$ is $C_{E,1}$, and
$\log|H_w|=(f\ell)\mathbb Z$ by the unit/uniformizer decomposition
of $E_w^\times$. These statements prove both the quotient and its
exact fibres. The analogous calculation over $\mathbb Q$ gives
$\ell\mathbb Z$.

Norm preserves the full idele modulus, $|Nc|_{\mathbb Q}=|c|_E$,
by GTL14. Its induced clock map and each of its fibres are therefore
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC17}
 \begin{gathered}
 \nu_w:\mathbb R/(f\ell)\mathbb Z\longrightarrow
                        \mathbb R/\ell\mathbb Z,
                  \qquad [T]\longmapsto[T],\\
 \nu_w^{-1}([T]_\ell)=
    \{[T+j\ell]_{f\ell}:0\le j<f\}.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
It is a covering of exact degree $f$. Taking the disjoint union over
the $g$ places gives total fibre cardinality $gf=n/e$, the dimension
of GFC4. Equations GFC5 and GFC14 give an explicit isomorphism from
the finite suspension to this disjoint union of clock circles after
choosing each $x_a$. It commutes with forward time and the map to
the base clock. Choice of a different representative in a cycle
changes that component's clock origin by an integer multiple of
$\ell$; no canonical origin is asserted. The statement concerns
the disjoint union of these specified quotients, and does not
change the ambient topology of the full adelic orbit union.

For the full return before the compact modulus-one quotient, choose
an exact flow section. At every archimedean place of $E$ set the
component of $\sigma_E(T)$ to the positive real number $e^{T/n}$,
and set its finite components to one. At a complex place the
Haar-module absolute value is the square of the usual modulus.
Writing $r_1+2r_2=n$ proves
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC18}
 |\sigma_E(T)|_E=e^T,\qquad
 N\sigma_E(T)=\sigma_{\mathbb Q}(T),
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_{\mathbb Q}(T)$ has real component $e^T$ and finite
components one. For the second equality, each real local norm of
$e^{T/n}$ is that number and each complex local norm is its square;
their full product is $e^T$, with every finite norm equal to one.

Define the actual retained compact return frames
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC19}
 \begin{gathered}
 T_w=\log q_w=f\ell,\qquad
 \mathfrak{k}_w=\sigma_E(T_w)j_w(\varpi_w)\in C_{E,1},\\
 N\mathfrak{k}_w=\sigma_{\mathbb Q}(f\ell)\iota_p(p^f\varepsilon_w)
                  =:\mathfrak{k}_{p,w}\in C_{\mathbb Q,1},\\
 \sigma_E(T_w)=\mathfrak{k}_wj_w(\varpi_w^{-1}),\qquad
 \sigma_{\mathbb Q}(f\ell)=\mathfrak{k}_{p,w}\iota_p(a_w^{-1}).
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The norm of a local idele at $w$ is precisely the local idele of
its local norm at $p$, since the other factors in GTL2 are norms
of one. This proves the middle equality. The last two identities
follow by multiplication, and retain the full unit contribution.

In the exact coordinates $C_{\mathbb Q}=\mathbb R_{>0}\times
\prod_r\mathbb Z_r^\times$ of PLP20, these frames and local ideles are
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC20}
 \begin{gathered}
 \iota_p(p^f\varepsilon_w)
  =\bigl(p^{-f},\ u_p=\varepsilon_w,
                         \ u_r=p^{-f}\ (r\ne p)\bigr),\\
 \mathfrak{k}_{p,w}
  =\bigl(1,\ u_p=\varepsilon_w,
                         \ u_r=p^{-f}\ (r\ne p)\bigr),\\
 \mathfrak{k}_{p,w}=\mathfrak{k}_{p,0}\iota_p(\varepsilon_w),\qquad
 \mathfrak{k}_{p,0}=\bigl(1,\ u_p=1,
                         \ u_r=p^{-f}\ (r\ne p)\bigr).
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Thus the away-$p$ return is exactly the $f$th iterate $u\mapsto
p^{-f}u$ of PHS2, while the $p$-unit remains explicitly in its
stabilizer frame. It disappears only from the clock quotient and
the zero-place orbit coordinate, where that stabilizer is divided
out. It need not disappear from a transverse coordinate or test.

\subsection{The exact norm map on the associated transverse spaces}

For clarity retain the associated transverse spaces and relations
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC21}
 \begin{gathered}
 \mathcal B_w=(C_E\times E_w)/
       ((c j_w(h),z)\sim(c,hz)),\\
 \mathcal B_p=(C_{\mathbb Q}\times\mathbb Q_p)/
       ((d\iota_p(b),y)\sim(d,by)),\\
 \mathcal N_w:\mathcal B_w\longrightarrow\mathcal B_p,
 \qquad[c,z]\longmapsto[Nc,N_{E_w/\mathbb Q_p}(z)].
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The local field norm at zero is zero. The map is well-defined:
the two representatives in the first line are sent respectively
to $[Nc\,\iota_p(Nh),Nz]$ and $[Nc,N(hz)]$, which agree by the
second relation and norm multiplicativity. It intertwines the
flows $\sigma_E(T)$ and $\sigma_{\mathbb Q}(T)$ by GFC18.
This is an exact map of the stated associated spaces. The local
fibre map $z\mapsto Nz$ is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
$ef$, not an additive linear identification of $E_w$ with
$\mathbb Q_p$.

In the compact frames of GFC19, the forward return at $T_w$ and

 [c,z]\longmapsto[\mathfrak{k}_wc,\varpi_w^{-1}z],\qquad
 [Nc,Nz]\longmapsto[\mathfrak{k}_{p,w}Nc,

\end{equation}
Indeed moving the local factor in the last line of GFC19 across

 [\mathfrak{k}_{p,0}\iota_p(\varepsilon_w)Nc,

       =[\mathfrak{k}_{p,0}Nc,p^{-f}Nz].
\end{equation}
The cancellation here is an explicit equivalence of two framed

GFC21--23 are the exact comparison to the $f$-step original

The equalities for $1-h$ follow from the ultrametric inequality:

\subsection{Full common labels and the different place-label norm fibres}

Let $L$ be the original nontrivial bounded distributive lattice, with
bottom $0_L$ and top $1_L$. On a complex amplitude space $V_0$ retain
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC26}
 \begin{gathered}
 G_L(V_0)=\{(0,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}\cup
                         (V_0\times\{1_L\}),\\
 (v,\lambda)+(w,\mu)=(v+w,\lambda\vee\mu),\qquad
 (a,\alpha)(v,\lambda)=(av,\alpha\wedge\lambda),\\
 \tau=(0,0_L),\qquad e_L=(0,1_L).
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Here $e_L$ denotes supported zero, to distinguish it from the
ramification degree $e$. Every linear map $T$ above has the exact lift
$T^\uparrow(v,\lambda)=(Tv,\lambda)$. It is well-defined because
a nonzero output forces a nonzero input and hence its top label.
Linearity proves preservation of addition and of the
$G_L(\mathbb C)$ scalar action using precisely the joins and meets
in GFC26. All compositions and linear inverse maps are retained.
In particular
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC27}
 \begin{gathered}
 G_L(V)^{I}=G_L(V^I)=G_L(W_p),\qquad
 (E_I^\uparrow)^2=E_I^\uparrow,\qquad
 P_p^\uparrow E_I^\uparrow=E_I^\uparrow\lambda(\phi)^\uparrow,\\
 \ker_{\rm set}\bigl(G_L(V)\longrightarrow V^I,
                         (v,\lambda)\mapsto E_Iv\bigr)
       =\{(v,1_L):E_Iv=0\}\cup\{(0,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The first equality follows directly because the lift fixes the
label and its amplitude is fixed exactly when $v\in V^I$.
The full preimage of $(w,\mu)$ under $E_I^\uparrow$ is
$\{(v,1_L):E_Iv=w\}$ if $\mu=1_L$, and is the singleton
$\{(0,\mu)\}$ when $\mu<1_L$ (in which case necessarily $w=0$).
Thus a nonzero vector killed by averaging maps to $e_L$, not to
$\tau$. The Fourier map GFC6 and its inverse preserve the same
single label on the whole $gf$-dimensional vector. They do not
create independent labels on the $g$ cycles or the $gf$ coefficients.
The joint operator GFC11a lifts on
$G_L(\mathbb C^{U_N}\otimes W_p)$ with one label on the entire
$n_Ngf$-dimensional amplitude. No identification with a tensor
product of independently labelled carriers is used.
On localizing at a support prime $J\subset L$, the original
support map $(v,\lambda)\mapsto[\lambda]_J$ commutes with all
these operators, as each has kept exactly that label.

The actual norm on field amplitudes and on adeles is multiplicative,
so its label lift is a different type of map. In the common carrier
it is $N_c(x,\lambda)=(Nx,\lambda)$ as in GTL5, retaining one label.
In the separately labelled local carrier its $p$-component is
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC28}
 ((x_{w'},\lambda_{w'}))_{w'\mid p}\longmapsto
   \left(\prod_{w'\mid p}N_{E_{w'}/\mathbb Q_p}(x_{w'}),
                               \bigwedge_{w'\mid p}\lambda_{w'}\right).
\end{equation}
There are $g$ actual place labels in this meet. The degree $ef$
of a local field norm does not produce $ef$ new labels; a repeated
meet of the same label is that label. Both maps preserve
multiplication, their multiplicative unit, and $\tau$. They are
not claimed additive or linear, unlike GFC27.

For a fixed local amplitude tuple $x=(x_{w'})_{w'\mid p}$ set
$A_p(x)=\{w'\mid p:x_{w'}=0\}$. If it is empty, the norm amplitude
is nonzero and every input and output label is forced to top.
If it is nonempty, the exact map on this amplitude fibre is
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC29}
 \begin{gathered}
 L^{A_p(x)}\longrightarrow L,\qquad
                (\lambda_{w'})\longmapsto\bigwedge_{w'\in A_p(x)}
                                                           \lambda_{w'},\\
 \text{fibre over }\mu=
     \{(\lambda_{w'})\in L^{A_p(x)}:
                       \bigwedge_{w'\in A_p(x)}\lambda_{w'}=\mu\}.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The omitted nonzero-coordinate labels in the last display have
all been fixed to top in GFC28. Surjectivity follows by putting
$\mu$ at one of the zero places and top at the others. It does
not require a complement, a Boolean lattice, or an infinite meet.
For two or more zero places the three patterns with one chosen
coordinate $\mu$, a different chosen coordinate $\mu$, or both
coordinates $\mu$, all have the same meet; for $\mu<1_L$ they
are distinct. Thus the map is generally many-to-one, with its
complete fibre given above. For exactly one zero place it is
the identity on that label.

At the level of all adeles, write $Z(x)=\{w:x_w=0\}$ and let
$\pi$ be the map of places. The complete fixed-amplitude fibre map
and the diagonal comparison are
\begin{equation}\tag{GFC30}
 \begin{gathered}
 L^{Z(x)}\longrightarrow L^{\pi(Z(x))},\qquad
 (\lambda_w)\longmapsto
       \left(\bigwedge_{w\in Z(x),\,w\mid v}\lambda_w\right)_v,\\
 N_{\rm loc}\Delta_E(x,\lambda)=\Delta_{\mathbb Q}N_c(x,\lambda).
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Each target fibre is a product of the finite fibres GFC29.
The diagonal square follows from finite idempotence of meet.
For a nonzero adelic $x$, its original common label is forced to
top, even when some coordinates are zero. Hence the original
one-zero-place orbit has only its top common lift. Allowing a
separate label at that zero place belongs to the larger local
carrier, where GFC28 indeed carries its single label unchanged
to the base zero place. For the all-zero common amplitude,
$N_c(0,\lambda)=(0,\lambda)$, so $e_L\ne\tau$ remains exact.
More generally the complete common-carrier inverse fibre of
$(0,\mu)$ with $\mu<1_L$ is just $(0,\mu)$; the inverse fibre of
$e_L$ is $\{(x,1_L):Nx=0\}$, and that of a nonzero $(y,1_L)$
is $\{(x,1_L):Nx=y\}$. These statements use only the carrier
constraint, not norm surjectivity.

All actual norm maps just used also lift the associated-space
map GFC21 with one label on each whole local fibre by
$[c,(z,\lambda)]\mapsto[Nc,(Nz,\lambda)]$. The equivalence relations
use multiplication by top-supported nonzero field elements, so
the proof of well-definedness in GFC21 keeps the label unchanged.
Its map at an actual zero-place label is consequently the
one-coordinate case of GFC29. This is an exact multiplicative
and equivariant receiving map; it does not turn the nonlinear
norm into the linear Frobenius projection or identify their fibres.
For the original common adelic point, its other nonzero coordinates
force this local fibre label to top. Allowing every label at its
zero coordinate instead belongs to the separately labelled adelic
carrier. This restriction is retained in the associated-space
comparison and is not removed by using a fibre coefficient lift.

\paragraph{Exact conclusion of the calculation.}
Inertia averaging retains the factor $1/e$ and yields $g$ cycles
of exact length $f$. Their determinant is the original Dedekind
$p$-factor inverse, their primitive clock is $\log q_w=f\log p$,
and the actual norm sends that clock to the base clock with
degree $f$. The unit $\varepsilon_w$ of the uniformizer norm
remains in the full compact and transverse return GFC19--23.
The inverse-pullback distribution uses the signs and factors
of GFC25. Common coefficient labels are carried by linear lifts,
whereas actual multiple-place norm labels are carried by the
finite meet with fibres GFC29. These proved maps relate the
objects without replacing one by the other. No positivity
theorem, RH conclusion, completed-zeta substitute, or assertion
that this finite suspension is the entire arithmetic
cohomological trace follows from this calculation.
