\subsection{The finite quotient and its exact components}

Fix a rational prime $p$, and retain
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS1}
 \ell=\log p,\qquad K^{(p)}=\prod_{r\ne p}\mathbb Z_r^\times,
 \qquad
 O_p=(\mathbb R\times K^{(p)})/
       ((x,u)\sim(x+\ell,pu)).
\end{equation}
The forward flow is $\Phi_t[x,u]=[x+t,u]$. It returns to the
$x=0$ transversal after time $\ell$ as $u\mapsto p^{-1}u$.
With the actual transverse coordinate of PLP28, that return is
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS2}
 R_p(u,z)=(p^{-1}u,p^{-1}z).
\end{equation}
No inverse time convention is substituted for this point action.

For a positive integer $N$ coprime to $p$ put
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS3}
 U_N=(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^\times,\quad n_N=|U_N|=\varphi(N),
 \quad d_N=\operatorname{ord}_{U_N}(p),\quad h_N=n_N/d_N.
\end{equation}
For $N=1$, use the one-element group, $\varphi(1)=1$, and
$d_1=h_1=1$. The reduction map $K^{(p)}\to U_N$ is onto:
factor $N$ into prime powers, reduce the corresponding $r$-adic
units, and apply the Chinese remainder theorem. It intertwines
multiplication by $p$ and gives
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS4}
 O_{p,N}=(\mathbb R\times U_N)/
       ((x,u)\sim(x+\ell,pu)),
 \qquad O_p\longrightarrow O_{p,N}.
\end{equation}
The permutation $u\mapsto p^{-1}u$ has precisely the cosets of
$\langle p\rangle$ as its cycles. Each has length $d_N$, since
$p^{-j}u=u$ is equivalent to $p^j=1$ in $U_N$.

Each coset supplies one circle component of $O_{p,N}$, with
the explicit parametrization
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS5}
 \mathbb R/(d_N\ell)\mathbb Z\longrightarrow O_{p,N},
 \qquad [t]\longmapsto[t,u_0].
\end{equation}
Indeed $[x,p^ju_0]=[x-j\ell,u_0]$, proving surjectivity onto
that component. Equality $[t,u_0]=[t',u_0]$ means
$t'-t=j\ell$ and $p^ju_0=u_0$, hence $d_N\mid j$.
This proves injectivity and the exact least period $d_N\ell$.
The quotient action is properly discontinuous in the real coordinate,
so its quotient is Hausdorff; the displayed continuous bijection from
a compact circle is consequently a homeomorphism onto the component.
There are exactly $h_N$ components, and forward motion increases
$t$ in every component. Its $d_N$-fold transversal return has
normal action $z\mapsto p^{-d_N}z$.

Use compact Haar measure of mass one on $K^{(p)}$, its pushforward
counting measure $du_N$ of mass $1/n_N$ at each $u$, and real
measure $dx$. A fundamental interval is $0\le x<\ell$. Therefore
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS6}
 \mu(O_p)=\mu_N(O_{p,N})=\ell,
 \qquad d\mu_N\text{ on a component}=\frac{dt}{n_N},
 \qquad\mu_N(\text{one component})=\frac{d_N\ell}{n_N}.
\end{equation}
For the component formula, split the interval of length $d_N\ell$
into its $d_N$ transversal pieces, each carrying $dx/n_N$.
This retains the original real length; it is not replaced by mass one.

\subsection{The two different return operators}

On functions on $U_N$ distinguish
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS7}
 C_Rf(u)=f(p^{-1}u),\qquad P_Nf(u)=f(pu)=C_{R^{-1}}f(u).
\end{equation}
Both preserve the measure in PHS6. The actual pullback convention
on the full orbit is
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS8}
 (U_tF)(x,u)=F(x-t,u),\qquad
 (U_\ell F)(0,u)=F(-\ell,u)=F(0,pu).
\end{equation}
The last equality is the original relation in PHS1. Thus the
Koopman return is $P_N$, whereas composition with the forward
point return is $C_R=P_N^{-1}$. In the delta-function basis,
$P_N\delta_v=\delta_{p^{-1}v}$ and
$C_R\delta_v=\delta_{pv}$; each basis vector has squared norm
$1/n_N$ in the specified measure.

For every character $\chi:U_N\to\mathbb C^\times$,
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS9}
 P_N\chi=\chi(p)\chi,\qquad
 C_R\chi=\chi(p)^{-1}\chi,\qquad
 \frac1{n_N}\sum_{u\in U_N}\chi(u)\overline{\psi(u)}
       =\begin{cases}1,&\chi=\psi,\\0,&\chi\ne\psi.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The orthogonality follows by translating the sum by an element where
the quotient character is not 1. These characters form a basis:
decomposing the finite abelian group into cyclic factors and taking
their roots-of-unity characters gives exactly $n_N$ orthogonal
nonzero vectors. Their squared norms are 1 with the retained finite
Haar measure.

Every character of $\langle p\rangle$ extends to $U_N$.
Here is a direct extension proof. Given a character on a subgroup
$H$ of a finite abelian group and $a\notin H$, let $j$ be the
least positive integer with $a^j\in H$. Choose $z\in\mathbb C$
with $z^j=\chi(a^j)$, and set
$\widetilde\chi(ha^k)=\chi(h)z^k$. If two representations
differ, their exponents differ by a multiple of $j$ and this exact
root equation proves equality of their values. Thus this extends
the character to $\langle H,a\rangle$. Finitely many repetitions
extend it to the whole group. The kernel of restriction consists
of the characters of $U_N/\langle p\rangle$, of which there
are $h_N$ by the same cyclic-factor count. Consequently every
$d_N$th root of unity occurs as $\chi(p)$ exactly $h_N$ times.

The original measure also verifies the permutation trace directly.
The integral kernel of $P_N^j$ in that measure is
$n_N1_{v=p^ju}$, so its diagonal integral is
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS10}
 \operatorname{Tr}(P_N^j)=
 \frac1{n_N}\sum_u n_N1_{u=p^ju}
 =\begin{cases}n_N,&d_N\mid j,\\0,&d_N\nmid j.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The factor $1/n_N$ in the measure therefore does not replace this
trace by the proportion of fixed points.

\subsection{Finite determinants and all Dirichlet factors}

Retain $q=p^{-s}=e^{-s\ell}$. The exact finite determinants are
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS11}
 \begin{split}
 D_{p,N}^{+}(s)&=\det(I-p^{-s}P_N)
       =\prod_{\chi\in\widehat U_N}(1-\chi(p)p^{-s})
       =(1-p^{-d_Ns})^{h_N},\\
 D_{p,N}^{-}(s)&=\det(I-p^{-s}C_R)
       =\prod_{\chi\in\widehat U_N}(1-\chi(p)^{-1}p^{-s})
       =(1-p^{-d_Ns})^{h_N}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The product over all $d_N$th roots is $1-q^{d_N}$, proving the
last equalities with their multiplicities. Equality of the total
determinants does not identify their individual character factors.

For $\Re s>0$, expand the logarithm with value zero at $q=0$.
Absolute convergence of the geometric series gives
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS12}
 \begin{gathered}
 -\log D_{p,N}^{+}(s)
       =\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{p^{-js}}j\operatorname{Tr}(P_N^j)
       =h_N\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{p^{-d_Nrs}}r,\\
 \frac{d}{ds}\log D_{p,N}^{+}(s)
       =\ell\sum_{j\ge1}p^{-js}\operatorname{Tr}(P_N^j)
       =\frac{n_N\ell\,p^{-d_Ns}}{1-p^{-d_Ns}}.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The corresponding formulas for $D^-_{p,N}$ use $P_N^{-j}$
and have the same total values. The logarithmic derivatives extend
meromorphically; the determinant zeros are exactly
$s=2\pi ik/(d_N\ell)$, $k\in\mathbb Z$, each of order $h_N$.

Extend a character of $U_N$ to integers by zero on nonunits.
Its original Dirichlet series and local factor, with no completion
multiplier, are
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS13}
 L(s,\chi)=\sum_{n\ge1}\chi(n)n^{-s},\qquad
 L_p(s,\chi)=(1-\chi(p)p^{-s})^{-1}\quad(p\nmid N).
\end{equation}
For $\Re s>1$, absolute convergence and unique factorization
prove its Euler product by expanding finite geometric products and
taking their dominated limit. Thus the actual Koopman character
factor in PHS11 is $L_p(s,\chi)^{-1}$. The composition operator
$C_R$ instead gives $L_p(s,\chi^{-1})^{-1}$; its character has
not been silently inverted in PHS13. In particular
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS14}
 -\frac{L_p'(s,\chi)}{L_p(s,\chi)}
       =\ell\sum_{j\ge1}\chi(p)^j p^{-js}
       =\frac{\ell\chi(p)p^{-s}}{1-\chi(p)p^{-s}},
 \quad\Re s>0.
\end{equation}

There is also an inverse-character dictionary between unit
coordinates and the original local-idele coordinate. Extend the
same finite character to $C_{\mathbb Q}$ by
$\widetilde\chi(r,u)=\chi(u\bmod N)$. The exact local uniformizer
image in PLP20 gives
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS14a}
 \widetilde\chi(\iota_p(p))=\chi(p)^{-1},\qquad
 L_p(s,\chi)=
 \bigl(1-\widetilde\chi^{-1}(\iota_p(p))p^{-s}\bigr)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Indeed the unit coordinates away from $p$ of $\iota_p(p)$ are
all $p^{-1}$, and $N$ has no $p$ factor. Thus an Euler factor
written using $\widetilde\chi$ itself at the local uniformizer
is the one for the inverse integer character. This is compatible
with the original convolution action: for any absolutely integrable
test $a$ and a unitary character $F$ of the orbit,
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS14b}
 (a*F)(x)=F(x)\int_{C_{\mathbb Q}}a(g)F(g)^{-1}d^{\times}g.
\end{equation}
This follows by inserting $F(g^{-1}x)=F(g)^{-1}F(x)$ into the
convolution integral. For $F(r,u)=r^{i\tau}\widetilde\chi(u)$,
the multiplier is the original Mellin integral with character
$\widetilde\chi^{-1}$ and exponent $-i\tau$. Neither character
inversion is a change in the forward point return.

All missing-prime factors must be stated in a global comparison.
For the present $p\nmid N$, the full original product is
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS15}
 L(s,\chi)=L_p(s,\chi)
       \prod_{\substack{r\ne p\\r\nmid N}}
                (1-\chi(r)r^{-s})^{-1},\qquad\Re s>1.
\end{equation}
Primes dividing $N$ do not occur because $\chi(r)=0$ there.
Let $f_\chi\mid N$ be the conductor and $\chi_*$ the inducing
primitive character. Explicitly, decompose $U_N$ by the Chinese
remainder theorem and take, at each prime, the least prime-power
level through which its character factors; their product is
$f_\chi$. Restriction to these quotients gives $\chi_*$ and
proves its minimality. Comparing each Euler factor yields
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS16}
 \begin{gathered}
 L(s,\chi)=L(s,\chi_*)
       \prod_{\substack{r\mid N\\r\nmid f_\chi}}
                 (1-\chi_*(r)r^{-s}),\\
 L(s,1_{U_N})=\zeta(s)\prod_{r\mid N}(1-r^{-s}).
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
At primes dividing $f_\chi$, the primitive character is already
zero and no additional factor is omitted. At every other prime
dividing $N$, the displayed factor cancels exactly the primitive
Euler factor. Both assertions follow first in $\Re s>1$,
where every product involved has its original absolute meaning.
For the product over all finite characters, the entire correction is
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS17}
 \prod_{\chi\in\widehat U_N}L(s,\chi)
 =\left(\prod_{\chi\in\widehat U_N}L(s,\chi_*)\right)
   \prod_{\chi\in\widehat U_N}
       \prod_{\substack{r\mid N\\r\nmid f_\chi}}
                  (1-\chi_*(r)r^{-s}).
\end{equation}
No primitive factor or omitted prime is identified merely because
several characters share its inducing character.
Characters genuinely ramified at $p$ are not characters of this
orbit: the stabilizer contains $\mathbb Z_p^\times$, so descent
requires the original character to be trivial on that subgroup.
For a primitive Dirichlet character whose conductor is divisible
by $p$, its integer extension has value zero at $p$ and its local
Euler factor is $1$. No nonzero phase $\chi(p)$ is assigned to
that omitted case. If only an imprimitive modulus adds $p$,
the missing unramified factor is exactly the correction in PHS16.

If $N\mid M$ and both are coprime to $p$, reduction
$U_M\to U_N$ is onto. This follows prime-power by prime-power
and then by the Chinese remainder theorem. Its fibres have size
$\varphi(M)/\varphi(N)$, so it preserves the specified probability
Haar measures. The induced maps $O_{p,M}\to O_{p,N}$ intertwine
both return operators and give isometric pullbacks on their
$L^2$ spaces with mass $\ell$. A character pulled back from
$U_N$ keeps the same value at $p$. Its global Dirichlet series,
however, has precisely the extra excluded-prime factors
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS18}
 L(s,\chi_M)=L(s,\chi_N)
       \prod_{\substack{r\mid M\\r\nmid N}}
                      (1-\chi_N(r)r^{-s}).
\end{equation}
Comparing the individual Euler factors proves this identity, so
the finite-level transition does not hide those exclusions.

\subsection{The normal Haar factors in both time directions}

The original transverse measure has
$\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb Z_p)=1$. For an integer $j>0$,
the forward point return is $z\mapsto p^{-j}z$, and
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS19}
 \begin{gathered}
 d(p^{-j}z)=p^jdz,\qquad
 |1-p^{-j}|_p=p^j,\qquad |1-p^j|_p=1,\\
 d_{\mathbb A_p}(p^{-j}a)=p^{-j}d_{\mathbb A_p}a,
 \qquad p^j\cdot p^{-j}=1.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The last line retains the hyperplane factor from the rational
product formula PLP32. It is not combined with the normal factor
before those two original measures have been identified.

For the inverse pullback convention $\vartheta(h)f(z)=f(h^{-1}z)$,
the kernel with respect to $dy$ is $\delta(y-h^{-1}z)$.
Its transverse diagonal is consequently
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS20}
 \int_{\mathbb Q_p}\delta((1-h^{-1})z)\,dz
       =|1-h^{-1}|_p^{-1}.
\end{equation}
This follows by the Dirac change of variables for the invertible
scalar $1-h^{-1}$, and only holds here for $h\ne1$.
Thus the complete forward and backward entries are
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS21}
 \begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
 \text{point time}&\text{normal point multiplier }h&
 \text{finite Koopman character}&
 \text{normal pullback diagonal}\\ \hline
 +j\ell&p^{-j}&\chi(p)^j&1\\
 -j\ell&p^j&\chi(p)^{-j}&p^{-j}
 \end{array}
\end{equation}
In particular using $|1-h|_p^{-1}$ for the forward pullback would
give the other row's weight and would be a convention error.
The norm on transverse test functions also retains its mass:
\[
 \|\vartheta(h)f\|_{L^2(dz)}^2=|h|_p\|f\|_{L^2(dz)}^2.
\]
For the forward multiplier this factor is $p^j$. No factor
$|h|_p^{-1/2}$ has been inserted to change that operator.
The table specifies local distributional factors away from the
singular identity. It does not replace the regularized identity
term or the adelic trace theorem.

\subsection{Exact character frequencies on the full compact orbit}

Let $\chi$ be a continuous character of $K^{(p)}$. It factors
through some $U_N$, $p\nmid N$. To prove this, continuity gives
an open subgroup whose image lies in a sufficiently short arc of
the unit circle containing no nontrivial subgroup. Such an open
subgroup has trivial character image. A congruence subgroup
defined at finitely many primes lies inside it, giving the asserted
finite level. In particular $\chi(p)$ is a root of unity.

The function $e^{i\tau x}\chi(u)$ descends to $O_p$ precisely when
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS22}
 e^{i\tau\ell}\chi(p)=1.
\end{equation}
Indeed this is exactly invariance under the deck generator in PHS1.
For any chosen argument $\theta_\chi$ of $\chi(p)$ all its
frequencies and its flow eigenvalues are therefore
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS23}
 \tau_{\chi,n}=\frac{2\pi n-\theta_\chi}{\ell},\qquad
 U_t(e^{i\tau_{\chi,n}x}\chi(u))
       =e^{-i\tau_{\chi,n}t}e^{i\tau_{\chi,n}x}\chi(u),
 \quad n\in\mathbb Z.
\end{equation}
Changing the chosen argument only changes the integer index. At
$t=\ell$, this eigenvalue is $\chi(p)$, exactly PHS9.

These functions form a complete orthogonal basis of
$L^2(O_p,dx\,du)$, each of squared norm $\ell$.
For two distinct compact characters, integration over $K^{(p)}$
is zero by translation invariance. For the same character, distinct
integer indices differ by $2\pi n/\ell$ and integrate to zero
over $[0,\ell)$. For completeness, first average a function over
successively smaller compact congruence subgroups. These averages
are the finite-level projections; they converge in $L^2$ to the
function. For continuous functions this follows uniformly from
uniform continuity on the compact orbit, and continuous functions
are dense in its finite Haar measure. At a fixed level, finite
Fourier expansion on $U_N$ reduces to real functions satisfying
the boundary condition
$a_\chi(x+\ell)=\chi(p)^{-1}a_\chi(x)$.
Multiplication by $e^{i\theta_\chi x/\ell}$ converts precisely
this boundary condition to periodicity, and ordinary Fourier
series on the interval give the frequencies in PHS23. Their
completeness follows, for example, from convergence of the Fejer
averages of continuous periodic functions and density in $L^2$.
The finite-level union proves the asserted full completeness.

In these original coordinates the self-adjoint generator is
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS24}\begin{gathered}
 U_t=e^{-itD},\qquad D=-i\partial_x,\\
 \operatorname{Dom}(D)=
 \left\{\sum_{\chi,n}a_{\chi,n}e^{i\tau_{\chi,n}x}\chi(u):
       \ell\sum_{\chi,n}|a_{\chi,n}|^2<\infty,
       \quad\ell\sum_{\chi,n}\tau_{\chi,n}^2|a_{\chi,n}|^2<\infty
       \right\}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Its action multiplies each coefficient by the real number
$\tau_{\chi,n}$. The same diagonal coefficient test describes
the adjoint domain, proving self-adjointness. Dominated convergence
on $\ell\sum|a_{\chi,n}|^2$ proves strong continuity of $U_t$
and its specified generator. At level $N$ the frequencies are
exactly $2\pi\mathbb Z/(d_N\ell)$, each of multiplicity $h_N$,
by the character-extension calculation after PHS9. This agrees
with all the component circles of PHS5.

The full set of frequencies is exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS25}
 \{\tau_{\chi,n}\}=\frac{2\pi}{\log p}\,\mathbb Q.
\end{equation}
One inclusion follows because every $\chi(p)$ is a root of unity.
For the converse, take $N=p^m-1$ for a positive integer $m$.
It is coprime to $p$. If $N>1$, its order is exactly $m$:
$p^m=1\pmod N$, whereas for $0<j<m$ the positive integer
$p^j-1$ is strictly less than $N$, so is not divisible by $N$.
The extension proof after PHS9 then realizes every $m$th root
as $\chi(p)$. For a rational number $a/b$, use $m=b$ and
choose $\chi(p)=e^{-2\pi ia/b}$; equation PHS22 gives
$\tau=2\pi a/(b\ell)$.
The exceptional case $p=2,m=1$ has $N=1$; its trivial character
gives precisely the required integer frequencies. This handles
that case without an order calculation in a nonexistent nontrivial
unit group. It follows that the eigenvalues are dense in
$\mathbb R$.

Every one of these eigenvalues has infinite multiplicity.
Fix the denominator $b$ of a rational frequency and let $m$ run
through its positive multiples. At $N=p^m-1$, the required value
of $\chi(p)$ has exactly $\varphi(N)/m$ character extensions.
These give distinct orthogonal eigenvectors at that fixed frequency.
Their number is unbounded. Indeed for any positive integer $n$,
prime-power factorization gives the elementary bound
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS26}
 \varphi(n)\ge\sqrt{n/2}.
\end{equation}
For an odd prime power $r^a$,
$\varphi(r^a)/\sqrt{r^a}=r^{a/2-1}(r-1)\ge1$;
for powers of 2 this ratio is at least 1 except at $2^1$,
where it is $1/\sqrt2$. Multiplying these exact inequalities
proves PHS26, also valid at $n=1$. Thus
$\varphi(p^m-1)/m\ge\sqrt{(p^m-1)/2}/m\to\infty$.
Continuous characters form a countable union of finite sets, so
each of these eigenspaces is countably infinite dimensional.

The spectrum of $D$ as a self-adjoint operator is consequently
all of $\mathbb R$, although its eigenvalue set is the specified
countable dense subset. For a real number $a$ choose frequencies
$\tau_j\to a$; the corresponding vectors have norm $\sqrt\ell$
and $(D-a)$-image norm $\sqrt\ell|\tau_j-a|$, proving that
$D-a$ has no bounded inverse. Nonreal numbers are outside its
spectrum because division by $\tau-z$ defines the bounded inverse,
of norm at most $1/|\operatorname{Im}z|$, on the full coefficient
space. The spectral measures are atomic in the complete basis
already proved; density of the eigenvalue set is not a claim that
every real number is an eigenvalue.
In fact the coefficient equation $DF=aF$ forces
$(\tau_{\chi,n}-a)a_{\chi,n}=0$ for every index, so no additional
eigenvalue can occur outside PHS25.

There is no compact resolvent. For example the zero eigenspace
already contains infinitely many pairwise orthogonal vectors of
norm $\sqrt\ell$. On that space $(D-i)^{-1}$ is multiplication
by $i$, so the bounded sequence of those vectors has no convergent
subsequence after applying the resolvent. This contradicts
compactness. At each finite level the multiplicities are finite
and the nonzero frequencies tend to infinity, so Fourier truncation
does give a compact resolvent there; the full space is not one
such finite level.

Likewise $P=U_\ell$ on the infinite full space is unitary and
not compact. For $q\ne0$, $qP$ is not trace class, since its
action on any infinite orthogonal sequence preserves the norm
up to the fixed nonzero factor $|q|$. Thus PHS11 is a genuine
finite determinant; no unproved infinite Fredholm determinant is
obtained by taking its limit.

\subsection{The actual common-label lifts}

For the arbitrary bounded distributive lattice of PLP1--14 and
any one of the specified complex amplitude spaces $V$, retain
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS27}
 G_L(V)=\{(0,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}\cup(V\times\{1_L\}),
 \qquad T^\uparrow(F,\lambda)=(TF,\lambda).
\end{equation}
Each linear return, flow, finite-level pullback, or Fourier map
above lifts by this formula. Zero amplitudes retain their labels;
linearity proves addition and the $G_L(\mathbb C)$ scalar action,
and the displayed inverses lift wherever an inverse exists.
There is one common label on the entire vector, not independently
chosen labels on its character coefficients.

For example the exact finite Fourier map and inverse are
\begin{equation}\tag{PHS28}
 \widehat f(\chi)=\frac1{n_N}\sum_{u\in U_N}
                         f(u)\overline{\chi(u)},\qquad
 f(u)=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat U_N}\widehat f(\chi)\chi(u).
\end{equation}
Orthogonality in PHS9 proves the inverse with its original
$1/n_N$ Haar factor. Its lifted map is
$G_L(\mathbb C^{U_N})\to G_L(\mathbb C^{\widehat U_N})$,
not a basis change on the larger independently labelled product
$G_L(\mathbb C)^{U_N}$. It is a semimodule isomorphism.
For pointwise multiplication of amplitudes, its transported
product is explicitly
$\widehat{fg}(\chi)=\sum_\psi\widehat f(\psi)
\widehat g(\chi\psi^{-1})$, as follows by inserting the inverse
sum and using the same orthogonality. It is not ordinary
coordinatewise multiplication on character coefficients.

The full Hilbert Fourier map in PHS23--24 is lifted only after
the convergent amplitude sum has been defined, with its one
unchanged label. No infinite join in $L$ is used. In particular
$U_t^\uparrow(e)=e$ and $U_t^\uparrow(\tau)=\tau$; these
remain distinct when $L$ is nontrivial. The actual support
localization sends $(F,\lambda)$ to $[\lambda]_J$, and every
label-preserving operator commutes with that map:
$\sigma_JT^\uparrow(F,\lambda)=[\lambda]_J
=\sigma_J(F,\lambda)$. This records exactly the localization
of PLP7 and ULC8, rather than retaining an amplitude at a stalk
where the proved localization removes it.

The original nonzero adelic orbit has its single common top label
as proved in PLP15--19. The lifts here concern its coefficient
spaces; they do not manufacture additional nonzero-point lifts
in the original carrier. All Haar factors above are measures on
the stated real, finite-unit, or local-field amplitudes. No
measure or spectral multiplicity on the arbitrary label set is
assigned. The finite Euler factors, their precise orientations,
and the infinite compact-orbit spectrum have been calculated;
none of these calculations asserts a new Weil-positivity theorem
or an RH implication.
