and \path{rational_witness.json}. The session, not this reconstruction,

\subsection{The unchanged test and quadratic form}
Retain the physical heat parameter $t$, original coordinate $Z$, and
\begin{equation}\tag{RWR1}
 H_t(Z)=\int_0^\infty e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)\cos(Zu)du,
 \quad \Phi(u)=\sum_{j\ge1}(2\pi^2j^4e^{9u}-3\pi j^2e^{5u})
                         e^{-\pi j^2e^{4u}}.
\end{equation}
Let $M_n(t)=\int_0^\infty u^{2n}e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)du$ and
$a_n(t)=(-1)^nM_n(t)/(2n)!$. Positivity of each theta summand proves
$M_0(t)>0$. In the Taylor disk at zero put
\begin{equation}\tag{RWR2}
 \frac{H_t'(Z)}{H_t(Z)}=-\sum_{n\ge1}S_n(t)Z^{2n-1},
 \qquad
 S_n=\frac{-2n a_n-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}a_jS_{n-j}}{M_0}.
\end{equation}
The coefficient identity follows by multiplying the two convergent
Taylor series. The full-root product in the accompanying proof identifies
$S_n=\sum_\zeta m_\zeta\zeta^{-2n}$, including both sign partners
and every multiplicity. No finite zero sample is used to compute $S_n$.

Write $D_{ii}=1000^i$, $i=0,\ldots,31$, and retain both matrices
\begin{equation}\tag{RWR3}
 K_d(t)=(S_{i+j+1}(t))_{0\le i,j<d},\qquad
 \widehat K_{32}=D K_{32}D.
\end{equation}
The congruence is the explicit invertible coefficient map $c=Db$.
It changes no quadratic value or inertia. The incoming nineteen integers
$N_i$ are retained verbatim in the supplied JSON, whose SHA256 is

\subsection{All remainders in the moment calculation}
The independent computation uses $1280$ binary working bits, $160$
Gauss--Legendre nodes in each of $24$ panels of $[0,3]$, and the first
$16$ theta summands. It computes moments $0\le n\le65$.
For each exact panel center $c=(2j+1)/16$, $j=0,\ldots,23$, put
\[
 \begin{gathered}h=1/16,\quad A=17/128,\quad B=15/128,\\
 x_-=c-A,\quad x_+=c+A,\quad R^2=x_+^2+B^2,
 \quad\kappa=1-(4B)^2/2>0.\end{gathered}
\]
The ellipse of parameter $4$ around the panel has semiaxes $A,B$.
On and inside it, $\cos(4\Im z)\ge\kappa$ and $|z|^2\le R^2$;
the latter follows since $c>0$ gives $|c-A|\le c+A$.
Using $3<\pi<4$ and $j^2\ge1$, the absolute value of the truncated
theta sum is at most
\begin{equation}\tag{RWR5}
 P_c=\left(32e^{9x_+}\sum_{j=1}^{16}j^4+
              12e^{5x_+}\sum_{j=1}^{16}j^2\right)
             e^{-3e^{4x_-}\kappa}.
\end{equation}
Indeed $\Re(\pi j^2e^{4z})\ge3e^{4x_-}\kappa$ and the
absolute value of each of the two prefactor terms is bounded separately.
The additional factor for real $t$ is at most
$T_c(t)=e^{t x_+^2}$ for $t\ge0$, and $e^{|t|B^2}$ for $t<0$.
The integrand's ellipse supremum is thus at most $P_cT_c R^{2n}$.

For completeness, write an analytic function on a Bernstein ellipse
as a Chebyshev series. Pullback by $(w+w^{-1})/2$ and the Cauchy
coefficient estimate bound the degree-$j$ coefficient by $2M4^{-j}$.
The remainder after degree $2N-1$ on $[-1,1]$ is bounded by
$2M4^{-(2N-1)}/3$. Gauss--Legendre quadrature is exact on that
polynomial; both the integral and the sum of positive quadrature
weights have mass $\ell=1/8$ on the physical panel. Therefore
\begin{equation}\tag{RWR6}
 |\text{panel integral}-\text{panel quadrature}|
 \le\frac{4\ell}{3}P_cT_c(t)R^{2n}4^{-(2N-1)},\qquad N=160.
\end{equation}
The software's prefactor $1/6$ is exactly $4\ell/3$.
The node intervals enclose all distinct roots of $P_{160}$ and the
positive weights enclose their exact Gauss weights. The implementation
also checks ordering, containment in $(-1,1)$, positivity and total mass.

Every omitted theta summand is positive on the original real domain.
On $[0,3]$,
$\Phi_{>16}(u)\le32e^{27}\sum_{j\ge17}j^4e^{-3j^2}$.
The ratio of successive terms is at most $16e^{-105}<1/2$.
Multiplying the geometric bound by the interval length, $u^{2n}\le3^{2n}$,
and $e^{tu^2}\le e^{9\max(t,0)}$ proves
\begin{equation}\tag{RWR7}
 \int_0^3u^{2n}e^{tu^2}\Phi_{>16}(u)du
 \le192\,17^4\,3^{2n}e^{27+9\max(t,0)-867}.
\end{equation}
For $u\ge3$, discard the negative part of each summand and apply the
same ratio argument, now from $j=1$, to obtain
$\Phi(u)\le64e^{9u}e^{-3e^{4u}}$.
For $n\le65$, $t\le1/5$ and $u\ge3$,
\[
 2n\log u+\max(t,0)u^2+9u
 \le139u+u^2/5\le47u^2\le e^{4u}.
\]
The last inequality follows by monotonicity of $e^{4u}/u^2$ on that
interval and $e^{12}>1000>423$. Convexity also gives
$e^{4u}\ge1000(1+4(u-3))$. Hence
\begin{equation}\tag{RWR8}
 \int_3^\infty u^{2n}e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)du
 \le64e^{-2000}\int_3^\infty e^{-8000(u-3)}du
 <e^{-2000}.
\end{equation}
The moment ball is enlarged symmetrically by the sum of (RWR6)--(RWR8).
Symmetric enlargement is conservative even for the positive omitted
tails. Thus it encloses the entire original integral.

Interval evaluation of (RWR2) is legitimate because the computed
$M_0$ interval is positive. In $\widehat K=L\mathcal P L^T$, the
ordinary recursive LDL equations give intervals for each successive
pivot and multiplier. No pivot enclosing zero is accepted. Induction
on the recursive equations proves enclosure of the exact factorization,
so the signs of all $32$ pivots determine the exact inertia by the
invertible triangular change of coordinates. The same moment balls
evaluate (RWR4) with its exact rational coefficients.

To verify it directly, put $R=H'/H$. Then

\subsection{The single residual object after the certified positive block}
All the following entries are evaluated at the original arithmetic time
$t=0$. Define $A=K_{64}(0)$ and, for every integer $i\ge64$,
\begin{equation}\tag{RWR18}
 v_i=(S_{i+a+1}(0))_{a=0}^{63},\qquad
 \mathscr S_{ij}=S_{i+j+1}(0)-v_i^TA^{-1}v_j\quad(i,j\ge64).
\end{equation}
Every $S_n(0)$ is the fixed theta integral recurrence (RWR1)--(RWR2).
The inverse exists by (RWR17) and can equivalently be written
$\operatorname{adj}(A)/\det A$; none of these entries is a free
parameter. The complete remaining space and form are
\begin{equation}\tag{RWR19}
 \mathscr V=\bigoplus_{i=64}^{\infty}\mathbb C\,e_i,
 \qquad \mathscr Q(b,c)=\sum_{i,j\ge64}\overline{b_i}\mathscr S_{ij}c_j.
\end{equation}
The direct sum means finite support; every displayed value is therefore
a finite sum of fixed, completely defined real numbers. There is no
cutoff on the indices admitted to $\mathscr V$.

Its exact map into the original rational-function test space is
\begin{equation}\tag{RWR20}
 Jb=\sum_{i\ge64}b_i\Psi_i(Z),\qquad
 \Psi_i(Z)=Z^{-2i-1}
       -\sum_{a=0}^{63}(A^{-1}v_i)_a Z^{-2a-1}.
\end{equation}
The coefficient of $Z^{-2i-1}$, $i\ge64$, in $Jb$ is $b_i$;
thus $J$ is injective. Let $E(a)=\sum_{j<64}a_jZ^{-2j-1}$.
By (ALM9), the full trace pairing gives exactly
\begin{equation}\tag{RWR21}
 \mathcal T_0(Ea,Jb)=0,\qquad
 \mathcal T_0(Jb,Jc)=\mathscr Q(b,c).
\end{equation}
For the first equality each column is $v_i-AA^{-1}v_i=0$.
Expanding both arguments in the second equality gives
$S_{i+j+1}-v_i^TA^{-1}v_j-v_i^TA^{-1}v_j+
v_i^TA^{-1}AA^{-1}v_j$, which is (RWR18).

For any original finite coefficient vector, split it at $64$ as
$(a,b)$ and put $Vb=\sum_{i\ge64}v_i b_i$. Its exact decomposition is
\begin{equation}\tag{RWR22}
 E(a)+\sum_{i\ge64}b_iZ^{-2i-1}
       =E(a+A^{-1}Vb)+Jb,
 \quad
 Q_0(a,b)=(a+A^{-1}Vb)^*A(a+A^{-1}Vb)+\mathscr Q(b,b).
\end{equation}
The map $F(\alpha,b)=E\alpha+Jb$ is a proved linear isomorphism
from $\mathbb C^{64}\oplus\mathscr V$ onto the original union of
Laurent test spaces. Reading the original low and high coefficients
$(a,b)$ gives its inverse $F^{-1}(a,b)=(a+A^{-1}Vb,b)$.
Equivalently the coefficient change $C(a,b)=(a+A^{-1}Vb,b)$ has
inverse $C^{-1}(\alpha,b)=(\alpha-A^{-1}Vb,b)$.
It retains the original quadratic value and all coefficients through
that explicit inverse. It is not an assertion that arbitrary free
split matrices are globally invertible.

The exact counterfactual receiver is now the one negative cone
\begin{equation}\tag{RWR23}
 \mathscr N=\{b\in\bigoplus_{i\ge64}\mathbb Q e_i:
                         \mathscr Q(b,b)<0\}.
\end{equation}
It is empty exactly when all original nontrivial zeta zeros lie on
their critical line. Here is the full proof of both directions.
If all zeros are on the line, (ALM24) and (RWR21) give
$\mathscr Q(b,b)\ge0$ for every $b$, so $\mathscr N$ is empty.
Conversely any off-line zero gives a finite rational negative vector
$(a,b)$ by (ALM12)--(ALM23). Its $b$ is rational, is nonzero because
$A\succ0$, and (RWR22) implies
$\mathscr Q(b,b)\le Q_0(a,b)<0$. Thus it belongs to $\mathscr N$.
Finally, a member of $\mathscr N$ maps under $J$ to a negative
original full-root test by (RWR21), which contradicts all roots being
real by (ALM8). The possibly nonrational low coefficients in $Jb$
cause no loss: the strict finite-dimensional inequality persists under
rational coefficient approximation, as quantified in (ALM21)--(ALM23).

All maps in (RWR20)--(RWR22) are fixed complex-linear maps. Their lifts
$(x,\lambda)\mapsto(Ax,\lambda)$ commute with the original full
$\Phi$ by (ALM29)--(ALM31). The residual pairing has the exact lift
\begin{equation}\tag{RWR24}
 \mathscr Q^L((b,\lambda),(c,\mu))=
          (\mathscr Q(b,c),\lambda\wedge\mu).
\end{equation}
If the amplitude is nonzero then both labels are top, so this lies
in $G_L(\mathbb C)$; the zero-amplitude cases keep their actual meet.
Lattice distributivity and the already proved sesquilinearity prove
the full lifted pairing identities. Consequently the complete remaining
object is $(G_L(\mathscr V),\mathscr Q^L,J^\uparrow,\Phi)$ with the
specified negative rational cone. Every entry is fixed by the original
theta kernel and every higher degree remains present. The certificate
has evaluated its eliminated block; it has not evaluated the sign of
the whole residual object.
The lifted decomposition uses $G_L(\mathbb C^{64}\oplus\mathscr V)$
with its retained common label. No identification with a product of
separately labelled carriers is asserted.

\subsection{The zero-value edge in that same residual space}
Let $\mathscr R=\{Z^{-1}p(Z^{-2}):p\in\mathbb C[X]\}$ and
$\mathscr U=E\mathbb C^{64}$. Define the exact linear observations
$r(f)_a=\mathcal T_0(Ee_a,f)$, $0\le a<64$. Then
\begin{equation}\tag{RWR25}
 \Pi f=f-EA^{-1}r(f),\qquad
 \Pi^2=\Pi,\quad\ker\Pi=\mathscr U,\quad
 \operatorname{im}\Pi=\mathscr U^\perp=J\mathscr V.
\end{equation}
Indeed $r(Ea)=Aa$, so $r(\Pi f)=0$ and $\Pi$ is the identity
on that kernel; its kernel is exactly $\mathscr U$. Formula (RWR22)
shows that every vector in the orthogonal complement has low coefficients
$-A^{-1}Vb$, hence belongs to $J\mathscr V$.

The complete pairing on $\mathscr R$ has zero algebraic radical,
without an RH assumption. To prove this, take a nonzero polynomial $p$
and choose a reciprocal node $\lambda_0$ with
$p(\bar\lambda_0)\ne0$. Such a node exists because the set of
nodes is infinite and a nonzero polynomial has finitely many zeros.
Use the complete finite set $S$, Lagrange polynomial $L_0$, bound $B$
and $r<|\lambda_0|$ of (ALM12), and put
$q_N(X)=(X/\lambda_0)^NL_0(X)$. The full mixed trace has target
term $M_{\lambda_0}\lambda_0\overline{p(\bar\lambda_0)}\ne0$,
all other terms in $S$ vanish, and the entire remaining tail is bounded by
\begin{equation}\tag{RWR26}
 V\|p\|_R B\left(\frac r{|\lambda_0|}\right)^N\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
This is (ALM3) and the pointwise coefficient bound, with no root omitted.
For some finite $N$ the mixed pairing is therefore nonzero.
Since $\mathscr R=\mathscr U\oplus\mathscr U^\perp$ and the first
summand has the nondegenerate matrix $A$, a vector in the radical of
the orthogonal complement would be in the radical of the whole space.
Thus the residual form $\mathscr Q$ also has zero radical.

This resolves its exact zero-value edge. If $b\ne0$ and
$\mathscr Q(b,b)=0$, choose $c$ with $\beta=\mathscr Q(b,c)\ne0$.
For every
$0<\varepsilon<2|\beta|/(|\mathscr Q(c,c)|+1)$,
\begin{equation}\tag{RWR27}
 \mathscr Q\left(b-\varepsilon\frac{\bar\beta}{|\beta|}c,
                 b-\varepsilon\frac{\bar\beta}{|\beta|}c\right)
 =-2\varepsilon|\beta|+\varepsilon^2\mathscr Q(c,c)<0.
\end{equation}
All vectors involved have finite support. Since the matrix is real
symmetric, at least one of the real and imaginary parts of a complex
negative vector is negative. Rational approximation on that finite
support then supplies a member of $\mathscr N$, with strict sign
preserved by continuity (or the bound (ALM21) after the exact map $J$).
Therefore any nonzero isotropic residual vector already produces a
negative witness; it is not an undetected neutral case. The projection,
inclusion and mixed trace in this argument have the same exact full
lattice lifts as (RWR24).
