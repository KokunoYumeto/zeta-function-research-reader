\section{The target and its exact morphism to the complex numbers}

Let $L$ be a bounded distributive lattice with bottom $0_L$ and top $1_L$.
The target is the given Split-Zero semiring
\begin{equation}\tag{CSP1}
 \begin{gathered}
 S=G_L(\CC)=\{(0,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}\cup
                   \{(a,1_L):a\in\CC\},\\
 (a,\lambda)+(b,\mu)=(a+b,\lambda\vee\mu),\qquad
 (a,\lambda)(b,\mu)=(ab,\lambda\wedge\mu).
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Pairs in the displayed union have their literal equality; thus the two
occurrences of $(0,1_L)$ are one element.  Put
\begin{equation}\tag{CSP2}
 z_\lambda=(0,\lambda),\quad \tau=z_{0_L},\quad e=z_{1_L},\quad
 \widehat a=(a,1_L),\quad 1=\widehat1,\quad \eps_S=\widehat{-1}.
\end{equation}
The multiplicative monoid is pointed by its absorbing element $\tau$.
In particular $e$ is not its distinguished absorbing element when
$0_L\ne1_L$.  Addition and multiplication in (CSP1) are closed: a
nonzero output amplitude forces every factor with a nonzero amplitude
to have top support.  The lattice laws and complex arithmetic prove
the semiring laws, with distributivity using
$\lambda\wedge(\mu\vee\nu)=(\lambda\wedge\mu)\vee
(\lambda\wedge\nu)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:units}
The units of $S$ and the solutions of the imaginary-generator equation are
\begin{equation}\tag{CSP3}
 S^\times=\{\widehat a:a\in\CC^\times\},\qquad
 \widehat a^{-1}=\widehat{a^{-1}},\qquad
 \{j\in S:j^2=\eps_S\}=\{\widehat i,\widehat{-i}\}.
\end{equation}
For every $\lambda\in L$ and positive integer $n$,
\begin{equation}\tag{CSP4}
 z_\lambda^n=z_\lambda,\qquad
 \eps_S z_\lambda=z_\lambda,\qquad
 z_\lambda z_\mu=z_{\lambda\wedge\mu}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
An inverse equation $xy=1$ forces the product of the amplitudes to be
$1$.  Both amplitudes are nonzero, so both supports equal $1_L$ and the
inverse has the displayed value.  Conversely these values multiply to
$1$.  If $j^2=\widehat{-1}$, its amplitude is nonzero and squares to
$-1$, so it equals $i$ or $-i$ and its support is $1_L$.  Formula
(CSP4) follows directly from lattice idempotence and (CSP1).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:amplitude}
The amplitude map and a pointed multiplicative section are
\begin{equation}\tag{CSP5}
 q:S\longrightarrow\CC,\quad q(a,\lambda)=a,\qquad
 b:\CC\longrightarrow S,\quad
 b(0)=\tau,\quad b(a)=\widehat a\ (a\ne0).
\end{equation}
Here $q$ is a unital semiring homomorphism; $b$ is the unique pointed
unital multiplicative section of $q$ and preserves $-1$.  For
$0_L\ne1_L$, $b$ is not additive, and no unital semiring homomorphism
from a nonzero ring to $S$ exists.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The formula for $q$ preserves each operation in (CSP1), together with
zero and one.  Multiplicativity of $b$ holds when both factors are
nonzero because $\CC$ is a field; when at least one is zero it follows
from absorption by $\tau$.  Any section must take each nonzero $a$ to
the only point with amplitude $a$, namely $\widehat a$, and pointedness
forces its value at zero.  However
$b(1)+b(-1)=e\ne\tau=b(0)$.  More generally, a unital semiring
homomorphism $f:R\to S$ from a ring would give
$1+f(-1)=f(0)=\tau$.  The support of the left side is
$1_L\vee\supp(f(-1))=1_L$, contradicting the bottom support of
$\tau$ when $0_L\ne1_L$.
\end{proof}

Complex conjugation is the semiring automorphism
$c_S(a,\lambda)=(\overline a,\lambda)$.  It fixes every $z_\lambda$,
preserves $\eps_S$, and exchanges $\widehat i$ with $\widehat{-i}$.

\section{All chart points and all overlaps}

Let $B=\{0,1,\eps\}$ with $\eps^2=1$ be the source's signed pointed
base monoid.  Its target structure sends $0$ to $\tau$, $1$ to $1$,
and $\eps$ to $\eps_S$.  The chart monoids, with the source's
generators unchanged, are
\begin{equation}\tag{CSP6}
 M_+=B[T,J_+]/(J_+^2=\eps),\quad
 M_-=B[S,J_-]/(J_-^2=\eps),\quad
 M_0=B[T,T^{-1},J]/(J^2=\eps).
\end{equation}
Each nonzero element of $M_+$ is uniquely $J_+^aT^m$ with
$a\in\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$ and $m\in\NN$; $M_-$ has the
corresponding form in $J_-,S$, and $M_0$ has $m\in\mathbb Z$.
Thus these presentations have no additive relation identifying
$1+\eps$ with the distinguished zero.  All homomorphisms in this
section are pointed unital multiplicative $B$-homomorphisms.

The restrictions are exactly the source's restrictions:
\begin{equation}\tag{CSP7}
 \rho_+(T)=T,\quad\rho_+(J_+)=J,\qquad
 \rho_-(S)=T^{-1},\quad\rho_-(J_-)=\eps J.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:charts}
The complete point sets and overlap maps are
\begin{equation}\tag{CSP8}
 U_\pm(S)=\Hom_B(M_\pm,S)=S\times\{\widehat i,\widehat{-i}\},
 \qquad U_0(S)=S^\times\times\{\widehat i,\widehat{-i}\},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{CSP9}
 \rho_+^*(t,j)=(t,j)_+,\qquad
 \rho_-^*(t,j)=(t^{-1},\eps_Sj)_-.
\end{equation}
No $z_\lambda$ belongs to the overlap, including the supported zero
$e$.  The glued chart-point set
\begin{equation}\tag{CSP10}
 X_L=U_+(S)\coprod_{U_0(S)}U_-(S)
\end{equation}
is the disjoint union of two copies of
\begin{equation}\tag{CSP11}
 P_L=\CC^\times\ \sqcup\ \{0_\lambda:\lambda\in L\}
                 \ \sqcup\ \{\infty_\lambda:\lambda\in L\}.
\end{equation}
The symbols $0_\lambda$ and $\infty_\mu$ are distinct for all
$\lambda,\mu$; equality within either family is precisely equality of
the lattice labels.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A map on $M_+$ is determined by $t=\phi(T)$ and
$j=\phi(J_+)$.  The relation forces $j^2=\eps_S$, whose solutions
are (CSP3), and imposes no condition on $t$.  Conversely, for any such
pair, the formula $\phi(J_+^aT^m)=j^at^m$ and $\phi(0)=\tau$
defines a unique map.  The same proof applies to $M_-$.  On $M_0$,
the equation $TT^{-1}=1$ forces $t$ to be a unit, and (CSP3) gives
all possibilities.  Precomposition with (CSP7) yields (CSP9).

Both overlap maps are injective.  Their gluing relation pairs exactly
$(t,j)_+$ with $(t^{-1},-j)_-$ for $t\in S^\times$.  The first
component consists of $U_+$ with $j=\widehat i$ and $U_-$ with
$j=\widehat{-i}$.  In it, a nonzero complex $a$ is represented by
$(\widehat a,\widehat i)_+$, equivalently
$(\widehat{a^{-1}},\widehat{-i})_-$.  Its two families of additional
points are represented by $(z_\lambda,\widehat i)_+$ and
$(z_\lambda,\widehat{-i})_-$, which define respectively
$0_\lambda$ and $\infty_\lambda$.  None is in an overlap, so none
can be identified by a chain of gluing relations.  Reversing the two
imaginary signs gives the second component and proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

This proof constructs $P_L$ as the two-chart gluing
$S\coprod_{S^\times}S$ with reciprocal transition.  It neither
declares a labelled zero invertible nor replaces the pointed zero by
$e$.

\section{The geometric quotient and its amplitude fibres}

The source's symmetry is given on chart points by
\begin{equation}\tag{CSP12}
 \alpha(t,j)_+=(\eps_St,j)_-,\qquad
 \alpha(s,j)_-=(\eps_Ss,j)_+,
\end{equation}
and on the overlap by
\begin{equation}\tag{CSP13}
 \alpha(t,j)=(\eps_St^{-1},\eps_Sj).
\end{equation}
These formulas are evaluations of
$\alpha_+^*(S)=\eps T$, $\alpha_+^*(J_-)=J_+$ and their
minus-chart counterparts, exactly as in the source.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:quotient}
Formulas (CSP12)--(CSP13) define an involution of $X_L$ exchanging
its two components.  Consequently the orbit set
$C_L=X_L/\langle\alpha\rangle$ is in bijection with $P_L$, by the
unique representative in the first component.  Amplitude gives the
surjection
\begin{equation}\tag{CSP14}
 \pi_L:C_L\cong P_L\longrightarrow\PP^1(\CC),\qquad
 a\longmapsto a\ (a\ne0),\quad
 0_\lambda\longmapsto0,\quad \infty_\lambda\longmapsto\infty.
\end{equation}
Its fibre is a singleton over every $a\in\CC^\times$ and an exact
copy of $L$ over each of $0$ and $\infty$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
To check the only gluing issue, start with the equivalent points
$(t,j)_+$ and $(t^{-1},-j)_-$.  Their images are
$(-t,j)_-$ and $(-t^{-1},-j)_+$.  These are equivalent because the
inverse of $-t^{-1}$ is $-t$ and the imaginary sign changes in the
transition.  Applying (CSP12) twice multiplies the spatial coordinate
by $\eps_S^2=1$ and leaves $j$ unchanged.  A first-component plus
point has $j=i$ and maps to a minus point with $j=i$, which lies in
the second component; the other three chart/sign combinations give
the same exchange.  Every orbit therefore meets the first component
exactly once.  The map $q$ in (CSP5) preserves all chart relations,
the reciprocal transition, and the symmetry.  Its resulting quotient
map is exactly (CSP14); Theorem~\ref{thm:charts} supplies all its
fibres.
\end{proof}

For every $\lambda\in L$, the formula
\begin{equation}\tag{CSP15}
 s_\lambda:\PP^1(\CC)\to P_L,\qquad
 s_\lambda(a)=a\ (a\ne0),\quad
 s_\lambda(0)=0_\lambda,\quad
 s_\lambda(\infty)=\infty_\lambda
\end{equation}
is an injective set-theoretic section of $\pi_L$.  Only $s_{0_L}$ is
induced by a pointed multiplicative section of $q$: it is induced by
$b$ in (CSP5).  Indeed a pointed map must send zero to $\tau$.
Thus using $s_{1_L}$ to retain supported zeros is a well-defined
section of the point map but is not an unmentioned pointed-monoid
section.  All of these distinctions disappear when $L$ has one element,
in which case $S=\CC$ and $P_L=\PP^1(\CC)$.

If $f:L\to L'$ is a bounded lattice homomorphism, the map
\begin{equation}\tag{CSP16}
 G_f:S\to G_{L'}(\CC),\quad z_\lambda\mapsto z_{f(\lambda)},
 \quad\widehat a\mapsto\widehat a\ (a\ne0)
\end{equation}
preserves addition, multiplication, zero, one, and $\eps$.  For
addition of two nonzero amplitudes with a zero sum, the image is
$z_{f(1_L)}=z_{1_{L'}}$, so cancellation is also checked.  All other
cases follow directly from meet and join preservation.  It therefore
induces $P_L\to P_{L'}$ taking each pole label $\lambda$ to
$f(\lambda)$ and fixing $\CC^\times$.  This is the exact change-of-
support map, including every zero.

\section{Real structure and every odd Frobenius}

Define two bijections of $P_L$:
\begin{equation}\tag{CSP17}
 \begin{array}{lll}
 \beta(a)=-a^{-1},&\beta(0_\lambda)=\infty_\lambda,
                      &\beta(\infty_\lambda)=0_\lambda,\\
 \sigma(a)=-\overline a^{-1},&\sigma(0_\lambda)=\infty_\lambda,
                      &\sigma(\infty_\lambda)=0_\lambda.
 \end{array}
\end{equation}
These are formulas on disjoint subsets; no inverse of $z_\lambda$
is present.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:real}
Postcomposition of chart points with $c_S$ commutes with $\alpha$,
and the induced real structure on $C_L=P_L$ is $\sigma$ of (CSP17).
It has no fixed points, retains every support label, and obeys
\begin{equation}\tag{CSP18}
 \pi_L\sigma(a)=-1/\overline{\pi_L(a)},\qquad
 \sigma s_\lambda=s_\lambda\sigma_{\CC}.
\end{equation}
The first formula uses the usual projective exchange of $0$ and
$\infty$ on its right side.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Complex conjugation preserves multiplication, inverse of units, and
$\eps_S$, hence commutes with (CSP12)--(CSP13) and respects (CSP9).
It exchanges the two components.  On a first-component plus point
$(t,i)_+$, apply conjugation and then $\alpha$ to return to the first
component: $(t,i)_+\mapsto(\overline t,-i)_+
\mapsto(-\overline t,-i)_-$.  If $t=\widehat a$ is a unit, the
first-component global coordinate is $-1/\overline a$; if
$t=z_\lambda$, the point is $\infty_\lambda$.  The minus-chart
calculation similarly takes $\infty_\lambda$ to $0_\lambda$.
For a unit, a fixed point would satisfy $a\overline a=-1$, which is
impossible in $\CC$.  The two labelled pole families are disjoint,
so there are no fixed points there either.  Equation (CSP18) follows
on all three disjoint subsets.
\end{proof}

For a positive integer $n$ let $P_n$ on $P_L$ raise $a\in\CC^\times$
to $a^n$ and fix every labelled pole.  Let
$d(n)\in\{0,1\}$ be the residue of $(n-1)/2$ modulo $2$ for odd
$n$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:frob}
Raising every element of the source chart monoids to its $n$th power
is a pointed $B$-endomorphism precisely when $n$ is odd.  These maps
commute with restriction and $\alpha$.  Their induced action on
$P_L$ is
\begin{equation}\tag{CSP19}
 F_n=\beta^{d(n)}P_n,\qquad
 F_n(a)=\begin{cases}a^n&n\equiv1\pmod4,\\-a^{-n}&n\equiv3\pmod4,
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{CSP20}
 \begin{array}{c|cc}
 &F_n(0_\lambda)&F_n(\infty_\lambda)\\\hline
 n\equiv1\pmod4&0_\lambda&\infty_\lambda\\
 n\equiv3\pmod4&\infty_\lambda&0_\lambda
 \end{array}.
\end{equation}
They satisfy, without any removal of labelled poles,
\begin{equation}\tag{CSP21}
 F_mF_n=F_{mn},\qquad F_n\sigma=\sigma F_n,\qquad
 \pi_LF_n=\Fr_n\pi_L,\qquad F_ns_\lambda=s_\lambda\Fr_n.
\end{equation}
Here $\Fr_n$ is the source's complex twistor action, from the
proposition labelled\\\texttt{prop:twistor\_frobenius}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Power maps are monoid endomorphisms because the chart monoids are
commutative.  Their restriction to $B$ fixes $\eps$ precisely when
$\eps^n=\eps$, namely for odd $n$.  For odd $n$ the relation
$(J^n)^2=\eps^n=\eps$ is preserved.  On the overlap, the two orders
of restriction send $J_-$ to $(\eps J)^n=\eps J^n$ and send
$S$ to $T^{-n}$, so they agree.  Power maps commute with every
monoid homomorphism, in particular the maps defining $\alpha$.

On chart points they take $(t,j)$ to $(t^n,j^n)$.  Since
$j^n=(-1)^{(n-1)/2}j$, for $n\equiv1\pmod4$ the first component
is preserved, while for $n\equiv3\pmod4$ it is exchanged with the
second.  In the latter case applying $\alpha$ returns the image to
the first component and changes a unit coordinate to $-a^{-n}$.
For $t=z_\lambda$, (CSP4) gives $t^n=z_\lambda$ and multiplication
by $\eps_S$ does not change it.  The chart exchange gives exactly
(CSP20), including $\lambda=0_L,1_L$.

For odd $n$, $\beta P_n=P_n\beta$: on units this is the identity
$(-a^{-1})^n=-a^{-n}$, and both sides have the same pole exchange.
Since $P_mP_n=P_{mn}$ and $d(mn)=d(m)+d(n)$ modulo $2$, (CSP19)
proves the first identity in (CSP21).  The map $\sigma$ is $\beta$
followed by amplitude conjugation; the latter commutes with every
$P_n$ and $\beta$.  This proves the second identity.  The other two
follow from (CSP14), (CSP15), (CSP19), and (CSP20) on every part of
$P_L$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fibresfixed}
For odd $n$, every unit point of $P_L$ has exactly $n$ distinct
$F_n$-preimages, and each labelled pole has exactly one preimage.
For $n>1$ the fixed-point sets are
\begin{equation}\tag{CSP22}
 \operatorname{Fix}(F_n)=
 \begin{cases}
 \{a\in\CC^\times:a^{n-1}=1\}\ \sqcup\
       \{0_\lambda,\infty_\lambda:\lambda\in L\},&n\equiv1\pmod4,\\
 \{a\in\CC^\times:a^{n+1}=-1\},&n\equiv3\pmod4.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
In particular
\begin{equation}\tag{CSP23}
 \bigcap_{n\text{ odd}}\operatorname{Fix}(F_n)=\varnothing.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a nonzero target $w$, the preimage equation is $a^n=w$ or
$a^n=-1/w$.  Its right side is nonzero and its $n$ roots in $\CC$
are distinct, since a repeated root would also annihilate $na^{n-1}$.
No labelled pole maps to a unit.  Formula (CSP20) gives the single
preimage of each pole, with its unchanged support label.  Setting
the expressions in (CSP19) equal to $a$ gives (CSP22); (CSP20)
settles the poles.  A simultaneous fixed point cannot be a pole
because $F_3$ exchanges the two pole families.  A unit fixed by $F_3$
satisfies $a^4=-1$, whereas one fixed by $F_5$ satisfies $a^4=1$.
Their intersection is empty, proving (CSP23).
\end{proof}

Statement (CSP23) concerns \emph{points}.  It does not contradict
the source's Frobenius-invariant \emph{sections} calculation: those
are different explicitly defined functors.  On the source monoid
$M_+$ the simultaneous fixed elements are exactly $B$: a nonzero
element $J^aT^m$ fixed by its third power has $3m=m$, whence $m=0$,
and $3a=a$ modulo $4$, whence $a=0$ or $2$.  Zero is also fixed.
On evaluated points, however, the image of $J$ must be one of the two
square roots of $-1$; neither is fixed by its third power.  This
calculates the difference directly.

\section{The precise Hopf limitation and the surviving torsor law}

The spatial core $B[T,T^{-1}]$ has the source's coproduct
$T\mapsto T\otimes T$, with the base sign fixed.  Its $S$-points
form exactly $S^\times=\CC^\times$.  The action on the enriched
overlap obtained by multiplying its spatial coordinate is
\begin{equation}\tag{CSP24}
 D_a(t,j)=(\widehat a t,j),\qquad a\in\CC^\times.
\end{equation}
It extends to $X_L$ by multiplying the plus coordinate by $a$ and
the minus coordinate by $a^{-1}$, preserving each component.  It
obeys $\alpha D_a=D_{a^{-1}}\alpha$.  If $a^2\ne1$, it does not
induce an action on $C_L$: for a generic point $x=(t,i)$ the two
points $D_a x$ and $D_a\alpha x$ would have to be $\alpha$-related,
but their second coordinates force that relation and their first
coordinates then force $-a/t=-1/(at)$, or $a^2=1$.

There is nevertheless an exact lift of the usual quotient scaling.
Write $\chi(\widehat i)=1$, $\chi(\widehat{-i})=-1$.  Define on
each affine chart, using its own local imaginary generator,
\begin{equation}\tag{CSP24a}
 A_a(t,j)_+=(\widehat{a^{\chi(j)}}t,j)_+,\qquad
 A_a(s,j)_-=(\widehat{a^{\chi(j)}}s,j)_-.
\end{equation}
On the overlap the sign of $j$ changes in the transition and $t$
is inverted, so the two formulas agree.  Formula (CSP12) preserves
the local $j$ while exchanging charts and multiplying by $-1$;
therefore $A_a$ commutes with $\alpha$.  Also $A_aA_b=A_{ab}$.
On the first component, (CSP24a) scales the global unit coordinate
by $a$ and fixes every labelled pole, hence descends to that precise
action $a\cdot x$ on $P_L$.  This is a lift that uses the imaginary
branch; it is not the unmodified action (CSP24).  It obeys
\begin{equation}\tag{CSP25}
 \sigma(a\cdot x)=\overline a^{-1}\cdot\sigma(x)
 \quad(a\in\CC^\times,\ x\in P_L).
\end{equation}
For unit $x$ this is complex arithmetic, and for a pole it follows
because both scaling maps fix the poles and $\sigma$ exchanges their
two families.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:heap}
The prescription $J\mapsto J\otimes J$ does not extend the spatial
diagonal to a $B$-coproduct on $M_0$.  On its full enriched point
set $U_0(S)=\CC^\times\times\{\widehat i,\widehat{-i}\}$ the
choice-free operation that survives is the ternary law
\begin{equation}\tag{CSP26}
 [(t_1,j_1),(t_2,j_2),(t_3,j_3)]
       =(t_1t_2^{-1}t_3,\ j_1j_2^{-1}j_3).
\end{equation}
It satisfies
\begin{equation}\tag{CSP27}
 [x,y,y]=x=[y,y,x],\qquad
 [[x,y,z],u,v]=[x,y,[z,u,v]].
\end{equation}
Both $\alpha$ and complex conjugation preserve it, as does every
odd power map.  Selecting any one point $o$ gives the group law
$x*_oy=[x,o,y]$; selecting $o=(1,\widehat i)$ gives the explicit
law
\begin{equation}\tag{CSP28}
 (t_1,j_1)*_o(t_2,j_2)=(t_1t_2,j_1\widehat{-i}j_2).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In the balanced monoid tensor product over $B$, $\eps\otimes1=
1\otimes\eps$.  Therefore
$(J\otimes J)^2=\eps\otimes\eps=1$, while a $B$-map must
send $J^2=\eps$ to $\eps\otimes1$.  These two elements are
different: evaluate both tensor factors at $T=1,J=\widehat i$
in $S$.  The balanced tensor evaluation sends $1$ to $1$ and
$\eps\otimes1$ to $\widehat{-1}\ne1$.  This is an obstruction
to this exact diagonal on the \emph{enriched} monoid; it is no
obstruction to the coproduct on the spatial core used in (CSP24).

In (CSP26), every displayed inverse exists, and
$(j_1j_2^{-1}j_3)^2=(-1)(-1)^{-1}(-1)=-1$, so the law lands in
the enriched point set.  The cancellation and association identities
(CSP27) follow by multiplying the coordinates; for example both
association expressions have first coordinate
$t_xt_y^{-1}t_zt_u^{-1}t_v$ and the corresponding second coordinate.
Equivalently its source ternary co-operation is
$T\mapsto T\otimes T^{-1}\otimes T$ and
$J\mapsto J\otimes J^{-1}\otimes J$: the square of the latter is
$\eps\otimes\eps^{-1}\otimes\eps=\eps$, so the source relation
is respected.  The image of $\eps$ is the base element, as required.

On the overlap $\alpha(t,j)=(-t^{-1},j^{-1})$.  Substitution into
(CSP26) shows the first coordinate becomes
$-t_1^{-1}t_2t_3^{-1}$ in either order, and the second becomes
$j_1^{-1}j_2j_3^{-1}$ in either order.  Conjugation preserves products
and inverses, and power maps do so in this commutative group; for
odd powers they also preserve the equation $j^2=-1$.  Hence they
preserve (CSP26).  Formula (CSP27) gives identity $o$, associativity,
and inverse $[o,x,o]$ for $*_o$, proving it is a group.  Formula
(CSP28) uses $(\widehat i)^{-1}=\widehat{-i}$.
\end{proof}

The last proposition supplies the exact enriched replacement when the
group-like imaginary-generator prescription fails.  A binary law
requires the stated choice of $o$; the ternary law and all chart,
support, real-structure, and Frobenius maps above require no such
choice.  The calculation retains the source's distinguished choice
of $i$ only when identifying the quotient with its first component.
