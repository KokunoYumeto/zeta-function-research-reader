\subsection{DCP1. The doubled source, its complete topology, and its
receiving
ring}\label{dcp1.-the-doubled-source-its-complete-topology-and-its-receiving-ring}

Let \[
 U=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z,\qquad
 X_\pm=U\cup\{\mathfrak m_\pm\}.
\] Each \(X_\pm\) is a separately labelled copy of the source
specialization space SS1: the only neighbourhood of \(\mathfrak m_\pm\)
in its chart is \(X_\pm\). Glue the copies by the identity on the open
\(U\). The resulting space is \[
 X^{\mathrm{dbl}}=U\cup\{\mathfrak m_+,\mathfrak m_-\}.
\tag{DCP1.1}
\] Its opens are precisely the opens \(V\subseteq U\), the two charts
\(X_+,X_-\), and the whole \(X^{\mathrm{dbl}}\). Indeed an open
containing \(\mathfrak m_+\) must contain \(X_+\), and similarly for the
minus chart. These are also sufficient, by the gluing topology. Every
rational prime point \((p)\), and the arithmetic generic point \((0)\),
remains a distinct point of \(U\).

The two sheaves
\(\mathcal R_\pm=j_*\mathcal O_U\times(i_\pm)_*\mathbb Z\) agree on
\(U\). Their identity comparison there glues them to a ring sheaf
\(\mathcal R^{\mathrm{dbl}}\). Its sections are \[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal R^{\mathrm{dbl}}(V)&=\mathcal O_U(V) &&(\varnothing\ne V\subseteq U),\\
 \mathcal R^{\mathrm{dbl}}(X_\pm)&=\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z,\\
 \mathcal R^{\mathrm{dbl}}(X^{\mathrm{dbl}})
 &=\{((a,b_+),(a,b_-)):a,b_+,b_-\in\mathbb Z\}
 \cong\mathbb Z^3 .
\end{aligned}
\tag{DCP1.2}
\] All products in these receiving rings are componentwise. The global
triples \((a,b_+,b_-)\) restrict to \((a,b_\pm)\) on their respective
chart and to the rational section \(a\) on \(U\). These formulas prove
the ring sheaf gluing directly.

Write the global complementary idempotents as \[
 \epsilon=(1,0,0),\qquad
 e_+=(0,1,0),\qquad e_-=(0,0,1),\qquad
 1_{\mathcal R}=\epsilon+e_++e_-.
\tag{DCP1.3}
\] These are receiving ring elements. Neither \(e_\pm\) is the source
zero or primitive \(\tau\). The supplied source map is \[
 [n]=(n,0,0)=n\epsilon,\qquad [\tau]=(1,1,1).
\tag{DCP1.4}
\] It preserves every supplied multiplication, every integer-input
addition, and the source zero. Its images of \(\tau\) and integer \(1\)
are distinct. Formula DCP1.4 is injective, because distinct integers
have distinct first coordinate and \((1,1,1)\) is not of the form
\((n,0,0)\).

\subsection{DCP2. The map to the original three-point
base}\label{dcp2.-the-map-to-the-original-three-point-base}

Use a different letter for Connes--Consani's base: \[
 Y=\{x_+,\eta,x_-\},\qquad
 Y_\pm=\{x_\pm,\eta\}.
\] Its opens are \(\varnothing,\{\eta\},Y_+,Y_-,Y\). Define \[
 f:X^{\mathrm{dbl}}\longrightarrow Y,\qquad
 f(u)=\eta\ (u\in U),\quad f(\mathfrak m_\pm)=x_\pm .
\tag{DCP2.1}
\] Its inverse images of the five opens are respectively
\(\varnothing,U,X_+,X_-,X^{\mathrm{dbl}}\), so it is continuous. Its
full fibres are \[
 f^{-1}(\eta)=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z,\qquad
 f^{-1}(x_\pm)=\{\mathfrak m_\pm\}.
\tag{DCP2.2}
\] The arithmetic primes remain distinct source points even though \(f\)
sends all of them to \(\eta\); their whole fibre is displayed. The map
is not injective and is not an isomorphism of spaces. It is not an
identification of primitive \(\tau\) with \(\eta\), or a claimed
morphism between the two structure sheaves.

The geometric part of the exact comparison is the commutative diagram \[
\begin{array}{ccccc}
 \mathfrak m_+&\subset&X^{\mathrm{dbl}}&\supset&\mathfrak m_-\\
 \downarrow&&\downarrow f&&\downarrow\\
 x_+&\subset&Y&\supset&x_-
\end{array}
\qquad
\begin{array}{ccc}
 U=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z&\hookrightarrow&X^{\mathrm{dbl}}\\
 \downarrow&&\downarrow f\\
 \{\eta\}&\hookrightarrow&Y .
\end{array}
\tag{DCP2.3}
\] The lower generic point receives the complete arithmetic fibre; the
source's localizations \(\mathbb Z_{(p)}\) are still the stalks of
\(\mathcal R^{\mathrm{dbl}}\) at \((p)\).

\subsection{DCP3. The original coefficient spaces and every restriction
factor}\label{dcp3.-the-original-coefficient-spaces-and-every-restriction-factor}

The specified finite-unit-invariant coefficient sheaf on \(Y\) is
denoted \(\Omega\). Its chart spaces are \[
 V_+=S_{00}^{\mathrm{even}}\oplus\mathbb C^2,\qquad
 V_-=S_{00}^{\mathrm{even}}\oplus\mathbb C^2,
\] \[
 S_{00}^{\mathrm{even}}
 =\{h\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R;\mathbb C):
 h(-v)=h(v),\ h(0)=0,\ \int_{\mathbb R}h(v)\,dv=0\}.
\tag{DCP3.1}
\] The labelled endpoint coordinates are \((c_0,c_1)\) on \(V_+\) and
\((d_0,d_1)\) on \(V_-\). Its overlap is the strong space \[
 A=\{b\in C^\infty(\mathbb R_{>0}):
 \sup_{u>0}(u^N+u^{-N})|(u\partial_u)^j b(u)|<\infty
 \text{ for all }N,j\ge0\}.
\tag{DCP3.2}
\] Define \[
 \Sigma h(u)=2\sum_{n\ge1}h(nu),\qquad
 Rb(u)=u^{-1}b(u^{-1}),
\] \[
 r_+(h,c_0,c_1)=\Sigma h,\qquad
 r_-(h,d_0,d_1)=R\Sigma h.
\tag{DCP3.3}
\] The factor2 retains both signs of rational summation in the even
sector. The reciprocal argument and the factor \(u^{-1}\) in \(R\) are
both retained. The Fourier convention is \[
 \widehat h(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}h(v)e^{-2\pi ivt}\,dv .
\] The full Poisson identity is \[
 \Sigma\widehat h(u)
 =u^{-1}\Sigma h(u^{-1})+u^{-1}h(0)-\int_{\mathbb R}h(v)\,dv .
\tag{DCP3.4}
\] For the stated source both final terms vanish by its two moment
conditions, and therefore \(R\Sigma h=\Sigma\widehat h\). The endpoint
spaces remain separately present even though their restrictions are
zero.

The original centered receiver is still related by the complete map \[
 (\mathcal T k)(u)=2u^{-1/2}k(u),\qquad
 \mathcal T^{-1}b(u)=\tfrac12u^{1/2}b(u).
\tag{DCP3.5}
\] For \(\mathcal E h(u)=u^{1/2}\sum_{n\ge1}h(nu)\), one has
\(\Sigma h=\mathcal T\mathcal E h\). These are specified invertible maps
of receiving test spaces, with all constants retained, not replacement
formulas for the original source.

Let \(J=\Sigma(S_{00}^{\mathrm{even}})\). SSI1--SSI7 proves that
\(\Sigma\) is a continuous bijection onto the closed subspace \(J\),
with continuous inverse. Its Mellin description is \[
 \mathcal M_0 b(s)=\int_0^\infty b(u)u^s\frac{du}{u},\qquad
 \mathcal M_0\Sigma h(s)=2\zeta(s)\int_0^\infty h(v)v^s\frac{dv}{v}.
\tag{DCP3.6}
\] The identity is first absolutely convergent for \(\Re s>1\), with the
continuation and endpoint/trivial-zero residue calculations proved in
SSI2--SSI6. In particular \[
 Q=A/J=A/\overline J
 \cong\mathcal B/I,
\tag{DCP3.7}
\] where \(\mathcal B\) is the strong entire Mellin space and \(I\)
imposes the full vanishing order at every actual original nontrivial
zero of \(\zeta\). This is the proved closed-image theorem, not an
inference from the name cohomology. The function in DCP3.6 is the
original \(\zeta\).

\subsection{DCP4. Calculate the inverse-image sheaf on every
open}\label{dcp4.-calculate-the-inverse-image-sheaf-on-every-open}

Form the inverse image of the underlying sheaf of abelian groups: \[
 \mathcal N=f^{-1}\Omega.
\tag{DCP4.1}
\] On a nonempty open \(V\subseteq U\), the smallest target open whose
inverse image contains \(V\) is \(\{\eta\}\). The inverse-image presheaf
therefore has value \(A\) on \(V\). This presheaf is already a sheaf
there: every nonempty open of the irreducible \(U\) contains the
arithmetic generic point, so all nonempty opens intersect; compatible
locally constant values must be the same value. Hence \[
 \mathcal N|_U=\underline A_U,\qquad
 \underline A_U(V)=A\quad(\varnothing\ne V\subseteq U).
\tag{DCP4.2}
\] Equivalently, if \(g:\{(0)\}\hookrightarrow U\) is the arithmetic
generic inclusion, this sheaf is \(g_*A\). All its nonempty restriction
maps are identity, so it is flabby and has zero higher sheaf cohomology
on \(U\).

On \(X_\pm\) the smallest target open is \(Y_\pm\), and a cover of
\(X_\pm\) contains \(X_\pm\) itself. Sheafification therefore changes
nothing. On the whole doubled space the gluing condition is exactly the
one on the original cover. Consequently \[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal N(X_\pm)&=V_\pm,\\
 \mathcal N(V)&=A &&(\varnothing\ne V\subseteq U),\\
 \mathcal N(X^{\mathrm{dbl}})
 &=\{(v_+,v_-):r_+v_+=r_-v_-\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{DCP4.3}
\] The restrictions from the two charts to any such \(V\) are exactly
\(r_\pm\), followed by the identity of \(A\).

Give this sheaf a receiving \(\mathcal R^{\mathrm{dbl}}\)-module action
as follows. At a chart, \((a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2\) acts on \(V_\pm\) by
scalar \(a\). On a nonempty arithmetic open, a section of
\(\mathcal O_U(V)\subset\mathbb Q\) acts on \(A\) by the specified
rational scalar in its complex vector space. Both restrictions preserve
this action because \(r_\pm\) are complex-linear. On global pairs the
triple \((a,b_+,b_-)\) acts by \(a\) on both entries. These maps prove
the module sheaf axioms on every open.

Thus the precise CGS datum on the plus or minus source chart is \[
 (\underline A_U,\ V_\pm,\ 0,\ r_\pm).
\tag{DCP4.4}
\] Here the middle entries mean the \(\epsilon=1\) and \(\epsilon=0\)
stalk components. In particular source \(\tau\) and integer \(1\) have
the same action on \(\mathcal N\). This nonfaithful action is retained
explicitly; it is not used to identify their source elements. DCP8
constructs a canonical larger coefficient sheaf that distinguishes them.

\subsection{DCP5. Direct images: exact recovery and the higher-image
qualification}\label{dcp5.-direct-images-exact-recovery-and-the-higher-image-qualification}

By DCP4.3 the adjunction unit \[
 \Omega\longrightarrow f_*f^{-1}\Omega
\tag{DCP5.1}
\] is identity on \(V_+\), \(V_-\), and \(A\). On \(Y\) it is identity
on their displayed fibre product. These exhaust the target opens and all
their restrictions, so DCP5.1 is an isomorphism of sheaves. It also
preserves the original section topologies because these section maps are
identities.

For \(q>0\), \[
 R^q f_*\mathcal N=0.
\tag{DCP5.2}
\] Here is the full local proof. The minimal target neighbourhoods are
\(Y_+\) at \(x_+\), \(Y_-\) at \(x_-\), and \(\{\eta\}\) at \(\eta\).
The stalks of the higher direct image are the corresponding cohomology
groups on their inverse images. The charts \(X_\pm\) have exact section
functor, equal to the stalk at \(\mathfrak m_\pm\), so their higher
groups vanish for every sheaf. The remaining inverse image is \(U\),
where DCP4.2 gives the flabby sheaf \(\underline A_U\). Its higher
groups vanish too. Every stalk of DCP5.2 is therefore zero.

It would be false to strengthen this proof into acyclicity over
\textbf{every} target open. For the whole target \(Y\), the inverse
image is \(X^{\mathrm{dbl}}\), whose first cohomology is \(Q\),
calculated below. The higher-direct-image sheaf is zero because the
local basis is acyclic; the global cohomology is carried by the original
base \(Y\). In particular the exact comparison is \[
 Rf_*\mathcal N\simeq\Omega,\qquad
 R\Gamma(X^{\mathrm{dbl}},\mathcal N)
 \simeq R\Gamma(Y,\Omega),
\tag{DCP5.3}
\] not a claim that either global complex has zero first cohomology.

\subsection{DCP6. The whole Cech complex and all four endpoint
lines}\label{dcp6.-the-whole-cech-complex-and-all-four-endpoint-lines}

The cover \(X_+,X_-\) and its intersection \(U\) are acyclic for
\(\mathcal N\), by DCP5. The exact Cech complex is therefore \[
 D^0=V_+\oplus V_-,\qquad D^1=A,\qquad
 d(v_+,v_-)=r_+v_+-r_-v_-.
\tag{DCP6.1}
\] Its topology and differential are precisely the original CC complex,
not merely isomorphic cohomology groups. Fourier preserves
\(S_{00}^{\mathrm{even}}\) and is an involution there; DCP3.4 therefore
gives \(r_+(V_+)=r_-(V_-)=J\).

Injectivity of \(\Sigma\) gives \[
 H^0(X^{\mathrm{dbl}},\mathcal N)
 =\{((\widehat h,c_0,c_1),(h,d_0,d_1)):
 h\in S_{00}^{\mathrm{even}},\ c_0,c_1,d_0,d_1\in\mathbb C\},
\tag{DCP6.2}
\] \[
 H^1(X^{\mathrm{dbl}},\mathcal N)=Q,\qquad H^q=0\quad(q\ge2).
\tag{DCP6.3}
\] Indeed the kernel equation is \(\Sigma f=\Sigma\widehat h\), hence
\(f=\widehat h\); the endpoint coordinates are unrestricted. The
differential has image \(J\), proving the cokernel formula. Its image is
closed by SSI, so ordinary sheaf cohomology with its displayed quotient
topology is already Hausdorff.

\subsection{DCP7. Every closed-support map, including the orientation
sign}\label{dcp7.-every-closed-support-map-including-the-orientation-sign}

Excision to a chart and CGS5 identify the single-support complex with \[
 K_\pm=[V_\pm\xrightarrow{r_\pm}A]
 \quad\text{in degrees }0,1.
\tag{DCP7.1}
\] Consequently its degree-zero cohomology is precisely its two labelled
endpoint lines, its degree-one cohomology is \(Q\), and its other groups
vanish. Its map to DCP6.1 is \(v_+\mapsto(v_+,0)\) and identity in
degree1 for the plus support. For the minus support it is
\(v_-\mapsto(0,v_-)\) and \textbf{minus identity} in degree1.
Substitution into DCP6.1 verifies both chain maps.

The restriction \(H^0(X^{\mathrm{dbl}},\mathcal N)\to V_-\) has the
continuous lift \[
 (h,d_0,d_1)\longmapsto
 ((\widehat h,0,0),(h,d_0,d_1)).
\tag{DCP7.2}
\] Its restriction is identity by inspection. The equation
\(r_+\widehat h=r_-h\) proves it lands in global sections. Thus its
connecting map
\(V_-\to H^1_{\{\mathfrak m_+\}}(X^{\mathrm{dbl}},\mathcal N)\) is zero
by this actual lift.

For both closed supports \(Z=\{\mathfrak m_+,\mathfrak m_-\}\), the
complete supported complex is \[
 K^0=V_+\oplus V_-,\qquad K^1=A\oplus A,\qquad
 d_K(v_+,v_-)=(r_+v_+,r_-v_-).
\tag{DCP7.3}
\] To verify it, take the mapping fibre of the restriction
\(D\to A[0]\), using \(r_+\) on degree0. In degree1 this fibre has
coordinates \((k,b)\) and differential \((r_+v_+-r_-v_-,r_+v_+)\). The
invertible change \((k,b)\mapsto(b,b-k)\) gives DCP7.3. Its inverse is
\((b_+,b_-)\mapsto(b_+-b_-,b_+)\). This supplies the complete chain
comparison and all signs.

The supported-to-global map is identity in degree0 and
\((b_+,b_-)\mapsto b_+-b_-\) in degree1. The boundary from the open
\(A\) is \(b\mapsto(b,b)\). The resulting sequence is \[
 0\to\mathbb C^4\to H^0(X^{\mathrm{dbl}},\mathcal N)
 \to A\xrightarrow{b\mapsto([b],[b])}
 Q\oplus Q\xrightarrow{(q_+,q_-)\mapsto q_+-q_-}Q\to0.
\tag{DCP7.4}
\] All four endpoint lines remain in its first term. The image of the
preceding restriction into \(A\) is \(J\), so after the displayed
quotient this is the exact diagonal/difference row \[
 0\to Q\xrightarrow{\Delta}Q\oplus Q\xrightarrow{\delta}Q\to0.
\tag{DCP7.5}
\] The continuous section \(q\mapsto(q,0)\) splits \(\delta\); the
mirror-compatible section is computed in DCP11.

\subsection{DCP8. Canonical scalar extension retaining the source
distinction}\label{dcp8.-canonical-scalar-extension-retaining-the-source-distinction}

Use the constant ring sheaf \(\underline{\mathbb Z}\) and its
\textbf{unital} map into \(\mathcal R^{\mathrm{dbl}}\). On the whole
space that map sends \[
 n\longmapsto(n,n,n).
\tag{DCP8.1}
\] This is different from the distinguished source-integer map
\(n\mapsto n\epsilon=(n,0,0)\) in DCP1.4. Define the scalar extension of
the underlying abelian sheaf by \[
 \mathcal M_{\mathrm{full}}
 =\mathcal R^{\mathrm{dbl}}\otimes_{\underline{\mathbb Z}}\mathcal N.
\tag{DCP8.2}
\] This operation is defined entirely in the already constructed
receiving module category. At \(\mathfrak m_\pm\), its stalk is \[
 (\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}V_\pm
 \xrightarrow{\sim}V_\pm\oplus V_\pm,\qquad
 (a,b)\otimes v\longmapsto(av,bv).
\tag{DCP8.3}
\] The inverse is \((v,w)\mapsto(1,0)\otimes v+(0,1)\otimes w\).

At any arithmetic stalk \((p)\), including the generic point, the
corresponding map is \[
 \mathcal O_{U,(p)}\otimes_{\mathbb Z}A\longrightarrow A,\qquad
 q\otimes a\longmapsto qa.
\tag{DCP8.4}
\] It is an isomorphism because \(A\) is a \(\mathbb Q\)-vector space.
Its inverse is \(a\mapsto1\otimes a\). To verify the nontrivial inverse
identity, for \(q=r/t\) use \(t(q\otimes a-1\otimes qa)=0\).
Multiplication by \(t\ne0\) on the tensor is invertible by
multiplication by \(1/t\) on its \(A\) factor, so the difference is
zero. The same construction on every open proves
\(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{full}}|_U\simeq\underline A_U\).

There are no hidden higher tensor terms: \(\mathcal R^{\mathrm{dbl}}\)
is flat over \(\underline{\mathbb Z}\). Its closed stalks are the free
group \(\mathbb Z^2\), and its arithmetic stalks are localizations of
\(\mathbb Z\), which are flat since localization carries injections to
injections by its explicit fraction equality. Tensor exactness is
stalkwise, proving the assertion. Thus DCP8.2 is also the derived scalar
extension for these degree-zero inputs.

The complete chart datum is now \[
 (\underline A_U,\ V_\pm,\ V_\pm,\ r_\pm).
\tag{DCP8.5}
\] The restriction is \[
 (v,w)\longmapsto r_\pm(v),
\tag{DCP8.6}
\] since the second scalar component restricts to zero on \(U\).
Therefore there is the canonical receiving-module decomposition \[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal M_{\mathrm{full}}
 \cong\mathcal N\oplus(i_+)_*V_+\oplus(i_-)_*V_- ,
 }
\tag{DCP8.7}
\] where the extra summands have \(\epsilon=0\). The projections and
inclusions are the global complementary idempotents DCP1.3. On the first
summand the earlier \(\epsilon=1\) module action on \(\mathcal N\) is
retained; on the extra closed copies the corresponding \(e_\pm\) acts as
identity. The restrictions DCP8.6 prove this equality as sheaves, not
only as stalk vector spaces.

At either closed stalk the source action is \[
 [\tau](v,w)=(v,w),\qquad [n](v,w)=(nv,0).
\tag{DCP8.8}
\] It is faithful as an action of the supplied source monoid. Distinct
integers act differently on the nonzero complex vector space in the
first component. Primitive \(\tau\) acts differently from every integer
on any nonzero vector in the second component. Products preserve the
supplied multiplication; integer-input addition preserves the supplied
integer addition. This assigns no source parity, coordinate, or addition
to \(\tau\).

There are two distinct natural maps that must not be conflated. The
scalar-extension unit of underlying abelian sheaves is \[
 \eta:\mathcal N\to\mathcal M_{\mathrm{full}},\quad v\mapsto1_{\mathcal R}\otimes v,
\] which is \(v\mapsto(v,v)\) at a closed stalk. It is not equivariant
for the distinguished source-integer action on the original
\(\epsilon=1\) comparison: for integer \(n\), \(\eta(nv)=(nv,nv)\),
whereas \([n]\eta(v)=(nv,0)\). The receiving-module section of the
projection onto \(\mathcal N\) is instead \[
 \iota_\epsilon(v)=\epsilon\otimes v=(v,0).
\tag{DCP8.9}
\] It is \(\mathcal R^{\mathrm{dbl}}\)-linear and source-equivariant.
Both maps have been constructed, and the exact difference is the extra
closed component \(v\mapsto(0,v)\).

\subsection{DCP9. Cohomology and direct images of the faithful scalar
extension}\label{dcp9.-cohomology-and-direct-images-of-the-faithful-scalar-extension}

Each extra closed sheaf in DCP8.7 is acyclic: its sections over any open
containing its point are the fixed stalk group and all remaining
sections are zero, so it is flabby. It follows by the proved
decomposition that \[
\begin{aligned}
 H^0(X^{\mathrm{dbl}},\mathcal M_{\mathrm{full}})
 &\cong H^0(X^{\mathrm{dbl}},\mathcal N)\oplus V_+\oplus V_-,\\
 H^1(X^{\mathrm{dbl}},\mathcal M_{\mathrm{full}})&\cong Q,\\
 H^q(X^{\mathrm{dbl}},\mathcal M_{\mathrm{full}})&=0\quad(q\ge2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{DCP9.1}
\] On the Cech complex this is also immediate: degree0 becomes
\((V_+\oplus V_+)\oplus(V_-\oplus V_-)\), and the differential is \[
 ((v_+,w_+),(v_-,w_-))
 \longmapsto r_+(v_+)-r_-(v_-).
\tag{DCP9.2}
\] There is no degree-one term from either \(w\) component. In
particular the extra complete Schwartz spaces and their endpoint lines
are retained in degree zero, not silently discarded.

At a single closed support the complex is \[
 [V_\pm\oplus V_\pm\longrightarrow A],
 \quad(v,w)\longmapsto r_\pm(v).
\tag{DCP9.3}
\] Its degree-zero group is \(\mathbb C^2\oplus V_\pm\), its degree-one
group is \(Q\), and all higher groups vanish. For both supports the
first term of DCP7.4 becomes \(\mathbb C^4\oplus V_+\oplus V_-\); the
subsequent supported degree-one row remains DCP7.5 with exactly the same
maps and signs.

The corresponding direct image is \[
 f_*\mathcal M_{\mathrm{full}}
 \cong\Omega\oplus(k_+)_*V_+\oplus(k_-)_*V_-,
\qquad R^qf_*\mathcal M_{\mathrm{full}}=0\quad(q>0),
\tag{DCP9.4}
\] where \(k_\pm:\{x_\pm\}\hookrightarrow Y\). These formulas are
checked on the five target opens using DCP8.7. Higher direct images
vanish on the same minimal-open basis as in DCP5, with the additional
flabby point summands. This does not claim
\(f_*\mathcal M_{\mathrm{full}}=\Omega\); its projection onto \(\Omega\)
and its extra kernel are displayed in DCP9.4.

\subsection{DCP10. Source integer action and independent spectral
action}\label{dcp10.-source-integer-action-and-independent-spectral-action}

The original unshifted positive-real action is \[
\begin{aligned}
 T_a b(u)&=b(u/a),\\
 \rho_+(a)(h,c_0,c_1)&=(h(\,\cdot/a),c_0,ac_1),\\
 \rho_-(a)(h,d_0,d_1)&=(a h(a\,\cdot),ad_0,d_1),
\end{aligned}
\qquad a>0 .
