\subsection{CS1. The adèlic source and its compact unit
projection}\label{cs1.-the-aduxe8lic-source-and-its-compact-unit-projection}

Write \[
\mathbb A_{\mathbb Q}=\mathbb R\times\mathbb A_f,\qquad
\widehat{\mathbb Z}=\prod_p\mathbb Z_p,\qquad
K=\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{\times}.
\tag{CS1.1}
\] Use Lebesgue measure at the real place and finite additive Haar
measure with \(\operatorname{vol}(\widehat{\mathbb Z})=1\). The adèlic
Bruhat--Schwartz space is the locally convex inductive limit of the
spaces of finite sums \(f_i(v)\phi_i(x_f)\), where
\(f_i\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R)\) and the \(\phi_i\) belong to one
finite-dimensional space of locally constant, compactly supported
functions on \(\mathbb A_f\). On each such stage the topology is a
finite direct sum of the ordinary real Schwartz topology.

Put \[
\mathcal S(\mathbb A_{\mathbb Q})_0
=\left\{F:F(0)=0,\ \int_{\mathbb A_{\mathbb Q}}F(x)\,dx=0\right\},
\quad
\mathcal R=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}
\{F-F(q\,\cdot):F\in\mathcal S(\mathbb A_{\mathbb Q}),\
q\in\mathbb Q^\times\},
\] \[
D=\mathcal S(\mathbb A_{\mathbb Q})_0/\overline{\mathcal R}.
\tag{CS1.2}
\] The closure is taken in \(\mathcal S(\mathbb A_{\mathbb Q})_0\) with
its subspace topology. Rational scaling preserves both endpoint
functionals, since \(|q|_{\mathbb A}=1\), so every displayed difference
belongs to that moment-null space even when its generating \(F\) does
not. This is the full-difference reading of the source's ``subspace of
\(\mathcal S(\mathbb A_{\mathbb Q})_0\) generated by the differences.''
The source line does not separately write a domain after its \(f\); the
distinction and its mathematically forced consequence are recorded in
CS2A.

The group \(K\) acts on finite coordinates. Its normalized Haar average
\(P_K\) is continuous on this source: each test function is fixed by an
open subgroup of \(K\), so its average is a finite average on that
test-function stage. The average commutes with rational scaling and
preserves both endpoint conditions. It consequently descends to a
continuous idempotent on \(D\).

Every idèle class has a unique representative \((u,k)\), with \(u>0\)
and \(k\in K\). To see this, multiply its finite component by the
positive rational with opposite finite valuations, and then use the sign
in \(\mathbb Q^\times\) to make its real component positive. Uniqueness
follows because a rational number with every finite valuation zero is
\(\pm1\), and positivity excludes the minus sign. Thus \[
C_{\mathbb Q}=\mathbb A_{\mathbb Q}^{\times}/\mathbb Q^\times
\ \cong\ \mathbb R_{>0}^{\times}\times K,\qquad |(u,k)|=u.
\tag{CS1.3}
\] This is a coordinate on the authors' already constructed idèle-class
group.

\subsection{CS2. An exact topological identification of the source
sector}\label{cs2.-an-exact-topological-identification-of-the-source-sector}

Define \[
\mathcal S_0^{\mathrm{even}}
=\left\{f\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R):
f(-v)=f(v),\quad f(0)=0,\quad\int_{\mathbb R}f(v)\,dv=0\right\},
\] \[
j:\mathcal S_0^{\mathrm{even}}\longrightarrow D,\qquad
j(f)=[f\otimes1_{\widehat{\mathbb Z}}].
\tag{CS2.1}
\] This is continuous, and its image is fixed by \(K\).

We construct its continuous inverse on \(P_KD\). A \(K\)-invariant
finite Schwartz function is a finite linear combination of \[
1_{d\widehat{\mathbb Z}},\qquad d\in\mathbb Q_{>0}.
\tag{CS2.2}
\] Here \(d\widehat{\mathbb Z}\) is the product of the balls
\(p^{v_p(d)}\mathbb Z_p\). To prove the claim, choose the finitely many
primes on which the function is nonstandard. At each such prime, compact
support bounds valuations below and local constancy makes the function
constant on one sufficiently small ball around zero. Unit invariance
leaves a finite list of valuation shells and that final ball. Each shell
is the difference of two nested ball indicators. Taking tensor products
gives (CS2.2).

These ball indicators are linearly independent. In one local factor,
evaluation successively on the shells of largest and then smaller
absolute values proves independence of a finite list of nested balls.
Finite tensor products of independent lists are independent, and every
proposed relation involves such a finite product. This also proves that
the following definition is independent of its expansion.

For \(f^{\mathrm{ev}}(v)=(f(v)+f(-v))/2\), define on \(K\)-averaged
finite sums \[
B\bigl(f\otimes1_{d\widehat{\mathbb Z}}\bigr)(v)
=f^{\mathrm{ev}}(dv),
\qquad
B(F):=B(P_KF)
\quad\text{on the full source.}
\tag{CS2.3}
\] On each finite stage this is a finite sum of fixed real dilations and
the even projection, hence is continuous into
\(\mathcal S^{\mathrm{even}}(\mathbb R)\). The defining inductive-limit
topology proves continuity on the full source.

The endpoint identities are exact: \[
B(F)(0)=F(0),\qquad
\int_{\mathbb R}B(F)(v)\,dv
=\int_{\mathbb A_{\mathbb Q}}F(x)\,dx.
\tag{CS2.4}
\] For a ball tensor the second identity follows from \[
\operatorname{vol}(d\widehat{\mathbb Z})
=\prod_p p^{-v_p(d)}=d^{-1},
\qquad
\int_{\mathbb R}f^{\mathrm{ev}}(dv)\,dv
=d^{-1}\int_{\mathbb R}f(v)\,dv.
\tag{CS2.5}
\] The first identity is immediate; averaging does not change either
endpoint functional. Thus \(B\) maps the source in (CS1.2) into
\(\mathcal S_0^{\mathrm{even}}\).

For \(q\in\mathbb Q^\times\), direct substitution on a ball tensor gives
\[
B\bigl((f\otimes1_{d\widehat{\mathbb Z}})(q\,\cdot)\bigr)(v)
=f^{\mathrm{ev}}(dv)
=B(f\otimes1_{d\widehat{\mathbb Z}})(v).
\tag{CS2.6}
\] Indeed the new finite ball is \((d/|q|)\widehat{\mathbb Z}\), while
the new even real function is \(f^{\mathrm{ev}}(|q|v)\). Since \(P_K\)
commutes with this scaling, (CS2.6) holds on the full source. Continuity
makes \(B\) vanish on \(\overline{\mathcal R}\), so it descends to a
continuous map \(\bar B:D\to\mathcal S_0^{\mathrm{even}}\).

The two compositions are \[
\bar B\,j=\mathrm{id},\qquad j\,\bar B=P_K\quad\text{on }D.
\tag{CS2.7}
\] For the second identity, individual tensors need not themselves be
moment-null, so first work with differences before taking a quotient
class. Put \(G=f\otimes1_{d\widehat{\mathbb Z}}\) and
\(H_d=G(d\,\cdot)=f(dv)\otimes1_{\widehat{\mathbb Z}}\). Let
\(\widetilde j\) denote tensoring with \(1_{\widehat{\mathbb Z}}\) on
all even Schwartz functions. The exact identity is \[
G-\widetilde j(BG)
=(G-G(d\,\cdot))+\tfrac12(H_d-H_d(-\,\cdot))
\ \in\mathcal R.
\tag{CS2.7a}
\] Both parenthesized differences have zero endpoint moments, whether or
not their generating functions do. Sum (CS2.7a) over a radial-ball
expansion of \(P_KF\), with \(F\) moment-null. Only then take its class
in \(D\). This proves \(j\bar B[F]=P_K[F]\), with every class taken on
its actual domain.

Finally \(P_KD=D^K\). One inclusion follows from averaging. For a fixed
quotient class, choose a representative \(F\). Its \(K\)-orbit lies in a
finite averaging orbit modulo an open subgroup. Averaging its equal
quotient classes therefore gives the same class. We have proved the
actual topological isomorphism \[
\boxed{j:\mathcal S_0^{\mathrm{even}}\ \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\ D^K,
\qquad j^{-1}=\bar B|_{D^K}.}
\tag{CS2.8}
\] In particular, this identification neither discards the closed
coinvariant relation nor assumes it has no kernel.

\subsection{CS2A. Why the generating domain must be
retained}\label{cs2a.-why-the-generating-domain-must-be-retained}

Let \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{null}}\) denote instead the span of
\(F-F(q\,\cdot)\) with \(F\) itself moment-null. Then
\(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{null}}\subset\mathcal R\), and there is a natural
continuous surjection \[
D_{\mathrm{null}}:=
\mathcal S(\mathbb A_{\mathbb Q})_0/
\overline{\mathcal R_{\mathrm{null}}}
\longrightarrow D.
\tag{CS2A.1}
\] This alternative quotient is genuinely larger. Here is a direct proof
which also identifies the source of the difference.

For a prime \(p\), expand a \(K\)-averaged Schwartz function as
\(\sum_d f_d\otimes1_{d\widehat{\mathbb Z}}\), and define the
two-component continuous functional \[
\ell_p(F)=\sum_d v_p(d)
\left(f_d(0),\ d^{-1}\int_{\mathbb R}f_d(v)\,dv\right),
\qquad \ell_p(F):=\ell_p(P_KF)
\text{ before averaging.}
\tag{CS2A.2}
\] The sum is finite, its expansion is unique by CS2, and continuity
follows on each finite test-function stage. Put
\(\epsilon(F)=(F(0),\int_{\mathbb A}F)\). On ball tensors, scaling
changes the index \(d\) to \(d/|q|\) and leaves the two displayed
endpoint values unchanged. Hence \[
\ell_p(F(q\,\cdot))
=\ell_p(F)-v_p(|q|)\epsilon(F).
\tag{CS2A.3}
\] It follows that \(\ell_p\) vanishes on
\(\overline{\mathcal R_{\mathrm{null}}}\), but not on all of
\(\mathcal R\). Indeed, take any even Schwartz \(f\) and \[
G=f\otimes1_{\widehat{\mathbb Z}}
-(f\otimes1_{\widehat{\mathbb Z}})(p\,\cdot).
\] Then \(G\) is moment-null, its summation is zero by rational
reindexing, and \[
\ell_p(G)=\left(f(0),\int_{\mathbb R}f\right).
\tag{CS2A.4}
\] The endpoint map on even Schwartz functions is onto \(\mathbb C^2\):
for example the functions \(e^{-\pi v^2}\) and \(v^2e^{-\pi v^2}\) have
endpoint pairs \((1,1)\) and \((0,1/(2\pi))\). Thus these are nonzero
classes in the alternative quotient whenever that pair is nonzero.

In particular, the injective summation and Fourier-graph claim in the
original source cannot hold on \(D_{\mathrm{null}}\) with this topology:
(CS2A.4) supplies an explicit kernel. The full-difference reading in CS1
removes exactly these displayed differences, and CS2--CS3 prove the
needed sector result for it. Even under the alternative reading, the map
(CS2A.1) and the commuting summation maps give the same summation image
and the same Hausdorff first-cohomology receiver. Its degree-zero kernel
contains additional classes, so its \(H^0\) must not be described by
(CS5.3).

The complete difference between the two quotient spaces can also be
computed. Give \[
V=\bigoplus_{p\ {\rm prime}}\mathbb C^2
\tag{CS2A.5}
\] the locally convex direct-sum topology, and put
\(\mathscr L(F)=(\ell_p(F))_p\). A fixed test-function stage has only
finitely many relevant primes in its radial-ball expansion. Thus
\(\mathscr L\) has finite support on every test function and is
continuous into \(V\).

Choose the continuous endpoint section
\(\sigma:\mathbb C^2\to\mathcal S(\mathbb A_{\mathbb Q})\) given by
tensoring \[
\sigma(a,b)_\infty(v)
=a(1-2\pi v^2)e^{-\pi v^2}
+b\,2\pi v^2e^{-\pi v^2}
\tag{CS2A.6}
\] with \(1_{\widehat{\mathbb Z}}\). The Gaussian moments give
\(\epsilon(\sigma(a,b))=(a,b)\). Define the continuous finite-sum map \[
\mathscr T(v)=\sum_p
\bigl(\sigma(v_p)-\sigma(v_p)(p\,\cdot)\bigr)
\in\mathcal R,\qquad
\mathscr L\mathscr T=\mathrm{id}_V.
\tag{CS2A.7}
\] The second identity is (CS2A.3) at every prime.

Write \(r_q(F)=F-F(q\,\cdot)\). Modulo \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{null}}\),
this depends only on \(\epsilon(F)\), because subtracting
\(\sigma(\epsilon F)\) makes its argument moment-null. Also \[
r_{qr}(F)\equiv r_q(F)+r_r(F)
\pmod{\mathcal R_{\mathrm{null}}}.
\tag{CS2A.8}
\] Indeed their difference is a rational difference with generating
function \(F(q\,\cdot)-F\), which is moment-null. The sign \(q=-1\)
contributes zero in this quotient, since
\(r_{-1}(F)=\tfrac12r_{-1}(F-F(-\,\cdot))\). Unique factorization,
including negative prime exponents, now gives \[
r_q(F)\equiv
\sum_p v_p(|q|)\,r_p(\sigma(\epsilon F))
=\mathscr T\mathscr L(r_q(F))
\pmod{\mathcal R_{\mathrm{null}}}.
\tag{CS2A.9}
\] Thus \((I-\mathscr T\mathscr L)\mathcal R\subset
\mathcal R_{\mathrm{null}}\). Continuity gives the same inclusion for
closures. It follows that the exact quotient map has the topological
splitting \[
\boxed{
D_{\mathrm{null}}\ \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\ D\oplus V,\qquad
[F]_{\mathrm{null}}\longmapsto([F],\mathscr L F).
}
\tag{CS2A.10}
\] For injectivity, if \(F\in\overline{\mathcal R}\) and
\(\mathscr LF=0\), the closure inclusion puts \(F\) in
\(\overline{\mathcal R_{\mathrm{null}}}\). For surjectivity, start with
any representative \(F\) of the required \(D\)-class and replace it by
\(F+\mathscr T(v-\mathscr LF)\). A continuous inverse sends \(([F],v)\)
to \([F-\mathscr T\mathscr LF+\mathscr T v]_{\mathrm{null}}\).
Independence of the representative and continuity follow from the
closure inclusion and the defining quotient topology. This proves the
full alternative receiver and its connecting maps; the additional
summand records two endpoint directions at every prime.

\subsection{CS3. The overlap, Fourier transform, and all rational
summands}\label{cs3.-the-overlap-fourier-transform-and-all-rational-summands}

On the trivial \(K\)-sector, the source overlap space
\(\bigcap_{\beta\in\mathbb R}\mu^\beta\mathcal S(C_{\mathbb Q})\) is the
Fréchet space \[
H=\left\{h\in C^\infty(\mathbb R_{>0}):
p_{N,j}(h):=\sup_{u>0}(u^N+u^{-N})
|(u\partial_u)^jh(u)|<\infty\quad(N,j\ge0)\right\}.
\tag{CS3.1}
\] To verify both the set and topology, use \(x=\log u\). The ordinary
multiplicative Schwartz seminorms are the polynomially weighted
derivative seminorms in \(x\); the source additionally requires every
factor \(e^{\beta x}\). Derivatives of \(e^{\beta x}h(e^x)\) are finite
sums of \(e^{\beta x}\partial_x^\ell h(e^x)\). Polynomial factors in
\(x\) are bounded by a constant times \(e^{|x|}\), and
\(e^{N|x|}\le e^{Nx}+e^{-Nx}\le2e^{N|x|}\). These identities bound every
source seminorm by finitely many (CS3.1) seminorms and conversely.
Integer \(N\) suffice for every real \(\beta\).

For \(f\in\mathcal S_0^{\mathrm{even}}\), summation of its actual adèlic
representative at \((u,1)\) gives \[
S f(u):=\Sigma(jf)(u)
=\sum_{q\in\mathbb Q^\times}f(qu)1_{\widehat{\mathbb Z}}(q)
=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}}f(nu)
=2\sum_{n\ge1}f(nu).
\tag{CS3.2}
\] The criterion in the middle step is
\(q\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}\cap\mathbb Q=\mathbb Z\). Both rational signs
are present, and the final factor two is retained.

Use the real Fourier convention \[
\widehat f(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}f(v)e^{-2\pi ivt}\,dv.
\tag{CS3.3}
\] Choose the corresponding character of
\(\mathbb A_{\mathbb Q}/\mathbb Q\) and self-dual measures; at finite
places the annihilator of \(\widehat{\mathbb Z}\) is itself, with volume
one. Direct Fourier integration of its indicator is therefore the same
indicator. Consequently the adèlic Fourier transform of
\(f\otimes1_{\widehat{\mathbb Z}}\) is
\(\widehat f\otimes1_{\widehat{\mathbb Z}}\). Fourier transformation
preserves both vanishing endpoint conditions and evenness, and
\(\widehat{\widehat f}=f\) on this even space.

Set \[
(Rh)(u)=u^{-1}h(1/u),\qquad R^2=\mathrm{id}.
\tag{CS3.4}
\] For an arbitrary even real Schwartz function, Poisson summation
retains the exact endpoint identity \[
S f(u)-u^{-1}S\widehat f(1/u)
=u^{-1}\int_{\mathbb R}f(v)\,dv-f(0).
\tag{CS3.5}
\] Indeed the full lattice sums have their zero summands \(f(0)\) and
\(u^{-1}\widehat f(0)\), and \(\widehat f(0)=\int f\). For our actual
moment-null source both terms on the right vanish, so \[
S\widehat f=R S f,\qquad
R S\widehat f=S f.
\tag{CS3.6}
\] This is the exact plus/minus gluing identity, including the factor
\(u^{-1}\).

The map \(S:\mathcal S_0^{\mathrm{even}}\to H\) is continuous. At
\(u\ge1\), each logarithmic derivative of the series is bounded by a
sufficiently high Schwartz seminorm times a convergent sum
\(\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-M}\), \(M>1\). For \(u\le1\), (CS3.6) expresses the
answer through the Fourier transform at \(1/u\ge1\), multiplied by
\(u^{-1}\). Fourier continuity and one additional endpoint weight prove
the required bounds. The same arguments justify termwise
differentiation.

It is also injective. For \(\Re s>1\), absolute convergence and the
substitution \(v=nu\) give \[
\int_0^\infty S f(u)u^s\,\frac{du}{u}
=2\zeta(s)\int_0^\infty f(v)v^s\,\frac{dv}{v}.
\tag{CS3.7}
\] If \(Sf=0\), choose any real \(c>1\). Since \(\zeta(c+it)\ne0\) on
this half-plane, the Mellin transform of \(f|_{\mathbb R_{>0}}\)
vanishes on the line \(c+it\). It is the Fourier transform of the
integrable function \(x\mapsto e^{cx}f(e^x)\), with Fourier frequency
sign fixed by \(e^{itx}\). Fourier injectivity gives \(f(e^x)=0\);
evenness gives \(f=0\) on \(\mathbb R\). This proves the source-sector
injectivity independently of the full adèlic coinvariant theorem.

\subsection{CS4. The actual three-point
sheaf}\label{cs4.-the-actual-three-point-sheaf}

Let \[
X=\{0,\eta,\infty\},\qquad
U_+=\{0,\eta\},\quad U_-=\{\eta,\infty\},\quad
U=\{\eta\}.
\tag{CS4.1}
\] The nonempty opens are \(U,U_+,U_-,X\). The point \(\eta\) is
generic; \(0,\infty\) are closed. These are the authors' base labels.

For each chart retain its two separate endpoint lines, \[
E_+=\mathbb C e_{+,0}\oplus\mathbb C e_{+,1},\qquad
E_-=\mathbb C e_{-,0}\oplus\mathbb C e_{-,1},
\quad
A_\pm=\mathcal S_0^{\mathrm{even}}\oplus E_\pm.
\tag{CS4.2}
\] The index 0 labels the value-at-zero endpoint functional, and index 1
labels the integral endpoint functional. These are the two explicit
summands in the source definition of \(\mathcal S(M)\), separately on
each chart.

Define \[
\mathscr F(U_+)=A_+,\quad
\mathscr F(U_-)=A_-,\quad
\mathscr F(U)=H,
\] \[
r_+(f,e_+)=S f,\qquad
r_-(h,e_-)=R S h=S\widehat h.
\tag{CS4.3}
\] Every endpoint restriction is zero, exactly as in restmaps. Define \[
\mathscr F(X)=\{(a,b)\in A_+\oplus A_-:r_+a=r_-b\},
\qquad \mathscr F(\varnothing)=0.
\tag{CS4.4}
\] Use the evident restrictions from \(X\). The only nontrivial sheaf
gluing condition is the cover \(X=U_+\cup U_-\), and it is precisely
(CS4.4). A cover of \(U_+\) must contain \(U_+\), since no smaller open
contains 0; the same holds for \(U_-\) and \(\infty\). The overlap is a
singleton. Thus these formulas construct a genuine sheaf, including its
topology on every section space.

Under \(j\) on the two source coordinates and (CS1.3) on the overlap,
this is the \(P_K\)-image sheaf of the actual Connes--Consani sheaf.
Averaging commutes with summation, restrictions, and Fourier transform,

idempotent and gives a direct-summand sheaf. This precise use of

representatives.

\subsection{CS5. Ordinary cohomology, including every endpoint
line}\label{cs5.-ordinary-cohomology-including-every-endpoint-line}

Fix the ordered cover \((U_+,U_-)\). Its complex is \[
C^0=A_+\oplus A_-,\qquad C^1=H,\qquad
d(a,b)=r_+a-r_-b.
\tag{CS5.1}
\] This computes ordinary sheaf cohomology in the category of complex
vector-space sheaves, before taking closures. Here is an elementary
derived-functor verification. A sheaf on this space is exactly a diagram
\(D_+\to H\leftarrow D_-\). Global sections are the kernel of the
displayed difference. Short exact sequences of diagrams give short exact
sequences of these two-term complexes and their cohomology long exact
sequences. The degree-one functor is effaceable: embed the diagram into
one with chart spaces \(D_+\oplus H,D_-\oplus H\), overlap \(H\), and
restriction maps the projections to \(H\); embed \(a\) as \((a,r_+a)\),
\(b\) as \((b,r_-b)\), and act identically on \(H\). The new difference
map is onto \(H\), so its degree-one cohomology vanishes. Thus this
effaceable cohomology functor is the right-derived global-section
functor. There are no higher terms, and \[
H^i(X,\mathscr F)=0\quad(i\ge2).
\tag{CS5.2}
\]

By (CS3.6) and injectivity of \(S\), \(d((f,e_+),(h,e_-))=0\) if and
only if \(f=\widehat h\), equivalently \(h=\widehat f\). Consequently \[
\boxed{
H^0(X,\mathscr F)
=\{((f,e_+),(\widehat f,e_-)):
f\in\mathcal S_0^{\mathrm{even}},\ e_+\in E_+,\ e_-\in E_-\}.
}
\tag{CS5.3}
\] This is a topological isomorphism from
\(\mathcal S_0^{\mathrm{even}}\oplus E_+\oplus E_-\) onto the kernel,
because Fourier transformation and the coordinate projections are
continuous. There are four independent endpoint lines, rather than two
endpoint lines identified across the charts.

Write \[
J=S(\mathcal S_0^{\mathrm{even}})\subset H.
\tag{CS5.4}
\] The minus restriction has the same image \(J\), since Fourier
transformation is a bijection of the source. Moreover
\(d((f,0),(0,0))=Sf\). Therefore \[
\operatorname{im}d=J,\qquad
\boxed{H^1_{\mathrm{alg}}(X,\mathscr F)=H/J.}
\tag{CS5.5}
\] The subscript emphasizes that the ordinary cokernel has not been
replaced by a closed-image quotient. Give it the quotient locally convex
topology. Its closure of zero is \(\overline J/J\), and its maximal
Hausdorff quotient is \[
H^1_{\mathrm{Haus}}(X,\mathscr F):=
H/\overline J,\qquad
0\longrightarrow\overline J/J
\longrightarrow H/J
\longrightarrow H/\overline J
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{CS5.6}
\] This sequence is algebraically exact without an additional image
theorem. CS11 subsequently proves \(J=\overline J\), so its left term is

\subsection{CS6. The exact comparison with the existing source

\subsection{CS7. Raw Mellin transform, original zeta, and the Hausdorff
quotient}\label{cs7.-raw-mellin-transform-original-zeta-and-the-hausdorff-quotient}

Retain the two Mellin maps separately: \[
\mathcal M_0h(s)=\int_0^\infty h(u)u^s\,\frac{du}{u},
\qquad
\mathcal Mk(s)=\int_0^\infty k(u)u^{s-1/2}\,\frac{du}{u}.
\tag{CS7.1}
\] They satisfy \[
\mathcal M_0h=2\mathcal M(Uh),\qquad
\Theta:=\mathcal M U=\tfrac12\mathcal M_0.
\tag{CS7.2}
\] The previous theorem S1 proves \(\mathcal M:\mathcal A\to\mathcal B\)
is a topological isomorphism, where \[
\mathcal B=\left\{F\text{ entire}:
b_{A,M}(F):=\sup_{|\Re s|\le A}
(1+|\Im s|)^M|F(s)|<\infty\ \text{for every }A,M\ge0\right\}.
\tag{CS7.3}
\] Consequently \(\Theta\) is a topological isomorphism. For example its
full inverse, on the real line \(c=1/2\), is \[
(\Theta^{-1}F)(u)
=\frac{u^{-1/2}}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
F(1/2+it)u^{-it}\,dt.
\tag{CS7.4}
\] This is \(2u^{-1/2}\) times S1's inverse with coefficient
\(1/(2\pi)\); both factors remain explicit.

Equation (CS3.7) is the raw arithmetic identity \[
\mathcal M_0Sf(s)=
2\zeta(s)\int_0^\infty f(v)v^s\,\frac{dv}{v},
\qquad
\Theta Sf(s)=
\zeta(s)\int_0^\infty f(v)v^s\,\frac{dv}{v}
\quad(\Re s>1).
\tag{CS7.5}
\] For the retained source \[
f_0(v)=\frac{\pi}{2}v^2(2\pi v^2-3)e^{-\pi v^2},
\tag{CS7.6}
\] S2's exact calculation gives \[
\Theta Sf_0(s)=F_0(s)
=\frac{s(s-1)}8\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s),
\qquad
\mathcal M_0Sf_0(s)=2F_0(s)
=\frac{s(s-1)}4\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s).
\tag{CS7.7}
\] The entire continuation and exceptional values are \[
F_0(0)=F_0(1)=\frac18,\qquad
\mathcal M_0Sf_0(0)=\mathcal M_0Sf_0(1)=\frac14,
\tag{CS7.8}
\] \[
F_0(-2r)
=\frac{r(2r+1)(-1)^r\pi^r}{2\,r!}\zeta'(-2r)
=\frac{(1+2r)(2r)}8\pi^{-(1+2r)/2}
\Gamma((1+2r)/2)\zeta(1+2r)\ne0
\quad(r\ge1).
\tag{CS7.9}
\] The raw Mellin value at every such point is \(2F_0(-2r)\). This
retains the endpoint contributions and the Gamma-pole/trivial-zero
cancellation. It does not make the completed product a replacement for
the original zeta function.

For additional endpoint precision, let
\(a_f(s)=\int_0^\infty f(v)v^s\,dv/v\). Its meromorphic continuation has
possible simple poles at \(-2r\), \(r\ge1\), with residues
\(f^{(2r)}(0)/(2r)!\), obtained by subtracting the real Taylor
polynomial on \(0<v<1\). At the original trivial zeros, \[
\mathcal M_0Sf(-2r)
=2\zeta'(-2r)\frac{f^{(2r)}(0)}{(2r)!}.
\tag{CS7.10}
\] At the two endpoints, \(a_f(1)=\tfrac12\int_{\mathbb R}f=0\), the
zeta residue at 1 is one, and \(\zeta(0)=-1/2\), so \[
\mathcal M_0Sf(1)=2a_f'(1),\qquad
\mathcal M_0Sf(0)=-a_f(0).
\tag{CS7.11}
\] Thus the source's two zero moment conditions do not erase these
receiver values.

Let \(\mathscr Z\) be the set of distinct actual nontrivial zeros of the

\mathcal I=

\text{for every }\rho\in\mathscr Z,\ 0\le j<m_\rho\},
\qquad \mathcal Q=\mathcal B/\mathcal I.
\tag{CS7.12}

\boxed{

H^1_{\mathrm{Haus}}(X,\mathscr F)
\xrightarrow[\ \ [h]\mapsto[\tfrac12\mathcal M_0h]\ \ ]{\ \sim\ }
\mathcal Q.

\tag{CS7.13}
\] The map and its inverse are continuous quotient maps induced by the
homeomorphism \(\Theta\), because it takes exactly one closed subspace
to the other. On ordinary cohomology it is the surjection \[
H^1_{\mathrm{alg}}(X,\mathscr F)=H/J
\longrightarrow\mathcal Q,\qquad
\ker=\overline J/J.
\tag{CS7.14}
\] This proves the Hausdorff identification without presupposing
