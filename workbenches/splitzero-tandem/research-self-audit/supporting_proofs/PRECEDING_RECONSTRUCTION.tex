\begin{abstract}
Supported zero is used here as the actual multiplicative identity of the original zero algebra, before localization. The full semilattice adjunction is carried through an independent-support cyclic receiver. At the collision the receiver has a generalized inverse between its original corner images and an invertible action on every zero-support label, while its amplitude is nonzero nilpotent. A reverse edge labelled by supported zero is essential. The full signed collision family, unlike its marked quadratic quotient, has a nonzero heat-equation defect. In the original reciprocal coordinate this defect is $R_{\rm heat}=-6T^2$ and its published holonomy residue satisfies $96\delta(T)=TR_{\rm heat}$. Relative and perfect residue traces detect it; the regular trace kills it through the Jacobian. The actual finite-order transport has a pole whose optimal positive invariant metric degenerates at a calculated rate. The calculated correction to genuine quartic heat preserves the full critical nilpotent and a nonzero, explicitly rescaled transport residue, while making its positive-time local trace positive definite. An independent calculation at the original zeta endpoints shows that pole cancellation contributes exactly $1/16$ to Rodgers--Tao's $H_0$: totalizing the pole first loses that term and creates spurious zeros at $Z=\pm i$. General collision expansions, their energy finite part and a finite error bound give a genuine possible time-zero negativity test. The original inverse-fibre fluid map is reconstructed with its Euclidean forcing and pullback metric, separating it from the released finite-energy Navier--Stokes construction. No RH proof or disproof is obtained; the surviving maps and exact nonzero corrections are the results.
\end{abstract}

\section{Sources, statements, and the question being tested}
This note tests an implication, not an objection based on the classical axiom $0x=0$: can the original invertibility of supported zero force failure of real-rootedness at the original time zero of the Riemann heat family? All local zeros, the absent element, and the nonreduced collision directions remain in the coefficient objects below. Ordinary complex amplitudes occur only through explicitly identified fibre maps.

The foundational inputs are the supplied globalization note, the original projective/monad paper, the semimodule classification in the secondary note, and the full lattice construction in version 11 \cite{Globalization,P1,Secondary,V11}. The original inverse-fibre polynomial and its incompressible lift are read from the complete 480-line programme source \cite{IFH}. They are used as explicit polynomial identities, irrespective of their historical announcement. The supplied original Rodgers--Tao v5 mathematical text was read in full, including its revised boundary-energy section \cite{RT}. The published programme's EHM, ZH, SZW and RD proofs are read at commit \texttt{ffffbe41d458728cbdda0c6e32702ff2c1a8fce0} \cite{EHM,ZH,SZW,RD}. They are mathematical inputs with stated scope, not independent verification certificates.

Rodgers--Tao use
\begin{equation}
 H_t(Z)=\int_0^\infty e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)\cos(Zu)\,du,
 \qquad H_0(Z)=\tfrac18\xi(\tfrac12+iZ/2),
 \quad \partial_tH_t=-\partial_Z^2H_t,                 \label{eq:rt}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 \Phi(u)=\sum_{n\ge1}(2\pi^2n^4e^{9u}-3\pi n^2e^{5u})e^{-\pi n^2e^{4u}}.
                                                        \label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
The statements imported from the analytic literature are that all zeros of $H_t$ are real exactly for $t\ge\Lambda$, that $\Lambda\ge0$, and that $\Lambda\le0.2$ \cite{RT,PT}. The last bound suffices here; no claim about a sharper current upper bound is made. A contradiction can begin with an actual time-zero witness. It need not begin with a numerical positive lower bound for $\Lambda$. Section~\ref{sec:origin} proves how such a witness would propagate.

The 2026 Navier--Stokes paper states a smooth-forced, initially resting, finite-energy solution with terminal velocity blowup \cite{NS}. Its existence proof is not re-established in this note. We compare its stated equation and class with the explicit inverse-fibre fluid construction; we do not replace the released theorem by an informal analogy.

\section{The original zero algebra and the full semilattice adjunction}
Let $R$ be a nonzero commutative unital ring and $L$ a nontrivial bounded distributive lattice. Define precisely
\begin{equation}
 S=G_L(R)=\{z_\lambda=(0,\lambda):\lambda\in L\}
 \cup\{r^\bullet=(r,1_L):r\in R\},\quad
 e=z_{1_L}=0_R^\bullet,\quad\tau=z_{0_L}.                 \label{eq:base}
\end{equation}
The overlap in this union is just $e$. The operations are
\begin{equation}
 (r,\lambda)+(s,\mu)=(r+s,\lambda\vee\mu),\qquad
 (r,\lambda)(s,\mu)=(rs,\lambda\wedge\mu).               \label{eq:ops}
\end{equation}
Nonzero amplitudes occur only at top support. Distributivity follows from distributivity in $R$ and $L$; all the mixed cases follow from $r^\bullet z_\lambda=z_\lambda$.

\begin{proposition}[The unlocalized inverse and its actual domain]
The ideal $eS$ is a semiring with its own identity $e$, and
\begin{equation}
 eS=Z_L\simeq L,\qquad 1_{eS}=e,\qquad e^{-1}=e.
                                                        \label{eq:inverse}
\end{equation}
The inclusion $i:eS\to S$ preserves addition, multiplication and additive zero. The map $p:S\to eS$, $p(s)=es$, is unital and $pi=\operatorname{id}_{eS}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Products with $e$ are exactly the $z_\lambda$, and $ez_\lambda=z_\lambda$. On these elements the two operations are join and meet, proving the semiring and inverse assertions. Also $(es)(et)=est$ and $e(s+t)=es+et$. Finally $p(1_S)=e=1_{eS}$, and the inclusion returns that same element, rather than $1_S$. No localization is involved.
\end{proof}
For every $\lambda$, the corner $z_\lambda S$ similarly has identity $z_\lambda$ and is isomorphic to the lattice $\downarrow\lambda$. This retains every zero label, not only the scalar pair $\{\tau,e\}$.

\begin{theorem}[Both original adjunctions, over the full lattice]
For an $S$-semimodule $M$, put $I_M=eM$. It is an $L$-semimodule. Let $J$ inflate an $L=eS$-semimodule along $p:S\to eS$. Then
\begin{align}
 \Hom_S(M,JN)&\simeq\Hom_L(eM,N),                         \label{eq:adjleft}\\
 \Hom_S(JN,M)&\simeq\Hom_L(N,eM).                         \label{eq:adjright}
\end{align}
The maps $p_M(m)=em$ and $i_M:eM\hookrightarrow M$ satisfy
\begin{equation}
 p_Mi_M=\operatorname{id}_{eM},\quad i_Mp_M=e(-),\quad
 e|_{eM}=\operatorname{id}_{eM}.                           \label{eq:retract}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since $e+e=e$, addition on $eM$ is idempotent, hence its join; its global zero is the bottom. The scalar action factors through $eS$ because $s(em)=(es)(em)$. This proves the $L$-module structure. An $S$-linear $f:M\to JN$ obeys $f(m)=ef(m)=f(em)$, so restricts uniquely to an $L$-linear map on $eM$; the inverse sends $g$ to $gp_M$. An $S$-linear $h:JN\to M$ obeys $h(n)=h(en)=eh(n)$, so factors uniquely through $i_M$. These are inverse constructions on homomorphisms, natural in both variables. Equation~\eqref{eq:retract} is $e^2=e$.
\end{proof}
Every support fibre $M_\lambda=\{m:em=\lambda\}$ is an $R$-module with local additive zero $\lambda$: $m+\lambda=(1_S+e)m=m$, and $m+(-1)^\bullet m=em=\lambda$. For $\lambda\le\mu$ the transition is $m\mapsto m+\mu$. These are exactly the original semilattice-of-modules operations \cite[semimodule section]{Secondary}. In particular, the inverse action of $e$ on $eM$ is compatible with nontrivial amplitude modules above the labels; it does not identify them.

For every $S$-linear $A:M\to M'$,
\begin{equation}
 p_{M'}A=(A|_{eM})p_M,\qquad Ai_M=i_{M'}(A|_{eM}).        \label{eq:naturality}
\end{equation}
Thus support can carry a nontrivial invertible map even when a fibre-amplitude action is nilpotent. Section~\ref{sec:cyclic} calculates precisely this situation.

\subsection{The supplied faithful tensor map}
Use $K=\R_{\ge0,\max}$ with addition $\max$, ordinary multiplication, zero $0$ and unit $1$. The map $\chi:K\to\B$, zero on $0$ and one on every positive number, is a semiring map and retracts $\B\hookrightarrow K$. Hence
\begin{equation}
 L\longrightarrow K\otimes_\B L,\quad\lambda\longmapsto1\otimes\lambda
                                                        \label{eq:tensor}
\end{equation}
is injective: $\chi\otimes\operatorname{id}$ is its left inverse. Consequently
\begin{equation}
 j:S\hookrightarrow R\times(K\otimes_\B L),\qquad
 (r,\lambda)\longmapsto(r,1\otimes\lambda)               \label{eq:jfull}
\end{equation}
preserves both operations and is injective. The amplitude and every join/meet label are retained jointly. In particular $j(e)=(0,1\otimes1_L)$ and $j(1_R^\bullet)=(1_R,1\otimes1_L)$ are distinct. We use this very map below, not a Boolean replacement of $L$.

\subsection{Prime and localization maps}
The ideals are either $\{z_\lambda:\lambda\in\mathfrak a\}$ for a proper lattice ideal $\mathfrak a$, or
\[
 Q_J=Z_L\cup\{r^\bullet:r\in J\},\qquad J\triangleleft R.
\]
Indeed, an ideal containing a top-supported element contains $e$ after multiplication by $e$, then every $z_\lambda$; its amplitudes form a ring ideal. Otherwise its labels are lower and join-closed. Testing multiplication proves that the primes are the lattice-prime ideals and the $Q_\mathfrak q$ for $\mathfrak q\in\Spec R$. Every support prime is below every arithmetic prime. The opens are
\[
 D(z_\lambda)=\{P_\mathfrak p:\lambda\notin\mathfrak p\},\qquad
 D(r^\bullet)=\Spec_{\rm Lat}L\cup D_R(r).
\]
This proves the full ordinal-sum spectrum of the foundation \cite{V11}.

There is also a different map $S[e^{-1}]\simeq L$: an invertible idempotent is its local unit, so every $r^\bullet$ becomes $1_L$, while $z_\lambda$ retains $\lambda$. The original inverse~\eqref{eq:inverse} already existed before this change. We do not use localization in its place.

At a nonreduced coefficient extension, primality must also be transported rather than asserted. In $G_L(\C[T]/T^2)$ the ideal $(e)$ is not prime because $(T^\bullet)^2=e$ with $T^\bullet\notin(e)$. Its arithmetic prime $Q_{(T)}$ contracts to $(e)$ in $G_L(\C)$. This is the exact way the original supported-zero prime meets a nilpotent thickening.

Substitution in~\eqref{eq:fable} verifies all three coordinates of each image.

 P_{a,b,c}(r)=cr^3-2r^2+br-2a,

At $c=0$ there is separately the original point $(0,b,a-4b^2)$. It is not deleted when the quadratic root chart is used.

 (a,b,c)\longmapsto(a+2\theta,b+6c\theta,c),\qquad

\end{equation}
For the fixed initial target $(-1/4,0,0)$, the nonzero-$x$ branches obey

 r^2=\tfrac14-2\theta,\quad \alpha=-2r,\quad x=-\frac1{2r},
 \quad\theta_c=\tfrac18.                                 \label{eq:esc}
\end{equation}
Their full source trajectories, with $q=1-8\theta>0$, are
\begin{equation}
 \gamma_\pm(\theta)=
 (\pm q^{-1/2},\mp\tfrac32q^{1/2},\tfrac{13}2q).
                                                        \label{eq:gamma}
\end{equation}
The third point is on the distinct $x=0$ component.

\begin{proposition}[Two explicit, different time identifications]
The direct Newman time is $t_{\rm dir}=-\theta$. In the original real coordinate $r$, its collision is at $t_{\rm dir}=-1/8$, not at zero. A second, rotated and reanchored identification is
\begin{equation}
 t=4\theta-\tfrac12,\qquad Z=-2ir,\qquad
 2P_{-1/4+2\theta,0,0}(iZ/2)=Z^2-2t.                   \label{eq:clock}
\end{equation}
It sends the original initial time to $t=-1/2$ and the escape to $t=0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:forward} changes to backward heat under $t_{\rm dir}=-\theta$. For~\eqref{eq:clock}, $P=-2r^2+1/2-4\theta$; substitution of $r=iZ/2$ gives $2P=Z^2+1-8\theta=Z^2-2t$. Both the factor two and the time shift are retained. Its roots at the original initial target are $Z=\pm i$; after the escape they are real. The involution $r^\#=-r$ and conjugation of scalars gives $Z^\#=Z$. Thus the new real structure is explicitly transported rather than identified with coefficient conjugation in the original $r$-chart.
\end{proof}
The polynomial $Z^2-2t$ is an actual backward heat solution with the same differential operator as~\eqref{eq:rt}. Equation~\eqref{eq:clock} does not assert equality of this polynomial's initial datum with $H_0$. The difference is now a specified initial-data question, not an untracked change of clock.

\section{The independent-support cyclic receiver at the collision}\label{sec:cyclic}
Work on the free semimodule $M=S^2$, with structural off-diagonal zeros $\tau$, for $S=G_L(\C)$. For real $t$ define
\begin{equation}
 C_t=\begin{pmatrix}\tau&(it)^\bullet\\(-2i)^\bullet&\tau\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \mathcal H_t=\begin{pmatrix}4^\bullet&\tau\\\tau&(2t)^\bullet\end{pmatrix}.
                                                        \label{eq:cyclic}
\end{equation}
Conjugate transpose preserves every label. Direct semiring multiplication gives
\begin{equation}
 C_t^2=(2t)^\bullet I,\qquad C_t^*\mathcal H_t=\mathcal H_tC_t.
                                                        \label{eq:cycliclaws}
\end{equation}
These equations are valid also at $t=0$, where $(2t)^\bullet=e$, not $\tau$.

\begin{theorem}[A genuine inverse on original corner images]
At $t=0$ put
\begin{equation}
 B_0=\begin{pmatrix}\tau&(i/2)^\bullet\\e&\tau\end{pmatrix},
 \quad P=\diag(\one,e),\quad Q=\diag(e,\one).
                                                        \label{eq:partialinverse}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{gather}
 B_0C_0=P,\quad C_0B_0=Q,\quad C_0B_0C_0=C_0,\quad B_0C_0B_0=B_0,\label{eq:regularinverse}\\
 P^2=P,\quad Q^2=Q,\quad P+Q=I,\quad PQ=QP=eI.           \label{eq:corners}
\end{gather}
Moreover $C_0:\im P\to\im Q$ is invertible with inverse $B_0$, and its restriction to $eM=L^2$ is the invertible exchange of the two independent labels.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The nonzero diagonal products of $B_0C_0$ are $(i/2)^\bullet(-2i)^\bullet=\one$ and $e^2=e$. In the other order they are reversed. All off-diagonal products are absent. The remaining equations follow by multiplication, using $1_R^\bullet+e=1_R^\bullet$.
The two images are $\im P=S\times Z_L$ and $\im Q=Z_L\times S$. For $(x,z_\lambda)$ the map $C_0$ gives $(z_\lambda,(-2i)^\bullet x)$ and $B_0$ returns it. On $(z_\lambda,z_\mu)$ the maps both give $(z_\mu,z_\lambda)$. Their square is the identity on $eM$ by~\eqref{eq:retract}. This retains every label of $L^2$.
\end{proof}
The intersection of the two images is $eM$, not the absent vector. Their amplitude images are complementary ordinary lines, but their full semimodule intersection is the common semilattice. This is precisely where forgetting supported zero loses information.

Let the structural one-edge map be
\[
 N=\begin{pmatrix}\tau&\tau\\\one&\tau\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then $N^2$ is the globally absent matrix, whereas
\begin{equation}
 C_0=(-2i)^\bullet N+eN^*,\qquad C_0^2=eI,
 \qquad \mathcal H_0C_0=e\begin{pmatrix}\tau&\one\\\one&\tau\end{pmatrix}.
                                                        \label{eq:ghost}
\end{equation}
The reverse edge $eN^*$ cannot be deleted. The last matrix is nonzero in the full semiring even though its amplitude is zero. Odd powers of $C_0$ have absent trace $\tau$; even positive powers have trace $e$. A cycle which has not returned and a returned cycle whose amplitude is zero therefore remain different.

For comparison, the common-support retract is
\[
 P_{\rm sync}=\begin{pmatrix}\one&e\\e&\one\end{pmatrix}.
\]
It is idempotent, commutes with $C_0$, and maps $(z_\lambda,z_\mu)$ to $(z_{\lambda\vee\mu},z_{\lambda\vee\mu})$. Its relative cyclic trace is $\Tr(C_0P_{\rm sync})=e$, unlike $\Tr C_0=\tau$. This is an explicit comparison to synchronization, not a substitution of that smaller object for the independent-support receiver.

\subsection{Positivity with the support still present}
Use the intrinsic ordered cone
\begin{equation}
 S_+=Z_L\cup\{a^\bullet:a\in\R_{>0}\}=\{x^*x:x\in S\}.
                                                        \label{eq:cone}
\end{equation}
The equality follows by taking square roots of positive amplitudes and by $z_\lambda^2=z_\lambda$. From~\eqref{eq:cyclic},
\begin{equation}
 v^*\mathcal H_tv\in S_+\ \text{for every }v\in S^2
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad t\ge0.                   \label{eq:positive}
\end{equation}
Sufficiency is the sum of the two nonnegative amplitudes, with the actual joined labels. For necessity take $v=(\tau,\one)$ when $t<0$. At the collision the second direction has supported-zero value, not absence. Thus the inverse in~\eqref{eq:regularinverse}, the nontrivial amplitude nilpotent, and full semiring positivity coexist.

In the ordinary trace basis $(1,Z)$ of $\C[Z]/(Z^2-2t)$, the amplitude companion is $M_Z=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&2t\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and its reflected trace form is $\diag(2,4t)$. The monomial coordinate map
\begin{equation}
 V=\diag(1/\sqrt2,-i\sqrt2),\quad VM_ZV^{-1}=\amp C_t,
 \quad V^*\amp(\mathcal H_t)V=\diag(2,4t)                \label{eq:traceiso}
\end{equation}
preserves independent support labels when lifted. It is the exact link to the original shell receiver, where $N=D/2$, $N^*N=P_{<}$ and $NN^*=P_{>}$:
\[
 C_t=-2iN+itN^*,\qquad\mathcal H_t=4P_{<}+2tP_{>}.
\]
The infinite shell version means the bounded direct sum of these maps on its stated two sectors; no finite trace is assigned to an infinite identity.

The positive norm energy and the signed trace energy are different even in these coordinates:
\begin{equation}
 (\amp C_t)^*(\amp C_t)=\diag(4,t^2),\qquad
 \amp\mathcal H_t-(\amp C_t)^*(\amp C_t)=\diag(0,2t-t^2).
                                                        \label{eq:energydefect}
\end{equation}
The matrix family is regular at zero: $C_0^{\rm amp}C'_0+C'_0C_0^{\rm amp}=2I$. Its individual root coordinates need not have finite derivatives there.

\section{The full length-four family has a heat defect}\label{sec:heatdefect}
\src{The local family and its reciprocal coordinate are EHM27--28. The following comparison with the whole heat equation, including its exact correction, is calculated here.}

Let $\eta=h-1/8$ be the original displacement and put
\begin{equation}
 \beta=\frac{\sqrt{9+64\eta}-3}{2},\quad\beta(\beta+3)=16\eta,
 \quad
 \mathscr L=\frac{\C\{\eta\}[\epsilon,T]}
 {(\epsilon^2-\beta\epsilon,\ T^2+16\eta-(6+3\beta)\epsilon)}.
                                                        \label{eq:localfamily}
\end{equation}
The square root is its analytic branch with value three. The free basis is $1,\epsilon,T,\epsilon T$. At $\eta=0$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathscr L_0=\C[\epsilon,T]/(\epsilon^2,T^2-6\epsilon)
 \simeq\C[T]/(T^4),\qquad \epsilon=T^2/6.                \label{eq:lengthfour}
\end{equation}
This last isomorphism is over $\C$; its integral counterpart is retained in Section~\ref{sec:residue}.

Use exactly the marked receiver's clock and coordinate
\begin{equation}
 t=4\eta,\qquad Z=-iT/\sqrt2,\quad
 t=\frac{\beta(\beta+3)}4,\quad\beta'=\frac4{2\beta+3}.
                                                        \label{eq:quarticclock}
\end{equation}
Eliminating $\epsilon$ in~\eqref{eq:localfamily} gives
\[
 T^4-\beta^2T^2-\beta^2(\beta+3)(2\beta+3)=0.
\]
The resulting full monic polynomial is
\begin{align}
 Q_t(Z)&=Z^4+\tfrac12\beta^2Z^2-\tfrac14\beta^2(\beta+3)(2\beta+3)\notag\\
       &=(Z^2-2t)\left(Z^2+\tfrac12\beta(2\beta+3)\right).
                                                        \label{eq:quartic}
\end{align}
The first factor is the marked quadratic receiver. The second factor is part of the same local algebra, not an optional extra.

\begin{theorem}[Exact heat defect and its correction]
In the unchanged $Z,t$ coordinates,
\begin{equation}
 (\partial_t+\partial_Z^2)Q_t
 =\frac{4(7\beta+9)}{2\beta+3}Z^2
 -\frac{6\beta(\beta+1)(\beta+3)}{2\beta+3}.              \label{eq:fullres}
\end{equation}
At the collision its class in $\mathscr L_0$ is
\begin{equation}
 R_{\rm heat}=12Z^2=-6T^2=-36\epsilon\ne0.               \label{eq:rheat}
\end{equation}
The genuine unforced quartic heat solution with initial value $Z^4$ is
\begin{equation}
 P_t^{\rm heat}=Z^4-12tZ^2+12t^2.                        \label{eq:trueheat}
\end{equation}
Its exact difference from the original signed family is
\begin{equation}
 Q_t-P_t^{\rm heat}
 =\frac{\beta(7\beta+18)}2Z^2
 -\frac{\beta^2(\beta+3)(5\beta+12)}4.                   \label{eq:truecorrection}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Differentiate the first line of~\eqref{eq:quartic} with $\beta'=4/(2\beta+3)$ and add $12Z^2+\beta^2$. This gives~\eqref{eq:fullres}. At zero, $Z^2=-T^2/2=-3\epsilon$, proving~\eqref{eq:rheat}. Direct differentiation of~\eqref{eq:trueheat} gives zero heat residual and its initial value is $Z^4$; coefficient recursion proves uniqueness among quartics. Substituting $t=\beta(\beta+3)/4$ and subtracting gives~\eqref{eq:truecorrection}.
\end{proof}
This calculation explains exactly why the positive marked quotient cannot certify the whole family. At $t=0$ the quotient is $\mathscr L_0\twoheadrightarrow\C[T]/T^2$, killing $\mathfrak N^2=(T^2)$, and it kills $R_{\rm heat}$. Away from zero, keeping that same marked factor is incompatible with unforced quartic heat:
\begin{equation}
 P_t^{\rm heat}\bmod(Z^2-2t)=-8t^2,\qquad
 (\partial_t+\partial_Z^2)Q_t\bmod(Z^2-2t)=16t.           \label{eq:quotientfailure}
\end{equation}
The second-order differential operator does not descend through this moving root quotient. Equations~\eqref{eq:truecorrection} and~\eqref{eq:quotientfailure} are the complete correction, rather than a claim that the two families cannot be related.


\subsection{A complete no-extension test, including the analytic unit}
The defect is not repaired merely by restoring a nonvanishing factor omitted in monic division. Suppose $G$ is analytic near $(0,0)$ and $G(0,0)\ne0$. Since $Q_0=Z^4$ and $\partial_tQ_t|_0=0$,
\begin{equation}
 (\partial_t+\partial_Z^2)(GQ)\big|_{t=0}
 =12G(0,Z)Z^2+8G_Z(0,Z)Z^3+(G_t+G_{ZZ})(0,Z)Z^4.
                                                        \label{eq:unitobstruction}
\end{equation}
Its coefficient of $Z^2$ is $12G(0,0)\ne0$. Thus no analytic unit makes the unchanged signed family an unforced heat solution.

Even adding further analytic colliding factors cannot make this moving quartic a divisor of a nonzero analytic heat solution. For $t>0$ small, it has the two roots
\[

\section{A complete local collision expansion and a time-zero test}\label{sec:origin}
This section calculates a singularity at the endpoint itself, rather than requiring the argument to start with a numerical positive time. It concerns an actual jointly analytic solution of the backward heat equation. Translating a proposed collision $(t_0,z_0)$ to local coordinates $(s,z)=(t-t_0,Z-z_0)$ does not identify $t_0$ with the original arithmetic time zero.

Suppose
\begin{equation}
 H_s=-H_{zz},\qquad
 H(0,z)=c_m z^m(1+a z+bz^2+O(z^3)),\quad c_m\ne0,\quad m\ge2.
                                                        \label{eq:localdata}
\end{equation}
Assume the germ is real for real $s,z$. Define the monic heat Hermite polynomials
\begin{equation}
 P_m(u)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}
 \frac{(-1)^km!}{k!(m-2k)!}u^{m-2k}.                    \label{eq:hermite}
\end{equation}
They are orthogonal for the positive Gaussian weight $e^{-u^2/4}$ by repeated integration by parts in the Rodrigues formula
$P_m=(-2)^m e^{u^2/4}(d/du)^m e^{-u^2/4}$. Thus they have $m$ simple real roots $\lambda_j$: otherwise multiplying the distinct real sign-changing factors of $P_m$ would give a polynomial of degree below $m$ whose product with $P_m$ has constant nonzero sign, contradicting orthogonality. This standard argument also rules out missing real roots or even multiplicities.

\begin{theorem}[Roots, full ordered energy, and determinant finite parts]
The $m$ roots near the collision have, for $s>0$ small,
\begin{equation}
 z_j(s)=\lambda_j\sqrt{s}+2as+(2b-a^2)\lambda_j s^{3/2}+O(s^2).
                                                        \label{eq:rootexpansion}
\end{equation}
Their ordered inverse-gap energy is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{E_m(s):=\sum_{j\ne k}(z_j(s)-z_k(s))^{-2}
 =\frac{m(m-1)}{8s}+\frac{m(m-1)}4(a^2-2b)+O(s).}       \label{eq:energyfinite}
\end{equation}
For the monic cluster polynomial $Q_s$, the positive-side discriminant satisfies
\begin{align}
 \log\Disc Q_s={}&\log D_m+\tfrac12m(m-1)\log s
                  +m(m-1)(2b-a^2)s+O(s^2),\notag\\
 D_m={}&2^{m(m-1)/2}\prod_{j=1}^m j^j.                  \label{eq:discfinite}
\end{align}
All $m$ roots are real and simple for positive sufficiently small $s$. On the negative side, all except the possible central branch for odd $m$ are nonreal.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $w=\sqrt{s}$ and divide $H(w^2,wu)$ by $c_mw^m$. Analyticity and the heat coefficient recursion give the convergent expansion on compact $u$ sets
\[
 F(w,u)=P_m(u)+a w P_{m+1}(u)+b w^2P_{m+2}(u)+O(w^3).
\]
The implicit function theorem at each simple $\lambda_j$ gives analytic roots $u_j(w)$. The recurrence $P_{m+1}=uP_m-2P'_m$ and differential equation $2P_m''-uP'_m+mP_m=0$ imply, at a root,
\[
 P_{m+1}=-2P_m',\quad P_{m+1}'=0,\quad
 P_m''/P_m'=\lambda_j/2,\quad P_{m+2}=-2\lambda_jP_m'.
\]
Substitute $u_j=\lambda_j+\alpha_jw+\gamma_jw^2+O(w^3)$ into $F=0$. The first coefficient gives $\alpha_j=2a$; the second gives $\gamma_j=(2b-a^2)\lambda_j$. Multiplication by $w$ proves~\eqref{eq:rootexpansion}.

For the leading gap sum, the logarithmic derivative identities at a simple root give
\[
 \sum_{k\ne j}\frac1{\lambda_j-\lambda_k}=\frac{\lambda_j}{4},\qquad
 \sum_{k\ne j}\frac1{(\lambda_j-\lambda_k)^2}
 =\frac{m-1}{6}-\frac{\lambda_j^2}{48}.
\]
The second identity follows from
$P_m'''/P_m'=\lambda_j^2/4-(m-1)/2$ and
$\sum_{k\ne j}(\lambda_j-\lambda_k)^{-2}
=(P_m''/(2P_m'))^2-P_m'''/(3P_m')$.
The coefficient of $u^{m-2}$ in~\eqref{eq:hermite} gives $\sum_j\lambda_j^2=2m(m-1)$. Summing proves the leading constant $m(m-1)/8$.

The common $2as$ root translation cancels in every difference. The next difference multiplier is $1+(2b-a^2)s$, so the energy's constant term is $-2(2b-a^2)m(m-1)/8$. The remainder improves from a possible half-power to $O(s)$ because the symmetric root-gap expression in the scaled roots is analytic and even in $w$: $F(-w,u)=(-1)^mF(w,-u)$ permutes and negates all the roots. This proves~\eqref{eq:energyfinite}.

For completeness, $P_m'=mP_{m-1}$. The recurrence and the resultant product over roots give recursively the Hermite discriminant in~\eqref{eq:discfinite}: $D_m=2^{m-1}m^mD_{m-1}$, with $D_1=1$. Each squared root difference supplies a factor $s[1+2(2b-a^2)s+O(s^{3/2})]$. Taking the product over $m(m-1)/2$ pairs gives~\eqref{eq:discfinite}; symmetry again makes the relative series analytic in $s$.

For real positive $s$, each scaled real-coefficient root disk contains exactly one zero and is invariant under conjugation, so that zero is real. For negative $s$, use $w=i\sqrt{|s|}$. The leading roots $i\lambda_j\sqrt{|s|}$ with $\lambda_j\ne0$ are nonreal; an isolated zero disk preserves this property. The odd central branch is the only possible exception.
\end{proof}

\begin{align}
 z_j'&=2\sum_{k\ne j}\frac1{z_j-z_k}+2\gamma_s(z_j),      \label{eq:environment}\\
 \frac d{ds}\log\Disc Q_s
 &=4E_m(s)+4\sum_{j<k}\frac{\gamma_s(z_j)-\gamma_s(z_k)}{z_j-z_k}.
                                                        \label{eq:discenv}
\end{align}
The first follows by differentiating $H_s(z_j)=0$ and using the heat equation; the second follows by differentiating the full product of gaps. In the pair sum, the three-index terms cancel, leaving $4E_m$. Thus the unit $g$ is not silently discarded when a cluster is isolated.

\begin{corollary}[An actual endpoint finite-part disproof test]
Suppose $z_0$ is a real multiple zero of the actual $H_0$, of multiplicity $m$. If
\begin{equation}
 a^2-2b<0,\quad
 a=\frac{H_0^{(m+1)}(z_0)}{(m+1)H_0^{(m)}(z_0)},\quad
 b=\frac{H_0^{(m+2)}(z_0)}{(m+1)(m+2)H_0^{(m)}(z_0)},    \label{eq:nativetest}
\end{equation}
then $H_0$ has a nonreal zero. Thus~\eqref{eq:nativetest}, if established for the actual function, would be a time-zero route to a disproof without first locating a positive-time collision.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Assume all zeros of $H_0$ are real. Its order-one Hadamard product, after removing the zero at $z_0$, gives for the nonvanishing factor $g$ at that point
\[
 (\log g)''(z_0)=-\sum_{\rho\ne z_0}\frac{m_\rho}{(z_0-\rho)^2}\le0.
\]
The sum converges absolutely and the linear exponential factor has zero second derivative. On the other hand $(\log g)''(z_0)=2b-a^2$. This contradicts~\eqref{eq:nativetest}.
\end{proof}
No such negative value or actual multiple zeta zero is established here. The pole $m(m-1)/(8s)$ alone is compatible with all zeros being real at the collision; its complete finite part is a more discriminating target.

There is also no zero at the spatial origin of~\eqref{eq:rt}: every summand of $\Phi(u)$ is positive for $u\ge0$ because $2\pi n^2e^{4u}>3$, hence $H_t(0)>0$ for all real $t$. This is a separate spatial statement, not a substitution of spatial zero for the user's time-zero question.

\subsection{A second exact heat-to-fluid map and its force defect}
For any nonvanishing heat germ $\phi(s,x)=H_{-\nu s}(x)$, the original backward heat equation gives $\phi_s=\nu\phi_{xx}$. Put
\begin{equation}
 v=-2\nu\partial_x\log\phi.
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
 \mathsf U_s+(\mathsf U\cdot\nabla)\mathsf U-\nu\Delta\mathsf U
 =(0,-vv_x,0).                                         \label{eq:burgersforce}
\end{equation}
For the original quadratic collision $\phi=x^2+2\nu s$,
\[
 v=-\frac{4\nu x}{x^2+2\nu s},\qquad

\]
The velocity singularity therefore comes with a singular, noncompact force under this explicit shear map. No smooth-force Navier--Stokes counterexample is supplied by this particular transport.
