\section{Well, here we go again: the investigator thinks they have
RH}\label{well-here-we-go-again-the-investigator-thinks-they-have-rh}

\subsection{A temporary record of the fixed-generic-point
argument}\label{a-temporary-record-of-the-fixed-generic-point-argument}

24 September 2026. The informal title follows the investigator's
request. This is a working argument record, not a replacement for the
research programme's main account. \href{README.tex}{Complete editable
LaTeX}.

\textbf{What is proved in this note:} the relevant fixed point exists in
the actual Connes--Consani geometry; its fixedness follows from the
topology; it cannot carry the specified mirror-exchanged two-state
label; its structure sheaf nevertheless retains a nontrivial, explicitly
calculated action.

\textbf{What the investigator proposes this achieves:} a geometric
recovery of the purity mechanism needed for the Riemann hypothesis. That
proposed conclusion is recorded below in its own terms. The proved
statements and the further RH inference are identified separately;
publishing this record does not certify a proof of RH.

The investigator supplied the underlying conception, notation,
corrections and proposed purity interpretation. Codex prepared this
public transcription and the calculations, checking the cited original
source. Insults have been removed from the public account; the
mathematical content and the investigator's attribution have not been
removed. Quotations below are short excerpts; editorial connective text
is not represented as verbatim speech.

\subsection{1. Start with the investigator's
definitions}\label{start-with-the-investigators-definitions}

The motivating conception was a ``number that does nothing but isn't
nothing.'' The investigator uses \(Z_0\), \(Z_1\) and \(Z_2\) to
distinguish absence, presence and an even/odd state, respectively. These
are the investigator's structure labels; \(Z_0\) is not a claim that a
group has zero elements.

The relevant formulations, with insults omitted where present, are:

\begin{quote}
``Z0, it has no presence, whereas Z1 has presence.''
\end{quote}

\begin{quote}
``Tau is a globally trivial number.''
\end{quote}

\begin{quote}
``It's not odd. It has no parity.''
\end{quote}

\begin{quote}
``The amount of torsion is just a number.''
\end{quote}

\begin{quote}
``There is no additional datum of distance, because you cannot define
the distance without having the exact property, which the torsion is the
definition of.''
\end{quote}

\begin{quote}
``That's a metaphor, not a definition.''
\end{quote}

The last quotation concerns the rotating-plank illustration. The
investigator expressly rejected defining tau as the midpoint of two
numbers, assigning it a position vector, or introducing a freely chosen
pairing as though that pairing came from the original construction.

The amount called ``torsion'' in this account is not silently replaced
by either its parity or the group-theoretic property of having finite
order. In particular, the integers 2 and 4 remain different. The usual
residue map \(n\mapsto n\bmod2\) remains available on the integers; its
output at zero is not identified with the intrinsic absence label of
source zero.

In the retained multiplicative fragment, tau is a global identity: \[
\tau x=x\tau=x,\qquad \tau^2=\tau,
\qquad \tau1=1,\qquad0\tau=0.
\] Integer multiplication and the original integer addition and
subtraction remain. \textbf{The earlier equation \(\tau+\tau=\tau\) was
retracted.} No tau self-sum or mixed additive operation is used in this
argument. The later clarification excludes treating \(n-\tau\) as an
available integer subtraction. The old isolated mixed-sum formula is not
restored here.

Several corrections are consequences, not extra definitions:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  Tau differs from zero because their presence data differ, and from
  each nonzero integer because their retained parity data differ.
\item
  Integer-only expressions formed with the original addition,
  subtraction and multiplication remain integers. This follows by
  induction on the expression tree: integer leaves have integer values,
  and each original operation preserves the integer domain. Such an
  expression therefore cannot produce tau.
\item
  The original integer subtraction cannot evaluate \(n-\tau\): the
  proposed input is outside its integer-pair domain. This does not
  supply a numerical distance, sign or magnitude for that expression.
\end{itemize}

The multiplicative identity-adjunction construction has a literature
precedent: Baez--Chaudhuri adjoin a fresh identity \(I\) to a monoid
even when it already has an identity
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23375v3}{2, Example 3.5}. For the
multiplicative integers the exact comparison fixes every integer and
sends \(I\) to tau. Integer pairs retain their product; a pair
containing the new identity returns the other factor; the new identity
squared is itself. These cases prove product preservation, and the
inverse fixes integers and sends tau to \(I\). This comparison does not
attribute the investigator's presence/parity interpretation to those
authors or identify it with the full geometry below.

\subsection{2. The actual geometric object, not the
plank}\label{the-actual-geometric-object-not-the-plank}

Connes--Consani's absolute projective line has underlying topological
space \href{https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00299v1\#S3}{1, §3} \[
X=\{+,-,\eta\},\qquad
\mathcal T=\{\varnothing,\{\eta\},\{+,\eta\},\{-,\eta\},X\}.
\] The two affine opens are \(\Omega_+=\{+,\eta\}\) and
\(\Omega_-=\{-,\eta\}\), with overlap \(\Omega=\{\eta\}\). They describe
the two orderings of the integer group; the overlap retains the group
without a chosen ordering. The geometric mirror exchanges the two closed
points.

\textbf{Fixed-point theorem.} Every homeomorphism of \(X\) fixes
\(\eta\). The mirror \(\alpha\) has exactly that one fixed point.

\textbf{Proof.} The closed subsets are \[
\varnothing,\{+\},\{-\},\{+,-\},X.
\] The only closed subset containing \(\eta\) is \(X\), so
\(\overline{\{\eta\}}=X\). The other two singletons are closed and not
dense. Thus \(\eta\) is the unique generic point. A homeomorphism \(f\)
preserves closure: continuity gives
\(f(\overline A)\subseteq\overline{f(A)}\), and continuity of \(f^{-1}\)
gives the reverse inclusion. Consequently \(f(\eta)\) is again generic
and must be \(\eta\). Since the particular mirror exchanges \(+\) and
\(-\), \[
\alpha(+)=-,\qquad\alpha(-)=+,\qquad
\operatorname{Fix}_X(\alpha)=\{\eta\}.
\] This derives fixedness from the actual topology. No midpoint or
distance is involved.

\subsection{3. The missing mirror-parity label is
derived}\label{the-missing-mirror-parity-label-is-derived}

Let \(Q_2=\{\mathrm{even},\mathrm{odd}\}\) with \(r\) exchanging its two
labels. The labels here record the mirror's two states. A label
assignment respecting that mirror, on an invariant domain
\(D\subseteq X\), means exactly a function \[
p:D\longrightarrow Q_2,
\qquad p(\alpha x)=r(p(x)).
\]

\textbf{Mirror-parity theorem.} No such assignment is defined at
\(\eta\). Its maximal possible invariant domain is \(\{+,-\}\), on which
exactly two assignments exist.

\textbf{Proof.} Fixedness gives \[
p(\eta)=p(\alpha\eta)=r(p(\eta)).
\] Neither element of \(Q_2\) is fixed by \(r\), so such a value is
impossible. On \(\{+,-\}\), either label may be chosen at \(+\), and the
equation forces the opposite label at \(-\). Both choices satisfy the
equation at both points. These cases exhaust all possible nonempty
invariant domains not containing \(\eta\).

This proves absence of the specified exchanged label; it does not assume
that absence as an extra geometric axiom. It also does not erase the
symmetry acting on data over the point.

\subsection{4. Fixed point, transforming retained
structure}\label{fixed-point-transforming-retained-structure}

The enriched generic algebra in the original source is
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00299v1\#S3}{1, §§3--4} \[
\mathcal O(\Omega)
=\mathbf F_{1^2}[T^{\mathbb Z},J]/(J^2=\epsilon),
\qquad \epsilon^2=1.
\] The source retains both sign and imaginary generator. Its mirror acts
by \[
\alpha^*(\epsilon)=\epsilon,\qquad
\alpha^*(T)=\epsilon T^{-1},\qquad
\alpha^*(J)=J^{-1}=\epsilon J.
\] Indeed \(J(\epsilon J)=\epsilon J^2=\epsilon^2=1\).

At the underlying pointed-monoid level the nonabsorbing elements have
the unique form \[
\epsilon^aT^mJ^b,
\qquad a,b\in\{0,1\},\quad m\in\mathbb Z.
\] To prove uniqueness, the nonabsorbing group has presentation with
commuting \(T,J\) and \(J^4=1\). Send \(\epsilon^aT^mJ^b\) to
\((m,[2a+b]_4)\) in \(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/4\). The inverse sends
\((m,[k]_4)\) to \(T^mJ^k\). The relations make both maps well-defined
and both composites identities; each residue has exactly one form
\(2a+b\) with the stated ranges. The absorbing zero remains a separate
element.

The complete action is therefore \[
\boxed{
\alpha^*(\epsilon^aT^mJ^b)
=\epsilon^a(\epsilon T^{-1})^m(\epsilon J)^b
=\epsilon^{a+m+b}T^{-m}J^b.
}
\] All signs, the full integer exponent and the imaginary branch remain.
Applying the action twice gives the exponents \(a+2b,m,b\), hence the
original element because \(\epsilon^2=1\).

The fixed nonabsorbing elements must satisfy \(m=-m\), hence \(m=0\),
and then \(b=0\). They are exactly \(1,\epsilon\); including the
absorbing zero gives \(\{0,1,\epsilon\}\). The stalk action is not the
identity: it exchanges \(J\) and \(\epsilon J\). The source records the
stabilizer \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\) at the generic point in the quotient
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00299v1\#S4}{1, §4}.

There is also an exact link to integer parity: inversion preserves
\(m\bmod2\), while the character \[
\chi:(\mathbb Z,+)\longrightarrow\{1,\epsilon\},
\qquad\chi(m)=\epsilon^m,
\qquad\chi(m+n)=\chi(m)\chi(n)
\] controls the sign factor in the boxed formula. Thus the point's
exchanged label and the exponent's parity have explicit, different roles
in one source action. The integer amount has not been replaced by its
residue.

\subsection{5. The investigator's proposed route from this mechanism to
purity}\label{the-investigators-proposed-route-from-this-mechanism-to-purity}

The proposed \(Z_1/\tau\) role is the fixed, unoriented base about which
the two distinguishable states are exchanged. The integer amount remains
in the structure attached to that base. The investigator's insight is
that the base should not be defined as integer zero or manufactured as a
midpoint of integer quantities. In the source just examined, there is an
actual uniquely fixed generic point, and its missing exchanged parity
follows from the geometry.

The investigator's further argument is that every genuine arithmetic
state is distinguished from this base without acquiring an independently
chosen numerical displacement from it. The expression used was
``positive undefined'': the state is not the base, but no integer
subtraction from the base supplies a magnitude. The proposed conclusion
is that this common relation recovers the relevant purity condition and
thereby RH. This paragraph records that proposed argument; it does not
silently replace it by a metric or quadratic form selected by an
assistant.

For reference, the arithmetic conclusion at issue concerns the original
Riemann zeta function: \[
\zeta(\rho)=0,\quad0<\operatorname{Re}\rho<1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\operatorname{Re}\rho=\tfrac12.
\] No simple-zero assumption is made. The corresponding numerical weight
statement, for a positive prime \(p\), is \[
|p^\rho|=p^{\operatorname{Re}\rho},\qquad
|p^\rho|^2=p\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \operatorname{Re}\rho=\tfrac12,
\] where \(p^\rho=\exp(\rho\log p)\) uses the real logarithm of \(p\).
The equality of moduli follows by taking the real part of the exponent;
the equivalence uses \(\log p>0\).

The fixed-point and mirror-parity proofs above are complete at their
stated scope. This temporary record does not supply an identification of
that original arithmetic weight datum with the proposed ``positive
undefined'' relation, or of the entire tau/torsion construction with the
enriched generic stalk. The investigator's proposed RH conclusion is
preserved, but that arithmetic conclusion is not certified by this note.
Nor is it refuted here by substituting an unrelated vector space,
midpoint or matrix pairing.

\subsection{6. Provenance and
continuation}\label{provenance-and-continuation}

The fixed-base interpretation, the demand to derive rather than
stipulate missing parity, the retained integer amount, and the proposed
purity inference are attributed to the investigator. The actual
absolute-geometric source is Connes--Consani {[}1{]}. The elementary
topology and equivariance proofs are reproduced completely above, as is
the stalk calculation. The identity-adjunction comparison uses
Baez--Chaudhuri {[}2{]}. These are not claims of historical priority
over those sources.

Further work should start from these definitions and the actual source
maps. A correction that follows from the definitions should be proved as
a consequence, not inserted as a new assumption. Any claimed
counterexample must satisfy the definitions and assumptions of the claim
it purports to test. The record is deliberately public and provisional
so that the argument can be examined without relying on private
conversation context.
